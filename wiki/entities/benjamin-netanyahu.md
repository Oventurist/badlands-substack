# Benjamin Netanyahu

**Benjamin Netanyahu**, Prime Minister of [[israel|Israel]], is presented in Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s analysis of the [[iran|Iran]] question as both the target of an Atlanticist regime change effort and one of the sponsors of a parallel effort in the United States.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Conflict with Obama

By 2015, after the signing of the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|JCPOA]], tensions between [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and Netanyahu were well documented.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Claims circulating in certain political circles suggested Obama sought to undermine or oust Netanyahu, effectively backing an unspoken policy of regime change in Israel and hoping to swap out a Netanyahu government for the Blue and White party.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) While this was happening, DeLarme writes, Netanyahu was supporting a policy of regime change in the United States by backing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Attempts at removal

During Obama's second term and the first part of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s presidency there were repeated attempts to have Netanyahu ousted.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) The article cites the ongoing corruption probe against him, the [[usaid|USAID]]-funded judicial-reform protests of 2023, and calls by then Senate Majority Leader [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] in March 2024 for new elections in Israel — a statement DeLarme reads as essentially declaring Netanyahu illegitimate.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) These attempts at Israeli regime change ended abruptly after October 7th.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Place in the Trump coalition

DeLarme's summary judgment is blunt: Netanyahu "may be an asshole, but to Trump world, he was their asshole"; his government formed part of the loosely knit, big-tent coalition that saw Trump to victory.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Because Trump world was populated by notorious China hawks, and because China had aligned with the Atlanticists and the Democrats, Trump was further incentivised to align with the Netanyahu government.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) The pressure Netanyahu's government places on Trump over Iran is described as tremendous.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## April 2026 Israel–Lebanon ceasefire

In April 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that Netanyahu and Lebanese President [[joseph-aoun|Joseph Aoun]] had agreed to a formal ten-day ceasefire beginning at 5 p.m. EST on Tuesday, following "excellent conversations" with both leaders.[2] Trump referred to him as "Prime Minister Bibi Netanyahu" and noted that Israeli and Lebanese officials had met in Washington, D.C. for the first time in thirty-four years alongside Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]].[2]

Badlands commentary was skeptical of Netanyahu's commitment to the process. [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that [[alan-dershowitz|Alan Dershowitz]] had publicly urged Netanyahu to subvert Trump and continue fighting — "even if it means confrontation with President Trump" — while assuring him that he and his friends would bring Trump around.[2] Netanyahu subsequently released a recorded statement setting two conditions for peace with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]]: lay down its weapons and surrender.[2] The writer argued that Netanyahu was antagonizing the public with demands he knew Hezbollah would reject, meaning he expected the war to continue, and that Trump was decoupling himself from the conflict while Netanyahu geared up to fight alone.[2]

## February 2026 Washington visit

Ahead of a Wednesday meeting with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in February 2026, Netanyahu traveled to Washington for what Badlands counted as at least the seventh trip in twelve months — roughly one every seven weeks — and at least the third made to press for war with [[iran|Iran]].[3] Trump said Netanyahu "wants a good deal" with Iran on its nuclear program and described Oman-mediated talks, following a brief 2025 Israel-Iran war and U.S. strikes on Iranian sites, as "very different" from past negotiations.[3] Netanyahu planned to meet Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] the morning before seeing Trump, which Badlands read as an attempt to gameplan how to handle the president.[3]

Days before departing, Netanyahu's Security Cabinet approved [[west-bank-annexation|West Bank measures]] granting effective Israeli sovereignty over the territory through executive power rather than legislation, drawing Trump's explicit opposition: "I am against annexation."[3] An Israeli source indicated Netanyahu would tell Trump that Phase 2 of the October 2025 [[gaza-ceasefire|Gaza ceasefire]] — requiring [[hamas|Hamas]] disarmament and Israeli troop withdrawal — "is not moving," and that a renewed military operation was needed to enforce demilitarization, alongside a demand that the United States immediately start a war with Iran.[3]

## Allegations regarding October 7

A report released by the Israeli government in early 2026 claims Netanyahu knew of Hamas's plan to invade southern Israel as far back as early 2018.[3] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that this timing means Netanyahu possessed the intelligence when he arranged for tens of millions of dollars per month to reach Hamas after the Palestinian Authority cut it off financially, appealing to [[qatar|Qatar]] on humanitarian grounds and having his people walk the cash into Gaza to families with known Hamas ties.[3] If accurate, the commentary concluded, Netanyahu knowingly funded October 7th.[3]

## June 2026: excluded from the Iran framework

When President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] cancelled planned strikes on [[iran|Iran]] and announced an imminent agreement in June 2026, reports attributed to Netanyahu's office stated that Israel was not part of the deal — a message amplified by American commentators including [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] in what Badlands characterized as open subversion of the president.[4]

The same period placed Netanyahu under domestic military strain: the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] retreated from mixed-gender tank crews after twelve [[hesder-yeshivas|Hesder yeshivas]] threatened to withhold students from the Armored Corps, exposing recruitment fragility at precisely the moment Netanyahu was reportedly weighing an expansion of the war and the opening of new fronts.[4] Badlands commentary argued that he desperately needs military success to remain in favor with the Israeli public, and framed the convergence of manpower shortfalls, Ultra-Orthodox tension, and exclusion from Washington's diplomacy as "a perfect storm."[4]

## April 2026: ceasefire extension and Iranian escalation

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced a three-week extension of the Israel–Hezbollah ceasefire after an Oval Office meeting with Israeli and Lebanese representatives, and expressed plans to host Netanyahu together with Lebanese President [[joseph-aoun|Joseph Aoun]].[5] At the same time Israel was preparing to renew its war with [[iran|Iran]] and awaiting an American green light, with Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] declaring the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] ready for offense and stating that Israel sought to eliminate the Khamenei dynasty.[5] Badlands commentary read Trump's repeated line that "Israel will have to defend itself" as feeding the hubris of the Netanyahu government while quietly decoupling the United States from the coming fight, and asked which would break first — the IDF or Israeli public support for Netanyahu.[5]

## Naming Greece as a Turkish target (July 2026)

Early in the week of July 6, 2026, Netanyahu identified [[greece|Greece]] as one of [[turkey|Turkey]]'s intended targets.[6] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the statement as completing "a constellation of datapoints" for his theory that [[israel|Israel]] was maneuvering toward a European confrontation with Turkey, following the Ukrainian drone strike on the [[blue-stream-pipeline|Blue Stream]] pipeline's Krasnodarskaya compressor station.[6] Under that reading, [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and Turkey might come into conflict with Greece aiding Ukraine, and Israel "would love nothing more than to see European countries at war with Turkey."[6]

## The 2026 Iran war

During the March 2026 [[iran-war|war with Iran]], [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] denied that Israel had dragged the United States into the conflict, saying instead that he may have "forced Israel's hand" into war with Iran's "lunatics."[7] Trump said he had spoken with Netanyahu fifteen times in the preceding two months and gave a green light once nuclear negotiations collapsed, and he confirmed that Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]] had been killed in an Israeli strike.[7] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry speculated that Netanyahu might end up submitting after biting off more than he could chew, reading the sequence as evidence of a pattern in which Trump adopts [[neoconservatives|NeoCon]] positions in order to bait adversaries into self-destructive action.[7]

## Board of Peace invitation

Netanyahu was invited to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]] in January 2026 and his office confirmed receipt of the invitation, but he did not attend the signing ceremony at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] meeting in Davos.[8] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Netanyahu faced a conflict of interest, since declining would forfeit any direct influence over Gaza while overt participation would trigger Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] and his ultra-Orthodox settler constituency — a dynamic he suggested explained the delay in disclosing the invitation.[8] He predicted Netanyahu would ultimately accept, convincing Smotrich that it is better to be at the head of the table than outside peering in.[8]

## Friction with Washington over Iran (March 2026)

After the United States joined [[israel|Israel]] in strikes on [[iran|Iran]] in early March 2026, Netanyahu asked the [[white-house|White House]] for clarification upon learning that [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] officials might be communicating with the Iranian government, according to two sources familiar with the matter.[9] The Israeli government was reportedly concerned that Washington might pursue a ceasefire or diplomatic arrangement with Tehran before Israel achieved its war objectives.[9]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the episode sardonically, framing Netanyahu as having Mossad monitor the American president's private communications and asking why a leader who had finally been given everything he wanted politically still could not enjoy the moment.[9] He suggested Netanyahu senses that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is playing him without being able to identify how, and argued that the discomfort of the party who should be happiest is itself a meaningful signal about the direction of the Iran conflict.[9]

## Pardon request and the Iran war (March 2026)

In March 2026 the [[israel|Israeli]] Justice Ministry unit responsible for reviewing clemency requests reportedly declined to recommend granting a pardon to Netanyahu, meaning any pardon request would face internal opposition within the justice system.[10] Netanyahu was standing trial on corruption charges including bribery, fraud and breach of trust, which he denies; Israeli law permits the president to grant pardons, though these typically follow conviction rather than occurring during an ongoing trial.[10]

On the war, Netanyahu said he did not know whether Iranians would overthrow their leadership, acknowledging uncertainty about internal regime change while [[israel-defense-forces|Israeli forces]] continued military operations against [[iran|Iran]].[10] He warned that Israel could target Iran's Supreme Leader if the conflict continued to escalate.[10] Foreign Minister [[gideon-saar|Gideon Saar]] said only the Iranian people could ultimately bring down their government but might need outside help, while Iranian official [[ali-larijani|Ali Larijani]] said the war would not end until Iran's enemies were made "sorry for their grave miscalculation."[10]

## ICC warrant and the Mamdani dispute (July 2026)

Debate over the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]]'s 2024 arrest warrant for Netanyahu resurfaced in July 2026 alongside Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]]'s campaign to dismantle the court.[11] New York City Mayor [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]] released a video declaring Netanyahu a war criminal and stating his intent to arrest him should he enter the state; President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said Netanyahu would not be arrested while visiting the United States.[11] Netanyahu responded on Fox News, criticizing Mamdani and accusing him of fomenting hatred and antisemitism.[11] [[israel|Israel]] rejects the ICC's jurisdiction and denies the war-crimes allegations underlying the warrant.[11]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used the episode to argue against the premise that Netanyahu personally is the root of Israeli warmongering, pointing to opposition frontrunner [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] — a secularist who calls himself "ten degrees more right-wing than Netanyahu" and now vows never to allow a Palestinian state — as evidence that no viable pro-peace faction exists in Israeli politics ahead of the October election.[11]

## June 2026 strike cancellation

In early June 2026 Netanyahu canceled what would have been Israel's largest strike on [[iran|Iran]] since the April ceasefire, after discussions with [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]], according to Axios.[12] Trump had warned him that Israel could find itself "on your own very soon" if it expanded military operations against Iran, comments Trump gave to Axios that Reuters later confirmed.[12] Trump publicly called on both countries to "immediately stop shooting" on June 8, spoke again with Netanyahu to ask him to halt planned strikes, and relayed a message that Iran did not intend to conduct further attacks.[12] Netanyahu subsequently said Israel would "hold fire" while reserving the right to respond if attacked again.[12]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the episode as confirmation that Trump had been waging a covert campaign against Netanyahu's government all along, and cited [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]]'s public complaints about Trump "bashing the Prime Minister of Israel" as evidence of a rupture.[12]

## Tense call over Lebanon (June 2026)

Trump and Netanyahu both confirmed reports of a tense phone call over Israel's military actions in Lebanon; Trump acknowledged frustration at continued Israeli strikes and said he sharply criticized Netanyahu during the conversation, while emphasizing that the two leaders still maintain a strong relationship.[13] Trump was reportedly concerned that further escalation in Lebanon could undermine US efforts to stabilize the region and advance diplomatic talks involving [[iran|Iran]], and the call focused on planned Israeli operations in Beirut and the risk of a broader regional conflict.[13] The dispute highlighted a growing divergence in priorities between Washington and Jerusalem: Netanyahu remains focused on pressuring [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] militarily while Trump has increasingly pushed for de-escalation, ceasefires and a broader regional settlement that could include an agreement with Iran.[13]

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry treated the exchange as the long-teased break, predicting Trump would nonetheless continue to "kill Bibi with kindness," and identified Trump's openness to meeting the Iranian supreme leader as the real tell that "heads exploded across Tel Aviv."[13] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] likewise framed the reported clash over Israel's planned escalations in Lebanon as narrative shielding for Trump's hard pivots toward an off-ramp.[13]

## Hungary invitation and ICC arrest threat (April 2026)

Hungary's incoming prime minister [[peter-magyar|Péter Magyar]] invited Netanyahu to visit Budapest later in 2026, including for a ceremony marking the 70th anniversary of the 1956 Hungarian uprising, following what officials described as a "warm" introductory call.[14] Magyar simultaneously stated that [[hungary|Hungary]] intends to remain in or rejoin the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] framework and acknowledged that ICC membership would obligate Hungary to detain any individual subject to a warrant — effectively warning that Netanyahu could be arrested under the court's 2024 warrant if he entered the country.[14]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the move "beyond savage," but argued that the affair is less about the persecution or prosecution of Netanyahu personally than about [[israel|Israel]] and its citizens being ridiculed and ostracized from the international community — a dynamic he expected to drive Israel's tensions with Europe to a fever pitch.[14] He linked it to reports that [[russia|Russia]] was treating Israeli travelers with suspicion and hostility, presuming them to be undercover [[mossad|Mossad]].[14]

## Washington meeting on Iran (February 2026)

Netanyahu met President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in Washington on Wednesday, February 11, 2026, for discussions that included Iran's nuclear program and regional security issues.[15] The meeting was held behind closed doors and marked the seventh meeting between the two leaders since Trump returned to office; no joint press conference followed.[15]

Trump described the encounter on Truth Social as "a very good meeting" in which "the tremendous relationship between our two Countries continues," while stating that "there was nothing definitive reached other than I insisted that negotiations with Iran continue to see whether or not a Deal can be consummated."[15] He said a deal would be his preference, warned that Iran's previous refusal had led to it being "hit with Midnight Hammer," and added that the two had discussed "tremendous progress being made in Gaza, and the Region in general," asserting that "there is truly PEACE in the Middle East."[15]

The meeting coincided with indirect US-Iranian nuclear talks in Oman and Tehran's insistence, voiced by [[ali-shamkhani|Ali Shamkhani]], that its ballistic missile program is non-negotiable.[15] Netanyahu has previously indicated that [[israel|Israel]] seeks firm measures to prevent Iran from developing nuclear weapons capabilities, and Israeli officials have raised concerns about Iran's ballistic missile program and its support for regional armed groups.[15]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] judged the outcome a "tough look" for Netanyahu, noting that after all the build-up there was no video, no photos, and no announcement or formal statement recapping the meeting — the prime minister flew all that way and walked away with nothing, though in the contributor's view no news was good news.[15]

## South Pars strike and AI video claims (March 2026)

After Israel struck the [[south-pars-gas-field|South Pars gas field]] in the Persian Gulf in March 2026, Netanyahu told a press conference that Israel "acted alone" in the attack and said he would comply with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s request to refrain from additional strikes on the field.[16] In the same press conference he said a ground invasion would take place.[16] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] doubted Netanyahu would honor the restraint pledge and noted online claims that the press conference video — posted on the official YouTube channel of the Israel Press Office, with an alleged artifact at timestamp 10:17 — may have been AI-generated.[16] He said he has a hard time believing Netanyahu is dead, citing the number of ruthless politicians surrounding him and across the Israeli political spectrum who would be unlikely to accept a cover-up absent a deal staving off a power struggle, and suggested that if the video is fake, [[mossad|Mossad]] may be attempting to bait or confuse the truth movement into discrediting itself.[16] He predicted Netanyahu will lead Israel into a position where it is surrounded narratively and perhaps physically by the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] if the ground invasion proceeds, and that Trump will create distance from Netanyahu in the interim.[16]

## July 2026 Trump meeting and the election bind

Netanyahu met President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in late July 2026 amid renewed concerns over [[iran|Iran]]'s nuclear program and regional military activity, immediately following coordinated US–Saudi airstrikes on Iran-backed militias in [[iraq|Iraq]].[17] Saudi Defense Minister [[khalid-bin-salman|Khalid bin Salman]] reportedly used the same window to tell Trump's team that Riyadh is not escalating against Iran and that Netanyahu and the Israelis are the parties hellbent on escalation into open war.[17]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the [[2026-israeli-elections|October 2026 Israeli elections]] place Netanyahu's rivals in a bind: because Trump has endorsed him and the two have been "joined at the hip" on Iran, figures such as [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] and [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] cannot attack Bibi's alleged failures without implicating Trump.[17] He framed the outcome as binary — either Netanyahu wins and his "iconic villainy" continues to degrade [[israel|Israel]]'s reputation worldwide, or he loses to opponents who had to campaign against Trump's ceasefire diplomacy, leaving Israel led by a figure hostile to the American president.[17]

## March 2026: pardon request and war position

In March 2026, the unit of the [[israel-justice-ministry|Israel Justice Ministry]] responsible for reviewing clemency requests reportedly did not recommend granting Netanyahu a pardon, meaning any request would face internal opposition within the justice system.[18] Netanyahu is standing trial on corruption charges including bribery, fraud, and breach of trust, which he denies; Israeli law permits presidential pardons, though these typically follow conviction rather than occurring mid-trial.[18]

Netanyahu said he did not know whether Iranians would overthrow their own leadership, and warned that Israel could target [[iran|Iran]]'s Supreme Leader if the conflict escalated further.[18] He was also reported to have welcomed the dropping of charges against soldiers accused of abusing a Palestinian detainee at [[sde-teiman|Sde Teiman]].[18]

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that the pending charges function as a sword of Damocles over Netanyahu, forcing him to press the [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] agenda without subtlety ahead of an October election in which defeat could mean facing trial and imprisonment.[18] The analysis held that Netanyahu was not winning the war — citing [[haaretz|Haaretz]] reporting that Iranian smart missiles had penetrated the [[iron-dome|Iron Dome]] to strike central Israel — while [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signaled a US off-ramp, leaving the prime minister unable to either de-escalate or effectively escalate.[18]

## Post-strike claims about Iran (July 2026)

## May 2024: Gaza day-after dispute and the Revisionist lineage

In May 2024, frustration in Israel with Netanyahu's handling of the war against [[hamas|Hamas]] and his growing split with U.S. President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] boiled over, raising fears that the public tensions could invite increased attacks from [[iran|Iran]] and its proxies across the region.[53] Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] became the most senior official to call out the prime minister, demanding in blunt Memorial Day remarks that Netanyahu decide on a day-after plan for Gaza and declare that Israel would establish neither civilian control nor military governance there.[53] It was the second time Gallant had directly challenged Netanyahu, having warned in March 2023 that the prime minister's pursuit of controversial judicial reforms was threatening the security of the country — division that some analysts said fed into Hamas's calculation to launch its October 7 attack.[53]

Netanyahu answered in a video statement that he was "not ready to replace Hamastan with Fatahstan," rejecting any handover of Gaza to the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] and its dominant [[fatah|Fatah]] faction.[53] "After the terrible massacre on October 7, I ordered the destruction of Hamas," he said. "As long as Hamas remains intact, no other party will step in to manage civilian affairs in Gaza, certainly not the Palestinian Authority. 80 percent of the Palestinians in Judea and Samaria support the terrible massacre of October 7."[53] The figure appeared to derive from a December poll by the Palestinian Center for Policy and Survey Research showing 82 percent of West Bank Palestinians supported the events of October 7, a number that had dropped to 71 percent in a March follow-up.[53] Netanyahu added that "the Palestinian Authority supports terror, educates for terror, funds terror," making elimination of Hamas the first condition for preparing the ground for any alternative.[53]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Netanyahu had developed no plan to hold or govern territory cleared by the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]], thereby allowing Hamas to regenerate, and suggested the siege of Gaza was "not about destroying Hamas, but growing it."[53]

### Family and ideological background

The same commentary traced Netanyahu's lineage, contending he "was groomed for this campaign since before he was born."[53] His grandfather, [[nathan-mileikowsky|Nathan Mileikowsky]], attended the [[world-zionist-organization|World Zionist Organization]]'s Sixth Zionist Congress in Basel in 1903, where he vocally opposed the Uganda Programme, which was defeated.[53] Mileikowsky immigrated to Mandatory Palestine in 1920, became a school director, and joined the [[revisionist-zionism|Revisionist Movement]] under [[zeev-jabotinsky|Ze'ev Jabotinsky]], with he and his sons affiliating with the [[irgun|Irgun]].[53] Irgun propaganda posters prominently displayed the "[[greater-israel|Greater Israel]]" map, identified as identical to the map on the podium at which [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] spoke in March 2023.[53]

Mileikowsky began signing his published articles as "Netanyahu," a Hebrew form of his first name, and his son Benzion adopted it as a surname.[53] The Irgun was formally disbanded in 1949 — the year Netanyahu was born — and its membership morphed into the [[herut-party|Herut Party]], which became the [[likud-party|Likud Party]] in 1977, the same period Netanyahu cycled out of the IDF and partnered with his father to plot his political career; he began publishing books that year framing the term "terrorism" for Western audiences and equating it with Islam.[53] On this reading Netanyahu is "the prodigal son of Ze'ev Jabotinsky and the Revisionist Movement," and the invasion of Rafah was designed to provoke the Arabs and Iran into attacking Israel, creating conditions of war that could have resulted in annexation far beyond the Jordan River; with that provocation unanswered, the argument runs, Netanyahu turned to growing Hamas to keep the ambition alive.[53]

## Call for an independent Israeli weapons industry (September 2025)

In September 2025, amid mounting international pressure over the war in Gaza, Netanyahu said [[israel|Israel]] needed to create an "independent weapons industry" capable of withstanding "international constraints."[67] Israel's military capability had been largely facilitated by American supplies, with Washington providing more than two-thirds of its major conventional arms in 2023 according to the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute.[67] European states, particularly Germany, had also traditionally supplied weapons but grew increasingly critical of Israeli conduct in Gaza; Berlin announced in August 2025 that it would not approve weapons exports to Israel for use in the enclave "until further notice," a major change of direction for one of Israel's staunchest allies.[67]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that Israel lacks a real manufacturing base and that the project would take years, noting that its largest industries are software (27% of GDP), pharmaceutical and chemical products (16%), and electrical components (12%), while steel, aluminum, plastics, concrete and rubber — the basic inputs for both weapons factories and weapons themselves — are all imported.[67] The same commentary predicted the world would isolate Israel's economy long before it could acquire those materials and hire the foreign contractors needed to build such factories.[67] In separate commentary on the U.S. veto of a Gaza ceasefire resolution at the [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]], the author suggested President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was letting Netanyahu show the world "the true face of Zionism."[67]

## Escalation of pressure on Hamas (March 2025)

## July 2025 Washington visits

On July 8, 2025, Netanyahu wrapped up his second White House meeting with President Donald Trump in as many days without any public announcement of a breakthrough in the ongoing Gaza ceasefire and hostage release talks, which had been the primary topic of the sit-down.[71] Expectations of such a declaration had risen after the meeting was publicly added to Netanyahu's schedule only hours in advance, with Trump saying they would discuss the Gaza Strip and US Mideast envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] expressing hope that a deal could be reached that week.[71] Netanyahu and Trump met for over an hour in the Oval Office along with Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], and two sources familiar with the matter said Witkoff decided to push back his flight to Doha, where he had been slated to join proximity talks between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]].[71]

Badlands commentary traced the sequence to a scrambled visit by Netanyahu confidant [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] the preceding Monday, who rushed to Washington to smooth relations after Trump publicly said that Israel and Iran "don't know what the f— they are doing," a reference to Israel's decision to violate the ceasefire and continue bombing Tehran.[71] Dermer was said to have scheduled the Monday Netanyahu visit — itself a last-minute arrangement — in an attempt to persuade Trump to support Israel's desire to annex Palestine, and the commentary read Trump's relegation of the visit to an after-hours dinner meeting as the definitive "tell" that it had been shoehorned in by the Israeli side.[71]

The unscheduled extension of the trip to a second day, with Netanyahu returning at 4:30 PM local time, was interpreted as evidence of disagreement at the Monday dinner — underscored by Netanyahu's response when Trump redirected a question about a two-state solution to him, in which he effectively stated that a Palestinian state would present an existential threat to Israel.[71] Before Netanyahu's return, a group of senior Qatari officials flew to the White House to meet Trump and Witkoff for over three hours, a session the commentary speculated involved wargaming scenarios for handling an increasingly belligerent Israeli government.[71] The persistent theme was Netanyahu's defiant insistence that the IDF continue fighting until Hamas no longer exists, a goal the commentary considered impossible given how ideology proliferates under violent warfare, and it predicted a third attempt at talks given what the [[likud-party|Likud party]] was then openly demanding.[71]

## Supreme Court strikes down the reasonableness law (January 2024)

On January 1, 2024, Israel's highest court struck down a controversial law enacted the previous year by Netanyahu's government as part of its [[israeli-judicial-overhaul|judicial overhaul]], which would have limited the justices' power.[83] The law, passed in July 2023 and akin to a constitutional amendment, would have taken away the court's power to abrogate government decisions it deems "unreasonable in the extreme"; eight justices voted to strike it down and seven against.[83] The ruling threatened to revive the deep political and social strife generated by the reform push, during which hundreds of thousands of Israelis had protested weekly before the October 7 [[hamas|Hamas]] attack, and came while the country was embroiled in the war in [[gaza|Gaza]].[83]

In April 2025, Reuters reported that Israel was considering a "limited strike" on Iran's nuclear facilities in the coming months despite Washington's refusal to support military action.[89] The deliberations came as the second round of U.S.-Iran negotiations concluded in Rome, with expert-level talks expected to continue on April 26 in Oman.[89] Israeli officials were said to be weighing an option requiring minimal U.S. support, unlike the larger and more prolonged bombing campaign previously under consideration, and West Jerusalem reportedly presented Washington with several strike options "including some with late spring and summer timelines"; a senior Israeli official said no final decision had been made.[89] The New York Times had reported days earlier that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] rejected an Israeli proposal for "extensive" strikes, opting instead for diplomacy, telling reporters, "I'm not in a rush to do it, because I think that Iran has a chance to have a great country and to live happily without death."[89]

## Florida visit to court Trump (July 2024)

On Friday, July 26, 2024, Netanyahu flew to Florida to meet [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with a single objective, according to [[politico|Politico]]: securing an unequivocal pledge of military and financial support for Israel should Trump win in November.[92] Such backing would have helped Netanyahu solidify his grip on power amid an increasingly hostile political scene at home, and two people familiar with his thinking said Trump's strong polling and [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s departure from the Democratic ticket had made goodwill with Trump feel more essential than ever.[92] Getting on Trump's good side, however, proved harder than it once was, and it was unclear whether Netanyahu obtained what he sought.[92]

## Gaza City capture decision (August 2025)

## Trump meeting and the Gaza deal, February 2025

The future of the Gaza hostage-release and ceasefire deal hung on the outcome of a meeting between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Netanyahu set for Tuesday, February 4, 2025 at the White House, three Israeli officials told Axios.[94] Netanyahu had reluctantly agreed to move forward with the first phase of the deal between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]], and families of the hostages as well as Israeli negotiators were concerned he would not follow through to the second phase as he fought for political survival.[94] Israeli officials said Netanyahu was waiting to see where Trump stood on the second phase — which was supposed to lead to a permanent ceasefire and a full withdrawal of Israeli forces from Gaza — before making any decisions.[94]

## Religious-prophecy lineage

The same commentary traced Netanyahu's ideological inheritance: his grandfather, [[nathan-mileikowsky|Nathan Mileikowsky]], was a prominent rabbi who attended the original [[world-zionist-organization|World Zionist Organization]] meetings at the turn of the twentieth century, where it was decided the Jewish state would be established in Palestine rather than Africa or South America — a decision Mileikowsky is said to have influenced — and who accepted the framework of Jewish religious prophecy regarding the return of Jews to their land as part of the messianic vision.[96] His father, [[benzion-netanyahu|Benzion]], was a secularist unmoved by prophecy but deeply influenced by his close friend and colleague [[zeev-jabotinsky|Ze'ev Jabotinsky]], described as the man who started Jewish terrorist organisations in both Ukraine and British-Mandate Palestine; the hyper-violent [[irgun|Irgun]] militia became the core group that later formed the IDF in May 1948.[96]

Netanyahu himself has referenced religious prophecy on a number of occasions and called Rabbi [[menachem-mendel-schneerson|Menachem Mendel Schneerson]] "the most influential man of our time," meeting him in 1990 while serving as Deputy Foreign Minister under Prime Minister [[yitzhak-shamir|Yitzhak Shamir]], a former Irgun member.[96] The exchange concerned the arrival of the Jewish messiah, whose coming Schneerson and members of Netanyahu's security cabinet believe will be summoned by construction of the [[third-temple|Third Temple]].[96] Whether Netanyahu is personally a fanatic was deemed largely irrelevant, since he is politically beholden to men who certainly are — among them [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] and [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]].[96]

## Corruption trial testimony (December 2024)

On 10 December 2024 Netanyahu became the first sitting prime minister in Israel's history to take the witness stand in his own corruption trial.[97] Accused of bribery, fraud and breach of trust across three separate cases, he repeated his claim that he is the subject of a witch hunt.[97] Appearing in Tel Aviv district court, he said: "I have waited eight years for this moment, to say the truth as I remember it, which is important for justice. There is no justice without truth," adding that the proceeding was an opportunity to dispel the allegations and that there was "a great absurdity in the charges and great injustice."[97] Police first opened an investigation into the alleged corruption in 2016, and then-attorney general Avichai Mandelblit formally indicted him in 2019.[97]

Badlands commentary observed that the story had largely escaped notice, noted that the charges include accepting gifts and embezzling money, and suggested that Netanyahu's incredulity may stem partly from the sense that many other politicians behave similarly and partly from the irony that comparatively minor charges might be what brings him down.[97] The commentary judged the charges easy to imagine as true given Netanyahu's persona, comparing the situation to hypothetical charges against Hillary Clinton or Nancy Pelosi, and speculated that events in Syria were conveniently distracting the world from a trial that had been delayed and pending since 2019 - possibly itself a plot to remove him.[97]

Because Netanyahu had claimed extensive credit for the toppling of the [[bashar-al-assad|Assad]] regime in Syria, Badlands questioned whether the timing was related to the trial.[97] Netanyahu made headlines by announcing he had ordered the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] to secure territory in the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]] - occupied by Israel since the Six Day War in 1967 - and said that this occupation would be permanent.[97] Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] separately said he intended to establish a "sterile zone" in southern Syria to prevent any "terrorist threat," a formulation Badlands mocked by asking whether he meant the Al Qaeda-linked fighters used to overthrow Assad, and predicted the zone would eventually extend to the Euphrates River.[97]

## Pausing the judicial overhaul (March 2023)

In late March 2023 Netanyahu paused his [[israeli-judicial-overhaul|judicial overhaul]] plan in order, as he framed it, to "avoid civil war," announcing the suspension in a televised 8pm speech while hundreds of thousands of protesters filled the streets.[106] The crisis followed his dismissal of Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] a day after Gallant publicly called on him to halt the overhaul; the firing was read as a signal that Netanyahu intended to press ahead that week, and it triggered massive nationwide protests, anger among military and business leaders, and concern among Israel's allies.[106] Netanyahu and his allies maintained that the plan would restore balance between the judicial and executive branches and rein in what they saw as an interventionist court with liberal sympathies, while critics argued the constellation of laws would remove checks and balances from Israel's democratic system and concentrate power in the governing coalition.[106] Badlands contributors read the protest wave as a [[color-revolution|color revolution]] rather than a spontaneous movement.[106]

## Collapse of the wartime government (June 2024)

On June 9, 2024, centrist opposition leader [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] announced his party's departure from Israel's emergency war government, the biggest internal shock to the government since October 7, accusing Netanyahu in a prime-time speech of putting personal interest ahead of the national interest and of meeting "fateful strategic decisions" with "hesitation and procrastination due to political considerations."[108] Commentary asked whether the move heralded the end of Netanyahu's rule, but analysts concluded there was less to the drama than headlines suggested.[108]

The reasoning turned on Israeli political math: the parties in Netanyahu's coalition were unpopular and had not received a majority of the vote in the November 2022 election, taking power only through a quirk of the electoral system, and since October 7 large majorities of Israelis had favored early elections while most surveys showed Gantz leading Netanyahu as preferred prime minister, with 62 percent saying they would not vote for any party backing Netanyahu's continued leadership.[108] None of those numbers bound Netanyahu, however, because his coalition held 64 of the 120 seats in the [[knesset|Knesset]] — enough to block the body from calling early elections and, absent internal defections, to postpone any contest until the scheduled 2026 date.[108] Badlands commentary suggested Gantz might be operating, even unwittingly, on behalf of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and that persuading Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] to resign alongside him would dissolve the government and force the elections Netanyahu was avoiding.[108]


## Decision to seek full occupation of Gaza, August 2025

In August 2025 Netanyahu told ministers that he would seek cabinet backing for a plan to fully occupy [[gaza|Gaza]], despite objections from the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]], The Times of Israel reported.[109] Several ministers said he used the term "occupation of the Strip" in private conversations, reflecting his vision for expanding military operations and marking a significant shift in tone as the government prepared to debate the future of the campaign.[109]

Israel controlled Gaza for 38 years, from 1967 until 2005; after the IDF and Israeli settlers withdrew, the enclave was governed by the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], which also controlled parts of the occupied West Bank, until [[hamas|Hamas]] took power after winning elections there in 2006, since when no elections have been held.[109]

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted the rhetorical reversal by which Israel had moved in under two years from indignation at the word "occupation" applied to the West Bank to proudly using it for its plans in Gaza, and argued the announcement was largely redundant given the IDF's de facto sovereignty over the Strip since the October 7 attacks.[109] He read the sequence — alongside the U.S. position on Morocco's claim to [[western-sahara|Western Sahara]] and the Algeria-backed [[polisario-front|Polisario Front]] — as [[donald-trump|Trump]] granting permission to annex disputed land, while noting that Gaza differs in having a third claimant in the Palestinian Authority, and that Trump had written to Palestinian President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] after Butler promising that everything would be all right, posting the letter nine days after receiving it and on the night before Netanyahu delivered a widely panned speech about Iran to Congress.[109] He concluded that the episode "feels like Trump bullying Bibi."[109]

## September 2025 amnesty signal and Trump peace plan

In late September 2025 Netanyahu said he was open to granting [[hamas|Hamas]] amnesty if the Palestinian militant group freed the remaining hostages and left [[gaza|Gaza]], a pledge marking a shift from his long-held stance that the war could end only with the complete dismantling of Hamas, including its leadership.[116] In a Fox News interview on Sunday 28 September he appeared to confirm leaked details of a peace plan by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], suggesting the group could be offered immunity: "If Hamas leaders, for example, are escorted out of the country, yeah, if they finish the war, release all the hostages, we'll let them out. Under Trump's plan, Hamas leaders would receive safe passage out of Gaza if the 48 remaining hostages were freed within 48 hours of Israel's public acceptance; in exchange Israel would release hundreds of Palestinian prisoners serving life terms, over 1,000 Gazans detained since the war began, and the remains of several hundred Palestinians killed in the conflict.[116] The plan provided amnesty for Hamas members committing to "peaceful coexistence" and safe passage to third countries for those choosing to leave, alongside immediate humanitarian aid delivery and a pledge by Israel not to launch further strikes on [[qatar|Qatar]] — a clause Netanyahu said he would discuss with the Trump administration while declining to say whether Israel would commit to it.[116]

## April 2025 Iran strike controversy and domestic pincer

On Thursday, April 17, 2025, Netanyahu's office touted his "countless" actions against Iran's nuclear program but did not deny a New York Times report that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had blocked a proposed Israeli strike on the Islamic Republic's nuclear facilities the previous month in order to pursue a diplomatic effort instead.[126] The statement came as Iranian and allied officials prepared for a second round of talks with the United States that Saturday; Oman confirmed the talks would be held in Rome, setting aside earlier Iranian claims that they would again take place in Muscat.[126]

Opposition Leader [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] responded by accusing Netanyahu of having scuttled past opportunities to strike Iran, saying, "Back in October, I proposed attacking Iran's oil fields," but "Netanyahu was afraid, and stopped it."[126] Former prime minister [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] implied that Netanyahu himself had leaked the stalled plan to the Times, stating that "the Netanyahu doctrine is to threaten, threaten, threaten and then leak that he meant to [do something] but was prevented from doing it," and warning there would not be another such opportunity to hit Iran.[126]

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry characterized the joint Bennett–Lapid assault as a political double envelopment, or pincer move.[126] He recalled that the two had served as co-Prime Ministers for 18 months between June 2021 and December 2022 — Bennett for 12 months and Lapid for six, each serving as Alternate Prime Minister while the other held the premiership — in an effort to end Netanyahu's career, only for Netanyahu to return by aligning with the most radical elements around Bennett's base and striking a Christmas Day 2022 deal that required modifying a 1994 law barring racial supremacists from the [[knesset|Knesset]], with the modification permitting such ideology where it formed part of religious doctrine.[126]

The analysis noted that Bennett, a former member of the Sayeret Matkal special forces unit, leads the [[new-right-party|New Right]], founded at the end of 2018 as the "most right-wing" party in Israel to supplant [[likud|Likud]], while rejecting the Kahanists of [[otzma-yehudit|Otzma Yehudit]] led by [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] — the very faction Netanyahu was forced to embrace for political survival.[126] Lapid, by contrast, leads the nominally centrist [[yesh-atid|Yesh Atid]], which has drawn both Likud centrists and members of the Labor Party.[126]

The commentary concluded that both the Israeli right and left had aligned against Netanyahu using the novel attack vector that he was insufficiently aggressive as a warmonger — a politically shrewd line given that his war-hawk posture had sustained him in office since the October 7th attack — and that Trump's hinting he might allow a strike on Iran functioned as a further chess move exposing that Netanyahu was not the one calling the shots.[126]

## May 2024: reelection intentions, Rafah, and the hostage talks

By early May 2024 it had become clear that Netanyahu had decided to run again for prime minister in the next Israeli elections. Although he had not been definitive on the question since the outbreak of the war in Gaza, he began making explicit statements about the need for him to continue leading the country after the next general elections — despite, and perhaps because of, the national tragedy and the failures of October 7.[127] He argued that Israel was in the midst of a war that would last for many years and that only he was fit to lead that effort, and he was reported to be convinced he would win an election, perhaps within months, if a hostage-deal proposal came to a vote and passed with the expected majority.[127]

At the same time, there was a reported "widespread belief" among US officials that Netanyahu's stated goal of "eradicating" [[hamas|Hamas]] in Gaza was unattainable. US intelligence agencies' March 2024 annual threat assessment said Israel would face "lingering armed resistance" for "years to come" and that the Israeli military would struggle to destroy Hamas's underground infrastructure, while reporting indicated that as early as October 2023 American officials doubted Israel had a definable and achievable military objective.[127]

Addressing the official state Yom Hashoah memorial ceremony at [[yad-vashem|Yad Vashem]], Netanyahu asserted that Israel would not halt the fighting in Gaza until Hamas was defeated, regardless of the pressure brought to bear.[127] Responding to reports that the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] in The Hague could issue arrest warrants for senior Israeli officials including himself, he said such action would "undermine" Israelis' "most basic right" to defend themselves and would place "a grave stain on the mere concept of international justice."[127] He declared: "From here in Jerusalem, I am sending a very clear-cut message: you will not chain our hands and even if Israel has to stand alone, it will stand alone and will continue to fight our enemies until victory," and, switching to English, said that during the Holocaust the Jewish people had been "totally defenseless against those who sought our destruction."[127]

In the hostage negotiations, Israel refused to send a delegation until Hamas formally responded to a three-phase proposal that would in its first stage have seen 33 of the 132 remaining hostages released in exchange for a 40-day pause in the fighting.[127] The United States hoped to begin with phase one without addressing a permanent ceasefire, but Netanyahu's repeated rejection of any permanent ceasefire short of an operation in Rafah made that difficult; he flatly ruled out ending the war in exchange for the hostages, insisting the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] must be free to destroy Hamas, including in Rafah.[127] [[cia|CIA]] Director [[william-burns|William Burns]] travelled to Doha for an emergency meeting with Qatari Prime Minister Mohammed Al-Thani aimed at exerting maximum pressure on both sides to keep negotiating.[127]

## Badlands commentary on the Netanyahu timeline

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] set out a long-run chronology to argue that Netanyahu's conduct in 2024 followed an established pattern. He noted that in December 2016 the [[israeli-police|Israeli Police]] opened three separate cases against Netanyahu over allegations of fraud, bribery, and breach of trust during his fourth and fifth terms; that by September 2017 protests had formed over the perception that Attorney General Avichai Mandelblit was interfering in the investigations after announcing a series of delays; and that in December 2018 a state prosecutor recommended indictment.[127] In 2019, Netanyahu asked the [[israeli-supreme-court|Israeli Supreme Court]] to stall publication of those recommendations; the requests were denied and the recommendations published the same day, and his indictment followed in October, legally forcing him to resign every post except prime minister — including health, agriculture, social affairs, and diaspora affairs minister, a list the writer cited as demonstrating the power he had accrued.[127]

The commentary further asserted that after the 2020 US election Netanyahu was the first world leader to call and congratulate Joe Biden, that COVID delayed the preliminary proceedings into 2023, and that by then Netanyahu had appointed Justice Minister [[yariv-levin|Yariv Levin]], whose plan to overhaul the judiciary would have reshaped the nine-member judicial selection committee to exclude the opposition and hand the ruling [[likud|Likud]] regime effective control of the courts.[127] It also recalled that in May 2020, months before the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] were signed, Netanyahu hinted at annexing portions of the West Bank — occupied by the IDF since 1967 — prompting UAE Ambassador to the US Yousef Al Otaiba to warn in an op-ed for Israeli newspapers that normalization with the Arab world would be impossible if annexation occurred, after which [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] and Al Otaiba spent the summer of 2020 persuading Netanyahu to step back and sign.[127] The writer drew a parallel to September 2023, when an impending Saudi–Israeli peace deal including Palestinian statehood was widely discussed and Netanyahu claimed to support it while later revealing a lifelong contempt for the two-state solution, and closed by asking whether [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had needed to organize a counterinsurgency against Netanyahu.[127]

On Sunday, April 28, 2024, Netanyahu asked President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in a phone call to help prevent the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] from issuing arrest warrants for senior Israeli officials in connection with the war in Gaza, according to two Israeli officials cited by Axios.[129] Israeli officials had grown increasingly concerned over the preceding two weeks that the court was preparing warrants for Netanyahu, Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] and [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] chief of staff [[herzi-halevi|Herzi Halevi]].[129] The same call, per a White House readout, covered hostage negotiations, Israel's defense against Iran's missile attack, and the need to increase humanitarian aid to Gaza.[129] In a statement on X the previous Friday, Netanyahu declared that "under my leadership, Israel will never accept any attempt by the ICC to undermine its inherent right of self-defense."[129]

## History of friction with Trump

Badlands commentary, drawing on [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]]'s December 21, 2021 interview with Trump, recounted that Trump had quietly backed former Israeli general [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] against Netanyahu in the April 2019 Israeli election.[130] After Gantz's Blue and White tied [[likud|Likud]] at 35 seats, President [[reuven-rivlin|Reuven Rivlin]] gave Netanyahu until May 29 to form a government; when he failed, Netanyahu convinced enough members of the [[knesset|Knesset]] to dissolve the legislature on May 30 rather than let Gantz try, forcing new elections.[130] Similar maneuvering across the September 2019 and March 2020 elections culminated in an emergency coalition in which Netanyahu remained Prime Minister and Gantz served as "Alternate Prime Minister."[130]

Trump explicitly opposed Netanyahu's plans to annex large portions of the [[west-bank-annexation|West Bank]] and construct Israeli settlements there, warning Netanyahu against further annexation just fifteen days after entering office in 2017; Netanyahu scoffed at the warning in a live press conference, and Trump retorted, "well that doesn't sound very promising."[130] Trump told Ravid that he had stopped Netanyahu from building those settlements in May 2020, and Ravid later said that this blockage is exactly what leveraged Netanyahu into agreeing to the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]].[130] Twenty-four hours before the Accords were to be signed, Israeli ambassador to the US [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] called the Trump White House to say Netanyahu would not sign, citing "new elections" — a pattern the author likened to Netanyahu backing out of the Soleimani strike the day before it happened in January 2020.[130] Trump pressed the matter and Netanyahu ultimately signed; after the 2020 election, Netanyahu was the first foreign leader to call and congratulate Biden.[130]

## Dependence on the far right

Badlands commentary argues that Netanyahu, having exhausted his moderate political allies through this sequence of subversion, was forced to ally with the most radical elements of Israeli politics to keep his coalition from dissolving, bringing members of what the author calls the "Messianic Movement" into his cabinet's inner circle.[130] That movement seeks to expand Israel's borders under [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]], codify a formal theocracy promoting radical Jewish supremacy under the [[smotrich-doctrine|Smotrich Doctrine]], and summon the Jewish messiah through [[red-heifer|red heifer]] sacrifice and construction of the [[third-temple|Third Temple]] on the site of the Al-Aqsa Mosque, a project associated with [[temple-institute|The Temple Institute]].[130] To solidify the alliance between Likud and the [[otzma-yehudit|Otzma Yehudit]] party, Netanyahu supported reversing the 1994 Knesset ban on the party of [[meir-kahane|Meir Kahane]] in 2022.[130]

In July 2024, this dependence produced open dissent within Likud when science minister [[gila-gamliel|Gila Gamliel]] demanded that Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] be fired for threatening to collapse the coalition over a hostage deal with [[hamas|Hamas]].[130] The same faction was described as the obstacle to any ceasefire deal being brokered by US envoy [[amos-hochstein|Amos Hochstein]] between Israel and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[130]

## Trump's June 2025 call to cancel the corruption trial

Days after Israel and Iran reached a ceasefire, President Donald Trump posted on Truth Social calling for an end to Netanyahu's corruption trial.[131] "Bibi Netanyahu was a WARRIOR, like perhaps no other Warrior in the History of Israel," Trump wrote, hailing his leadership during the conflict with Iran and describing the allegations against him as politically motivated.[131] "Such a WITCH HUNT, for a man who has given so much, is unthinkable to me," he added. "Bibi Netanyahu's trial should be CANCELLED, IMMEDIATELY, or a Pardon given to a Great Hero, who has done so much for the State."[131] Badlands commentators read the post as kayfabe cutting both ways: Trump trolling while propping up "his guy," and simultaneously drawing attention to the corruption trial itself, which they characterized as one of several leverage points the Trump team is applying to Netanyahu.[131] Contributors also revived allegations surrounding Hollywood producer and former Mossad operative Arnon Milchan, including that he gifted a Bugs Bunny doll to Yair Netanyahu at his parents' request and that he helped divert hundreds of nuclear detonation triggers to Israel's undeclared weapons program.[131]

## Dismissal of Yoav Gallant (November 2024)

On November 5, 2024 — the day of the US presidential election — Netanyahu announced that he was firing Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]], a longtime rival within [[likud|Likud]], citing a lack of mutual trust.[132] A terse letter released by the prime minister's office told Gallant that "your tenure will end 48 hours from the receipt of this letter," and thanked him for his service.[132]

In a video statement Netanyahu said that although the first months of the war had seen trust and "very fruitful work," that trust had cracked in recent months; he accused Gallant of making statements and taking actions that contradicted cabinet decisions and of indirectly aiding Israel's enemies, who "enjoyed it and derived a lot of benefit from it."[132] The resulting "crisis of faith," he said, did not permit the proper continuation of the military campaign.[132]

Netanyahu named Foreign Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] as the new defense minister, with minister without portfolio [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] moving to the foreign ministry.[132] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that the firing had been speculated for months but that its timing on US Election Day was notable, and that Netanyahu had bypassed the widely expected Sa'ar in favor of Katz — a choice read as making enforcement of the Supreme Court's nullification of the [[haredi-judaism|Haredi]] draft exemption less likely.[132]

## Blocking Gallant's Washington trip (October 2024)

Hours before Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] was due to travel to Washington for an October 9, 2024 meeting with U.S. Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] and [[white-house|White House]] National Security Adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]], Netanyahu reportedly told him he did not approve of the visit and that he should wait until Netanyahu had spoken with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[133] The two leaders had not spoken in nearly 50 days amid tensions over the Gaza war and the conflict with Hezbollah in Lebanon.[133] Netanyahu also delayed the trip pending his political-security cabinet's approval of Israel's planned actions against Iran; his office told Israel's N12News that the postponement was a normal order of operations because the trip made no sense until Israel had decided on Iran.[133]

The episode coincided with reporting that the Biden administration had grown deeply distrustful of Israeli statements about its plans, with one U.S. official saying trust was "very low right now and for a good reason."[133] Journalist [[bob-woodward|Bob Woodward]]'s book *War*, whose contents emerged the same week, quoted Biden calling Netanyahu "a f----ing liar" after Israel's incursion into Rafah.[133] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry recounted the longer Netanyahu–Gallant feud — the 2023 firing and immediate rehiring after Gallant joined a rally calling for Netanyahu's resignation, Benny Gantz's June resignation and the dissolution of the War Cabinet, and Netanyahu's elevation of [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] to the Security Cabinet and a new three-man war cabinet — arguing that Netanyahu had reduced his dependence on Gallant and that Sa'ar was widely expected to replace him.[133]

## Gaza seizure plan (May 2025)

In an early morning vote on May 5, 2025, Israeli Cabinet ministers approved plans to seize the [[gaza|Gaza Strip]] and to remain in the Palestinian territory for an unspecified period, a move that would vastly expand Israel's operations and was expected to draw fierce international opposition.[134] The plan also called for hundreds of thousands of Palestinians to move to Gaza's south, likely amounting to forcible displacement and worsening an already dire humanitarian crisis.[134]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that conquering Gaza had been Netanyahu's purpose in facilitating the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]], and noted the timing as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] prepared to visit multiple Arab countries — but not Israel — while promising a new nuclear deal with [[iran|Iran]].[134] Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] told Israelis to "embrace Gaza occupation," while [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] Chief of Staff [[eyal-zamir|Eyal Zamir]] warned the operation could cost the remaining hostages, a price the commentary said Netanyahu's government was ready to pay.[134]

## Gallant letter and the UNRWA ban (October 2024)

In late October 2024, Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] sent Netanyahu and the security cabinet a letter stating that "the war is being conducted without a compass" and that Israel's war objectives needed updating, copying it to [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]] director [[ronen-bar|Ronen Bar]], [[mossad|Mossad]] chief [[david-barnea|David Barnea]] and IDF Chief of Staff [[herzl-halevi|Herzl Halevi]].[135] The letter, sent hours before Israel's strike on [[iran|Iran]], proposed four distinct war goals covering [[gaza|Gaza]], [[lebanon|Lebanon]], Iran and the West Bank.[135] Badlands commentary traced the Gallant–Netanyahu rift to March 2023, when Gallant attended a protest against the judicial overhaul and Netanyahu fired him only to rehire him the next day, and predicted that Netanyahu would now dismiss and replace him.[135]

That choice was described as genuinely dangerous for Netanyahu: [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] was the widely expected successor, while elevating [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] risked mutiny from the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] brass, in part over Ben-Gvir's opposition to ending the Haredi conscription exemption that the Israeli Supreme Court had struck down.[135] The analysis noted that Israeli law allows simultaneous cabinet titles — Netanyahu held five until his corruption indictment forced him to resign all but the premiership — and concluded that he would likely surround himself with religious hardliners precisely because he faces domestic or international consequences once the war ends.[135]

In the same week, the [[knesset|Knesset]] voted to ban [[unrwa|UNRWA]]; Netanyahu reiterated accusations that its employees were involved in terrorist activity and his office said UNRWA personnel "must be held accountable," while pledging to work with international partners during the ninety-day implementation window so that humanitarian aid to Gaza could continue without threatening Israeli security.[135]

## 2025 White House invitation and Phase Two pressure

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] invited Netanyahu to the [[white-house|White House]] for a February 4, 2025 meeting, making him the first foreign leader to visit in Trump's second term, as announced by Netanyahu's office and the White House on Tuesday, January 28.[136] The invitation came as the United States pressed Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]] to sustain a ceasefire that had paused a devastating 15-month war in Gaza, with talks on the more difficult second phase set to begin the following Monday.[136] The White House letter shared by Netanyahu's office said, "I look forward to discussing how we can bring peace to Israel and its neighbors, and efforts to counter our shared adversaries."[136] The meeting offered Netanyahu, under political pressure at home, a chance to showcase Trump's longstanding support and defend Israel's conduct of the war.[136]

Trump's administration worked privately and publicly to ensure Netanyahu would move forward in the negotiations despite the political ramifications for his government, and that he intended to free all the hostages, reach a cease-fire, and withdraw from the Gaza Strip.[136] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] explained that Netanyahu's finance minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] was being pressured by hardliners in the [[religious-zionism-party|Religious Zionism]] party and the Israeli settler movement to resign, which would collapse Netanyahu's coalition and force new elections, and that Smotrich threatened to do so if the Phase Two exchange proceeded without the war resuming.[136] He argued that Trump dispatched Middle East envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] to [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to gameplan with its foreign ministry, and that the White House visit sweetened the pot for Netanyahu.[136]

An added benefit for Netanyahu was the avoidance of a scheduled court deposition tied to the corruption prosecution filed against him in 2019; the court agreed to delay the hearing on condition that he submit to a longer interview when rescheduled.[136] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that Netanyahu therefore could not risk Trump cancelling the meeting, which would likely prompt the court to reverse the delay and generate political fallout from an Israeli public that had been gathering outside his Tel Aviv office in large numbers demanding he uphold the hostage deal.[136]

## October 2025 call with Trump over the Hamas response

When Hamas answered President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Gaza peace proposal with a "yes, but" on Friday, October 3, 2025, Trump telephoned Netanyahu to discuss what he regarded as good news, but the prime minister disagreed.[137] "Bibi told Trump this is nothing to celebrate, and that it doesn't mean anything," a U.S. official with knowledge of the call told Axios, prompting Trump to fire back: "I don't know why you're always so f***ing negative. This is a win. Take it."[137] A second U.S. official confirmed the exchange, which Axios said reflected how determined Trump was to push through Netanyahu's reservations and convince him to end the war if [[hamas|Hamas]] would make a deal.[137]

In private consultations that Friday, Netanyahu stressed that he viewed the Hamas response as a rejection of Trump's plan and said he wanted to coordinate with the United States on its reply to avoid a narrative that Hamas had answered positively, an Israeli official told Axios.[137] Two U.S. officials said Trump had been concerned Hamas would reject the plan outright and saw the actual response as an opening, so the prime minister's tepid reaction was not what he expected.[137]

Badlands commentators read the leak itself as significant: [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Trump having an official relay the anecdote to [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]] and [[marc-caputo|Marc Caputo]] revealed the state of the Trump–Netanyahu relationship, praised Trump for calling Netanyahu "a crybaby," and predicted that if Netanyahu wrecked the deal Trump would have a public mandate to walk away.[137] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] offered the terser judgment that [[israel|Israel]] would be blamed if the deal fell apart.[137]


## Washington visit and Gaza terms (July 2025)

As Netanyahu departed Israel for Washington in July 2025 to meet President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], he emphasised his opposition to any hostage deal that would leave [[hamas|Hamas]] in power in Gaza.[138] "Twenty living hostages remain and 30 who are fallen. I am determined, we are determined, to bring back all of them," he told reporters before boarding the Wing of Zion state plane.[138] He added that "we are determined to ensure that Gaza will no longer constitute a threat to Israel," meaning Israel would not allow a situation encouraging "more kidnappings, more murders, more beheadings, more invasions," and that this required "eliminating Hamas's military and governing capabilities."[138] An Israeli negotiating team travelled to Doha for indirect talks with the group.[138]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Netanyahu as defiant as ever, and argued that with Trump publicly expecting to negotiate an end to the Gaza war and Hamas reportedly responding positively to proposed ceasefire terms, the meeting would force Netanyahu to reveal what he called the true face of political [[zionism|Zionism]].[138]

## Likud annexation letter

Separately, fifteen ministers from Netanyahu's [[likud|Likud]] party, along with Knesset speaker [[amir-ohana|Amir Ohana]], signed a letter urging [[west-bank-annexation|annexation of the West Bank]] before the end of the parliamentary summer session, arguing that a Palestinian state would be an existential threat.[138] Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] endorsed the initiative.[138] Commentary in the Badlands News Brief noted that the pressure came from Netanyahu's own party's junior ministers rather than the Security Cabinet, whose sole Likud member was Justice Minister [[yariv-levin|Yariv Levin]].[138]

## Gaza City operation amid Egyptian truce push (August 2025)

As Egypt pressed Israel for a response to a ceasefire proposal that [[hamas|Hamas]] had approved, Netanyahu announced in August 2025 that he was "shortening the timelines" for the IDF's plans to conquer Gaza City, a day after Strategic Affairs Minister Ron Dermer met Qatari officials in Paris to discuss the negotiations.[139] While momentum had mounted toward a hostage release deal, Netanyahu continued to dismiss such efforts and focused instead on the cabinet-approved plan to intensify fighting in the Gaza Strip, though he did not outright reject the latest proposal.[139] In an evening statement he said he had instructed the army to "shorten the timelines for seizing the last terror strongholds and for the defeat of Hamas."[139]

Badlands commentary framed the decision as Netanyahu navigating political obstacles that could bring down his coalition, end his career and lead to prosecution and possible imprisonment.[139] Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] was reported to be threatening resignation if Netanyahu accepted the partial hostage deal, saying "If the prime minister goes through with the deal, I will make difficult decisions and resign from the government."[139] Meanwhile longtime opponents [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] and [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] offered to join the coalition and keep Netanyahu in power if he accepted a deal for immediate hostage releases.[139] The commentary judged that trading Smotrich for Lapid or Gantz would be foolish, since the latter would likely collapse the government at the first opportunity, whereas Smotrich — who shares Netanyahu's ambitions for military conquest — recognizes that Netanyahu is his best chance at holding real power.[139]

## Alleged cash transfers to Hamas-linked families

Badlands Media has alleged that after [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] sanctioned [[hamas|Hamas]] and cut off its funding in 2018, Netanyahu devised a scheme under which Israel physically carried briefcases containing tens of millions of dollars in cash into Gaza and handed them to families with ties to the organization, thereby sustaining it.[140] According to the account, Netanyahu was exposed by one of his own cabinet ministers, who was so appalled by the plot that he resigned from the government and went public with the story.[140] The outlet cited this history in arguing that Abbas, not Netanyahu, had been the figure genuinely attempting to suppress Hamas.[140]

In September 2024, ahead of Netanyahu's address to the United Nations General Assembly, Badlands writers noted that he had recently been disparaging Abbas publicly, and recalled that in July 2024 — the night before Netanyahu addressed Congress — [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had posted a letter from Abbas expressing concern over the attempt on Trump's life, complete with a hand-written note.[140]

## Humanitarian ceasefire decision and coalition crisis (July 2025)

Over the weekend of July 26–27, 2025, Netanyahu decided — without the knowledge of Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] — to enact "localized humanitarian ceasefires" in [[gaza|Gaza]], which the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] announced early Sunday morning.[141] An official told *The Jerusalem Post* that the resulting rift with Smotrich amounted to a "real" crisis, with relations "very, very tense and complicated," and that the Religious Zionist Party's seven MKs could exit a coalition already reduced to 60 members, likely toppling the government.[141] National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] separately called Netanyahu "morally bankrupt" for allowing food into Gaza.[141]

## Dispute with Trump over Gaza starvation

Netanyahu insisted that it was a "bold-faced lie" to say [[israel|Israel]] was fuelling hunger in Gaza, a claim publicly contradicted by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who said there was "real starvation" in the territory and that "those children look very hungry."[141]

## July 2025 Washington visit

Netanyahu concluded a week-long trip to the United States on the night of Thursday, July 10, 2025, returning to Israel.[142] Shortly before departing he visited Andrews Air Force Base, where he flew in a U.S. military helicopter, and he and his wife [[sara-netanyahu|Sara Netanyahu]] also visited Monticello, the home of Thomas Jefferson.[142] In an interview with Newsmax before leaving he said he hoped a ceasefire deal would be reached and that ten living hostages would be released in an upcoming agreement.[142]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] assessed that Netanyahu left Washington with nothing to show for the week, speculating that his aim had been to negotiate terms with [[donald-trump|Trump]] permitting Israel to annex all of Palestine rather than Gaza alone, beginning with the installation of governments led by handpicked figures — an offer Trump evidently declined given the absence of any announced deal.[142] Reports indicated Netanyahu had intended to stay until at least Saturday to close an agreement, and that political opponents including [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] warned him not to come home empty-handed.[142]

He was simultaneously pressured from within his own Security Cabinet, where [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]] and [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] publicly warned against any deal that would prevent Israel from continuing its war with [[hamas|Hamas]] if it chose to.[142] Compounding matters at home, [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] generals were drawing up orders to begin conscripting Orthodox Jews, who had been exempt from mandatory service since Israel's founding, and the Orthodox community threatened to dissolve the government unless Netanyahu blocked the orders and codified the exemption into law — the [[haredi-conscription-crisis|Haredi conscription crisis]].[142] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry concluded that these conditions explained the empty-handed return and that drastic measures would now be needed to perpetuate the war.[142] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] offered a shorter reading: "The wars we're told about are actually construction projects."[142]

## Rafah tent camp strike (May 2024)

On Monday, May 27, 2024, Netanyahu said that a "tragic mistake" had been made in an Israeli strike in the southern Gaza city of Rafah that set fire to a camp housing displaced Palestinians and, according to local officials, killed at least 45 people.[143] He did not elaborate on the error.[143] Israel's military had initially said it carried out a precise airstrike on a [[hamas|Hamas]] compound killing two senior militants, and as details of the strike and fire emerged it said it had opened an investigation into the civilian deaths.[143] The strike added to surging international criticism of Israel over its war with Hamas, with even its closest allies expressing outrage, and came a week after the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]] demanded that Israel halt its offensive in Rafah.[143] The episode is covered in full at [[2024-rafah-tent-camp-strike|the 2024 Rafah tent camp strike]].[143]

## April 2024: preparing for scenarios beyond Gaza

On April 11, 2024, Netanyahu said [[israel|Israel]] was continuing its war in Gaza while also preparing for "scenarios in other areas," amid concern that [[iran|Iran]] was readying a strike in retaliation for the killing of senior Iranian commanders.[144] Speaking after a visit to the Tel Nof air force base in southern Israel, he said: "Whoever harms us, we will harm them. We are prepared to meet all of the security needs of the State of Israel, both defensively and offensively."[144] The comments came as Israeli troops and warplanes launched an overnight operation in central Gaza aimed at destroying the infrastructure of armed Palestinian groups, and days after the April 1 airstrike on the Iranian embassy compound in Damascus that killed a senior general and six other Iranian officers — an attack Israel did not claim.[144] President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had pledged "ironclad" American support for Israel against Iranian reprisals despite his criticism of Netanyahu over civilian casualties in Gaza.[144] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded with the Tolkien line "Drums... drums... in the deep," describing the drums of war as thundering and asking what had become of the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]].[144]

## June 2025 Israel–Iran war and international reaction

Israel began bombing [[iran|Iran]] in mid-June 2025, claiming Tehran was nearing completion of a nuclear bomb — an accusation Iran dismissed.[145] Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] condemned the campaign along with Israel's conduct in Gaza, telling his AK Party in Parliament that "the most horrifying photos and videos from World War II pale in comparison to what we're seeing from Gaza" and asserting that Netanyahu had "long surpassed the tyrant Hitler in the crime of genocide"; Foreign Minister [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] dismissed the remarks as incitement from "the sultan, in his own eyes."[145] Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] briefed Chinese leader [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] on his conversations with Netanyahu and Iranian President [[masoud-pezeshkian|Masoud Pezeshkian]], with Moscow and Beijing jointly condemning Israel's actions as violations of the UN Charter.[145]

The war carried a heavy fiscal price: early estimates put Israel's costs at hundreds of millions of dollars a day, with missile interceptors alone running from tens of millions to $200 million daily and building damage projected to require at least $400 million to repair — pressures that could constrain Israel's ability to conduct a lengthy war.[145] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Netanyahu had "delivered on the promises" of becoming the villain the story required, attacking Iran as the only move available to remain in office and avoid prison, and that Israel had thereby bitten off more than it could chew while being geopolitically isolated.[145] He also noted the Netanyahu government's vocal desire to annex Damascus and southern Syria and reported Israeli interest in a corridor through Syria to Kurdistan as a staging ground for a protracted war with Iran.[145]

## June 2024 coalition crisis

By late June 2024 Netanyahu's government showed all the signs of heading for an early election, probably the following spring: coalition party leaders were openly campaigning, his [[likud|Likud]] party was beginning to crack, senior military and civil-service figures were clashing openly with him, and he remained broadly unpopular despite general public support for the war.[146] A state commission of inquiry accused him of putting Israel's security at risk and harming the country's foreign relations and economy by mishandling a submarine-procurement deal between 2009 and 2016; Netanyahu shrugged the commission off as politically biased but had yet to answer the accusation.[146] The same week [[israeli-supreme-court|Israel's Supreme Court]] declared the long-held draft exemption for ultra-Orthodox men illegal, straining his coalition of nationalist and religious parties.[146]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the situation as doomed for Netanyahu, noting reports that his wife [[sara-netanyahu|Sara]] had told hostage families that [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] leadership was "staging a coup" against her husband, prompting one bereaved father to say Netanyahu was "spreading delusional conspiracies" and that there would be no hostage deal and no revival for Israel as long as he remained in power.[146] He observed that Netanyahu faced threats of resignation from [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] and [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] if the ultra-Orthodox were conscripted — resignations that would legally dissolve the government — while [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] campaigned publicly and Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] was described in the mainstream press as Biden's "inside man."[146] He also recounted Netanyahu accusing Ben-Gvir of leaking to the press and demanding proof of loyalty before granting him a seat in any reconstituted war cabinet, and speculated that a coming 9/11 disclosure would implicate Netanyahu as a co-conspirator.[146]

## Haaretz assessment after the 2024 debate

Following the June 2024 [[2024-presidential-debate|presidential debate]], a Haaretz opinion column argued that Netanyahu had been waiting for a signal months ahead of the U.S. election as to whether it would pay him, after the failure of October 7, to keep clinging to power until [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was replaced — and that on debate night he received his answer.[147] The columnist contended that despite excitement among Netanyahu's supporters over Biden's collapse, a [[donald-trump|Trump]] presidency would be bad for the Israeli prime minister, because Trump "feeds on weakness" and does not like losers.[147] In that assessment Netanyahu qualified as easy prey: he had not destroyed Hamas, was sinking in the Gaza quagmire without a rescue plan, had rejected a regional arrangement that would have secured the return of the hostages, presided over a deteriorating economy, lacked public support, and faced a likely International Criminal Court arrest warrant.[147]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] agreed that Trump's win looked inevitable and would unsettle Netanyahu, but rejected the "loser persona" explanation, arguing instead that the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] had been wielded as a political weapon to ignite a peaceful counterinsurgency in the region and inside Israel against Netanyahu and what he calls the Israeli Deep State.[147]

## Gantz withdrawal and Trump's Channel 12 remarks

On June 9, 2024, [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] withdrew his National Unity party from Netanyahu's emergency wartime government, accusing the prime minister of preventing Israel from achieving "true victory" over [[hamas|Hamas]] and calling for elections, after Netanyahu failed to accept an agreed-upon vision for the Gaza conflict by a June 8 deadline Gantz had set.[148] Badlands commentary noted that Gantz had been widely seen as the moderate in the war cabinet and that his exit would leave Netanyahu surrounded by Zionist-maximalists in his security cabinet.[148]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had waged a propaganda campaign against Netanyahu in December 2021 through Israel's Channel 12, quoting Trump as saying he told Netanyahu "you don't want to make a deal," that "I don't think Bibi ever wanted to make peace; never did," and that Netanyahu merely "tapped us along."[148] Trump was said to have contrasted Netanyahu unfavorably with Gantz and [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]], and to have been polling above 80% with Israeli citizens at the time, which the commentary linked to subsequent Israeli civil unrest against Netanyahu.[148]

## Move to dismiss Shin Bet chief Ronen Bar (March 2025)

On Sunday, March 16, 2025, Netanyahu announced his intention to dismiss [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]] chief [[ronen-bar|Ronen Bar]], summoning him to an urgent evening meeting and informing him that the cabinet would vote on his removal later that week; the attorney general responded that no dismissal could proceed before a legal review.[149] Netanyahu said in a video statement that "at all times, but especially during an existential war such as the one we face, there must be full trust between the prime minister and the head of the Shin Bet," declaring an "ongoing distrust" that had grown over time while professing appreciation for the service's personnel.[149]

The move followed Netanyahu's police report the previous week alleging that former Shin Bet director [[nadav-argaman|Nadav Argaman]] was blackmailing him after Argaman cryptically threatened in an interview to expose truths about Netanyahu and the October 7 attack.[149] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Argaman had cited Netanyahu's exposed funding of [[hamas|Hamas]] in 2018–19 after the group lost Palestinian Authority backing, a factor said to have cost Netanyahu moderate Zionist support and pushed him toward Kahanist coalition partners, and that Bar had signaled willingness to further expose those ties through a report showing the money originated in [[qatar|Qatar]].[149] The same commentary raised the question of whether Netanyahu had been involved in planning the October 7 attack, noting its convenient timing as he faced pressure to normalize relations with Palestine and accept a two-state solution he had long opposed, and reported that he was attempting to sabotage [[adam-boehler|Adam Boehler]]'s effort to free American hostages.[149]

## Coalition strain over the January 2025 Gaza deal

Israeli reporting in January 2025 held that the intervention of [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Middle East envoy, revived hostage talks with [[hamas|Hamas]] and left Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu with a deal he had repeatedly rejected; what happened inside his coalition would determine whether he approved it.[150] Netanyahu's aides were surprised when Witkoff insisted on meeting during the Sabbath, and the prime minister broke with official practice by appearing at his office for the meeting before Witkoff returned to Qatar to close the deal.[150]

Netanyahu's allied media pushed what critics called a "no-choice" narrative blaming Trump, with laments on Channel 14 that "Trump isn't what we thought"; on the Patriots program Yotam Zimri complained that all the senior officials in the U.S. administration were saying the same thing and concluded "so all his people have been lying – it's a big disappointment," while Yinon Magal answered that Trump himself was pressing for the deal and that its impact would be very difficult.[150] On the Monday afternoon Netanyahu briefed Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] and National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]]; the government's survival was linked to whether Netanyahu could pass the 2025 budget amid the crisis over the Haredi draft law and the internecine ultra-Orthodox struggle, and observers speculated he might prefer to bring the coalition down over a deal popular with the wider public rather than over the whims of the Rebbe of Gur.[150] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Netanyahu, having lost the ability to pass a budget after the Israeli Supreme Court ended the [[ultra-orthodox-conscription-in-israel|Haredi conscription exemption]], might use the ceasefire as a pretext for collapse and new elections, but would be left a "deep state orphan" once the radicals departed.[150]

## Lebanon deal deliberations (October 2024)

## Trump relationship and the February 2025 Washington visit

Reporting by Axios correspondent [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]] in February 2025 described lasting friction beneath the public alliance between Netanyahu and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[175] Trump had concluded at a relatively early stage that Netanyahu was dragging his feet on reaching peace with the Palestinians, but kept his bitterness private because the public "bromance" was good politics.[175] The relationship suffered a severe blow when Netanyahu congratulated Joe Biden on the 2020 election result; Trump did not speak to him for almost four years and criticized him after the October 7 attack, meeting him again only at Mar-a-Lago in summer 2024.[175] A U.S. source close to Trump said that although relations had improved, "Trump still does not like Bibi, does not trust Bibi."[175]

Following an Oval Office meeting with Netanyahu, Trump announced that the United States could "take over" Gaza and turn it into the "Riviera of the Middle East," a [[gaza-relocation-proposal-2025|relocation proposal]] that stunned regional governments and much of his own team.[175] During the same White House visit Netanyahu presented Trump with a gold-plated pager commemorating the [[2024-lebanon-pager-attacks|Hezbollah pager operation]], a gift confirmed by his adviser Dmitry Gendelman.[175]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Netanyahu appeared to have torpedoed the hostage ceasefire deal by changing its terms to demand that [[hamas|Hamas]] leadership leave Gaza permanently, a demand Hamas rejected, and suggested he had been emboldened by Trump's rhetoric.[175] He argued that if Netanyahu wrecked the deal after everything he had been given, Trump would be "free and clear to drop the hammer on Bibi."[175] The same commentary noted the admission by Netanyahu's fired defense minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] that the [[hannibal-directive|Hannibal Directive]] had been used during the war on Gaza, recalling that Netanyahu had cited Gallant's opposition to expanding the war against Hezbollah as the reason for his November dismissal.[175]

## Badlands assessment during the Trump transition (November 2024)

In November 2024, with hostage families and deal-minded Israeli officials placing their hopes in President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to succeed where President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had failed in persuading Netanyahu to end the war in Gaza in exchange for the release of hostages held by [[hamas|Hamas]], Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote bluntly that "Trump is about to cuck Netanyahu."[176] The commentary argued that the only thing Netanyahu had achieved since October 7 was turning most of the world against Israel, and noted the growth over the preceding year of an Israeli public movement to bring the hostages home, which had merged with the earlier protest movement against his proposed judicial reforms.[176]

The writer inferred from Trump's posture that Trump did not believe there were living hostages left — which would imply Trump considered Netanyahu to be lying about the intelligence he was broadcasting to the world.[176]

Badlands attributed Netanyahu's power projection less to institutional office than to pedigree: he is the son of the personal secretary of [[zeev-jabotinsky|Ze'ev Jabotinsky]], and has been described as the self-proclaimed heir of the 18th-century rabbi the Vilna Gaon.[176] The piece recalled that 34 years earlier the Lubavitcher Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem Schneerson, met Netanyahu on camera — when Netanyahu was Israel's Foreign Minister — and said that "he, Benjamin Netanyahu, will be Israel's prime minister, who will pass the scepter to the Messiah."[176] The writer said his strong feelings about Netanyahu stem from the sense that the prime minister is prepared to bring about great cataclysm in order to fulfil the destiny prophesied over him, making him "a very dangerous animal that is now being cornered before it can hurt more people."[176]

The analysis framed the appointment of [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] and other pro-Israel cabinet members as Trump playing a hand in a high-stakes poker game: Netanyahu must know Trump is not truly his friend and that they do not share ambitions, yet may not be able to afford open defiance given the visible concessions Trump has made to accommodate Israel.[176] Open questions posed included whether Netanyahu would stop the war if told to, whether he would bend the knee at a genuine peace table — knowing that the day the fighting stops his postponed 2019 corruption prosecution resumes — and whether the Israeli public would retain any appetite for his wars if Trump delivered the hostages.[176]

An effigy of Netanyahu was set alight by demonstrators during the [[2024-montreal-nato-summit-riots|Montreal NATO summit riots]] later the same month.[176]

## Congratulating Trump on the 2024 election

Netanyahu was the first world leader to congratulate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on his November 2024 election victory, posting on social media: "Dear Donald and Melania Trump, Congratulations on history's greatest comeback!"[177] He added that Trump's "historic return to the White House offers a new beginning for America and a powerful recommitment to the great alliance between Israel and America."[177] The message came as Israel remained at war with Hamas and Hezbollah following the October 7, 2023 attack, described in reporting on the congratulations as the deadliest single-day antisemitic attack since the Holocaust.[177]

## Decision to retaliate after the April 2024 Iranian attack

Despite reports that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had asked him not to retaliate, and with neither the Arab states that helped shield [[israel|Israel]] nor the United States willing to assist a counter-strike, Netanyahu decided to attack [[iran|Iran]] anyway, requesting a list of targets from the Israeli Defense Force.[179] [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] Director General [[rafael-grossi|Rafael Grossi]] warned in the same period that he was concerned Israel might target Iranian nuclear facilities, urging "extreme restraint."[179] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] predicted that if Netanyahu followed through on what he called foolish arrogance, the Arab guarantors of the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] might turn around and defend Iran from Israel in order to preserve a fragile regional peace.[179]

## Washington visit and Mar-a-Lago meeting (July 2024)

Netanyahu traveled to Washington in July 2024 at a moment of extreme political flux in the United States, with American officials wary of his history of injecting himself into U.S. domestic politics.[179] The main purpose of the trip was an address to a joint meeting of Congress, but his meetings with administration officials also carried hopes for progress in U.S.-led efforts to mediate an end to the nine-month Israel-Hamas war, as the Palestinian death toll from Israeli strikes in Gaza neared 40,000 and new deaths were reported among surviving hostages.[179] The trip had been planned weeks before the July 13 assassination attempt on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s July 21 withdrawal from the presidential race in favor of [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[179]

On July 23, 2024 Trump announced he would host Netanyahu that Friday at Mar-a-Lago in Palm Beach, Florida — their first meeting since the end of Trump's presidency — writing on Truth Social that he looked forward to welcoming "Bibi."[179] Netanyahu had requested the in-person meeting during the Washington trip, according to Politico.[179] Vice-presidential nominee [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] announced he would not attend Netanyahu's congressional address.[179]

## Badlands assessment of the Trump-Netanyahu relationship

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Netanyahu and Trump are in fact enemies.[179] The cited evidence included Netanyahu being the first world leader to congratulate Biden after the 2020 election — read as consecrating a stolen election, something Trump has publicly attacked him for in Israeli media — the February 15, 2017 joint press conference at which Trump called him out over illegal West Bank settlements and told him to hold back pending negotiations with the Palestinians, and the strained body language of Trump's May 2017 visit to Israel following his Saudi Arabia stop.[179] The account further alleged a pattern of subversion of Middle East peace efforts: Netanyahu backing out of the [[qassem-soleimani|Soleimani]] strike the day before it was scheduled in January 2020, and dispatching ambassador [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] to tell the White House that Israel would withdraw from the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] the day before signing, before Trump personally forced the issue by phone.[179]

The pro-Zionist outlet [[the-forward|The Forward]], historically supportive of Netanyahu, published an opinion piece arguing that he should stay in Washington rather than return home, framing the trip as being about preserving his own power and evading corruption charges he had faced since 2020 rather than saving Israel.[179] An Israeli student recently returned from reserve duty in Gaza was quoted saying, "Honestly, no one here cares. We all have bigger problems."[179]

## History of addresses to Congress

The July 2024 speech was Netanyahu's fourth address to Congress — or a fifth appearance if his September 12, 2002 congressional testimony as a private citizen is counted, in which he urged the United States to invade Iraq and kill [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]], promising peace in the Middle East as a result, and also urged invasions of Iran and Afghanistan.[179] Badlands noted that his claims that Saddam possessed nuclear weapons and that [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] was operating from Iraq both proved false, and that Iraq became a haven for Al Qaeda and [[isis|ISIS]] only after Saddam's removal in April 2003.[179] His May 24, 2011 address on "security in the Middle East" was linked by the same commentary to the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] operation to overthrow [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] and the transfer of Libyan weapons to fighters opposing [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] in Syria, who would later become ISIS.[179] Badlands anticipated that the 2024 speech would call for war with Iran, and noted that his afternoon rather than primetime slot was a deliberate slight.[179]

## The Azaria precedent and the 2024 base riots

Netanyahu's handling of the 2016 [[elor-azaria|Elor Azaria]] case was cited in July 2024 as a precedent for the normalisation of misconduct: having initially joined IDF commanders in condemning Azaria's killing of a wounded Palestinian as a breach of military ethics requiring prosecution, Netanyahu shifted under right-wing pressure, criticised his generals for investigating the soldier and eventually joined calls for a pardon.[182] Badlands commentary in the same period predicted an approaching civil war inside Netanyahu's cabinet between military men such as [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] and religious hardliners such as [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] over the [[sde-teiman-detention-facility|Sde Teiman]] detainee-abuse affair.[182] The writer also noted Netanyahu's claim to be a direct descendant of the Vilna Gaon.[182]

## The Bibi Files (September 2024)

On September 9–10, 2024, leaked police interrogation videos of Netanyahu were shown publicly for the first time at the Toronto film festival world premiere of [[the-bibi-files|*The Bibi Files*]], after Israeli courts rejected his request to block the screening.[184] In the footage Netanyahu is seen furiously denying allegations of bribery and corruption; the audience was tense and vocal, with many carrying signs reading "Bring Them Home" and "Deal Now" in reference to hostages held in Gaza.[184]

The film, directed by [[alexis-bloom|Alexis Bloom]] and produced by [[alex-gibney|Alex Gibney]], argues that Netanyahu is prolonging the war in Gaza — which had amassed more than 40,000 casualties — to avoid possible prison time stemming from corruption charges, casting a humanitarian crisis that flouts international law as an act of self-preservation.[184] Insiders including the journalist [[raviv-drucker|Raviv Drucker]], former Shin Bet head [[ami-ayalon|Ami Ayalon]] and a childhood friend trace a long-running pattern of Netanyahu serving his own interests while clinging to power — from deliberate ploys to sabotage an alliance between the West Bank and Gaza by enabling Hamas, to his alliance with the violent far right and his attempted overhaul of the supreme court.[184] Gibney said of him: "I've never seen the depth of moral corruption as I've seen in this man."[184]

The interrogation videos were recorded by police between 2016 and 2018, before charges were formally brought.[184] They include Netanyahu addressing allegations that he and his wife [[sara-netanyahu|Sara Netanyahu]] accepted expensive champagne, Cuban cigars and jewelry from the producer [[arnon-milchan|Arnon Milchan]]; Netanyahu minimized the champagne and cigars as gifts from a friend and denied knowledge of the jewelry, and defended a marginal tax break extension benefiting Milchan as being for the good of the state.[184] According to the documentary, Netanyahu's lawyer filed a motion to delay the trial then scheduled for December 2024, citing the ongoing war as the reason.[184]

## Warning ahead of the January 2025 Gaza ceasefire

Hours before the [[gaza-ceasefire|Gaza ceasefire]] was due to take effect on Sunday, January 19, 2025, Netanyahu said in a televised speech that Israel was ready to resume the war against [[hamas|Hamas]] should talks on a second phase of the agreement fail.[185] He stressed that the ceasefire was "temporary," that Israel reserved the right to resume strikes in Gaza, and that it had the backing of President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to do so.[185] Netanyahu also recounted what he called the success of Israel's 15-month military campaign, including the killing of Hamas leader [[yahya-sinwar|Yahya Sinwar]], saying "We changed the face of the Middle East" and that Hamas was now "completely alone."[185] The ceasefire was due to come into force at 08:30 local time.[185]

That same morning, [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]]'s [[otzma-yehudit|Otzma Yehudit]] party quit Netanyahu's coalition over the deal, withdrawing five or six seats and leaving the government only a few seats short of collapse; a further exit by [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]]'s [[religious-zionism-party|Religious Zionism]] party would trigger new elections.[185] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Netanyahu was unlikely to allow Phase 2 to proceed, because a Smotrich walkout would remove him from office and leave him exposed to the corruption charges pending against him, and framed the open question as what Netanyahu would do to subvert Trump's demand for an end to the violence in order to remain in power.[185]

## ICC warrant application (May 2024)

On May 20, 2024, ICC chief prosecutor [[karim-khan|Karim Khan]] announced he was seeking an arrest warrant against Netanyahu for war crimes and crimes against humanity, alongside Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] and three [[hamas]] leaders — the first time the Hague court had targeted the national leader of a close US ally.[186] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited a Judge Napolitano interview with Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]] in support of the contention that Netanyahu and the power structure in Tel Aviv control the US government, a claim the commentator saw reinforced by the bipartisan American outrage at the ICC filing.[186] The same commentary noted that when Israel Diamond Exchange founder [[moshe-schnitzer|Moshe Schnitzer]] — grandfather of mining billionaire [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] — died in 2007, it was Netanyahu who delivered the eulogy at his funeral, and that Netanyahu had reportedly approved former [[mossad]] director [[yossi-cohen|Yossi Cohen]]'s 2019 trips to the Democratic Republic of the Congo, though that approval was barred from public disclosure.[186]

## Bus bombings and West Bank operation (February 2025)

After three empty buses exploded in the Tel Aviv suburbs of Bat Yam and Holon on the night of February 20, 2025 in a suspected terror attack, Netanyahu's office said the prime minister was receiving constant updates and held a late-night security assessment, after which it announced that he had instructed the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] to embark on a massive operation in the West Bank against terrorist hubs.[188] The same day, [[hamas|Hamas]] returned hostage remains that Israel said failed to include Shiri of the [[bibas-family|Bibas family]].[188]

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the sequence "convenient for Bibi Netanyahu," an opportunity for more war "just like October 7th," and said that given what he described as the Israeli government's record of lying to the public he was disinclined to believe assertions that escalated the conflict.[188] He also recalled that Netanyahu had routinely used the parents of [[omer-neutra|Omer Neutra]] as props in the press to build sympathy for the war effort, as recently as a week before Israel disclosed in December 2024 that the 21-year-old dual-citizen IDF soldier had been killed on October 7.[188] He predicted that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], having turned against [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], would soon adopt a similar posture toward Netanyahu.[188]

## War cabinet rift over post-war Gaza, May 2024

In May 2024 the fissures within Netanyahu's cabinet widened into open conflict when Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] used a televised address to demand that the prime minister publicly rule out Israeli military or civil rule of [[gaza|Gaza]] after [[hamas|Hamas]] and instead advance governance by non-Hamas Palestinian entities backed by international actors.[189] Netanyahu had declared only hours earlier that discussion of the "day after" was meaningless until Hamas was defeated, and separately released a video saying, "I am not prepared to replace Hamastan with Fatahstan," a reference to the two factions associated with the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]].[189]

Coalition members urged Netanyahu to fire Gallant, but [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] came to the defense minister's defense, saying Gallant spoke the truth.[189] Badlands commentary observed that with Gallant and Gantz aligned, Netanyahu was outvoted two to one in his own three-member [[israeli-war-cabinet|war cabinet]], and that military strategists broadly agreed Hamas could not be eradicated, meaning armed resistance would continue against any occupying force.[189]

## Weighing West Bank annexation (September 2025)

In early September 2025 Netanyahu was reported to be set to gather top ministers and aides for a Thursday discussion exploring the possibility of applying Israeli sovereignty to parts of the [[west-bank|West Bank]], a step tantamount to annexation.[190] According to Hebrew media reports the meeting was to focus on the implications of international recognition of a Palestinian state and how Jerusalem might respond, with annexation one of multiple options the small forum would weigh.[190] The discussion came ahead of that month's [[united-nations-general-assembly|United Nations General Assembly]], where a wave of Western nations intended to recognize a Palestinian state against Israeli and American wishes.[190] It also coincided with efforts by [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] officials, through various channels, to persuade Washington to reverse its decision to bar President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] and some 80 other officials from the UN meeting.[190]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued Netanyahu "can't help himself" as he scrambles to stave off collapse through a [[knesset|Knesset]] vote, with the trick being to keep every faction of his coalition satisfied amid ever-evolving demands.[190] He noted the shifting rationale from the October 7 aftermath — when the Gaza invasion was framed as defense of Israel's sovereign borders rather than expansion of them — to annexation of the West Bank, which he said had nothing to do with October 7.[190]

## Declining Israeli influence in Congress (2025)

In a Monday interview with the Daily Caller reported in early September 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he was surprised that Israel had lost its control over [[congress|Congress]].[190] "Israel had the strongest lobby in Congress of anything or body, or of any company or corporation or state that I've ever seen. Today, it doesn't have that strong a lobby. It's amazing," Trump said, adding that Israel "had total control over Congress, and now they don't."[190] Journalist Reagan Reese cited a March Pew poll finding 53% of US adults held an unfavorable view of Israel, up from 42% in 2022, and that half of Republicans polled viewed Israel unfavorably.[190] [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] responded that "Netanyahu can cry and Fox can spin, but the truth is clear: Israel is a protectorate, not the boss," a framing Badlands host [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] read as speaking reality to the enemy while using the enemy's own narrative language.[190]
