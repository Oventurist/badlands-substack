# Edward Lozansky

Dr. Edward Lozansky is the founder of the American University in Moscow and a long-standing advocate of United States–Russian cooperation.[1] His autobiography, *Building U.S.–Russia Bridges*, is cited as one of the principal accounts of the back-channel efforts in the late 1980s and early 1990s to build a post-Cold War order based on mutual development, trust-building measures and scientific progress.[1]

## Documentation of the "lost chance"

According to the article, few observers are aware of what it describes as the ripe potential for a new age of cooperation and abundance driven by forces within the American intelligentsia and their Russian counterparts, who saw in the Soviet crisis an opportunity to turn swords into plowshares.[1] Lozansky's memoir, together with the 2008 [[schiller-institute|Schiller Institute]] documentary *The Lost Chance of 1989*, tells the story of these plans and of the possibility of an age of cooperation premised on large-scale industrial progress.[1]

Lozansky's institution, the American University in Moscow, is also where the article's author [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] serves as a Senior Fellow.[1]
