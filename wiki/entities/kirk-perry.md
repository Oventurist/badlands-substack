# Kirk Perry

Kirk Perry is a former United States Department of Agriculture program director who pleaded guilty in April 2025 to his role in a kickback scheme that billed the federal government nearly $400,000 for work that was never performed.[1]

## The scheme

Perry, 60, of Lorain, Ohio, admitted that from August 2015 through November 2022 he arranged for his nephew, Jamarea Grant, 31, of Cleveland, Ohio, to be hired by two companies under contract with the [[usda|USDA]] Office of Assistant Secretary for Civil Rights.[1] Grant reported directly to Perry, who also approved the invoices billing for Grant's time, and the two conspired to bill the government for work Grant did not do.[1] Grant received nearly $400,000 for that non-existent work.[1] Perry additionally had access to Grant's bank account and transferred roughly $125,000 of the USDA payments from Grant's account into his own.[1]

## Prosecution

Perry pleaded guilty before U.S. District Court Judge [[colleen-kollar-kotelly|Colleen Kollar-Kotelly]] to conspiracy to commit money, property, and honest services wire fraud.[1] Grant had previously pleaded guilty to the same charge on November 27, 2024.[1] The plea was announced by U.S. Attorney Edward R. Martin Jr. and Special Agent in Charge Jeldrys Lowry of the USDA Office of Inspector General.[1] Kollar-Kotelly was to determine sentences after considering the U.S. Sentencing Guidelines and other statutory factors; Perry was scheduled for sentencing on December 4, 2025, with Grant's sentencing pending.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary reframed the phrase "bill the government" as defrauding taxpayers, noting that a USDA program director knowingly defrauded the public of more than half a million dollars, and took the case as evidence that the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration was prosecuting public corruption.[1]
