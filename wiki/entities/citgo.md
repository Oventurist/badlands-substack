# Citgo

Citgo is a United States refiner and marketer of petroleum products that has been central to the commercial dimension of American policy toward [[venezuela|Venezuela]].[1] As of late 2025 the company was pumping oil off the Venezuelan coast after acquiring [[hess-corporation|Hess]] and its assets in an acquisition.[1]

## Sanctions exemption

In July 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] ordered an exemption to the sanctions he had previously placed on Venezuela, allowing Citgo to bring Venezuelan crude back to the United States for refinement.[1] Under the exemption, Citgo is not permitted to pay any taxes or fees to the Venezuelan government; however, the fine print of Trump's order allows Citgo instead to give crude oil to the Venezuelan state oil company [[pdvsa|PDVSA]], which PDVSA can then sell on the market.[1]

## Litigation with Exxon

The exemption followed a July 2025 lawsuit that Citgo won against [[exxonmobil|Exxon]] over its purchase of Hess.[1] Exxon claimed that it held a first-right-of-refusal deal with Hess covering Hess's oil rights in Venezuela should they ever go on sale.[1] Exxon, which already has an operational presence in the oil belt under licences issued by the neighbouring Guyanese government, sued Citgo and demanded that Hess's rights be turned over — an outcome that would effectively have granted it a monopoly over the belt, described as the largest known oil reserve in the world.[1] Citgo and Hess argued that because Citgo was buying Hess itself rather than only its oil rights, Exxon's first right of refusal was inapplicable; the judge agreed and denied Exxon's claim.[1] Within days of the judgment, Trump issued the exemption.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the inflow of rich Venezuelan crude has helped keep fuel prices low while the administration tries to tackle inflation, and questioned what role Trump himself played in the sequence between Citgo, Exxon and Hess, concluding that "none of this can be a coincidence."[1] The same commentary observed that because Venezuela plainly does not control American land or oil, Trump's simultaneous demand that a "Hostile Regime" return "our Oil, Land, or any other Assets" likely referred to something else — in this reading, the transnational cartel said to have controlled the US government for decades.[1]

## December 2025: forced sale and the Venezuela standoff

Badlands analysis published during President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s December 2025 naval blockade of [[venezuela|Venezuela]] identified Citgo — not [[exxonmobil|Exxon]] — as the holder of the Venezuelan oil rights at the center of the dispute.[2] Citgo obtained those rights by acquiring [[hess-corporation|Hess]] outright in July 2025, bypassing Exxon's first right of refusal; Exxon's lawsuit challenging the deal was denied, finalizing it.[2]

Citgo is owned by [[pdvsa|PDVSA]], the Venezuelan state oil company — or was, until US judges ordered Venezuela to sell Citgo to satisfy owed debts, a court order issued on December 2, 2025.[2] Days after Exxon's claim was denied in July, Trump granted Citgo an exemption from the Venezuela sanctions he imposed in 2019, allowing the company to bring Venezuelan crude to the United States for refining and sale; while the exemption forbids Citgo from paying taxes or fees to the Venezuelan government, it permits Citgo to give PDVSA crude oil that can then be sold for money.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] concluded that whatever is going on, "it isn't the official story we are being told."[2]
