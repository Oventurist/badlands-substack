# Anthony J. Trenga

Anthony J. Trenga is a United States District Judge for the Eastern District of Virginia who presided over the 2022 trial of [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] in Alexandria.[1]

## Pre-trial rulings on the opening day

Before prospective jurors were brought in for voir dire, Trenga asked both sides to raise outstanding issues, and the resulting argument produced several of the day's disclosures.[1] The matters put to him included the defence's failure to produce certain documents, the permissible scope of testimony about the earlier counterespionage investigation of Danchenko, late-delivered translations of Russian-language emails between Sergei Millian and a Mr. Zlodorev, and the extent to which [[dossier-report-2016-95|dossier report 2016/95]] should be admitted.[1]

Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] asked Trenga to unseal the witness list.[1] On the prior investigation, Durham proposed telling the jury either that "there was an investigation" closed because the [[fbi|FBI]] mistakenly thought Danchenko had left the country and so was never resolved, or simply that an unresolved investigation had been conducted; defence counsel [[stuart-sears|Stuart Sears]] ultimately asked that it not be mentioned at all, and the judge said he would rule before opening statements.[1] On report 2016/95 Trenga said he would have to rule within the context of the testimony of Danchenko's handling agent.[1]

Trenga also handled the striking of jurors on the basis of their questionnaire answers, and a jury was seated by noon after a short recess.[1] Although he declined to rule definitively on the morning's disputes, he directed that neither the prior counterintelligence investigation, the failure to produce evidence, nor the matter of publicly available articles be mentioned in opening statements.[1]

## Post-opening dispute

After opening statements and the jury's excusal, Durham raised [[danny-onorato|Onorato]]'s accusation that Assistant Special Counsel [[michael-keilty|Michael Keilty]] had lied in his opening.[1] Trenga did not budge on the point, and when Onorato pressed further the judge declined to entertain it.[1] At the close of the first day of testimony Durham proposed a stopping point, which Trenga accepted.[1]

## Day two rulings

Before the jury and witness were brought in on the second day, counsel argued over emails and other messages the defense wished to admit but which could realistically be introduced only if [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]] himself testified.[2] After hearing both sides, Trenga framed the question as "whether the present sense exception for forward-looking information as an exception to the hearsay rule trumps the rule against admissibility of exculpatory statements by a defendant absent his testifying."[2] Later that day the defense introduced a May 26, 2016 email from [[dmitri-zlodorev|Dmitri Zlodorev]] to [[sergei-millian|Sergei Millian]] that the prosecution had earlier sought to admit over defense objection; the prosecution did not object but pointed the reversal out to Trenga for the record.[2] A sidebar over Danchenko's statement that he had received information from Millian was resolved in [[john-durham|Durham]]'s favor.[2]

## Rulings on day three

Trenga's evidentiary rulings shaped the third day of testimony. Objections by the defense to hearsay were repeatedly sustained: [[amy-anderson|Anderson]]-style second-hand accounts aside, when Agent [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]] began relaying what [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]] had said [[christopher-steele|Steele]] told him, Trenga sustained the objection.[3] Government exhibits were nonetheless admitted in volume, including roughly a dozen recordings and transcripts of Helson's meetings with Danchenko and screenshots of [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Dolan]]'s communications.[3]

Commentary on the day identified the cross-examination of Helson by [[stuart-sears|Stuart Sears]], concerning how the questioning about [[sergei-millian|Millian]] had been handled, as the exchange that contributed to Trenga's later dismissal of Count One of the indictment.[3]
