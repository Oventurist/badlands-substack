# Fatah

Fatah is the political faction of the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] led by [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]].[1]

Badlands commentary described Fatah as the Palestinian group that has spent the 32 years since 1993 seeking peace with Israel, dating from Abbas's signing of the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]] with Israeli Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin — an agreement under which the Palestinian government formally recognized the State of Israel.[1]

In the context of the March 2025 anti-war protests in Gaza, Fatah was presented as the likely governing alternative should [[hamas|Hamas]] be driven from power, a prospect the commentary argued the hardline elements of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s security cabinet would resist precisely because it would force negotiations with a partner already committed to recognition and peace.[1]

## Netanyahu's "Hamastan or Fatahstan" framing (2024)

In May 2024, Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] declared that he was "not ready to replace Hamastan with Fatahstan," rejecting any transfer of postwar Gaza governance to the Fatah-dominated [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] led by [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]].[2] The statement was a response to Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]]'s public demand that Israel rule out civilian or military governance of Gaza and raise a governing alternative to [[hamas|Hamas]] immediately.[2] Netanyahu argued that "the Palestinian Authority supports terror, educates for terror, funds terror," making the elimination of Hamas the precondition for any alternative.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized this as an obvious attempt to conflate two rival factions, and traced Fatah's trajectory since the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]].[2] The Palestinian Authority was established in 1993 with the signing of the Accords between Israeli Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin and Palestine Liberation Organization leader [[yasser-arafat|Yasser Arafat]], with the peace talks first initiated by the then-new Russian Federation.[2] Although Arafat is credited with the Accords, it was a young Mahmoud Abbas who signed on behalf of Palestine at the Clinton White House ceremony — a symbolic gesture signaling that the PLO would ride off into the sunset and let a peace-seeking Palestinian Authority govern.[2] Since that signing, Abbas and the Fatah faction have maintained a consistent message: a two-state solution.[2]

Fatah's challenges mounted after Arafat's death in 2004: Hamas entered Gaza politics, defeated Fatah in the 2006 election, attacked Fatah security forces and drove them out of the Strip.[2] The Badlands analysis contends that Hamas is an insurgency weapon meant to unseat Fatah in the West Bank as well, and that Abbas, Saudi Crown Prince MBS, and Jordanian King Abdullah have been restraining the Arab street while conducting a counterinsurgency against Netanyahu designed to protect Abbas from being ousted by Hamas.[2]

## 2017 Gaza handover attempt (Badlands account)

Badlands commentary published in February 2025 recounted that in 2017 the Fatah faction of the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] negotiated an agreement with [[hamas|Hamas]] under which Hamas would cede political control of Gaza and allow Fatah to enter and provide government services to the population.[3] When Palestinian President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] sent his prime minister to Gaza in March 2018 to finalize the details, Hamas bombed the convoy and nearly killed him.[3] Abbas responded by sanctioning Hamas and cutting off its funding, leaving the group bankrupt — an outcome the commentary argued could have ended Hamas in 2018 had [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] not, in its telling, kept the group afloat with large cash transfers.[3]
