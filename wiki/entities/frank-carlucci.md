# Frank Carlucci

Frank C. Carlucci was an American intelligence and defense official who became vice chairman and later chairman of the [[carlyle-group|Carlyle Group]].[1] Badlands Media treats his career as the clearest evidence that Carlyle was, in its telling, "a CIA operation from the beginning."[1]

## Career

Carlucci was the number two man at the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] during the [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]] administration, then served as national security adviser to [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]], and finally as Secretary of Defense after Caspar Weinberger was forced out over the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra]] scandal.[1] The Nation described a career that took him "from the CIA to the highest ranks of the defense and national security establishment and, finally, to the top of one of the world's largest private equity funds."[1] In November 2001 he addressed a national security conference sponsored by the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] and the Institute for Foreign Policy Analysis, a conservative think tank on whose board he sat, speaking on "Employing the Instruments of National Power in a Complex Environment."[1]

## Cold War arms buildup and Carlyle

Carlucci was a close friend of [[donald-rumsfeld|Donald Rumsfeld]] and advocated a huge arms buildup to end the Cold War — a program Badlands Media argues benefited the Bush family, the military industrial complex, the CIA and ultimately the Carlyle Group.[1]

## Citicorp advice to Prince Alwaleed

Carlucci was one of three Carlyle figures — alongside [[david-rubenstein|David Rubenstein]] and [[fred-malek|Frederick Malek]] — identified as the "American advisers" who in 1991 encouraged [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] to take a controlling stake in Citicorp.[1] All three had long histories with [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]].[1]
