# Gaza

Gaza is the Palestinian coastal territory at the center of the conflict between [[israel|Israel]] and [[hamas|Hamas]], and the subject of a transitional governance framework backed by US President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Post-war governance framework

In March 2026, representatives of Hamas met with members of the [[board-of-peace|Gaza Peace Board]] to discuss governance and security arrangements for the territory.[1] The board is part of a Trump-supported framework intended to establish a transitional governing structure for Gaza after the current conflict.[1] The reported meeting addressed potential security coordination and administrative control during a transition period and involved regional mediators and representatives connected to the peace board structure, though participants and outcomes were not fully disclosed.[1]

The talks formed part of broader international efforts to settle post-war governance for the territory following the Israel–Hamas war.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that Hamas was reportedly represented by [[aryeh-lightstone|Aryeh Lightstone]], and noted that after the meeting Israel opened a crossing from Gaza into Egypt that had been closed since the [[iran|Iran]] conflict began.[1] He argued the significant fact was that the meetings are no longer run by Israel but by a coalition of nations negotiating with Hamas, with Israel only one member — raising the question of what happens if Israel attempts to bail out or sabotage the process late.[1] He further proposed that anyone who contributed to the events of October 7th, including Israeli leadership, should be held accountable through a full investigation by the Board of Peace, along with allegations of false flag attacks by Israel against Arab nations.[1]

## Gaza in the June 2026 Iran talks

Gaza remained a condition of negotiation in June 2026, when [[iran|Iran]] suspended indirect talks with the United States and tied any resumption to an immediate end to Israeli military operations in Gaza and [[lebanon|Lebanon]] and a complete Israeli withdrawal from southern Lebanon.[2] Tasnim News Agency reported that Iran and allied resistance groups remained committed to retaliatory measures unless their demands regarding Gaza and Lebanon were met, with options including closure of the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and pressure on the [[bab-el-mandeb-strait|Bab el-Mandeb Strait]].[2]

## April 2024 escalation in northern Gaza

Israeli strikes intensified across Gaza on Tuesday, April 23, 2024, in some of the heaviest shelling in weeks, with residents reporting nearly 24 hours of non-stop bombardment by air and by tank shelling in the central and southern areas of the Strip.[3] The Israeli army ordered fresh evacuations in the north, warning civilians they were in a "dangerous combat zone"; army spokesperson Avichay Adraee urged residents of four zones in Beit Lahiya on Gaza's northern edge to move to two designated areas, saying the military "will work with extreme force against terrorist infrastructure and subversive elements."[3] The military later stated that it "follows international law and takes feasible precautions to mitigate civilian harm."[3] The renewed campaign came almost four months after the army announced it was drawing down troops in the north, saying [[hamas|Hamas]] no longer controlled those areas.[3]

Humanitarian conditions were dire: millions faced the possibility of starvation under what critics described as an illegal Israeli blockade, and at least 28 children under the age of 12 had starved to death in the weeks before April 23, 2024.[3] Senator [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]] proposed amendments to strip offensive military funding for Israel from the supplemental aid package, protect humanitarian operations in Gaza, and restore U.S. funding to [[unrwa|UNRWA]].[3]

## Rafah and the closure of the border crossings (May 2024)

By May 2024 the war in Gaza had left tens of thousands of Palestinians dead, and roughly a million refugees had settled in [[rafah|Rafah]] after fleeing fighting in the north.[4] The [[white-house|White House]] urged [[israel|Israel]] not to send forces into Rafah without a credible civilian evacuation plan, while President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] threatened to halt offensive weapons shipments if a full invasion proceeded.[4]

The [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]]'s seizure of the [[rafah-border-crossing|Rafah crossing]] gave Israel control of all of Gaza's border crossings for the first time in nearly twenty years — a reversal, in Badlands' framing, of the 2005–2006 withdrawal of Israeli soldiers and settlers from the territory that followed an occupation dating to 1967.[4] Commentary noted continued guerrilla activity in northern Gaza despite IDF claims those areas had been cleared, and highlighted that more than 600,000 children were among the displaced population sheltering in the south.[4]

## Ceasefire and hostage release (October 2025)

On Monday, October 13, 2025 the final 20 living hostages were released from Gaza under a ceasefire deal brokered by the United States, an event US President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] marked in [[2025-knesset-address-by-donald-trump|an address to the Knesset]] declaring "the historic dawn of the new Middle East."[5] Trump told Israeli lawmakers that [[israel|Israel]] had achieved all it could by force of arms and should now pursue peace, and said he would carry the same message to regional leaders in Egypt.[5] Badlands commentary observed that no one in Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s circle had articulated a plan for Gaza or the West Bank beyond asserting ownership, and argued that some in the Israeli government intended an ethnic cleansing of Palestine.[5]

Former Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] subsequently claimed that part of the framework had been "developed" by the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration after months of discussion with Arab partners, Israel and the Palestinian Authority — a credit claim mocked by Trump and conservative commentators.[5]

## Casualties and international reaction (October 2023)

By late October 2023, Israel's counteroffensive following the October 7 [[hamas|Hamas]] attack had produced an estimated 5,000 deaths in Gaza and set off a humanitarian crisis, according to figures cited in [[gallup|Gallup]]'s polling analysis of the period.[6] Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said it was wrong that innocent women, children and old people in Gaza were being punished for other people's crimes, and warned that continued bloodshed risked spilling far beyond the borders of the Middle East.[6]

## July 2025 relocation proposal

In July 2025 Israeli defense minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] announced plans to establish a "humanitarian city" on the ruins of Rafah in the southern Gaza Strip into which the enclave's entire population — an estimated 2.2 million people — would eventually be moved, beginning with some 600,000 displaced persons from the coastal Mawasi area.[7] Critics branded the proposed compound an internment camp, and Katz acknowledged that the scheme was designed to displace the whole Gaza population and encourage "voluntary" emigration, with residents barred from returning to other parts of the strip.[7] Badlands commentary noted the geographic absurdity of the plan, observing that Gaza measures roughly five miles by twenty-five miles and that the defense ministry was effectively arguing this was too large an area for the Israeli military to secure.[7] Ceasefire and hostage release negotiations over Gaza dominated [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s two July White House meetings with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], which ended without any announced breakthrough.[7] See [[humanitarian-city-plan|Humanitarian City Plan]].[7]

## Arab world backlash over US support (November 2023)

In November 2023 American diplomats warned the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] that strong U.S. support for [[israel|Israel]]'s destructive and deadly military campaign in Gaza "is losing us Arab publics for a generation," according to a diplomatic cable obtained by CNN.[8] The cable, from the US Embassy in [[oman|Oman]], said "we are losing badly on the messaging battlespace" and reported that robust US backing for Israel's actions was seen "as material and moral culpability in what they consider to be possible war crimes."[8] The anger followed Israel's operations against [[hamas|Hamas]] after the October 7 attacks that left over 1,400 Israelis dead.[8]

## January 2024 troop drawdown

On January 1, 2024, the Israeli military said it would begin withdrawing several thousand troops from the Gaza Strip, at least temporarily, citing the mounting toll on the Israeli economy of nearly three months of mobilization.[9] More than 20,000 people had been killed in Gaza since the war began, most of them civilians according to local health authorities, primarily in Israeli bombing, and with aid delivery restricted the [[united-nations|United Nations]] assessed that half of Gaza's roughly 2.2 million people were at risk of starvation.[9] Rear Admiral [[daniel-hagari|Daniel Hagari]] stressed that the [[israeli-troop-withdrawal-from-gaza-2024|drawdown]] did not signal any compromise in [[israel|Israel]]'s intention to destroy [[hamas|Hamas]], and that fighting across the territory remained intense.[9]

## Trump ownership proposal (February 2025)

On Tuesday, February 4, 2025, President Donald Trump suggested that displaced Palestinians in Gaza be permanently resettled outside the war-torn territory and proposed that the United States take "ownership" in redeveloping the area, declining to rule out deploying U.S. troops to support reconstruction and describing a potential long-term American stake.[10] The proposal came as talks ramped up to extend the tenuous ceasefire between Israel and Hamas and to secure the release of remaining hostages, amid promises of surging humanitarian aid and reconstruction supplies after more than fifteen months of devastating conflict.[10] Roughly 1.8 million people would be pushed to leave the land they had called home under the plan.[10] Saudi Arabia rejected the displacement of Gazans within hours, restating that relations with Israel require a Palestinian state on the 1967 borders.[10] Badlands commentary on the proposal is collected at [[gaza-relocation-proposal-2025|the Gaza relocation proposal]].[10]

## UN-authorized stabilization force (November 2025)

On November 17, 2025, the [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]] approved a U.S.-led resolution establishing an [[international-stabilization-force|international stabilization force]] (ISF) to deploy to Gaza for at least two years, endorsing the [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]] that will temporarily administer the enclave during the transition period.[11] Thirteen Council members supported the measure; Russia and China abstained and none opposed.[11] The resolution gave the United States and participating countries a broad mandate to govern Gaza, demilitarize it, and begin reconstruction, with deployment sought as early as January 2026 and troops drawn from several Arab and Muslim countries.[11]

The ISF is to operate under unified command acceptable to the Board of Peace, to train and work alongside a "newly trained and vetted Palestinian police force," and to coordinate closely with [[israel|Israel]] and Egypt.[11] Its mission includes securing border areas, stabilizing the security environment, and facilitating humanitarian aid, using "all necessary measures to carry out its mandate consistent with international law, including international humanitarian law."[11] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that bringing Turkish — and therefore [[nato|NATO]] — soldiers into Gaza means Palestinians would effectively be shielded by Article 5, since an IDF attack on ISF troops would put Israel at war with the United States and the rest of NATO.[11]

## Trump peace plan, September 2025

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] unveiled a 20-point plan to end the war in Gaza at a White House press conference with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] on 29 September 2025, saying Israel had accepted the proposal and that Arab and Muslim states had committed to disarming [[hamas|Hamas]].[12] The plan provided for safe passage out of Gaza for Hamas leaders if the 48 remaining hostages were freed within 48 hours of Israel's public acceptance, the release by Israel of hundreds of Palestinian prisoners serving life terms and more than 1,000 Gazans detained since the war began, the return of several hundred Palestinian bodies, amnesty for Hamas members committing to peaceful coexistence, safe passage to third countries for those choosing to leave, and immediate humanitarian aid deliveries.[12] Egyptian and Qatari mediators presented the proposal to Hamas negotiators that night.[12]

## Starvation and US pressure on Israel (May 2025)

In May 2025, as [[israel|Israel]] intensified its offensive to take "total control" of the territory, reporting described mass starvation in the enclave and warned that allies including the United States might withdraw their support.[13] The Washington Post reported that the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] had told [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s government the US would "abandon" Israel if it did not end the war, an account a US official denied.[13] White House Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] said "the president wants the war in Gaza to end," and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], speaking during a Middle East tour of Arab states, said "a lot of people are starving" in Gaza, adding, "People are starving. Terrible things are happening there."[13]

## Humanitarian ceasefires and the starvation dispute (July 2025)

The [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] announced "localized humanitarian ceasefires" in Gaza early on Sunday, July 27, 2025, following a decision by Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] taken without the knowledge of Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]], triggering a coalition crisis in [[israel|Israel]].[14] The following day, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said there was "real starvation" in Gaza, contradicting Netanyahu's claim that such assertions were a "bold-faced lie" and remarking that "those children look very hungry."[14] The UN's humanitarian chief said "vast amounts" of food were needed to stave off starvation.[14] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that "all assets are being deployed" to make the American public complicit in what he called a potential war crime, and welcomed Trump's break with those denying hunger in the territory.[14]
