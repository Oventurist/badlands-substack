# Gaza

Gaza, or the Gaza Strip, is the Palestinian coastal territory governed by [[hamas|Hamas]] and the theatre of the war that followed the [[october-7-attacks|October 7, 2023 attacks]] on southern [[israel|Israel]].

The February 11, 2026 Badlands Brief describes Gaza as a live point of friction ahead of Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s Washington visit. Phase 2 of the October 2025 U.S.-brokered ceasefire — requiring Hamas disarmament and Israeli troop withdrawal — was reported to be stalled because of Hamas's refusal to give up its weapons, with Israel arguing that a renewed military operation is required to enforce demilitarisation in line with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Gaza vision. Incidents continued alongside IDF strikes on suspected ceasefire violators.

Badlands contributor [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] adds that Netanyahu intended to tell Trump that the [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]]'s Phase Two plan to take control of Gaza will not happen, and that the United States should instead move immediately toward war with [[iran|Iran]]. The same commentary alleges that funds solicited from [[qatar|Qatar]] on humanitarian grounds were walked into Gaza and distributed to families with known ties to Hamas.


## April 2026: Smotrich's reoccupation calls

The Brief of April 20, 2026 reports that Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Smotrich]] renewed calls for Israel to occupy the Gaza Strip and establish settlements there, saying the war should conclude with territorial expansion — going against [[donald-trump|Trump]] and the [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]]. The Brief also cites the trauma of [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] reservists who witnessed abuses against Palestinian civilians in Gaza and the [[west-bank|West Bank]].

## American opinion polling on the genocide question (July 2026)

The July 8, 2026 Badlands Brief cites [[times-of-israel|Times of Israel]] polling according to which one third of all American Jews say that [[israel|Israel]] committed a genocide in Gaza, and only 20% of all Americans say Israel did not commit a genocide and that the "complete obliteration of the Gazan civilization" was morally justified. Commentator [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argues the polling's accuracy is irrelevant — "you can sense it in the air that the winds have changed" — and that Israel has become widely recognized in the U.S. as a liability, making the war an existential crisis for the Israeli political elite.
