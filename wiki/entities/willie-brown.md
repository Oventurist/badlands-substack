# Willie Brown

Willie Brown is a California Democratic politician, longtime Speaker of the state Assembly and later Mayor of San Francisco, described in Badlands Media coverage as a Bay Area political boss.[1]

Brown appears in Badlands commentary chiefly through his early patronage of [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]. Contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], writing sarcastically about Democratic officials souring on Harris, characterized her career as having begun by "riding the coattails" of Brown before moving on to her tenure as California Attorney General.[1]

## Patronage of Gavin Newsom

Brown played a comparable role in launching the career of [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]].[2] During the mid-1990s, then-mayor Brown recognized Newsom's considerable social network and sought to gain favor with the [[getty-family|Getty family]], to whom Newsom's father [[william-newsom|William Newsom, Jr.]] served as financial consigliere.[2] Seeing an opportunity, Brown appointed Newsom to a local traffic commission and subsequently filled a vacancy on the city's board of supervisors with him.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described the elevation as a no-brainer for Brown: in Newsom he had a youthful moderate with unfettered access to San Francisco's donor class and seemingly boundless ambition, whose early political career benefited from rubbing elbows with Pacific Heights socialites.[2]

## Patronage of Kamala Harris

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] traced [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s entry into California politics to a 1994 relationship with Brown, then Speaker of the California State Assembly and considered the second-most-powerful man in the state, wielding immense control over statewide legislative affairs and political appointments.[3] Brown, a married man of 60, began dating the 29-year-old Harris; her mother defended the relationship by remarking, "Why shouldn't she have gone out with Willie Brown? He was a player. And what could Willie Brown expect from her in the future? He has not much life left."[3]

In addition to running the State Assembly, Brown maintained a legal practice on the side, which the article characterizes as a conduit for lobbyists and companies seeking favorable treatment in Sacramento.[3] He was investigated several times over his career by the State Bar of California, the Fair Political Practices Commission, and the Federal Bureau of Investigation, and in 1986 was found to have "received at least $124,000 in income and gifts … from special interests that had business before the legislature."[3]

Brown boosted Harris's career and put money in her pocket, appointing her to the State Unemployment Insurance Appeals Board and the California Medical Assistance Commission — well-paid posts that required no legislative confirmation and were worth around six figures a year as of 2002.[3] More important was access to his network of political supporters, donors and sponsors.[3] Harris appeared regularly at his side during his 1995 campaign for Mayor of San Francisco; speculation that he would divorce his wife and marry her came to nothing, and the two broke up soon after his election, with most accounts holding that Brown ended it.[3] They nonetheless remained closely affiliated, and having the mayor in her support system proved tremendously beneficial.[3]

## The 2003 District Attorney race

When District Attorney [[terence-hallinan|Terence Hallinan]] passed Harris over for the number two position in his office, Brown shifted from qualified supporter of Hallinan to public critic, attacking him for "not doing his job"; a Brown insider told the San Francisco Chronicle, "This whole thing was about Kamala Harris … cross one of Willie's friends and there will be hell to pay."[3] The Brown political machine then geared up to run Harris against Hallinan, staffing her campaign with proven operatives including former Brown aide Rebecca Prozan as campaign manager, Democratic mega-donor Mark Buell as finance chair, and Philip Mueller working for Harris on Brown's behalf.[3]

Brown made the maximum personal contribution of $500 and sent Mueller to the San Francisco elite to solicit maximum contributions, producing a flow of cash unlike any previous District Attorney race and allowing Harris to more than double Hallinan's fundraising.[3] Brown confidant [[mohammed-nuru|Mohammed Nuru]], deputy director of operations at the Department of Public Works, ran the San Francisco League of Urban Gardeners, which internal reports described as a machine resource whose workers were dispatched on the public's dime to fill sparse crowds at rallies for Harris and [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]].[3]
