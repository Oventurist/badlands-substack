# Alan Greenspan

Alan Greenspan was the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] chairman whom [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes as a "former JP Morgan tool" and as the popularizer of the derivatives instruments that set the modern financial time bomb.[1]

## Response to the 1987 crash

In September 1987 a twenty-year foray into speculation culminated in a 23% collapse of the Dow Jones on October 19, 1987.[1] Within hours of the crash, international emergency meetings were convened, at which Greenspan introduced a "solution" that Ehret argues carried the future echoes of hyperinflation and fascism.[1]

## Derivatives

"Creative financial instruments" was the name given to the new financial asset Greenspan popularized, otherwise known as [[derivatives|derivatives]].[1] New supercomputing technologies — whose NASA origins had been intended to support nation building and space exploration — were instead used to create complex formulas assigning values to price differentials on securities and insured debts that could be hedged on the spot and futures markets made possible by the destruction of the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]] in 1971.[1] Ehret argues this produced an exponentially self-generating monster that could end nowhere but in a meltdown, even as "market confidence" rallied on the new flux of easy money while the physical potential to sustain human life continued to plummet.[1]

## Trilateral Commission ties

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 2" records that Greenspan replaced [[paul-volcker|Paul Volcker]] as chairman of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] during the [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]] administration, and that he was, like Volcker, a member of the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]], the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] and the [[bilderberg-group|Bilderberg group]].[2] The article presents this continuity of membership at the top of the central bank as one reason so many people believed U.S. policy was being directed by Rockefeller-dominated organizations.[2]

## Writings on the gold standard

Badlands Media's "The Midas Touch" quotes Greenspan's argument that "under the gold standard, a free banking system stands as the protector of an economy's stability and balanced growth," and that abandoning the [[gold-standard|gold standard]] "made it possible for the welfare statists to use the banking system as a means to an unlimited expansion of credit."[3] The passage concludes: "In the absence of the gold standard, there is no way to protect savings from confiscation through inflation."[3]

Contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] uses the quotation to argue that inflation discourages saving because deposit interest falls far below the inflation rate, and that rising living costs have driven an explosion in credit card debt — conditions he says can only be reversed by restoring gold-backed money and curbing the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[3]
