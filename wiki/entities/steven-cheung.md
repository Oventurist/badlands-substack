# Steven Cheung

Steven Cheung is a spokesman for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s presidential campaign.[1]

In September 2024 Cheung disclosed that Trump had been briefed by the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] "regarding real and specific threats from [[iran|Iran]] to assassinate him in an effort to destabilize and sow chaos in the United States."[1] He added that intelligence officials had identified continued and coordinated attacks that had heightened over the preceding months, and that law enforcement officials across all agencies were working to ensure Trump's protection and that the election remained free from interference.[1] An ODNI spokesperson confirmed a briefing had occurred but declined to address specifics.[1]

## Response to Woodward's *War* (October 2024)

Cheung responded to Axios's reporting on [[bob-woodward|Bob Woodward]]'s book *War* in October 2024 by calling its claims — including that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had held as many as seven calls with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] since leaving office and had sent him scarce COVID-19 tests — "made up stories," and saying Trump gave Woodward "absolutely no access for this trash book."[2] He added that "Woodward is a total sleazebag who has lost it mentally, and he's slow, lethargic, incompetent and overall a boring person with no personality."[2] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that the statement was "obviously written by The Donald."[2]

## Response to the Epstein document release (January 2024)

Following the [[epstein-court-document-unsealing|unsealing of court documents]] in the settled [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] civil case in early January 2024, Cheung, then spokesperson for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], responded to the former president being named by saying that any claims regarding Trump's relationship with Epstein were "thoroughly debunked."[3]

## Response to the 2024 Ukraine plan reporting

After Reuters reported in June 2024 that advisers [[keith-kellogg|Keith Kellogg]] and [[fred-fleitz|Fred Fleitz]] had presented [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with a plan to condition US military aid to Kyiv on peace talks with Moscow, Trump spokesperson Steven Cheung said only statements made by Trump or authorized members of his campaign should be deemed official.[4]
