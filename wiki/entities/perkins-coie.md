# Perkins Coie

Perkins Coie is an American law firm long associated with Democratic Party political and election work.[1]

The firm is best known in the context of election litigation as the former home of attorney [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]], who was a partner there before founding the [[elias-law-group|Elias Law Group]].[1] Elias's departure carried with it the bulk of major Democratic institutional clients, and his subsequent [[democracy-docket|Democracy Docket]] voting-rights litigation program grew out of the practice he built.[1] Reporting on the April 2023 split between Elias and the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]] identified his Perkins Coie background as central to his standing in Democratic legal circles.[1]

## Colorado Secretary of State appointment (2025)

In 2021 attorney [[andrew-kline|Andrew Kline]] joined Perkins Coie full time, remaining with the firm until March 2025, when according to Badlands reporting he was secretly appointed to run Colorado Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]]'s office without any public announcement.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the move while covering President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s demands for the release of [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]], treating the placement of a Perkins Coie lawyer atop the office that prosecuted Peters and sought to remove Trump from the Colorado ballot as evidence of "the whole corrupt temple."[2]

## Trump executive order and the Howell injunction (2025)

On March 6, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] issued an executive order asserting that "the dishonest and dangerous activity of…Perkins Coie has affected this country for decades," citing the firm's 2016 representation of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], during which it hired [[fusion-gps|Fusion GPS]], "which then manufactured a false 'dossier' designed to steal an election."[3] The order further alleged that Perkins Coie had "worked with activist donors including [[george-soros|George Soros]] to judicially overturn popular, necessary, and democratically enacted election laws," and accused the firm of racial discrimination by reference to its publicly announced percentage quotas in 2019 for hiring and promotion on the basis of race and other protected categories.[3]

The order suspended security clearances for the firm's lawyers, barred them from federal buildings, prohibited the government from engaging the firm, directed federal contractors to disclose whether they used the firm's services, and referred the firm for investigation over civil rights violations; it was one of several similar orders issued or contemplated against leading law firms.[3]

Federal District Court Judge [[beryl-howell|Beryl Howell]] enjoined implementation of the order.[3] Commentary in Chronicles Magazine, excerpted by [[badlands-media|Badlands]], argued that Howell's injunction "spoils a righteous core with judicial activism": while she was right that the First Amendment and principles of American justice require that lawyers be able to deliver candid advice and zealous advocacy, she went too far in ignoring the case that Perkins Coie conspired with Clinton and Fusion GPS to improperly influence the 2016 election and destabilize the Trump presidency through the fraudulent [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]], and then misled government investigators about its provenance.[3]
