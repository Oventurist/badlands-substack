# Asaad Al Shaibani

Asaad Al Shaibani is the Foreign Minister of [[syria|Syria]].[1]

## August 2025 warning to Israel

Speaking from Ankara on August 13, 2025 alongside his Turkish counterpart, Al Shaibani joined [[turkey|Turkey]] in warning [[israel|Israel]] not to stir up chaos in Syria and demanded an end to all external interventions aimed at destabilising the war-torn country.[1]

"We are also confronting multiple foreign interventions, both direct and indirect... (that) push the country toward sectarian and regional strife," he said, declining to give details but warning against "any reckless attempts to exploit events here."[1]

Al Shaibani also said Syria is committed to holding accountable those responsible for violations in the recent deadly violence that gripped the southwestern [[druze|Druze]]-majority province of Sweida, and reiterated Damascus's assurance that the Druze community is part of Syria and that their protection is the responsibility of the state.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the statements as confirmation of an anticipated Turkey–Israel confrontation in Syria, predicting that [[russia|Russia]] would intervene on Turkey's behalf and speculating about a return of Bashar al-Assad.[1]

## Moscow visit (July 2025)

Speaking alongside Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] in Moscow on Thursday, July 31, 2025, al-Shaibani said Syria was seeking Russia's support in restoring national unity after continued outbreaks of sectarian violence.[2] He acknowledged that Syria faces "challenges and threats" but described the moment as "an opportunity to build a united, strong Syria."[2] "Relations between the Russian and Syrian peoples are of a historical nature," he said, as cited by TASS, adding: "Today we are representing a new Syria... We are currently working to ensure the reunification of the people in Syria. And, of course, we are interested in Russia being there with us on this path."[2]

The visit came against the backdrop of a volatile post-Assad landscape: Bashar al-Assad, a close ally of Moscow, was ousted late the previous year by a coalition led by the Islamist group Hayat Tahrir al-Sham and replaced by HTS leader [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]], while Assad and his family received political asylum in Moscow and Russia pledged continued support for Damascus.[2] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that the Syrian government had approached Turkey the previous week for "security assistance" against an IDF incursion in southern Syria and was now turning to Russia for help with domestic unity, arguing that the two powers long expected to arbitrate Syria's future were both being summoned to do so.[2]
