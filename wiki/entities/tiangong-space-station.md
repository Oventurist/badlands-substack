# Tiangong Space Station

Tiangong is [[china|China]]'s crewed space station, operated by the [[china-manned-space-agency|China Manned Space Agency]] and staffed by rotating crews flown to and from orbit aboard Shenzhou spacecraft.[1]

## Crew rotation model

Crews serve roughly six-month rotations aboard the station, with each Shenzhou mission delivering a new crew and leaving its capsule docked as that crew's dedicated return vehicle.[1] The Shenzhou-21 mission followed this pattern, bringing replacements to the station for the Shenzhou-20 crew.[1]

## 2025 return-vehicle crisis

In November 2025 the station became the site of an unplanned crisis when the Shenzhou-20 spacecraft was damaged by suspected space debris, delaying its crew's return by more than a week.[1] The outgoing crew flew home aboard the newly arrived Shenzhou-21 capsule instead, leaving three astronauts aboard Tiangong with no dedicated means of returning to Earth pending the launch of a replacement Shenzhou-22 vehicle.[1] The episode is covered in detail at [[2025-shenzhou-space-station-stranding|the 2025 Shenzhou space station stranding]].[1]

## Origins and strategic significance

Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes Tiangong — rendered as "the Heavenly Kingdom" and also as "Harmony of the Heavens" — as the outpost that will soon leave China as the owner of the only functional international space station, once the [[international-space-station|ISS]] is retired without replacement.[2] He traces its existence to the 2011 [[wolf-act|Wolf Act]], which banned Chinese participation in space science with [[nasa|NASA]] and forced China to rely on its own creative powers, and to the founding work of [[qian-xuesen|Qian Xuesen]] under Premier [[zhou-enlai|Zhou Enlai]].[2] The station is presented as one element of a Chinese space advance that also produced the first far-side lunar landing in January 2019 and the [[chang-e-6|Chang'e-6]] far-side sample return of June 2024.[2]
