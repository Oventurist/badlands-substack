# Catherine Colonna

Catherine Colonna is a French diplomat who served as France's foreign minister and who appeared in Badlands Media coverage in connection with European efforts to sustain support for Ukraine as United States funding stalled in the autumn of 2023.[1]

## Kiev Foreign Affairs Council, October 2023

Colonna took part in the October 2, 2023 meeting of [[european-union|EU]] foreign ministers held in Kiev, an event billed by the bloc as "historic" because the Foreign Affairs Council was convening for the first time outside the Union's borders.[1] The meeting occurred in the twentieth month of the war and immediately after President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] signed a stopgap funding bill that excluded aid for Ukraine, Congressional Republicans having blocked its inclusion.[1]

Colonna framed the gathering as a message to Moscow that Europe was prepared to stand by Ukraine over the long term even as American support looked set to wane or halt altogether for the first time, stating in a press statement that the meeting was "a demonstration of our resolute and lasting support for Ukraine, until it can win."[1] Her remarks accompanied those of EU foreign policy chief [[josep-borrell|Josep Borrell]], who described Ukraine as a candidate country and future member of the Union while cautioning that the session was not designed to produce concrete decisions, and of Ukrainian foreign minister [[dmytro-kuleba|Dmytro Kuleba]], who praised the EU at a moment of visible disappointment with Washington.[1]

## Badlands Framing

Badlands commentary treated the European pledge less as a rebuke of Washington than as evidence of a deliberate American pivot, with contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] arguing that the absence of Ukraine funding from the stopgap represented a return to America First priorities and that "any day the EU pays in our stead is a good day."[1] In that reading, statements such as Colonna's marked the point at which the financial burden of the conflict began shifting from the United States to the [[european-union|European Union]].[1]
