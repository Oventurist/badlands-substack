# MoveOn.org

MoveOn.org is an American progressive advocacy organization and political action committee that Badlands Media identifies as one of the core nodes of the private-organization network it calls the [[george-soros|Soros]] "shadow network" — a cluster of nonprofits, PACs and foundations described as exercising hidden but decisive influence over the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic Party]] apparatus.[1] Alongside the [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]], [[black-lives-matter|Black Lives Matter]], [[actblue|ActBlue]] and the [[gill-foundation|Gill Foundation]], MoveOn is presented as part of an amalgamation of activists and billionaires holding de facto control over the party's campaign machinery.[1]

## Role in the network

Badlands argues that MoveOn's significance lies less in its membership base than in its funders and operatives, tracing the organization back to "billionaire globalist and messianic financier" George Soros.[1] The article observes that MoveOn "keeps popping up" wherever progressive organizing infrastructure is examined, and treats that recurrence as evidence of a single coordinating hand rather than coincidence.[1]

The 2004 election cycle is cited as the moment the network's pervasiveness became evident: a cadre of leftist billionaires contributed over $300 million to the Democratic war chest that year, using heavy donations and independent media campaigns to shape the narrative and cultivate a new generation of voters reacting against the George W. Bush administration.[1] Democrats lost at the polls, but by the account given the machine itself had grown enormously.[1]

On 9 December 2004, [[eli-pariser|Eli Pariser]], who headed MoveOn PAC, told members: "Now it's our party. We bought it, we own it."[1] Badlands treats this quotation as the network's clearest self-description, and notes that Pariser is a Soros confidant.[1]

## Personnel

[[zack-exley|Zack Exley]] served as organizing director at MoveOn.org, and while there was, per the article, caught up in a rigging scandal favouring Howard Dean during the Dean campaign.[1] Exley later co-founded the [[justice-democrats|Justice Democrats]] PAC with Cenk Uygur of The Young Turks and Kyle Kulinski of Secular Talk.[1]

## Assessment

The article's framing is that organizations such as MoveOn function as the recruitment and messaging layer of a broader "playbook": establish foundations and non-profits, staff them with ideologically activated personnel, then use them to place believers into government positions, fund campaigns and purchase influence in media.[1] Readers should note that these are the interpretations of a Badlands Media contributor rather than established findings; the underlying quotations and donation figures are sourced, but the coordinating-network thesis is contested.

## Role in the post-2024 "Resistance 2.0" coalition

MoveOn, described as having started as an emailed petition in 1998, was named as one of the sponsor organizations that joined with the [[working-families-party|Working Families Party]], the [[indivisible|Indivisible Project]], Public Citizen and the [[american-civil-liberties-union|American Civil Liberties Union]] ahead of the 2024 election to organize opposition to a second [[donald-trump|Trump]] term.[2] Organizers said the coalition aligned more than 100 groups along with Representative [[pramila-jayapal|Pramila Jayapal]], and set eight roughly weekly "Mass Calls" starting November 7, 2024.[2] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterized the effort — dubbed [[resistance-2-0|"Resistance 2.0"]] — as a domestic [[color-revolution|color revolution]] run by NGO world against MAGA.[2]
