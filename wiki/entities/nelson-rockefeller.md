# Nelson Rockefeller

Nelson Rockefeller was an American politician and member of the Rockefeller family who was nominated for vice president by President [[gerald-ford|Gerald Ford]] in 1974.[1]

## Sanders' open letter opposing the nomination

In 1974, while running for the United States Senate on the [[liberty-union-party|Liberty Union]] ticket in Vermont, [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]] sent an open letter to President Ford warning of a "virtual Rockefeller family dictatorship over the nation" if Rockefeller were named vice president.[1] In the same letter Sanders called for the [[central-intelligence-agency|Central Intelligence Agency]] to be disbanded immediately, in the wake of revelations about the agency's misdeeds.[1] A 2023 Badlands Media essay by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] singled out these two positions as among the most defensible things Sanders had ever said, contrasting the young Sanders' apocalyptic and conspiratorial rhetoric with what the author characterized as his later accommodation to establishment power.[1]

## Career

Nelson Aldrich Rockefeller was a son of [[john-d-rockefeller-jr|John D. Rockefeller Jr.]] who carved out a career in politics.[2] Before the Second World War he travelled to Venezuela, where he encountered both South American culture and the lucrative oil business; his knowledge of the region led fellow New Yorker President [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin D. Roosevelt]] to appoint him coordinator for inter-American affairs, launching his government vocation.[2] He later served four terms as governor of New York State after holding various posts in the family oil and banking businesses.[2]

When the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare was created in 1953, Rockefeller was named undersecretary on the recommendation of Oveta Culp Hobby, and from that post he pushed through a litany of social programs according to author Jim Marrs.[2] President [[dwight-eisenhower|Eisenhower]] subsequently appointed him special assistant for foreign affairs, a post later held by his friend [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] during the Nixon era.[2]

## Vice presidency and death

Rockefeller made several unsuccessful attempts to secure the presidency before reaching the White House in 1974 as vice president to [[gerald-ford|Gerald R. Ford]].[2] "The man who could not be president" died at age 70 in 1979 under controversial circumstances involving a young female staff assistant.[2] He is also the source of an anecdote about his grandfather [[john-d-rockefeller|John D. Rockefeller]] being taught by his own father never to trust anyone completely.[2]
