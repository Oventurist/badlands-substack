# Letitia James

Letitia James is the Attorney General of the State of New York. In January 2026 a federal judge invalidated subpoenas that had been issued to her by the office of US Attorney [[john-sarcone|John Sarcone III]] for the Northern District of New York, after finding that Sarcone had been serving unlawfully.[1]

## The Sarcone ruling

US District Judge [[lorna-schofield|Lorna Schofield]], an Obama appointee, disqualified Sarcone in a 24-page opinion and tossed the subpoenas issued to James, writing that when the executive branch "skirts restraints put in place by Congress and then uses that power to subject political adversaries to criminal investigations, it acts without lawful authority."[1]

## Related prosecutions

The ruling followed a series of court decisions rejecting the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s efforts to install US attorneys without Senate confirmation.[1] In Virginia, the dismissal of [[lindsey-halligan|Lindsey Halligan]] led to the invalidation of indictments against both James and former FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]]; subsequent attempts to re-indict James in Virginia failed.[1]

## FHFA insurance referral (March 2026)

In March 2026 [[federal-housing-finance-agency|Federal Housing Finance Agency]] Director [[william-pulte|William Pulte]] again referred James to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] for potential criminal prosecution, alleging that she may have provided false information on a homeowner's insurance application.[2] Pulte sent letters to US Attorneys in Florida and Illinois asking them to "authenticate and investigate" the claims, citing analysis by attorney Mike Davis, who pointed to court filings he said showed discrepancies in James' insurance representations.[2]

According to the letters, one application submitted to a Florida-based insurer indicated that James' Norfolk, Virginia property would remain unoccupied for five months each year, while testimony referenced in the referral stated that her niece, Nakia Thompson, had lived in the home for several years.[2] A separate filing cited in the Illinois referral stated the home would be occupied by a single adult with no children, whereas public reporting cited by Pulte said the property was occupied by the niece and three children, prompting the request for further federal review.[2]

## March 2026: Trump demand for Cohen communications

In March 2026, [[donald-trump|President Trump]] demanded that James turn over all communications between her office and his former attorney [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]], after Cohen claimed he had been pressured to provide damaging statements about Trump.[3] Cohen said he was coerced into making statements during investigations tied to the New York civil fraud case brought by the attorney general's office, and Trump's team argued the claim raised questions about the conduct of the prosecutors and investigators involved.[3] The demand came as part of Trump's continuing effort to challenge the New York civil fraud judgment entered against him and his business during the Biden administration.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the episode as New York reaping the consequences of partnering with Cohen to "get Trump" — "when you lie down with dogs, you wake up with fleas" — while floating an alternative reading in which Cohen functioned throughout as an inside operative documenting a RICO-style meeting of the minds.[3]

## October 2025 indictment and the Mamdani rally

In October 2025 James was indicted on federal charges of bank fraud and making a false statement, following an extended public conflict with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[4] Days later she made her first public appearance since the indictment at a Manhattan rally billed as the closing argument of [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]]'s New York City mayoral campaign, where the two presented themselves as a united front against Trump and argued that he was pursuing political retribution.[4] Speaking defiantly and raising her fist, James told the crowd, "You come for me, you've got to come for all of us, all of us, every single one of us — we're all in this together."[4]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called it a "bold move to double down on insurrectionist behavior" while under fraud indictment, faulted James for allying with an avowed socialist, and argued that abolition of private property as government policy would violate Fifth and Fourteenth Amendment protections.[4] The commentary held that James, having sworn an oath to defend the [[us-constitution|Constitution]], had accumulated a thick record of violations moving her toward "sedition and treason territory."[4]

## 2024 civil fraud judgment pause

On March 25, 2024, a mid-level New York state appeals court paused the $454 million civil fraud judgment James's office had obtained against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], allowing him to post a $175 million bond within ten days and blocking her office from beginning to seize his assets.[5] Her office said in response that Trump was "still facing accountability for his staggering fraud."[5] Badlands commentary treated the ruling as a humiliating reversal for James and for expectations that Trump's properties would be seized.[5]

## Civil fraud trial opening (October 2023)

The civil fraud case James brought in September 2022 against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], his eldest sons, their companies and several executives was scheduled to begin at 10 a.m. on Monday, October 2, 2023, in Lower Manhattan.[6] Trump announced the night before that he would attend in person, posting on Truth Social that he was going "to fight for my name and reputation," and law enforcement and court staff had already prepared security for his appearance.[6]

## August 2023 motion for pre-trial liability finding

On August 30, 2023, James asked a state judge to declare — before the start of the [[new-york-civil-fraud-trial|New York civil fraud trial]] — that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had committed fraud by submitting false statements to bankers and insurers and overstating his net worth by up to $2.23 billion.[7] In filings in a Manhattan state court she said the evidence showed "repeated and persistent fraudulent use" by Trump and his family business of false and misleading financial statements between 2011 and 2021, and that inflating the values of more than a dozen assets by hundreds of millions of dollars established liability for fraud under the state's Executive Law.[7] James wrote that "the cumulative effect of these numerous deceptive schemes to inflate Mr. Trump's assets, and hence his net worth, is staggering," and was "just the tip of a much larger iceberg of deception" the state was prepared to expose at trial.[7]

Within an hour, Trump's lawyers filed papers seeking to dismiss the entire case and end what they called James' "crusade against President Trump," arguing that many challenged transactions were too old to be reviewed under a recent appellate ruling and that no proof of harm had been offered for the rest.[7] Badlands writer [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] called the request for a pre-trial liability finding unusual and "a big red flag," arguing it precluded the defendant from arguing before the court and amounted to trying Trump in the court of public opinion.[7]

## Trump's $175 million bond (April 2024)

On April 1, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted a $175 million bond in the New York civil fraud case, halting collection of the more than $454 million judgment and preventing James's office from seizing his assets while he appeals.[8] The bond followed an appellate panel's decision the previous month to slash the amount required to stop enforcement, and it foreclosed James's plans, reported as imminent, to begin collecting the judgment by seizing some of Trump's marquee properties.[8] James, a Democrat, had brought the lawsuit on the state's behalf.[8]

## Asset seizure moves against Trump (March 2024)

In March 2024, James took her first concrete step toward seizing the assets of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in connection with the more than $450 million civil fraud judgment against him.[9] Her office filed judgments with the Westchester County Clerk's Office on March 6, one week after Judge [[arthur-engoron|Arthur Engoron]] ordered Trump to pay the fine and well before Trump's deadline to satisfy the judgment, in a move indicating the state was preparing to seize Seven Springs, Trump's golf course and private estate.[9] Filing judgments is the first step a creditor takes to recover property; subsequent steps could include placing liens on assets or attempting to force a foreclosure.[9] Trump had offered the previous month to post a $100 million appeal bond rather than the full $464 million required to appeal, but Engoron denied the proposal.[9]

Badlands contributor [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] described the seizure effort as "the very definition of 'Lawfare,'" arguing that Trump broke no laws and that the goal was to drain his personal funds ahead of the 2024 election, and characterized the action as "pure panic" on the part of his opponents.[9]

## NFL workplace discrimination investigation (2023)

On May 4, 2023, James and California Attorney General [[rob-bonta|Rob Bonta]] announced a joint investigation into allegations of workplace discrimination at the [[national-football-league|National Football League]], citing employee lawsuits describing sex, racial and age bias, sexual harassment and a hostile work environment.[10] The two attorneys general issued subpoenas to NFL executives as part of an examination of workplace culture at the league's corporate offices in New York and California, seeking information on gender pay disparities, harassment, and gender and racial discrimination.[10] The inquiry targeted the league's corporate operations rather than individual teams or players.[10]

## October 2025 prosecution debate

In October 2025 an essay in [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] contrasted the [[john-bolton|John Bolton]] indictment with the cases against James and former FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]], calling the latter two so weak that a U.S. attorney — [[erik-siebert|Erik Siebert]] — resigned rather than present them to a grand jury, after which career prosecutors told his replacement the government would probably lose at trial.[11] Reuters had earlier reported that the Trump administration threatened to fire the U.S. prosecutor overseeing the Letitia James case.[11]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the distinction, describing the counts against James and Comey as "starter charges" and predicting superseding indictments adding new charges and new defendants as the administration developed evidence of criminal conduct.[11] She also questioned why The Atlantic appeared to be rewriting the Siebert story relative to its original headline treatment.[11]

## Dismissal and appeal (November 2025)

James, who had been indicted on charges including bank fraud and making false statements to a financial institution concerning information on mortgage applications, saw her case thrown out in November 2025 when U.S. District Judge [[cameron-mcgowan-currie|Cameron McGowan Currie]] ruled that prosecutor [[lindsey-halligan|Lindsey Halligan]] had been illegally appointed.[12] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said she and [[james-comey|James Comey]] "got out on a technicality" and that a fair reading of the charges would conclude "boy, are they guilty," while noting the court had not barred re-bringing or appealing the case.[12] The administration announced on November 25, 2025 that the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] would appeal the dismissals.[12]

## Trump appeal of the civil fraud judgment

In February 2024, after Judge [[arthur-engoron|Arthur Engoron]] entered a $355 million judgment against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the case James brought, Trump's principal lawyer [[chris-kise|Chris Kise]] announced a challenge to Engoron's definition of fraud and accused James and the judge of trying to run Trump out of New York, calling the campaign bad for the city.[13]

## Threat to seize Trump assets (February 2024)

On February 20, 2024, James said she was prepared to seize former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s buildings and assets if he could not pay the nearly $355 million penalty imposed in the state's [[new-york-civil-fraud-trial|civil fraud case]], in which Justice [[arthur-engoron|Arthur Engoron]] also barred him from doing business in New York for three years.[14] In an ABC News interview she said it was "really not my business" whether Trump had the funds, adding, "If he does not have funds to pay off the judgment, then we will seek, you know, judgment enforcement mechanisms in court, and we will ask the judge to seize his assets."[14] She singled out the Trump Building at 40 Wall Street in Lower Manhattan, saying, "We are prepared to make sure that the judgment is paid to New Yorkers, and yes, I look at 40 Wall Street each and every day."[14]
