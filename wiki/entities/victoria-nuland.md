# Victoria Nuland

Victoria Nuland is a U.S. diplomat identified in "A Bright Red Line" as one of the officials who, during the Bush administration, affirmatively discussed expanding [[nato|NATO]] to include Ukraine specifically.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The article describes her as "perpetually bloodthirsty" and notes that at the time she was serving as the U.S. ambassador to NATO, alongside [[condoleezza-rice|Condoleezza Rice]] in those discussions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

Her role is used to establish that the intention to bring Ukraine into the alliance long predates the 2022 invasion, and that the likelihood of a Russian military response to such a move has been common knowledge at the highest levels of government for many years — what was needed was time and sufficient propaganda to bring the public along.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) Nuland was also a subject of reporting by the late [[gonzalo-lira|Gonzalo Lira]], cited among the independent voices who surfaced the omitted side of the story.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Syria arms denial, 2012

Nuland appears in Badlands Media's Benghazi reporting as the [[state-department|State Department]] spokeswoman who publicly denied U.S. involvement in arming the Syrian opposition.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The day before a June 2012 New York Times report describing [[cia|CIA]] operatives directing a smuggling operation of rifles, rocket-propelled grenades, ammunition and antitank weapons across the Turkish border — funnelled through intermediaries including the [[muslim-brotherhood|Syrian Muslim Brotherhood]] and paid for by [[qatar|Qatar]], [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and [[turkey|Turkey]] — Nuland reiterated the administration's public line: "We have repeatedly said that we are not in the business of arming in Syria."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Badlands treats the juxtaposition as evidence that the administration was publicly denying an operation it was running, and that the exposure of that operation intensified congressional and media scrutiny in the months before the September 2012 Benghazi attack.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Niger mission, August 2023

As Acting Deputy Secretary of State — the number two at the [[state-department|State Department]] under Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] — Nuland made a surprise visit to Niamey, the capital of [[niger|Niger]], on Monday, August 7, 2023, for what she called "difficult" talks with the junta that had ousted President [[mohamed-bazoum|Mohamed Bazoum]] in late July.[3] She announced that she had "traveled to Niamey to express grave concern at the undemocratic attempts to seize power and urged a return to constitutional order," though U.S. officials had still not used the word "coup" to describe the situation.[3] Briefing reporters afterward, she said the "conversations were extremely frank and at times quite difficult because, again, we were pushing for a negotiated solution," and noted the military leaders were "quite firm in their view on how they want to proceed, and it does not comport with the constitution of Niger."[3] Her request to see the detained Bazoum was denied, and she conceded "it was difficult today, and I will be straight up about that," effectively confirming that Washington was unlikely to get its way.[3] Among the junta officials she met was self-declared defense chief General [[moussa-salaou-barmou|Moussa Salaou Barmou]], whom Nuland confirmed had previously received military training from elite American operatives.[3]

Nuland also publicly warned the junta against forging ties with Russia's [[wagner-group|Wagner]] group, at a time when the neighboring West-friendly bloc [[ecowas|ECOWAS]] was threatening full military intervention.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that "when Victoria Nuland arrives on the scene, color revolution is a fast follow," describing Niger as the apparent next front of confrontation with Russia and cautioning that sanctions on the Nigerien people could backfire.[3] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized her as a "Deep State spox" and linked the episode to the Kenya summit attended by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Prigozhin and to the multipolar world promised by the BRICS nations.[3]

## Crimea demilitarization remarks

In March 2023 an [[nbc-news|NBC News]] correspondent reporting from inside [[crimea|Crimea]] singled out Nuland's stated United States position that, "at the very least," Washington wanted Crimea demilitarized, telling viewers that standing amid the peninsula's pervasive military presence he wondered "how on earth does that happen?"[4] The segment was cited by Badlands contributors as evidence that official demilitarization goals of the kind Nuland articulated were disconnected from conditions on the ground.[4]

## Praise for Viktor Shokin

Badlands coverage in September 2023 recalled reporting from the prior month that Nuland, the [[state-department|State Department]] point person for Ukraine during the Obama administration, had praised Ukrainian Prosecutor-General [[viktor-shokin|Viktor Shokin]] and stated on the record that his efforts justified a third tranche of U.S. aid.[5] That position was presented alongside a December 2015 [[european-commission|European Commission]] progress report crediting Shokin's anti-corruption work, as evidence contradicting then-Vice President Joe Biden's claim that allies shared his demand for Shokin's removal.[5]

## Retirement, March 2024

On Tuesday, March 5, 2024, Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] announced Nuland's retirement from her post as Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs, the number three position in the [[state-department|State Department]].[6] Former [[cia|CIA]] analyst [[ray-mcgovern|Ray McGovern]] speculated in an interview with Russia's Sputnik that the timing was significant, coming while the administration was attempting to hold a strong line on Ukraine and while Biden was still seeking tens of billions of dollars in defense aid from Republicans in the House.[6] Although rumors circulated that declining health explained the departure, McGovern argued that the notoriously hawkish Nuland had become a liability at a moment when NATO and Russia were inching closer to direct nuclear-armed confrontation, saying his \"best guess\" was that the CIA, the Defense Department and the NSA had circulated the message that \"Victoria's got her own agenda here.\"[6]

Her announced temporary replacement was career diplomat [[john-bass|John Bass]], then Under Secretary of State for Management and a former ambassador to Afghanistan who had overseen Biden's botched withdrawal from Kabul — an irony noted by commentators given that he would also oversee Ukraine policy at a juncture when Kiev was clearly against the ropes.[6] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Nuland as \"one of the most hawkish anti-Russia Deep State nodes in the west,\" pointed to her key role in the 2014 Maidan coup, and argued that she had sought further U.S. entrenchment in both Syria and Iraq; her removal, in his reading, was part of a \"Botched Boogaloo 2.0\" in which the 2021 Kabul withdrawal pattern would repeat in Syria and Iraq, consistent with [[just-human|Just Human]]'s thesis about the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]] and with [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]]'s [[devolution|devolution]] framework.[6]

## Ukraine biolabs remark

Badlands Media commentary published in April 2024 cited Nuland — derisively called "Tori 'The War Pig' Nuland" — as the official who "let the cat out of the bag" regarding biological research facilities in [[ukraine|Ukraine]], after what the writer described as breathless media denials that such laboratories existed.[7]
