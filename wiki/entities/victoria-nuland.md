# Victoria Nuland

Victoria Nuland is a U.S. diplomat identified in the Badlands Media corpus as a long-standing advocate for NATO expansion to Ukraine. During the George W. Bush administration, she served as the U.S. ambassador to NATO, a role in which she was "affirmatively discussing the expansion of NATO to include Ukraine specifically," according to the source article.

## Role in NATO Expansion Policy

The article places Nuland among the officials who advanced the policy trajectory that [[william-burns]] later warned against in his February 2008 cable. She is described with the epithet "perpetually bloodthirsty," reflecting the article's critical framing of officials who pursued NATO enlargement despite internal intelligence assessments of Russian red lines. The piece characterizes her as part of a broader cohort—including Condoleezza Rice—that pushed for Ukraine's inclusion in the alliance during the Bush administration.

## Context Within the Corpus

Nuland appears in the Badlands Media corpus as part of the institutional lineage connecting the Clinton-era initiation of NATO expansion through the Bush-era acceleration and into the Biden-era fulfillment, as represented by [[anthony-blinken]]'s declaration that Ukraine "WILL become a member of NATO." She is also associated with the [[burns-cable-2008]], the diplomatic document that explicitly warned against the policy she is said to have advanced.

## Related Figures and Concepts

Nuland's tenure at NATO is linked in the article to the broader [[nato]] enlargement project and to the analytical framework of foreign policy continuity across successive U.S. administrations. Her actions are framed as evidence that the U.S. Security State pursued a deliberate strategy of encirclement despite knowing its impact on Russian security calculations.
