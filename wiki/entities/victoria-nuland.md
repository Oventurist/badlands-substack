# Victoria Nuland

Victoria Nuland is a U.S. diplomat identified in "A Bright Red Line" as one of the officials who, during the Bush administration, affirmatively discussed expanding [[nato|NATO]] to include Ukraine specifically.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The article describes her as "perpetually bloodthirsty" and notes that at the time she was serving as the U.S. ambassador to NATO, alongside [[condoleezza-rice|Condoleezza Rice]] in those discussions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

Her role is used to establish that the intention to bring Ukraine into the alliance long predates the 2022 invasion, and that the likelihood of a Russian military response to such a move has been common knowledge at the highest levels of government for many years — what was needed was time and sufficient propaganda to bring the public along.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) Nuland was also a subject of reporting by the late [[gonzalo-lira|Gonzalo Lira]], cited among the independent voices who surfaced the omitted side of the story.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Syria arms denial, 2012

Nuland appears in Badlands Media's Benghazi reporting as the [[state-department|State Department]] spokeswoman who publicly denied U.S. involvement in arming the Syrian opposition.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The day before a June 2012 New York Times report describing [[cia|CIA]] operatives directing a smuggling operation of rifles, rocket-propelled grenades, ammunition and antitank weapons across the Turkish border — funnelled through intermediaries including the [[muslim-brotherhood|Syrian Muslim Brotherhood]] and paid for by [[qatar|Qatar]], [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and [[turkey|Turkey]] — Nuland reiterated the administration's public line: "We have repeatedly said that we are not in the business of arming in Syria."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Badlands treats the juxtaposition as evidence that the administration was publicly denying an operation it was running, and that the exposure of that operation intensified congressional and media scrutiny in the months before the September 2012 Benghazi attack.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Niger mission, August 2023

As Acting Deputy Secretary of State — the number two at the [[state-department|State Department]] under Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] — Nuland made a surprise visit to Niamey, the capital of [[niger|Niger]], on Monday, August 7, 2023, for what she called "difficult" talks with the junta that had ousted President [[mohamed-bazoum|Mohamed Bazoum]] in late July.[3] She announced that she had "traveled to Niamey to express grave concern at the undemocratic attempts to seize power and urged a return to constitutional order," though U.S. officials had still not used the word "coup" to describe the situation.[3] Briefing reporters afterward, she said the "conversations were extremely frank and at times quite difficult because, again, we were pushing for a negotiated solution," and noted the military leaders were "quite firm in their view on how they want to proceed, and it does not comport with the constitution of Niger."[3] Her request to see the detained Bazoum was denied, and she conceded "it was difficult today, and I will be straight up about that," effectively confirming that Washington was unlikely to get its way.[3] Among the junta officials she met was self-declared defense chief General [[moussa-salaou-barmou|Moussa Salaou Barmou]], whom Nuland confirmed had previously received military training from elite American operatives.[3]

Nuland also publicly warned the junta against forging ties with Russia's [[wagner-group|Wagner]] group, at a time when the neighboring West-friendly bloc [[ecowas|ECOWAS]] was threatening full military intervention.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that "when Victoria Nuland arrives on the scene, color revolution is a fast follow," describing Niger as the apparent next front of confrontation with Russia and cautioning that sanctions on the Nigerien people could backfire.[3] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized her as a "Deep State spox" and linked the episode to the Kenya summit attended by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Prigozhin and to the multipolar world promised by the BRICS nations.[3]

## Crimea demilitarization remarks

In March 2023 an [[nbc-news|NBC News]] correspondent reporting from inside [[crimea|Crimea]] singled out Nuland's stated United States position that, "at the very least," Washington wanted Crimea demilitarized, telling viewers that standing amid the peninsula's pervasive military presence he wondered "how on earth does that happen?"[4] The segment was cited by Badlands contributors as evidence that official demilitarization goals of the kind Nuland articulated were disconnected from conditions on the ground.[4]

## Praise for Viktor Shokin

Badlands coverage in September 2023 recalled reporting from the prior month that Nuland, the [[state-department|State Department]] point person for Ukraine during the Obama administration, had praised Ukrainian Prosecutor-General [[viktor-shokin|Viktor Shokin]] and stated on the record that his efforts justified a third tranche of U.S. aid.[5] That position was presented alongside a December 2015 [[european-commission|European Commission]] progress report crediting Shokin's anti-corruption work, as evidence contradicting then-Vice President Joe Biden's claim that allies shared his demand for Shokin's removal.[5]

## Retirement, March 2024

On Tuesday, March 5, 2024, Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] announced Nuland's retirement from her post as Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs, the number three position in the [[state-department|State Department]].[6] Former [[cia|CIA]] analyst [[ray-mcgovern|Ray McGovern]] speculated in an interview with Russia's Sputnik that the timing was significant, coming while the administration was attempting to hold a strong line on Ukraine and while Biden was still seeking tens of billions of dollars in defense aid from Republicans in the House.[6] Although rumors circulated that declining health explained the departure, McGovern argued that the notoriously hawkish Nuland had become a liability at a moment when NATO and Russia were inching closer to direct nuclear-armed confrontation, saying his \"best guess\" was that the CIA, the Defense Department and the NSA had circulated the message that \"Victoria's got her own agenda here.\"[6]

Her announced temporary replacement was career diplomat [[john-bass|John Bass]], then Under Secretary of State for Management and a former ambassador to Afghanistan who had overseen Biden's botched withdrawal from Kabul — an irony noted by commentators given that he would also oversee Ukraine policy at a juncture when Kiev was clearly against the ropes.[6] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Nuland as \"one of the most hawkish anti-Russia Deep State nodes in the west,\" pointed to her key role in the 2014 Maidan coup, and argued that she had sought further U.S. entrenchment in both Syria and Iraq; her removal, in his reading, was part of a \"Botched Boogaloo 2.0\" in which the 2021 Kabul withdrawal pattern would repeat in Syria and Iraq, consistent with [[just-human|Just Human]]'s thesis about the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]] and with [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]]'s [[devolution|devolution]] framework.[6]

### Additional detail on the March 2024 departure

Associated Press coverage on March 5, 2024 described Nuland as the third-highest ranking U.S. diplomat and a frequent target of criticism for her hawkish views on Russia and Ukraine, reporting that the State Department said she would retire and leave her post that month.[9] A career foreign service officer, she had served as Assistant Secretary of State for Europe during the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration, retired after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was elected president, and returned to government as Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs under [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[9] She had been a candidate to succeed [[wendy-sherman|Wendy Sherman]] as Deputy Secretary of State and served as acting deputy for the seven months after Sherman's retirement, but lost an internal personnel battle when Biden nominated [[kurt-campbell|Kurt Campbell]] to the number two spot; Campbell took office the previous month.[9] Her earlier career included a posting at the U.S. embassy in Moscow during the tumultuous 1990s, where she was present during the attempted coup against Russian President [[boris-yeltsin|Boris Yeltsin]], followed by service as U.S. ambassador to [[nato|NATO]] and then as State Department spokeswoman under Secretary [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Rodham Clinton]] during Obama's first term.[9]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the timing as auspicious, noting that the "Deep State Department" was seeing departures just weeks after [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s sit-down with Tucker Carlson allowed the Russian leader to present his version of the centuries-long narrative war between Russia and what Burning Bright calls the Prussian Globalists of the western hegemon.[9] He again identified Nuland as one of the prime architects of the CIA-backed [[euromaidan|Maidan coup]] that destabilized Ukraine in 2014 and led to the Russian seizure of Crimea, and pointed readers to the Deep State Spotlight installment on Nuland by Badlands colleague [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]].[9] He concluded that she would not be missed by most, and wished "continued misfortune" upon anyone who lamented her departure.[9]

## Deep State Spotlight profile (August 2023)

In an August 23, 2023 "Deep State Spotlight" feature, Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] presented Nuland as the paradigmatic example of an official whose power survives every change of party control, arguing that her career disproves both the claim that bipartisanship is dead and the claim that the excesses of the Bush-era [[war-on-terror|War on Terror]] have been repudiated.[10] The piece quoted [[glenn-greenwald|Glenn Greenwald]]'s description of her as "the living, breathing embodiment of everything that is rotted, deceitful and destructive — not in one of the two political parties, but in the DC ruling class."[10] DeLarme dubbed her "the queen of regime change" and described her promotion by [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to Deputy Secretary of State, the second-ranking post at the [[state-department|State Department]] behind [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]], as near-assurance that the Ukraine war would continue.[10]

The article treated her August 2023 mission to [[niger|Niger]] as self-parody: she demanded that the junta reinstate ousted president [[mohamed-bazoum|Mohamed Bazoum]], threatened armed forces chief of staff General [[moussa-salaou-barmou|Moussa Salaou Barmou]] with economic consequences, and justified the trip by declaring that the United States "stands opposed to coups" and maintains a "resolute commitment to supporting democracy and constitutional order" — claims DeLarme called rich coming from the official caught on a leaked 2014 phone call plotting to install a "pro U.S." leader in Ukraine after the removal of democratically elected president [[viktor-yanukovych|Viktor Yanukovych]].[10] The junta rejected her demands outright and refused her request to meet the house-arrested Bazoum.[10] DeLarme linked her Niger performance to the contemporaneous emergence of a State Department cable showing officials pressuring the Pakistani military to remove Prime Minister [[imran-khan|Imran Khan]] over his declared neutrality on Ukraine; Khan was duly removed, convicted on what DeLarme called dubious corruption charges, imprisoned and barred from office despite being Pakistan's most popular politician, a template he suggested was being reused domestically against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[10] In his reading, Nuland's outrage over coups was staged for a domestic corporate-media audience, since the rest of the world was not buying it.[10]

### Career across administrations

DeLarme traced her ascent as a textbook case of grooming by the [[military-industrial-congressional-complex|Military-Industrial-Congressional Complex]]: an Ivy League background centered on [[yale-university|Yale]], affiliation with the [[brookings-institution|Brookings Institution]], the [[center-for-a-new-american-security|Center for a New American Security]] and the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]], and the post of U.S. permanent representative to [[nato|NATO]] from 2005 to 2008.[10] She entered government in the Clinton administration as chief of staff to Deputy Secretary of State [[strobe-talbott|Strobe Talbott]], then served as deputy director for former Soviet Union affairs, effectively running U.S. policy toward Russia.[10] Under [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] she became top foreign policy adviser to Vice President [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney]], guiding him to advocate and implement the invasion of Iraq while also serving as ambassador to NATO.[10] She returned to Russia policy under [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s State Department and later ran Ukraine policy under [[john-kerry|John Kerry]], was sidelined during the Trump administration, and returned under Biden to work on Ukraine.[10] DeLarme argued that her survival as a neoconservative extremist in an era supposedly past Bush-era hawkishness, and her central role in the Iraq invasion that Washington now calls its worst modern policy error, are the clearest evidence that the [[deep-state|deep state]] is indifferent to elections — with the partial exceptions of anti-establishment figures such as Trump and [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|RFK Jr.]].[10]

### Reinvigorating NATO

The article devoted a section to Nuland's role in repurposing NATO after the Cold War, when many Europeans questioned the alliance's viability given that the state it existed to contain no longer existed — a point Trump also made in 2016, which DeLarme cited as one reason the [[us-security-state|U.S. security state]] turned hostile to him.[10] While serving simultaneously as Cheney adviser and NATO ambassador, Nuland pressed for expansion up to the Russian border, including [[ukraine|Ukraine]], despite a warning letter from future [[cia|CIA]] director [[william-burns|Bill Burns]] to [[condoleezza-rice|Condoleezza Rice]] that all of Russia, including the liberal opposition, viewed Ukrainian membership as a red line and that the course would likely produce war in the Donbas and Crimea.[10] A 2005 Radio Free Europe piece quoted her saying the U.S. wanted the alliance "retooled for the 21st century" and turned into the world's "multilateral security trainer of first resort," citing NATO's Rustamiya training center for Iraqis; she also declared that "if the divisive debate over Iraq taught us one thing, it is that NATO must be the place where we talk about all the issues affecting our future."[10] DeLarme read this as an explicit program to bypass the [[united-nations|UN]] and its Security Council — which China, Russia, France and Germany had used to block the Iraq invasion — and argued that using NATO in this way makes Nuland responsible for more death than Osama bin Laden, Saddam Hussein and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] combined.[10] The same mechanism, he wrote, was used in the Obama years when Nuland worked under Hillary Clinton while Clinton and [[samantha-power|Samantha Power]] drove the NATO regime-change war against [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] in Libya.[10]

Nuland, called "Toria" by fellow operatives, is the daughter of [[sherwin-b-nuland|Sherwin B. Nuland]], the surgeon and writer who taught bioethics, history of medicine and medicine at the Yale School of Medicine.[10] Her husband is neoconservative scholar [[robert-kagan|Robert Kagan]], co-founder with [[william-kristol|William Kristol]] of the [[project-for-the-new-american-century|Project for the New American Century]], a think tank tied to the [[american-enterprise-institute|American Enterprise Institute]] whose policy document [[rebuilding-americas-defenses|Rebuilding America's Defenses]] openly advocated global military domination.[10] DeLarme noted that many PNAC members held top posts in the George W. Bush administration, that Kagan was a leading booster of the Iraq war and author of an essay on the "growing split" between the U.S. and Europe, and that the instability, strengthened Iran and ISIS-producing power vacuum France and Germany predicted were precisely what the [[neoconservatives|neoconservatives]] wanted.[10] He closed by describing an "unholy matrimony" between the establishment left and the neoconservatives, cemented by shared hatred of Trump and bipartisan support for the Ukraine war, and compared PNAC's pre-9/11 agitation for war with Iraq to the establishment's pre-January 6 desire for a domestic war on MAGA Republicans — arguing that Nuland's career is nonetheless useful common ground with disillusioned liberals.[10]

## Career trajectory and NATO expansion

In "The End of History Delusion," Matthew Ehret traces Nuland's rise from assistant to [[strobe-talbott|Strobe Talbott]] during the Clinton-era Russia policy to a new post as [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney]]'s assistant and then US Ambassador to [[nato|NATO]], where she oversaw the military bloc's expansion from 16 to 24 nations by 2008 and welcomed Georgia's and Ukraine's aspirations to join the alliance.[13]

Ehret writes that Nuland worked closely with the CIA front group [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] and [[george-soros|George Soros]] in setting the stage for a new era of regime-change operations in the form of color revolutions in Georgia in 2003 and Ukraine in 2004, alongside the bombing campaigns across the Middle East in the wake of 9/11.[13] Her husband [[robert-kagan|Robert Kagan]] was an early co-founder of the [[project-for-a-new-american-century|Project for a New American Century]], the neoconservative think tank behind [[rebuilding-americas-defenses|Rebuilding America's Defenses]].[13] Later, under [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and Nuland's lead, Ukraine was "lit on fire" as the pro-Russian government of [[viktor-yanukovych|Viktor Yanukovych]] was overturned in a second color revolution and a regime chosen by the [[state-department|US State Department]] was installed in power.[13]
