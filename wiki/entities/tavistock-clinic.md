# Tavistock Clinic

The **Tavistock Clinic** was, by the conventional account, founded in 1920 in London, England, to research and treat victims of "shell shock" — the trauma-based incapacitation suffered by soldiers during the First World War.[1] The clinic was headed by the psychiatrist [[hugh-crichton-miller|Hugh Crichton-Miller]], who was heavily influenced by [[carl-jung|Carl Gustav Jung]], the founder of analytical psychology.[1] Crichton-Miller had built his reputation developing psychological treatments for shell-shocked soldiers during and after the war, yet the first patient treated at the clinic was in fact a child — a detail Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] treats as an early signal of the clinic's broader interest in the malleability of the developing mind.[1]

## Trauma as a research subject and as a tool

Carlson's argument is that through the research and treatment of trauma-based mental disorders, psychiatrists at the clinic discovered that trauma itself could be used to control minds, and that this control could be applied on both micro and macro levels.[1] On the micro level, individuals could be brought into a clinical setting and exposed to trauma, drugs such as LSD, and other psychological techniques, producing a mind-controlled subject; this line of work is presented as the direct ancestor of the CIA's [[mk-ultra|MK Ultra]] program in the United States, which began in 1953.[1] On the macro level, trauma was said to work on whole populations, with constant media-driven fear punctuated by periodic traumatic events producing a public governed by fear.[1] The JFK assassination, 9/11, and Covid are named as such events, each said to have opened a portal for the fear narratives that followed.[1]

## Funding and transition to an institute

Prior to the creation of the [[tavistock-institute|Tavistock Institute]], the Tavistock Clinic was funded by the Rothschild and British Royal families.[1] The transition from a clinic treating shell-shocked soldiers into an influential think tank is attributed to major financial donations from the Rockefeller family.[1] The institute was initiated in 1946, developing out of the clinic, and was formally established as a separate entity in September 1947.[1] Key figures who carried over from the clinic to the institute include the British military psychiatrist [[john-rawlings-rees|John Rawlings Rees]] and the German-American psychologist [[kurt-lewin|Kurt Lewin]].[1]

## The gender identity clinic

In the modern era the Tavistock name became attached to the Gender and Identity Development Service, the NHS gender identity clinic operated under the Tavistock umbrella.[1] An *Independent* article of 11 August 2022, quoted at length in the source, reported that the clinic faced mass legal action over claims children were misdiagnosed and rushed into transitioning, that an independent review criticised the quality of care, and that staff, patients and parents alleged children were "rushed into taking life-altering puberty blockers without adequate consideration or proper diagnosis" amid pressure to adopt an "unquestioning affirmative approach."[1] The clinic, which [[nhs-england|NHS England]] moved to shut down, had treated 19,000 children with gender dysphoria since 1989.[1] Carlson stresses that almost all of those cases occurred between 2012 and the clinic's 2022 closure, framing 1989–2012 as a research phase and 2012 onward as the operational push.[1]
