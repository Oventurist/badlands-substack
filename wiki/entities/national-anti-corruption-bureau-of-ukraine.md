# National Anti-Corruption Bureau of Ukraine

The National Anti-Corruption Bureau (NABU) is the Ukrainian law-enforcement agency responsible for investigating high-level corruption offenses.[1]

## Yermak investigation

Together with the Specialized Anti-Corruption Prosecutor's Office, NABU investigated [[andriy-yermak|Andriy Yermak]], the former chief of staff to President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]], who was named the sole suspect in a case involving roughly 460 million hryvnia (about $10.5 million) allegedly laundered through elite construction projects near Kyiv.[1] Investigators searched Yermak's home in November, the same month he resigned, but as of May 2026 had not filed formal charges — a step that could take months.[1] The case has been described as the biggest threat to Zelenskyy's government since [[russia|Russia]]'s full-scale invasion.[1]

## Nuclear energy corruption scheme

In November 2025, the bureau and the Special Anti-Corruption Prosecutor's Office uncovered a $100 million corruption scheme involving Ukraine's state-owned nuclear energy operator.[2] The scandal led to the resignations of Justice Minister [[german-galushchenko|German Galushchenko]], Energy Minister [[svetlana-grinchuk|Svetlana Grinchuk]] and presidential chief of staff [[andriy-yermak|Andrey Yermak]].[2] Ukrainian media reported in February 2026 that Galushchenko had been detained while attempting to flee to Poland.[2]

## July 2025: raids, subordination, and the Kolomoisky case

In July 2025, NABU and the [[specialized-anti-corruption-prosecutors-office|Special Anti-Corruption Prosecutor's Office]] (SAPO) were placed under executive oversight after security raids on NABU, with President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] suggesting that the anti-corruption infrastructure had been penetrated by Russian influence.[3] Hundreds of Ukrainians protested across the country on July 22 against what many characterized as an authoritarian consolidation of power.[3]

On July 23, 2025, NABU announced it had completed its investigation into businessman [[ihor-kolomoisky|Ihor Kolomoiskyi]] in a case involving the misappropriation of UAH 9.2 billion from [[privatbank|PrivatBank]] — the fourth episode in the broader PrivatBank embezzlement investigation and the first directly implicating Kolomoiskyi.[3] The announcement came one day after Prosecutor General [[ruslan-kravchenko|Ruslan Kravchenko]] publicly criticized the bureau for the slow pace of its Kolomoisky cases.[3] Badlands commentary noted that the anti-corruption offices were created by Zelensky's Western backers in 2015, making their sponsors' anger over the raids difficult to explain.[3]

## In the 2015 European Commission progress report

The December 18, 2015 [[european-commission|European Commission]] progress report on Ukraine, published by the New York Post in September 2023, noted that a newly created specialized anti-corruption prosecution office — whose head Prosecutor-General [[viktor-shokin|Viktor Shokin]] had just appointed — would help the newly established National Anti-Corruption Bureau combat corruption.[4] The Commission described the specialized office as "an indispensable component of an effective and independent institutional framework for combating high-level corruption" and urged Ukrainian leadership to ensure both bodies were "fully operational" by the first quarter of 2016.[4]
