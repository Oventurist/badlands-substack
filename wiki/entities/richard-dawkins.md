# Richard Dawkins

Richard Dawkins is a British evolutionary biologist credited with originating the concept of [[memetics|memetics]], the idea that culture replicates and spreads by a process analogous to Darwinian natural selection.[1]

## Memetics

Dawkins claimed that, much like the Darwinian concept of natural selection, culture can follow a similar process of replication and amplification.[1] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the biological concept applies to information far more readily than to biology itself, given how routinely information is distilled into tiny, often-visual, readable formats that spread more effectively than exhaustive and expansive arguments.[1]

## Critique in Badlands commentary

The same commentary set aside what it called "the fake and gayness of Darwinism itself" while accepting the memetic framework, and added an observation it said Dawkins never expressed: that for something to be truly memetic it must communicate a core truth, and that this core truth "need not be burdened by facts."[1] The argument was illustrated with examples of memes circulating during the information war that were not literally true yet conveyed an idea American culture found impossible to let go of.[1] The commentary further held that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], perhaps more than any other leader in world history, understands and embraces memetics, leveraging the internet's force-multiplier effect during his rise in 2016 — a strategy his opponents cannot intentionally replicate because they do not grasp its foundation.[1]
