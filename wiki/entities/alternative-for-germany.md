# Alternative for Germany (AfD)

Alternative for Germany, commonly abbreviated AfD (from the German *Alternative für Deutschland*), is a German political party generally described in mainstream coverage as right-wing populist or "far-right." It is a recurring subject in Badlands Media commentary as an example of a nationalist movement whose electoral support grows despite sustained institutional and media opposition.

## Electoral performance and the firewall

As reported in the March 24, 2026 edition of the Badlands Brief, [[badlands-brief-50d|Alternative Annexations & New(ish) Regime Change]], the AfD posted gains in recent German state elections, particularly in the country's eastern regions. Despite these gains, the party remains excluded from governing coalitions, as mainstream parties continue to enforce a longstanding refusal to cooperate with it. German domestic intelligence authorities have classified the AfD as "right-wing extremists," and mainstream German parties have maintained what is commonly termed a "firewall" policy — declining to form coalitions or governing alliances with the AfD regardless of its vote totals. The source notes that despite the firewall, the party continues to increase its vote share.

## Badlands interpretation

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treats the AfD's trajectory as evidence that delegitimization tactics have diminishing returns. She compares the German case to the American MAGA movement, arguing that Joe Biden's so-called "red sermon" increased MAGA's credibility and was followed by an increase in its vote share. In her framing, whether the AfD is genuinely radical "is irrelevant," and she adds the broader claim that "elections are fake all over the world"; what matters is the narrative that attacks on the party are failing to neutralize its popularity. She characterizes "far right" as "the globalists' pejorative for nationalists," and argues that nationalism is more popular than ever worldwide as ordinary people recognize that globalist arrangements benefit a narrow few. She frames the party's rise alongside a reference to [[benito-mussolini|Benito Mussolini]]'s definition of fascism as corporatism — "the marriage of government and corporate power" — which she applies to the contemporary global order rather than to the AfD.
