# Pete Buttigieg

**Pete Buttigieg** served as United States Secretary of Transportation in the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1]

## East Palestine criticism

Buttigieg became a frequent target of criticism in the aftermath of the [[east-palestine-train-derailment|East Palestine train derailment]] of February 2023, in which a [[norfolk-southern|Norfolk Southern]] freight train carrying hazardous chemicals derailed in eastern Ohio.[1] On the disaster's first anniversary, [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the environmental catastrophe remained unresolved — creeks still polluted, residents still displaced, and no meaningful change or oversight achieved — and charged that Buttigieg would "continue chestfeeding and brunching rather than improving US transportation infrastructure."[1] The same commentary noted that President Biden still had not visited the site a year after the derailment despite state officials asking him to do so.[1]

## Neuralink investigation (2023)

As Secretary of Transportation, Buttigieg received a February 9, 2023 letter from the [[physicians-committee-for-responsible-medicine|Physicians Committee for Responsible Medicine]] alerting him to records the group had obtained concerning [[neuralink|Neuralink]].[2] The same day, the [[us-department-of-transportation|U.S. Department of Transportation]] he led confirmed it was investigating [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s company over the potentially illegal movement of hazardous pathogens, with a spokesperson saying the agency took the allegations "very seriously."[2] Badlands Media contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] emphasized that Buttigieg was heading the department running the probe and questioned its timing.[2]

## Silence during the initial derailment response

In the first weeks after the February 2023 derailment, Badlands contributor [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] noted that Buttigieg had made no public mention of the disaster even as reports of sickened residents, dead animals and arrested journalists accumulated.[3] The same commentary contrasted that silence with Buttigieg's public remarks about there being too many white employees working on the country's railroads.[3]

## Face the Nation interview (May 2024)

Buttigieg appeared on CBS's *Face the Nation* over the weekend of May 25–26, 2024, in an interview that [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] reported went badly for him.[4] Early in the segment, host [[margaret-brennan|Margaret Brennan]] confronted him over the state of federal electric-vehicle charging infrastructure: after more than $7 billion in spending since 2021, the nation had fewer than 10 EV chargers to show for it.[4] The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law signed by [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in November 2021 had allocated $7.5 billion for EV charging, of which $5 billion went to states as formula funding for the National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure program to establish a network of fast chargers along major highways; the result was seven chargers with a total of 38 parking spots.[4] When pressed for an answer, Buttigieg tried to deflect, prompting an involuntary laugh from Brennan.[4] During the same interview he suggested that increased airline turbulence was occurring as a result of climate change.[4] ZeroHedge framed the exchange as significant because criticism of the program had spread from the Washington Post to CBS.[4]
