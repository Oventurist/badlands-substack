# John Roberts

John Roberts is the Chief Justice of the United States and a member of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[1]

## Mississippi mail ballot case

In the Court's June 2026 decision on absentee ballot deadlines, Roberts joined Justice [[amy-coney-barrett|Amy Coney Barrett]] and the Court's three liberal justices in a 5-4 majority holding that federal law does not bar states from counting ballots postmarked by Election Day but received afterward.[1] His vote was decisive in rejecting the challenge brought by the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] to [[mississippi|Mississippi]]'s mail ballot law.[1]

## Stay in the independent labor board removals (2025)

On April 9, 2025 Chief Justice Roberts temporarily halted lower court decisions that had voided President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s firing of a member of the [[national-labor-relations-board|National Labor Relations Board]] and a member of the [[merit-systems-protection-board|Merit Systems Protection Board]], allowing the removals to go back into effect for the time being.[2] The administrative stay gave the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] more time to consider a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] request to permit the removals of [[cathy-harris|Cathy Harris]] and [[gwynne-wilcox|Gwynne Wilcox]], and Roberts gave their lawyers until 5 p.m. on April 15 to respond.[2] Solicitor General [[d-john-sauer|D. John Sauer]] had argued in the emergency application that the reinstatement orders harmed the president and the separation of powers.[2]

## 2024 year-end report

In his annual year-end report, released by the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] on Tuesday, December 31, 2024, Chief Justice Roberts condemned what he described as "dangerous" talk by some officials about ignoring federal court rulings, using the occasion to stress the importance of an independent judiciary weeks before President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took office.[3] Officials "from across the political spectrum have raised the specter of open disregard for federal court rulings," Roberts wrote. "These dangerous suggestions, however sporadic, must be soundly rejected."[3]

Roberts did not detail which officials he had in mind, and CNN noted that both Republicans and Democrats had hinted at ignoring court rulings in recent years.[3] The message landed days before the January 20 inauguration of a president who had repeatedly decried the federal judiciary as rigged, and whose agenda — particularly on immigration — was expected to put him on a collision course with a Court he had helped build by naming three conservative justices in his first term.[3] Badlands contributor Burning Bright treated the report as evidence that the country was entering "an era of mass change," arguing that change comes on the back of conflict and that the machine was gearing up to disrupt the disruptors.[3]


## America First Legal lawsuit (2025)

In May 2025 the [[america-first-legal|America First Legal Foundation]], founded by White House aide [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]], sued Roberts in his capacity as official head of the [[united-states-judicial-conference|U.S. Judicial Conference]], naming [[robert-conrad|Robert J. Conrad]] of the [[administrative-office-of-the-us-courts|Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts]] as a co-defendant.[4] The complaint accuses both bodies of performing regulatory actions that exceed the resolution of cases or controversies — which it identifies as the judiciary's "core functions" — and argues that records held by the Roberts-led Judicial Conference should be subject to Freedom of Information Act requests.[4] Badlands commentators [[canncon|CannCon]] and [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used the story to argue that administrative entities created under the judiciary operate almost entirely free from constitutionally-mandated oversight.[4]

## Rebuke of Trump over judicial impeachment (March 2025)

In March 2025 Roberts issued a rare public rebuke of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] after the president called for the impeachment of Judge [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]], who had ruled against the deportation of hundreds of Venezuelans.[5] The statement came as House Republicans introduced articles of impeachment against Boasberg within hours of Trump's remarks, part of an unprecedented wave of impeachment filings against federal judges who had ruled against the administration.[5] Boasberg was at least the fifth such judge targeted.[5]

The rebuke coincided with two further adverse rulings the same day, from Judges [[ana-reyes|Ana Reyes]] and [[theodore-chuang|Theodore Chuang]], intensifying what commentators described as an extraordinary conflict between the executive branch and the federal judiciary.[5]

## Criticism after the April 2025 Alien Enemies Act order

Roberts drew pointed criticism from conservative legal commentators after the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] issued its middle-of-the-night order on April 19, 2025 blocking removals of Venezuelan detainees under the [[alien-enemies-act|Alien Enemies Act]].[6] A Reason essay on [[process-formalism|process formalism]] concluded that "John Roberts is ill equipped to keep the Court away from an actual constitutional crisis," adding that "at this point, he is squirming in a pit of quick sand. The more he flails his arms, the quicker he will sink. Anyone who reaches out to the Chief will descend just the same."[6] [[mike-davis|Mike Davis]] of the [[article-iii-project|Article III Project]] separately called the order "shameful lawlessness and cowardice from the conservative justices."[6]

## Foreign aid payment pause (February 2025)

On Wednesday, February 26, 2025, Roberts paused an order by U.S. District Judge [[amir-ali|Amir H. Ali]] that had required the Trump administration to pay roughly $2 billion in foreign aid funds to contractors by midnight.[7] The pause followed an emergency application by the administration seeking to block the release of [[usaid|U.S. Agency for International Development]] funding, which officials said they could not comply with in the time allotted.[7] Responses from the groups litigating against the administration were due before Friday at 12 p.m., making the pause potentially short-lived.[7]

## Tennessee gender-care arguments (December 2024)

During the December 4, 2024 oral arguments in the challenge to Tennessee's ban on gender-affirming care for minors, Roberts repeatedly suggested — as did Justice [[brett-kavanaugh]] — that the question of such treatments for minors should be left to legislatures rather than resolved by courts.[8] His skepticism toward judicial involvement was read as a particularly worrying sign for the challengers, given that he had sided with federal transgender employees in the 2020 decision finding them covered by the workplace discrimination protections of the Civil Rights Act.[8] See [[united-states-v-skrmetti]].[8]

## Stay of the foreign aid payment order (February 2025)

On February 26, 2025, Chief Justice Roberts paused a federal judge's order that had required President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s administration to pay foreign aid funds to contractors and grant recipients.[9] The interim order placed on hold an action by U.S. District Judge [[amir-ali|Amir Ali]] imposing an 11:59 p.m. deadline that Wednesday night.[9] Roberts provided no rationale for the administrative stay, which afforded the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] additional time to consider the administration's more formal request to block Ali's ruling.[9]
