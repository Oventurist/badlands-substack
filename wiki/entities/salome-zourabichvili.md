# Salome Zourabichvili

Salome Zourabichvili is a French-born Georgian politician who served as President of Georgia and who, after leaving office, took up a fellowship at the [[mccain-institute|McCain Institute]] at Arizona State University.[1]

## Background and presidency

Born in France, Zourabichvili maintained a consistently pro-Western stance during her tenure as Georgia's head of state.[1] Her presidency ended amid a bitter dispute over Georgia's 2024 elections: in December, Georgian members of parliament elected Mikhail Kavelashvili as president, and Zourabichvili refused to recognize him as her successor, claiming that the October parliamentary vote — a convincing victory for the ruling Georgian Dream party — had been rigged.[1] Despite failing to provide proof of fraud, she backed the pro-Western opposition, which protested for weeks demanding a rerun of the election, and she appeared among the demonstrators herself.[1] The 72-year-old also threatened not to leave the presidential palace in Tbilisi, but eventually departed in late December.[1]

## McCain Institute fellowship

The McCain Institute announced in a statement that Zourabichvili had been chosen for its 2025 Kissinger Fellowship, named after former US Secretary of State [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]].[1] The institute itself is named after the late US senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]], described in coverage of the appointment as a renowned foreign policy hawk who backed the Western-supported change of government in Ukraine in 2014.[1]

Georgian parliament speaker Shalva Papuashvili sharply criticized the appointment, asserting that she was returning to "the entity that employed her."[1] Writing on X, Papuashvili drew a parallel with former Georgian President Mikheil Saakashvili: "Almost 12 years ago, a similar gesture was extended to ..Saakashvili, at Tufts University," adding that "despite having pledged allegiance to Georgia alone, Saakashvili later became a Ukrainian citizen and Zourabishvili too, eventually, is likely to return to her native France."[1] Papuashvili concluded that neither had truly served Georgia.[1]

## Foreign agents law dispute

The same reporting noted that Georgia's political opposition strongly criticized the country's foreign-agents bill, labeling it a "Russian law" and accusing the ruling party of basing it on legislation enacted in Russia in 2012, while the ruling party maintained that the law was inspired by the US Foreign Agents Registration Act of 1938 and was far more lenient than its American counterpart.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry contrasted the treatment of Zourabichvili with domestic US rhetoric about election denial, asking why a Kissinger Fellow at the McCain Institute who rejected an election result and refused to vacate the presidential palace was not characterized as an insurrectionist or a threat to democracy.[1]

## Foreign agents law veto pledge

As president of the Republic of Georgia, Zourabichvili vowed to veto the [[georgia-foreign-agents-law|"foreign agents" law]] adopted by parliament on May 14, 2024, which would impede foreign nongovernmental organizations' ability to operate in the country.[2] Her pledge carried little practical force: parliament approved the measure 84–30, well above the 76 votes required to override a presidential veto.[2] The [[white-house|White House]] said it was "deeply troubled" by the legislation, warning it would move Georgia further from the values of the [[european-union|European Union]] and [[nato|NATO]].[2]

## Fraud accusations against Georgian Dream (December 2024)

Amid the eleven-day Tbilisi protests over the suspension of European Union accession talks, opposition parties and President Zourabichvili accused the ruling [[georgian-dream|Georgian Dream]] party of electoral fraud carried out with alleged Russian assistance following the disputed 26 October 2024 parliamentary election.[3] Badlands commentary dismissed the accusation as unoriginal, arguing that the Georgian public appeared broadly to support Prime Minister [[irakli-kobakhidze|Irakli Kobakhidze]].[3]
