# Sabbatai Zevi

Sabbatai Zevi was a 17th-century Jewish figure declared the Jewish Messiah sometime before 1666 by his self-appointed public-relations man, [[nathan-of-gaza|Nathan of Gaza]].[1] He is the namesake of [[sabbateanism|Sabbateanism]], described by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] as one of the most successful, popular and destructive Jewish reform movements in history.[1]

## Mission and arrest

Zevi believed it was necessary to restore Israel to the Jews, his people, as part of his redemption and reform efforts.[1] Upon entering the holy land in the late 17th century, Zevi and his followers were almost immediately arrested by the Muslim Sultan of the era, who demanded that Zevi convert to Islam or prove his messiahship by allowing the Sultan to rain arrows upon him — reasoning that a genuine Messiah could either defend himself or rise from the dead.[1]

## Conversion and aftermath

Zevi chose to convert to Islam, a decision that sent shockwaves through his movement.[1] His conversion gave rise to the crypto-Sabbateans, followers who pursued their Kabbalistic work in secret while presenting outwardly as Jews, Muslims, Christians or atheists, believing that redemption of the twelve sephiroth of creation required acts of sin and evil.[1] A hundred years later [[jacob-frank|Jacob Frank]] carried the doctrine further into explicit antinomianism.[1]

Deschamps places Zevi within a longer arc of Holy Land messianic politics extending from [[neoplatonism|Neoplatonism]] and the Crusades to modern [[zionism|Zionism]] and the commentary surrounding the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]].[1]
