# Claudia Sheinbaum

Claudia Sheinbaum is the President of Mexico and the purported protégé of her predecessor, Andrés Manuel López Obrador.[1]

## Relations with the Trump administration

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued in January 2026 that Sheinbaum "sings a different tune" from Obrador, who — despite being described as a hardcore communist — respected [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and deployed 29,000 Mexican troops to the border to secure it from the Mexican side.[1] The contributor sensed frustration from Trump in dealing with Sheinbaum, noting that the United States had yet to finalize a bilateral trade deal with her government.[1] In May of the preceding year, Trump accused Sheinbaum of being afraid of the cartels after she rejected his offer to send US troops into Mexico to help fight them.[1]

Badlands advanced the interpretation that Mexico is "the real Deep State hub of Latin America" and that Sheinbaum was an agent dispatched to conduct damage control after Obrador gave Trump everything he asked for.[1]

## Defense of Maduro

Sheinbaum publicly defended [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], requesting that he be given a fair trial.[1] Badlands connected this to emerging claims that the US government's case against Maduro hinges on the testimony of a single Venezuelan general offered as part of a plea deal for clemency, raising the possibility that the prosecution could collapse in court and Maduro be publicly exonerated of the cartel-boss allegations.[1]

Sheinbaum's government also warned against any violation of Mexican national sovereignty after Trump said the US may begin conducting land strikes against cartels operating in Mexico.[1]

## Jalisco raid and the SEAL training invitation (February 2026)

In February 2026 Mexican military forces killed Nemesio Oseguera Cervantes, known as [[el-mencho|"El Mencho"]], the alleged leader of the [[jalisco-new-generation-cartel|Jalisco New Generation Cartel]], during an operation in Jalisco state in which a reported 25 members of the Mexican national guard also died.[2] Sheinbaum's government had not released a full after-action report as of the following days, and security forces remained deployed in Jalisco and surrounding areas amid concerns of further cartel retaliation, which included vehicle burnings and road blockades across parts of western Mexico.[2]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that [[donald-trump|President Trump]] had affirmed he offered to send US troops into Mexico to fight the cartels but was turned down by Sheinbaum, an outcome the contributor read as consistent with the claim that the cartels effectively control Mexico.[2] Two weeks before the raid, however, a Mexican Senate commission approved a proposal for SEAL Team 2 to train Mexican special forces against the cartels, and Sheinbaum formally invited the SEALs the following week — though strictly in a training capacity.[2] Separate reporting cited by the Brief said the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] had quietly secured the transfer of roughly 100 cartel leaders, reportedly including El Mencho's brother, from Mexican to US custody before the Jalisco operation.[2]

## Denial of the El Paso drone incursion (February 2026)

Sheinbaum publicly rejected reports that cartel-linked drones had crossed into US airspace near El Paso, Texas, in February 2026, telling reporters that "There is no information about the use of drones on the border," according to *Newsweek*.[3] Her denial directly contradicted the Trump Administration's confirmation that drones had been detected and disabled by the [[federal-aviation-administration|FAA]] and the [[department-of-war|Department of War]], and stood at odds with Representative [[tony-gonzales|Tony Gonzales]]'s characterization of daily cartel drone incursions as routine along the border.[3] Badlands commentary treated the exchange as further evidence for its contention that the Mexican government and the cartels are difficult to distinguish, and that [[donald-trump|Trump]] regards Mexico's government as complicit and therefore a threat.[3]

## Defense of indicted governors (2026)

After US prosecutors in New York indicted several Mexican officials — including a sitting governor — for allegedly conspiring with the [[sinaloa-cartel|Sinaloa Cartel]], Sheinbaum publicly defended those governors and demanded that Washington produce proof of their crimes.[4] Badlands noted that this followed [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]]'s earlier accusation that Sheinbaum was a cartel puppet, and her public rejection of US offers to send American soldiers into Mexico to dismantle the cartels.[4] The pattern was read as vindicating Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]]'s assessment that Mexico was the central node of the cartel structure.[4]

## Cartel indictments and US pressure (July 2026)

By July 2026 the Trump administration had indicted ten Mexican government officials, accusing them of ties to the [[sinaloa-cartel|Sinaloa cartel]], a step Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described as "moving up the food chain" of cartel-controlled Mexican officials.[5] The indictments followed growing public tension between [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Sheinbaum, who was described as reluctant to assist the US government in fighting the cocaine cartels — behavior that provoked Trump into accusing her of being a cartel puppet.[5] The same commentary argued that if the Mexican government forms part of a drug trade killing American youth, then invading [[mexico|Mexico]] and toppling its government would be a righteous cause, while allowing that better remedies might exist.[5]

## May 2026: CIA deaths in Chihuahua and cartel indictment fallout

After a vehicle crash in the El Pinal area of Chihuahua killed two [[cia|CIA]] officers and two Mexican officials returning from a drug raid, Sheinbaum ordered an investigation into why the American agents were in the country, stating that "neither had formal accreditation to participate in operational activities" and that one had entered Mexico as a tourist.[6] Chihuahua attorney general [[cesar-jauregui-moreno|César Jáuregui Moreno]] resigned days later, admitting "omissions" about the agents' presence after initially claiming they had been giving drone lessons and coincidentally caught a ride with a police convoy.[6]

Sheinbaum also responded to U.S. [[indictment-of-mexican-officials-in-the-sinaloa-cartel-case|indictments of Mexican officials]] — including the governor of Sinaloa, who took temporary leave — by publicly demanding that Washington produce proof.[6] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read that demand, together with her exclusion from knowledge of the CIA presence, as evidence that the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration does not trust her and that she carries the optics of a [[sinaloa-cartel|Sinaloa Cartel]] puppet.[6]

## Response to Operation Absolute Resolve (January 2026)

In early January 2026, Sheinbaum criticized [[operation-absolute-resolve|Operation Absolute Resolve]] and the capture of Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], reaffirming [[mexico|Mexico]]'s non-intervention policy in response to remarks by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[7] "We categorically reject intervention in the internal matters of other countries," she said, dismissing the idea of US military action inside Mexico and emphasizing that bilateral cooperation must not amount to subordination.[7]

Her statement followed Trump's comments aboard Air Force One, in which he said an operation against Colombia — "run by a sick man" — "sounds good to me," and warned that "you have to do something with Mexico… We'd love Mexico to do it, they're capable of doing it, but unfortunately the cartels are very strong in Mexico."[7]

## Tariff delay celebration (March 2025)

In March 2025, tens of thousands of people packed Mexico City's main plaza on a Sunday to celebrate with Sheinbaum a decision by the United States to postpone tariffs on many Mexican goods, clutching large Mexican flags and shouting "Mexico is to be respected!"[8] "Fortunately, dialogue and respect have prevailed," she told the crowd.[8] The celebration came days after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] postponed 25% tariffs on many imports from Mexico for a month, saying Sheinbaum had made progress on drug smuggling and illegal immigration.[8] Sheinbaum had planned to announce retaliatory measures that Sunday but instead held a mass celebration, saying she was optimistic tariffs would not be applied to Mexico and that she would continue to act with a "cool head" in the face of Trump.[8] Badlands commentators contrasted the Mexican celebration with Canada's confrontational posture in the [[2025-us-canada-trade-war|2025 US–Canada trade war]].[8]

## Migrant caravans broken up (December 2024)

Activists said in early December 2024 that Mexican immigration authorities had broken up two small migrant caravans headed for the U.S. border, busing some migrants to cities in southern Mexico and offering others transit papers.[9] The action came a week after President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened 25% tariffs on Mexican products unless the country did more to stem migration, and after Trump wrote that Sheinbaum had agreed to stop unauthorized migration across the border; Sheinbaum posted the same day that "migrants and caravans are taken care of before they reach the border."[9] Migrant rights activist Luis García Villagrán said the breakups appeared to be part of "an agreement between the president of Mexico and the president of the United States."[9] The first caravan had set out from Tapachula, near the Guatemalan border, on November 5 — the day Trump was elected — reaching about 2,500 people at its height and covering roughly 270 miles to Tehuantepec in Oaxaca over almost four weeks.[9]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the episode as Sheinbaum "getting in line" in a subtler fashion than [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]], and applied to Mexico the same argument he made about Canada: that pressure on corrupt intermediaries would ultimately redound to the benefit of ordinary Mexicans, and that "the Awakening is spreading worldwide."[9]

## Migration cooperation with the incoming Trump administration (December 2024)

In late November 2024, President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened 25% tariffs on Mexican products unless Mexico did more to stem the flow of migrants toward the U.S. border.[9] Trump wrote on the Wednesday following that Sheinbaum had agreed to stop unauthorized migration into the United States; Sheinbaum posted the same day that "migrants and caravans are taken care of before they reach the border."[9]

Activists said Mexican immigration authorities subsequently broke up two small migrant caravans heading north, busing some migrants to cities in southern Mexico and offering others transit papers.[9] Migrant rights activist Luis García Villagrán said the dispersal appeared to be part of "an agreement between the president of Mexico and the president of the United States."[9] The first caravan had set out from Tapachula, near the Guatemalan border, on November 5 — the day Trump was elected — and at its height numbered about 2,500 people, covering roughly 270 miles in nearly four weeks to reach Tehuantepec in Oaxaca.[9]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that Sheinbaum was "getting in line," albeit more subtly than [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]], and argued the pattern showed a worldwide awakening against entrenched bureaucratic rule.[9]

## Rejection of US military entry into Mexico (August 2025)

In August 2025, after reporting that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had secretly directed the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] to use military force against Latin American drug cartels designated as terrorist organizations — including operations at sea and on foreign soil — Sheinbaum flatly rejected any US troop presence in Mexico.[11] "The United States is not going to come to Mexico with the military. We cooperate, we collaborate, but there is not going to be an invasion. That is ruled out, absolutely ruled out," she said, adding that such action "is not part of any agreement, far from it. When it has been brought up, we have always said no."[11]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the sequence "weird," noting that a supposedly secret directive was leaked to the *New York Times* and that Sheinbaum then responded to the leak, and asked whether Sheinbaum herself had been the source given that she and Trump had spoken roughly a week earlier.[11] The commentary argued that if the United States defeated the cartels on its own soil, expelled them southward, and prosecuted their American co-conspirators, Sheinbaum would end up begging for US military help — by which point it would be "not our problem anymore."[11]


## Retaliatory tariffs, February 2025

On Saturday, February 1, 2025, Sheinbaum ordered retaliatory tariffs in response to the US decision to impose 25% tariffs on all goods coming from Mexico, as a trade war broke out between the two neighbors.[12] In a lengthy post on X she said her government sought dialogue rather than confrontation with its top trade partner to the north, but that Mexico had been forced to respond in kind.[12] "I've instructed my economy minister to implement the plan B we've been working on, which includes tariff and non-tariff measures in defense of Mexico's interests," she wrote, without specifying which US goods would be targeted.[12]

Reporting noted that decades of growth in cross-border trade had produced a highly integrated auto industry along with massive two-way flows of crude oil, natural gas and motor fuels, and a booming farm trade in which Mexico ships avocados and tomatoes north while US farmers supply corn and other grains.[12] The United States is by far Mexico's most important foreign market, and Mexico in 2023 overtook China as the top destination for US exports.[12] Badlands commentary argued that Mexico and Canada were realizing Trump does not bluff, yet were still "checking on the back of his call."[12]

## Response to Trump's tariff threats (December 2024)

Badlands commentary in December 2024 argued that Mexico under Sheinbaum publicly capitulated within 48 hours of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 25% tariff threat, with Mexico reportedly seizing its largest fentanyl operation in years - a result the writer read as evidence that authorities had known all along exactly where and how the operation functioned.[13] Sheinbaum was listed alongside [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] and [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] among the leaders the commentary urged to "take the deal."[13]

## November 2024 call with Trump on migration

On Wednesday, November 27, 2024 — days after Trump threatened sweeping new tariffs on Canada and Mexico as part of his crackdown on illegal immigration and drugs — Sheinbaum spoke with the president-elect by phone.[14] Trump afterward declared a win, saying Sheinbaum "agreed to stop Migration through Mexico" and posting on Truth Social that this was "effectively closing our Southern Border" after a "very productive conversation."[14] Sheinbaum, however, suggested nothing had changed: she indicated on social media that she had told Trump Mexico was already "taking care of" migrant caravans, called it an "excellent conversation," and added, "We reiterate that Mexico's position is not to close borders but to build bridges between governments and between peoples."[14]

AP reported that while the status of the proposed tariffs remained unclear, the exchange appeared to confirm for Trump the value of threatening to disrupt trade with import taxes; his initial post moved financial markets and produced a response he was quick to cast as a victory, so that even if the tariffs never materialized he could continue relying on tariff threats as a policy tool.[14]


## Record fentanyl seizure (December 2024)

Speaking at a press conference on December 4, 2024, Sheinbaum addressed the largest fentanyl seizure in Mexican history, in which security forces impounded 1,100 kilograms of the synthetic opioid in the state of Sinaloa: "This is an investigation that has been going on for a long time, and yesterday, it gave these results."[15] Her top security official, [[omar-garcia-harfuch|Omar Garcia Harfuch]], announced the seizure alongside the arrest of two men and the confiscation of several guns, and pledged continued operations until violence in Sinaloa — driven by factional fighting within the [[sinaloa-cartel|Sinaloa Cartel]] since the July capture of Ismael "El Mayo" Zambada — subsides.[15] The seizure came days after a tariff warning from [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[15]

## Authorization of US Special Forces training (February 2025)

At Sheinbaum's urging, the [[mexican-senate|Mexican Senate]] approved on Tuesday, February 11, 2025 the entry of a US Special Forces group into the country, ostensibly to train Mexico's marines under a program called "Strengthening the Capacity of the Special Operations Forces of the Navy."[16] The training arrangement, approved by unanimous vote, would see a contingent of US military personnel serve as advisors and trainers for Mexico's marine forces with a special focus on combating the country's drug cartels.[16]

The National Pulse reported the move as a further overture by Mexico to avert President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s imposition of tariffs on Mexican exports to America, and noted that a similar agreement had been approved during the six-year term of Andrés Manuel López Obrador, which saw 11 US military personnel enter Mexico to oversee marine training.[16]
