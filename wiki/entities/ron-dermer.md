# Ron Dermer

Ron Dermer is Israel's Strategic Affairs Minister and a close confidant of Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1]

## Clash over US–Hamas direct talks (March 2025)

Dermer "lashed out" at US hostage envoy [[adam-boehler|Adam Boehler]] during a Tuesday phone call held after Israel learned of an unprecedented meeting the Trump aide had conducted with a senior [[hamas|Hamas]] delegation led by [[khalil-al-hayya|Khalil al-Hayya]] in Doha earlier that day, a meeting convened to discuss the potential release of American hostages from Gaza.[1] A Western official told The Times of Israel that Dermer fumed over Boehler's discussion of how many Palestinian prisoners Israel would release in exchange for the five Israeli-American hostages still held in Gaza — one of them alive and four believed dead — without Netanyahu's consent, confirming reporting by [[axios|Axios]].[1]

Boehler attempted to explain that these were only initial discussions and that nothing would be finalized without Israel's approval.[1] Hours after the tense call, Boehler's meeting with al-Hayya was leaked to the media in a move the United States believed Israel was behind, further eroding trust between the two governments.[1] The US decision not to inform Israel in advance followed Netanyahu's expressed opposition when the idea was first proposed to him by the Trump administration the previous month; Israel objected in particular to such a meeting proceeding without preconditions for Hamas.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited this episode as the reason many commentators had lost patience with the government of Israel, describing the "levels of hubris and entitlement" as "off the charts" given that Israel demands unlimited American funding and military support while presuming to dictate US policy.[1] He further asked whether Dermer should be reminded of the role he and Netanyahu allegedly played in secretly laundering tens of millions of dollars to Hamas in 2018–2019 to keep the group financially solvent and in power, and argued that all US aid to Israel, especially weaponry, should stop.[1]

## July 2025 Washington shuttle

Badlands commentary identified Dermer, a close confidant of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], as the architect of the Israeli prime minister's July 2025 Washington visits.[2] According to that account, Dermer rushed to Washington the preceding Monday to smooth relations after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly said that Israel and Iran "don't know what the f— they are doing," a reference to Israel's decision to violate the ceasefire and continue bombing Tehran.[2] Dermer then scheduled Netanyahu's own last-minute visit in what the commentary characterized as an attempt to persuade Trump to back Israel's desire to annex Palestine.[2]

## January 2021 sanctions reversal

Badlands commentary identifies Dermer as central to an episode cited as evidence for the [[devolution|Devolution]] thesis.[3] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that on January 15, 2021, in the final days of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term, Dermer — then Israeli ambassador to the United States — infiltrated the [[state-department|State Department]] and used his contacts there to secretly reverse sanctions imposed on Israeli oligarch [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]], an ally of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[3] The commentary treats this as a demonstration that the Deep State, and Israel, retained considerable control over the federal bureaucracy at the transition.[3]

## Mar-a-Lago mission on Lebanon (November 2024)

In November 2024, as Netanyahu's minister of strategic affairs, Dermer made Mar-a-Lago the first stop of a U.S. tour, meeting President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] before travelling to the White House to brief Biden administration officials on the state of the Lebanon talks.[4] According to three current and former Israeli officials briefed on the meeting, Dermer conveyed that Israel was rushing to advance a [[2024-lebanon-ceasefire|Lebanon ceasefire]] with the aim of delivering an early foreign policy win to the president-elect.[4] The Washington Post treated the ordering of his itinerary as a sign of how swiftly America's political center of gravity had shifted after Trump's electoral victory, and one Israeli official said there was "an understanding that Israel would gift something to Trump."[4] Dermer's office confirmed only that he had discussed a wide range of issues during the trip.[4]

## Biography and background (Badlands profile, July 2024)

A July 2024 Badlands News Brief devoted an extended profile to Dermer, describing him as [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s equivalent of [[dan-scavino|Dan Scavino]] — born and raised in Miami and an American citizen, yet Netanyahu's "most influential advisor and closest confidante" since 2000, when he helped the Israeli leader plot the political comeback that followed his ouster as prime minister, an ouster the commentary attributed largely to his subversion of the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]].[5] Dermer attended the Wharton School of Business at the University of Pennsylvania, a choice he has said was prompted by reading Donald Trump's *Art of the Deal*, and studied there under pollster [[frank-luntz|Frank Luntz]], who called him "the most talented student I've ever had."[5] In 1995, while still studying at Oxford, Dermer conducted the polling and formulated the strategy for [[natan-sharansky|Natan Sharansky]]'s [[yisrael-baaliyah|Yisrael BaAliyah]] party ahead of the 1996 Knesset elections; the party was later folded into Netanyahu's [[likud|Likud]].[5]

Because Yisrael BaAliyah was built to represent Russian-speaking immigrants — the so-called "Refuseniks," or Soviet Jews originating in the [[pale-of-settlement|Pale of Settlement]] — the Badlands account treated Dermer's early career as a thread tying him to Ukraine and to the Ukrainian-Jewish criminal networks said to have migrated to Israel in 1989.[5] The same commentary noted that Dermer and Sharansky co-authored *The Case for Democracy* (2004), a book favored by President George W. Bush, and cited Politico's judgment that had Dermer remained in the United States he might have become Republican National Committee chairman or a leading presidential campaign manager — grounds, in the writer's view, for calling him "a total swamp creature" beloved by Bush-era neoconservatives.[5]

## Trump–Netanyahu meeting in Florida (July 2024)

Dermer, then minister of strategic affairs, was seated beside Netanyahu at the prime minister's July 26, 2024 meeting with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in Florida, and Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] identified his presence as the most significant detail of the encounter.[5] The commentary framed the meeting — held without [[melania-trump|Melania Trump]] present, despite Sarah Netanyahu attending — as strictly transactional, and read Trump's two-handed handshake, informal greeting and refusal to touch Netanyahu's gift hat reading "Total Victory" as a rejection of Israeli war aims.[5] The writer argued Trump would have been aware of Dermer's January 2021 [[state-department|State Department]] intervention on behalf of [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] going into the meeting, and characterized the encounter as the opening of a year-long negotiation over whether a third world war would occur.[5]

## Resignation from ministerial post (November 2025)

In November 2025 Dermer submitted his letter of resignation as Strategic Affairs Minister to Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[6] The Times of Israel had revealed the previous week that the resignation was imminent and that he would hand over some of his responsibilities to Israeli Ambassador to the United States [[yechiel-leiter|Yechiel Leiter]].[6] In the letter Dermer noted that upon entering the government in December 2022 he had promised his family he would serve as minister for only two years.[6]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described the timing of the departure as both symbolically and narratively important, and characterized Dermer as "Netanyahu's Dan Scavino" — an aide who had attended to Netanyahu's every need and traveled the world starting or extinguishing fires in service of the prime minister's personal agenda while, in his assessment, damaging Israel's long-term interests.[6] He did not read the resignation as a definitive blow against Netanyahu, speculating instead that Dermer might be redeployed as a "Special Envoy" on the model of [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], operating without formal oversight or accountability as an Israeli official.[6] Noting Israeli President [[isaac-herzog|Isaac Herzog]]'s visit to the DR Congo, which he read as an attempt to bring the country back under Israeli influence, he suggested Dermer might soon appear in Africa pursuing regime change and rare earth mineral rights stripped away by British courts during the Biden administration — an outcome he attributed to [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]].[6]

## Advance work for the July 2025 Netanyahu visit

Badlands commentary on the July 2025 Gaza ceasefire push credited Dermer with preparing the ground for another high-profile visit by Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] to Washington.[7] Contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that "Ron Dermer did his duty when he traveled to the White House on Monday, seeking to clear the road" for the trip, which was subsequently scheduled for Monday, July 7, 2025.[7] The visit came as [[donald-trump|President Trump]] said he intended to be "very firm" with Netanyahu on ending the Gaza campaign, having announced that Israel had agreed to the conditions for a 60-day ceasefire.[7]

## Role in the Abraham Accords signing

As Israeli ambassador to the United States, Dermer called the Trump White House twenty-four hours before the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] were set to be signed to say that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] would not sign, using "new elections" as the stated reason.[8] Badlands commentary presented this as one instance of a recurring pattern of Netanyahu backing out of commitments at the last moment — comparable to his withdrawal from the Soleimani strike the day before it occurred in January 2020 — and noted that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] refused to let the deal collapse, pressing Netanyahu until he signed.[8]

## Sullivan call on Iran retaliation (October 2024)

Amid a reported collapse of U.S. confidence in Israeli statements about its military and diplomatic plans, two U.S. officials told Axios that during a Friday call in early October 2024, [[white-house|White House]] national security adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] told Dermer, then Israel's minister for strategic affairs, that the United States expected "clarity and transparency" from Israel about its plans to retaliate against Iran, because those plans carried implications for U.S. forces and interests in the region.[9] One U.S. official said, "Our trust of the Israelis is very low right now and for a good reason," while the administration signalled it did not oppose an Israeli response but wanted it to be measured.[9]
