# Foreign Affairs

**Foreign Affairs** is an American magazine of international relations and U.S. foreign policy, widely regarded as a house organ of the Western foreign-policy establishment.[1]

## June 2024 Gaza op-ed

In June 2024 the magazine published an op-ed by Audrey Kurth Cronin, director of the Carnegie Mellon Institute for Strategy and Technology, arguing that Israel's military operation in Gaza was a "strategic disaster" because support for [[hamas|Hamas]] among Gazans was rising rather than collapsing.[1] The piece, summarized by [[tass|TASS]] and carried in the Badlands News Brief, cited Palestinian Center for Policy and Survey Research polling showing Hamas support above 50% in March 2024, up 14 points since December 2023, and attributed the shift to mass civilian casualties, restrictions on humanitarian aid and the Israeli operations in [[rafah-invasion|Rafah]].[1]

Badlands treated the appearance of such an assessment in an establishment journal as notable in itself — a sign that criticism of the campaign had migrated from dissident outlets into mainstream foreign-policy discourse.[1]

## Role as a CFR policy signal

Ryan DeLarme's "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 1" describes Foreign Affairs as the publication produced by the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]], whose inner workings are otherwise obscured from the public, and notes that some believe the journal is used to signal desired policies and future direction to those in power.[2] The article cites Encyclopaedia Britannica's acknowledgment that "ideas put forward tentatively in this journal often, if well received by the Foreign Affairs community, appear later as U.S. government policy or legislation; prospective policies that fail this test usually disappear."[2] DeLarme concludes from this that laws and government policies are in effect written not by the people but by the CFR for the benefit of the ruling class.[2]

## Brzezinski's trilateral proposal

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 2" notes that in the early 1970s [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]] used the [[council-on-foreign-relations|CFR]]'s publication *Foreign Affairs* to argue that "a new and broader approach is needed—creation of a community of the developed nations which can effectively address itself to the larger concerns confronting mankind," proposing "a council representing the United States, Western Europe, and Japan, with regular meetings of the heads of governments as well as some small standing machinery."[3] That article is presented as the public seed of the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]].[3]
