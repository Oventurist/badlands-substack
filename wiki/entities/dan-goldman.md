# Dan Goldman

Dan Goldman is a Democratic member of the United States House of Representatives from New York, previously known for his work as a lead counsel in impeachment proceedings against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Whistleblower hearing exchange

Goldman drew attention during a July 2023 congressional hearing featuring two [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]] whistleblowers who testified about the handling of the [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] tax investigation.[1] In commentary by [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] excerpted in the Badlands News Brief of July 26, 2023, Turley observed that Democratic members of the committee had largely avoided asking specific questions about the underlying allegations, ranging instead over unrelated subjects and returning repeatedly to Trump.[1]

Goldman was described as the only member who chose to engage the allegations directly, and Turley judged the result "disastrous for the Democrats," arguing that the questioning ended up reinforcing rather than rebutting the whistleblowers' account.[1] Turley's framing held that the release of new documentary evidence had left little room for maneuver, so that any substantive question risked tripping "a wire on the Bidens."[1]

## "Eliminated" remark and apology (November 2023)

During an interview with former White House press secretary [[jen-psaki|Jen Psaki]] on MSNBC on Sunday, November 19, 2023, Goldman — who represents New York's 10th district — said of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] that "he is not only unfit, he is destructive to our democracy, and he has to be eliminated."[2] He framed the remark around what he called Trump's increasingly "dangerous" rhetoric, citing January 6 and a recent [[truth-social|Truth Social]] post he described as "incredibly, incredibly scary for anyone that might be trying to work in government."[2]

Critics on social media argued the word choice could incite violence against the 2024 Republican presidential front-runner, then 77 years old.[2] Goldman apologized the following Monday in a post on X, writing that he had "mistakenly used the wrong word to express the importance for America that Donald Trump doesn't become President again" and that "while he must be defeated, I certainly wish no harm to him and do not condone political violence," adding, "I apologize for the poor choice of words."[2]
