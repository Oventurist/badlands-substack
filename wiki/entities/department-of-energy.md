# Department of Energy

The United States Department of Energy (DOE) is the federal agency responsible for national energy policy and the nuclear weapons and reactor enterprise.[1]

## Nuclear microreactor air transport

In February 2026 DOE partnered with the [[pentagon|Department of War]] to conduct the first air transport of a [[nuclear-microreactor|nuclear microreactor]] prototype, part of a Defense Department demonstration program aimed at developing small, transportable reactors capable of powering remote or military locations. The demonstration showed that the reactor system can be safely moved by aircraft.[1]

Announcing the milestone on X, the department noted that "on May 23, 2025, President Donald Trump signed an executive order to deploy advanced nuclear reactor technologies for national security purposes. Yesterday, we took the next step toward achieving this historic mission."[1] Energy Secretary [[chris-wright|Chris Wright]] framed the test as delivery on [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s promise to "unleash American energy dominance," calling it "the next chapter for U.S. energy."[1]

The test coincided with heightened nuclear "arms race" rhetoric following the expiration of the [[new-start-treaty|New START]] treaty, though no new nuclear weapons policy was announced.[1]

## Fusion strategy

In June 2026 the department released its finalized [[fusion-science-and-technology-roadmap|Fusion Science and Technology Roadmap]], a comprehensive national strategy to accelerate the development and commercialization of fusion energy, targeting fusion pilot plants and commercial fusion power in the mid-2030s.[2] The roadmap was developed with input from more than 800 scientists and engineers from over 15 private companies, 10 national laboratories and 70 universities.[2] It is organized around three pillars — critical infrastructure, innovation through advanced research and high-performance computing and artificial intelligence, and expansion of the domestic fusion ecosystem — and is implemented through the department's newly established [[office-of-fusion|Office of Fusion]] in alignment with its [[genesis-mission|Genesis Mission]].[2]

## February 2025 layoffs and NNSA firings

In February 2025 the Department carried out broad layoffs that included the firing of staff at the [[national-nuclear-security-administration|National Nuclear Security Administration]], the agency responsible for the U.S. nuclear weapons stockpile.[3] Four people with knowledge of the matter told CNN that more than 300 NNSA staffers were fired on the night of Thursday, February 13, and that the officials involved did not appear to know the agency oversees America's nuclear weapons.[3] A Department spokesperson disputed the figure, saying "less than 50 people" were dismissed and that they held primarily administrative and clerical roles; the terminations began to be rescinded the following morning.[3]

## Closure of the Northeast Gasoline Supply Reserve (2024)

In May 2024 the Department of Energy announced the sale of the nearly one million barrels of gasoline held in the U.S.-managed stockpile in northeastern states, effectively closing the [[northeast-gasoline-supply-reserve|Northeast Gasoline Supply Reserve]].[4] The step followed a provision in the March 2024 government funding bill authorizing the sell-off and shuttering of the reserve.[4]

## FASST artificial intelligence initiative (2024)

In July 2024 the Department of Energy formally proposed a new agency-wide effort to harness and advance artificial intelligence for the public's benefit.[5] On Tuesday, July 16, the department unveiled its roadmap for the [[frontiers-in-artificial-intelligence-for-science-security-and-technology|Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence for Science, Security and Technology]] (FASST) initiative, first mentioned in May, which would leverage the agency's seventeen national laboratories.[5]

The program is aimed at helping the United States achieve top capabilities in AI for scientific, energy, and national security aims.[5] DOE said it would build energy-efficient AI supercomputers to address the risk of skyrocketing energy consumption, a concern Energy Secretary Jennifer Granholm had identified as a top priority.[5] The agency already runs some of the world's fastest supercomputers, used for climate change and energy research as well as nuclear weapons modernization, and envisions helping its scientists and the broader scientific community create trustworthy AI models yielding breakthroughs in fields including clean energy technologies.[5]

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the announcement as a timed disclosure connected to [[operation-warp-speed|Operation Warp Speed]], "The Switch" theory, and the Q drops, noting that the reference to seventeen national laboratories was potentially a Q communication.[5]

## Small modular reactors and AI power demand (2024)

In September 2024, Energy Secretary [[jennifer-granholm|Jennifer Granholm]] used a CNBC appearance to indicate that [[small-modular-reactor|small modular reactors]] were all but confirmed as a future power source for data centers, responding to questions about how the electricity demands of generative artificial intelligence would be met.[6] The exchange referenced the prospect of bringing a reactor at [[three-mile-island-nuclear-power-plant|Three Mile Island]] back online and recommissioning other nuclear sites, with national power needs potentially 20% higher within a few years.[6] Badlands Media characterized the department's posture as a sharp pivot away from the wind-and-solar rhetoric of the Green New Deal era toward nuclear generation.[6]
