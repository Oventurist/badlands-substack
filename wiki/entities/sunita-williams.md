# Sunita Williams

Sunita "Suni" Williams is a [[nasa|NASA]] astronaut whose planned eight-day stopover on the International Space Station turned into a mission of nine and a half months.[1]

## Return to Earth

Williams returned to Earth on Tuesday, March 18, 2025, when the [[spacex|SpaceX]] Dragon capsule *Freedom* splashed down off the coast of Tallahassee, Florida, at 5:57 pm EDT.[1] She was accompanied by fellow long-duration crew member [[butch-wilmore|Butch Wilmore]], NASA astronaut Nick Hague, and Roscosmos cosmonaut Aleksandr Gorbunov.[1]

Conditions at splashdown were described as idyllic, with 78 degree Fahrenheit weather and a pod of curious dolphins swimming around the capsule as ground crews prepared to open it.[1] The capsule was lifted from the water and placed on a recovery ship, where the astronauts took their first breath of fresh air in over nine months.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary used public reaction to the "Spaceman Splashdown" as a lens on media attention, with Chris Paul suggesting it explained why so few people care about the integrity of elections.[1] Stormy Patriot Joe noted the numerology of the 5:57 pm splashdown time, observing that 5+5+7 equals 17 and linking it to the "17,000" miles per hour figure quoted for orbital velocity.[1]

## Arrival of replacement crew (March 2025)

On Sunday, March 16, 2025, just over a day after launch, a [[spacex|SpaceX]] crew capsule docked with the [[international-space-station|International Space Station]], delivering the replacements for Williams and fellow stranded [[nasa|NASA]] astronaut [[butch-wilmore|Butch Wilmore]].[2] The four newcomers, representing the United States, Japan and Russia, were to spend time learning the station's operations from the pair before Williams and Wilmore strapped into their own SpaceX capsule later that week to close out an unexpectedly extended mission that had begun the previous June.[2] The two had expected to be gone about a week when they launched on [[boeing|Boeing]]'s first crewed Starliner flight, but the capsule encountered so many problems that NASA insisted it return empty, leaving the test pilots aboard for what reached the nine-month mark earlier in March 2025.[2]
