# Israeli Supreme Court

The Supreme Court of Israel is the country's highest judicial authority, sitting both as a court of final appeal and, in its capacity as the High Court of Justice, as a forum for direct petitions against government action. Its rulings on the boundary between religious accommodation and state obligation have repeatedly reshaped Israeli coalition politics.[1]

## 2024 conscription ruling

In the summer of 2024 the court ruled that yeshiva students from the Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) communities are not exempt from Israel's mandated two-year conscription into the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]].[1] The decision ended the legal basis for a long-practiced administrative exemption and set in motion a sustained political crisis for Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1]

Following the ruling, the ultra-Orthodox parties [[united-torah-judaism|United Torah Judaism]] and [[shas]] pressed Netanyahu to codify the exemption in legislation passed by the [[knesset]].[1] In the year after the decision, thousands of yeshiva students received draft letters; the Haredi parties objected that the exemption bill drafted by Netanyahu's committee excluded those students, leaving them exposed to prosecution as draft dodgers.[1] Badlands commentary identified the ruling as the origin point of the [[haredi-conscription-exemption-law]] fight, tying it to the near-collapse of Netanyahu's government in June 2025 and to the July 2025 move by IDF generals to begin drafting the 54,000 eligible yeshiva students.[1]
