# Seth Rich

Seth Rich was a staffer for the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]] whose killing in Washington, D.C. became one of the most persistent unresolved controversies of the 2016 U.S. election cycle.[1] Rich was 27 years old when he was shot on July 10, 2016, while walking home from a night out in the capital; he was speaking with his girlfriend on the telephone when the shots were heard.[1]

## Death and investigation

No killer has been identified in the years since the shooting. Washington police have said the available evidence indicates Rich was the victim of a botched robbery.[1] Mainstream outlets have characterized subsequent claims about his death as theories advanced by right-wing activists to make broader allegations about Democrats.[1]

## The laptops

Central to continued interest in the case are Rich's personal laptop, his DNC work laptop, and associated media. In a ruling reported in late November 2023, Texas federal judge [[amos-mazzant|Amos L. Mazzant]] ordered the [[fbi|FBI]] to produce material from those devices, directing "a timeline for the disclosure of information on Seth Rich's personal laptop, Seth Rich's work laptop, the DVD and tape drive within 14 days following issuance of this Memorandum of Opinion and Order."[1] The litigation arose from Freedom of Information Act requests, with the court having addressed the FBI's responses in September 2022.[1]

## Significance in the Truth Community

Badlands Media writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the Rich matter and its "infamous laptop" as among the most synonymous conspiracies in the [[truth-community|Truth Community]], and one of the "key, foundational mysteries that provoked the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]]" before and during the advent of the [[q-operation|Q Op]].[1] He read the timing of the court order through the [[shark-fin-template|Shark Fin Template]] and [[narrative-convergence|Narrative Convergence]] frameworks, noting that storylines historically braided with the Rich narrative — [[erik-prince|Erik Prince]] of Blackwater, the [[pizzagate|Pizzagate]] controversy, and [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] with the 2016 [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]] disclosures — were resurfacing simultaneously.[1] He conceded it is impossible to know how many rumors surrounding Rich and the various laptops hold water until the underlying material is released, while asserting that one side of the [[information-war|information war]] was "quaking in their boots" at the prospect.[1]

## Badlands commentary on the DNC leak (2023)

A 2023 Badlands Media essay by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] revisited the 2016 [[democratic-national-committee|DNC]] email leak, noting that [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]] published almost 20,000 emails sent or received by a handful of top committee officials and even provided an online tool for searching them.[2] While WikiLeaks did not reveal the source of the leak, the essay stated it is widely believed to have been Rich, a disillusioned DNC staffer who met an untimely end in the immediate wake of the disclosure.[2] WikiLeaks founder [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] appeared to suggest that Rich was murdered for his participation in the leak, and the essay asserted that a thorough and successful shame campaign has since been mounted by establishment gatekeepers against anyone who suspects foul play.[2]

## Badlands account of the Podesta leak (December 2023)

Badlands Media's December 2023 essay "From Ye to Oy Vey!" described Rich as the "Bernie Bro" DNC staffer who was angered that the Democratic nomination had, in the author's telling, been fraudulently given to Hillary Clinton over Bernie Sanders in 2016.[3] The essay stated that Rich used a thumb drive to download the files he then sent to [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]], the watchdog organization headed by [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]], and that those files included the [[john-podesta|John Podesta]] emails that gave rise to [[pizzagate|Pizzagate]].[3]

After the emails were published, Rich was killed while walking home from a bar late at night in Washington, D.C.; the killing was described as a robbery even though nothing was taken from him.[3] The essay repeated the claim that two MS-13 assassins were later found dead execution-style blocks from Rich's body, speculating they had been hired to kill him and then eliminated to tie up loose ends.[3] It added that the controlled media attributed the leak to Russian hackers despite forensic analysis showing the files were copied at a speed consistent only with a thumb drive, and argued that this framing primed the ground for the [[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate hoax]].[3]
