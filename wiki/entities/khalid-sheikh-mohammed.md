# Khalid Sheikh Mohammed

Khalid Sheikh Mohammed is a Guantanamo Bay prisoner accused of plotting the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks and the most prominent of three defendants whose plea agreements were voided by the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] and subsequently held non-binding by a federal appeals court in July 2025.[1]

## Plea agreement litigation

Plea agreements covering Mohammed and co-defendants Walid bin Attash and Mustafa al-Hawsawi would have ruled out death sentences.[1] The deals were signed in July 2024 by Brigadier General [[susan-escallier|Susan Escallier]], the convening authority for the military commissions; two days later, then–Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] voided the agreements and reinstated death-penalty prosecutions against the three men.[1] On July 11, 2025 a panel of the [[dc-circuit-court-of-appeals|D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals]] concluded that the agreements were not binding on the government, with Judges Patricia Millett, an Obama appointee, and Neomi Rao, a Trump appointee, writing in a joint majority opinion that "the Secretary of Defense indisputably had legal authority to withdraw from the agreements."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the failure to fully prosecute the alleged 9/11 organizers nearly 24 years after the attacks "boggles the mind" and implies that something else is going on, linking the delay to broader public openness to alternative accounts of the attacks.[1]

## August 2024 revocation of the plea agreement

On July 31, 2024, retired Brigadier General [[susan-escallier|Susan K. Escallier]] signed pretrial agreements with Mohammed and alleged accomplices [[walid-bin-attash|Walid bin Attash]] and [[mustafa-al-hawsawi|Mustafa al-Hawsawi]], exchanging guilty pleas for sentences of at most life in prison.[2] Two days later Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd J. Austin III]] overruled the overseer of the war court at [[guantanamo-bay|Guantánamo Bay]], relieved Escallier of oversight of the capital case, assumed direct authority, and withdrew from the agreements, effectively reinstating a death-penalty prosecution over attacks that killed nearly 3,000 people in New York City, at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], and in a Pennsylvania field.[2]

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the plea deal had already been turned to advantage in the information war by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and allied figures, who used it to depict the sitting administration as accommodating toward the accused while implying that the defendants still hold information leading back to the true masterminds of September 11 — a trail he said figures such as [[ezra-cohen-watnick|Ezra Cohen-Watnick]] understand in detail.[2]

## Review of the rescinded plea agreement (2024)

In August 2024, military judge Air Force Col. [[matthew-mccall|Matthew McCall]] ordered an examination of whether it was legal for Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] to rescind the plea agreements that would have spared Mohammed and his alleged accomplices [[walid-bin-attash|Walid bin Attash]] and [[mustafa-al-hawsawi|Mustafa al-Hawsawi]] the death penalty.[3] The ruling meant the years-long effort to prosecute the three men for the murders of nearly 3,000 people on September 11, 2001 could face another extended delay.[3] The [[guantanamo-bay|Guantánamo]] military commissions had already been bogged down for years amid disputes over government secrecy, conflicts of interest and the documented U.S. torture of many defendants, and longtime observers of the war court had viewed the deals as a way of providing overdue resolution and some measure of justice for those killed in the deadliest attack on American soil and the ensuing twenty years of war.[3]

## 2024 plea agreement

On Wednesday, July 31, 2024, the Department of Defense announced that prosecutors had agreed to plea deals with three of the masterminds of the [[september-11-attacks|September 11 terror attacks]] who were awaiting trial at [[guantanamo-bay|Guantánamo Bay]], Cuba.[4] The Convening Authority for Military Commissions, [[susan-escallier|Susan Escallier]], entered into pretrial agreements with Khalid Shaikh Mohammad, [[walid-bin-attash|Walid Muhammad Salih Mubarak Bin 'Attash]], and [[mustafa-al-hawsawi|Mustafa Ahmed Adam al Hawsawi]].[4] The terms and conditions were not disclosed, but three relatives of 9/11 victims were told by the Office of Military Commissions that the defendants would be spared the death penalty, and families of victims were reported as "very disappointed."[4]
