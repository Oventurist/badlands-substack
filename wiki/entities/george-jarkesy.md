# George Jarkesy

George Jarkesy is an American hedge fund manager whose dispute with the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]] produced one of the most consequential administrative-law decisions of the U.S. Supreme Court's 2023–24 term. Jarkesy ran two hedge funds that the SEC alleged were fraudulent, and rather than filing suit in federal district court, the agency brought its enforcement case before one of roughly 2,000 administrative law judges who work inside federal agencies.[1]

## SEC v. Jarkesy

In [[sec-v-jarkesy|SEC v. Jarkesy]], decided on Thursday, June 27, 2024, the Supreme Court held that defendants accused of fraud by the SEC are entitled to a jury trial when the agency seeks civil penalties, grounding the ruling in the Seventh Amendment.[1] Coverage at the time described the decision as a serious blow to the administrative state and as part of a years-long project by the financial sector and conservatives to weaken federal agency power.[1]

The decision's reach extends well beyond the SEC: agencies such as the [[environmental-protection-agency|Environmental Protection Agency]], the Labor Department and OSHA also rely on administrative law judges, who report to executive-branch agencies rather than sitting in the judicial branch.[1] Commentators noted that Jarkesy was one of several cases that term chipping away at agency authority, with the pending question of judicial deference to agency interpretations still outstanding.[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

Writing in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] News Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] listed the ruling first among four decisions released that day and welcomed it with the phrase "Hooray for the Bill of Rights."[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the decision as evidence that "the promised neutering of the (Deep) Administrative State is WELL underway," reproducing the objections of Slate legal writer Mark Joseph Stern as inverted confirmation of the outcome's significance.[1]
