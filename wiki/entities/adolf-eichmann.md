# Adolf Eichmann

**Adolf Eichmann** was the Nazi official who served as chief minister responsible for "Jewish Affairs," overseeing the creation of the ghetto system in Germany.[1]

## Role and the Haavara Agreement

Eichmann worked closely with Reichsführer [[heinrich-himmler|Heinrich Himmler]] during Himmler's management of the [[ahnenerbe|Ahnenerbe]].[1] As part of the program to eliminate Jews from Europe, Eichmann — described in Matthew Ehret's account as a kabbalist-practicing Nazi — led the crafting of the 1933 [[haavara-agreement|Haavara Agreement]] with the British Empire, establishing Nazi support for the transfer of Jews to British-Mandate Palestine in preparation for the creation of a Jewish state.[1] Nazi Germany minted a commemorative coin in 1933 celebrating the agreement.[1]

## Statements on Zionism

Eichmann learned Hebrew in order to study the Kabbalah.[1] Before his execution, he was interviewed by *Life* magazine about his relationship with [[zionism|Zionism]] and stated: "[H]ad I been a Jew, I would have been a fanatical Zionist. I could not imagine being anything else. In fact, I would have been the most ardent Zionist imaginable."[1] The interview appeared as "Eichmann Tells His Own Damning Story," *Life*, Volume 49, Number 22 (28 November 1960).[1]

## Interpretation

Ehret situates Eichmann's Zionist sympathies within an argument that the Nazi high command, while attacking both Christianity and Judaism, saw value in the institutions of Christianity and Zionism as tools of geopolitical control — a reading under which [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]]'s simultaneous alliance with Grand Mufti Haj Amin al-Husseini and his pro-Zionist transfer policy are not contradictory but complementary elements of a strategy of managed chaos.[1]
