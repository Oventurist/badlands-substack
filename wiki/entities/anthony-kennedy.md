# Anthony Kennedy

Anthony Kennedy is a retired Associate Justice of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]].[1] In October 2025 he re-entered public commentary with an interview and a forthcoming memoir in which he warned that partisanship was "creeping its way into the court."[1]

## October 2025 remarks

Speaking with the Associated Press in his court office on Wednesday, October 8, 2025, ahead of the publication of his memoir "Life, Law & Liberty," Kennedy said he was troubled that partisanship appeared to be entering the Court, and that the state of American political discourse had become so vulgar and vile that he worried for the country.[1]

Kennedy emphasized that the tone of recent opinions bothered him more than the outcomes of the cases themselves.[1] "The justices have to resist thinking of themselves as being partisan," he said.[1] He declined to identify any particular justices or opinions, but returned repeatedly during the nearly hourlong interview to what he described as the personal nature of some recent writing.[1]

"Of course, when you disagree, you criticize the other, but you criticize the opinion and the reasoning. You don't criticize the author," Kennedy said.[1] "And that point seems to be eclipsed. Some of the recent opinions are attacks on your colleagues, on the judges. I was astounded, very worried about it."[1] He also said he was routinely put off by the coarseness of public speech generally, from members of [[congress|Congress]] using profanity in public to [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] used Kennedy's lament as a springboard for a broader argument about narrative control rather than engaging his institutional concerns directly.[1] Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that "The [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian Dialectic]] is not an invention that provokes nature within human beings. It is a pattern. Your desire to have something make sense to you is a vulnerability."[1] Burning Bright added that the information war is a war of stories, that stories are patterns, and that patterns can be learned and ultimately inverted.[1]
