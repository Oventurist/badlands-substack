# David Bowdich

David Bowdich is a former career official of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] who rose to become the bureau's associate deputy director and, in 2018, its deputy director. In the Badlands Media article "Setting the Stage," author Joe Lange treats Bowdich as the single most overlooked figure of the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] period — the "number three man at the FBI" during the effort to entrap [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] and frame [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who nonetheless never appears on any list of officials fired or forced out as conspirators.[1]

## Career background

According to the FBI's own biography, Bowdich began his career as a special agent in 1995 in the San Diego Field Office, where he investigated violent crimes and gangs and served as a SWAT team operator and sniper.[1] In 2003 he was promoted to FBI Headquarters in Washington, D.C., serving in the Criminal Investigative Division and the Director's Office, before returning to San Diego to supervise a multiagency gang task force and then serving as assistant special agent in charge over all non-white-collar criminal violations, the Imperial County Resident Agency, the SWAT team, and the Evidence Response Team.[1]

From September 2012 to December 2014 he was special agent in charge of the Counterterrorism Division in the Los Angeles Field Office, and from December 2014 to April 2016 he was assistant director in charge of that field office, where he oversaw high-profile matters including the San Bernardino terror attack and the LAX active shooter investigation.[1] He became associate deputy director in April 2016 — immediately before the 2016 election — with responsibility for FBI personnel, budget, administration, infrastructure, and the inspection and insider threat programs.[1]

## Promotion under Christopher Wray

Bowdich replaced [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] as acting deputy director after McCabe stepped down in January 2018 amid allegations of bias and misconduct relating to the investigation of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s email practices; FBI Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] subsequently named him deputy director outright.[1] Newsweek noted at the time that Republican lawmakers who had demanded a "purge" of Comey-era officials might be disappointed by the choice, given Bowdich's proximity to [[james-comey|James Comey]] and McCabe.[1]

## Role in the obstruction-of-justice predicate

In June 2017 testimony to the Senate Intelligence Committee, Comey said he had spoken to senior colleagues including Bowdich about Trump's alleged request that the FBI back off its probe of Flynn.[1] That made Bowdich a potential witness on whether Trump obstructed justice by firing Comey, and Senator [[dianne-feinstein|Dianne Feinstein]], ranking member of the Senate Judiciary Committee, formally requested that the committee interview Bowdich and other FBI officials as it examined Comey's termination.[1]

Lange argues that Comey's allegation was the entire predicate both for the appointment of a special counsel and for the obstruction-of-justice charges the media expected [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] to bring at the end of his investigation — making the promotion of a central witness to that scheme, rather than his removal, a "HUGE SIGNAL."[1]

Bowdich was also involved in internal FBI conversations following press reports of the secret [[clinton-lynch-tarmac-meeting|tarmac meeting]] between then-Attorney General [[loretta-lynch|Loretta Lynch]] and former President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] during the Clinton email probe; documents show Bowdich and Comey discussed those reports by email.[1] Lange reads this as evidence that Bowdich holds documentary "dirt" on Comey's cover-up efforts, including Comey's attempt to identify who had leaked the tarmac story — leaked, in Lange's telling, by military intelligence "[[wizards-and-warlocks|Wizards and Warlocks]]."[1]

## The "PLANT" thesis

Lange's central claim is that Bowdich was a deliberate "PLANT" inserted into Comey's FBI to gather evidence against the conspirators — a mirror image of the tactic Lange says the enemy used with [[paul-manafort|Paul Manafort]], who he argues was planted in the Trump campaign to serve as a Russian-collusion target but was ultimately charged only with old tax offenses.[1] On this reading, Bowdich's gang- and violent-crime pedigree explains both his usefulness to Wray and the aggressive campaign against [[ms-13|MS-13]] during Trump's first term, which Lange ties to an Obama-era Justice Department decision redefining "fugitive" and purging roughly 500,000 records from the NICS background-check system, and to the later importation of [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] members under Joe Biden.[1]

Lange further notes that Bowdich publicly defended Trump against a false allegation while testifying before Feinstein, placing blame where he believed it belonged, and that Bowdich was eventually promoted and later retired from the FBI with full benefits "after accomplishing his mission."[1]

## Significance

Under Lange's framework, Bowdich is one of three reasons the enemy's plan to stage an "FBI versus Donald Trump" obstruction case collapsed: he was a planted witness holding the evidence, Trump held [[declas|declassification]] authority, and Mueller had already cut a deal.[1] McCabe had reportedly told the bureau's highest-ranking managers to consider themselves potential witnesses in an obstruction probe, a plan that would have paraded the FBI's leadership against the president; it never materialized.[1]
