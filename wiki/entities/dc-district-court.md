# United States District Court for the District of Columbia

The United States District Court for the District of Columbia, commonly called the D.C. District Court, is the federal trial court seated in Washington, D.C. Its chief judge in March 2025 was [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]].[1]

## Role in 2025 disputes with the Trump administration

The court became a focal point of conflict between the federal judiciary and the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration. Boasberg, as chief judge, ruled against the administration's deportation of hundreds of Venezuelans, prompting Trump to call for his impeachment and House Republicans to introduce articles within hours.[1] Boasberg was described as at least the fifth federal judge to face a House GOP impeachment attempt after issuing a ruling unfavourable to the administration.[1] Judge [[ana-reyes|Ana Reyes]], also sitting in Washington, D.C., blocked enforcement of the executive order barring transgender people from military service on the same day.[1]

The court had previously handled two cases central to Badlands coverage: the Russiagate prosecution of former FBI lawyer [[kevin-clinesmith|Kevin Clinesmith]] and the January 6 case of [[ray-epps|Ray Epps]], both before Boasberg.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary treated the impeachment fight as an existential moment for the court itself. Ashe in America wrote that the Boasberg case "feels like the death rattle of the DC District Court's tyrannical reign," and framed it as one of many final stands by what the author termed probably illegitimate judges.[1] The accompanying legal argument, drawn from Rep. [[brandon-gill|Brandon Gill]]'s impeachment resolution, held that determinations under the [[alien-enemies-act|Alien Enemies Act]] are committed to the President's sole and unreviewable discretion, and therefore that the court lacked jurisdiction.[1]
