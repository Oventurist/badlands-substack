# 17th Special Citizens Operations Group (17SCOG)

The 17th Special Citizens Operations Group, abbreviated 17SCOG, is the pseudonymous author of a body of Substack and X (formerly Twitter) content that seeks to debunk the [[q-operation|Q operation]].[1] The Badlands Media article "Countering an Ocean of Deceit," published 24 June 2025 by the author behind [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], treats 17SCOG as the most technically capable of Q's contemporary detractors and devotes several sections to engaging with his arguments.[1]

## Claims about Q

17SCOG's central thesis, expressed in a Substack series titled "Q Was Never a Movement, It Was a Weapon" and in accompanying X threads, is that "Q was not an organic movement. It was a structured influence operation."[1] The Badlands article accepts this characterization outright, arguing that Anons have long understood the operation to be structured and professionally executed rather than spontaneous.[1]

17SCOG's second principal claim is that the operation must be illegal, because the legal standards governing military involvement in covert influence activity directed at American citizens have not been satisfied.[1] He cites Title 50 of the U.S. Code § 3093 (covert action findings), DoD Directive 3600.01 (information operations), and [[executive-order-12333|Executive Order 12333]] in support of this argument.[1]

17SCOG additionally states that he is a former Q supporter, and recounts an episode in which he sought a court order to protect his child from a mandated COVID-19 vaccination and was rebuked in connection with his association with "QAnon."[1] From this he concludes that one purpose of Q was to make it possible for the legal system to identify and persecute dissidents.[1]

## Forensic methodology

17SCOG's X thread applies five categories of computational-forensic analysis to the Q corpus, each of which the Badlands author reproduces approvingly as evidence of professional tradecraft:[1]

1. Stylometric feature extraction.[1]
2. Clustering analysis with silhouette score validation.[1]
3. Vocabulary drift analysis.[1]
4. Emotional drift analysis.[1]
5. Time-series cadence modeling and survival analysis.[1]

## Badlands Media response

The article's response is that 17SCOG "correctly identified that Q is a military-grade operation" while drawing an unwarranted conclusion about its purpose, a miscalculation attributed to two faulty premises: that all psychological operations are inherently malign, and that ordinary legal rules cannot be suspended in a wartime scenario.[1] Invoking the DoD [[dod-law-of-war-manual|Law of War Manual]], the author contends that the United States meets the manual's criteria for hostile occupation and that a different set of rules therefore applies, citing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s self-description as a "wartime president."[1]

On the persecution claim, the article argues that Q is not a necessary condition for state harassment of dissidents, that governments fabricated pretexts for surveillance long before 2017, and that holding Q responsible constitutes a misattribution of guilt.[1] The author, who reports having personally faced false accusations of domestic terrorism as a Q supporter, characterizes misattribution — ascribing to Q statements and consequences for which Q was not responsible — as one of the primary methods used to debunk the operation.[1]

The broader framing of the article is that 17SCOG exemplifies a pattern in which detractors "can't help but accidentally support" Q, because rigorous adversarial research tends to corroborate the operation's professional character.[1] The author expressly declines to speculate on whether 17SCOG is a paid asset or a sincere critic, treating the question as irrelevant to the analysis.[1]
