# House of Saud

The House of Saud — Al Saud, where "Al" is a dynastic designation rather than the definite article — is the ruling family of [[saudi-arabia]] and the subject of the Badlands Media essay [[arabian-nights]], subtitled "The Redemption Arc of the House of Saud."

## Origins

The source traces the dynasty to the Banu Bakr clan and the Hanifa, an ancient tribe pre-dating the Prophet. It notes that modern Arab scholars count the Banu Bakr within the Hanifa, but that before Islam the Banu Bakr was a Christian clan and among the more significant resisters of Muhammad's conquest of the region. In 627 the Banu Bakr were defeated; their Christian emir, tied to a pole and threatened with execution, refused to renounce Christ and instead recited a wisdom three times demonstrating his devotion to God and his absence of fear of death. Muhammad released and embraced him as a brother, and he later converted of his own volition.

The family's seat was Diriyah, a small farming settlement on the outskirts of Riyadh — "The Gardens," named for the string of oases around which its communities formed. The source is emphatic that the founder [[mohammed-bin-saud]] was neither a Bedouin nomad nor a tribal warlord but the emir of this agricultural community, and that the region's politics grew out of the symbiosis between oasis farmers and the bedouin nomads who depended on the oases to cross the desert.

## The Saudi states

The dynasty's political history begins with the 1744 [[pact-of-diriyah]], the alliance between Mohammed bin Saud and the preacher [[muhammad-ibn-abd-al-wahhab]], sealed by a mutual oath of loyalty and by the marriage of bin Saud's son to al-Wahhab's daughter. The resulting [[emirate-of-diriyah]], the First State of Saud, is dated by the source from 1727 to 1814, when Mohammed bin Saud's successor Abdullah attempted to seize the Hashemite kingdom of Hejaz from the ottoman empire and was captured, taken to Istanbul and publicly beheaded, his head thrown into the Bosphorus.

The source promises a subsequent article on Abdulaziz bin Abdul Rahman Al Saud, founder of the modern Kingdom of Saudi Arabia and grandfather of the current crown prince, whom it describes as having been used by british intelligence to usurp the Hashemites' stewardship of the Islamic holy places.

## The modern dynasty

In the contemporary period the essay centres on [[mohammed-bin-salman]] and his father king salman, and on the November 2017 detention of some forty royals and officials at the Riyadh Ritz-Carlton, including muhammad bin nayef and alwaleed bin talal. The source argues that the mainstream press, and the [[new-york-times]] in particular, attempts to distinguish Alwaleed from the royal family proper. It notes that no tax is imposed or collected by the Saudi government, tracing the practice to al-Wahhab's argument in 1744 that taxation would create animosity between a ruler and his people. Saudi Arabia is described as both the leader of the Islamic world and the heartland of sunni islam, alongside Jordan and Syria.
