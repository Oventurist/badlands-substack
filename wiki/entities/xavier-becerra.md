# Xavier Becerra

Xavier Becerra served as Secretary of Health and Human Services in the Biden administration, heading the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] during the period in which congressional Republicans opened their review of the federal response to COVID-19.[1]

## COVID-19 origins oversight

In February 2023, Becerra was among the senior federal health officials whose records were sought by the [[house-select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]]. Committee chairman [[brad-wenstrup|Brad Wenstrup]] requested phone records, calendars and dozens of sets of internal documents from Becerra, [[national-institutes-of-health|National Institutes of Health]] Director [[francis-collins|Francis Collins]] and former White House COVID adviser [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]].[1] HHS subsequently released roughly 900 pages of records to the panel, with an agency spokesperson stating that the department was "committed to working in good faith to address the Select Subcommittee's oversight requests."[1]

Wenstrup indicated that he would consider issuing subpoenas against Becerra, Fauci and other key officials if the panel did not receive full responses to its questions about the origins of COVID-19 and the federal agencies' handling of the outbreak.[1]

## Extension of vaccine liability protections (December 2024)

In the final weeks of the Biden administration, outgoing [[department-of-health-and-human-services|HHS]] Secretary Becerra signed a twelfth amendment to the declaration under the [[prep-act|Public Readiness and Emergency Preparedness Act]] for "COVID-19 Medical Countermeasures," extending product-liability protections for manufacturers of COVID-19 and flu vaccines through 2029.[2] The 27-page notice, dated December 6 and published in the Federal Register on December 11, 2024, drew little public attention beyond regulated entities and a page buried on the HHS website.[2]

The amendment was enacted 19 months after President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] formally ended the COVID public health emergency, and gave what Just the News called a perfunctory justification: "I have determined there is a credible risk that COVID-19 may in the future constitute such an emergency," the notice says in Becerra's voice.[2] He also wrote that "Congress delegated to me the authority to strike the appropriate federal-state balance with respect to Covered Countermeasures through PREP Act Declarations."[2] The extension arrived on the cusp of an incoming administration expected to scrutinize vaccine injuries and release long-hidden injury-reporting data.[2]
