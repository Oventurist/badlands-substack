# Yasir Al-Rumayyan

Yasir Al-Rumayyan is the governor of Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth vehicle, the [[public-investment-fund|Public Investment Fund]] (PIF), and chairman of the Saudi national oil company [[saudi-aramco|Aramco]].[1] Badlands Media has described him as one of the most consequential financial figures in the court of Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], and as a central architect of the Kingdom's effort to diversify its wealth away from petroleum.[1]

## Public Investment Fund

The PIF holds roughly $930 billion in total assets, making it one of the largest sovereign wealth funds in the world.[1] Under Al-Rumayyan's stewardship the fund has divested from a narrow oil base, floated shares of Aramco to the public for the first time in the company's history, founded international sports leagues, developed world-class resorts, and invested heavily across economic sectors, most visibly in high technology and video games.[1]

## Origins of his mandate

According to the Badlands account, when King Abdullah died in January 2015 and Salman ascended the throne, the new king immediately named his son Mohammed bin Salman as Defense Minister.[1] MBS is said to have concluded that the PIF had been mismanaged — perhaps deliberately — and was losing on the order of $100 billion a year, roughly a quarter of the entire annual government budget, putting the fund on course for insolvency by the end of 2017 and threatening the solvency of the Saudi state itself.[1] MBS reportedly locked himself in a room with his four closest advisers and refused to let anyone leave until they had produced a plan to restructure the Saudi government from scratch; the plan was drafted in twelve hours and executed on paper within ten days.[1] Badlands links that restructuring to the Saudi royal purge of the weekend of November 4, 2017, which coincided with the period in which the PIF had been projected to go bankrupt.[1]

Al-Rumayyan was one of those four advisers, and was subsequently tasked with repairing and running the fund.[1]

## Diplomatic role

Badlands argues that Al-Rumayyan's investment strategy has operated in concert with the public charm offensive conducted by MBS and with the diplomacy of Saudi Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] over roughly five years.[1] In this reading, foreign leaders greet the Saudi delegation with unusual enthusiasm because they judge MBS to be serious about transforming the Kingdom — evidenced by his detention of corrupt relatives and the removal of radical clerics from government — and because allying with a rising power likely to have the same leader for fifty years offers a diplomatic consistency no other state can match.[1]

## UFC 309 appearance

In November 2024 Al-Rumayyan sat beside President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at UFC 309 in Madison Square Garden, with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] on Trump's other side, as Jon Jones defeated Stipe Miocic.[1] Australian outlet News.com.au reported that Al-Rumayyan and Musk together control some $1.2 trillion in assets across companies including SpaceX and Aramco, and that with Trump included the trio's combined net worth approached $1.8 trillion — a figure equal to the then-current US Congress spending bill.[1] The photograph of the three men was described as "the richest picture ever seen in world sport."[1] Badlands treated the seating arrangement as deliberate signalling by Trump rather than coincidence.[1]
