# Becky Pepper-Jackson

Becky Pepper-Jackson is a West Virginia middle-school student who became the focus of national litigation over [[transgender-athletes|transgender participation in girls' school sports]].[1] Described in reporting as a 13-year-old who takes puberty-blocking medication and estrogen hormone therapy, Pepper-Jackson won a girls' shot put event at an April 18, 2024 track and field competition.[1]

Although West Virginia law bans transgender girls from playing on girls' sports teams, a federal appeals court ruled that the law could not lawfully be applied to the eighth-grader, allowing her to compete.[1] Five girls from Lincoln Middle School stepped up to the circle at the shot put event and refused to throw in protest; they were subsequently barred from future meets by the Harrison County Board of Education.[1] West Virginia Attorney General [[patrick-morrisey|Patrick Morrisey]] filed suit on the protesting students' behalf and asked the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] to weigh in on transgender student-athlete bans for a second time.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that female athletes had been barred from competing because they peacefully and respectfully protested having to compete against biological males, and that the episode inverted years of arguments about inclusion: the girls without Y chromosomes, she wrote, are the ones who will suffer from their school district's embrace of the latest political fad.[1]
