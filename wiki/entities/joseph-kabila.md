# Joseph Kabila

Joseph Kabila is a former President of the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] who, according to Agenzia Fides, was killed on January 25, 2026 in an explosion while meeting with representatives of a guerrilla group.[1] The explosion reportedly killed around 40 people in addition to Kabila, though no official source had confirmed the former president's death at the time of reporting.[1]

Badlands commentary described the event as a rumored drone strike during a meeting with [[m23-rebels|M23]] rebels in Uvira, the latest town seized by the group.[1] Kabila had been accused of backing militias that crossed into eastern Congo from [[rwanda|Rwanda]] and seized control of mining towns.[1] In 2025 he was accused of aiding and abetting the M23 rebels and charged with treason; the prosecutor sought the death penalty, which was granted when he was convicted in absentia of the failed coup.[1]

Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Kabila was president of the DRC when mining financier Dan Gertler "bribed his way into a relationship" with him, a recurring theme in Badlands coverage of Congolese mineral corruption.[1]

## Asset seizure and party ban (April 2025)

In April 2025 the government of the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] banned Kabila's political party and ordered his assets seized for allegedly supporting [[m23|M23]] rebels, who had taken control of large areas of the country's eastern provinces.[2] The move on Saturday followed reports that Kabila, then 53, had returned to the country after two years in South Africa, where he had been living since stepping down amid deadly mass protests against his rule.[2] He first took power in 2001 following the assassination of his father, President Laurent-Désiré Kabila, and remained in office until 2019.[2]

Kabila was said to have returned to Goma, the city seized by the rebels in January, and had reportedly expressed interest in helping end the conflict.[2] The Congolese Interior Ministry criticized his "deliberate choice to return to the country through the enemy-controlled city of Goma, which curiously ensures his security," and accused him and his People's Party for Reconstruction and Democracy (PPRD) of an "ambiguous attitude" and of failing to condemn the M23 aggression.[2]

Badlands commentary framed the seizure as part of an arc beginning with Executive Order 13818 of December 20, 2017, which froze the assets of Israeli mining oligarch [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] over his exploitation of the DRC, including the use of child slavery.[2] Contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described Kabila as the man Gertler bribed to secure his monopoly over DRC mineral rights, and speculated that pressure applied to three American mercenaries repatriated by the DRC to the Trump administration might ultimately implicate Gertler and former Mossad director [[yossi-cohen|Yossi Cohen]] as well.[2] The seizure coincided with the U.S.-brokered [[drc-rwanda-declaration-of-principles|DRC–Rwanda Declaration of Principles]].[2]
