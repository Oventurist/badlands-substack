# Solomon Loeb

**Solomon Loeb** was the head of the New York investment banking firm [[kuhn-loeb-and-co|Kuhn, Loeb & Company]] and the father-in-law of [[jacob-schiff|Jacob Schiff]].[1]

Ryan DeLarme's "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 6" records that in 1875 the young Schiff married Loeb's daughter, and that Schiff became head of the firm in 1885 upon Loeb's death.[1]

## Intermarriage among banking houses

The article cites history professor Howard M. Sachar on the pattern of intermarriage that bound the great banking families together: "Solomon Loeb and [[abraham-kuhn|Abraham Kuhn]], it is recalled, married each other's sisters and Jacob Schiff became an instant partner by marrying Loeb's daughter Nina … another partner, Otto Kahn, married Adelaide Wolffe, daughter of one of the firm's original investors. At Goldman, Sachs, and Co., two Sachs boys married two Goldman Daughters."[1] DeLarme presents intermarriage, combined with the use of frontmen or "front families," as the strategy by which the [[rothschild-family|Rothschilds]] built an unspoken international empire while most of the world remained unaware.[1]
