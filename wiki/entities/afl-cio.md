# AFL-CIO

The AFL-CIO is described as America's largest labor federation, and — together with its international arm, the [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]] — as functioning as a covert agent of the U.S. foreign policy establishment, using taxpayer funds to advance political and geopolitical objectives.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The article argues that behind the mythos of unions as bulwarks of the working class lies a network in which organized labor is harnessed to the "[[democracy-promotion|democracy promotion]]" agenda.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Origins

The federation is the product of a December 1955 merger between the American Federation of Labor (AFL) and the Congress of Industrial Organizations (CIO), uniting the AFL's anti-communist hardliners with the more progressive CIO.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) Its use as an instrument of statecraft predates the merger: in the closing days of World War II the [[office-of-strategic-services|Office of Strategic Services]], the [[cia|CIA]]'s precursor, began collaborating with American unions to counter emerging communist influence in Europe.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) AFL leader [[matthew-woll|Matthew Woll]] and CIO president [[philip-murray|Philip Murray]] both treated organized labor as a frontline in the ideological battle, believing labor's collective strength could counter Soviet influence and advance Western interests.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

The AFL's [[free-trade-union-committee|Free Trade Union Committee]], formed in 1944, targeted communist-led unions in France and Italy, funnelling resources to splinter pro-communist groups and providing intelligence to U.S. diplomats.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) By 1945 the AFL had sabotaged the [[world-federation-of-trade-unions|World Federation of Trade Unions]] by withdrawing and denouncing it as a communist front, setting a pattern in which U.S. agencies used unions to fracture international labor solidarity.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## "AFL-CIA"

Recognizing unions' credibility among workers, the CIA forged a secret partnership with the federation, earning it the nickname "AFL-CIA."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) A cited Jacobin article explains that interventionists in government saw that the CIA would struggle to influence foreign labor movements directly, but that working through the AFL — with real American union leaders participating — produced more success because foreign workers were more likely to trust fellow unionists.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

By 1949 the CIA funnelled millions through the FTUC to split European labor federations along Cold War lines, funding non-communist rivals such as France's Force Ouvrière and Italy's non-CGIL unions.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) In Italy, AFL-CIO operatives backed by CIA cash orchestrated strikes and propaganda against the communist-leaning CGIL, helping the Christian Democrats retain power in the 1948 elections.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Latin America, Asia and Africa

The federation's [[american-institute-for-free-labor-development|American Institute for Free Labor Development]] (AIFLD), founded in 1962 and bankrolled by [[usaid|USAID]] and the CIA, trained more than 25,000 Latin American unionists in anti-communist tactics.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) AIFLD's documented role in covert U.S. statecraft spans Guyana, Brazil, Guatemala, Honduras, the Dominican Republic, El Salvador and Chile, and involved propaganda, union splitting, training anti-communist leaders and direct support for regime change.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

Under [[lane-kirkland|Lane Kirkland]] (1979–1995) the ties between labor and intelligence deepened, with unions backing Reagan's Central American counterinsurgency and AIFLD training Salvadoran death-squad affiliates.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The [[asian-american-free-labor-institute|Asian-American Free Labor Institute]] received [[pentagon|Department of Defense]] contracts to counter communist unions in the Philippines and Indonesia, even collaborating with death squads against the left-leaning KMU federation.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Exposure and continuity

Scandals in the 1960s — exposed by Ramparts magazine and a 1967 New York Times series — revealed CIA funds flowing through unions such as the UAW into European operations and prompted congressional hearings.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) Rather than reform, the article argues, the deep state pivoted, and the federation's foreign work was consolidated in 1997 into the Solidarity Center.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) Its conclusion is that unions meant to be the voice of the working class too often amplify the interests of the deep government and the corporatocracy.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Solidarity Center connection

"Two Wings, One Ugly Bird" notes that the [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]], officially the American Center for International Labor Solidarity, was launched in 1997 under the AFL-CIO banner and became another spoke in the [[usaid|USAID]]–[[national-endowment-for-democracy|NED]] wheel.[2] The article says the Center trained AFL-CIO rank-and-file leaders in organising techniques that aligned with Democratic Party priorities or Republican-friendly trade deals depending on the funder, keeping labour a loyal foot soldier of the two-party machine rather than a wild card.[2]
