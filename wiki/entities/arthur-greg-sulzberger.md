# Arthur Greg Sulzberger

Arthur Greg Sulzberger is the publisher of [[new-york-times|The New York Times]], having succeeded his father, longtime publisher Arthur Ochs Sulzberger Jr., at the end of 2017.[1] He is the sixth member of the Ochs-Sulzberger family to hold the publisher's role, and all voting shares of the company are controlled by the Ochs-Sulzberger Family Trust, whose SEC filings state that its "primary objective" is that the Times continue "as an independent newspaper, entirely fearless, free of ulterior influence and unselfishly devoted to the public welfare."[1]

## Reuters Institute interview (February 2024)

In an interview with Oxford University's Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism published in February 2024, Sulzberger defended the paper's reporting on President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s age, saying it had made the [[white-house|White House]] "extremely upset."[1] Asked whether journalists should adopt a different approach to covering an "authoritarian candidate," Sulzberger warned of the risk of the media "leaning into becoming the opposition to these candidates and becoming emotionally invested and trying to undermine them," rather than helping the public understand who they are.[1]

He said the path forward was "to fully and fairly convey this and do it unapologetically and with clear language," and pledged that the Times would continue reporting "not just on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] but also on President Joe Biden," whom he described as "a historically unpopular incumbent and the oldest man to ever hold this office."[1] Sulzberger also acknowledged that a "disproportionate number" of conservative voters distrust any news source that recognises Biden as the legitimate winner of the 2020 presidential election.[1]

## Commentary

[[simon-esler|Simon Esler]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], argued that although Sulzberger's stated position sounded reasonable, he represents "the old guard personified" rather than any injection of new ideas or an undoing of the weaponisation of the Times.[1] Because Sulzberger took over in the middle of Trump's presidency, Esler contended, the paper's continued adversarial posture shows he cannot represent a recalibration, and that reporting on Biden's decline is better read as a coordinated operation — "theatre to entrench us into a new story, with slightly altered villains and heroes."[1]
