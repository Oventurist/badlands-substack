# Boko Haram

Boko Haram is a militant Islamist organization that operated in [[nigeria|Nigeria]] and the wider Lake Chad Basin before [[islamic-state|Islamic State]]-branded groups became dominant in the region.[1]

## Affiliation and alleged sponsorship

Badlands commentary states that Boko Haram preceded ISIS in West Africa and affiliated itself with [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]], which the commentary characterizes as a [[cia|CIA]]-created terrorist organization.[1] The same analysis holds that terrorist groups and militias in West Africa have been funded through the [[state-department|State Department]], the CIA, the [[united-nations|United Nations]], [[nato|NATO]], and an associated network of NGOs, keeping the region destabilized for decades.[1]

## Chibok kidnappings and aftermath

The organization's most widely known operation was the 2014 kidnapping of hundreds of Nigerian schoolgirls, whom Badlands commentary asserts were most likely trafficked as sex slaves.[1] 110 of the girls were rescued in Nigeria in March 2018, in an area where U.S. Special Forces were operating according to analysis tied to the [[tongo-tongo-ambush|Tongo Tongo Ambush]].[1] [[abu-bilal-al-manuki|Abu-Bilal Al-Manuki]], killed in a joint Nigerian–U.S. strike in May 2026, was described in that commentary as Boko Haram's former leader.[1]

## Cited in the 2025 US threat against Nigeria

Boko Haram was cited in November 2025 reporting on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s order that the [[department-of-war|Department of War]] prepare for possible military action in [[nigeria|Nigeria]] over the alleged killing of Christians by Islamist militants.[2] The group is responsible for massacres, kidnappings, and bombings across Nigeria, a country split between a Muslim-majority north and a largely Christian south; its attacks have killed tens of thousands and displaced over two million people since around 2009, targeting both Christians and Muslims.[2] Though often framed as sectarian, analysts cite land disputes, resource competition, and ethnic tensions as key drivers of the violence.[2]
