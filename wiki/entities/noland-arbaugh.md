# Noland Arbaugh

Noland Arbaugh is an American man who became the first human recipient of a [[neuralink|Neuralink]] brain implant, a mind-controlled chip developed by the neural research company founded by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[1]

## Background

Arbaugh was 29 years old at the time his case was publicized in March 2024 and had been paralyzed from the shoulders down in a diving accident eight years earlier.[1]

## Implant and recovery

Neuralink installed the implant in Arbaugh in January 2024.[1] The company reported that the trial was a complete success, and doctors said no cognitive impairment was detected, allowing Arbaugh to be discharged just one day after the procedure.[1]

## Demonstration video

On Wednesday, March 20, 2024, Neuralink released a nine-minute video showing Arbaugh's progress with the device.[1] In it he is seen playing a game of chess on his computer — something he had not believed possible until a few months earlier — as well as playing the video game *Civilization* and receiving Japanese and French lessons.[1] "It's crazy, really. It's great," he said in the video.[1]

Arbaugh, who explained that he could now move his computer mouse cursor using the implant, cautioned that the milestone represented only the beginning of a long process: "There is a lot of work to do, but it has already changed my life. I don't want people to think that this is the end of the journey."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] used Arbaugh's case to describe his own ambivalence about brain-computer interfaces, acknowledging the merit of using such devices to help people overcome debilitating neurological conditions such as Parkinson's disease, epilepsy or Alzheimer's, or to restore vision or limb function, while warning of the risks if the technology fell into the wrong hands or outstripped human control.[1]
