# Gabon

Gabon is a central African nation and a member of [[opec|OPEC]], the oil producers' cartel, and a former French colony.[1] In late August 2023 it became the focus of international attention when soldiers seized power four days after a disputed presidential election, an event covered by Badlands Media in its news brief of August 31, 2023.[1]

## 2023 military takeover

Army officers appeared on Gabonese state television to announce that they had cancelled the August 26, 2023 vote and dissolved the country's institutions, as reported by the BBC and Agence France-Presse.[1] President [[ali-bongo|Ali Bongo]] had sought in that election to extend his family's 56-year hold on power, in a contest the opposition described as fraudulent.[1] Gabon's dollar bonds sank on news of the takeover.[1]

If the takeover held, it would mark the ninth military seizure of power in sub-Saharan Africa in three years, following [[2023-niger-coup|a coup in Niger]] the previous month.[1] Bloomberg attributed the wave of takeovers to economic malaise and weak governance that had fed civilian frustration, and — in West Africa particularly — to the spread of extremist violence.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the seizure as "another blow to France and the west," counting it as the eighth such coup in west and central Africa since 2020, and predicted that Western officials would eventually blame Russia, as they habitually do "when the west trips over their colonialist history and globalist policies in Africa."[1] She argued the timing, geographic proximity and consistent messaging of the African uprisings indicated a coordinated effort, while leaving open whether that effort aimed to reclaim Africa from globalist forces or to advance a "great reset" toward a new world order.[1] Gabonese military forces urged the population toward "calm and serenity."[1]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that reporters on the ground described Gabonese citizens celebrating the "coup" in a festive spirit, echoing scenes from [[niger|Niger]] weeks earlier, even as what he called the Media Industrial Complex, the "(Deep) State Department" and the EU continued to characterise these transfers of power as violent takeovers.[1] He observed that most of the eight West African coups since 2020 had been directly or indirectly supported by China and Russia, and asked whether the pattern represented "total chaos or Sovereign Alliance?"[1]

Ashe in America also tied the Gabon events to French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]]'s announcement, days earlier, that France would not leave Niger despite repeated warnings from the ruling military there, and that the French ambassador would stay put — evidence, in her reading, that "things are escalating in Africa."[1]
