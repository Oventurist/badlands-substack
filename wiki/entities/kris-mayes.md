# Kris Mayes

Kris Mayes is an Arizona Democrat who won the state's 2022 election for attorney general against Republican [[abe-hamadeh|Abe Hamadeh]] by one of the narrowest margins in American statewide electoral history.

## The 2022 attorney general race

As of the December 5, 2022 certification of the [[2022-united-states-midterm-elections|2022 midterm elections]], Mayes led Hamadeh by 511 votes out of approximately 2.5 million cast — a margin of less than 0.1 percentage points. Because Arizona law requires an automatic recount in any race decided by less than 0.5 points, the contest went to a recount that began on Wednesday, December 7, alongside recounts in the state superintendent race and a state House race. Hamadeh separately filed a lawsuit challenging the result after certification, having had an earlier suit dismissed as premature.

The Badlands Media article "Arizona Earthquake!", citing *The Hill*, treats Mayes's lead as provisional and predicts that Hamadeh will prevail once the recount is complete.

## The 2020 electors prosecution

As attorney general, Mayes pursued criminal charges against Arizona's alternate Republican electors from the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 presidential election]]. A judge dismissed the resulting indictment after finding that prosecutors had failed to present the grand jury with the precise statutory language tied to the alleged conduct.

The June 5, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that after the [[arizona-supreme-court|Arizona Supreme Court]] rejected an appeal in the case, Mayes announced plans to seek a new indictment and restart the prosecution. Her spokesperson Richie Taylor said, "The Arizona Attorney General's Office will return this case to the grand jury," declining to comment further.

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the decision as "a regime puppet outing themselves," arguing that Mayes is "doubling down on weaponized government at a time when weaponized government is more acknowledged than ever." She contends that a majority of the electoral franchise no longer believes the 2020 vote totals were genuine, and that on those grounds the conduct of the so-called "fake electors" was justifiable.
