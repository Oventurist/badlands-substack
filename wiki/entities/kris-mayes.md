# Kris Mayes

Kris Mayes is the Democratic candidate who narrowly won the race for Attorney General of Arizona in the [[2022-arizona-general-election|2022 general election]], defeating Republican [[abe-hamadeh|Abe Hamadeh]].[1]

As of certification on December 5, 2022, Mayes led Hamadeh by 511 votes out of roughly 2.5 million cast — a margin of less than 0.1 percentage points, and by Hamadeh's account the closest statewide race in Arizona history.[1] Because Arizona law mandates an automatic recount in any race decided by less than 0.5 points, the attorney general contest went to a recount that began, alongside recounts in the state superintendent race and a state House race, on the Wednesday following certification.[1] Hamadeh separately filed a contest of the general election results within the five-day statutory window.[1]

## Prosecution of the 2020 Trump electors

As Attorney General, Mayes brought the state criminal case against allies of [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] over Arizona's 2020 alternate presidential elector slate.[2] A judge dismissed the original indictment after finding that prosecutors had failed to present the grand jury with the precise statutory language tied to the alleged conduct.[2] On June 4, 2026 the [[arizona-supreme-court|Arizona Supreme Court]] rejected the state's appeal of that dismissal, and Mayes announced she would seek a new indictment to restart the prosecution.[2] Her spokesperson Richie Taylor said, "The Arizona Attorney General's Office will return this case to the grand jury," adding, "We decline to comment further at this time."[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the decision as "a regime puppet outing themselves," arguing that Mayes was doubling down on [[weaponization-of-government|weaponized government]] at a moment when public awareness of such tactics was at its height.[2] The commentary further contended that widespread doubt about the authenticity of the certified 2020 vote totals would, if warranted, render the alternate electors' actions justifiable.[2] See [[arizona-fake-electors-case|Arizona fake electors case]].[2]

In an earlier phase of the same litigation, Maricopa County Superior Court Judge [[sam-j-myers|Sam J. Myers]] ruled on May 19, 2025 that prosecutors had failed to give the grand jury the text of the 1887 [[electoral-count-act|Electoral Count Act]] and ordered the case sent back, a decision described as a major setback for Mayes.[3] She promised to appeal in order to keep the prosecution going, while attorneys said the ruling would otherwise require her to assemble a new grand jury and present the case together with a copy of the statute.[3] Badlands commentator Chris Paul dismissed the development by calling Arizona's attorney general "fake and illegitimate."[3]

## Lawsuit against Musk and DOGE (February 2025)

As Attorney General of Arizona, Mayes joined 13 other states in a lawsuit filed on Thursday, February 13, 2025 against [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], arguing that the authority the [[white-house|White House]] granted Musk and the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] was unconstitutional.[4] The suit, brought by Democratic attorneys general from states including Arizona, Michigan, and Rhode Island, targeted the magnitude and scale of Musk's power over the federal workforce, agency structures, and sensitive government data.[4]

In a statement, Mayes said: "The founders of this country would be outraged that, 250 years after our nation overthrew a king, the people of this country—many of whom have fought and died to protect our freedoms—are now subject to the whims of a single unelected billionaire."[4]
