# Frederick Dodson

Frederick Dodson is an author cited in Badlands Media's [[the-werewolf-game|Werewolf Game]] series for his book *Levels of Heaven and Hell*.[1]

The passage quoted challenges sceptics of [[hell|hell]]'s existence by asking whether the ancients of Japan, America, Greece, Scandinavia, India, China, Siberia, Polynesia and South America — together with Christians, Muslims, Jews, ancient Roman scribes, the Celts, the ancient Iberians, the Maya and the Aztec — were all liars.[1] [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]] uses the quotation as an opening appeal to the cross-cultural ubiquity of hell traditions, a theme he later develops through the Tibetan Buddhist realm of hungry ghosts and the general claim that many religions hold hell to have levels.[1]

## Later citations

The Werewolf Game installment "What is Clown World?" cites Dodson twice.[2] It draws on *Levels of Heaven and Hell* for the claim that the dominant hue of the [[astral-plane|astral plane]] is the color red, which the Badlands author says matches his own experience — a point he links to red being a sacred color of the [[koryos|Koryos]] and a prominent color of the circus.[2] The same article also references Dodson's *Lost Secret of Ancient German* in support of claims that red-haired albino figures recur across cultures, including Maori accounts of red-haired albino giants who ate children.[2]

## Language research

The installment "Our Vast, Undiscovered History" describes Dodson as a multimillionaire success coach and language researcher who has published many books using the [[ancient-german-language-theory|Ancient German language]] to make accurate predictions about people, culture and geography.[3] It quotes the first chapter of his *The Secret History of Polynesia*, in which he recounts a roughly 40-kilometre bike tour from Kaikohe to Horeke in New Zealand and says he knew in advance the ride would be mostly downhill because the German *hohe* means "heights," adding that he could ride around the country predicting rivers, lakes, plains, glaciers, shrubs, forests and views simply from town names.[3] His *Levels of Heaven and Hell*, *Extraterrestrial Linguistics* and *The Secret History of Polynesia* are cited as containing hundreds of pages of comparisons between Ancient German and languages worldwide.[3]

The same article draws on *The Secret History of Polynesia* for the Maori tradition that a race of red-haired, pale-skinned cannibals occupied New Zealand before the Maori arrived, a claim used to link the islands to the [[aztecs|Aztecs]] and the [[koryos|Koryos]] through alleged trans-Pacific contact.[3]
