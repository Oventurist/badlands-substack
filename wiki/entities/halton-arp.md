# Halton Arp

Halton Arp was an American astronomer known for his catalogue of "peculiar galaxies" and for challenging the standard interpretation of galactic redshifts.[1] In his June 5, 2025 Badlands Media essay on climate science, [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] invokes Arp's work against the claim that the redshifts of galaxies prove the universe is dying — or, inversely, that it had a starting point "in time" some 13.6 billion years ago.[1]

Ehret's argument is that the universe, like the biosphere, is best understood as an open, anti-entropic system rather than a closed one tending toward heat death, and he refers readers to his own Rising Tide Foundation study "A Look Into Halton Arp's 'Peculiar Galaxies' and its Implications for the World We Live In" for the astronomical case.[1] Arp's redshift dissent thus functions in the essay as cosmological support for the same open-system paradigm underlying [[astroclimatology]] and [[vladimir-vernadsky|Vladimir Vernadsky]]'s [[noosphere]] concept.[1]
