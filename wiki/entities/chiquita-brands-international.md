# Chiquita Brands International

**Chiquita Brands International** is one of the world's largest banana companies and the modern successor to the [[united-fruit-company|United Fruit Company]].[1]

## 2024 Florida verdict over AUC payments

On Monday, June 10, 2024, a jury in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida found Chiquita Brands International liable for making payments that funded a paramilitary group in Colombia.[1] The lawsuit argued that the company should be held responsible for the murders of eight individuals by the [[autodefensas-unidas-de-colombia|Autodefensas Unidas de Colombia]] (AUC), a group designated a terrorist organization by the United States and known for its violence in the South American country before its disbandment in 2006.[1]

The jury in the civil case ordered Chiquita to pay the victims' families a total of $38.3 million.[1] The verdict followed the company's 2007 guilty plea to making more than 100 payments to the AUC totaling over $1.7 million, though a source told the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] the payments had been made under threat of violence.[1] The jury determined that "Chiquita knowingly provided substantial assistance to the AUC to a degree sufficient to create a foreseeable risk of harm to others," and that the company failed to "act as a reasonable businessperson would have acted under the circumstances."[1]

## Origins as United Fruit

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], drawing on a February 7 episode of [[breaking-history|Breaking History]] with [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] and researcher War Hamster, traced the company's lineage to [[minor-cooper-keith|Minor Cooper Keith]]'s Central American railroad and banana operations and the 1899 merger with the Boston Fruit Company that created United Fruit.[1] After being exposed for its [[cia|CIA]] connections and related activities in the 1980s, United Fruit rebranded itself as Chiquita Banana.[1] The Badlands commentary held that the company remained "involved in some pretty dark stuff in Central America," with the 2024 verdict offered as evidence.[1]
