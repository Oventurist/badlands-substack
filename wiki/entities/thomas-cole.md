# Thomas Cole

Thomas Cole was an American painter, a direct student of [[benjamin-west|Benjamin West]] and a collaborator of [[samuel-morse|Samuel F. B. Morse]], and one of the thirty leading talents present at the inception of the [[national-academy-of-design|National Academy of the Arts of Design]] alongside Asher B. Durand, Frederick Agate and Martin E. Thompson.[1]

## Work

In Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], Cole is credited with mastering the copying of nature in high fidelity while rarely producing a landscape that did not convey a higher idea transcending the literal limits of a natural scene.[1] His series on the course of life depicts the journey of a man from infancy through the wonders, lessons and turmoil of adolescence, adulthood, maturity and ultimately death; Ehret argues Cole did this not out of a romantic impulse but to bring into his audience's minds the profound truths of mortality and deeper reflection on the purpose of life.[1] He is treated as a founding figure of the [[hudson-river-school|Hudson River School]], the movement that emerged out of Morse's academy.[1]
