# Bob Marley

Bob Marley appears in Badlands Media's essay "Heroes of the Great Awakening" as one of several cultural figures whose deaths the author places within a pattern of suppressed dissent.[1] Writing about the 1960s and the decades that followed, the piece states that "beyond the assassination of the Kennedys, John Lennon, Bob Marley, MLK, and others were also killed in a seeming attempt to stop the spread of awareness in America."[1]

The article offers no separate evidentiary discussion of Marley's death; he is invoked alongside [[john-lennon|John Lennon]] and [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr.]] as an example of the "culture creator" whose generational reach, in the author's argument, made him intolerable to an entrenched power structure that "do their best—and worst—work in the shadows."[1] The essay's broader claim is that popular artists and unifying public figures pose a distinct kind of threat to the [[deep-state|Establishment]], because cultural influence spreads awareness faster and more durably than conventional politics.[1]

Marley's inclusion supports the article's framing of the present-day [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]] as a continuation of an earlier one: the author argues that history "rhymes," and that the songs, articles and videos of the current era are the successors to the protest culture of the 1960s and 1970s.[1]
