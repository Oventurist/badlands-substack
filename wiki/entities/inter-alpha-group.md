# Inter-Alpha Group

The Inter-Alpha Group is a consortium of European banks described by Matthew Ehret as a Rothschild creation established in 1971 to usher in a new age of globalization.[1]

Its founding coincided with [[george-shultz|George Shultz]]'s orchestration of the removal of the U.S. dollar from the gold-reserve, fixed exchange rate system in the same year.[1] Together these two 1971 developments, in Ehret's telling, opened the paradigm of consumerism, post-industrialism and deregulation that hollowed out the productive economies of the West.[1]

## Rothschild's stated aim

Ehret writes that the Inter-Alpha Group was formed in 1971 under the umbrella of the Royal Bank of Scotland and that it today controls upwards of 70% of the global financial system.[2] He cites a 1983 speech by [[jacob-rothschild|Lord Jacob Rothschild]] stating that "two broad types of giant institutions, the worldwide financial service company and the international commercial bank with a global trading competence, may converge to form the ultimate, all-powerful, many-headed financial conglomerate."[2] Ehret reads this policy as demanding the destruction of the sovereign nation-state system and the imposition of a new feudal structure of world governance through control of the money system.[2] Its founding coincided with [[richard-nixon|Richard Nixon]]'s destruction of the fixed-exchange-rate [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]], together inaugurating what Ehret calls [[post-industrial-monetarism|post-industrial monetarism]].[2]
