# Chip Roy

Chip Roy is a Republican member of the United States House of Representatives and the sponsor of the [[save-america-act|SAVE America Act]].[1]

## SAVE America Act

Roy introduced the SAVE America Act (H.R. 7296) on January 30, 2026, and it was referred to the House Committee on House Administration.[1] The bill would amend the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act of 1993]] to require documentary proof of US citizenship to register to vote in federal elections, listing acceptable documents such as a US passport or a REAL ID–compliant identification indicating citizenship.[1]

Although no final House vote had occurred as of early February 2026, House leadership moved toward floor consideration, and Roy indicated the chamber would vote that week.[1] If passed by the House, the measure would still require Senate consideration before reaching the President's desk.[1]

## June 2026 rule revolt

Roy was a leading figure in the late-June 2026 Republican rebellion that defeated the House procedural rule 224-198 and froze most floor business before the July 4 recess.[2] He cited Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]]'s broken promise to hold a vote on the House-passed immigration bill HR 2 before recess as his main reason for opposing the rule, while other rebels demanded action on defense policy amendments, pensions for Delphi auto parts retirees, and the SAVE America Act itself.[2] The impasse forced leadership to cancel the annual [[pentagon|Pentagon]] policy bill and [[state-department|State Department]] appropriations work; see [[june-2026-house-rule-failure|the rule failure]].[2]

## Position on the One Big Beautiful Bill (July 2025)

In a July 2, 2025 appearance on Fox News's "The Will Cain Show," Representative Chip Roy (R-Texas) said the [[medicaid|Medicaid]] cuts in the Senate-passed [[one-big-beautiful-bill-act|"big, beautiful bill"]] were "a bit better" than he had originally anticipated, while adding that he still had "concerns."[3] "I will note that I have now gotten a little bit more information on some of the Medicaid stuff that I feel like it's a little bit better than I originally anticipated, but I still have concerns," he told host Will Cain.[3]

Roy said he had voted against the rule in committee "because I wasn't at a place where I felt like we should advance the product last night," adding that he remained in that position and was still working through the bill, which members had received only the previous day.[3] His stance came as the [[us-house-of-representatives|House]] began a procedural vote on the measure with Republican leaders able to afford only three defections.[3] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded by asking what mandate Roy could claim relative to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s.[3]

## COVID-19 vaccine liability bill (March 2024)

On March 5, 2024, Roy introduced legislation that would retroactively strip COVID-19 vaccine manufacturers of the liability protections conferred by the [[prep-act|Public Readiness and Emergency Preparedness Act]], enabling Americans injured by the shots to sue the companies.[4] The bill states that "no federal law ... may make the manufacturer of a COVID-19 vaccine immune from suit or liability, or limit the liability of such a manufacturer," with respect to claims for loss arising from the administration or use of a COVID-19 vaccine.[4] In a statement, Roy said that "millions of Americans were forced to take a COVID-19 shot out of fear of losing their livelihoods and under false pretenses," and that many who suffered injury had been afforded little recourse.[4] Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that if the bill passed it would not only deliver justice to the injured but also undercut future use of what he called the "virus-vaccine Hegelian dialectic."[4]
