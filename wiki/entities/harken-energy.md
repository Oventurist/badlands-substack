# Harken Energy

**Harken Energy** is a Texas oil company that in the late 1980s acquired the ailing oil business in which [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] was a major stockholder and chairman, following several failed ventures.[1] The [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] material quoted in Badlands Media commentary describes Harken as "another foreign-influenced operation."[1]

## BCCI connections

The Wall Street Journal commented in 1991 that "the mosaic of BCCI connections surrounding Harken Energy may prove nothing more than how ubiquitous the rogue bank's ties were," but added that the number of [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]]-connected people who had dealings with Harken — all since George W. Bush came on board — raised the question of whether they masked an effort to cozy up to a presidential son.[1]

## Bahrain drilling contract

Further hints of cronyism emerged in 1990 when the inexperienced company won a major contract to drill in the Persian Gulf for the government of Bahrain.[1] Time magazine reporters Jonathan Beaty and S.C. Gwynne, in their book *The Outlaw Bank*, concluded that [[khalid-bin-mahfouz|Khalid bin Mahfouz]], or other BCCI players, must have had a hand in steering the drilling contract to the president's son.[1]

## Interpretation

The article characterizes Harken Energy as "the 'Burisma' for the [[bush-family|Bush family]]" — a corporate vehicle through which George W. Bush allegedly took payments and laundered money on behalf of his father, with [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] cast as "the original Big Guy."[1] It further asserts that Harken was a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] front company with deep BCCI connections.[1]

## Assessment

The contract and ownership facts derive from quoted journalism; the front-company and money-laundering characterizations are the author's interpretation. Badlands Media notes that contributor articles represent the opinions of their authors.[1]
