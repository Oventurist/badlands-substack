# Kristi Noem

Kristi Noem is the Secretary of the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] in the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]], with responsibility for the mass deportation program carried out by [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] and [[customs-and-border-protection|CBP]].[1]

## March 2026 Senate testimony

On March 3, 2026, Noem faced sharp bipartisan criticism during a hearing of the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]] over the administration's mass deportation operations and controversial departmental spending.[1] She testified for nearly five hours in a session that Badlands contributor Ashe in America described as combative, with protesters removed from the room and senators from both parties grilling the Secretary.[1]

Noem defended aggressive ICE and CBP activity in Minnesota, including [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]], during which two U.S. citizen protesters — Renee Good on January 7 and Alex Pretti on January 24 — were fatally shot amid clashes in Minneapolis and St. Paul. She blamed "violent protesters" for the chaos, while Democrats accused officers of excessive force and criticized Noem for labeling the victims "domestic terrorists."[1]

## Spending controversies

Lawmakers from both parties criticized the department for a $220 million advertising campaign featuring Noem herself, and for a $143 million no-bid contract awarded to a company created only eleven days earlier, which subcontracted work to a firm tied to the husband of a former top DHS spokeswoman.[1] Noem denied any personal involvement in the contracts and defended the ad spending as effective.[1]

Senator [[thom-tillis|Thom Tillis]] (R-NC) called for her resignation, citing misplaced priorities, and delivered what he framed as a performance review during which the Secretary was not permitted to respond.[1] Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]]'s critical posture was also noted as a surprise.[1]

## Analysis

Ashe in America framed the hearing as a study in contrasts within the administration: Noem, who reports to [[donald-trump|Trump]], absorbed bipartisan evisceration from what the author called the campaign class, while border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]], who also reports to Trump, operated in the field and was repeatedly cited approvingly by the same senators rebuking Noem. Throughout, immigration enforcement continued unimpeded.[1]

## Fifth Circuit detention ruling (February 2026)

Following the [[fifth-circuit-court-of-appeals|Fifth Circuit]]'s February 2026 ruling that the government may detain certain "applicants for admission" without bond during removal proceedings, Noem said courts had ordered releases based on claims that [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] was breaking the law, and described the decision as supporting the department's position.[2]

## Replacement at DHS and move to Shield of the Americas (March 2026)

On March 6, 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that Noem would be replaced as Secretary of Homeland Security by Oklahoma Senator [[markwayne-mullin|Markwayne Mullin]], effective March 31, 2026.[3] Trump said Noem "has served us well, and has had numerous and spectacular results (especially on the Border!)," and that she would move to become Special Envoy for [[shield-of-the-americas|The Shield of the Americas]], a new Western Hemisphere security initiative to be announced in Doral, Florida.[3]

Badlands writer [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] questioned whether the reshuffle amounted to a rugpull of the [[save-america-act|Save America Act]] by removing a Republican senator from the chamber, noting the comic possibility of the bill reaching a floor vote timed to March 31.[3] He also described a hostile online exchange in which a reader accused him of being too emotional to be objective and of being disappointed that Noem was not truly fired, before deleting the posts and unfollowing.[3]

## 2025 deportation flights contempt probe

As then-Homeland Security Secretary, Noem was among the senior officials whom U.S. District Judge James Boasberg found probable cause to believe had willfully violated his temporary restraining order directing March 2025 deportation flights to El Salvador to turn around, under the administration's invocation of the Alien Enemies Act.[4] In April 2026 a divided D.C. Circuit panel ordered Boasberg to end the resulting criminal contempt investigation, calling it a clear abuse of discretion that had reached a legal dead end.[4]

## Democratic demand for removal (February 2026)

In February 2026, minority leaders [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] and [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] attached ten conditions to [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] funding negotiations — covering warrants, agent masks and identification, sensitive locations, racial profiling, use of force, oversight, detention standards, body cameras, and uniform standards — and called for Noem to be removed from her position as Secretary of Homeland Security.[5]

## Operation Metro Surge arrest milestone (January 2026)

On January 19, 2026, Noem announced that the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] had made more than 10,000 arrests under [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]] in Minnesota.[6] "We have arrested over 10,000 criminal illegal aliens who were killing Americans, hurting children and reigning terror in [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]] because [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] and [[jacob-frey|Jacob Frey]] refuse to protect their own people and instead protect criminals," she said, adding that DHS law enforcement had arrested 3,000 criminal illegal aliens in the previous six weeks, including "vicious murderers, rapists, child pedophiles, and incredibly dangerous individuals."[6] She was named as a defendant in the federal lawsuit filed on January 12, 2026 by Minnesota Attorney General [[keith-ellison|Keith Ellison]] and the cities of Minneapolis and [[saint-paul|Saint Paul]] challenging the operation's tactics.[6]

## Termination of Somali TPS (January 2026)

In January 2026 the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]], under Noem, ended [[temporary-protected-status|Temporary Protected Status]] for Somali nationals, requiring those currently protected to depart the United States by March 17.[7] Noem stated that "temporary means temporary," citing improved country conditions in [[somalia|Somalia]] that no longer meet the statutory requirements for TPS, and asserted that continued protection is contrary to U.S. national interests under an "Americans first" approach.[7]

TPS for Somalia was originally designated in 1991 because of ongoing civil war and was most recently extended by the Biden administration in September 2024.[7] USCIS data indicated 2,471 Somali nationals held TPS with 1,383 applications pending, and an estimated 600 beneficiaries residing in [[minnesota|Minnesota]].[7]


## Response to the Minneapolis ICE shooting

As Secretary of Homeland Security, Noem defended the [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] agent who fatally shot [[renee-nicole-good|Renee Nicole Good]] in south Minneapolis on January 7, characterizing the shooting as self-defense.[8] She announced the deployment of hundreds of additional federal officers to Minnesota to support ongoing enforcement operations, a move local leaders rejected as they disputed the federal account of the shooting.[8]

## South Dakota antisemitism edict

As governor of South Dakota, Noem issued an antisemitism measure titled "Ensuring the Security of God's Chosen People."[9] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the title a horrifying slight against Christians, arguing that in the New Testament Jesus states that the "Chosen People" are those who choose to believe in Him rather than Jews as an ethnic category, and noted that there are approximately 345 Jewish people living in South Dakota.[9] He grouped the edict with measures in roughly 40 states — mostly Republican-controlled — that cite the [[international-holocaust-remembrance-alliance|International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance]] definition to classify criticism of the government of [[israel|Israel]] as hate speech, a template he said [[project-esther|Project Esther]] would extend to federal policy.[9]

## State antisemitism legislation

Badlands commentary on the [[antisemitism-awareness-act|Antisemitism Awareness Act]] cited Noem, as governor of South Dakota, among the Republican governors who adopted the [[international-holocaust-remembrance-alliance|IHRA]] definition of antisemitism at the state level, alongside counterparts in Texas, Arkansas, Virginia, and Florida.[10] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Noem titled her state legislation the "God's Chosen People Act," which he criticized as implying that Christians are not God's chosen people despite scripture, and cited her as an example of celebrated GOP figures serving foreign lobby interests.[10]

## Impeachment infighting among House Democrats (December 2025)

In December 2025 House Democrats split over attempts to impeach Trump administration Cabinet members, after articles of impeachment were filed against Health Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] and Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]].[11] House Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] dismissed the effort, telling Axios: "You know what I'm focused on? Making sure that the American people don't have their health care ripped away from them."[11] Representatives [[pete-aguilar|Pete Aguilar]] of California and [[katherine-clark|Katharine Clark]] of Massachusetts joined Jeffries in a joint statement denouncing the moves, arguing the process would require an immense amount of work to succeed.[11] Badlands contributor [[canncon|CannCon]] mocked the episode with a quip about aliens and lizard-people living among us.[11]

## Purse theft (April 2025)

Noem's purse was stolen while she was out having lunch with her family on Easter 2025, in what authorities described as part of a series of thefts in Washington, D.C.[12] A first arrest was made of an illegal immigrant with an extensive criminal history believed tied to the theft, and hours later a second suspect — the alleged accomplice — was apprehended in Miami on Sunday.[12] A Secret Service spokesperson said "the individual is believed to be a co-conspirator in those incidents with the primary defendant who is accused of committing a robbery against Secretary of Homeland Security Kristi Noem."[12] The accomplice's identity was not released, and officials said the suspect was being held on an immigration detainer while charges were finalized.[12]

## Antifa terrorist designation roundtable

At an October 2025 [[white-house|White House]] roundtable with right-wing media figures, Noem joined [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] and other administration officials in discussing whether to designate [[antifa|Antifa]] a [[foreign-terrorist-organization|foreign terrorist organization]].[13] As Homeland Security Secretary she compared Antifa to designated terrorist groups such as [[hamas|Hamas]] and ISIS, stating that they are equally dangerous and aim to destroy the United States.[13]

## Los Angeles press conference and the Padilla incident (June 2025)

On Thursday, June 12, 2025, Noem held a press conference in Los Angeles to discuss protests in the city over President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s immigration crackdown and the federal immigration raids that triggered them.[15] Democratic Senator [[alex-padilla|Alex Padilla]] of California interrupted the event, announcing "I am Senator Alex Padilla. I have questions for the secretary," and was shoved, forced to the ground and handcuffed by security agents after being ushered from the room.[15] Video shared by Padilla showed three agents pushing him down and cuffing his hands behind his back; Reuters said it could not immediately verify the footage.[15] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] defended the agents, arguing that Padilla was captured on camera interfering with [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] agents and resisting arrest.[15] The episode is covered further at [[2025-los-angeles-immigration-raids-and-national-guard-deployment|the 2025 Los Angeles immigration raids and National Guard deployment]].[15]

## Promotion of the REAL ID deadline (April 2025)

As Homeland Security Secretary, Noem publicized the approaching May 7, 2025 [[real-id|REAL ID]] deadline, after which standard driver's licenses would no longer suffice for domestic air travel by passengers aged 18 and older.[14] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]], describing her as an "Instagram influencer," mocked her accompanying suggestion that the credential would also fix American elections, responding that "voter ID is a panacea and a talking point that does not fix fake elections."[14]


## Insurrection Act Assessment (2025)

As Homeland Security Secretary, Noem was tasked alongside Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] with evaluating whether to recommend invoking the [[insurrection-act|Insurrection Act of 1807]] in response to immigration at the southern border, with an announcement expected in late April 2025.[16]

## Confirmation hearing scheduling (January 2025)

Badlands Media noted that South Dakota Governor Kristi Noem's confirmation hearing was scheduled for 9:00 AM on January 17, 2025, following the previous day's hearings for Doug Burgum and Lee Zeldin.[17]

## January 2025 actions as Homeland Security Secretary

In one of her first official appearances as Secretary of Homeland Security, Noem attended President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s signing of the [[laken-riley-act|Laken Riley Act]] on January 29, 2025, alongside Republican senators who championed the measure and Democratic Senator [[john-fetterman|John Fetterman]].[18]

On Tuesday, January 28, 2025, Noem announced the revocation of [[temporary-protected-status|temporary protected status]] deportation protections for Venezuelan migrants, affecting more than 600,000 Venezuelan nationals, after former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had extended the designation for an additional 18 months shortly before Trump assumed office on January 20.[18]

## Venezuelan TPS terminations upheld

Noem ended a 2023 designation that had made 350,000 Venezuelans eligible for [[temporary-protected-status|Temporary Protected Status]] and attempted to revoke two extensions announced under the Biden administration that would have let all qualifying Venezuelans keep the status through October 2026.[19] A federal district judge in San Francisco blocked the attempt in the spring of 2025, but on October 3 the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] lifted that ruling, allowing the terminations to proceed in the [[2025-supreme-court-ruling-on-venezuelan-temporary-protected-status|2025 Supreme Court ruling on Venezuelan temporary protected status]].[19]
