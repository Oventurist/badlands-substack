# Venetian Empire

The Venetian Empire is cast by Matthew Ehret as the principal antagonist of the ecumenical alliance among Jewish, Christian, Muslim and Confucian civilizations in the 8th–10th centuries. Together with the Ultramontane Church, Venice is described as heir to the recently collapsed Roman oligarchy, hating the rise of the Carolingian Empire under [[charlemagne|Charlemagne]] and the Augustinian humanist educational and economic reforms of his reign.[1] The article calls Venice and the Byzantine Empire "that ugly center of spiritual pus," noting that Venice would later undo Byzantium in 1251.[1]

## Strategy and the Crusades

Ehret describes Venice operating under a primitive version of Samuel Huntington's Clash of Civilizations doctrine and exclusively by "divide to rule" tactics.[1] Venetian-steered wars with Islam made Mediterranean trade impossible and made it unsafe for Christian or Muslim merchants to move through each other's territories, which is why the [[khazaria|Khazarian]] northern corridor and the role of Jewish traders became indispensable.[1] After a couple hundred years of effort the oligarchy succeeded in breaking Charlemagne's unified kingdom into warring factions, and in 1095 Venice and the Ultramontane Papacy launched the First Crusade against Islam.[1] The trade routes established by [[radhanite-traders|Radhanite Jews]] were the first things destroyed in Europe by the Crusaders, organized by the new [[knights-templar|Templar]] cultists, who then seized that infrastructure to wage war.[1]

## Anti-Jewish laws and the Ghetto

In the late 10th century Jews were cut off from Khazaria as all east-west trade routes were taken over by Genoa and Venice.[1] Venice was the first state to ban Jews from all international trade, with the Venetian Senate passing a law in 945 CE forbidding any ship bound for Asia from carrying a Jew.[1] Laws were soon passed across Europe at Venice's direction forbidding Jews from owning land, joining the trade guilds of weavers, dyers, carpenters or blacksmiths, or owning trade companies; the 1181 English Assize Laws forbade Jews from owning weapons, serving in the military or farming.[1] Anti-Jewish laws were imposed at unprecedented rates from the 11th through 16th centuries of Venetian global dominance and forever wars.[1]

The word "ghetto" itself began in Venice, where Jews were relegated to a small neighborhood of that name and excluded from normal professions, forced into dealing in old rags, pawn broking or money lending on behalf of nominally Christian oligarchical families who used them as HofJuden servants.[1] Historian Cecil Roth is quoted at length describing how the least lucrative and most unpopular branches of lending were forced upon Jews while non-Jewish capitalists lent to kings and magnates under cover of various devices, and how in Venice the Jewish community was tolerated down to the close of the eighteenth century only on condition that it maintain four loan-banks in the Ghetto.[1] Roth adds that the only other legally permitted professions were old-clothes dealing and the wholesale export trade to the Levant, which did not compete with Christian traders, and that any attempt by Jews to broaden their economic status was systematically blocked.[1]
