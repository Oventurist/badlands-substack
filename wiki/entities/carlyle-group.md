# Carlyle Group

The **Carlyle Group** is a Washington-based private equity firm that [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] joined in 1993 after losing his bid for a second presidential term.[1]

## Defense specialization and Bush involvement

Under the leadership of former officials including [[james-baker|James A. Baker III]] and former Defense Secretary Frank C. Carlucci, Carlyle developed a specialty in buying defense companies and doubling or quadrupling their value.[1] The former president became not only an investor in Carlyle but a member of the company's Asia Advisory Board and a rainmaker who drummed up investors.[1] Twelve rich Saudi families, including the Bin Ladens, were among those investors.[1]

In 2002 the Washington Post reported that "Saudis close to Prince Sultan, the Saudi defense minister ... were encouraged to put money into Carlyle as a favor to the elder Bush."[1] Bush retired from the company in October 2003, while Baker — who had lobbied U.S. allies to forgive Iraq's debt — remained a Carlyle senior counselor.[1]

## Interpretation

Badlands Media commentary presents Carlyle, alongside [[halliburton]], as one of the principal beneficiaries of the destruction and subsequent reconstruction of [[kuwait|Kuwait]], and as evidence that the [[bush-family|Bush family]]'s promotion of fear and war in the Middle East was ultimately about money.[1]

## Advising Prince Alwaleed on Citicorp (1991)

The New York Times reported in March 1991 that after weeks of deliberation [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] spent roughly half a billion dollars on a sizable piece of Citicorp "on the recommendation of American advisers" — the Carlyle Group, then relatively new, based in Washington, and led by people with little experience in the investment business but strong connections, especially in Washington.[3] The three figures named were founding partner [[david-rubenstein|David M. Rubenstein]], a domestic policy adviser to President [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]]; vice chairman [[frank-carlucci|Frank C. Carlucci]], Defense Secretary under [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]]; and adviser [[fred-malek|Frederick V. Malek]], described as close to President [[george-hw-bush|Bush]].[3]

Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues the Carlyle Group "was a CIA operation from the beginning," pointing to Carlucci's path from the number two post at the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] to the Pentagon and then to the top of one of the world's largest private equity funds.[3] He contends all three advisers' long histories with George H.W. Bush explain why Alwaleed was steered into Citicorp precisely as the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] scandal threatened the shadow government's dark-money channels.[3]

Carlyle's later record is presented in the same light: reporting in The Chronicle described private equity firms buying up mobile home parks and sharply raising rents on largely low-income residents, with Carlyle buying community land and "gouging" residents who could not afford to move, while the firm also made billions "investing in the defense and national security markets."[3] Lange argues such figures cultivate reputations as philanthropists rather than oppressors through high-profile donations.[3]
