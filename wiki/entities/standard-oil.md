# Standard Oil

Standard Oil was the Rockefeller petroleum monopoly dissolved by order of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|United States Supreme Court]] in 1911. In the Badlands corpus it is invoked as the historical template for what might be done about contemporary corporate concentration.

## The 1911 breakup

Erik Carlson's America Has a CIA Run Economy states that in 1911 the Rockefeller monopoly was broken up after the Supreme Court ruled that Standard Oil was in violation of the federal anti-trust laws, and that the company was divided into 34 separate entities.

## As a model for big tech

The essay raises the breakup immediately after describing how top-heavy the American market has become — the top five companies holding 33% of the [[sp-500|S&P 500]] weighting, and [[apple-inc|Apple]] standing ten times larger than the twentieth-ranked firm. Carlson asks whether antitrust dissolution "is this the path forward to limit the power and influence the top 1% of publicly traded American companies have on the American economy and society?"

Among his forward-looking expectations, the article predicts that "some mega corporations will be broken up into separate smaller companies," making the Standard Oil precedent a live element of the essay's prognosis rather than merely a historical aside.
