# Leonid Brezhnev

Leonid I. Brezhnev was the leader of the [[soviet-union|Soviet Union]] at the time of the December 1979 intervention in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]].[1]

## Afghanistan

Brezhnev attempted to prevent the murder of Afghan president [[nur-mohammad-taraki|Nur Mohammad Taraki]], but Taraki was killed in October 1979 on the orders of his deputy [[hafizullah-amin|Hafizullah Amin]] — a move that deeply rattled the Soviet leadership.[1]

In December 1979, KGB chief [[yuri-andropov|Yuri Andropov]] sent Brezhnev a handwritten memo warning that Amin's behind-the-scenes activities might indicate a political reorientation toward the West and that Amin was concealing his contacts with the American chargé d'affaires. Andropov and defense minister Dmitri Ustinov also cited the danger of American missiles being deployed in Afghanistan.[1]

Badlands Media notes that senior Soviet generals and officials had warned against getting mixed up in an Afghan civil war, pointing to the unhappy precedent of Vietnam, but that the leadership went ahead anyway largely because it could not think of a better alternative — hoping to change the Afghan leader, train up his army and police, and withdraw within months. Instead the Soviets were drawn into a quagmire and were soon looking for a way out.[1]

## Legacy in Badlands coverage

The article argues that the decision Brezhnev's leadership made was defensive — taken to protect the security of the Soviet southern frontier against Islamic unrest and American intrigue — and that the resulting decade-long war drained Soviet resources, contributed to the bankruptcy of the state, and preceded the union's dissolution in 1991.[1]
