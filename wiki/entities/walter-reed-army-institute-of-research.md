# Walter Reed Army Institute of Research

The **Walter Reed Army Institute of Research** (WRAIR) is the biomedical research arm of the [[united-states-army|United States Army]] and the developer of the Spike Ferritin Nanoparticle (SpFN) COVID-19 vaccine candidate that features prominently in Badlands Media's account of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s vaccine policy.[1]

## Pilot Bioproduction Facility

WRAIR operates the Pilot Bioproduction Facility, described by the Army as the Department of Defense's unique small-scale manufacturing facility for vaccines and biologics.[1] When SARS-CoV-2 emerged in early 2020, the facility was offline, emerging from a multi-year reconstruction intended to modernize, improve and expand its capabilities.[1] According to an Army release of May 6, 2021, PBF staff worked around the clock to complete the renovations, calibrate and qualify equipment to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration's current good manufacturing practices, and develop a manufacturing process for WRAIR's COVID-19 vaccine.[1]

## Spike Ferritin Nanoparticle vaccine

On December 21, 2021, Defense One reported that WRAIR scientists expected within weeks to announce a vaccine effective against COVID-19 and all its variants, including Omicron, as well as previous SARS-origin viruses.[1] The Army laboratory had received its first DNA sequencing of the virus in early 2020, and Walter Reed's infectious diseases branch decided very early to target not just the existing strain but all potential variants.[1] SpFN completed animal trials earlier in 2021 with positive results, and Phase 1 human trials wrapped up in December 2021, again with positive results undergoing final review, according to Dr. Kayvon Modjarrad, director of the institute's infectious diseases branch.[1] The candidate still required Phase 2 and Phase 3 trials.[1] Unlike existing vaccines, SpFN uses a soccer-ball-shaped protein with 24 faces, allowing scientists to attach spikes from multiple coronavirus strains to different faces.[1]

## Role in the Badlands "supervax" thesis

Badlands contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] characterises SpFN as a "supervax" and asks why nothing further was heard about it publicly, noting that the Army article remains published.[1] She speculates that the vaccine may have been distributed secretly, and that American service members who appeared to be receiving mandated COVID-19 shots may in fact have received the Army product — a possibility she frames as the enemy's plan to weaken the U.S. military backfiring.[1] These claims are speculative and are presented by the author as such.[1]
