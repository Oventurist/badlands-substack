# Maersk

Maersk is one of the largest container shipping companies in the world and became a central commercial actor in the Red Sea shipping crisis that unfolded at the end of 2023.[1]

## Red Sea suspension

At the turn of the year, Maersk suspended all container ship sailings in the Southern Red Sea after Iranian-backed Houthi militants attacked one of its vessels.[1] The suspension came only a day after the shipper had reportedly attempted to restart sailings through the critical waterway under the security umbrella of the United States' [[operation-prosperity-guardian|Operation Prosperity Guardian]].[1]

## The Maersk Hangzhou incident

[[centcom|US Central Command]] reported on X that the container ship Maersk Hangzhou was struck by a missile while transiting the Southern Red Sea, and that the vessel requested assistance from the US military.[1] Two Arleigh Burke-class destroyers, USS Gravely (DDG 107) and USS Laboon (DDG 58), responded, with the Gravely shooting down two anti-ship ballistic missiles fired from Houthi-controlled areas of [[yemen|Yemen]] toward the ships.[1] CENTCOM characterised the episode as the 23rd "illegal attack" by [[houthis|Houthi]] rebels on commercial vessels in the waterway since 19 November.[1]

In a separate account, CENTCOM described Houthi fighters approaching the Maersk Hangzhou in four small boats and attempting to board her; the vessel's private security team repelled the boarding attempt.[1] Attack helicopters from the nuclear-powered aircraft carrier USS Dwight D. Eisenhower and from USS Gravely answered the distress call, were fired upon by the militants, and in self-defence destroyed three of the boats, killing their crews, while a fourth boat escaped.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the Maersk attacks as evidence of the failure of Operation Prosperity Guardian and of the broader ineptitude of the Biden administration in the Middle Eastern theatre, arguing that a [[devolution|Devolution]]–National Essential Function reading explains why the US military appears unable to fully secure the corridor.[1]
