# Ray Epps

Ray Epps is an American man who became one of the most disputed figures associated with the events of January 6, 2021 at the United States Capitol. Video of Epps urging crowds toward the Capitol circulated widely, and questions about why he was not prosecuted alongside other participants made him a recurring subject of interest within the alternative-media and "Truth Community" ecosystem covered by [[badlands-media]].[1]

## Coverage in the Tucker Carlson footage release

In March 2023, [[tucker-carlson]] used his Fox News program to present material drawn from roughly 42,000 hours of Capitol surveillance video. Carlson's March 6 broadcast argued that several of the narratives advanced by the [[january-6-committee|January 6 committee]] were false, and he specifically presented what he described as evidence that Epps had perjured himself in sworn testimony before the committee — a direct contradiction of the committee's portrayal of Epps as a cooperative, patriotic whistleblower.[1]

The Epps segment aired alongside Carlson's claims regarding [[brian-sicknick]] and [[jacob-chansley]], and together those items formed the core of a broadcast that [[the-gateway-pundit]] characterized as "explosive."[1] Commentary aggregated in the [[badlands-news-brief]] treated the Epps material as an example of official narratives being contradicted by primary-source video, and argued that all of the footage should have been released publicly rather than routed through a single network.[1]

## Boasberg connection

Badlands commentary in March 2025 cited Epps's January 6 case as evidence of the political character of Judge [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]], who presided over it.[2] Writing amid the House Republican push to impeach Boasberg over his ruling blocking deportation flights, Ashe in America paired the Epps matter with Boasberg's handling of the Russiagate case of former FBI lawyer [[kevin-clinesmith|Kevin Clinesmith]], describing the judge as "a political animal" whose "bias is on the record."[2]

## Sentencing (January 2024)

On Tuesday, January 9, 2024, Epps was sentenced to a year of probation for joining the January 6, 2021 attack on the U.S. Capitol, having pleaded guilty in September 2023 to a misdemeanor charge.[3] A former Arizona resident who had been driven into hiding by death threats, he received no jail time and no restrictions on travel during probation, but was ordered to perform 100 hours of community service; prosecutors had recommended a six-month term of imprisonment.[3] Epps appeared remotely by video conference and was not present in the Washington, D.C. courtroom when Chief Judge [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]] imposed the sentence.[3] The sentencing took place in the same building where [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was attending the appeals court hearing on his claim of [[presidential-immunity|immunity from prosecution]] over charges that he plotted to overturn the results of the 2020 election.[3]

## Fox News lawsuit and impending charges (July 2023)

In a lawsuit filed Wednesday, July 12, 2023 against [[fox-news]] — see [[epps-v-fox-news]] — Epps accused former host [[tucker-carlson]] of defaming him, and his attorneys disclosed that the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] had notified him in May 2023 that it would seek to charge him criminally for events on January 6, 2021, two and a half years later.[5] The filing argued that "the relentless attacks by Fox and Mr. Carlson and the resulting political pressure likely resulted in the criminal charges."[5] Epps, then 62, had in March 2023 retained attorney [[michael-teter|Michael Teter]], formerly of [[perkins-coie]], who demanded that Carlson retract statements describing Epps as a January 6 government plant.[5] Reporting aggregated by [[zerohedge]] noted a text message in which Epps told his nephew, "I was in the front with a few others. I also orchestrated it."[5]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that on the night of January 5, 2021 Epps, in the company of [[john-sullivan|John "JaydenX" Sullivan]], shouted "Tomorrow, we're gonna go into the Capitol!" and that the crowd immediately began chanting "Fed! Fed! Fed!" — evidence, in her view, that public suspicion of Epps arose from his own conduct rather than from Fox's coverage.[5] She argued that despite the tape evidence against him Epps was removed from the watch list and became "the darling of the sham J6 Committee," with former Rep. [[adam-kinzinger|Adam Kinzinger]] coaching him through his testimony.[5] The charging news broke hours after Rep. [[troy-nehls|Troy Nehls]] pressed FBI Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] on the failure to charge Epps and Wray declined to comment beyond objecting to the suggestion that the [[fbi|FBI]] would incite an insurrection.[5]

## September 2023 disorderly conduct charge

On September 19, 2023, Epps was charged with a single count of disorderly or disruptive conduct in a restricted building or grounds, according to a filing from federal prosecutor Michael Gordon.[6] The court document stated that Epps did "engage in disorderly and disruptive conduct" at the Capitol with the intent to "in fact impede and disrupt the orderly conduct of Government business and official functions."[6] The penalty for the offense is a fine, imprisonment for not longer than one year, or both.[6] Reporting noted that Epps had travelled from Arizona to Washington, D.C. on January 5, 2021, where he was filmed encouraging people to go "into the Capitol," and was seen again protesting outside the Capitol the following day.[6] Forbes framed the charge as arriving after the absence of charges had led figures such as [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] to claim Epps was a government provocateur.[6]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded that Epps had received one count of disorderly conduct "after two and a half years of being protected by the government," citing again his text to his nephew reading "I orchestrated it."[6] She called the charge poor cover-up material, argued that Epps was widely understood to be an asset for [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] and the coup-plotters, and noted polling indicating that roughly two thirds of Americans believed the "Insurrection" had been orchestrated by the government.[6] Coming a day after former Capitol Police Chief [[steven-sund|Steven Sund]]'s testimony at the [[2023-house-capitol-security-oversight-hearing|House Capitol security oversight hearing]], the charging decision struck her as an afterthought meant to distract from the ongoing exposure of the [[fedsurrection|FedSurrection]].[6]

## Calls for exposure (2023 commentary)

The Badlands essay "Patsies of the CIA and FBI," published in March 2023 after the release of Capitol footage on [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s program, expressed the hope that more would be exposed and said Epps "should be exposed as being a CIA/FBI stooge," asking whether he would be thrown under the bus and answering that time would tell.[7] The essay situates Epps within its broader argument that [[january-6-capitol-protest|January 6]] was a staged show coordinated by government agencies to make [[donald-trump|Trump]] and his supporters seem like violent nut jobs — a narrative it says the Left absorbed easily because it is what they had been conditioned to believe.[7] See also [[intelligence-patsy|intelligence patsy]].[7]
