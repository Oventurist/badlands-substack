# Christopher Cooper

Christopher Cooper is a judge of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, appointed by former President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[1]

## Kennedy Center ruling, May 2026

On May 29, 2026, Cooper issued a decision ordering the removal of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s name from the [[john-f-kennedy-center-for-the-performing-arts|John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts]] and blocking a planned two-year closure of the venue for renovations.[1] He ruled that only [[congress|Congress]] has authority to change the institution's name, and that the center could not formally bear a name other than the one Congress established.[1] The case was brought by Rep. [[joyce-beatty|Joyce Beatty]] of Ohio, and the ruling halted a renovation plan approved by the center's Trump-appointed leadership.[1]

Trump responded on Truth Social by criticizing Cooper as an "Obama judge" and defending the renovation project, saying materials had already been ordered and that the building's rust, failing pipes, aging mechanical systems, pest problems, and structural issues required extensive work.[1]

## Federal workforce layoffs ruling (February 2025)

On February 20, 2025, Cooper declined for the time being to block downsizing efforts by the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration, including mass firings and buyout programs.[2] "Federal district judges are duty-bound to decide legal issues based on even-handed application of law and precedent — no matter the identity of the litigants or, regrettably at times, the consequences of their rulings for average people," he wrote.[2] Unions representing hundreds of thousands of federal workers had sued Trump and the heads of several government agencies, arguing that they were overstepping the executive branch's authority and that slashing agency size would cause "irreparable harm" through lower union dues revenue and lost bargaining power; they asked the court to declare the program unlawful and halt any similar program.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote for [[badlands-media|Badlands]] that such legal challenges matter because they fortify the authorities, since laws and rights mean nothing if they are not tested and affirmed within the system meant to protect them, and that restoring all three branches to their constitutional mandates would necessarily involve conflict among them.[2]
