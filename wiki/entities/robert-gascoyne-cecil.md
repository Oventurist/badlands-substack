# Robert Gascoyne-Cecil

Lord Robert Cecil, three times Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, appears in Badlands Media's history of Anglo-American relations as a candid parliamentary voice for British hostility to the American Union during the [[american-civil-war|Civil War]].[1]

## Statement on the American war

As the war raged, Cecil told the British Parliament that "the Northern States of America never can be our sure friends because we are rivals, rivals politically, rivals commercially."[1] Of the Confederacy he said the case was "entirely reversed": the population were an agricultural people who furnished the raw material of British industry and consumed the products manufactured from it, so that "every interest must lead us to cultivate friendly relations," and "when the war began they at once recurred to England as their natural ally."[1]

## Significance

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents the quotation as an unusually explicit statement of the structural logic underlying British policy toward the United States — a rivalry masked in later decades by the rhetoric of the [[anglo-american-special-relationship|Anglo-American special relationship]].[1] It is placed alongside [[lord-palmerston|Lord Palmerston]]'s 1860 threat to recognise Southern independence and the covert British military support for the Confederacy that was exposed by the end of the war and settled through the [[alabama-claims|Alabama Claims]] payment of $15 million in 1872.[1]
