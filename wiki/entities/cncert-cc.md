# CNCERT/CC

The National Computer Network Emergency Response Technical Team/Coordination Centre of China (CNCERT/CC) is [[china|China's]] national internet emergency response center, which describes itself as a non-governmental technical body protecting the country's cybersecurity.[1]

## December 2024 accusations against a US intelligence agency

On December 18, 2024, CNCERT/CC announced that it had identified and addressed two incidents of cyberattacks by a United States intelligence agency targeting Chinese technology firms in order to "steal trade secrets," dating back to May 2023.[1] The organization said the attacks targeted an advanced materials research unit and a major high-tech company specializing in intelligent energy and digital information, and it accused an unnamed U.S. intelligence agency of orchestrating the operations, which it said resulted in the theft of significant trade secrets.[1]

CNCERT/CC said the hacks underscored the risks posed by foreign cyber threats. The center had previously made similar accusations against the United States, reflecting Beijing's growing pushback against long-standing Western claims of Chinese cyber espionage.[1] The announcement coincided with guidance from the U.S. [[cisa|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]] urging senior government officials and political leaders to adopt end-to-end encrypted messaging platforms following confirmed breaches at major American telecom providers that officials blamed on Chinese hackers.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that she did not trust China, but did not doubt the premise that a U.S. intelligence agency would steal Chinese trade secrets.[1] Her principal argument was that the mutual accusations functioned as a "fake cyber showdown" whose net effect was plausible deniability for U.S. bureaucrats and elected officials: an ongoing hacking emergency justified moving government communications onto encrypted, auto-deleting platforms such as [[signal|Signal]] in tension with sunshine laws, during the final weeks before the January 2025 transfer of power.[1]
