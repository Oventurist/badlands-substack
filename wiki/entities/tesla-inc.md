# Tesla, Inc.

**Tesla, Inc.** is the American electric vehicle manufacturer led by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[1] In 2025 the company became the focal point of political protest against Musk's simultaneous role in the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]].[1]

## Target of the Tesla Takedown campaign

More than 200 coordinated protests took place across the United States on a Saturday in late March 2025 under the banner of the [[tesla-takedown|#TeslaTakedown]] movement, which urged supporters to divest from Tesla stock as a means of pressuring Musk.[1] Demonstrations were staged at Tesla retail and service locations in Washington, D.C., Chicago, Boston, and Rockville, Maryland, with organizers claiming thousands of participants nationwide.[1]

The protests were driven less by grievances with Tesla's products than by opposition to the budget cuts and federal workforce reductions carried out by the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] under Musk's direction, making the company a proxy target for Musk's governmental influence.[1]

## DOGEQUEST doxxing campaign (March 2025)

In March 2025 far-left hackers published detailed information on every Tesla owner in the United States, including home addresses, as part of a campaign to terrorise the company and its owner [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], then frontman of the Department of Government Efficiency.[2] The website [[dogequest|DOGEQUEST]], reportedly linked to far-left anarchists, listed addresses, email addresses and phone numbers for Tesla owners and offered to remove a customer's information only upon proof that they had sold their Tesla vehicle.[2] The group behind the site also published personal information for DOGE employees, and the address of FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] appeared on the site.[2]

## Las Vegas arson and owner doxxing (March 2025)

In March 2025 several Tesla vehicles were deliberately set on fire at a service center in Las Vegas in an attack involving Molotov cocktails and gunfire, which Las Vegas police described as a "targeted attack" involving political graffiti.[3] Around the same time a website named [[dogequest|Dogequest]] emerged, doxxing Tesla owners across the United States and encouraging them to sell their cars or remain on a public list.[3]

Authorities investigated the incidents, which generated extensive social media discussion of possible motives, widely understood as political hostility toward Tesla chief executive [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] arising from his role in the second Trump administration.[3]
