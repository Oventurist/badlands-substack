# Maryellen Noreika

Maryellen Noreika is a United States District Judge for the District of Delaware who presided over the federal criminal case against [[hunter-biden|Robert Hunter Biden]].[1]

## Role in the Hunter Biden case

Noreika drew national attention in July 2023 when she declined to rubber-stamp the plea agreement negotiated between Hunter Biden and [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] prosecutors, questioning its unusual immunity provisions and causing the deal to unravel in open court.[1]

In August 2023 Noreika granted a Justice Department request to dismiss without prejudice the two tax misdemeanor charges that had been filed against Biden in Delaware.[1] Dismissal without prejudice preserved the government's ability to bring charges elsewhere, specifically raising the prospect of a prosecution in Washington, D.C. or in the Central District of California.[1] The day after that ruling, three of Biden's defense attorneys from [[latham-and-watkins|Latham & Watkins LLP]] moved to withdraw as counsel.[1]

Commentators at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] treated the sequence — collapsed plea deal, venue-shifting dismissal, and the departure of counsel — as evidence that the case had escaped the containment its architects intended, reopening public scrutiny of material first catalogued in the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]] record.[1]

## Scrutiny ahead of the July 2023 plea hearing

Noreika was scheduled on 26 July 2023 to decide whether to accept [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s plea deal with prosecutors over two tax misdemeanours.[2] Ahead of that hearing, Republican lawmakers publicly called on her to toss out what they described as a "slap on the wrist" and "sweetheart" deal, after [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] whistleblowers who had investigated Biden for five years said he could instead have been charged with a raft of more serious tax and corruption crimes.[2] A legal filing revealed that the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] was itself considering delaying the finalisation of the agreement amid the controversy, which also encompassed allegations that Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] had lied to [[congress|Congress]] about the criminal tax probe.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the possibility that a federal judge might reject the deal as a sign that the tide could be turning on justice in America.[2]

## The July 26, 2023 hearing

At the hearing itself, Noreika deferred [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s attempt to plead guilty to two federal tax misdemeanors, citing concerns about the "broad" immunity prosecutors had afforded him and the "unique" structure of the arrangement, which she described at points as potentially "unconstitutional" and as lacking "legal precedent."[3] The proceeding ran for hours and included multiple dramatic recesses before Biden entered a not guilty plea.[3] Noreika repeatedly asked counsel on both sides whether any precedent or authority supported the agreement and was told each time that there was none, stating, "I cannot accept the plea agreement today."[3]

She directed both the defense and federal prosecutors to file additional paperwork justifying the legal legitimacy of the plea deal and of the separate agreement to dismiss a weapons felony, and ordered the parties to clarify language that might immunize Biden against future prosecution arising from his foreign investment and consulting work.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the judge's decision showed "her honor Judge Maryellen Noreika agrees with the people" who had objected to the deal's leniency.[3]
