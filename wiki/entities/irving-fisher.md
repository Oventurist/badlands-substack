# Irving Fisher

**Irving Fisher** was an American economist, statistician, inventor, eugenicist and progressive social campaigner.[1] In 1935 he proposed a system of [[full-reserve-banking|full reserve banking]] — also known as 100% reserve banking or the sovereign money system — as an alternative to [[fractional-reserve-banking|fractional reserve banking]].[1]

Under Fisher's proposal, banks would be barred from lending demand deposits and could lend only from time deposits, holding the full amount of each depositor's funds in cash and available for immediate withdrawal.[1] The proposal was never adopted by any national banking system, though its ideas resurfaced decades later in the [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]] study [[the-chicago-plan-revisited|The Chicago Plan Revisited]] and in the [[need-act|N.E.E.D. Act]].[1]
