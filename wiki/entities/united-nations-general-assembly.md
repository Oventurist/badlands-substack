# United Nations General Assembly

The United Nations General Assembly (UNGA) is the plenary deliberative organ of the United Nations, in which every member state is represented and heads of state and government deliver annual addresses during its high-level general debate in New York.[1]

## 2024 general debate and the Israel–Lebanon escalation

The September 2024 session became a focal point of the escalating war between Israel and [[hezbollah]]. [[benjamin-netanyahu]] traveled to New York to address the assembly at the same moment Israeli forces prepared to cross into southern [[lebanon]]; according to reports summarized by Badlands, Israel used the diplomatic window to assure officials in [[iran]] that new peace terms were about to be delivered, asking Iran to restrain Hezbollah in the interim — an assurance that proved false.[1]

Netanyahu's address itself was noted for his accusation that the assembly amounted to a body of antisemitic flat-earthers, and for his display of a map identifying his "Arab partners" under the [[abraham-accords]], with an assurance that those partners would back him as he confronted Iran.[1] The rhetorical effect was immediately contested from the same podium: foreign ministers claiming to speak for all fifty-seven nations of the [[organization-of-islamic-cooperation]] expressed indignation and pressed the assembly to demand that Israel define its "endgame," with Saudi foreign minister [[faisal-bin-farhan]] among those voicing exasperation.[1] Badlands read the exchange as evidence supporting [[douglas-macgregor|Colonel Douglas Macgregor's]] contention that the Abraham Accords framework was effectively dead.[1]

## Two-state solution declaration vote (September 2025)

On Friday, September 12, 2025, the General Assembly voted overwhelmingly to endorse a declaration outlining "tangible, timebound, and irreversible steps" toward a [[two-state-solution|two-state solution]] between [[israel|Israel]] and the Palestinians, ahead of a meeting of world leaders.[2] The seven-page declaration was the product of an international conference held at the UN in July, hosted by [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and [[france|France]], which the United States and Israel boycotted.[2] The resolution endorsing it received 142 votes in favor and 10 against, with 12 abstentions, coming ahead of a September 22 leaders' meeting on the sidelines of the high-level session at which Britain, France, Canada, Australia and Belgium were expected to formally recognize a Palestinian state.[2]

The declaration, endorsed by the 193-member body, condemns the October 7, 2023 attacks against Israel by [[hamas]] that triggered the war in Gaza, and also condemns Israeli attacks against civilians and civilian infrastructure in Gaza, siege and starvation, "which have resulted in a devastating humanitarian catastrophe and protection crisis."[2] US Representative to the UN [[morgan-ortagus|Morgan Ortagus]] dismissed the declaration as a "publicity stunt" and a "gift to Hamas," drawing sharp criticism from Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], who noted the terms she demanded were already contained in the [[new-york-declaration|New York Declaration]].[2]
