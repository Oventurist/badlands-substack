# Fordow Fuel Enrichment Plant

The Fordow Fuel Enrichment Plant is a fortified Iranian uranium enrichment site built deep within a mountain, making it effectively invulnerable to conventional munitions.[1] It became a central object of military and diplomatic attention during the [[june-2025-israeli-strikes-on-iran|June 2025 Israeli strikes on Iran]].[1]

## Role in the June 2025 campaign

As [[israel]] continued its assault on Iranian targets, Israeli officials warned that they lacked the capability to destroy Fordow alone, since the underground facility lies beyond the reach of Israel's conventional weapons; United States forces in the region, by contrast, possess the bunker-busting bombs and bomber aircraft required to strike it.[1] Israeli officials accordingly asked the Trump administration to join military operations targeting [[iran|Iran's]] nuclear program, including a strike on Fordow, according to reporting by [[axios]] on June 14, 2025, citing two Israeli officials.[1]

An Israeli official told Axios that [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] had suggested in a recent conversation with Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] that he would consider joining the operation if needed, though a White House official denied that claim on June 13.[1] A second U.S. official confirmed that Israel had urged American participation but said the administration was not then considering involvement.[1] Israeli Ambassador to the U.S. [[yechiel-leiter|Yechiel Leiter]] told Fox News on June 13 that "the entire operation… really has to be completed with the elimination of Fordow."[1] An Israeli source told Axios that the U.S. was considering the request and that Israel hoped Trump would agree to it.[1]

Separately, nonproliferation and Iranian experts assessed that Israeli airstrikes on Iran's nuclear sites had damaged some aboveground research facilities and infrastructure but had not eliminated the thousands of centrifuges buried deep underground, nor the hundreds of pounds of near-weapons-grade material already produced — an assessment that underscored why Fordow in particular remained intact.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary questioned both the moral framing and the evidentiary basis of the escalation, arguing that decisions about who may justifiably be killed are being outsourced to television narratives.[1]

## June 2025 B-2 strike

Fordow was the target of the 37-hour B-2 Spirit mission code-named Operation Midnight Hammer in June 2025.[2] Seven bombers flew from Whiteman Air Force Base in Missouri and back, delivering fourteen 30,000-pound bombs on their targets according to the White House.[2] Because the enrichment halls sat deep beneath mountain rock, commentators noted that the strike produced no radiological contamination event, leaving only surface bore holes visible.[2] The Iranian foreign ministry subsequently conceded that the country's nuclear facilities had been "badly damaged" by the strikes.[2]

## April 2024 IAEA findings

For roughly fifteen years the most important clues about Iran's nuclear programme lay underground at Fordow, a factory built inside a mountain on the edge of Iran's Great Salt Desert and described as the heavily protected inner sanctum of the Iranian nuclear complex.[3] International inspectors visited the plant in February 2024, and their report — couched in the clinical language of a United Nations nuclear watchdog document — contained indications of alarming change.[3]

In factory chambers that had ceased producing enriched uranium under the 2015 [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|nuclear accord]], inspectors observed newly installed equipment producing enriched uranium at ever faster speeds and an expansion underway that could soon double the plant's output.[3] Fordow was also scaling up production of a more dangerous form of nuclear fuel — highly enriched uranium just short of weapons grade — while Iranian officials in charge of the plant began speaking openly about achieving "deterrence," suggesting Tehran had everything it needed to build a bomb if it chose.[3]

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] dismissed the coverage as a revival of the "he's got nukes in his toolshed" formula intended to ramp up fear and disable public discernment, arguing that Iran already possessed hypersonic missiles capable of penetrating the [[iron-dome|Iron Dome]] and would not need a warhead to strike Israel.[3] He characterised the reporting as groundwork for a preemptive strike that would invite retaliation.[3]
