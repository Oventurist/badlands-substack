# Development Finance Corporation

The U.S. International Development Finance Corporation (DFC) is the federal development finance agency that [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] designated in March 2026 as the vehicle for underwriting maritime trade through the Persian Gulf during the [[iran-war|U.S.–Iran war]].[1]

## Persian Gulf shipping guarantees

Trump announced that the United States would immediately provide political risk insurance and financial guarantees — at what he called "very reasonable" prices — for all maritime trade, especially energy shipments, transiting the Persian Gulf, delivered through the DFC.[1] He added that the U.S. Navy would escort tankers through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] "if necessary."[1]

The measure came after Iranian attacks and threats to close the strait triggered war-risk policies that froze tanker traffic, sent supertanker rates to all-time highs, and spiked oil volatility. Oil prices tumbled on the announcement as market fears of major supply disruption eased.[1]

## Role in the DR Congo minerals deal (2025)

According to Financial Times sources cited in April 2025, the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation was expected to underwrite portions of planned American investments under the prospective [[us-dr-congo-critical-minerals-deal|US–DR Congo critical minerals deal]], enabling U.S. firms to take a larger role in exploiting the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|DR Congo]]'s lithium, copper and cobalt deposits.[2]
