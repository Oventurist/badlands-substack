# US Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit

The United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit is a federal appellate court with jurisdiction over district courts in the western United States.[1]

## Executive Order 14251 ruling

In late February 2026, a three-judge panel of the Ninth Circuit vacated a lower court's preliminary injunction that had blocked implementation of [[executive-order-14251|Executive Order 14251]], the order excluding certain federal agencies from collective bargaining requirements under the Federal Service Labor-Management Relations Statute.[1]

The panel held that the unions challenging the order were unlikely to succeed on the merits of their claim that the order constituted retaliation violating the First Amendment, and that the injunction had therefore been wrongly imposed.[1]

## Asylum processing ruling and Supreme Court review (2025)

In 2025 the Ninth Circuit issued a ruling on the processing of asylum seekers at ports of entry that the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration argued "has already caused—and, if left in place, will continue to cause—'untold interference with the Executive Branch's ability to manage the southern border.'"[2] The administration petitioned the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] in July 2025, and on November 17, 2025 the justices agreed to review the decision.[2] Solicitor General [[d-john-sauer|D. John Sauer]], echoing Judge Bress's dissent below, told the Court that the Ninth Circuit's ruling "defies the plain text of the governing statutes," arguing that "an alien thus does not 'arrive in' the United States while he is still in Mexico."[2] The immigrant rights group and asylum seekers who brought the suit urged the justices to leave the ruling intact, warning that the government's position would render federal asylum-processing law "wholly inoperable at ports of entry."[2]
