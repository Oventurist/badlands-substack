# Project Veritas

Project Veritas is an American undercover-journalism organization founded by [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]]. In February 2023 it became the subject of intense scrutiny within independent media after O'Keefe delivered a resignation speech to the organization's staff, video of which circulated on Rumble and was highlighted in the Badlands News Brief of February 22, 2023.[1]

## 2023 leadership crisis

O'Keefe's departure followed a dispute with the organization's board of directors. Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] wrote that "the board lied to the staff, O'Keefe, and the public," and characterized the board's conduct as malfeasance indicating that the organization had "likely" been infiltrated, with pre-existing institutional weaknesses "eventually used to take it out."[1]

Deschamps drew two lessons from the episode. The first was that O'Keefe's decision to speak publicly against what Deschamps called the fraud pushed by the board limited the damage; had he stayed silent, in this reading, the board "would have smeared a good man and people would have fallen for it to a certain extent," making courage in the face of fraud a practical harm-reduction measure.[1] The second concerned governance: Deschamps suspected the board was populated by "professional board members," whom he described as often untrustworthy and susceptible to compromise, and who in turn compromised the organization.[1]

## Governance critique

Deschamps further argued that Project Veritas's bylaws did not explicitly define the values and procedures of the organization and contained no oaths binding board members and staff.[1] Properly drafted oaths combined with explicit bylaw provisions, he contended, would have given O'Keefe greater power to prevent the board from "ruining the organization he founded."[1] He framed the point as a general legal maxim — "Rights not invoked have no power" — noting that as founder O'Keefe possessed the right to define precisely what his organization was to embody.[1] The broader warning was that many organizations "doing amazing work for humanity" carry similar exploitable weaknesses that ought to be examined and patched.[1]

## Pfizer myocarditis disclosure (March 2023)

On March 16, 2023, Project Veritas published what it described as confidential [[pfizer|Pfizer]] documents revealing that the pharmaceutical company had "evidence" suggesting an "increased risk of myocarditis" following COVID-19 vaccination in early 2022, quoting the language: "There is evidence that suggests patients who receive a COVID-19 vaccine are at an increased risk."[2] The release, posted to X at 10:02 PM that evening, drew roughly 7,150 reposts and 14,300 likes and was carried as a lead item in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[2]

Badlands contributor [[absolute-1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] responded that the documents showed "they knew" the myocarditis risk was elevated and released the product anyway, describing those responsible as "sick" and "evil" and predicting that many behind the decision would "soon face justice."[2]

## Ashley Biden diary litigation

In December 2023, U.S. District Judge [[analisa-torres|Analisa Torres]] of the Southern District of New York ruled that Project Veritas must turn over all documents in its possession detailing how it came into possession of the alleged diary of [[ashley-biden|Ashley Biden]] in the fall of 2020, adopting a special master's recommendation.[3] Torres rejected the organization's argument that journalistic non-disclosure privileges under the First Amendment shielded the records.[3]

The ruling opened the way for federal prosecutors to obtain more than 900 documents, with a government evidentiary filter team ordered to screen out attorney-client privileged material and deliver the rest to investigators by January 5.[3] The legal battle began in the fall of 2021, when federal agents executed search warrants at the homes of several Project Veritas employees, including founder and then-CEO [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]]; the organization argued the seizure violated its First Amendment rights as a press organization and sought return of the records.[3]

## February 2023 leadership shakeup

In February 2023 the organisation's founder and chief executive [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]] left the company he had built, after a group of his staff reportedly moved to oust him.[4] O'Keefe was first placed on paid leave while the board weighed removal options, amid employee dissatisfaction with his management and allegations that he wasted money and was "outright cruel" to staff, as reported from an internal memo by The Daily Beast.[4]

On Monday, February 20, 2023, O'Keefe told staff in an emotional fifteen-minute video that he had been stripped of his authority as CEO and removed from the board of directors.[4] He recalled a former board member's warning that "Project Veritas will never be stopped from the outside, it will only be because we stopped ourselves," and argued that the only material change in thirteen years of consistent conduct was that the organisation had just "broke the biggest story in our organization's history" — its [[pfizer|Pfizer]] exposé.[4] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] treated the removal as terminal for the organisation while predicting O'Keefe himself would continue.[4]

## Influence on Accuracy in Media

The Project Veritas hidden-camera model was carried into education reporting by [[accuracy-in-media|Accuracy in Media]], led by Veritas alumnus [[adam-guillette|Adam Guillette]], which in January 2023 released undercover video of employees in school districts around Columbus, Ohio describing how they mislead parents opposed to [[critical-race-theory|critical race theory]].[5] Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]], crediting "the footage procured by Project Veritas," argued that such recordings help dispel misplaced trust in an education system he characterised as an instrument of indoctrination.[5]
