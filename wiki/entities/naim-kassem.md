# Naim Kassem

Sheikh Naim Kassem is the deputy leader of [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], the Lebanese militant and political movement.[1] In July 2024 he gave a 40-minute interview to the Associated Press at the group's political office in Beirut's southern suburbs, in which he set out the conditions under which Hezbollah would halt its cross-border fighting with [[israel|Israel]].[1]

Kassem said the only sure path to a ceasefire on the Lebanon–Israel border was a full ceasefire in Gaza: "If there is a cease-fire in Gaza, we will stop without any discussion."[1] He characterized Hezbollah's participation in the Israel–Hamas war as a "support front" for its ally [[hamas|Hamas]], adding that "if the war stops, this military support will no longer exist."[1] He cautioned, however, that a partial de-escalation would leave matters ambiguous: if Israel scaled back operations without a formal agreement and full withdrawal from Gaza, "we can't answer (how we would react) now, because we don't know its shape, its results, its impacts."[1]

In the same interview Kassem disclosed that White House envoy [[amos-hochstein|Amos Hochstein]] had recently asked, via intermediaries, that Hezbollah press Hamas to accept the ceasefire and hostage-exchange proposal advanced by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] Kassem said Hezbollah had rejected the request, stating that "Hamas is the one that makes its decisions and whoever wants to ask for something should talk to it directly."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read Kassem's posture as strategic restraint rather than weakness, arguing that Hezbollah was "doing everything it can to avoid a direct conflict with Israel" because global sentiment had turned against Israeli prime minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], and that avoiding provocation was the surest way to deny him sympathy or rally-around-the-flag support.[1] The same commentary praised Kassem's refusal to act as an intermediary for Washington as a matter of "First Principles," and noted that the 22 nations of the [[arab-league|Arab League]], led by [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], had recently removed Hezbollah from their list of designated terrorist organizations, while [[russia|Russia]] had never so designated the group.[1]
