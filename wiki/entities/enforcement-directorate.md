# Enforcement Directorate

The Enforcement Directorate (ED) is the Indian government agency responsible for enforcing economic and foreign-exchange laws, including investigations into foreign direct investment that allegedly violates statutory guidelines.[1] The agency drew international attention in March 2025 when it conducted searches tied to the Indian operations of the [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]].[1]

## Bengaluru searches of Soros-linked entities

On Tuesday, March 18, 2025, the ED "conducted extensive searches on Open Society Foundations (OSF), promoted by US billionaire currency speculator and hedge fund operator [[george-soros|George Soros]]," an organisation the Bharatiya Janata Party has accused of acting against India's interests.[1] The raids took place in Bengaluru at OSF offices and at linked entities, and were described as the fallout of an inquiry into foreign direct investment sourced by OSF in alleged violation of Foreign Exchange Management Act guidelines.[1]

Reporting cited sources stating that the Open Society Institute (US) had been placed under the home ministry's prior reference category (PRC) since 2016 over allegedly undesirable activities, meaning OSI must secure prior ministry sanction before sending funds to FCRA-registered organisations in India.[1] Investigators reportedly found that the [[soros-economic-development-fund|Soros Economic Development Fund]], OSI's social impact investment arm, had provided funds to three Indian companies: Rootbridge Services Pvt Ltd, Rootbridge Academy Pvt Ltd, and ASAR Social Impact Advisors Pvt Ltd.[1]

## Framing in Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the ED action as one node in a broader multinational squeeze, arguing that "Soros is getting pinched and pincered by the Sovereign Alliance" and that the timing coincided with pressure on Soros in the United States following [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s election.[1] The commentary situated India's enforcement action alongside Russia's 2015 ban on the Open Society network and [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orban]]'s campaign against Soros-funded institutions in Hungary, presenting the ED raids as "yet another narrative that acts as a stepping stone in disclosure of how NGOs are used to launder money and topple governments."[1]
