# Sergey Naryshkin

Sergey Naryshkin is the head of Russia's Foreign Intelligence Service (SVR), the country's principal civilian foreign-intelligence agency.[1] In Badlands Media coverage he appears primarily as the official voice attributing alleged Western-directed sabotage plots inside Russia to British and American intelligence services.[1]

## Kursk nuclear plant allegations

Speaking at a meeting of intelligence and security principals of post-Soviet states held in Astana, Naryshkin alleged that British and American spies had helped Ukraine develop plans for blowing up the [[kursk-nuclear-power-plant|Kursk Nuclear Power Plant]], a scheme he said would have plunged Europe into a second radiation catastrophe.[1] His remarks were made public on a Monday in early October 2024, roughly two months after Ukrainian troops crossed into Russia's Kursk Region in August but were stopped short of the plant at Kurchatov.[1]

According to Naryshkin, "the planned terrorist attack included taking and mining the Kursk NPP," and had it been carried out "Europe would have faced an environmental and humanitarian disaster comparable to Chernobyl."[1] He further claimed that Western intelligence agencies, "primarily the British [[mi6|MI6]]," had systematically prepared Ukrainian sabotage and reconnaissance groups to stage provocations at a number of Russian nuclear power plants, and that British and Ukrainian operatives were developing an operation to blow up the power lines connecting nuclear plants to the Russian national energy grid.[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

Badlands writers treated Naryshkin's claims as corroboration of a longstanding thesis in the community: that terrorism functions as an industry substantially sustained by UK and US civilian intelligence agencies, which would otherwise lose their institutional rationale, since threats from foreign state militaries fall to military intelligence.[1]

## Call with CIA Director Ratcliffe (March 2025)

On Tuesday, March 11, 2025, Naryshkin, as chief of the Russian [[foreign-intelligence-service-of-russia|Foreign Intelligence Service (SVR)]], spoke by telephone with [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] amid peace talks over Ukraine, with the two endorsing a 30-day ceasefire.[2] The SVR press office told [[tass|TASS]] that the parties "discussed the issues of interaction of both intelligence agencies in areas of common interest and the settlement of crisis situations," and agreed "on maintaining regular contact between the SVR and CIA directors with the aim of facilitating international stability and security and reducing confrontation in relationships between Moscow and Washington."[2] The CIA declined to comment on the record.[2]

Axios reported that the call was intended in part to open a line of communication between the two services; it was the first such contact since President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took office on January 20, and followed by one day Ukraine's declared readiness to accept a ceasefire under the [[2025-jeddah-ceasefire-agreement|Jeddah agreement]].[2]

## Offer to meet Ratcliffe (January 2025)

Two days after [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] was confirmed by the US Senate as [[cia|CIA]] director, Naryshkin said he was ready to meet him, telling TASS on a Saturday in late January 2025 that Moscow is always ready for dialogue if Washington is interested.[3] "We are always ready for negotiations," he said in answer to a question about contact between the heads of the two agencies, adding that the services maintain contact through relevant officials: "We have an official representative in Washington, the Central Intelligence Agency has [one] in Moscow."[3] He had repeatedly signaled such readiness in recent years despite strained relations between the two capitals.[3]

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry paired the overture with the CIA's newly announced preference for the Wuhan lab-leak explanation of Covid-19, noting that the late Russian Lt. General [[igor-kirillov|Igor Kirillov]] had publicly said the virus originated in a US-funded bioweapons laboratory in Ukraine, and framed a prospective Naryshkin–Ratcliffe meeting as a channel through which such evidence might surface.[3] See [[2025-cia-lab-leak-assessment|the 2025 CIA lab leak assessment]].[3]
