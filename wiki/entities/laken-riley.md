# Laken Riley

Laken Riley was a 22-year-old nursing student who was murdered in Athens, Georgia, in February 2024. Her killing became one of the most politically consequential crimes of the 2024 United States election cycle because her assailant was an illegal alien who had been released into the interior of the United States by federal immigration authorities.[1]

## Background

Riley was an avid runner and a nursing school student at Augusta University. On the morning of February 22, 2024, she went for a jog on the University of Georgia (UGA) campus along a route she had run often without any prior incident.[1]

## Death

When Riley did not return from her jog, her roommates searched for her and called UGA police. Her body was discovered bludgeoned in a wooded area on the UGA campus. The following day, [[jose-antonio-ibarra|Jose Antonio Ibarra]], an illegal alien from Venezuela and a member of the [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] gang, was arrested and charged with her murder.[1]

## Trial and conviction

On Wednesday, November 20, 2024, an Athens judge found Ibarra guilty on all counts of murdering Riley after stalking her during her early morning jog. Ibarra faced life in prison without the possibility of parole.[1]

## Political significance

President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] drew sustained attention to the case during the 2024 campaign and met with Riley's parents. He was reported to be pleased by the guilty verdict, while noting that it would not make the pain any easier on the Riley family.[1]

Badlands Media commentary framed the case as evidence of deliberate federal misconduct rather than administrative failure, arguing that the [[joe-biden|Biden]]–[[kamala-harris|Harris]] administration had admitted Ibarra, arranged his transport to Georgia and issued him a work permit, and characterizing the outcome as "the intentional destabilization of American communities."[1]

## Biden's State of the Union remarks

Riley's name featured prominently in the March 7, 2024 [[2024-state-of-the-union|State of the Union address]], during which President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] mispronounced her name as "Lincoln" and described her alleged killer as an "illegal."[2] Biden later apologized for the word "illegal," saying he should have said "undocumented," a reversal that [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called "literal disinformation" on the grounds that [[jose-antonio-ibarra|Jose Ibarra]] had been well documented by police before the murder.[2]

Speaking at a rally in Rome, Georgia, outside Atlanta on Saturday, March 9, 2024 — his first campaign event after his Super Tuesday primary victories — [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] tore into Biden over the flub and over his border policies, having just met with Riley's family backstage.[2] Trump mocked the president for "mixing her up with the football coach," a reference to University of Southern California coach Lincoln Riley, and his campaign distributed posters bearing Riley's face with the phrases "Remember our angels" and "Say her name."[2] Ashe in America argued that the episode showed the administration had a messaging problem and had "wildly underestimated the level of American rage at their handling of the border crisis."[2]
