# Armenian Mafia

The **Armenian mafia** is a transnational organized crime network of Armenian origin operating across Europe, the Caucasus and the United States. In the Badlands Media analysis by Joe Lange, it functions as the criminal infrastructure layer of the narcotics corridor running from [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] through [[iran|Iran]] and [[armenia|Armenia]] into Europe, and is presented as directly connected to [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]]'s worldwide money-laundering operations.[1]

## Scope of operations

A summary cited in the article describes Armenia as a transit point on trafficking routes between Latin America, Turkey and Europe, as well as Iran, the UAE and Malaysia, involved particularly in the transit of cocaine and heroin.[1] The author argues that Armenia was permitted to develop nuclear weapons "under the radar" precisely because of its value as a heroin transit point into Europe.[1] The article also notes the existence of a large Armenian-American population in California with a substantial mafia contingent.[1]

## The 2020 Spanish and Catalan raids

Near the end of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s presidency, a coordinated multi-country raid took down a large portion of the network.[1] Citing HETQ and Catalan News, the article reports that Catalan and Spanish police launched a joint raid against an Armenian criminal gang on June 26, arresting 142 people suspected of involvement in drug and arms trafficking, tobacco smuggling, money laundering, sports betting fraud and other crimes committed across Europe.[1] Most of those arrested were citizens of Armenia, and police carried out 73 raids in towns across Catalonia and the rest of Spain.[1] According to OCCRP reporting cited in the piece, Spanish law enforcement considered this gang the biggest Armenian criminal organization operating in Europe; its leadership included six "thieves-in-law," among them the Shushanashvili brothers and Erik Djhaginyan.[1]

## The French operation

In January, some 200 officers from the French Gendarmerie, with on-the-field support from Europol, targeted suspects across France believed to belong to a Eurasian mafia group composed mainly of Armenian individuals engaged in large-scale poly-criminal activity including cigarette smuggling, extortion and money laundering.[1] Twenty-one suspects were arrested, including a believed high-ranking member of the group.[1] The sting followed a lengthy investigation supported by Spanish and German authorities, and similar coordinated raids occurred in Germany.[1] The author reads the international sequence as evidence of a genuine, largely unreported war on drugs driven by the Trump administration.[1]

## The cars-for-drugs modus operandi

Investigators noted that the suspects used a distinctive method in which cars served as a means of payment for further criminal actions — the exchange of drugs or cigarettes for vehicles.[1] The article stresses this as a direct link to Hezbollah, which laundered drug money in the United States through used car dealerships, and claims [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s "Cash for Clunkers" program assisted that laundering operation.[1] This convergence of method is offered as one of the article's recurring proofs that "everything is connected."[1]
