# Brian Auten

Brian Auten is an FBI Supervisory Intelligence Analyst who worked on [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] and the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller Special Counsel investigation]] and who was a central witness at the 2022 trial of [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]].[1]

## Role

Auten supervised analyst [[brittany-hertzog|Brittany Hertzog]] and brought her onto the Mueller Special Counsel investigation in the summer of 2017, where she was tasked with "looking into" the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]].[1] Auten also participated in the January 2017 interviews of Danchenko; at trial, [[john-durham|John Durham]] questioned Special Agent [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]] about whether Auten had discussed with Danchenko dossier report 105 and a section titled "Election Related Reporting About Paul Manafort."[1]

## Trial and aftermath

Durham was described as having put the [[fbi|FBI]] itself on trial through the agents he called, and Auten in particular was subjected to two full days of questioning, none of it friendly, during which the failures of Crossfire Hurricane and the Mueller Special Counsel's Office were aired.[1] It also emerged that Auten had been under investigation by the Office of the Inspector General for some time, and that he had been recommended for suspension for his role in the [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|Carter Page FISA]] applications.[1] The article's author argued that if Helson had been better informed by Auten and others, he might have asked sharper questions of Danchenko and improved the prosecution's chances of conviction.[1]

## Testimony on day one of the Danchenko trial

Auten was the first witness called by the prosecution on the opening day of the Danchenko trial, and [[john-durham|Durham]] used him to lay out the structure and origins of [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] for the jury.[2] Auten testified that on the analytical side he reported to Jonathan Moffa, who reported to Bill Priestap; his operational counterpart was Joe Pientka, who reported to [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]], who also reported to Priestap; Priestap reported to [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] and McCabe to Director [[james-comey|James Comey]], and Auten understood that everything was being reported up to Comey.[2] He described Crossfire Hurricane as a full investigation opened on a "suggestion" from a friendly foreign government, containing four further full investigations, an approach that unlocked more powerful tools such as [[fisagate|FISA]] surveillance; he helped lead the analysts across all of them.[2] Auten also divided the operation into phases he called 1.0 (to November 2016), 2.0 (December 2016 to March 2017) and 3.0 (March 2017 onward).[2]

Under questioning Auten conceded that the [[fbi|FBI]] had been unable to corroborate or confirm the [[steele-dossier|dossier]] information placed into the [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|Carter Page FISA applications]], despite searching its own systems and conferring with other agencies, and that the unverified material was carried into each renewal.[2] He recounted travelling with other agents to interview [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]] in early October 2016, at which Steele could provide no corroboration for his own reporting even against an offer of up to $1,000,000, and could not then identify his primary sub-source.[2] The warrant on Page was nevertheless taken to the FISA court weeks later, and Auten agreed the Bureau had not known the identity of the source when it did so.[2]

Auten further testified that the name [[sergei-millian|Sergei Millian]] had surfaced before the FBI met Steele and therefore before [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]] was identified as the source in December 2016, that Millian had at one time been a confidential human source for the Atlanta field office, that he had headed the Russian American Chamber of Commerce and supported [[donald-trump|Trump]], and that a file opened on him had closed without charges.[2] He read the January 2017 immunity letter, Government Exhibit 118, signed by [[david-laufman|David Laufman]], Danchenko and counsel Mark Schamel, into the record for the jury, and described Danchenko's account of a claimed ten- to fifteen-minute phone call with a person he believed to be Millian as "peculiar" and "inconsistent."[2] Auten said he was not involved in recruiting Danchenko as a source, that being the operational side's function, and that Agent Amy Anderson joined with the transition to the Mueller Special Counsel to perform "dossier validation," assisted by analyst [[brittany-hertzog|Brittany Hertzog]].[2]

Pre-trial argument on the same day revealed that Auten had inquired about Danchenko by name to the Baltimore field office in 2012, some two years after an earlier counterespionage investigation of Danchenko had been closed on the mistaken belief that he had left the country.[2]

## Testimony on day two of the Danchenko trial

Auten returned to the stand for a second day, in a session the Badlands correspondent summarized with the line "Danchenko is in the courtroom, but the FBI is on trial."[3] On direct, Durham used Exhibit 100 to walk Auten through the January 2017 interviews, drawing out that Danchenko's account of his contacts with Millian shifted between days — two emails and a call, then one email, a call, and a later September email — without any follow-up by the interviewing agents.[3] Auten acknowledged that Danchenko had supplied only a single Russian-language email about Millian, and that additional emails existed which neither the Crossfire Hurricane team nor the Mueller Special Counsel had ever obtained; he first saw several of them while preparing for trial, and read them into the record at Durham's direction, including exchanges routed through [[dmitri-zlodorev|Dmitri Zlodorev]].[3] He confirmed the Bureau had never determined whether Millian was even in the United States at the time of the alleged New York meeting.[3]

Durham then took Auten through [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]]: the FBI had "developed some information" on Dolan between October 2016 and January 2017, yet no one asked Danchenko about him during three days of interviews despite the immunity agreement.[3] A binder of [[kglobal|KGlobal]] emails and business records followed, and Durham closed his direct by introducing [[olga-galkina|Olga Galkina]] — a line he could not press far, because Auten, though he had travelled to Cyprus to interview Galkina, was absent when Danchenko was questioned about her.[3]

On cross, defense counsel [[danny-onorato|Danny Onorato]] established that Auten had sat for four interviews with the Department of Justice Inspector General, one of roughly twenty hours, and that he had not been subpoenaed by Durham to appear at trial.[3] Auten conceded that the Bureau could have asked for Danchenko's emails and phone but did not, that he and Special Agent Somma held further questions in reserve expecting later opportunities, and that the initial meetings amounted to "breaking the ice."[3] On redirect Durham confronted him with Inspector General [[michael-horowitz|Michael Horowitz]]'s report on Crossfire Hurricane and with the team's failure to obtain Millian's phone and travel records, and the article predicted this would not be the last time Auten appeared in a courtroom over his role in Crossfire Hurricane and the Mueller Special Counsel.[3]

## Context from day three

Auten's first two days on the stand were described as brutal, with both Special Counsel [[john-durham|John H. Durham]] for the prosecution and [[danny-onorato|Danny Onorato]] for the defense treating him as a hostile witness, before the trial moved on to [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]] and Agent [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]] on day three.[4] Helson's testimony that day repeatedly implicated Auten's role as the conduit between the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] team and the source handler: Auten and Supervisory Special Agent [[amy-anderson|Amy Anderson]] asked Helson to question [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]] about Dolan at the recorded June 15, 2017 meeting, and Auten tasked Helson again in October 2017 to raise the alleged [[sergei-millian|Millian]] phone call.[4]

At the same time, Helson testified that he had never been shown the Danchenko–Dolan emails, the [[olga-galkina|Galkina]] messages or Danchenko's Facebook records — material Auten and the Crossfire Hurricane team held but did not share — and defense counsel [[stuart-sears|Stuart Sears]] catalogued these omissions at length on cross-examination.[4] The Badlands author blamed Auten directly for failing to inform Helson adequately enough for him to pursue the Millian questioning effectively, a shortcoming credited with contributing to the judge's later dismissal of Count One.[4]
