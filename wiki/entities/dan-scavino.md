# Dan Scavino

Dan Scavino is a longtime aide to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and served as White House Deputy Chief of Staff for Communications during the Trump administration. He is best known within the Truth Community for his social-media posting, which followers parse for coded or anticipatory messaging.[1]

## Role in the "Biden returns" narrative (August 2024)

In August 2024, Trump posted on Truth Social speculating that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] — whom he called "Crooked Joe" and described as having had the presidency "Unconstitutionally STOLEN from him by Kamabla, Barrack HUSSEIN Obama, Crazy Nancy Pelosi, Shifty Adam Schiff, Cryin' Chuck Schumer, and others on the Lunatic Left" — might crash the Democratic National Convention and attempt to reclaim the nomination.[1] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the significance of the episode lay less in its plausibility than in who was seeding it, noting that both [[michael-flynn|General Michael Flynn]] and Scavino were amplifying the same narrative.[1]

In that reading, coordinated messaging by Trump, Flynn, and Scavino functioned as an instrument of psychological pressure on the Deep State rather than a literal prediction; if Biden actually did turn against his former allies, it would be treated as among the most definitive available evidence for [[devolution|Devolution]], short of explicit disclosure by Trump himself.[1] Even absent that outcome, the argument ran, the narrative deployment alone was sufficient to make opponents "think twice," and that hesitation might be enough to disrupt their plans around the election.[1]

## "Enter Sandman" post (2025)

In May 2025 Scavino posted a video of Metallica's "Enter Sandman" to X, a message widely read as a signal by Trump-aligned commentators.[2] Badlands contributor [[brad-cologero-getz|Brad Cologero Getz]] noted that Scavino is from New York, not far from the Bronx, where for nearly two decades the song played over the loudspeaker whenever the Yankees held a lead and were about to close out a game.[2] From 1996 to 2013 in New York, Getz wrote, "Enter Sandman" meant simply: "Game Over."[2]
