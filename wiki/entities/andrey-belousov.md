# Andrey Belousov

Andrey Belousov is [[russia|Russia's]] Defense Minister. In the Badlands Brief's coverage of the Pyongyang "Kursk liberation" museum, Belousov is identified as one of the senior Russian officials present at the ceremony, photographed alongside [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] in front of captured Western armor including an M1A1 Abrams tank and a Bradley Fighting Vehicle.[1]

His attendance is presented as evidence of the symbolic and operational integration emerging between Moscow and Pyongyang, occurring alongside expanded defense cooperation agreements described as long-term or "comprehensive" military partnerships covering weapons transfers, training, and strategic alignment.[1]

## Appointment as defense minister

Belousov, a former deputy prime minister specializing in economics, was tapped by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in May 2024 to become Russia's defense minister, replacing [[sergei-shoigu|Sergei Shoigu]], who moved to head the Security Council.[2] The Biden administration and European capitals treated the unexpected shuffle as a sign of strain, with [[state-department|State Department]] spokesman [[vedant-patel|Vedant Patel]] calling it evidence of Putin's "desperation" and British officials describing Putin's decision-making as showing "instability."[2] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the appointment instead as a deliberate signal to Western financial elites that, after two years of sanctions and the weaponization of the dollar, Russia now regards economic defense as national defense.[2]

## Nomination as defense minister, May 2024

On 12 May 2024 the [[federation-council|Federation Council]] announced that President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had proposed Belousov, then acting First Deputy Prime Minister, to replace [[sergei-shoigu|Sergey Shoigu]] as Russian defense minister, with Shoigu moving to head the [[russian-security-council|Russian Security Council]].[3] Consultations by senators were scheduled for 13 and 14 May 2024, and [[denis-manturov|Denis Manturov]] was nominated to succeed Belousov as first deputy prime minister.[3]

Commentary in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] stressed that Belousov has virtually no experience in the defense realm and is instead an economist with long service in the Russian banking sector, interpreting the appointment as Putin signaling that economic capacity is now treated as national defense in the next stage of asymmetric conflict.[3]
