# Paul Ingrassia

Paul Ingrassia is an American lawyer and political appointee who was nominated by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to lead the [[office-of-special-counsel|Office of Special Counsel]], the federal agency charged with protecting whistleblowers and enforcing the Hatch Act. He withdrew his nomination in October 2025 after the publication of text messages attributed to him triggered a collapse in Republican support.[1]

## Withdrawal of nomination

Ingrassia withdrew on Tuesday, October 21, 2025, two days before the [[senate-homeland-security-and-governmental-affairs-committee|Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee]] was scheduled to consider his confirmation.[1] Announcing the decision on social media, he wrote that he would be "withdrawing myself from Thursday's HSGAC hearing to lead the Office of Special Counsel because unfortunately I do not have enough Republican votes at this time."[1]

The withdrawal followed reporting by [[politico|Politico]] that Ingrassia, then 30 years old, had referred to having a "Nazi streak" and had used an Italian racial slur in private messages, while calling for the elimination of Martin Luther King Jr. Day and Black History Month.[1] Ingrassia disputed that characterization, saying through his attorney that any such messages amounted to "self-deprecating and satirical humor" mocking the accusation that supporters of the MAGA movement are "Nazis."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentator [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] treated the episode less as a scandal about Ingrassia personally than as evidence of an attempted revival of cancel culture, writing that he did not know Ingrassia but considered withdrawal over the texts "pathetic," and arguing that the outrage was performative behavior modeling aimed at rebooting cancel culture, this time targeting alleged "Nazis."[1] Paul contended that Big Tech and what Badlands writers call "ConInc" would assist in that effort, exploiting the demographic weight of American Judeo-Christian audiences and what he described as their dispensationalist eschatology to coerce narrative compliance.[1]
