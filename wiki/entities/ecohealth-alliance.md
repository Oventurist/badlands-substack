# EcoHealth Alliance

EcoHealth Alliance Inc. is a U.S.-based nonprofit research organization that became a central subject of congressional COVID-19 origins oversight, chiefly over its role in channeling U.S. taxpayer funds to coronavirus research.[1]

## Debarment and funding suspension

In May 2024 the U.S. [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] agreed to a recommendation from the House [[select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]] to initiate formal debarment proceedings against EcoHealth Alliance and to impose a government-wide suspension of U.S. taxpayer funds to the organization, including a halt on all active grants.[1] Select Subcommittee Chairman [[brad-wenstrup|Brad Wenstrup]] (R-OH) released a statement confirming that HHS had accepted the recommendation to formally debar the group.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the action as a test of equal application of professional sanctions, contrasting it with the disbarment proceedings brought against attorneys John Eastman and Jeffrey Clark, and characterized the long-awaited EcoHealth accountability as a potential "boomerang."[1]

## The Morens back-channel email

Correspondence involving EcoHealth Alliance's former president [[peter-daszak|Peter Daszak]] became central to congressional scrutiny of federal records practices during the COVID-19 era.[2] In a 2021 email to Daszak, [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]]'s senior advisor [[david-morens|David Morens]] wrote that he could send material to Fauci on a private Gmail account or hand it to him at work in order to dodge Freedom of Information Act requests.[2] Citing that message, [[house-select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|House COVID-19 Committee]] chairman [[brad-wenstrup|Brad Wenstrup]] requested access to Fauci's personal email and cellphone records on May 29, 2024 and opened an investigation into a "secret back channel."[2]

## Role in "Behold, a Trojan Horse" (May 2023)

A May 2023 Badlands Media article, "Behold, a Trojan Horse," noted that EcoHealth Alliance — headed by [[peter-daszak|Peter Daszak]] — funneled research funding from the U.S. [[national-institute-of-allergy-and-infectious-diseases|National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID)]] to the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute of Virology]] for coronavirus research.[3] The article cited EcoHealth Alliance in building its argument that the proposed [[pandemic-treaty|pandemic treaty]] and [[international-health-regulations|IHR amendments]] ignore the danger of U.S.-funded laboratories creating bioweapons, rather than addressing that perceived threat.[3] Daszak, as the organization's head, was quoted describing the economic incentives for stoking public demand for coronavirus countermeasures, and was cited as having boasted about manipulating SARS-like viruses in Wuhan.[3]
