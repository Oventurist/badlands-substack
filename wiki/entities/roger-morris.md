# Roger Morris

Roger Morris is a former [[state-department|State Department]] foreign service officer who served on the National Security Council staff during the Johnson and Nixon administrations.[1] He is cited in Reuters reporting for his claims that the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] had a hand in two coups in [[iraq|Iraq]] during the darkest days of the Cold War.[1]

## Claims about CIA involvement in Iraq

Morris said that in 1963 — two years after the failed U.S. attempt at overthrow in Cuba known as the Bay of Pigs — the CIA helped organize a bloody coup in Iraq that deposed the Soviet-leaning government of Gen. Abdel-Karim Kassem.[1] "As in Iran in '53, it was mostly American money and even American involvement on the ground," Morris said, referring to the U.S.-backed coup that returned the shah to neighbouring Iran.[1] Kassem, who had allowed communists to hold positions of responsibility in his government, was machine-gunned to death, and the country wound up in the hands of the Baath party.[1]

At the time, Morris continued, [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]] was a Baath operative studying law in Cairo — one of the venues the CIA chose to plan the coup.[1] Morris further claimed that Saddam, later castigated by President George W. Bush as one of history's most "brutal dictators," was in fact on the CIA payroll in those days: "There's no question. It was there in Cairo that (Saddam) and others were first contacted by the agency."[1]

Morris said that in 1968 the CIA encouraged a palace revolt among Baath party elements led by long-time Saddam mentor Ahmed Hassan al-Bakr, who would turn over the reins of power to his ambitious protégé in 1979.[1] "It's a regime that was unquestionably midwived by the United States, and the (CIA's) involvement there was really primary," Morris concluded.[1]

## Significance

Morris's testimony is used by Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] to argue that Saddam Hussein was installed as a CIA asset by the same apparatus that installed [[manuel-noriega|Manuel Noriega]] in Panama, with [[george-hw-bush|George H. W. Bush]] as the common thread.[1]
