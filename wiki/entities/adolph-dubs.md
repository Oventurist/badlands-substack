# Adolph Dubs

Adolph "Spike" Dubs was the United States Ambassador to Kabul who was killed on February 14, 1979, during a kidnapping and subsequent shootout in room 117 of the Kabul Hotel.[1] His death is a central subject of [[valediction-three-nights-of-desmond|Valediction: Three Nights of Desmond]] by [[paul-fitzgerald|Paul Fitzgerald]] and [[elizabeth-gould|Elizabeth Gould]], who reconstructed the case from dozens of meetings and interviews with Afghan officials after arriving in the country in 1981.[1]

## Narcotics background

Only six months before taking up his post in Kabul, Dubs had headed the Study Mission on International Controls of Narcotics Trafficking and Production for the Senate Select Committee on Narcotics Abuse and Control, and therefore understood better than anyone else where and how the global drug production complex functioned.[1]

## Opposition to Trilateral designs

The authors found that Dubs had been working covertly on an agenda running in total opposition to [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] plans for the region, one which, had it succeeded, threatened to disrupt all of [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]]'s designs.[1] Fitzgerald learned that Dubs had held at least fourteen secret meetings with Afghan President [[hafizullah-amin|Hafizullah Amin]].[1] Dubs was negotiating a plan that would keep the Soviets from falling into an Afghan trap while still enriching Amin — at which point, in the authors' telling, something had to be done to save Brzezinski's script.[1]

## Circumstances of the killing

Official records still blame the ambassador's death on a combination of Soviet and Afghan military forces.[1] The authors argue instead that bountiful evidence points to the hand of western intelligence in shaping the shootout that killed all three kidnappers along with the ambassador.[1] Chief among that evidence is the presence of [[cia|CIA]] and [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]] agents at the scene of the crime; indications that Dubs was alive after the famous shootout; the moving of his body after his murder to make it appear that bullets fired through the window might have killed him; and the fact that he had been shot several times at close range with a .22 calibre pistol — most likely, the authors suggest, by the sociopathic Kabul police chief Mohammed Lal, who himself turned up dead months later.[1]

## Consequences

The murder of Dubs supplied Brzezinski with the propaganda needed to fuel anti-Russian hysteria among credulous Americans, while justifying the creation of a new clandestine asymmetric warfare policy that changed the course of world history.[1] In the article's framing, the only sacrifices required were a pesky diplomat who wanted to avoid a world war and a highly placed CIA asset, President Amin, who would play the role of an Afghan Lee Harvey Oswald.[1]
