# Charles Michel

Charles Michel is a Belgian politician who served as President of the European Council, the body convening the heads of state and government of the [[european-union|European Union]].[1]

## Role in the €50 billion Ukraine facility

On 1 February 2024, Michel announced that EU leaders had reached a unanimous deal to provide €50 billion in aid to Ukraine within the EU budget, after the sole holdout — Hungarian Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] — was persuaded to drop his veto.[1] "All 27 leaders agreed on an additional €50 billion support package for Ukraine within the EU budget," Michel wrote on X a few minutes after the formal start of the Council meeting, adding that the agreement "locks in steadfast, long-term, predictable funding for Ukraine."[1]

Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] publicly thanked Michel and EU leaders for establishing what became known as the Ukraine Facility, calling it proof of "strong EU unity" and arguing that continued financial support was no less important than military assistance and sanctions pressure on Russia.[1] The summit at which the deal was struck coincided with large [[2024-european-farmer-protests|farmer protests]] outside the [[european-parliament|European Parliament]] building in Brussels.[1]

## 2024 European Parliament candidacy

In January 2024 Michel confirmed to several Belgian newspapers that he would stand as a candidate in the European Parliament elections — the first time an incumbent president of the [[european-council|European Council]] had run in an EP election.[2] Analysts noted that if he were elected and left the presidency, the post would remain vacant and, under the applicable rules, the member state holding the rotating presidency — [[hungary|Hungary]] after the elections — would chair Council meetings, raising the prospect of [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] presiding over the body.[2] The permanent presidency Michel held had been created only in 2009 by the Lisbon Treaty, in order to ensure "continuity" in the EU's ambitions, though critics argued it undermined diversity and equal representation among member states.[2]
