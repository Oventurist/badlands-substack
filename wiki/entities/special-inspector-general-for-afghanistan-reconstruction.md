# Special Inspector General for Afghanistan Reconstruction

The Special Inspector General for Afghanistan Reconstruction (SIGAR) is the United States government watchdog office responsible for auditing and investigating American reconstruction and assistance spending in Afghanistan. It was led in 2023 by [[john-sopko|John Sopko]].[1]

## 2023 findings on post-withdrawal aid

A SIGAR report issued in early November 2023 found that Afghanistan had received approximately $11 billion in U.S. foreign aid since the country's collapse in August 2021.[1] The report described cash shipments of roughly $80 million arriving in Afghanistan every 10 to 14 days since the [[taliban|Taliban]] seized power ahead of the final withdrawal of American forces.[1]

SIGAR relayed assurances from the [[united-nations|United Nations]] that the funds were held in designated U.N. accounts at a private bank rather than deposited in the Afghan central bank or handed to the Taliban, and that the U.N. Assistance Mission in Afghanistan monitored, audited, inspected, and vetted the shipments under U.N. financial rules.[1] The watchdog nevertheless cautioned that the Taliban had a record of stealing foreign aid and of blocking the poorest recipients from receiving it, using methods such as siphoning cash from U.N. shipments, collecting royalties, and charging fees on shipments.[1]

## Reception

The report was covered by Breitbart and American Greatness and picked up in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], where [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the continuing flows as consistent with a negotiated post-withdrawal arrangement traceable to the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]].[1]

## August 2023 report on Afghan aid

A SIGAR report publicized in August 2023 found that the Biden administration had given $2.35 billion to [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] over the preceding two years, despite the country's rule by the [[taliban|Taliban]] following the 2021 US withdrawal.[2] The report noted that approximately $1.7 billion "remained available for possible disbursement," and observers warned that a sizable portion of these funds would likely end up in the group's coffers.[2] SIGAR characterized the Taliban as treating international aid as a "revenue stream," describing the group as "comfortable accepting foreign support insofar as they can closely monitor the organizations, including restricting and controlling them, and claim some credit for the provision of the benefits."[2]

The findings followed testimony by SIGAR head [[john-sopko|John Sopko]] to the House Foreign Affairs Committee in April, in which he said he "cannot assure this committee or the American taxpayer we are not currently funding the Taliban" and accused the administration of blocking his efforts to establish the facts.[2] Coverage of the report appeared in the Washington Free Beacon and Summit News before being taken up in the Badlands brief, where [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the continuing flows as a [[devolution|Devolution]] proof consistent with the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]] framework.[2]
