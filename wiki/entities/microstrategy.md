# MicroStrategy

**MicroStrategy** (ticker MSTR) is a software company whose name has become, in the words of Barron's, "practically synonymous" with [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] because of the scale of its corporate cryptocurrency holdings.[1] The company is closely identified with executive chairman [[michael-saylor|Michael Saylor]].[1]

## April 2025 Bitcoin purchase

MicroStrategy stock rose on Monday, April 21, 2025 after the company revealed it had bought still more Bitcoin.[1] A Form 8-K filed with the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission]] showed the purchase of 6,556 digital tokens for $555.8 million in cash between April 14 and April 20, 2025, working out to roughly $84,785 per Bitcoin.[1]

## Financing and market impact

Badlands commentary paired the acquisition with the company's simultaneous equity issuance, noting that MicroStrategy sold 1.755 million MSTR shares over the same April 14–20 window for net proceeds of $547.7 million — almost exactly funding the Bitcoin purchase — and summarizing the arrangement with the remark that "Michael loves his ATM."[1]

Analysts at TD Cowen argued that the relationship between MicroStrategy and Bitcoin is largely one-directional: if the company were to stop buying Bitcoin outright, the move would have little to no impact on the cryptocurrency's price.[1]

## February 2025 rebrand to Strategy

On February 5, 2025, MicroStrategy announced that it was now doing business as Strategy, describing itself as the world's first and largest Bitcoin Treasury Company, the largest independent publicly traded business intelligence company, and a Nasdaq 100 stock.[2] The company characterized the change as a brand simplification and natural evolution reflecting its focus and broad appeal; the new logo incorporated a stylized "B" signifying its Bitcoin strategy, and the brand's primary color became orange, said to represent energy, intelligence, and Bitcoin.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that [[michael-saylor|Michael Saylor]]'s company had been deployed to trigger a monetary transition that forces the fiat currency class to fund its own destruction, and said the rebrand did nothing to dispel that reading.[2]
