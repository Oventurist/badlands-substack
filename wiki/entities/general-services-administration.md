# General Services Administration

The **General Services Administration (GSA)** is the U.S. federal agency responsible for supporting government operations, including administering the support package provided to presidential transition teams — federal funding, office space and cybersecurity support, conditioned on signing an agreement that includes an ethics pledge.[1]

## Role in the 2024–25 Trump transition

In late November 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s transition team signed a transition agreement with the [[joe-biden|Biden]] [[white-house|White House]] allowing arrangements for the transfer of federal agencies to begin, but declined to sign the GSA agreement.[1] By turning down roughly $7 million in federal funding, the transition became free to raise unlimited private donations; incoming chief of staff [[susie-wiles|Susie Wiles]] said the team would not accept contributions from foreign donors and would disclose its donors publicly.[1] The team said it would instead adhere to its own ethics plan.[1] Trump's team separately refused to sign an agreement with the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] that would have allowed the [[fbi|FBI]] to conduct security clearances for transition officials, which in practice prevented Biden officials from sharing classified information with many members of the incoming team.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that federal law says nominees are "expected" — not required — to sign the agreements, so Trump was bucking a norm rather than breaking the law.[1] She wrote that the GSA and the FBI had interfered with the peaceful transfer of power in 2016, that the American people do not trust either institution, and that the refusal to sign should be understood as part of Trump's promise to remove corruption from federal institutions.[1] She also dismissed criticism from Senator [[elizabeth-warren|Elizabeth Warren]] over transition norms as "entertainment at this point."[1]


## Approval of Meta's Llama for federal use

In September 2025 the GSA, which oversees federal procurement, added [[meta-platforms|Meta]]'s artificial intelligence system Llama to its approved list of AI platforms after determining that it met government security and legal standards, a decision confirmed by procurement lead Josh Gruenbaum.[2] The move followed GSA approvals in preceding months for AI products from [[xai|xAI]], Amazon, Microsoft, Google, [[anthropic|Anthropic]], and OpenAI, with the companies agreeing to steep discounts while adhering to federal requirements.[2] Gruenbaum said the aim was to give agencies tools to streamline contract reviews, troubleshoot IT issues faster and improve efficiency, adding: "It's not about currying favor. It's about that recognition of how do we all lock in arms and make this country the best country it could possibly be."[2] See [[llama-federal-approval|Llama federal approval]].
