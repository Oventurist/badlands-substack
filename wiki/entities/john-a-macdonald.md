# John A. Macdonald

Sir John A. Macdonald was Canada's leading "father of Confederation" and first Prime Minister, presented by Matthew Ehret as an exemplar of the monarchical spirit that animated the 1867 [[british-north-america-act|British North America Act]].[1]

Macdonald famously declared, "a Britisher I was born, and a Britisher I will die," and in 1867 said of the wilderness west of Toronto: "I would be quite willing, personally to leave the whole country a wilderness for the next half century, but I fear if Englishmen do not go there the Yankees will."[1]

During the 1870 Ottawa negotiations for [[british-columbia|British Columbia]]'s entry into Confederation, Macdonald promised to construct a rail line linking the new province with Montreal and Quebec "within ten years," a commitment Ehret argues was necessary to sabotage the intention of American [[manifest-destiny|Manifest Destiny]] policy.[1] When lack of progress by 1878 revived British Columbian demands for annexation to the United States, Macdonald responded with the "National Policy of 1878-1885," which forced construction of Canada's own transcontinental railway; the inaugural train cars arrived at Port Moody from Montreal on 4 July 1885.[1]
