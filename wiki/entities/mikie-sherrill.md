# Mikie Sherrill

Mikie Sherrill is the Governor of [[new-jersey|New Jersey]]. In July 2026 she was a central figure in the dispute over roughly 6,600 noncitizens who were mistakenly registered to vote in the state between June 2023 and June 2024, an outcome the state attributed to an alleged software error in its Motor Vehicle Commission system.[1]

## The noncitizen registration dispute

Sherrill said that fewer than 400 of the affected individuals ultimately cast ballots, and that the affected registrations were being removed from the voter rolls.[1] The software vendor disputed the state's account, claiming that New Jersey had knowingly approved the noncitizen registrations.[1]

The [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] subsequently launched an investigation and demanded that New Jersey provide detailed records — names, nationalities, addresses, registration information, and voting records for those who cast ballots — within five business days, with Assistant Attorney General [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]] saying the information was necessary to determine whether federal voting laws had been violated.[1] New Jersey officials under Sherrill rejected the demands, arguing that the federal government lacks legal authority to compel production of the requested voter data and characterizing the investigation as politically motivated.[1]

## Executive Order No. 12 and the DOJ sanctuary lawsuit

In February 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] filed suit against [[new-jersey|New Jersey]] and Governor Sherrill over [[new-jersey-executive-order-12|Executive Order No. 12]], a sanctuary directive barring [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] from conducting secure arrests of criminal illegal aliens in nonpublic areas of state property, including correctional facilities.[2] Federal officials argued the policy obstructs immigration enforcement and results in the release of individuals otherwise eligible for removal, including illegal aliens convicted of aggravated assault, burglary, and drug and human trafficking offenses.[2]

## 2025 election as governor

Sherrill, then a member of the U.S. House, won the New Jersey gubernatorial race on November 4, 2025, becoming the state's first female Democratic governor.[3] Her victory formed part of a Democratic sweep of the first major elections of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second presidency, which also saw [[abigail-spanberger|Abigail Spanberger]] elected in Virginia and [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]] projected to win New York City's mayoralty.[3] On election day, bomb threats — later found to be non-credible — forced voters out of multiple New Jersey polling places, prompting Mamdani to link the disruptions to the Trump administration and drawing a rebuke from White House press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]].[3]
