# Fang Fang

"Fang Fang" is the name by which a suspected Chinese intelligence operative became publicly known after reporting revealed that she had cultivated relationships with California politicians, most prominently Congressman [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]], before U.S. counterintelligence authorities intervened.

In the Badlands corpus the name functions as shorthand for the porousness of American legislative institutions to foreign penetration. The July 22, 2026 Badlands Brief invokes it twice in a single passage: first as an argument for why criminal background checks on House staff, as proposed in [[kat-cammack|Kat Cammack]]'s H.Res. 500, should have been standard practice long ago; and second in the discussion of Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]]'s proposal to bar lawmakers from sexual relationships with any congressional staff, where the case illustrates how personal relationships can serve as an intelligence vector.

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] pairs the episode with [[dianne-feinstein|Feinstein]]'s driver as evidence that [[congress|Congress]] has repeatedly declined to close known vulnerabilities, concluding that "problems don't get to design their own solutions."
