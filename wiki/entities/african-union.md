# African Union

The African Union (AU) is the continental body representing African states, and one of the mediators in the negotiations that produced the November 2025 [[dr-congo-peace-agreement-2025|DR Congo peace agreement]].[1]

Alongside [[qatar|Qatar]] and the United States, the AU spent months mediating between Kinshasa and the [[congo-river-alliance|Congo River Alliance]], the coalition of armed groups including [[m23|M23]] that signed the Doha framework accord on November 15, 2025.[1] The resulting document sets out eight protocols to be negotiated, from an immediate cessation of hostilities to the full restoration of state authority over the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|DR Congo]]'s national territory.[1]

## Campaign to redraw the world map (2025)

In August 2025, the African Union backed campaigners calling for the world map to be redrawn to show the "greatness of the continent," arguing that most maps skew the size of land masses and make Africa appear much smaller relative to other countries and continents.[2] The 55-nation bloc contended that the distortion downplays the scale and importance of the continent while making America and Europe look larger than they are.[2] The Mercator projection, in use since the 16th century and among the world's most common maps, inflates land masses further from the equator, so that Greenland appears almost the same size as Africa when it is in fact smaller than the Arabian Peninsula and Africa is 14 times larger.[2] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] quipped: "Wait, the maps were falsified? Man, I hope that's the only aspect of our physical existence about which they've lied to us."[2]

## G20 permanent membership bid (2023)

Ahead of the September 2023 [[g20|G20]] summit in New Delhi, [[european-union|EU]] officials were reported to be preparing to endorse the African Union's bid to become a permanent member of the Group of 20, while positioning Europe as the more stable partner for investment and engagement on the continent.[3] Bloomberg reported that this would be the aim of a high-level meeting with African leaders on the summit's sidelines, held at a sensitive moment for Europe given the coup in Niger and a succession of junta governments voicing anti-colonial sentiment directed particularly at France.[3] The absence of [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] from the summit was seen as easing consensus on such agenda items, with [[li-qiang|Li Qiang]] and [[sergei-lavrov|Sergei Lavrov]] attending in their place.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the EU outreach was an afterthought beside the groundwork China and Russia had laid in Africa, and predicted African peoples would reclaim their sovereignty from both.[3]
