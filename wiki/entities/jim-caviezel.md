# Jim Caviezel

Jim Caviezel is an American actor best known in the Truth Community for starring in the 2023 anti-child-trafficking film [[sound-of-freedom|Sound of Freedom]].[1] In the film he portrays [[tim-ballard|Tim Ballard]], the founder of [[operation-underground-railroad|Operation Underground Railroad]].[1]

The low-budget film featuring Caviezel generated $100 million at the box office within weeks of its debut despite sustained negative coverage from mainstream media outlets such as Rolling Stone and Bloomberg News, and earned an A+ CinemaScore alongside a 100 percent Rotten Tomatoes score.[1] Caviezel's association with the project has made him a prominent figure in public discussion of child trafficking and of the media's handling of that subject.[1]

## QAnon association and Sound of Freedom controversy (2023)

In July 2023 [[bloomberg|Bloomberg]] published an opinion column arguing that [[sound-of-freedom|Sound of Freedom]] and [[qanon|QAnon]] both rely on "tired Hollywood tropes," and identified Caviezel's associations as one source of the film's controversy.[2] The piece noted that Caviezel, who plays former Homeland Security operative [[tim-ballard|Tim Ballard]], had spoken at multiple QAnon events, and that in an interview with former Trump adviser [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] he promoted the claim that child traffickers drain their victims' blood to create a serum to prevent ageing.[2] Ballard himself was cited as having promoted the claim that the furniture retailer Wayfair was involved in child trafficking.[2]

The column appeared as the film crossed $50 million at the United States box office and after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced plans to screen it at his New Jersey golf club, with critics describing the film as embraced by the far right and by Christian audiences.[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] characterised the attacks as a Streisand effect, arguing that the campaign against the film was producing a convergence between Sound of Freedom and Q that led many viewers to reconsider the claims anons had been making for years.[2]

## Rolling Stone review (July 2023)

A [[rolling-stone|Rolling Stone]] review of [[sound-of-freedom|Sound of Freedom]] by [[miles-klee|Miles Klee]] attacked Caviezel as "a prominent figure on the conspiracist right" and slammed the actor's past claims about elite paedophile rings that kidnap, rape and murder children to harvest [[adrenochrome|adrenochrome]], a compound the review described as produced in the brain and reputed to have psychedelic effects.[3] Commentary aggregated by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] argued that Caviezel's personal beliefs were being used to invalidate the premise of a film inspired by the real work of [[tim-ballard|Tim Ballard]] and [[operation-underground-railroad|Operation Underground Railroad]].[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterised the wider critical response as a coordinated instruction not to see the film.[3]
