# Tom Clancy

Tom Clancy was an American novelist whose military and intelligence thrillers, including the Jack Ryan series, were widely adapted for film. Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats him as an instrument of [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] narrative management rather than an independent storyteller.[1]

## Assessment in the article

Lange writes that he used to enjoy Clancy's films before concluding that "his only purpose was to glorify the CIA as heroes."[1] He accepts that Clancy plainly had inside sources and wrote his books on the basis of insider information, but argues that he never wrote about the truth of what those sources represented.[1] Specifically, Lange says Clancy never wrote about the CIA being the biggest drug trafficker in the world, nor about the agency standing behind nuclear proliferation by rogue nations and terrorist groups; instead he wrote about the CIA defending America against enemies that the agency itself "controls and created."[1] Lange concludes bluntly that Clancy was "a propagandist and a clown, in my opinion."[1]

His 1991 novel *[[the-sum-of-all-fears|The Sum of All Fears]]*, filmed in 2002 with Ben Affleck as CIA analyst Jack Ryan, is the specific work the article dissects, on the grounds that its plot of rogue Russian nationalists detonating a nuclear bomb in Baltimore both demonises Russia and demonises nationalism in favour of globalism — a "purposeful narrative" in Lange's reading.[1] These characterisations reflect Badlands Media opinion rather than established fact.
