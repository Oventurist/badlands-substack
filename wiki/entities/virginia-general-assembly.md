# Virginia General Assembly

The Virginia General Assembly is the commonwealth's legislature and the driving actor in the 2026 Virginia congressional redistricting fight.[1] After the 2020 census, Virginia adopted a bipartisan redistricting commission to draw congressional and legislative maps with the aim of reducing gerrymandering, and the congressional delegation stood at roughly six Democrats to five Republicans.[1]

## 2025 constitutional amendment

In late 2025, the General Assembly — with Democratic majorities in both chambers — pushed a constitutional amendment to temporarily override the commission and allow lawmakers to redraw the state's 11 congressional districts mid-decade, before 2031.[1] The measure was framed on the ballot as a defensive response to aggressive Republican map-drawing elsewhere, while critics called it a blatant power grab and partisan gerrymander.[1] As required for constitutional changes, the amendment passed the legislature twice, but it drew multiple lawsuits and conflicting court rulings: one judge blocked it, the Virginia Supreme Court allowed the referendum to proceed, and further challenges questioned procedural compliance.[1]

## Referendum and litigation

The special election went forward on a Tuesday in April 2026 and voters narrowly approved the amendment, roughly 51.45 percent to 48.55 percent with about three million votes cast.[1] A "Yes" vote authorizes the Democratic legislature to adopt a new congressional map through October 2030, after which the standard commission process resumes following the 2030 census.[1] Democratic leaders have proposed a map that could shift the delegation to as lopsided as 10 Democrats to one Republican, potentially netting up to four additional seats and boosting the party's chances of regaining control of the narrowly divided U.S. House in November 2026.[1]

The result was not final: Republicans immediately challenged it, and a Tazewell County circuit court judge ruled the referendum unconstitutional over legislative procedural violations, issuing an injunction blocking certification.[1] The case is heading to appeal, likely to the Virginia Supreme Court, which has already weighed in on related issues and could ultimately invalidate the amendment or the process.[1]

## February 2026 clearance of the referendum

On February 13, 2026, the [[virginia-supreme-court|Virginia Supreme Court]] ruled that the April 21, 2026, referendum on the Assembly-passed amendment could proceed even as legal challenges continued, reversing a lower court that had blocked it on procedural grounds.[2] The measure, passed by the Assembly's Democratic majority, would permit [[mid-decade-redistricting|mid-decade redistricting]] of U.S. House districts in a departure from the traditional post-census process, and if approved could expand Democratic representation in Virginia's 11-member delegation from six seats to as many as ten.[2]
