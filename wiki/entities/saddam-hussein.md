# Saddam Hussein

Saddam Hussein was the ruler of Iraq whose acquisition and use of chemical weapons became both an instrument of U.S. policy and, later, the stated justification for his overthrow.[1]

## Chemical weapons transfers

According to [[scott-horton|Scott Horton]], speaking in an interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] and relayed by Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], the United States in 1980 sold illegal chemical weapons to Saddam and encouraged him to use them against Iran during the [[iran-iraq-war|Iran-Iraq War]].[1] Horton characterized that conflict as arguably the most gruesome war of the twentieth century, employing the same mass-slaughter tactics used in the First World War.[1]

## 2003 invasion

The same category of weapons later served the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] and the [[cia|CIA]] as the justification for invading Iraq in 2003 and overthrowing Saddam, on the grounds that such weapons were illegal under the [[chemical-weapons-convention|Chemical Weapons Convention]].[1] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that, measured against the treaty chronology, the 1980 transfer itself amounted to a war crime by the United States government.[1]

## Netanyahu's 2002 congressional testimony

Badlands' July 2024 commentary recounted that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], testifying as a private citizen before Congress on September 12, 2002, urged the United States to invade Iraq and kill Saddam Hussein, promising that his death would bring peace to the Middle East, while also urging invasions of Iran and Afghanistan.[2] Netanyahu claimed Saddam possessed nuclear weapons and that [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] used Iraq as a base of operations; the article stated both claims were false, noting that Saddam had in fact been fighting Al Qaeda and keeping it out of Iraq.[2] After U.S. forces took control of Baghdad on April 9, 2003 and removed Saddam from power, Iraq became a haven for groups including Al Qaeda and [[isis|ISIS]].[2]


## In "Profiting From War"

Joe Lange's "Profiting From War" argues that fear of Saddam Hussein as "the next Hitler" was one of two narratives — the other being fear of terrorism — used by [[central-intelligence-agency|the CIA]] and an associated shadow government to turn the Middle East into a war zone, and describes Saddam himself as a CIA creation and puppet.[3] Quoting the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]], the article recounts the hidden U.S. aid to Saddam's Iraq during its 1980s war with Iran — the [[iraqgate]] affair — including biological cultures usable for weapons, nuclear know-how and conventional arms.[3] ABC's Ted Koppel observed in June 1992 that [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]], operating behind the scenes through the 1980s, initiated and supported much of the financing, intelligence and military help that built Saddam's Iraq into the power the United States ultimately had to destroy.[3]

The article further contends that Bush wanted Saddam to invade [[kuwait|Kuwait]] so that the [[gulf-war|Gulf War]] would distract from the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] scandal and destroy evidence of [[bush-family|Bush family]] dealings there, and that [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] later invaded [[iraq-war|Iraq]] on the false premise of weapons of mass destruction.[3]
