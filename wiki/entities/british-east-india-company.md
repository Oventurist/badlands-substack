# British East India Company

The **British East India Company** (EIC) was an English, later British, chartered trading corporation that acquired territorial and military power in India and was formally dissolved by an act of Parliament in 1873.[1] Badlands Media describes it as the most powerful corporation of all time, noting that at the time of its dissolution its private army was twice the size of the British Army.[1]

## Alleged transformation into MI6

Badlands commentary advances the thesis that the EIC did not simply disappear but transformed itself into [[mi6|MI6]], observing that the company was dissolved in the same year, 1873, that the War Office created the body that would become known as British Intelligence.[1] The reasoning offered is that corporate espionage long predated the notion of a state intelligence agency, which is comparatively recent in historical terms, and that the EIC had already mastered the craft.[1]

## Proxy warfare and color revolutions

The company is also credited in this account with cultivating the modern practice of proxy war and color revolution during the 1730s, while competing with the French East India Company for influence in India.[1] The French are described as the first to conceive of "buying" the support of local warlords and turning their armies against the British, but British diplomats proved more cunning and better funded, out-manoeuvring their rivals and taking control of more than half of India.[1] Badlands invokes this history to explain the large English-speaking Indian population in London as a product of specific imperial circumstances, unlike migration patterns elsewhere in Europe.[1]
