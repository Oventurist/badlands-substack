# James Goldsmith

Sir James Goldsmith was an Anglo-French financier who appears in Badlands commentary as a co-seller, alongside [[jacob-rothschild|Jacob Rothschild]], of the gold-mining shares bought by [[george-soros|George Soros]] in 1993.[1]

## Background

Goldsmith came from a rich family and fled from France to England in 1940 to escape the Nazis — a pattern Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] calls "a really common theme" among the figures he profiles.[1] Goldsmith went on to serve in the British Army.[1] Carlson speculates about what kind of [[mk-ultra|MK Ultra]]-type activity occurs in the British military, asserting that the British "pretty much create and perfect the mind control system we know today."[1]

## Rise as a financier

While attending college in England, Goldsmith went to the horse races and placed a bet worth about $482 in today's money, winning $382,000 — the seed money readers are asked to believe launched his career as a financier.[1] He began by investing in the food industry and became a billionaire specialising in hostile takeovers.[1] He somehow knew to cash out of the stock market in 1987, immediately before the crash, which Carlson sardonically attributes to his being "another investment genius like Soros."[1] In 1990 he started a private equity firm, in keeping with the pattern of early-1990s launches Carlson identifies across the network.[1]

## Climate advocacy

Goldsmith was a prominent climate change advocate.[1] His older brother was the founding editor and publisher of The Ecologist, a founding member of the Green Party, and likewise an early climate change activist.[1] Carlson ties this to his claim that the [[british-cabal|British Cabal]] is the origin of what he calls the climate change hoax, with [[king-charles-iii|King Charles]] now its leading advocate.[1]

## The 1993 gold trade

Later in 1993 Soros bought 10 million shares of North America's largest gold producer from Goldsmith and Rothschild, who retained substantial holdings.[1] Investors followed Soros in, driving the gold price up, and at its peak Goldsmith and Rothschild sold their shares — leaving Soros and other investors holding the bag.[1] Carlson presents this as the occasional price of being created and controlled by the British Cabal elite: sometimes the controllers ask you to take one for the team.[1]
