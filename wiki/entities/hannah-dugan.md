# Hannah Dugan

Hannah Dugan is a former Milwaukee County Circuit Court judge in Wisconsin who was convicted of felony obstruction for interfering with a federal immigration arrest at her courthouse.[1]

## Case and conviction

The case stemmed from an April 2025 incident in which prosecutors said Dugan directed [[eduardo-flores-ruiz|Eduardo Flores-Ruiz]] through a private jury door while federal immigration agents waited to arrest him; agents ultimately apprehended him outside the courthouse.[1] Dugan was convicted of felony obstruction but acquitted of a separate concealment charge.[1]

## Arrest (April 2025)

Dugan was arrested by federal authorities on the morning of Friday, April 25, 2025 and charged with obstruction, accused of helping an undocumented immigrant evade arrest by [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] officers after he appeared in her courtroom on April 18.[2] Her arrest came one day after federal authorities arrested former New Mexico judge [[joel-cano|Joel Cano]] and his wife on charges related to the harboring of an undocumented immigrant allegedly tied to [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]], and the two cases were reported together as a sharp escalation in the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s removal efforts.[2] Border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]] said in a statement that "Nobody should be surprised by the arrest of two judges," warning that anyone who impeded enforcement or knowingly harbored illegal aliens from ICE would be prosecuted.[2]

The arrest triggered resistance within the Wisconsin judiciary: Sawyer County judge [[monica-isham|Monica Isham]] emailed judges across the state threatening to refuse to hold court absent guidance and support, prompting Representative [[tom-tiffany|Tom Tiffany]] to call for her resignation or removal.[2]

## Sentencing

In July 2026 Dugan was sentenced to a $5,000 fine with no prison time.[1] Presiding judge [[lynn-adelman|Lynn Adelman]] said the punishment should reflect Dugan's lengthy public service and characterized the incident as an isolated lapse in judgment.[1] Prosecutors had argued that federal sentencing guidelines supported a prison term of 15 to 21 months, while the defense maintained the prosecution was politically motivated and said it planned to appeal the conviction.[1]

## Commentary and asymmetrical-justice framing

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the sentence as a perversion of justice, writing that Dugan had used the authority of the bench to help a "criminal invader" evade federal immigration enforcement inside a public courtroom and received only a fine.[1] The commentary contrasted Dugan's sentence with the eight-year, three-month prison term imposed on [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]], arguing the disparity sets a precedent that weaponizing government office for ideological ends carries little consequence — and concluding that Americans should "expect more Hannah Dugans."[1]

## Suspension by the Wisconsin Supreme Court (April 2025)

On Tuesday, April 29, 2025 the [[wisconsin-supreme-court|Wisconsin Supreme Court]] suspended Dugan, saying it was in the public interest to relieve the Milwaukee County Circuit judge of her duties while she faced two federal charges.[3] The [[fbi|FBI]] had taken her into custody the previous Friday morning at the county courthouse on charges of concealing an individual to prevent his discovery and arrest and of obstructing or impeding a proceeding.[3] According to an FBI affidavit filed in court, Dugan escorted [[eduardo-flores-ruiz|Eduardo Flores-Ruiz]] and his lawyer out of the courtroom through the jury door on April 18 as a way to help avert his arrest by [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]].[3] Flores-Ruiz, a Mexican national, had been charged with battery for allegedly punching someone thirty times in the face after that person complained about his loud music.[3]
