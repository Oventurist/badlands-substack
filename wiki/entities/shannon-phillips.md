# Shannon Phillips

Shannon Phillips is a former regional manager for [[starbucks|Starbucks]] who won more than $25 million in damages from the company after a jury found she had been fired because she is white.[1] Her termination followed the April 2018 arrest of two Black men at a Starbucks in the Rittenhouse Square neighborhood of Philadelphia, an incident that drew national accusations of racism, protests, and boycott threats.[1]

## The case

Two Black men had entered the Rittenhouse Square store for a business meeting with a white man who had not yet arrived; before ordering, one asked to use the bathroom and was refused, and when the pair declined to leave an employee called police.[1] Video of the arrests was viewed millions of times online. Starbucks' chief executive publicly apologized, calling the treatment of the men "reprehensible," and the company temporarily closed 8,000 stores for racial bias training.[1]

Phillips said she was fired shortly after balking at an order to suspend a white manager, that she had not previously been told she was performing poorly, and that the only explanation offered was that "the situation is not recoverable."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] congratulated Phillips on "making Starbucks pay for their racism," and used the verdict to argue that corporate [[diversity-equity-and-inclusion|diversity, equity and inclusion]] programs adopted after the death of [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] produced groupthink rather than fairness.[1] In that reading, training alone "wasn't enough" to satisfy activist pressure and Phillips became a sacrifice after refusing to fire an employee on the basis of race, with the resulting judgment costing Starbucks $25.6 million.[1]
