# Ryan Routh

Ryan Routh is the man identified as the would-be assassin who allegedly targeted [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with an AK-pattern rifle while the former president was golfing in September 2024. Badlands Media's coverage, drawing on reporting from [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], focused less on the shooting itself than on Routh's extensive prior history as a self-appointed recruiter for Ukraine's foreign fighter formations and on the question of what agencies of the U.S. government knew about his activities.[1]

## Ukraine activism and the International Legion

Routh traveled to Ukraine in 2022 to recruit foreign volunteers for the country's International Legion, and for a period was treated as a sympathetic figure by Western mainstream media, being quoted on behalf of the recruitment cause by outlets ranging from the Financial Times to Newsweek to [[new-york-times|The New York Times]].[1] According to those same reports, the 58-year-old Routh was himself judged too old to fight alongside the Ukrainian army. "So plan B," he told one outlet, "was to come to Kiev and promote the idea of many others coming to join the International Legion. We need thousands of people here to fight alongside Ukrainians."[1]

He framed the war in absolute moral terms, telling Newsweek in a video interview that the conflict with Russia was as simple as "good vs. evil" and comparable to the good guys and bad guys of Hollywood movies — not a gray conflict, he said, but "black and white."[1] In March 2022, a month into the Russian invasion, he tweeted that people around the globe should be willing to volunteer and "fight and die" in Ukraine. He also argued that with roughly 190 countries on the planet, "if the governments are not officially sending soldiers here, then we civilians should pick up this torch and make it happen."[1] Despite the scale of his rhetoric, much of his time in the country was reportedly spent in a hotel room in the far western city of Lviv.[1]

## Afghan recruitment scheme and government-knowledge questions

Among the more unusual elements of Routh's campaigning was his effort to recruit U.S.-trained Afghan special forces soldiers to serve on Ukraine's front lines. By his own account the initiative involved transferring hundreds or even thousands of Afghan fighters — a scale large enough that, after the assassination attempt, commentators asked publicly: "Who was he working with in the US government that was allowing him to do this?"[1] Badlands treated this as the central unanswered question of the episode, noting that an operation of that size would be difficult to conduct without official acquiescence.[1]

## Azov and propaganda imagery

Reporting cited by Badlands placed Routh in the company of the [[azov-battalion|Azov Brigade]], the Ukrainian formation widely described as neo-Nazi in orientation, and indicated that he appeared in at least one of the unit's propaganda videos.[1] Badlands commentators contrasted this with the favorable coverage he had received, asking why [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] would interview and effectively glorify a man associating with such groups.[1]

## Alleged intelligence and administration links

Badlands raised the possibility that Routh functioned as a contact on the ground connected to [[cia|CIA]] efforts to move [[isis|ISIS]]-linked fighters into the Ukrainian theater, referencing separate reporting on deals involving Syrian militants.[1] The commentary also highlighted a photograph placing Spanish-American chef [[jose-andres|José Andrés]] — appointed in 2022 as co-chair of the President's Council on Sports, Fitness, & Nutrition under the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration — alongside Routh in Kiev, asking why a figure with a White House appointment was photographed with him.[1] Badlands further noted that Routh had vocally called on Iran to kill Trump and, when that did not materialize, apparently took matters into his own hands.[1]

## Conviction and courtroom self-harm attempt (2025)

On Tuesday, September 23, 2025, a federal jury in Florida found Routh guilty on all charges after he attempted to assassinate then-presidential candidate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at his West Palm Beach golf club in September 2024.[2] Routh, 59, had been charged on five federal criminal counts, including attempting to assassinate a major presidential candidate, assaulting a federal officer, and multiple firearms offenses, carrying a maximum sentence of life in prison.[2] Jurors deliberated for several hours following a nearly three-week trial.[2]

After the verdict was read, Routh reportedly appeared to try to stab himself in the neck with a pen before four U.S. Marshals restrained him.[2] His daughter, Sara Routh, stood up while the jury was still in the room and shouted that he "didn't hurt anybody," that the proceeding was unfair and "rigged."[2] Two weeks earlier, the judge had cut short opening statements when Routh, who was representing himself, veered off topic to discuss Adolf Hitler and the wars in Ukraine and Gaza.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the outcome as a "nice and tidy conclusion to a very strange case," noting that a courtroom sketch artist captured the self-harm attempt because no other visuals existed.[2] She argued sardonically that the apparent suicide attempt was being treated as proof of guilt while Routh's Ukrainian connections were dismissed as unimportant — so unimportant, she wrote, that the self-representing defendant was not permitted to raise them in his own criminal defense.[2]

## Request for new counsel (July 2025)

Ahead of trial, in July 2025, Routh asked the court to appoint a new defense team, with his motion stating in part that "Ryan Routh respectfully requests the Court set a hearing to terminate the representation of his appointed counsel and their office."[3] Contemporaneous reporting recounted that Routh was accused of staking out [[donald-trump|Trump]] for twelve hours at his Florida golf course and of writing of his desire to kill him, and that investigators said he pointed a rifle through a tree line on the course where Trump was staying.[3]
