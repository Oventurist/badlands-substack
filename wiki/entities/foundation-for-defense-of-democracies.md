# Foundation for Defense of Democracies

The Foundation for Defense of Democracies (FDD) is a Washington-based foreign policy think tank whose research staff analyzes Middle East security questions, including the reliability of casualty statistics produced during the war in Gaza.[1]

## Role in the Gaza casualty-figures dispute

In May 2024 the FDD featured prominently in coverage of the [[united-nations|United Nations]]' abrupt revision of its Gaza casualty breakdown. On 6 May 2024 the UN published data showing 34,735 people reportedly killed in Gaza, including more than 9,500 women and more than 14,500 children; two days later, on 8 May, the UN published figures showing 34,844 people killed but only 4,959 women and 7,797 children — roughly halving the women-and-children counts.[1]

[[david-adesnik|David Adesnik]], FDD's Senior Fellow and Director of Research, published an analysis arguing that the change "may signal that the UN has finally recognized the lack of evidence behind Hamas's original claims that more than 14,000 children and 9,000 women have been killed in Gaza," and urging the UN to state plainly that it had lost confidence in sources whose credibility it had affirmed for months. He characterized the revision — even if it reflected only one UN office among many operating in Gaza — as "a clear step forward."[1]

The episode was covered by the [[jerusalem-post|Jerusalem Post]] and [[breitbart|Breitbart]], with the [[jewish-news-syndicate|Jewish News Syndicate]] reporting that UN officials attributed the overcount to the "fog of war."[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary on the revision, written by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], treated the correction as confirmation that all sides in the conflict engage in wartime propaganda, arguing that if the "fog of war" defense is accepted then nothing the UN publishes should be taken at face value, and predicting that the downward revision would be largely ignored because the underlying information operation had already succeeded.[1]
