# Iran

Iran is the Middle Eastern state which, in the Badlands Media essay [[america-for-sale-part-3|America for Sale — Part 3]], serves as the primary case study for the argument that [[sanctions|sanctions]] are an instrument of covert finance rather than punishment.

## Clinton-era sanctions

Quoting Wikipedia's account of United States sanctions against Iran, the article records that President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] "imposed some of the toughest sanctions against Iran in March 1995, during the presidency of Akbar Hashemi Rafsanjani, in response to the Iranian nuclear program and Iranian support for Hezbollah, Hamas, and Palestine Islamic Jihad," using Executive Order 12957 to prohibit American participation in Iran's oil industry. In May 1995 Executive Order 12959 barred trade with Iran altogether, abruptly ending commerce that had been growing since the end of the Iran–Iraq War.

The essay stresses that these orders came only five months after Clinton's 1994 [[agreed-framework|Agreed Framework]] with [[north-korea|North Korea]], which promised sanctions relief and oil deliveries. Since both countries were pursuing nuclear weapons and sponsoring designated terrorist groups — and North Korea's program was the more advanced — the author treats the asymmetry as proof that the policy was driven by "OIL and MONEY," specifically a scheme to obtain Iranian oil for delivery to North Korea.

## Closed financial system

Iran is offered, alongside North Korea, as an example of a "closed financial system": a state cut off from the world banking network and therefore, in the author's account, able to evade money-laundering law and congressional tracking, making it useful to the [[cia|CIA]] for laundering taxpayer funds.

## Oil, uranium and Marc Rich

The article links Iran to the commodities trader [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]], who was indicted for tax fraud in the United States following his purchases of Iranian oil during the 1979 hostage crisis. Rich bought embargoed Iranian oil below market and resold it above market to apartheid South Africa, and, according to Foreign Policy, "pioneered the practice of commodity swaps, like the uranium-for-oil deals he brokered in the 1980s between apartheid South Africa and Islamic Iran and Soviet Russia" — a claim the author uses to explain how Iran and North Korea obtained uranium for their weapons programs.

## The Obama-era deal

The essay closes its Iran thread with Q drop #1345 (May 12, 2018), which stated that policy toward Iran "was never about WW safety & security... It was about opening a new untapped market. It was about securing a black site. The 'Exchange'. U1... Money. Organized/planned by BC/HRC. Carried out by Hussein," and referenced a "$1.7b in-cash transfer to Iran [4 routes][5 planes]." The author argues that after the financial success of [[uranium-one|Uranium One]], [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] negotiated an Iran nuclear deal worth billions on the same model.
