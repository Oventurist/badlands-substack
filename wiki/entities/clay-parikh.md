# Clay Parikh

Clay Parikh is a cybersecurity specialist from Alabama who became a recurring figure in American election-integrity activism and, by 2026, a subject of mainstream media profiles. He is described in the Badlands corpus as a friend of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], a frequent guest on the programs [[why-we-vote|Why We Vote]] and SitRep, and a multi-time attendee of the [[great-american-restoration-tour|Great American Restoration Tour]].

## Profile in The Atlantic

A May 2026 [[the-atlantic|Atlantic]] article summarized in the [[badlands-brief-323|Badlands Brief of May 15, 2026]] opened with Parikh's name, characterizing him as "a cybersecurity expert from Alabama" who "spent years as a bit player in the world of election denial." The magazine contrasted him with better-known figures — he "wasn't a star with his own media platform, like the MyPillow guy," a reference to [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]] — while crediting him with a modest following built by circulating what it called conspiracy theories about [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s 2020 defeat. Among the claims attributed to him were that poll workers gave Trump supporters, but not other voters, felt-tip markers to fill out ballots, rendering them invalid and unreadable by voting machines, and, more recently, that a group of federal lawmakers is covering up foreign election interference.

## Badlands response

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the framing as "a lazy assessment of the full range of the [[election-denial|election denialism]] at play," noting that the cited claims were among the least provocative of Parikh's assertions about what she calls the fake elections. She described him as a certified ethical hacker and "the only public expert that has been hands-on-keys for penetration testing on the voting systems," stating that he has been certified as an expert witness in court proceedings around the nation and has worked at the highest levels of classification, specifically on critical infrastructure and elections. She objected to the "bit player" characterization with the line "Shame, *The Atlantic*," while noting that the article's author had been invited onto a Badlands show.
