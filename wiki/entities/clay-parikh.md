# Clay Parikh

Clay Parikh is a cybersecurity expert from Alabama who has become one of the most frequently cited technical voices in the American election integrity movement.[1] He is described by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] as a friend of the network, a frequent guest on the programs *Why We Vote* and *SitRep*, and a multi-time attendee of the Great American Restoration Tour events.[1]

## Professional background

Parikh is a certified ethical hacker and, according to Badlands, the only public expert who has been "hands-on-keys" performing penetration testing on voting systems.[1] He has been certified as an expert witness in court proceedings around the nation and has worked at the highest levels of security classification, specifically on critical infrastructure and elections.[1]

## Media portrayal

Parikh became the subject of a May 2026 feature in [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]], which opened with his name and characterized him as "a bit player in the world of election denial" — a figure who lacked his own media platform, unlike the MyPillow founder, but who gained a modest following by circulating conspiracy theories about President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2020 defeat.[1] Among the claims the magazine attributed to him were that poll workers gave Trump supporters, but not other voters, felt-tip markers that rendered ballots unreadable by voting machines, and that a group of federal lawmakers is covering up foreign election interference.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the framing, calling it "a lazy assessment of the full range of the election denialism at play" and arguing that the cited claims were among the least significant assertions Parikh has made about the elections.[1] She noted, however, that Badlands host Brian had invited the article's author onto the show, and characterized the coverage itself as an admission that the movement is prevailing: "the election deniers are winning."[1] The episode was treated at Badlands as a milestone in the broader [[election-denial|election denial]] narrative fight, with commentary framing the mainstream attention as validation rather than indictment.[1]
