# Andriy Melnyk

Andriy Melnyk (also rendered Andrey Melnik) was a leader of the [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists]] (OUN), the wartime Ukrainian nationalist movement that collaborated with Nazi Germany during the Second World War. He co-founded the OUN in 1929 alongside [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]], and, as noted in the Badlands Brief edition "[[badlands-brief-3d4|Canadian Separatists & Guerilla Campaigns]]," was born an Austro-Hungarian citizen of German/Cossack descent in the western regions of what is now [[ukraine|Ukraine]].

## 2026 state reburial and international reaction

In May 2026 the Ukrainian government held a state reburial ceremony in Kyiv honouring Melnyk, whose remains were repatriated from Luxembourg and who was described at the ceremony as a national hero. Multiple reports stated that President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] and senior officials attended.

[[israel|Israel]]'s Foreign Ministry condemned the event, saying there is "no place for ignoring historical truth and the memory of the victims murdered by the Nazis and their collaborators." [[yad-vashem|Yad Vashem]] also criticised the honours, warning that celebrating leaders tied to movements that collaborated with Nazi Germany undermines [[holocaust|Holocaust]] remembrance.

## Contested legacy

Melnyk, like Bandera, is regarded by many Ukrainians as an anti-Soviet independence fighter, while critics — including Israeli and Polish officials — point to documented collaboration between OUN factions and Nazi Germany, including involvement in atrocities against Jews and Poles. Israel and Poland jointly condemned state-sponsored glorification of Bandera and Melnyk as early as 2020; Ukrainian officials at the time responded that the matter was internal to Ukraine.
