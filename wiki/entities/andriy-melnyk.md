# Andriy Melnyk

Andriy Melnyk was a leader of the wartime [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists]] (OUN) who collaborated with Nazi Germany during World War II.[1] Born an Austro-Hungarian citizen in the western regions of modern-day [[ukraine|Ukraine]], he co-founded the OUN in 1929 alongside [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]], pursuing the creation of a homogeneous Ukrainian ethno-state.[1]

## 2026 state reburial

In May 2026 the Ukrainian government held a state reburial ceremony honoring Melnyk in Kyiv after his remains were repatriated from Luxembourg; he was described at the ceremony as a national hero, and President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] and senior officials attended.[1] Israel's Foreign Ministry condemned the event, stating that there is "no place for ignoring historical truth and the memory of the victims murdered by the Nazis and their collaborators," and [[yad-vashem|Yad Vashem]] warned that such honors undermine Holocaust remembrance.[1]

## Contested legacy

Melnyk, like Bandera, is viewed by many Ukrainians as an anti-Soviet independence fighter, while Israeli and Polish critics cite documented OUN collaboration with Nazi Germany, including involvement in atrocities against Jews and Poles.[1] [[israel|Israel]] and Poland jointly condemned state-sponsored glorification of both men as early as 2020.[1]

## Gladio lineage

Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] describes Melnyk, together with [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]] of the [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists]], as having been trained by SS officer [[otto-skorzeny|Otto Skorzeny]].[2] She writes that Ukraine's [[right-sector|Right Sector]] and [[azov-battalion|Azov]] battalions trace their heritage to the two men, and that the OUN is now a political party of Ukraine — evidence, in her argument, that the [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]] network remains active.[2]
