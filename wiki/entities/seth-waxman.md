# Seth Waxman

Seth Waxman is an American appellate attorney who served as counsel to [[harvard-university|Harvard University]] in the litigation over the use of race in college admissions before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[1]

## Role in the affirmative action litigation

During the Supreme Court oral arguments over [[affirmative-action]], Waxman advanced what commentators described as a difficult two-sided argument: that consideration of race was necessary to maintain existing minority admissions levels, while simultaneously maintaining that race was not a major factor in admissions decisions.[1] He characterized the effect of race consideration on Asian applicants as producing only a "slight disparity" that "had no effect with respect to outcomes."[1]

Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] highlighted the tension in this position, noting that after the Court barred the use of race criteria, the same schools that had described race as merely one of a number of minor "tips" began arguing publicly that its removal would substantially reduce minority admissions.[1] The Badlands News Brief carried Turley's analysis as part of its coverage of the post-ruling debate.[1]
