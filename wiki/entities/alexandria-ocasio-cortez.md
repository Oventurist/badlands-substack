# Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez

Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, commonly referred to by the initials AOC, is a Democratic member of the United States House of Representatives and one of the most nationally visible figures of the party's progressive wing. In July 2026 she was named by Axios as one of the three strongest early contenders for the Democratic presidential nomination in 2028, alongside former Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] and California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]].[1]

## 2028 presidential positioning

Axios' Democratic "power rankings," compiled from interviews with party strategists, donors, elected officials, and insiders, placed Ocasio-Cortez at the top of the field based on measures such as political influence, fundraising strength, and national profile.[1] The outlet stressed that the rankings reflected early positioning rather than declared candidacies, and that the field remained fluid as additional governors, senators, and other national figures weighed potential bids.[1]

## Commentary in Badlands coverage

Writing in the Badlands Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the ranking as evidence of a talent vacuum within the Democratic Party, arguing that it was "really quite something that there is no new talent on the Democrat side" and describing the prospect of Ocasio-Cortez approaching executive power as implausible.[1] The commentary contrasted the current field with a hypothetical "Kennedy-esque" moderate who might tack toward the center and appear to restore normalcy — a candidate the author suggested would represent a genuine contender in the public mind — and instead characterized Harris, Newsom, and Ocasio-Cortez as "punchlines" rather than serious threats.[1] The same commentary framed the rankings as further proof that the party was not attempting to field competitive candidates, tying this to the recurring Badlands thesis that elections are performative, and speculated about how far a "DSA Rising op" would be carried and whether a Bernie Sanders comeback would follow.[1]


## Condemnation of Randy Fine (February 2026)

In February 2026 Ocasio-Cortez joined Sen. [[jacky-rosen|Jacky Rosen]], Jewish organizations and other public figures in denouncing Republican Congressman [[randy-fine|Randy Fine]] of Florida after he tweeted that if forced to choose between dogs and Muslims the decision would be easy.[2] The remark, made in response to comments by a pro-Palestinian activist, was condemned as vile, Islamophobic and dehumanizing, and drew calls for Fine's censure or resignation; Fine stood by the statement.[2]

## 2028 presidential speculation

A poll from The Argument/Verasight released in mid-December 2025 showed Ocasio-Cortez narrowly edging Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] 51–49 in a hypothetical 2028 matchup, a margin within the poll's margin of error and therefore a statistical tie.[3] PJ Media dismissed the result as coming from an obscure outfit.[3] Asked by a reporter about the poll and her chances of beating Vance, Ocasio-Cortez replied, "You know, these polls three years out…they are what they are. But let the record show, I will stomp him," laughing as she said it.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the episode as the uniparty establishment coping with the onset of the Golden Age by fantasizing about 2028 elections, writing that [[erika-kirk|Erika Kirk]] had already scheduled Vance's coronation while AOC held her election in her head, and predicting that neither figure would have the political capital to "wrap a bus" by 2028.[3]

## ICE avoidance presentation referral (February 2025)

In February 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]] said he had referred Ocasio-Cortez to the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] after her office produced a presentation advising illegal migrants on how to avoid U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement.[4] Homan, describing her as among the Democratic Party's most liberal members, said he was working with the Department to determine "where is that line that they crossed," and made the referral to the deputy attorney general.[4]
