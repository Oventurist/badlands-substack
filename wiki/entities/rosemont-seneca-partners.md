# Rosemont Seneca Partners

Rosemont Seneca Partners is the investment firm associated with [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] that served as the professional address through which he conducted business during his father's vice presidency.[1] In reporting surfaced by congressional investigators and covered in the Badlands News Brief of August 18, 2023, the firm's significance lies less in its investment record than in its role as the email domain at which Hunter Biden received internal communications from the Office of the Vice President.[1]

## Role in the Biden email controversy

Documents recovered from Hunter Biden's abandoned laptop indicated that Biden aide John Flynn copied Hunter on ten emails containing then-Vice President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s daily schedule between May 19 and June 15, 2016.[1] Those messages were directed to Hunter's Rosemont Seneca Partners email address rather than to a personal account, embedding the firm directly in the chain of official vice-presidential correspondence.[1] One of the emails detailed plans for a telephone call between the Vice President and Ukraine's then-president [[petro-poroshenko|Petro Poroshenko]].[1]

At the time these emails were transmitted, Hunter Biden was serving on the board of the Ukrainian energy company [[burisma|Burisma]], an entity the Obama-Biden [[state-department|State Department]] itself had assessed as corrupt.[1] The overlap between the Rosemont Seneca address, the Burisma board seat, and the receipt of the Vice President's Ukraine-related scheduling material forms the core of the congressional interest in the firm.[1]

## Significance

For critics, Rosemont Seneca functions as the connective tissue between the family's private commercial interests and the conduct of United States foreign policy toward Ukraine, undercutting Joe Biden's repeated assertion that he never discussed his son's overseas business dealings.[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the revelation as evidence that the correct journalistic question was the inverse of the one usually asked — not whether the president discussed business with his son, but how often he engaged Hunter in his official capacity as Vice President.[1] The firm's name recurs throughout the [[biden-pseudonym-emails|pseudonymous email]] records sought by the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] from the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]].[1]
