# Alexander Parvus

Alexander Israel Helphand, known by the pseudonym Parvus, was a Marxist theoretician, arms dealer and financier whose career Matthew Ehret presents as one of the clearest illustrations of the overlap between revolutionary socialism and imperial geopolitical strategy.[1]

## Mentor to Trotsky

Parvus was closely associated with [[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]] throughout the revolution of 1905 and beyond, sharing a prison cell with him after the revolution failed and supplying the intellectual seed of Trotsky's doctrine of [[permanent-revolution|Permanent Revolution]].[1] Trotsky, who lived with Parvus in Munich after the Second Congress of the Russian Socialist Labor Party, called him "unquestionably one of the most important of the Marxists at the turn of the century," praising his "fearless thinking" and "virile, muscular style."[1]

## Arms dealer and Young Turk operative

Parvus was, in Ehret's description, "a strange sort of communist": from 1910 to 1917 he worked as an arms dealer in Turkey, where he helped organize the Balkan Wars of 1912–13 and colluded with allies in the Young Turk government to entangle the collapsing Ottoman Empire in the First World War.[1] Through this work he became a millionaire and a financier in his own right.[1] His role as propagandist and arms dealer for the Young Turk leadership — deployed to set a weakened Ottoman Empire on fire — is described as still being felt across the Turkish world today.[1]

Parvus' association with Vladimir Jabotinsky, shared with Trotsky, is credited with setting the stage for the most fascist elements of Zionism to emerge after the Second World War.[1]

## Pan-Europa connection

Parvus' association with the [[pan-european-union|Pan-European Union]], founded by Count [[richard-coudenhove-kalergi|Richard von Coudenhove-Kalergi]] in 1923, is presented as a further anomaly leading into the deeper power structures lurking below the surface of recorded history.[1]
