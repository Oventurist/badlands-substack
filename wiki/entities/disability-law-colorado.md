# Disability Law Colorado

Disability Law Colorado (DLC) is a disability rights advocacy organization in the state of Colorado that monitors conditions in state institutions and presses federal and state authorities to remedy what it describes as systemic failures in the treatment of disabled people in custody.[1]

## Campaign for a federal investigation of Colorado corrections

DLC spent years urging the United States Department of Justice to examine Colorado's corrections system, filing repeated complaints alleging "systematic failures" within the [[colorado-department-of-corrections|Colorado Department of Corrections]] (CDOC).[1] That effort culminated on December 8, 2025, when the DOJ announced an investigation into 33 facilities operated by the CDOC and the state's Department of Youth Services.[1]

Reporting by the Denver alternative weekly *Westword* argued that the investigation was not in fact triggered by DLC's complaints, characterizing it instead as a politically motivated action tied to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s continued efforts to secure the release of former Mesa County Clerk [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] from state prison.[1] The DOJ official who announced the probe, Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]], reshared posts on X celebrating the investigation as "how we FREE TINA PETERS."[1]

Despite what the outlet called the federal government's "dubious intentions," DLC and other local disability advocates publicly welcomed the intervention, describing it as "an important step toward accountability."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the investigation was "long overdue," praising DLC while criticizing *Westword* and the administration of Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]] for not having pursued such an inquiry during their years in power.[1] She reported hearing from sources close to the matter that staff at the La Vista Correctional Facility, where Tina Peters is held, were "buzzing around and trying to clean the place up," and described photographs of degraded air handlers at the CDOC mental health hospital in the same Pueblo government complex, arguing that "prison shouldn't be a resort, but we should at least commit to breathable air."[1]
