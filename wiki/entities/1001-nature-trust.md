# 1001 Nature Trust

The **1001 Nature Trust** (also known as the 1001 Club) was, in [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s account, created in 1970 by [[prince-philip|Prince Philip Mountbatten]] and [[prince-bernhard-of-the-netherlands|Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands]] alongside 999 other wealthy figures — whom Ehret calls "misanthropes" — in order to fund the blossoming environmental movement.[1]

The trust is presented as an outgrowth of the same network that produced the [[world-wildlife-fund|World Wildlife Fund]] and the 1954 founding of [[bilderberg|Bilderberg]].[1] Canadian environmental organizer [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]] is identified as a 1001 Nature Trust founder as well as a Bilderberger and vice-president of the World Wildlife Fund under Prince Philip.[1]

## Founding and mechanism

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that the 1001 Trust was founded in 1970 by [[prince-bernhard-of-the-netherlands|Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands]] in order to finance the green paradigm shift.[2] Bernhard — whom Ehret calls a card-carrying Nazi and the founder of the Bilderberg Group in 1954 — had worked alongside his associates [[prince-philip|Prince Philip Mountbatten]] and Sir [[julian-huxley|Julian Huxley]] to create the [[world-wildlife-fund|World Wildlife Fund]] a few years earlier.[2] The plan was simple: each of the 1001 founding members put $10,000 into the trust, which was then directed toward the green paradigm shift.[2]

## Membership

Other prominent members included international royalty, billionaires and technocrats who, in Ehret's characterization, wanted nothing more than to manage the promised Brave New World as "alphas."[2] Many were also members of the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]], including Canada's [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]], who later became Vice President of the WWF under Prince Philip's presidency.[2] Strong replaced another WWF Vice President and 1001 Club member, [[louis-mortimer-bloomfield|Louis Mortimer Bloomfield]], whom New Orleans District Attorney Jim Garrison identified as central to the Montreal-based assassination of the anti-Malthusian President [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] in 1963.[2]
