# Select Subcommittee on the Weaponization of the Federal Government

The Select Subcommittee on the Weaponization of the Federal Government is a panel of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] created to investigate the alleged politicization of federal law enforcement and intelligence agencies. Under chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], the subcommittee's early work centered on testimony from current and former [[fbi|FBI]] employees describing internal retaliation and misallocated resources.[1]

## May 2023 interim report

On Thursday, May 18, 2023, the subcommittee and the parent Judiciary Committee released an interim staff report revealing that at least three FBI agents had been suspended or had their security clearances revoked in connection with the January 6, 2021 Capitol incursion.[1] The report concluded that the bureau "weaponized the security clearance adjudication process to silence employees who fight against the politicized 'rot' within the FBI leadership," and said the disclosures "highlight egregious abuse, misallocation of law enforcement resources and misconduct with the leadership ranks of the FBI."[1] The agents identified as suspended pending review were [[marcus-allen|Marcus Allen]], [[stephen-friend|Stephen Friend]] and [[brett-gloss|Brett Gloss]].[1]

Whistleblowers appearing before committee Republicans also stated that the FBI had become "enveloped in politicization" and alleged "serious abuses" over an extended period, including the transfer of customer transaction data from [[bank-of-america|Bank of America]] to the bureau without customer knowledge or consent.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] wrote that the report vindicated long-standing suspicions that the bureau was persecuting its own agents for refusing to conform to leadership's political line.[1] He argued that critics of the subcommittee's work offered no substantive rebuttal to the whistleblower designation Jordan had applied to the witnesses, relying instead on procedural complaints about document handling and on attacks on the witnesses' character — which he read as evidence that the underlying claims were credible.[1]
