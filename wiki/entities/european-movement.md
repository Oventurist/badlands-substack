# European Movement

The European Movement was a postwar organization founded to promote the political and economic unification of Europe, and is identified in [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 4" as one of the vehicles that led to the establishment of the [[european-union|European Union]].[1]

DeLarme names the Polish socialist [[jozef-retinger|Józef Retinger]] as a founder of the movement after the Second World War, a role that helped earn him the description "father of the Bilderbergers."[1] In DeLarme's telling, the movement's unification goal was one that had been pursued for centuries by sub-groups hidden within [[freemasonry|Freemasonry]] and the [[knights-templar|Templars]], and it ran in parallel with Retinger's work in creating the informal Bilderberg conferences of 1952–54 and the [[bilderberg-group|Bilderberg Group]] itself.[1]

Retinger's associated campaign for an "Atlantic Community" was intended to make Europe's development an important political aim for American politicians and prevent a U.S. retreat into political isolation, since he believed control of world affairs could not be achieved without American participation.[1]
