# Serzh Sargsyan

**Serzh Sargsyan** is an Armenian politician who served as President of [[armenia|Armenia]] and subsequently, briefly, as Prime Minister before resigning in April 2018 amid mass anti-government demonstrations.[1] In the Badlands Media analysis by Joe Lange, Sargsyan is presented as a textbook example of the authoritarian, corrupt national leader installed or protected by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] to run a proxy state, and his fall is read as part of a worldwide awakening rather than a purely domestic Armenian political crisis.[1]

## Constitutional maneuver

Armenia imposed term limits on the office of the President, and Sargsyan had served his two five-year terms.[1] He had publicly promised not to seek any higher office; the country then amended its constitution in a 2015 referendum to change the government from a presidential to a parliamentary system, making the presidency largely ceremonial and strengthening the office of prime minister — a position not constrained by term limits.[1] Sargsyan then became Prime Minister, which the article characterizes as an attempt to make himself a permanent ruler over Armenia.[1] Former Armenian foreign minister Vartan Oskanian told Al Jazeera that the demonstrations were sparked precisely by Sargsyan reneging on his promise not to seek high office if the constitutional change passed, noting that the new prime ministership combined the powers of both offices and that "that was the entire protest against him."[1]

## Resignation

Thousands of anti-government protesters took to the streets of Yerevan beginning on April 13, 2018, accusing his government of corruption and authoritarian rule.[1] Sargsyan resigned on a Monday later that month, saying he would adhere to the protesters' demands and step down.[1] Opposition leader [[nikol-pashinyan|Nikol Pashinyan]] immediately demanded a snap parliamentary election and told a rally in Yerevan that he was "ready to discuss conditions of Sargsyan's resignation and transfer of power."[1] Observers quoted in the coverage described soldiers, priests, children and parents joining the demonstrations, and framed the moment as Armenians realizing they had the power to change a system widely regarded as corrupt.[1]

## Aftermath

Following Sargsyan's resignation a new anti-corruption agency was established in Armenia, and Sargsyan himself was indicted for corruption; he was not the only Armenian official indicted.[1] The article links the timing of this "velvet revolution" to the subsequent shutdown of four alleged CIA supercomputer systems in Armenia on August 30, 2018, arguing the sequence was not coincidental.[1] It further connects the episode to [[qanon|Q]] drop material posted in April 2018 that referenced Armenia and declared that "clowns" were losing control.[1]
