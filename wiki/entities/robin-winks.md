# Robin Winks

Robin Winks was an author and historian whose work documented the relationship between American universities and the intelligence services.[1]

## Cloak and Gown

Winks is the author of *Cloak and Gown: Scholars in the Secret War*, a lengthy study of the recruitment of academics into wartime and Cold War intelligence work.[1] In that book he argues that [[norman-holmes-pearson|Norman Holmes Pearson]] used [[yale-university|Yale University]]'s American Studies program as a recruiting tool for U.S. intelligence agencies, particularly the newly formed [[central-intelligence-agency|Central Intelligence Agency]].[1]

Winks also assessed Pearson's wartime output for the [[office-of-strategic-services|Office of Strategic Services]], writing that "Some of his best work, done for the OSS in its final months, were analyses of the intelligence services of other nations."[1]

## Significance

Winks's scholarship is cited as primary documentation of the scholar-to-spook pipeline, establishing that the liaison between intelligence and academia predates the CIA and reaches back to the OSS era.[1]
