# Scott Adams

Scott Adams is the creator of the Dilbert comic strip and a commentator known for his predictions about American political and social outcomes. In late 2023 he became a peripheral figure in coverage of the killing of Philadelphia journalist [[josh-kruger|Josh Kruger]].[1]

## Kruger episode

Kruger, a left-wing journalist who frequently mocked conservatives on X, had sarcastically referred to Adams as "Nostradamus" days before his death, mocking Adams's prediction that people would be dead "within the year" of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s election.[1] After Kruger was shot dead in his Philadelphia home in the early hours of October 2, 2023, Adams responded to reports of the killing by claiming he had an alibi — a quip that circulated widely alongside victory laps taken by right-leaning influencers.[1]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary on the episode, by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], did not begrudge the reaction but argued the focus belonged on the underlying problem of rising urban crime rather than on the irony of the moment.[1]

## Role in the Matt Gaetz allegations

In September 2021, after a federal grand jury indicted former prosecutor [[david-mcgee|David McGee]] for attempting to extort [[don-gaetz|Don Gaetz]], Adams came forward and posted screenshots of a text conversation between himself and [[jake-novak|Jake Novak]], broadcast media director of the Consulate General of Israel in New York.[2] Adams explained that the two had a relationship because Novak often sought to have pro-Israel messaging inserted into the Dilbert comic strip.[2] "People with connections to Israel had a high interest in me during the Trump days. Presumably to influence me," Adams told POLITICO, adding that he and Novak shared an interest in the mechanics of persuasion and in political stories with an Israel or persuasion angle.[2]

Novak texted Adams attempting to convince him that [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] was guilty of the sexual-misconduct allegations, citing sources inside the investigation.[2] Adams pushed back, saying the claims were not credible while Gaetz's extortion allegations were credible and backed by documents; when Novak argued the documents did not prove extortion, Adams refuted him.[2] The Israeli government subsequently distanced itself from Novak, though it neither fired nor disciplined him.[2]
