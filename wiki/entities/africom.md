# United States Africa Command

United States Africa Command (AFRICOM) is the combatant command with responsibility for Libya, and figures in the article's Benghazi timeline.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

According to the [[house-select-committee-on-benghazi|House Select Committee]] report as relayed by The Hill, AFRICOM learned of the attack just thirty minutes after it began, citing the 2014 Leidig transcript.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article observes that this left ample time to respond, given that the assault lasted more than seven hours, yet no help came.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

It further states that the African Command already knew about the attack and was preparing to respond, and that the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] was "spinning up" assets to deploy — but that the order to launch was never given.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) AFRICOM's readiness is used alongside the Aviano Air Base squadron's account to argue that the failure at Benghazi was a matter of withheld orders rather than distance or capability.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Metele strike (May 2026)

The Nigerian Army said a May 2026 strike that killed the [[islamic-state|Islamic State]] figure [[abu-bilal-al-manuki|Abu-Bilal Al-Manuki]] took place in Metele, Borno State, in close coordination with U.S. Africa Command, according to Reuters.[2] [[nigeria|Nigeria]]'s State House said early assessments confirmed Al-Manuki and several lieutenants were killed at his compound in the Lake Chad Basin, and the Nigerian Army reported the operation ran from about 12:01 a.m. to 4 a.m. on a Saturday with no casualties or asset losses.[2]

## Presence in Zambia

In April 2022, Washington announced the launch of AFRICOM's Office of Security Cooperation at the US Embassy in Lusaka, the capital of [[zambia|Zambia]], a move the US government said aimed to improve "military-to-military relations" and expand cooperation in force management, modernization and professional military education for Zambian security forces.[3]

By mid-2024 the deployment had become politically contentious.[3] Opposition Patriotic Front lawmaker [[stephen-kampyongo|Stephen Kampyongo]] told [[rt|RT]] that Zambia had no need for a US military presence and that AFRICOM's objectives in the country were "not well spelled out," which he said had sparked fears in Zambia and among neighbours including [[zimbabwe|Zimbabwe]].[3] Regional concerns and lawmakers' demands for withdrawal were reported alongside Zimbabwean complaints, relayed to Russia, that the United States was consolidating military strength in Zambia and driving a wedge between the two neighbours.[3]
