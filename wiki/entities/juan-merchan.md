# Juan Merchan

Juan Merchan is a New York State Supreme Court justice who presided over the Manhattan criminal prosecution of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] arising from allegations of hush-money payments.[1]

In May 2023, Merchan granted prosecutors' request and issued a social-media gag order barring Trump from posting any evidence in the case, after prosecutors argued the former president had a history of targeting witnesses.[1] The order, issued on a Monday, also prohibited Trump from publicly discussing details about witnesses or other evidence prosecutors intended to use against him.[1] It came as Trump's legal team sought to move the state criminal case to federal court.[1]

The underlying charges stemmed from Trump's April 4, 2023 arraignment on 34 counts of falsifying business records, with prosecutors alleging he covered up a $130,000 non-disclosure payment to [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] two weeks before the 2016 election; Trump denied both the affair and any wrongdoing.[1] Reporting also noted that Merchan had been placed under heightened protection.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] dismissed the order's effectiveness, writing that "the gag won't work" and that the evidence would emerge eventually, while what he called "the Soros-backed Deep State AGs of the world look the fool."[1]

## Third recusal motion (August 2024)

In a court filing made public on Thursday, August 1, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s defense attorneys asked Merchan for a third time to recuse himself from the [[trump-hush-money-case|criminal hush money case]], arguing that a change in the national political landscape required it.[2] The motion centered on an alleged conflict involving the judge's daughter and Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], by then the presumptive Democratic presidential nominee.[2] "Your Honor's daughter has a long-standing relationship with Harris, including work for political campaigns. She has obtained — and stands to obtain in the future — extensive financial, professional, and personal benefits from her relationship with Harris," wrote defense lawyers Todd Blanche and Emil Bove.[2] Describing Harris as Trump's "presumptive opponent," they argued that Merchan's daughter had an "extremely beneficial working relationship" with Harris because her company had been a top vendor to Harris' 2020 presidential campaign.[2] New York's Advisory Committee on Judicial Ethics had determined the previous year that Merchan's impartiality "cannot reasonably be questioned" on the basis of his daughter's work as a political consultant.[2]

## Gag order appeal

In May 2024 former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] asked New York's highest court to rescind the limited gag order Merchan had imposed in his criminal trial, which barred him from commenting publicly about witnesses, jurors, and lawyers.[3] The sealed filing followed a ruling by an intermediate appeals court upholding the order, in which the court said Merchan had appropriately balanced Trump's free speech rights against the court's need to control the trial.[3] Trump has frequently attacked the judge — conduct the order permits — and has called the gag order unconstitutional; the Manhattan District Attorney's office offered no immediate comment.[3]

## March 2024 gag order

On March 26, 2024, Merchan issued a gag order against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the Manhattan criminal case he oversees.[4] The order barred Trump from speaking publicly about witnesses and counsel involved in the case — excepting District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] himself — and from commenting on staff of the court and the district attorney's office, including their family members, as well as on any prospective or seated juror.[4] It issued hours after Trump attacked Merchan and his daughter in a Truth Social post that morning, and one day after Merchan set the trial date for April 15 in a case carrying 34 counts of alleged falsification of business records.[4] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the order as Merchan taking his "turn" at election interference following the appellate rebuke of [[arthur-engoron|Arthur Engoron]].[4]

## April 2024 Juror Anonymity Ruling

During the third day of jury selection in the Manhattan hush-money trial, Merchan abruptly dismissed a previously seated juror after she told him she had developed concerns about her identity becoming public.[5] Although he had kept prospective jurors' names private, they had disclosed employers and other identifying information in court, and reporters had published descriptions of the seven jurors already sworn.[5] After excusing her, Merchan barred the press from reporting jurors' employer information, declaring "I have the legal authority to do it."[5] Badlands writer Ashe in America characterized the move as Merchan "changing the rules again" — defensible for the protection of jurors but doing nothing to answer the underlying question of whether Trump could receive a fair trial in New York.[5]

## May 2024 second contempt ruling

On Monday, May 6, 2024, Merchan ruled that Trump had again violated the gag order in the hush-money trial, and warned that he would consider jailing the former president if the violations continued.[6] After the ruling, testimony resumed with retired Trump Organization comptroller [[jeffrey-mcconney|Jeffrey McConney]] describing the business records and accounting practices at the heart of the case, and Merchan sent the jury home shortly after 4 p.m.[6] Speaking to reporters before leaving the courthouse, Trump called Merchan "a disaster" and said, "This judge has given me a gag order and said, 'You'll go to jail if you violate it.' Frankly our Constitution is much more important than jail. I'll do that sacrifice any day."[6] Trump also complained about the trial timeline, saying the government had asked for two to three more weeks, which he said was intended to keep him off the campaign trail ahead of a scheduled rally in New Jersey.[6]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the standoff as "a major game of chicken," arguing that Trump's escalation was a continuation of a deliberate baiting strategy: on a practical level building precedent for future prosecutions of his opponents, and on a narrative level reinforcing his "Martyr-Champion" template each time the system was seen wielding a hammer against him.[6] The commentary closed by addressing the judge directly: "Next move is yours, Merchan."[6]

## Loren Merchan and the Authentic Campaigns controversy

Reporting circulated in May 2024 renewed scrutiny of Merchan's family connections to Democratic politics.[7] His daughter, Loren Merchan, is president and a partner of the political consulting firm Authentic Campaigns, which investigative reporter [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] said had been retained as a vendor by Michigan Governor [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]]'s political action committee "Fight Like Hell" and by the Arizona Democratic Party — payments totaling roughly $412,000 and nearly $160,000 respectively.[7] [[alex-soros|Alex Soros]], son and chosen successor of [[george-soros|George Soros]], reportedly met with Whitmer and various Arizona Democrats after those contracts were signed, and while Trump's trial before Merchan was under way.[7] Authentic Campaigns has also worked for Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] and for Representative [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]], and Loren Merchan has been linked to the Democratic Attorneys General Association.[7]

Merchan, himself a donor to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], has faced repeated calls to recuse over these familial ties but has refused, and instead imposed a gag order barring [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from discussing his family members.[7] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], characterized the arrangement as evidence of billionaire oligarchs functioning as an organized crime syndicate exerting control over American government and courts.[7]

## Stormy Daniels testimony (May 2024)

During [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]]' testimony in the Manhattan trial, Merchan sustained objections from Trump's attorneys as she described in graphic detail her alleged 2006 sexual encounter with the former president, including her account of emerging from a hotel bathroom to find him waiting in his underwear.[7]

## July 2024 postponement of sentencing

On Tuesday, July 2, 2024, Merchan postponed sentencing in the [[trump-hush-money-case|hush money case]] until at least September, agreeing to weigh the impact of the Supreme Court's ruling on [[presidential-immunity|presidential immunity]] issued the previous day.[8] Trump had been scheduled for sentencing on July 11, days before the Republican nominating convention; the new date was set for September 18 at the earliest, with Trump's lawyers arguing the immunity ruling justified vacating the conviction altogether.[8] Manhattan prosecutors offered no immediate comment on the postponement.[8] Badlands commentary by Ashe in America cited the decision as evidence that "the boomerangs are coming around now," noting pointedly that "Judge Merchan just vacated President Trump's sentencing."[8]

## May 2024: close of the defense case

After the defense rested on 21 May 2024, Merchan advised jurors that the court session might extend late into the day to accommodate both prosecution and defense summations, which would represent the jury's final opportunity to hear from either side.[9] He dismissed the jury until 28 May, when closing arguments were scheduled, and attorneys were set to return later that Tuesday to discuss his instructions for deliberations.[9] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the proceeding as a partisan political attack conducted in Merchan's courtroom and called for an inquiry into the judge's potential abuses of discretion.[9]

## April 2024 trial start and conflict allegations

As the Manhattan trial was set to open on Monday, April 15, 2024, Merchan denied a defense bid to adjourn the case over what Trump's lawyers described as overwhelming pretrial publicity, calling adjournment "not tenable."[10] Trump, who took his 2024 campaign to New York for the proceeding and derided it as a "Fake Biden Trial," posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that Merchan was "perhaps the most highly conflicted Judge in New York State history" and complained that his legal team had been given insufficient time to analyze "hundreds of thousands of pages of documents that D.A. [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] illegally hid, disguised, and held back from us."[10] The Wall Street Journal noted that the 34 felony counts in the indictment were all tied to records prosecutors said Trump falsified while reimbursing [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] for the [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] payment, comprising 11 invoices, 12 general ledger entries and 11 checks.[10]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read Trump's "Fake Biden Trial" framing as evidence of his mastery of [[narrative-warfare|narrative warfare]], arguing that those interpreting his rhetorical deployments at face value were reacting to the War of Stories in a manner consistent with the Deep State-controlled media industrial complex, and concluding that Trump was not trapped in the information war with his adversaries — "they're trapped in the Info War with him."[10]

## Hush Money Sentencing After the 2024 Election

Merchan postponed a sentencing hearing set for November 26, 2024, in the New York hush money case following [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s reelection.[11] In a filing on November 19, 2024, prosecutors suggested they were open to postponing sentencing further, acknowledging that "consideration must be given" to pausing the case until the end of Trump's second presidential term in 2029.[11] Trump's lawyers had urged Merchan to throw the case out entirely "to facilitate the orderly transition of executive power" and had also requested a new trial, a decision on which remained pending.[11]

## Loosening of the Trump gag order

On June 25, 2024, Justice Merchan loosened the gag order in [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Manhattan criminal case, freeing the former president to criticize witnesses who testified against him, including [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]], and others involved in the seven-week trial he had presided over that spring.[12] He ruled that once Trump was sentenced on July 11 he could also publicly assail prosecutors and their relatives, while a separate order barring disclosure of juror identities or attacks on jurors by name remained in force.[12] Merchan appeared conflicted about allowing broad complaints about the jury, writing that "it would be this court's strong preference to extend those protections" but that he believed the law required him to drop the restrictions.[12]

## Juror comment disclosure (June 2024)

In early June 2024 Merchan provided the lawyers in the [[trump-hush-money-case|Trump hush money case]] with information about a comment posted on the court's Facebook page reading, "My cousin is a juror and says Trump is getting convicted. Thank you folks for all your hard work!!!" — a remark indicating that a juror may have disclosed a predetermined guilty verdict.[13] Conservative Treehouse called the public release "very sketchy," arguing that Merchan "doesn't have an integrity bone" and that the comment he chose to highlight was innocuous and random.[13] The outlet speculated that either something bigger was being diluted by the story, or Merchan was provoking [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] into discussing jurors and thereby violating his gag order ahead of sentencing, or he was seeking to create a mistrial in order to exit the case or restart it with an extended gag order.[13]

## March 2024 gag order

On the eve of the "hush money" trial concerning alleged payments to adult film actress [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] during the 2016 election, Merchan issued a gag order blocking [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from making public comments about court staff, jurors, witnesses or prosecutors, writing that "given that the eve of trial is upon us, it is without question that the imminency of the risk of harm is now paramount."[14] Trump responded on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that the decision imperiled his First Amendment rights, said Merchan was "suffering from an acute case of Trump Derangement Syndrome," and asserted that the judge's adult daughter "represents Crooked Joe Biden, Kamala Harris, Adam 'Shifty' Schiff, and other Radical Liberals" and had posted a picture of him behind bars, making a fair trial impossible.[14] He further accused [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] "and their Hacks and Thugs" of tracking him across the country in an effort to persecute him, while constitutional law professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] suggested appealing the order.[14]

## Jury instructions in the hush money trial

Merchan's charge to the jury on May 28, 2024 drew intense criticism from legal commentators. Constitutional law professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] described one element as "the coup de grace instruction," noting that Merchan told jurors there was no need to agree on what occurred and that they could disagree over which of the three predicate crimes had been committed.[15] Turley argued that this meant a jury could split 4-4-4 and still be treated as unanimous.[15]

Turley also observed that the jury would not be given written copies of the instructions but could ask for portions to be read back, prompting Missouri Attorney General [[andrew-bailey|Andrew Bailey]] to respond, "This is absolute insanity."[15] Mid-afternoon on the first day of deliberations the jury sent a note to the court containing four specific requests.[15] Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that if the Trump prosecutions constituted a corruption exposure operation, "Merchan's courtroom is doing a lot of heavy lifting."[15]

## Day one of the 2024 trial

On the first day of the Manhattan hush-money trial, Monday April 15, 2024, Merchan barred [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from attending his son's high school graduation and threatened him with arrest should he go, and separately refused to permit him to attend a Supreme Court hearing in Washington on presidential immunity.[16] The day was otherwise consumed by evidence questions, scheduling and the beginning of jury selection, during which more than 50 prospective jurors were dismissed.[16] Merchan also left open the possibility of admitting evidence from the [[e-jean-carroll|E. Jean Carroll]] case, reasoning that excluding it would unfairly bind the state's hand.[16] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] opened its account of the day by calling the judge "a complete dick," a characterisation [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] endorsed while arguing that his rulings showed a continuing pattern of judicial discretion prejudicing the former president.[16]

## Clash with Robert Costello (May 20, 2024)

On Monday afternoon, May 20, 2024, Justice Merchan cleared the courtroom in order to reprimand the defense's witness [[robert-costello|Robert J. Costello]], who had scoffed at one of his rulings; at one point Merchan asked Costello, "Are you staring me down?"[17] At the end of the day, Trump attorney [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] asked the judge to dismiss the case, and Merchan reserved decision, leaving the verdict to the jury.[17]

## Postponement of sentencing (September 2024)

On Friday, September 6, 2024, Merchan delayed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s sentencing in the New York [[trump-hush-money-case|hush money case]] until November 26, after the November 5 election.[18] "This is not a decision this Court makes lightly but it is the decision which in this Court's view, best advances the interests of justice," he wrote.[18] The ruling responded to a defense request to postpone the September 18 sentencing so that Trump's attorneys could appeal a pending ruling on presidential immunity expected by September 16, and it averted what would have been the first ever sentencing of a former president on criminal charges.[18] Defense counsel [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] and [[emil-bove|Emil Bove]] had argued in an August 14 filing that "a single business day is an unreasonably short period of time" for such an appeal.[18]
