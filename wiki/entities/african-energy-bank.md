# African Energy Bank

The African Energy Bank is a multilateral lending institution being organized by African states to finance oil, gas, and other energy projects on the continent, conceived as an alternative to Western lenders that have curtailed hydrocarbon financing under pressure from climate activists.[1]

## Background and rationale

African governments have faced boycotts by banks and, more recently, by insurers, both of which are themselves under growing pressure from climate campaigners who argue that Africa must remain the least-emitting continent and should leapfrog the hydrocarbon era, moving directly from charcoal to wind and solar.[1] International lenders such as the World Bank and the African Development Bank have been described as eager to finance such renewable projects.[1]

The article ingested here argues there is a practical problem with that prescription: transmission.[1] Solar and wind farms must be sited where sun and wind conditions are optimal rather than where grid infrastructure already exists, and many African countries lack transmission networks extensive enough to accommodate utility-scale renewables economically. That transmission remains a bottleneck even in mature markets such as Europe was cited as evidence of the scale of the challenge in Africa.[1]

## Structure and funding

The 18 members of the African Energy Bank initiative have agreed to contribute $83 million each, for a total of $1.5 billion. Afrexim Bank agreed to match that amount, leaving a roughly $2 billion gap intended to be filled by external institutions such as sovereign wealth funds, private funds, and other banks.[1]

According to the African Energy Chamber, a group advocating development of local oil and gas resources, some 125 billion barrels of oil and 620 trillion cubic feet of natural gas remain to be tapped on the continent. Major oil companies, if not major banks, have shown interest and are already moving to explore these resources despite activist pressure.[1]
