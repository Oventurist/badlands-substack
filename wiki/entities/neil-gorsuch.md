# Neil Gorsuch

Neil Gorsuch is an Associate Justice of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]]. In the Badlands Media corpus he appears as the justice most identified with a willingness to reexamine modern jury practice against the historical understanding of trial by jury at the time of the American Founding.

## The six-person jury case (June 2026)

The Badlands Brief of June 16, 2026, citing SCOTUSblog, reports that as the Court entered the final weeks of its term it agreed to hear three new cases, among them a challenge to whether the Constitution requires twelve-person juries in criminal trials. The case arises from Florida, one of six states that still permit smaller juries in certain criminal proceedings, and could prompt the Court to revisit [[williams-v-florida|*Williams v. Florida*]], its 1970 decision holding that the [[sixth-amendment|Sixth Amendment]] does not require twelve jurors.

The Brief specifically names Gorsuch among the several current justices who have signaled interest in reexamining that precedent, on the argument that the historical understanding of a jury at the time of the Founding may have required twelve members. Argument is expected in the term beginning in October.

## Reception in the Badlands corpus

Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treats the grant of certiorari as an opportunity for civic education, writing that if the case allows a reexamination of modern jury practice against the Founding-era understanding, "the American people could get a much needed education and maybe we could rapid top down change to constitutional juries." She connects the case to the [[badlands-jury|GART Jury]] experience run by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]. Commentator Jonathan Drake, quoted in the same edition, argues that because the Constitution "declined to define" trial by jury, "the objective external standard of historical precedent would determine what it meant" — and that this reasoning reaches beyond the number twelve to "any of their perversions of trial by jury."
