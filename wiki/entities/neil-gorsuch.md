# Neil Gorsuch

Neil Gorsuch is an Associate Justice of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] identified with originalist interpretation of constitutional text.[1]

## Jury size and the Sixth Amendment

In June 2026, as the Court agreed to hear a challenge to whether the Constitution requires twelve-person juries in criminal trials, reporting noted that Gorsuch was among several sitting justices who had signaled interest in reexamining the 1970 precedent [[williams-v-florida|Williams v. Florida]].[1] The argument advanced by those justices is that the historical understanding of a jury at the time of the Founding may have required twelve members, an approach rooted in the [[sixth-amendment|Sixth Amendment]]'s reliance on common-law meaning rather than on modern functional judgments.[1]

## Tariffs and the major questions doctrine

Gorsuch wrote a concurrence in the [[supreme-court-ieepa-tariff-ruling-2026|February 2026 decision]] holding that the [[international-emergency-economic-powers-act|International Emergency Economic Powers Act]] did not authorize President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s global tariffs.[2] He argued that Chevron deference had created "a presumption that was nearly the opposite of the [[major-questions-doctrine|major questions doctrine]]," putting a thumb on the scale in favor of delegated power whenever Congress failed to speak clearly, and that with Chevron overruled the conflict between the two doctrines had dissolved, making the Court's approach "not invention so much as return to form."[2]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] said she always notes where Gorsuch lands on opinions because he is "waging a personal war on the administrative state," a project she endorsed.[2]

## Chiles v. Colorado (2026)

Gorsuch wrote the majority opinion in [[chiles-v-colorado|Chiles v. Colorado]], decided 8-1 at the end of March 2026, striking down Colorado's 2019 ban on so-called "conversion therapy" for minors as a violation of the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[3] He held that the statute discriminated on the basis of viewpoint by barring licensed counselors from offering talk therapy directed at reducing unwanted same-sex attractions or aligning gender identity with biological sex while permitting "acceptance, support, and understanding" for clients exploring gender transition.[3] The opinion emphasized that speech does not lose constitutional protection simply because the government labels it "treatment" or a "therapeutic modality," and reversed the Tenth Circuit.[3]

## Dissent in the Hoffman execution (March 2025)

In March 2025 Gorsuch was the lone member of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] singled out by Badlands writers for defending religious liberty in the case of Jessie Hoffman Jr., whom [[louisiana|Louisiana]] executed by nitrogen gas after the Court denied last-minute relief in a 5-4 vote.[4] Hoffman's lawyers had argued that the method violated his constitutional rights, including his ability to practice his Buddhist religion in his final moments.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] gave a "shout out to Gorsuch for being the only Justice to actually believe in religious freedom," linking to his published opinion in the matter.[4] She wrote that she had "a great deal of respect for Justice Gorsuch," noting that he emerged from the Colorado legal community and, based on his book, "remained committed to equal justice under the law" — a difficult stance in an environment she characterised as dominated by "social justice communists," citing the *Masterpiece Cakeshop, Ltd.* litigation referenced in the dissent.[4] Details of the case are covered at [[louisiana-nitrogen-gas-execution|the Louisiana nitrogen gas execution]].[4]

## Silence in the Tennessee gender-care arguments (December 2024)

During the December 4, 2024 oral arguments over Tennessee's ban on gender-affirming care for minors, Gorsuch — widely regarded as a key vote — said nothing at all, a silence that observers described as one of the most notable twists of the session.[5] His significance stemmed from his authorship of the majority opinion in the 2020 case Bostock v. Clayton County, which held that transgender employees are protected from workplace discrimination, a decision cheered by LGBTQ+ advocates and roundly criticised by conservatives including colleagues on the bench.[5] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the "rainbow movement" had reportedly been looking for Gorsuch to signal his position during the arguments and did not get one.[5] See [[united-states-v-skrmetti]].[5]


## Confirmation in Badlands commentary

Gorsuch's April 2017 confirmation to the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] was cited by Badlands Media in 2023 as the first of three high court appointments by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]], with all three appointees credited by the author for a pivotal role in the overturning of [[roe-v-wade|Roe v. Wade]].[6]
