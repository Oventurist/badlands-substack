# Francis Galton

**Sir Francis Galton**, a half-cousin of [[charles-darwin|Charles Darwin]], is considered the father of [[eugenics]] and coined the term itself, from the Greek *eu* ("good") and *genés* ("generation" or "origin").[1]

In 1863 Galton theorized that if "talented" people married only other talented people, the result would be more preferable offspring; Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] notes that no context was ever offered for how this amorphous "talent" was to be measured.[1]

Galton did not stop at theory. He carried out an experiment in which the Galton, Darwin, Huxley and Wedgwood families bred only with one another, in an attempt to produce a "superhuman" breed. The result was the opposite of what was hoped: within two generations over 90% of the offspring either died at birth or were physically or mentally disabled, and the family tree contracted rather than expanded.[1] DeLarme observes that proponents of the eugenics agenda are frequently related to one another and evidently did not regard themselves as the inferior bloodlines.[1]

Though the four-family experiment failed, the movement Galton named continued to grow, particularly in the United States, where it was funded by [[john-d-rockefeller|John D. Rockefeller]].[1]
