# Lisa Page

Lisa Page is a former [[fbi|FBI]] lawyer best known for the text messages she exchanged with FBI agent [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]] criticizing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during the 2016 presidential campaign.[1]

## Role at the FBI and departure

Page briefly served on special counsel [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]]'s team and resigned from her role as an FBI lawyer after her messages with Strzok — with whom she was having an extramarital relationship — were discovered during an internal investigation.[1] The release of the texts to the media in 2018 became political fodder for Trump in his effort to discredit the Russia investigation during his presidency.[1]

## Privacy Act settlement (2024)

In July 2024, Page and Strzok reached settlements with the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] totaling $2 million in their lawsuits over the department's 2018 disclosure of the messages, which they alleged violated the Privacy Act.[1] Page received $800,000 and Strzok $1.2 million under settlement agreements obtained by [[cnn|CNN]] and finalized in court filings on Friday, July 26, 2024.[1] "While I have been vindicated by this result, my fervent hope remains that our institutions of justice will never again play politics with the lives of their employees," Page said in a statement provided by her attorneys.[1] The Justice Department declined to comment on the matter.[1]

## Texts about an Obama presidential daily briefing

The August 2023 Badlands article "Clowns in America," citing PJ Media, reported that text messages between FBI counterintelligence agent [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]] and Page — an FBI lawyer described as his former mistress — show that Strzok and then-FBI Assistant Director William Priestap were concerned about sharing sensitive evidence with the departing [[barack-obama|Obama]] White House.[2] Other texts from the same day show the two discussing apparent drama over one of Obama's [[presidents-daily-brief|presidential daily briefings]], on the same day in April 2014 that Obama fired [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] as head of the [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]].[2] The messages were not released by the FBI but were made available to John Solomon in September 2018.[2] Badlands writer [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] read the timing as evidence that Flynn had set a trap for the Obama administration.[2]

## The Rosenstein wire memo and cooperating-witness status

The July 2025 article "Shall We Play a Game?" by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats Page as a pivotal witness against Deputy Attorney General [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]].[3] Citing the Washington Examiner, it reports that Page — who was in the room in May 2017, days after [[james-comey|James Comey]]'s firing, and who wrote a memo about the meeting — told the Daily Beast that she took Rosenstein's suggestion of secretly recording President [[donald-trump|Trump]] seriously, contradicting later claims that the remark was sarcasm.[3] The memos she and [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] wrote were, according to the article, part of the basis for the New York Times story of September 21, 2018 and were withheld from Inspector General [[michael-horowitz|Michael Horowitz]].[3]

Lange writes that [[declas|DECLAS]] exposed the text messages between Page and [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]], that both were forced to resign from the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller]] team when the texts became public, and that Page then took a deal to become a cooperating witness and testified against Strzok, who in turn took a deal to testify against higher-ranking figures.[3] He argues Q's reminder that Page had flipped came immediately before the Rosenstein "wear a wire" story broke, which he does not regard as coincidental.[3]

## The Flynn emails and texts (Badlands account)

In "[[the-biggest-boomerang-in-history|The Biggest Boomerang In History]]," [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] cites Boston Herald reporting on an email exchange between Page, then an [[fbi|FBI]] lawyer, and agent [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]] in which the two discussed how agents interviewing [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] should remind him that lying to the FBI is a crime, with Page ultimately recommending that Strzok "slip it in" at some point in the conversation.[4] Lange treats the exchange as evidence of a conspiracy to commit by [[james-comey|Comey]]'s FBI, since the interview was designed to catch Flynn in a lie so that he would be fired.[4]

The declassified texts between Page and Strzok are described as narrative-war material — explosive when they reached the media and effective in shifting coverage from Trump–Russia collusion to [[fbi|FBI]] bias in [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] — while the emails released by Attorney General [[william-barr|William Barr]], which underpinned the dismissal of the Flynn case, were in Lange's view the substantive revelation.[4] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
