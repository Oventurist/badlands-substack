# Catherine Austin Fitts

Catherine Austin Fitts is a former official of the George H. W. Bush administration who has become one of the most cited independent critics of American financial governance, and in April 2025 she drew renewed attention through a wide-ranging interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] on his independent media platform.[1] Reporting on the interview by Lawyer Monthly described her as offering "a sharp critique of America's ruling class and the legal system," alleging that both played a central role in the nation's economic and institutional decline.[1]

## The 1990s asset-stripping thesis

Fitts' central argument in the interview was that a decisive shift occurred during the 1990s, in which America's political, corporate and legal elites abandoned national interests in favor of their own financial gain.[1] She framed this not as drift or incompetence but as deliberate policy: "It wasn't an accident," she said. "It was a decision. A decision to strip the country of its assets, hollow out its communities, and leave ordinary Americans to deal with the wreckage."[1] The framing places the legal profession — normally treated as a neutral referee — among the active participants in the transfer of public wealth into private hands.[1]

## Topics raised in the Carlson interview

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary on the interview, contributed by [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]], emphasized the unusual breadth of subject matter that Fitts and Carlson covered on what is arguably one of the largest podcasts in the world: deep underground bases, trillions of dollars of missing government money, a claimed "breakthrough" in free energy, secret space programs, and magnetic pole flips.[1] Sather characterized the conversation as possibly Carlson's most important interview to date, reflecting the view within the independent-media ecosystem that mainstreaming these subjects on a large platform is itself the significant event.[1]

The "missing money" theme is the through-line connecting Fitts' policy critique to her more speculative claims: if trillions in federal outlays cannot be accounted for, then in her account the beneficiaries are the same elite networks she blames for the hollowing out of American communities.[1]
