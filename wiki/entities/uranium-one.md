# Uranium One

Uranium One was a uranium mining company, originally a South African firm with assets in Africa and Australia, whose sale to Russian state interests forms the centerpiece of the article's argument that American strategic assets were monetized by political insiders.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Origins and merger

The article traces the company's path back to 2005, when Canadian mining financier [[frank-giustra|Frank Giustra]] orchestrated his first big uranium deal in [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]] with [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] at his side, and his fledgling company [[urasia-energy|UrAsia Energy Ltd.]] signed a preliminary deal for stakes in three uranium mines controlled by the state-run agency [[kazatomprom|Kazatomprom]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) In 2007 UrAsia merged with Uranium One, then a South African company, in what was described as a $3.5 billion transaction; the combined entity kept the Uranium One name and was controlled by UrAsia investors including [[ian-telfer|Ian Telfer]], a Canadian who became chairman.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Giustra's personal stake was estimated at about $45 million and, through a spokeswoman, he said he sold it in 2007.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

The article emphasizes that Uranium One's South African origin is a "key data point," linking it to [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]]'s deep South African commodity-trading ties and his expertise in uranium-for-oil swaps.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Sale to Rosatom

Uranium One controlled roughly 20% of uranium production capacity in the United States, and also held Kazakh mines described as among the most lucrative in the world.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Because uranium is a strategic asset with national-security implications, the sale had to be approved by a committee of United States government agencies; among those signing off was the [[state-department|State Department]], then headed by [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Russians assumed control in three separate transactions between 2009 and 2013, with [[rosatom|Rosatom]] acquiring the company through its subsidiary ARMZ, approved in 2010.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Export controversy

The [[nuclear-regulatory-commission|Nuclear Regulatory Commission]] assured Congress and the public in a November 2010 press release that "no uranium produced at either facility may be exported," and then-Chairman Gregory Jaczko repeated the assurance a year later in a letter to Senator John Barrasso of Wyoming, noting that neither Uranium One nor ARMZ held a specific NRC export license.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The Hill's review of NRC memos, quoted in the article, found that the commission did approve shipment of yellowcake uranium from the Russian-owned U.S. mines to Canada in 2012 through a third party, and that the Obama administration later approved some of that uranium going on to Europe.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

Rather than issue Rosatom a direct export license — which would have raised red flags in a Congress already suspicious of the deal — the NRC in 2012 amended an existing export license held by a Paducah, Kentucky trucking firm, RSB Logistics Services Inc., simply adding Uranium One to the list of clients whose uranium it could move to Canada.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The license, dated March 16, 2012, raised the volume RSB Logistics could ship to the Cameco Corp. plant in Ontario from 7,500,000 to 12,000,000 kilograms and added Uranium One to the "other parties to Export."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The Hill reported that the move escaped notice in Congress, and exports flowed from Wyoming to Canada and on to Europe between 2012 and 2014.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Donations to the Clinton Foundation

Canadian records showed a flow of cash to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] as the Russians gradually assumed control: Uranium One's chairman used his family foundation to make four donations totaling $2.35 million, none publicly disclosed by the Clintons despite Hillary Clinton's agreement with the Obama White House to identify all donors.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Shortly after the Russians announced their intention to acquire a majority stake, Bill Clinton received $500,000 for a Moscow speech from a Russian investment bank with Kremlin links that was promoting Uranium One stock.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Reported contributions from a constellation of people tied to Uranium One or UrAsia totaled between $1.3 million and $5.6 million.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)
