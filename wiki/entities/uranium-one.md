# Uranium One

Uranium One was a uranium mining company, originally South African, that after a 2007 merger with [[urasia-energy|UrAsia Energy Ltd.]] became a major producer with assets in Africa, Australia, [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]] and the United States. Its acquisition by the Russian state nuclear agency [[rosatom|Rosatom]] between 2009 and 2013 became one of the most contested episodes of the Obama administration, and is examined at length in the Badlands Media essay [[america-for-sale-part-3|America for Sale — Part 3]].

## Formation

According to the New York Times reporting quoted in the article, the path to Russian acquisition of American uranium began in 2005 in Kazakhstan, where Canadian mining financier [[frank-giustra|Frank Giustra]] orchestrated his first uranium deal with [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] at his side. After the two flew aboard Giustra's private jet to Almaty and dined with president Nursultan Nazarbayev, Giustra's company UrAsia Energy signed a preliminary deal within days for stakes in three uranium mines controlled by the state agency Kazatomprom.

In 2007 UrAsia merged with Uranium One, described by the Times as "a South African company with assets in Africa and Australia," in a transaction valued at $3.5 billion. The combined firm retained the Uranium One name and was controlled by UrAsia investors, including the Canadian [[ian-telfer|Ian Telfer]], who became chairman. Giustra's stake was estimated at about $45 million and, through a spokeswoman, he said he sold it in 2007.

## The Russian acquisition

Uranium One controlled roughly 20 percent of U.S. uranium production capacity. Because uranium is a strategic asset, the sale required approval by a committee of U.S. government agencies; among those signing off was the [[state-department|State Department]], then headed by [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]. Rosatom took control in three transactions from 2009 to 2013 through its subsidiary ARMZ. The Badlands article argues that the "Russian Reset" launched by Clinton and [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] in March 2009 was designed to clear the political pathway for this deal, citing Q drop #48, which asked whether the reset provided "clearance/pathway to complete the U1 deal."

## Donations to the Clinton Foundation

Canadian records cited by the New York Times showed a flow of cash to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] as the Russians assumed control: Uranium One's chairman used his family foundation to make four donations totalling $2.35 million, none publicly disclosed despite Hillary Clinton's agreement with the Obama White House to identify all donors. Publicly, only Telfer appeared as a Uranium One donor, at no more than $250,000 in 2007. Canadian tax filings for his Fernwood Foundation, however, showed $1 million in 2009 — the year the company appealed to the American Embassy to help keep its Kazakh mines — $250,000 in 2010, $600,000 in 2011 and $500,000 in 2012. Between $1.3 million and $5.6 million more came from people tied to Uranium One or UrAsia. Shortly after Rosatom announced its intention to take a majority stake, Bill Clinton received $500,000 for a Moscow speech from a Russian investment bank promoting Uranium One stock. Russian investor [[sergei-kurzin|Sergei Kurzin]] donated $1 million.

## Exports

The [[nuclear-regulatory-commission|Nuclear Regulatory Commission]] assured Congress in a November 2010 press release that "No uranium produced at either facility may be exported," and Chairman Gregory Jaczko repeated the assurance in a letter to Senator John Barrasso of Wyoming. Reporting by The Hill, quoted in the article, found that the NRC nevertheless approved shipments of yellowcake from the Russian-owned U.S. mines to Canada between 2012 and 2014, with some material later approved to travel on to Europe. Rather than granting Rosatom a direct export license — which "would have raised red flags inside a Congress already suspicious of the deal" — the NRC amended an existing license held by the Kentucky trucking firm [[rsb-logistics-services|RSB Logistics Services Inc.]] on March 16, 2012, adding Uranium One as an "other party to Export" and raising permitted volumes to the Cameco Corp. plant in Ontario from 7,500,000 to 12,000,000 kilograms. In The Hill's words, "the move escaped notice in Congress."

## Interpretation in the corpus

The Badlands article treats Uranium One as the clearest example of its thesis that American strategic assets were sold for private gain, quoting Q drop #1345 — "U1... Why? Money. Organized/planned by BC/HRC. Carried out by Hussein" — and stressing the company's South African origins as a link to the commodity trader [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]] and his uranium-for-oil swaps.
