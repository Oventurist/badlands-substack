# Brandon Smith

Brandon Smith is an alternative-economics writer and the author behind Alt-Market.us, whose analysis of the March 2023 banking crisis was excerpted and endorsed in Badlands Media's news brief for Monday, March 13, 2023.[1]

## Liquidity-crunch thesis

Writing after the failure of [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]], Smith argued that the bank's shortfalls were not really the cause of the crisis but a symptom of a wider liquidity drought he had predicted at Alt-Market months earlier, including the timing of the event.[1] He noted that SVB had been the sixteenth-largest bank in the United States until it abruptly failed and went into insolvency on March 10, 2023, following a $2 billion liquidity loss on bond sales that drove its stock down more than 60% and triggered a depositor run.[1]

On the trajectory of the crisis, Smith held that everything depended on the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]]'s response.[1] He wrote that the Fed might drag the crash out for a few months with various stop-gaps, and that a return to stimulus would save the banks and equities for a while, but rising inflation would then suffocate consumers within a year and companies would still falter.[1] His own expectation was that policymakers would rely on contained interventions and would not reverse rate hikes as many analysts anticipated, instead goosing markets at times with jawboning and false hopes of aggressive quantitative easing or near-zero rates while credit markets and stocks trended steadily downward.[1] He likened the dynamic to "a brush fire in a wind storm," where once the flames are sparked there is no way to put things back the way they were, and observed that if a liquidity crunch had in fact been the goal, "mission accomplished."[1]

## Reception at Badlands

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Smith's takes on the financial system as consistently compelling, characterizing him as a heterodox thinker ahead of the curve on macroeconomic predictions.[1] Burning Bright judged that Smith's analysis lined up with the broader Badlands view that the Fed and central bankers — and by extension the US economy — were caught in a pincer move between destroying insolvent institutions through further tightening and reigniting inflation through a pivot.[1]
