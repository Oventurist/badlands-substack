# Edward House

Colonel Edward House was an American political operative who served as the closest adviser to President [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]]. In the account given by Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]], House functioned as Wilson's "handler" rather than merely his counselor — a wealthy Texan from a wealthy British family who is characterized as a British asset placed alongside the American presidency.[1]

## Role in the Wilson presidency

Carlson writes that House was responsible for getting Wilson elected President in 1912, and that the creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] followed in 1913 under Wilson's signature despite being, in Carlson's description, very unpopular.[1] Wilson was re-elected in 1916 on a promise to keep the United States out of the First World War; Carlson argues that as soon as the re-election was secured, Wilson brought the United States into what the article calls "England's war," where American entry bailed out Britain against Germany.[1]

## Interpretation within the British Cabal thesis

House appears in the article as a case study in the [[british-cabal|British Cabal]]'s preferred method of control: rather than exercising power openly, the [[british-empire|British Empire]] is said to work through intermediaries who appear to be domestic actors of the country being steered.[1] The claim that "England" controlled Wilson is presented alongside the assertions that England was behind the formation of the Federal Reserve and behind the entry of the United States into the First World War.[1] These are contested revisionist claims presented as opinion commentary rather than established history.
