# Rand Paul

Rand Paul is a Republican United States Senator from Kentucky and Chairman of the Senate Homeland Security Committee.[1]

## Minnesota Somali welfare fraud investigation

In January 2026 Paul was one of the congressional leaders investigating a major welfare fraud scandal involving Minnesota's Somali immigrant community, working alongside House Oversight Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] (R-Ky.) and House Majority Whip [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]] (R-Minn.).[1] The investigators characterized the fraud as a "coordinated effort" and a "scam" to manipulate elections, congressional apportionment, and federal funding in blue states, with estimates that up to half of $18–20 billion in federally funded welfare spending had been lost to fraud.[1] Whistleblowers, including Minnesota state workers, provided affidavits claiming they had warned Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] and Attorney General [[keith-ellison|Keith Ellison]] about the problems without action — allegations both officials deny.[1]

## Fauci Covid origins hearing (July 2026)

Paul was the central antagonist at the July 2026 [[fauci-senate-covid-origins-hearing-2026|Senate hearing on Covid origins]], at which Dr. [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] invoked the Fifth Amendment and refused to answer questions.[2] Fauci's opening statement accused Paul of an "obvious obsession" with putting him "behind bars," citing the senator's calls for prosecution, "slanderous comments," and release of Fauci's personal journal.[2]

After Fauci pleaded the fifth, Paul stated: "The chairman has denied your assertion of privilege, and directed you to answer them. But you nonetheless refuse and stand on privilege, despite the existence of the pardon. The committee will have to consider after this hearing what appropriate action should be taken against you for the failure to testify after being directed to do so. It's against the law to obstruct an investigation of congress. There will be repercussions to your refusal to testify today."[2]

Ahead of the hearing Paul had warned of possible perjury charges if Fauci lied, and when asked whether he was trying to trap Fauci into perjury he replied: "There's no risk to perjury if you tell the truth. … The only thing he can't do is lie again."[2] Paul afterward said he would pursue Fauci for contempt of Congress, the charge that put Steve Bannon and Peter Navarro behind bars.[2]

## Fauci subpoena (July 2026)

On July 7, 2026, Paul announced a subpoena for [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] to testify before the Senate.[3] The move came amid a broader wave of legal and administrative actions against scientists affiliated with the [[national-institute-of-allergy-and-infectious-diseases|National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases]] over COVID-19 research and alleged efforts to obscure the virus's origins, with congressional subpoenas listed alongside debarments, indictments, arrests, and leadership removals as instruments of the campaign.[3]

## Release of the Fauci diaries (July 2026)

In late July 2026 Paul released more than 1,100 pages of [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]]'s private diary entries spanning late 2019 to 2022 as part of his investigation into COVID-19's origins and related research — material dubbed the [[fauci-files|Fauci Files]].[4] The journals allegedly show Fauci privately noting as early as January 2020 that the Wuhan wet market was an "amplifier" rather than the source of the virus, and recording early expert discussions treating features such as the furin cleavage site as potentially non-natural.[4]

Paul subsequently redacted an entry concerning Fauci's own alleged vaccine injury, a change noted by Badlands writers who speculated it might be a privacy measure.[4] Paul chaired the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee hearing at which Fauci was scheduled to testify, having long alleged that Fauci misled [[congress|Congress]] on gain-of-function research and having referred the matter for potential prosecution.[4] He stated that if Fauci lied in that testimony, his pardon — characterized in Badlands commentary as an illegitimate [[autopen-scandal|autopen]] pardon — would not protect him.[4] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that some of the disclosed material was "so criminal that it's hard to believe Rand Paul and his legislative friends have known this and Fauci still walks free."[4]

## Senate GOP leader speculation (March 2024)

In March 2024 Paul hinted that he might enter the race to become the next Senate GOP leader, and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] quickly voiced support for the idea.[5] Republicans were seeking a successor to [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]], who had announced he would step down from leadership in November while intending to hold his Senate seat until his term ended in January 2027.[5] The announced field consisted of [[john-cornyn|John Cornyn]] of Texas and [[john-thune|John Thune]] of South Dakota, while John Barrasso of Wyoming said he would instead seek the number two slot as party whip.[5]

Paul used X to tease the potential bid, telling his 5.1 million followers that many people had asked about his interest, then posting a poll in which he took a 96 percent share against Cornyn and Thune, each below 2 percent.[5] Musk backed the idea while daring Cornyn and Thune to submit their own names for consideration by the X user base.[5] ZeroHedge characterized Paul as a stalwart opponent of security-state mass surveillance, foreign interventionism including the Ukraine proxy war, and out-of-control spending, noting his Senate-floor ridicule of the latest stopgap spending package.[5]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that while Paul's record of blocking Deep State agendas or advancing America First bills was lacking, he had been operating on the same log-jammed battlefield as other good representatives, and that his value lay in his "Narrative Deployments" and their effect on the Collective Mind.[5] Burning Bright noted Paul's standing against Big Pharma — via his public war with [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] — and against the Central Banking Cartel, in keeping with the legacy of his father [[ron-paul|Ron Paul]].[5] Winning direct endorsements from Musk and [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|RFK Jr.]] was described as no small thing, potentially signalling that Paul would take center stage in 2024–25.[5]

## Opposition to the 2025 tariffs

In April 2025 Paul emerged as the lone Republican voice publicly supporting repeal of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s tariffs, saying he had heard similar views privately from colleagues who offered only "quiet whispers" of dissent.[7] "They whisper in my ear, 'free trade is good,'" Paul told CNBC. "Keep going. Keep going. But they don't want to say it because of the politics of it."[7] An avid supporter of free trade, Paul had been resolutely against the new trade policy since its announcement, viewing the [[liberation-day-tariffs|tariffs]] as unnecessary barriers to the flow of goods between countries.[7] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] rebutted the free-trade position by quoting President [[william-mckinley|William McKinley]]'s 1889 argument that "cheap merchandise means cheap men, and cheap men mean a cheap country."[7]

## Push to prosecute Fauci and test the Biden pardons (July 2025)

In July 2025, Paul urged the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] to bring charges against former senior national health advisor [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]], accusing him of lying to Congress about [[gain-of-function-research|gain-of-function research]] and asking the Department to test whether former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s blanket pardons would survive judicial scrutiny.[8] As chairman of the Senate Homeland Security Committee, Paul told Turning Point USA founder [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] that he believed Fauci "committed a felony by lying to Congress."[8] He argued that the only way to resolve the pardon question was through litigation: "You have to charge him with a felony; take him to court, and then the court will decide whether or not the pardon is upheld."[8] Badlands commentators praised the proposal as a potential catalyst for accountability on the [[autopen-scandal|autopen scandal]].[8]

## Fort Knox audit support (February 2025)

In February 2025 Paul publicly backed a physical audit of the gold reserves at [[fort-knox|Fort Knox]] after [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] suggested that [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] investigate the depository; the suggestion went viral on X and Paul, whose home state of Kentucky hosts the installation, replied to Musk with "Let's do it."[9]

## Push for federal prosecution of Fauci (November 2023)

In a November 2023 interview with Jan Jekielek on EpochTV's "American Thought Leaders," Paul said he was pursuing federal criminal action against Dr. [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] for perjury before [[congress|Congress]], having asked Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] to prosecute him for false testimony given during multiple congressional hearings.[10] "We haven't had a lot of luck," Paul said. "Garland is not paying attention to the referral, but [Dr. Fauci] did lie to Congress."[10]

Paul alleged that Fauci and multiple other parts of the federal government engaged in an "extensive" coverup of the origin, threat, and potential treatments for COVID-19, a two-year misdirection he detailed in his book *Deception: The Great Covid Cover-Up*, published in October 2023.[10] "Virtually everything they said in private, they said the opposite in the public at the same time," he said.[10] At the time Fauci was employed by Georgetown University, which he had joined in July 2023 as a distinguished university professor in its school of medicine's department of medicine.[10]


## Challenge to the Caribbean boat strikes (December 2025)

On December 2, 2025, amid escalating U.S. strikes on alleged drug-trafficking vessels in the Caribbean, Paul posted that "Coast Guard records show that prior to the reign of 'blow-them-to-smithereens' 21 percent of boats stopped off the coast of Venezuela possessed NO drugs!"[11] The statement was circulated in Badlands coverage as a caution against the lethal-strike policy, though contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] inverted the framing by noting that the same figure implied 79 percent of the boats stopped did carry drugs.[11]


## Tariff repeal resolution (October 2025)

Paul was the Republican sponsor of a bipartisan Senate joint resolution to repeal President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s global tariffs, which passed on Thursday, October 30, 2025 with four Republicans joining Democrats.[12] Paul was joined by [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]], [[susan-collins|Susan Collins]] and [[lisa-murkowski|Lisa Murkowski]]; the same proposal had failed on a 50-49 vote in late April after Vice President Vance cast the tiebreaking vote against it.[12] The one-page resolution declared terminated the national emergency Trump invoked on April 2, "[[liberation-day|Liberation Day]]," to authorize sweeping reciprocal tariffs — a symbolic victory unlikely to be taken up by the House or survive a veto.[12]

## Opposition to arming the Syrian rebels

The Badlands Media feature "Transparency and Prosecution" credits Paul with publicly taking on Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] over the funding of Syrian fighters, refusing to accept the claim that the armed opposition consisted of moderates.[13] Paul's argument, as summarized in the article by way of Reason, was that arming the Syrian rebels is not categorically different from arming [[isis|ISIS]], because those weapons eventually reach ISIS hands anyway.[13]

McCain, unaccustomed to being challenged, was visibly flustered; while criticizing Paul on Sean Hannity's program he slipped and implied that he had met with ISIS, when he had meant to vouch for the [[free-syrian-army|Free Syrian Army]].[13] The author treats the gaffe as an unintended admission.[13]
