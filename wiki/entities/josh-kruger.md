# Josh Kruger

Josh Kruger was a left-wing Philadelphia journalist who was shot and killed inside his home in the early hours of October 2, 2023, at the age of 39. His death drew national attention in part because Kruger had publicly mocked concern over rising violent crime in Democrat-run cities.[1]

## Killing

Kruger was shot seven times after someone entered his home, shooting him at the base of his stairs before fleeing. Wounded, Kruger ran outside seeking help from neighbors and collapsed on the 2300 block of Watkins Street, where police found him after responding to a call just before 1:30 a.m. He was rushed to Penn Presbyterian Medical Center and died shortly before 2:15 a.m.[1]

No arrests had been made in the immediate aftermath, and investigators reported no sign of forced entry. Deputy Police Commissioner Frank Vanore told reporters, "Either the door was open, or the offender knew how to get the door open. We just don't know yet."[1]

## Investigation

Detectives believed the killing may have resulted from a domestic dispute or may have been drug-related, according to three law enforcement sources cited by the Philadelphia Inquirer who spoke on condition of anonymity. Investigators reportedly recovered troubling text messages between Kruger and a former partner, as well as methamphetamine inside Kruger's bedroom.[1]

Kruger, who had publicly discussed his own struggles with addiction and homelessness, had written in April 2023 that an ex-partner had broken into his home: "The door was locked, so he had somehow obtained a copy of my keys."[1] About two weeks before his death he posted on Facebook that someone had come to his house looking for their boyfriend, identifying themselves as "Lady Diabla, the She-Devil of the streets."[1]

## Commentary and controversy

Kruger frequently mocked conservatives on X, and days before his death had sarcastically called Dilbert creator [[scott-adams|Scott Adams]] "Nostradamus" for having predicted that people would be dead "within the year" of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s election.[1] Adams responded to reports of Kruger's death by joking that he had an alibi, and a number of right-leaning commentators took a victory lap when the news broke.[1]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] declined to celebrate the death while noting that the timing was "eyebrow-raising," observing that regime-aligned online voices mock the [[america-first-movement|America First]] movement and the broader [[truth-community|Truth Community]] for defying the central narrative. The commentary argued that the case should force an honest conversation about rising crime and urban violence stripped of political charge, while expressing doubt that such a conversation would occur.[1]

## Family account of the accused

In October 2023 the family of [[robert-davis|Robert Davis]], the 19-year-old accused of the killing, told [[philadelphia-inquirer|The Philadelphia Inquirer]] that Davis was just 15 when he and Kruger began a years-long relationship involving drugs, and that Davis said Kruger had threatened to post sexually explicit videos of him online before the shooting.[2] The assertions by Davis's mother and older brother, drawn from recent conversations with him and years of watching his life unravel as he hid the relationship and his addiction, added troubling new complexities to a case that had drawn national attention.[2]

Kruger, 39, was shot seven times at the base of the stairs in his Point Breeze home after a man entered in the middle of the night; surveillance video and tips from friends and family led detectives to Davis, and a warrant was issued days later.[2] Police described the pair as acquaintances and said Kruger "was trying to help [Davis] get through life."[2] Separately, detectives discovered explicit photos and messages on Kruger's phone that law enforcement sources called "disturbing" and turned over to the department's Special Victims Unit for further analysis, without saying whether the material was connected to Davis.[2]
