# Prince Bernhard

Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands (rendered "Prince Bernhardt" in the source) is described by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as a leading SS operative who went on to create the [[bilderberg-group|Bilderberg Group]], the private transatlantic forum that Ehret says has managed the takeover of nation states since 1955.[1]

Ehret notes reporting that a Nazi membership card proved Bernhard had joined Hitler's party despite repeated denials maintained until the day he died, even while nominally in charge of Dutch resistance forces in 1944.[1] Bernhard's postwar prominence is offered as evidence for Ehret's central claim that Nazi personnel were not punished after the war but recycled into the institutions of the Western alliance, alongside those absorbed by [[allen-dulles|Allen Dulles]], MI6 and NATO under [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]].[1]

## Biography in DeLarme's account

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] gives the prince's full name and title as Bernhard Julius Coert Karel Godfried Pieter, Prince of the Netherlands and Prince of Lippe-Biesterfeld, and identifies him as the primary impetus behind the Bilderberg meetings.[2] He was a former member of the Nazi Schutzstaffel and an employee of Germany's [[ig-farben|I.G. Farben]] in Paris.[2] In 1937 he married Princess Juliana of the Netherlands and became a major shareholder and officer in Dutch Shell Oil, alongside Britain's Lord [[victor-rothschild|Victor Rothschild]].[2]

After the German invasion of Holland the royal couple moved to London, and it was there after the war that Rothschild and [[jozef-retinger|Józef Retinger]] encouraged Bernhard to create the Bilderberg Group.[2] He chaired the group personally until 1976, when he resigned following revelations that he had accepted large payoffs from Lockheed to promote the sale of its aircraft in Holland.[2]

## Statement on sovereignty

DeLarme presents one of Bernhard's remarks as a possible glimpse of the group's true agenda: "It is difficult to reeducate the people who have been brought up on nationalism to the idea of relinquishing part of their sovereignty to a supernational body."[2]
