# Matthew McCall

Matthew McCall is a U.S. Air Force colonel serving as the military judge overseeing the [[guantanamo-bay|Guantánamo]] military commission cases against [[khalid-sheikh-mohammed|Khalid Sheikh Mohammed]] and two co-defendants accused of plotting the 9/11 attacks.[1]

In an order issued Monday, August 12, 2024, and reported via a memo obtained by the [[washington-post|Washington Post]], McCall said he would examine whether it was legal for Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] to rescind plea agreements that would have spared the defendants the death penalty.[1] The ruling meant that the years-long effort to prosecute Mohammed and his alleged accomplices [[walid-bin-attash|Walid bin Attash]] and [[mustafa-al-hawsawi|Mustafa al-Hawsawi]] in the murders of nearly 3,000 people on September 11, 2001, could face another extended delay, as defense lawyers and prosecutors turned their attention to the propriety of Austin's eleventh-hour maneuver.[1]

Austin had intervened on August 2, barely 48 hours after the Defense Department announced that a senior official overseeing the Guantánamo proceedings had entered into pretrial agreements with the three men; Austin said the significance of such a decision meant responsibility for it "should rest with me."[1] The commissions McCall presides over have been bogged down for years amid disputes over government secrecy, conflicts of interest and the documented U.S. torture of many defendants, and the rescinded deals had been viewed by longtime observers as a route to overdue resolution for victims' families.[1] The intervention drew praise from some victims' families and blowback from critics of the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]'s oft-maligned judicial proceedings at Guantánamo Bay, Cuba.[1]
