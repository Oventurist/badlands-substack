# Adolf Hitler

Adolf Hitler was the dictator of Nazi Germany who shot himself in his Berlin bunker in 1945.[1] In November 2025 he re-entered the news cycle as the subject of a genetics documentary claiming that researchers had verified and sequenced his DNA.[1]

## 2025 DNA sequencing claim

A research effort that took more than four years was led by geneticist [[turi-king|Turi King]], a professor at the UK's University of Bath, who is known for identifying the remains of King Richard III.[1] King said she verified that a piece of material taken from a couch in the bunker where Hitler shot himself in 1945 was soaked in the dictator's blood, by comparing DNA recovered from the blood with a sample from a confirmed relative of Hitler's.[1]

The most striking reported finding was a mutation on the gene PROK2.[1] Variants in this gene are a cause of Kallmann syndrome and congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism; in boys these conditions can delay puberty and cause undescended testicles.[1] King described the conditions as characterized by low testosterone levels, with either absent or partial puberty, adding that roughly 5% of cases are associated with a micropenis — a small but normally structured penis.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the finding sardonically as four years of scientific research culminating in the "Prussian Micropenis" becoming "a matter of trusting the science."[1] She also flagged a broader pattern, noting that an unusual volume of Nazi-invoking news items appeared that weekend — including a cancelled German auction of concentration camp artefacts — and suggested that media reminders that "Nazis are bad" typically precede an attempt to label a contemporary political figure a Nazi.[1] A parallel commentary from [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] on the antifascist-designation story argued that a planned "Weimar phase" of American politics was meant to set up blaming a Nazi narrative on nationalists, populists, and Christians.[1]
