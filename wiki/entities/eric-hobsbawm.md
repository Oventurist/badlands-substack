# Eric Hobsbawm

Eric Hobsbawm was a Marxist scholar and historian best known in contemporary political commentary for introducing the concept of [[social-banditry|social banditry]] into the historical and sociological lexicon in 1959.[1]

Hobsbawm's framework described bandits — real and legendary — who operated outside the law and were revered for meting out justice in an unjust world, and rested on a Marxian analysis of power and economic relationships in agrarian societies in which banditry functioned as resistance to rampant inequality.[1] His examples spanned centuries and continents, from Robin Hood in 14th-century England to Jesse James and Billy the Kid in the post-Civil War United States and Pancho Villa in early 20th-century Mexico.[1] Historians continue to debate the theory.[1]

In December 2024, Politico revived Hobsbawm's concept as an explanatory framework for the widespread online celebration that followed the killing of UnitedHealthcare chief executive [[brian-thompson|Brian Thompson]].[1]
