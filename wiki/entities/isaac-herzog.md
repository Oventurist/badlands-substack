# Isaac Herzog

Isaac Herzog is the President of Israel, the office holding the constitutional power of pardon in the Israeli system.[1]

## Netanyahu pardon dispute

In February 2026, following a Washington meeting with Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly attacked Herzog for declining to pardon Netanyahu in his ongoing corruption cases, saying Herzog "should be ashamed" and adding, "You have a president that refuses to give him a pardon. I think that man should be ashamed of himself ... he's disgraceful."[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the pardon pressure campaign did not demonstrate, as commonly assumed, that Trump was subordinate to Netanyahu.[1] In his reading, either outcome benefited Trump: if Herzog granted the pardon, the optics would be that Trump had saved Netanyahu and that Bibi owed him everything; if Herzog refused, Netanyahu faced at least two likely convictions and a possible ten-to-fifteen-year prison sentence, given divisions in Israel severe enough that a judge might seek to "bury the Netanyahu era from view and memory."[1] Either way, the writer contended, Trump gained additional leverage over the Israeli prime minister.[1]

## Advocacy for the January 2025 Gaza Deal

In an address to the nation on the evening of January 15, 2025, Herzog urged members of Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s cabinet to approve the ceasefire and hostage release deal announced earlier that day between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]], declaring that he supported "the prime minister and the negotiating team for [their] efforts to reach a deal."[2] He called the agreement the "right" and "necessary move," stating that "there is no greater moral, human, Jewish, or Israeli obligation than to bring our sons and daughters back to us — whether to recover at home, or to be laid to rest."[2] Herzog cautioned against optimism about implementation, warning that the deal "when signed, approved, and implemented — will bring with it deeply painful, challenging, and harrowing moments" and that it ranked "among the greatest challenges we have ever known."[2] He nonetheless argued that the security cabinet and the full cabinet, both expected to vote on the framework the following day, had to approve it "to bring our sons and daughters home."[2]
