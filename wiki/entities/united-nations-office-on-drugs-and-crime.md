# United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime

The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) is the UN body responsible for monitoring global narcotics production and trafficking and for publishing the annual World Drug Report.[1]

## 2024 World Drug Report and the Afghan opium collapse

In its World Drug Report published in late June 2024, the UNODC warned that the collapse in opium production in Afghanistan could drive up overdose deaths as heroin users switch to synthetic opioids already proving deadly in Europe.[1] Cultivation of opium — the raw material for heroin — fell by 95 percent in Afghanistan in the year after the [[taliban|Taliban]] banned narcotics production in 2022, and although output in Myanmar rose 36 percent, global production still fell by roughly 75 percent.[1]

The agency cautioned that a prolonged shortage of Afghan opiates would have consequences in Afghanistan and in transit and destination countries alike, and that the purity of heroin on the market was expected to decline.[1] Preliminary field observations pointed to a possible slight increase in Afghan cultivation in 2024, but not a return to pre-ban levels.[1] No real shortages had been reported in the main destination markets of Europe, the Middle East and South Asia through early 2024, though the UNODC said that could change if future harvests remained small.[1]

## Badlands criticism

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the report as an admission against interest, arguing that the same institutions that condemn the Taliban were now complaining that the end of the heroin industry would cause more overdose deaths, and that the real culprit was the pharmaceutical industry's synthetic opiates such as fentanyl.[1] The take tied the episode to a longer claimed history in which the [[state-department|State Department]] — the Brief's deliberate euphemism for the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] — pursued control of drug production in Vietnam and later in Afghanistan after 9/11, culminating in the opioid crisis and the role of the [[sackler-family|Sackler Family]] and OxyContin.[1]
