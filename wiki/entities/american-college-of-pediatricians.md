# American College of Pediatricians

The American College of Pediatricians (ACP) is a United States physicians' organization that in June 2024 publicly urged its fellow doctors to reject the clinical protocols underpinning so-called "gender-affirming care" for minors.[1]

## "Doctors Protecting Children" declaration

In a joint declaration titled "Doctors Protecting Children," the ACP called upon the medical professional organizations of the United States "to follow the science and their European professional colleagues and immediately stop the promotion of social affirmation, puberty blockers, cross-sex hormones and surgeries for children and adolescents who experience distress over their biological sex."[1] The declaration argued that a number of U.S.-based medical organizations still base their treatment protocols for gender dysphoria on the "demonstrably flawed" Standards of Care developed by the World Professional Association for Transgender Health.[1]

The declaration specifically named the [[american-academy-of-pediatrics|American Academy of Pediatrics]], the Endocrine Society, the Pediatric Endocrine Society, the [[american-medical-association|American Medical Association]], the American Psychological Association, and the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry, faulting each for failing to update its recommendations in light of research revealing serious defects in the prevailing approach.[1] The ACP stated that "evidence-based medical research now demonstrates there is little to no benefit from any or all suggested 'gender affirming' interventions for adolescents experiencing Gender Dysphoria."[1]

## Reception in the Badlands corpus

Badlands Media commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] praised the organization's position, framing pediatric gender medicine as a practice that destroys childhood innocence and functions as cover for the sexual exploitation of children, and describing the ACP's stance as "better late than never."[1] The same commentary predicted that "reality will reassert itself" on the question of pediatric transition, citing an anecdotally more muted 2024 Pride season as evidence of shifting public sentiment.[1]
