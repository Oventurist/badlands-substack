# Rapid Support Forces

The Rapid Support Forces (RSF) are a Sudanese paramilitary organization and one of the two main combatants in the conflict that engulfed [[sudan|Sudan]] beginning in April 2023.[1]

## Role in the 2023 conflict

Deadly violence between the [[sudanese-armed-forces|Sudanese Armed Forces]] and the RSF broke out in mid-April 2023, initiating an escalating military struggle for control of the country.[1] The opacity of the factional picture on the ground was itself a notable feature of the episode: analysts covering the war reported that it was exceedingly difficult to determine reliably who was fighting whom and to what end.[1]

The RSF–SAF fighting created the security conditions that stranded dozens of American citizens seeking to leave Sudan and drew criticism of the [[state-department|State Department]]'s response.[1]

## Khartoum bio-lab seizure (2023)

In late April 2023 the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] declared a "high risk of biological hazard" in Khartoum after a military group seized one of Sudan's main bio-laboratories, a facility [[reuters|Reuters]] reported held cholera, measles and other deadly pathogens.[3] U.S. Special Forces had days earlier conducted an emergency extraction of personnel from the U.S. Embassy in Khartoum amid fighting between the Sudanese army and the RSF.[3]

Badlands writer [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] asked whose proxy the RSF might be if it was indeed the group that had taken the lab, and whether the RSF was "a force divided" between [[abdel-fattah-al-burhan|General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan]] and [[mohamed-hamdan-dagalo|General Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo]] ("Hemedti").[3] He pointed to Hemedti's February 2022 Moscow visit — attended without Burhan — and to Russia's Red Sea naval base arrangement with Sudan as indicators that outside powers were shaping the group's trajectory.[3]

## Port Sudan drone attacks (2025)

The Sudanese army blamed the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces for [[port-sudan-drone-attacks-2025|drone strikes on Port Sudan]] in early May 2025, which hit the airport's military base and, two days later, its civilian section, along with an army base and the country's main fuel depot.[2] The attacks grounded all flights at Sudan's main international port of entry and struck a city that had until then been considered a safe haven for hundreds of thousands of displaced people fleeing a two-year war.[2]

## Trump pledge on Sudan (November 2025)

Since the outbreak of war in April 2023, fighting between the [[sudanese-armed-forces|Sudanese army]] and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces has killed tens of thousands of people and displaced nearly 12 million.[4] In November 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would begin working on ending the conflict at the request of Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], condemning the "tremendous atrocities" of the civil war and pledging to coordinate with Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and Egypt.[4]
