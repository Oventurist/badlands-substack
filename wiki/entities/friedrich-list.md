# Friedrich List

Friedrich List (rendered in some accounts as Frederick List) was a German economist credited by Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] with being the first to codify the economic program of [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]] as the [[american-system-of-political-economy|American System of Political Economy]], in his 1827 work *Outlines of American Political Economy*.[1]

## Journey to America

List came to America in 1824 in the company of [[marquis-de-lafayette|Marquis Lafayette]], described as the last surviving leader of 1776, as part of an international effort to revive the sabotaged plans for a world of sovereign republics modeled on the American experience of 1776.[1] That effort failed — Lafayette acquiesced in 1830 to the scheme of re-instating a French king rather than declaring himself president — but during his American sojourn List studied Hamilton's system directly and set about codifying it.[1]

## Theory of productive forces

In the foreword to his 1827 book, List described his intellectual break from the value-based economics of [[adam-smith|Adam Smith]]. He wrote that he had previously understood transportation only through "the theory of values," observing its effects merely in market expansion and price reduction, but came to consider it "from the point of view of the theory of productive forces and in its total effect as a national transportation system," including its influence upon "the entire mental and political life, social intercourse, the productivity and the power of nations."[1] He claimed to have recognized the reciprocal relationship between manufacturing power and a national transportation system, and to have cast light on the necessity of entire national railroad systems before any economist in England, France, or North America had considered the matter from that standpoint.[1]

## Legacy in Germany and Russia

List transported this system back to Germany, where it drove rail development, industrial growth, and protectionism under the German Zollverein and finally blossomed under the rule of Chancellor [[otto-von-bismarck|Otto von Bismarck]].[1] His system was also studied, translated, and applied in Russia by a school of "American System economists," the greatest of whom was Transport Minister and Prime Minister [[sergei-witte|Sergei Witte]].[1]
