# Dutch East India Company

The Dutch East India Company was the European chartered trading enterprise whose posts gave rise to the colony of the Dutch East Indies, and which Badlands commentary describes as the one East India Company that exceeded the British East India Company in total profit generated.[1]

## Badlands framing

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], the company is introduced while discussing the family background of NATO secretary-general [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]], whose father Izaak Rutte was an importer in the Dutch East Indies.[1] The author noted that whether Izaak Rutte had any familial ties to the Dutch East India Company, or to any East India Company, was unknown, but described the association as striking.[1]

The same passage advanced the view that the British East India Company dropped its commercial trading licence in 1833 and spent the following four decades raising private armies and infiltrating or overthrowing governments in Southeast Asia, before, in the author's opinion, transforming into British intelligence around 1873.[1] The Dutch company's profitability is contrasted against that trajectory as part of a broader Badlands argument that mercantile empires functioned as forerunners of modern intelligence services.[1]

## Founding and the creation of the City of London

Erik Carlson's history of the financial system holds that the [[venetian-bankers|Venetian Bankers]] left Venice for Holland and formed the Dutch East India Company in 1602, and that at the same time they took control of the [[house-of-orange|House of Orange]], later the Dutch royal family.[2] The Dutch and [[british-east-india-company|British East India Companies]] formed around the same time in the early 1600s and competed in creating trade and trade routes between Europe and Asia, predominantly with India and China, giving rise to Dutch and British colonialism and to a British Empire that ruled a quarter of the world by the end of the 19th century.[2]

Carlson further claims that in 1688 the Dutch East India Company created the [[city-of-london|City of London]], making a deal with the British royal family to pay off its debt in exchange for the Crown abdicating power to the newly formed City; King James II ceded power to his daughter Mary II, who married William of Orange and ruled jointly with him, while the British parliamentary system was born and the [[deep-state|Deep State]] [[cabal|Cabal]] acquired a new home.[2]

## Association with fractional reserve banking

In "False Flags and the Occult" (March 2023), [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] noted that the first fractional reserve bank was launched within a decade of the Dutch East India Company's founding, and characterized the company as involved in black-market cartel activities including the drug trade, weapons trading, early analogues of color revolutions, slavery and high finance.[3] The pairing supports his broader argument that modern banking depends on manufactured instability — a dynamic he links to [[chaos-sorcery|chaos sorcery]] and to the use of [[false-flag-operation|false flag]] events.[3]

## Role in the colonial American economy

A February 16, 2023 Badlands Media essay on the legal origins of [[federal-reserve|the Federal Reserve]] holds that during the eighteenth century the American colonies were treated much like slave states by the [[british-crown|British Crown]], with colonists disempowered by statutes of Westminster that gave egregiously unfair advantages to Crown agents and to wealthy Dutch merchants in all manner of American trade and commerce.[4] These parties saw America as their own private cash crop, and the Dutch East India Company was, in the essay's account, responsible for the lion's share of the slave trade — citing [[anthony-napoleon|Anthony Napoleon]]'s book *Shadow Men* — while seeking a monopoly in every conceivable sphere of economic activity.[4]

The article adds that it was the Dutch merchants who founded Wall Street in New Amsterdam, later renamed New York.[4] It argues that for the Crown and the Dutch merchants the loss of the colonies meant the loss of some of their most precious investments and real estate, giving them a standing motive to recapture American commerce by other means.[4]
