# Smartmatic

Smartmatic is an election technology company that, in the account presented in the Badlands corpus, occupies a foundational place in the software lineage of American voting systems despite being directly contracted in only one remaining U.S. jurisdiction.

## Origins

According to research by [[canncon|CannCon]] summarized by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] in Badlands Brief: Elections are Fake; as is Trump's Friendship with Bibi, Smartmatic Corp. was incorporated in Delaware in April 2000 by Alfredo Jose Anzola and headquartered in Boca Raton, Florida, in Palm Beach County — seven months before the disputed 2000 presidential recount in Broward and Palm Beach counties.

## Venezuelan contracts and government investment

In 2004 Smartmatic obtained its first contract with [[venezuela|Venezuela]], ahead of the recall referendum on President [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]], months after receiving investment from the Venezuelan government. The New York Times reported that seven months before that contract was awarded, a Venezuelan government financing agency invested more than $200,000 into a smaller technology company owned by some of the same people as Smartmatic, which joined Smartmatic as a minor partner in the bid; in return the agency received a 28 percent stake and a board seat occupied by a senior official who had previously advised Chávez on elections technology. Venezuelan officials later characterized the money as a small-business loan repaid before the referendum. The smaller company, [[bitza|Bitza]], simultaneously wrote the software used in Smartmatic's voting machines.

## Acquisition and divestiture of Sequoia

In 2005 Smartmatic purchased [[sequoia-voting-systems|Sequoia Voting Systems]] from the British firm [[de-la-rue|De La Rue PLC]] for $16 million. Two years later it announced it would divest Sequoia, but an April 2008 Delaware court filing showed that Smartmatic retained a $2 million unsecured promissory note from the transaction and that Sequoia continued to use Smartmatic's intellectual property. Sequoia was subsequently acquired by [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]], which the Badlands account treats as the mechanism by which Smartmatic-derived code entered the broader American election infrastructure.

## Continuing role in Los Angeles

Ashe in America, citing CannCon, states that [[los-angeles|Los Angeles]] is the only U.S. jurisdiction still using Smartmatic directly, a fact she raises in connection with the city's 2026 mayoral primary and the elimination of [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]] from the runoff.
