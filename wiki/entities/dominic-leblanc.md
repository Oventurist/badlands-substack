# Dominic LeBlanc

Dominic LeBlanc is a Canadian politician who served as Canada's public safety minister.[1]

## Response to Trump's tariff threat

After President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced in November 2024 that he intended to impose tariffs on all Canadian, Mexican and Chinese exports to the United States until those countries curbed migration and drug flows, officials in the three countries scrambled to respond.[1] LeBlanc, together with Deputy Prime Minister [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]], touted [[canada|Canada]]'s "balanced and mutually beneficial" economic ties with the United States.[1]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized such responses from the leaders of Canada and [[mexico|Mexico]] as bluster and bravado that was promptly answered by flash crashes in their respective currencies against the U.S. dollar — evidence, in his reading, that markets were responding to narrative rather than actual policy.[1]

## The "51st state" dinner

LeBlanc, as Public Safety Minister, attended a Friday dinner at Trump's Mar-a-Lago club in Florida alongside Prime Minister [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] over the American Thanksgiving long weekend in late November 2024.[2] After Fox News reported that Trump had suggested Canada become the 51st U.S. state in response to Trudeau's concerns that threatened tariffs would damage Canada's economy, LeBlanc told reporters in Ottawa that the remark was made in jest: "The president was telling jokes. The president was teasing us. It was, of course, on that issue, in no way a serious comment."[2]

LeBlanc described the event as a three-hour social evening at the president's residence and said "the conversation was going to be light-hearted," calling relations warm and cordial and arguing that Trump's ability "to joke like that for us" indicated good relations.[2] Trump appeared to continue the joke the following Tuesday, posting on Truth Social an AI-generated image of himself standing on a mountain beside a Canadian flag captioned "Oh Canada!"[2]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the episode differently, describing it as part of a "massive public humiliation ritual" of Trudeau and the globalist proxy states of Canada and Mexico ahead of Trump's second term, and as a signal to the would-be sovereign peoples of both nations that their own governments, not Trump, had sold them out.[2]
