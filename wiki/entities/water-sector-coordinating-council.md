# Water Sector Coordinating Council

The Water Sector Coordinating Council is the industry-side coordinating body for the U.S. water and wastewater [[critical-infrastructure|critical infrastructure]] sector, operating within the sector partnership framework overseen by the [[cisa|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]].[1] [[kevin-morley|Kevin Morley]] of the [[american-water-works-association|American Water Works Association]] is a member.[1]

At the February 6, 2024 [[house-committee-on-homeland-security|House Homeland Security Committee]] hearing on securing [[operational-technology|operational technology]] in the water sector, Morley told Representative [[troy-carter|Troy Carter]] that the council "has made … a mercy water supply, a critical priority," referring to emergency or alternative water supply planning in response to problems such as saltwater intrusion in Louisiana, and that evaluating those options requires collaboration among the [[environmental-protection-agency|EPA]], the Army Corps of Engineers, and CISA.[1]

The Badlands account identified the council's leading position on digitization — captured in [[robert-m-lee|Robert M. Lee]]'s statement that infrastructure "can no longer operate manually disconnected or in an intranet" — as the sector's governing consensus, and argued it sat in tension with the council's simultaneous prioritization of alternative water supplies for climate-driven contingencies.[1]
