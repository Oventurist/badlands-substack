# Duane Owen

Duane Owen was a Florida murderer executed in June 2023 for two killings committed in 1984. His execution became a national controversy after the [[american-civil-liberties-union|American Civil Liberties Union]] publicly complained that the state had not provided him with gender-affirming care, including a sex change operation, before he was put to death.[1]

## Crimes

Owen was convicted of the murders of Karen Slattery, a 14-year-old babysitter, and Georgianna Worden, a 38-year-old mother of two, in two separate 1984 attacks.[1] Slattery was repeatedly stabbed and raped in a home in Delray Beach while two children in her care slept; the children were not harmed during the attack.[1] Two months later, in May 1984, Worden was asleep in her Boca Raton home when Owen struck her several times with a hammer and raped her, according to court records; one of Worden's children discovered her body the next morning while getting ready for school.[1] Owen raped the corpses of his victims.[1] He attacked two other women before his capture; both survived.[1]

He was executed on a Thursday in June 2023.[1]

## ACLU statement and reaction

The ACLU stated that "the state of Florida never provided medically necessary gender affirming care to Duane Owen—causing her enormous suffering and violating her right to be free from cruel and unusual punishment for the 30 years in state custody."[1] The organization faced widespread mockery and scorn for the position.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that Owen had never presented as female during his life, suggesting the defense strategy was either a legal Hail Mary premised on the cultural leverage of transgender issues or an attempt to generate publicity.[1] He characterized the implicit claim of the argument as absurd — that denial of special accommodation produces homicidal offenders — and questioned whether the attorneys or the corporations adopting similar rhetoric held any genuine concern for the mental health of transgender people.[1] DeLarme placed the ACLU within what he described as a shadow network of tax-exempt foundations and charities exerting hidden influence over the modern Democratic Party, comparing it to the Southern Poverty Law Center and citing [[george-soros|George Soros]]'s $50 million grant in 2014 supporting the ACLU's campaign to reduce incarceration rates.[1] He named Soros and his son [[alexander-soros|Alexander Soros]] as actors working to reshape American culture, treating the Owen statement as an example of deliberate exacerbation of cultural division.[1]
