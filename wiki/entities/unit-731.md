# Unit 731

Unit 731 was a group of Japanese doctors and scientists that conducted lethal human experimentation on captive populations during Japan's occupation of Manchuria and China. Japan invaded and occupied Manchuria in 1931 and China in 1937, and the unit used Manchurian and Chinese prisoners as human guinea pigs, along with Russian and Korean captives.[1] In the framing of Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]], Unit 731 belongs to the category of history the [[deep-state|Deep State cabal]] "CAN'T allow us to know about," in contrast to widely publicized programs such as [[operation-paperclip|Operation Paperclip]].[1]

## Experiments

The Japanese scientists infected occupied prisoners with diseases in order to test bioweapons they hoped to use on their enemies.[1] They removed organs from living people without anesthesia, so that decomposition or drugs would not affect the tissue being studied.[1] Limbs were amputated and reattached on the opposite side of the body to study war injuries and improve treatment of Japanese soldiers, and limbs were crushed and frozen to mimic battlefield conditions.[1]

Weapons were tested directly on captives: rounds were fired from varying distances and with varying ammunition sizes to gauge effectiveness, nerve gas was tested, and heavy objects were dropped on prisoners.[1] Captives were starved and deprived of water to simulate wartime scenarios, exposed to X-rays to determine tolerable radiation levels, subjected to extreme G-force conditions, and deliberately infected with venereal diseases.[1] Women were raped so that tests could be performed on their unborn children, and prisoners were given experimental drugs and subjected to physical and psychological trauma intended to break their minds.[1]

## War aims

The stated end goal of the program was the creation of weapons of mass destruction by 1939, including the active development of a plague weapon.[1] It has been surmised that toward the end of the Second World War Japan planned to release this plague on the United States, and some hold that this danger explains why the US felt compelled to drop the atomic bombs on Japan even as the war appeared to be ending — to prevent a last-ditch plague release.[1]

## Postwar transfer claim

Carlson argues that the United States brought Japanese scientists and doctors to America just as it brought Nazi personnel under Operation Paperclip, and that these Japanese researchers were vital to the creation of mind-control systems and biological weapons; at a minimum, he holds, their research was confiscated and furthered in the US.[1] He regards it as implausible that the US would import Nazi specialists but not their Japanese counterparts.[1] The suppressed status of this history, in his account, follows from its implications: it is acceptable for the public to know Nazis helped build the American space program, but not that Unit 731 was brought to America to build bioweapons and mind-control systems — and that the same bioweapons apparatus may have unleashed a bioweapon on the United States in 2020.[1]
