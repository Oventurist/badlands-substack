# Department of Government Efficiency

The Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) is the temporary program office established by executive order at the outset of the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration in January 2025 to modernize federal government technology and reduce bureaucratic waste.[1]

## Establishment

Rather than creating an entirely new agency, Trump's executive order renamed the existing [[us-digital-service|United States Digital Service]] — originally established under President Obama to repair federal technology programs after the "Obamacare Website" disaster — as the United States DOGE Service, and established a temporary program office within it.[1] The order provides that "there shall be a [United States DOGE Services] USDS Administrator established in the Executive Office of the President who shall report to the White House Chief of Staff," placing the effort under [[susie-wiles|Susie Wiles]].[1] The order also establishes DOGE teams within each federal agency, dedicated to modernizing that agency's technology.[1]

## Analysis

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the DOGE executive order as the blueprint for a corporate-style "business transformation enabled by technology," arguing that the next eighteen months constitute a transformational change program.[1] She observed that every technology transformation is fundamentally a people project: optimizing technology requires streamlining processes and automating away redundancies, which leaves the resulting talent model radically different from the one it started with.[1] Private corporations undergo such transformations routinely, she wrote, whereas the federal government does not internally optimize for efficiency or cost reduction and rarely fires employees regardless of performance — having had no incentive to do so until now.[1]

## Litigation

DOGE and Trump's related order making it easier to dismiss career federal employees drew immediate legal challenges. The [[national-treasury-employees-union|National Treasury Employees Union]] filed suit late on January 20, 2025, arguing that the order would violate civil service law.[1]
