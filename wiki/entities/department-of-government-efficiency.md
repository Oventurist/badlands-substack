# Department of Government Efficiency

The Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) is the temporary program office established by executive order at the outset of the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration in January 2025 to modernize federal government technology and reduce bureaucratic waste.[1]

## Establishment

Rather than creating an entirely new agency, Trump's executive order renamed the existing [[us-digital-service|United States Digital Service]] — originally established under President Obama to repair federal technology programs after the "Obamacare Website" disaster — as the United States DOGE Service, and established a temporary program office within it.[1] The order provides that "there shall be a [United States DOGE Services] USDS Administrator established in the Executive Office of the President who shall report to the White House Chief of Staff," placing the effort under [[susie-wiles|Susie Wiles]].[1] The order also establishes DOGE teams within each federal agency, dedicated to modernizing that agency's technology.[1]

## Analysis

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the DOGE executive order as the blueprint for a corporate-style "business transformation enabled by technology," arguing that the next eighteen months constitute a transformational change program.[1] She observed that every technology transformation is fundamentally a people project: optimizing technology requires streamlining processes and automating away redundancies, which leaves the resulting talent model radically different from the one it started with.[1] Private corporations undergo such transformations routinely, she wrote, whereas the federal government does not internally optimize for efficiency or cost reduction and rarely fires employees regardless of performance — having had no incentive to do so until now.[1]

## Litigation

DOGE and Trump's related order making it easier to dismiss career federal employees drew immediate legal challenges. The [[national-treasury-employees-union|National Treasury Employees Union]] filed suit late on January 20, 2025, arguing that the order would violate civil service law.[1]

## Disbandment

In November 2025, Reuters reported exclusively that DOGE had disbanded with eight months left on its mandate, ending an initiative launched with fanfare as a symbol of Trump's pledge to slash the size of government but which critics said delivered few measurable savings.[2] Asked about DOGE's status, [[office-of-personnel-management|Office of Personnel Management]] Director [[scott-kupor|Scott Kupor]] told [[reuters|Reuters]] that "that doesn't exist," adding that it was no longer a "centralized entity" — the first public comments from the Trump administration on the end of DOGE.[2] The agency, set up in January 2025, had made dramatic forays across Washington in the early months of Trump's second term to rapidly shrink federal agencies, cut their budgets, or redirect their work to Trump priorities.[2] The Office of Personnel Management, the federal government's human resources office, subsequently took over many of DOGE's functions, according to Kupor and documents reviewed by Reuters.[2] At least two prominent DOGE employees became involved with the [[national-design-studio|National Design Studio]], a body created by executive order in August 2025 and headed by Airbnb co-founder [[joe-gebbia|Joe Gebbia]], whom Trump directed to beautify government websites.[2]

## Reaction to the wind-down

Badlands commentator [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded that he had said a year earlier that DOGE was "a narrative op."[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] offered a competing reading, noting that the founding executive order's first phase of work was embedding DOGE's people and tools inside every agency, and arguing that this phase completed when [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] departed.[2] On her account the DOGE teams were by then largely automated and embedded within the agencies, central DOGE was always temporary, and its early shutdown reflected being ahead of schedule on a transformational change program.[2] Efficiency metrics, she suggested, would be reported through the agencies themselves, with residual functions moving back to OPM — or possibly to the [[presidential-personnel-office|Presidential Personnel Office]] now headed by [[dan-scavino|Dan Scavino]] — and with automated fraud prevention, tracking, and auditing operational as the steady state.[2] Under that framing the goal was never public messaging but identifying, removing, and prosecuting waste, fraud, and abuse, with disclosure timed strategically once the work was complete.[2]

## February 2025 Oval Office appearance and early targets

On 11 February 2025 [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] appeared in the Oval Office to answer media questions for the first time since arriving in Washington to run DOGE, claiming he was providing maximum transparency but offering no evidence for sweeping assertions that the federal bureaucracy had been corrupted by cheats and by officials approving money for "fraudsters."[3] Musk spoke for roughly thirty minutes beside the Resolute Desk while President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] sat behind it chiming in with approval.[3] Badlands commentary characterised DOGE as "a narrative translation layer for truths Anons have known for years."[3]

DOGE's early work spanned several agencies. The team publicised its discovery that [[fema|FEMA]] had sent $59 million to luxury New York City hotels to house illegal migrants, a finding that led the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] to fire four employees including FEMA's chief financial officer.[3] DOGE simultaneously pored over the [[consumer-financial-protection-bureau|Consumer Financial Protection Bureau]]'s internal finance and procurement data while that agency, under acting director [[russ-vought|Russell Vought]], cancelled more than $100 million in vendor contracts.[3]
