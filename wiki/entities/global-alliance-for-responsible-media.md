# Global Alliance for Responsible Media

The Global Alliance for Responsible Media (GARM) is an advertising-industry initiative created in 2019 by the World Federation of Advertisers. Its stated purpose is "brand safety" — steering member advertising spend away from content deemed harmful — but a July 2024 congressional investigation characterized it as the coordinating mechanism of an advertising boycott cartel used to demonetize and suppress disfavored speech online.[1]

## Structure and market power

GARM's parent body, the World Federation of Advertisers, is a global association representing more than 150 of the world's biggest brands and more than 60 national advertiser associations. Through that membership base the alliance quickly amassed significant market power, representing roughly 90 percent of global advertising spend — nearly one trillion dollars annually.[1]

Its Steer Team was described as reading "like a who's who of corporate America," including Unilever, Mars, Diageo, Procter & Gamble, GroupM, AB InBev, L'Oréal, Nestlé, IBM, Mastercard and PepsiCo. These corporations were said to be leveraging their combined economic influence to control online discourse under the guise of brand safety.[1]

## House Judiciary Committee report and litigation

On July 10, 2024, the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] released an interim staff report titled "GARM's Harm: How the World's Biggest Brands Seek to Control Online Speech," detailing a coordinated effort by the WFA and GARM to suppress content across the internet.[1]

The following day, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] announced that [[x|X]] would sue the "perpetrators and collaborators" behind the arrangement. "Having seen the evidence unearthed today by Congress, 𝕏 has no choice but to file suit against the perpetrators and collaborators in the advertising boycott racket," Musk wrote, adding, "Hopefully, some states will consider criminal prosecution."[1]

## X and Rumble litigation (August 2024)

On Tuesday, August 6, 2024, [[x|X]] filed suit against GARM and several of its members — including [[cvs-health|CVS Health]], [[mars-incorporated|Mars]], Orsted and [[unilever|Unilever]] — alleging that the group had abused its influence over marketers and advertising agencies to discriminate unfairly against the platform and to prompt an advertiser boycott.[2] The complaint characterized the boycott as economically irrational for individual advertisers and explicable only as a conspiracy sustained by the confidence that competitors were behaving identically.[2] [[rumble|Rumble]] joined the suit shortly afterward, describing GARM as an initiative created by the [[world-federation-of-advertisers|World Federation of Advertisers]] that set arbitrary content standards and used them to boycott disfavored platforms, and noting that GARM counted the six largest ad agency holding companies — including defendant [[wpp|WPP]] — among its members.[2]

The litigation followed an interim staff report released in July 2024 by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], which detailed a coordinated effort by the World Federation of Advertisers and its GARM initiative to demonetize and suppress disfavored content across the internet.[2] X chief executive [[linda-yaccarino|Linda Yaccarino]] said in an open letter that GARM's conduct was "a stain on a great industry" and could not be allowed to continue, while [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] publicly raised the prospect of RICO exposure.[2]
