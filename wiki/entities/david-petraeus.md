# David Petraeus

David Petraeus is a retired United States Army general who served as Director of the [[central-intelligence-agency|Central Intelligence Agency]].[1]

## Founding of the McCain Institute

Petraeus attended the April 2012 meeting in Sedona, Arizona at which Sen. [[john-mccain|John McCain]] gathered longtime political allies to discuss his vision for a nonprofit intended to preserve his political legacy.[1] According to the *Arizona Republic* reporting cited by the *Washington Post*, Petraeus — then serving as CIA Director — was joined at the meeting by then-Sen. Jon Kyl (R-Ariz.), Sen. [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] (R-S.C.), former U.S. ambassador to NATO Kurt Volker and the actor Ben Affleck.[1] The [[mccain-institute|McCain Institute]] for International Leadership was created that December with an $8.7 million donation of unused funds from McCain's 2008 presidential campaign.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

The Badlands article highlights the presence of the sitting head of the CIA at the institute's founding meeting as evidence that the nonprofit was not the arms-length entity McCain later described, and frames it within a broader claim that Prince [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Alwaleed bin Talal]] controlled the CIA and through it the media narrative.[1]

## The Gmail drafts revelation

"[[the-biggest-boomerang-in-history|The Biggest Boomerang In History]]" cites a 2012 Politico report that Petraeus, then the former [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] director, and his biographer Paula Broadwell conducted their extramarital correspondence using an email technique favoured by Al Qaeda: rather than sending messages, the two shared an account and left notes in the drafts folder, each reading, deleting and replacing them so as to avoid creating a traceable email trail.[2] Politico noted that PBS's "Frontline" had reported the tactic's use by Al Qaeda and other terrorist groups as early as 2005.[2]

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats the episode as the moment the public first learned of [[gmail-draft-communications|Gmail drafts]], and argues it is no coincidence that a CIA director knew the technique — suggesting the agency most likely taught it to Al Qaeda.[2] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
