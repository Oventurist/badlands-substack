# Cea Weaver

Cea Weaver is an American housing organizer appointed in January 2026 by New York City Mayor [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]] as director of the city's Mayor's Office to Protect Tenants.[1] Prior to the appointment she served as coordinator of the tenant coalition "Housing Justice for All" and was a key figure in the passage of New York's 2019 Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act, the state law that substantially expanded rent regulation and tenant protections.[1]

## Appointment and role

Weaver's appointment accompanied an executive order signed by Mamdani on January 1, 2026 "revitalizing" the Mayor's Office to Protect Tenants, an office that coordinates city agencies to defend tenants' rights, improve housing affordability, address complaints about unsafe or unlawful conditions, and hold landlords accountable.[1]

## Controversy over recorded remarks

Shortly after the appointment, video clips circulated online in which Weaver discussed shared-equity housing models and related policy prescriptions, prompting sharp criticism.[1] Among the widely circulated statements attributed to her were: "People like home ownership because they like control, which is rooted in a racist and classist society…This is why rent control is important"; "We've really treated property as an individualized good and not a collective good"; and a remark that a transition to shared equity "will mean that families, especially white families – but some POC families who are homeowners as well – are going to have a different relationship with property."[1] Another circulated clip had her describing municipal action against landlords: "you are not maintaining this building, and we are the City of New York… we're gonna take this building away from you."[1]

Critics also highlighted an October 2025 clip in which Weaver proposed using public schools, parent-teacher associations, and tenant associations as "sites of resistance" to federal immigration policy.[1] Weaver reportedly deactivated her social media account amid the controversy, according to Newsweek, though screenshots and clips of her prior posts continued to circulate.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media contributors treated the appointment as evidence of an openly collectivist turn in New York City governance. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that "generations from now, the surviving cockroaches of the inevitable and ostensibly violent NY Revolution will insist, with unwarranted arrogance, that it 'wasn't real communism,'" and described Weaver as "a piece of work."[1]
