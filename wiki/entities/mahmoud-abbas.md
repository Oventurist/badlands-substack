# Mahmoud Abbas

Mahmoud Abbas is the President of the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in September 2025 after Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] called for the Palestinian Authority to be dismantled in response to a shooting attack in Jerusalem, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that an online influence operation was underway against Abbas, intended to justify annexation of Palestinian territory.[1] In that view, Abbas had been "the reasonable partner in the room" for some thirty-five years — including during the period when Yasser Arafat and the PLO were still engaged in armed confrontation with the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] — and it was precisely his reasonableness that made him a target.[1]

## Condemnation of Hamas (April 2025)

In April 2025, Abbas called [[hamas|Hamas]] "sons of dogs" in a fiery speech in which he demanded the group release the hostages it still held, disarm, and hand over control of Gaza in order to end the war with Israel.[2] Speaking to a meeting in the occupied West Bank, the president of the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] said Hamas had given Israel "excuses" to continue its attacks on Gaza and told the group to "release the hostages and be done with it" — the strongest remarks he had delivered against Hamas in the 18 months since the war began.[2] A Hamas official condemned what he called Abbas's "derogatory language" toward "a significant proportion... of his own people," and the group had the previous week rejected an Israeli ceasefire proposal that included disarmament in return for a six-week pause in hostilities and the release of 10 of the 59 remaining hostages.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said the remarks were unsurprising, noting that since the October 7 attack he had written about Abbas being at odds with Hamas ever since the group rose to power in Gaza in 2006 and waged kinetic war on the Palestinian Authority, expelling it from Gaza.[2] He recalled that in 2017 Abbas negotiated with Hamas for the PA to take over government services in the Gaza Strip, an arrangement thwarted when Hamas attacked the Palestinian prime minister's caravan in 2018 in an apparent assassination attempt.[2] He added that, as Abbas himself argued, nobody has benefited more from the October 7 attack and the subsequent war than [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and what he called nefarious elements in the Israeli government.[2]

## Target of the "Fatahstan" framing (2024)

In May 2024 Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] declared he was "not ready to replace Hamastan with Fatahstan," explicitly rejecting a postwar Gaza governed by the [[fatah|Fatah]]-dominated [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] that Abbas leads.[3] Netanyahu asserted that the Palestinian Authority "supports terror, educates for terror, funds terror," and cited polling that 80 percent of Palestinians in Judea and Samaria supported the October 7 massacre.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that although [[yasser-arafat|Yasser Arafat]] is credited with the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]], it was a young Abbas who actually signed on behalf of Palestine at the Clinton White House ceremony in 1993 — a symbolic gesture toward the West that the PLO would give way to a peace-seeking Palestinian Authority.[3] In the 31 years since, Abbas and Fatah maintained a consistent message of a two-state solution.[3]

The same analysis identifies Abbas as the intended target of [[hamas|Hamas]] used as an instrument by Netanyahu, and argues that Abbas, Saudi Crown Prince MBS, and Jordanian King Abdullah have been holding back the Arab street while conducting a counterinsurgency against Netanyahu designed to protect Abbas from being ousted by Hamas — closing with the question of what would happen if Abbas and Fatah were removed from the equation.[3]

## Days of mourning and the canceled Amman summit (October 2023)

Abbas declared days of mourning following the [[al-ahli-baptist-hospital-explosion|al-Ahli Baptist Hospital explosion]] in Gaza on October 17, 2023.[4] The [[white-house|White House]] cited that declaration, along with consultations with [[king-abdullah-ii|King Abdullah II]], in canceling President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s planned Amman meeting with Abbas, Abdullah II and Egyptian President [[abdel-fattah-el-sisi|Abdel Fattah el-Sisi]].[4]

## Moscow visit and praise for Putin (August 2024)

In August 2024 Abbas travelled to Moscow, where Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] told him that Moscow was pained by the plight of the Palestinian people and backed their aspiration to create a fully fledged state.[5] Abbas told Putin he was "one of the dearest friends" of the Palestinians.[5] Putin said Russia was paying attention to events in the Middle East despite the demands of its own war in Ukraine, without directly referring to Ukraine's week-old incursion into western Russia, an operation that had caught Russia's military off guard and forced more than 130,000 people to flee their homes.[5]

"Everyone is well aware that Russia today, unfortunately, must defend its interests and defend its people with arms in hand. But what is happening in the Middle East, what is happening in Palestine, of course, does not go unnoticed on our part," Putin said according to a Kremlin transcript, adding that Moscow was watching "with great pain and anxiety the humanitarian catastrophe that has unfolded in Palestine."[5]

### Badlands analysis

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that Abbas's praise for Putin echoed, nearly verbatim, the praise he had directed at [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during their May 3, 2017 press conference — seventeen days before the Riyadh "Sword Dance" summit — and that his optimism toward Trump had persisted into headlines from September 2017.[5] The commentary compared what Trump and Putin each said about Abbas and about helping the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] achieve freedom from military occupation, and noted that both leaders had soured on the [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] regime while distinguishing its leadership from the Israeli people.[5] Citing a December 2021 interview recorded by the Israeli press, the piece highlighted Trump's contrasting remarks on Netanyahu versus Abbas and his statement that he believed Netanyahu never wanted peace with Palestine.[5] The author concluded that Trump, Putin and Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman would unite over the issue to restore peace and stability, describing this as a "Sovereign Alliance" forming to end the globalist war machine.[5]

## Correspondence with Trump

Commentary in the Badlands News Brief noted that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] wrote to Palestinian president Abbas after the Butler assassination attempt in 2024, promising him that everything was going to be okay.[6] Trump posted the exchange nine days after receiving Abbas's letter, on the night before [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was scheduled to deliver a widely panned speech about Iran to Congress.[6] Analysts framed the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] under Abbas as a "third suitor" for control of [[gaza|Gaza]], distinguishing the situation from disputed territories with only two claimants.[6]


## September 2025 UN address by video

At the September 22, 2025 summit in New York supporting [[palestinian-statehood|Palestinian statehood]], Abbas said the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] would form the government of the West Bank and Gaza, with no role for [[hamas|Hamas]].[7] The United States denied him a visa to attend the events in New York, so he addressed the meeting by video.[7] Speaking to the people of Israel, he said: "Our future and yours depends on peace. Enough war."[7]

## September 2024 United Nations appeal on Gaza

In a September 2024 address, Abbas urged the United Nations to stop the war in Gaza, calling for a comprehensive and permanent ceasefire, an end to attacks by Israeli settlers in the West Bank and East Jerusalem, the delivery of humanitarian aid throughout Gaza, and a full withdrawal of the Israeli military from the enclave.[8] He argued that the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] should have control over all Palestinian territories and said it would hold elections once the war ended.[8] In remarks quoted by Badlands Media, Abbas demanded that the world "stop the killing of children and women," described the situation as genocide, called for an end to weapons shipments to Israel, and said Gaza was "no longer fit for life" after the destruction of homes, educational institutions and medical centres.[8]

Israel's UN ambassador [[danny-danon|Danny Danon]] responded by accusing Abbas of speaking about a peaceful solution only at the United Nations and of failing to condemn the October 7 attack by [[hamas|Hamas]] that triggered the war.[8] Badlands commentary rejected that characterization, noting that Abbas had criticized Hamas for not informing the Palestinian Authority of its planned attack and had blamed the group for the ensuing destruction visited on the Palestinian people.[8]

## Longstanding conflict with Hamas

Badlands Media compiled a record of Abbas's confrontations with Hamas to argue that he has been the principal Palestinian figure attempting to curb the organization.[8] In 2007, after Hamas drove both Palestinian Authority security forces and the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] out of Gaza, Abbas stated that his government would hold no dialogue with the "murderous terrorists" of Hamas.[8] In 2017 he said he would not appoint Hamas officials to the Palestinian government unless they recognized the state of Israel.[8] In 2018, after Hamas attempted to assassinate his prime minister with a roadside bomb during a mission to negotiate the peaceful transfer of Gaza's government to the Palestinian Authority, Abbas sanctioned Hamas and cut it off financially in an effort to destroy it.[8] According to the same commentary, Hamas survived because [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] devised a scheme in which Israel carried briefcases containing tens of millions of dollars in cash into Gaza for families with ties to Hamas — a plot exposed by a cabinet minister who resigned and went public.[8] The outlet further noted that in July 2024, on the night before Netanyahu addressed Congress, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted a letter Abbas had sent him expressing concern over the attempt on Trump's life, complete with a hand-written note.[8]
