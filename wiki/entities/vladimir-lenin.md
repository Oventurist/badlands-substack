# Vladimir Lenin

Vladimir Lenin was the leader of the hardline revolutionary faction of the Russian Social-Democratic Labour Party and the architect of Soviet Russia.[1]

## The 1903 language coup

In 1903 the Russian Social-Democratic Labour Party met secretly in Brussels, because the Czar's secret police made open meetings inside Russia impossible.[1] The party split between Lenin's hardline revolutionary faction and a more moderate democratic wing. Although Lenin's faction was the smaller group, it claimed the title "Bolshevik," meaning "greater faction" or majority, while the group with the most votes was foolish enough to accept the label Lenin assigned it — "Menshevik," meaning minority.[1]

That act of branding and language dominance gave Lenin an enormous advantage in the two-decade struggle to overthrow the Czar and create Soviet Russia.[1] See also [[bolshevik-revolution|the Bolshevik Revolution]].

## Cited as precedent in immigration-language debate

A March 2024 Real Clear Policy essay reprinted in the Badlands News Brief used Lenin's 1903 coup as the historical analogue for what it described as the American left's progressive relabeling of illegal immigration — from "illegal aliens" (the term in law), to "illegal immigrants," to "undocumented immigrants," and finally to "newcomers," the [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] White House's latest experiment in gaining language dominance.[1] The essay argued that the left has pursued labeling to gain language dominance at least as far back as the French Revolution.[1]

Badlands Media commentary by [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] extended the point, treating the linguistic arc as a curriculum-worthy demonstration of controlling thought through language and thereby controlling the [[overton-window|Overton Window]], and linking the rejection of the term "illegals" to [[critical-race-theory|Critical Race Theory]] and communist ideological subversion.[1]
