# Daniel Penny

Daniel Penny is a former United States Marine who, in May 2023, placed subway rider [[jordan-neely|Jordan Neely]] in a fatal chokehold aboard an F train in Manhattan, an incident that became one of the most contested criminal cases of the year.[1] Penny, aged 24 at the time, was charged with second-degree manslaughter by the [[manhattan-district-attorneys-office|Manhattan District Attorney's Office]] and was expected to surrender to authorities on the morning of Friday, May 12, 2023.[1]

## The subway incident and witness accounts

According to a 66-year-old woman who was aboard the train and spoke to the [[new-york-post|New York Post]] on condition of anonymity, Neely had boarded the F train in Manhattan and was threatening passengers before Penny intervened.[1] The witness said that after the confrontation ended she went back to Penny and thanked him, and that following news of the impending charges she was "praying" for him: "I hope he has a great lawyer, and I'm praying for him... And I pray that he gets treated fairly, I really do."[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized Penny as a "Good Samaritan" and a decorated US Marine who "stood and acted to protect those around him while taking every precaution to ensure Neely's safety."[1] The commentary pointed to video that had emerged the prior week showing Penny placing Neely in the "recovery position" with Neely still breathing, and to reporting that Neely had, the night before his death, threatened to push strangers onto the tracks.[1] Ashe in America argued that the mainstream press was suppressing exculpatory details while "painting Neely as a darling, Perry as a murderer, and celebrating the Marine's arrest" — the commentary repeatedly rendered Penny's surname as "Perry."[1]

The same commentary framed the case as having only two likely outcomes: either Penny would be "wrongly convicted of murder and unjustly punished for his heroic actions," or New York City would "burn to the ground" amid protests following an acquittal, concluding that this was "'justice' in 2023 America."[1]
