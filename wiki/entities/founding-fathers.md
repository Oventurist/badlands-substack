# Founding Fathers

The **Founding Fathers** are the men who led the American Revolution and framed the United States' founding documents, among them [[george-washington|George Washington]] and [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]].[1] They recur in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary as a reference point for debates about civic duty, self-government and the nature of political struggle.[1]

## Treatment in Badlands commentary

Episode 10 of [[the-blitz|The Blitz]], published 14 March 2026, closed its list of framing questions by asking what people get wrong when it comes to remembering the Founding Fathers.[1] The question was posed alongside discussions of trust in [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the internal division of the [[truth-community|Truth Community]], implying that popular memory of the founding generation is idealised in ways that distort present-day political expectations.[1] Hosts [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the subject as one of the "prime sifting" issues defining the [[information-war|Info War]] circa 2026.[1]

## Appeals to Natural Law

A Badlands Media essay of 5 October 2025, "On Nations and Natural Law," argues that [[natural-law|Natural Law]] was so embedded in the common vernacular of the Colonial era that many of the Founding Fathers made impassioned appeals to its principles in their public debates and writings, and that its refrains were preserved as the basis for the claims of liberty in the founding documents — "the Laws of Nature and of Nature's God" and "certain inalienable rights endowed by our Creator."[2] The essay imagines common patriots of the period invoking the same principle to decry tyranny in the [[green-dragon-tavern|Green Dragon Tavern]].[2]

It further contends that the absence of explicit provisions for natural rights in the original Constitution prompted the amendments that delineated them, so that free speech, the bearing of arms and the free exercise of religion stand as logical conclusions drawn from Natural Law.[2] While noting that the founding generation's work was imperfect — [[lysander-spooner|Lysander Spooner]] is said to have had "choice words" for the outcome of their revolution — the essay holds that they demonstrated what is possible when enough people approach the creation of a government with the right mindset, and offers their successes and failures as teachers for a comparable present-day epoch.[2]
