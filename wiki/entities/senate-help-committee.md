# Senate HELP Committee

The Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor and Pensions — commonly called the Senate HELP Committee — is the standing [[united-states-senate|Senate]] panel with jurisdiction over the [[department-of-education|Education Department]], federal labor policy, and the confirmation of nominees to lead those agencies.[1]

## Role in the 2025 Trump nominations

During the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's confirmation wave, the HELP Committee served as the procedural gateway for two Cabinet-level nominees. On Thursday, February 20, 2025, the committee advanced [[linda-mcmahon|Linda McMahon]]'s nomination to lead the Education Department to the Senate floor on a 12–11 party-line vote, one week after McMahon appeared before the panel to explain President Trump's plans for the department's future.[1]

Committee chairman [[bill-cassidy|Bill Cassidy]], a Louisiana Republican, framed the vote as a mandate for reform, saying McMahon faced "the tall task of reforming a Department of Education that has lost its purpose," that the department had for four years "focused on everything but student learning with bureaucracy and red tape standing in the way of student success," and that she had "demonstrated a strong vision" for the agency.[1] At her hearing the previous Thursday, senators pressed McMahon on the fate of programs housed within the agency — including special education and grants for low-income schools — should the department be dismantled or shuttered.[1]

The committee also held jurisdiction over the pending nomination of [[lori-chavez-deremer|Lori Chavez-DeRemer]] as Secretary of Labor, whose committee action was scheduled for February 27, 2025.[1]
