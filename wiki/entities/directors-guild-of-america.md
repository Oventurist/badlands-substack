# Directors Guild of America

The Directors Guild of America (DGA) is the American labor union representing film and television directors and members of the directorial team. It is one of the three principal Hollywood creative guilds, alongside the [[writers-guild-of-america|Writers Guild of America]] and [[sag-aftra|SAG-AFTRA]], each of which negotiates separately with the [[alliance-of-motion-picture-and-television-producers|Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers]].[1]

## 2023 contract settlement

During the [[2023-hollywood-labor-strikes|2023 Hollywood labor strikes]], the DGA broke from the pattern of escalating confrontation. In what [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described in a June 2023 Badlands News Brief as "a surprise move," the Directors Guild struck a tentative deal with the studios that secured wage and benefit increases, streaming residuals, protections against artificial intelligence, and other gains.[1] The settlement came in the sixth week of the writers strike and the same week SAG-AFTRA voted by a roughly 98 percent margin to authorize its own strike.[1]

Badlands commentary treated the DGA agreement as a breach of inter-guild solidarity, noting the oddity that it was unclear who would write the scripts for, or act in, whatever DGA members would be directing.[1] The broader Badlands framing held that technology — particularly streaming — had reshaped the industry faster than creative contracts had adapted, and that the studios had retained traditional terms that denied creators a fair share of streaming gains.[1]
