# House Judiciary Committee

The House Judiciary Committee is the standing committee of the U.S. House of Representatives with jurisdiction over the federal courts and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]]. Under Chairman [[bob-goodlatte|Bob Goodlatte]] it conducted the investigation that Badlands Media credits with exposing the Obama-era DOJ settlement slush fund.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## The slush fund investigation

An extended committee investigation found that the DOJ had engaged in a pattern or practice of systematically subverting Congress's budget authority by using settlements from financial institutions to funnel money to what Goodlatte described as "left-wing activist groups."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Internal DOJ documents obtained by the committee confirmed the existence of the fund and showed that officials "went out of their way" to exclude conservative groups.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The committee calculated that approximately $1 billion had been directed to outside organizations over a two-year period, and in January it formally requested that the DOJ preserve all documents and emails related to the settlement money.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

Goodlatte released what he called "smoking gun" emails on the House floor in October 2017, ahead of a vote on legislation prohibiting government officials from entering into settlement agreements that provide payments to third parties rather than to the United States.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Broader role

The committee's work, alongside that of [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]], is described as generating evidence later transferred to [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] and the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]], and as one of the triggers for Attorney General [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]]'s appointment of [[john-huber|John Huber]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Hur recordings (2026)

The committee was one of three recipients — with the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]] and [[mike-howell|Mike Howell]] — to whom the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] agreed to release redacted audio recordings and transcripts from Special Counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s investigation into [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s handling of classified documents.[2] Biden intervened in the litigation to block the release, and in July 2026 the [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-dc-circuit|D.C. Circuit]] denied his emergency request for an injunction, extending only an administrative stay until August 3.[2]

## ActBlue report (April 2026)

On 20 April 2026 the committee's Republicans issued a new report on [[actblue|ActBlue]], stating: "New reporting has confirmed our findings: ActBlue accepts illegal foreign donations en masse. ActBlue tried to cover this up by misleading and withholding documents from Congress. But it didn't work."[3] The report followed the committee's earlier staff report on ActBlue fraud, which had said the investigation was not concluded and that investigators were seeking transcribed interviews with current ActBlue employees — including those responsible for the platform's fraud-prevention operation — and reviewing suspicious activity reports.[3] Badlands reported that those employees invoked the Fifth Amendment 146 times to avoid self-incrimination.[3]

## Bondi Epstein hearing (February 2026)

In February 2026 the committee held what was described as a fiery hearing with Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] on the [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] matter, after which both Bondi and the legislators faced criticism for their conduct.[4] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] praised Bondi's performance on Truth Social while attacking committee Republican [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]], and argued that the [[epstein-narrative|Epstein story]] was a Democratic distraction from Republican achievements.[4]

## Brazil censorship report (April 2026)

The committee released an interim staff report outlining new evidence that [[brazil|Brazil]]'s censorship regime is targeting speech inside the United States and pressuring American social media companies to comply or face legal action and removal from the country.[5] According to the report and newly obtained nonpublic documents, the system led by Brazilian Supreme Court Justice [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]] issues global takedown orders, coordinates with censorship efforts in other countries, and removes legal protections for platforms.[5]

The committee said these actions have directly targeted US-based speech, including posts from Brazilian journalists and political commentators living in the United States, as well as content praising President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and criticizing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[5] It stated that when platforms such as [[x-corp|X]] and [[rumble|Rumble]] refused to fully comply, Brazilian authorities fined them and threatened or ordered them to cease operations in Brazil, warning that forcing American companies to censor US residents' speech creates a broader threat to [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] protections.[5]

The report also said Brazil's censorship network is coordinating with similar foreign censorship regimes and with [[stanford-university|Stanford University]], alleging that Stanford previously played a central role in "laundering US government censorship requests" to social media companies in an effort to influence the 2020 US election and has since shifted from enabling domestic censorship to aiding foreign censorship efforts.[5] The committee said it would continue oversight and pursue legislative responses to counter foreign censorship threats.[5]

## Criminal referral of Jack Smith (July 2026)

On July 22, 2026 the committee sent a letter to Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] referring former Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] for criminal prosecution, citing strong evidence that he knowingly made false statements during his December 17, 2025 sworn deposition.[6] The committee said Smith's testimony directly contradicted new DOJ documents it had obtained.[6]

The referral centered on the [[arctic-frost|Arctic Frost]] probe into the 2020 election, in which Biden-Harris DOJ investigators subpoenaed the phone records of more than a dozen Republican Members of [[congress|Congress]] and used gag orders to conceal that fact.[6] The letter detailed how Smith's team proposed and he approved the vast majority of additional toll record subpoenas despite internal DOJ experts warning the actions might be unconstitutional.[6] Smith had testified that he did not receive the content of Members' text messages, which the committee said appeared false given records it now possesses, and the referral highlighted potential violations of 18 U.S.C. § 1001 and of the Speech or Debate Clause.[6]

## Durham hearing (June 2023)

On June 21, 2023 the committee, chaired by [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], heard testimony from Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] on the [[durham-report|Durham Report]].[7] Durham told members that the [[cia|CIA]] knew of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s plan to smear [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with Russia allegations and that the [[fbi|FBI]] had relied on the uncorroborated [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]].[7] Ranking Democrat [[jerrold-nadler|Jerrold Nadler]] and [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] defended the collusion investigation, while Republican [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] attacked Durham for producing too few results.[7] See [[durham-house-judiciary-testimony-2023|Durham House Judiciary Testimony (2023)]].[7]

## Zwonitzer subpoena (March 2024)

In March 2024 committee Republicans, led by chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], subpoenaed [[mark-zwonitzer|Mark Zwonitzer]], the ghostwriter of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s memoir, to obtain recordings and a transcript of conversations cited by special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]].[8] The subpoena was described as one of numerous efforts by House Republicans to obtain materials related to Hur's investigation, and followed Zwonitzer's decision to reverse an earlier agreement to produce the materials voluntarily.[8]

## GARM report and X lawsuit announcement (July 2024)

On July 10, 2024, the committee released an interim staff report titled "GARM's Harm: How the World's Biggest Brands Seek to Control Online Speech," documenting a coordinated effort by the [[world-federation-of-advertisers|World Federation of Advertisers]] and its [[global-alliance-for-responsible-media|Global Alliance for Responsible Media]] initiative to demonetize and suppress disfavored content across the internet.[9] The report detailed how the alliance, representing roughly 90 percent of global advertising spend and a Steer Team including Unilever, Mars, Diageo, Procter & Gamble, GroupM, AB InBev, L'Oréal, Nestlé, IBM, Mastercard and PepsiCo, leveraged that market power under the banner of "brand safety."[9]

The following day, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] announced that [[x-corp|X]] would sue the "perpetrators and collaborators in the advertising boycott racket," writing that the platform had "no choice" after seeing "the evidence unearthed today by Congress," and suggesting that some states should consider criminal prosecution.[9]

## Pfizer COVID-19 vaccine trial investigation (May 2025)

In May 2025 the committee launched an investigation into allegations that [[pfizer|Pfizer]] executives deliberately slowed COVID-19 vaccine clinical trials to influence the outcome of the 2020 presidential election, based on comments by former Pfizer vaccine R&D head [[philip-dormitzer|Philip Dormitzer]].[10] Chaired by Rep. [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], the committee demanded that Dormitzer produce all relevant documents related to Pfizer's vaccine trials from March 1, 2020 to the present, and asked him to schedule a transcribed interview by May 29.[10] Dormitzer reportedly requested a transfer to Canada after [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s 2024 re-election, citing fears of investigation.[10]

## Hearing on exploitation of unaccompanied alien children (2023)

On April 26, 2023, the committee's Subcommittee on Immigration Integrity, Security and Enforcement held a hearing titled "The Biden Border Crisis: Exploitation of Unaccompanied Alien Children," at which [[department-of-health-and-human-services|HHS]] whistleblower [[tara-lee-rodas|Tara Lee Rodas]] testified that federal placement practices were delivering migrant children into a "sophisticated network" of trafficking that ended when the [[office-of-refugee-resettlement|Office of Refugee Resettlement]] handed a child to a sponsor.[11] Rodas called on [[congress|Congress]] to crack down on the U.S. role in that network.[11] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] mocked the hearing's institutional posture — acknowledging trafficking while declining to verify or dwell on it — but argued the exposure still made the issue harder to suppress over time.[11]

## January 2024 Hunter Biden associate interviews

Alongside the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]], the Judiciary Committee scheduled closed-door transcribed interviews in late January 2024 with two business associates of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] as part of the House impeachment inquiry into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[12] [[mervyn-yan|Mervyn Yan]], who worked with Hunter Biden through the Chinese energy company [[cefc-china-energy|CEFC]] and Hudson West III, was set to appear before both committees on Thursday, and [[rob-walker|Rob Walker]] on Friday.[12]

## ActBlue subpoenas, June 2025

The committee's Republican chairman joined the chairmen of House Oversight and House Administration in subpoenaing ActBlue officials in June 2025 over alleged widespread fraud on the Democratic fundraising platform.[13] The summonses went to a current senior workflow specialist and to former vice president of customer service Alyssa Twomey, and were issued amid a parallel Justice Department investigation into "straw" or "dummy" contributions authorized by President Donald Trump.[13]
