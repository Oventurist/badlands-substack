# MQ-9 Reaper

The MQ-9 is a United States unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) operated by American military forces for surveillance and strike missions. One such aircraft became the subject of an international incident on March 14, 2023, when it went down over the Black Sea following an encounter with Russian fighter aircraft.[1]

## Black Sea incident, March 2023

According to reporting compiled by the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] alleged that a Russian Su-27 aircraft dumped fuel on the MQ-9 drone before it crashed.[1] The [[russian-ministry-of-defence|Russian Defense Ministry]] declined to address that allegation directly and instead attributed the crash to the drone's own maneuvering, stating that the "MQ-9 fell into the Black Sea on Tuesday morning due to its own sharp maneuvering" and that Russian fighters "did not come into contact with it and did not use weapons."[1]

The Russian statement placed the event at roughly 09:30 Moscow time (06:30 GMT), describing the aircraft as having entered "an uncontrolled flight with a loss of altitude" before colliding with the water surface, with the Russian fighters returning safely to their home airfield.[1] Russian air control forces said they had recorded the MQ-9 flying over the Black Sea in the region of the Crimean peninsula in the direction of the Russian state border, and asserted that the drone had been operating with its transponders turned off.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the episode as a "very small-scale kinetic event," while cautioning that it was difficult or impossible to determine what actually caused the drone to run afoul of a Russian fighter jet.[1] He argued that the occurrence of any kinetic contact between United States assets — even unmanned ones — and Russian forces was beginning to "seed the possibility of escalation in the Collective Mind of American Society."[1]

## Black Sea loss, March 2023

An MQ-9 Reaper crashed into the Black Sea following an intercept encounter on Tuesday, March 14, 2023, prompting the first phone call in months between U.S. Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] and Russian Defense Minister [[sergei-shoigu|Sergei Shoigu]].[2] Both Russia and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] raced to retrieve the wreckage, but the drone reportedly went down in a very deep part of the sea, making recovery unlikely.[2] See [[2023-black-sea-drone-incident|the 2023 Black Sea drone incident]].[2]
