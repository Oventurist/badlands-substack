# Thomas Massie

Thomas Massie is a Republican member of the United States House of Representatives who emerged as one of the most persistent congressional critics of the Department of Justice's handling of the Jeffrey Epstein records.[1]

## Lone Republican vote against House Resolution 771 (2023)

On October 25, 2023, Massie was the only Republican to vote against [[house-resolution-771-standing-with-israel|House Resolution 771]], the Israel-support resolution that passed the House 412–10 as the first floor action under new Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]].[14] Explaining his opposition on X, Massie objected to the measure's sanctions provisions and its pledges of military support, saying it "contains an open-ended promise of military support that is so broad that it could be interpreted to commit US soldiers to the conflict."[14] He added that "US troops should not be engaged in this conflict."[14]

## Criticism of the DOJ Epstein production

Appearing on ABC's *This Week* in February 2026, Massie said he did not have confidence in Attorney General Pam Bondi, faulting her conduct during a House Judiciary Committee hearing. "I don't think she did very well," Massie said, adding that "she came with a book full of insults, one for each congressperson. She obviously had one for me."[1]

Massie, described in Badlands coverage as a former DeSantis surrogate, argued that the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] lacks accountability and that work on the Epstein files is not finished. He pointed specifically to shifting redactions across successive productions and to the absence of explanatory context when names were unredacted.[1]

Massie's posture placed him alongside Rep. [[nancy-mace|Nancy Mace]] among Republicans pressing for fuller disclosure under the [[epstein-files-transparency-act|Epstein Files Transparency Act]], a dynamic Badlands writers framed as part of a broader "flooding the zone" information environment around the [[epstein-narrative|Epstein narrative]].[1]

## 2026 primary defeat

On "Primary Tuesday" in May 2026, Massie lost his Republican primary in Kentucky's 4th Congressional District to Trump-backed challenger [[ed-gallrein|Ed Gallrein]], a former Navy SEAL.[2] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] personally endorsed Gallrein, campaigned in Kentucky, and repeatedly attacked Massie on social media as "a bad guy" and "an obstructionist and a fool"; the race became the most expensive U.S. House primary in history.[2] Massie had positioned himself as an independent "America First" voice who opposed some Trump priorities, including certain foreign aid votes and tax legislation, while Gallrein ran on loyalty to Trump and his military background.[2] Trump's influence extended beyond the House race, as his endorsed candidate Rep. [[andy-barr|Andy Barr]] won the GOP nomination for an open U.S. Senate seat in the same primary.[2]

In his concession speech, Massie told supporters chanting "no more wars" and "America First" that lawmakers must follow the Constitution over party loyalty, and hinted at a possible 2028 presidential run.[2] Gallrein is heavily favored to win the general election against Democrat Melissa Strange in the deeply Republican district.[2]

## Reaction within Badlands

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described pronounced "libertarian rage" online after the loss, said a local content creator friend was "overdosing on black pills," and joked that "nothing could stop what is coming except Thomas Massie, so thankfully, what is coming has not been stopped"; she also asked whether [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]] would be the operation's next target and repeated that "elections are still fake."[2] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Massie's defeat would push reluctant Republicans to support the [[lankford-rosen-antisemitism-bill|Lankford–Rosen antisemitism bill]] and similar measures, writing that "if Thomas Massie can't beat the Israel lobby in a fake election, nobody can."[2]

## Trump attacks over the Bondi hearing (February 2026)

After the February 2026 [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] hearing with Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] on the [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]] matter, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] denounced Massie on Truth Social as a "'Republican' Loser, Sanctimonious RINO Congressman" who "made a total fool of himself yesterday, fighting aimlessly against a hopeless agenda of Hate and Stupidity."[3] Trump cited what he called Massie's crashing job approval numbers in Kentucky and claimed that challenger [[ed-gallrein|Ed Gallrein]], whom he described as a military hero, was "crushing him in the Polls," adding that Massie was "now in 'Wacky' Liz Cheney territory."[3]

## February 2026: clash with Bondi over Epstein redactions

In February 2026 Massie intensified pressure on the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] to fully unredact and disclose [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] documents, directly confronting Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] during a tense [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] hearing.[4] The Kentucky Republican, who co-sponsored the legislation that forced the release of roughly three million pages, highlighted suspicious redactions that the DOJ quickly corrected after his objections.[4] He predicted growing Republican defections from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s agenda as lawmakers tire of rubber-stamping policies.[4]

## Amendment to end military aid to Israel (July 2026)

On July 15, 2026, Massie introduced an amendment that would have eliminated approximately $3.3 billion in annual US military assistance to [[israel|Israel]].[5] The measure failed by a vote of 314-104, but more than 100 House Democrats — 103 in total — joined Massie in supporting it, while 98 Democrats voted against and 10 voted "present."[5] House Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] opposed the amendment while House Minority Whip [[katherine-clark|Katherine Clark]] and former Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] voted in favor, underscoring divisions within Democratic leadership; the defeat left US military assistance to Israel unchanged.[5] See [[july-2026-house-vote-on-military-aid-to-israel|the vote]] for fuller coverage.[5]

## Call for Lutnick's resignation (February 2026)

As the release of more than three million pages of [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]]-related documents continued into February 2026, Massie publicly called on Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] to step down over alleged ties to Epstein revealed in the files.[6] Badlands presented the demand as evidence of mounting pressure on U.S. public officials named in the documents, at a time when European governments had moved more aggressively — including the resignation of a senior Norwegian ambassador — while American figures largely faced no sanctions.[6]

## Sexual misconduct settlement disclosure resolution (2026)

On June 30, 2026, the House passed a resolution offered by Massie by a near-unanimous vote of 420-0, with Rep. [[nancy-mace|Nancy Mace]] voting "present."[7] The measure directs the [[house-ethics-committee|House Ethics Committee]] and the [[office-of-congressional-workplace-rights|Office of Congressional Workplace Rights]] to release within 60 days all records identifying which current or former House members used taxpayer funds to settle sexual misconduct allegations against them, including the amounts involved.[7] Massie argued that disclosure was needed "to convince the people and assure the people that we are conducting the people's business with the utmost integrity."[7] See [[house-resolution-on-disclosure-of-congressional-sexual-misconduct-settlements|the resolution]] for fuller coverage.[7]

## Support for the May 2024 motion to vacate

Massie was one of only two Republicans who openly expressed support for [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]]'s [[may-2024-motion-to-vacate-against-speaker-mike-johnson|motion to vacate]] against Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]], and he accompanied Greene onto the House floor on 8 May 2024 when she brought the resolution forward.[8] The measure was tabled 359 to 43 after Democrats joined Republicans to shelve it.[8]

## Opposition to the WHO Pandemic Accord

In March 2023 Massie publicly rejected the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration's stated commitment to a [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] "Pandemic Accord," asserting that United States citizens would live under the Constitution and laws made by the representatives they elect rather than by globalist bodies.[9] The administration's [[state-department|Department of State]] had announced days earlier that the United States was "committed to a #PandemicAccord," which it said would "vastly improve the global health architecture for preventing, preparing for, and responding to future pandemic emergencies."[9] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] endorsed the stance, arguing the WHO had misled the public during COVID-19, obscured the virus's origins, and functioned as an entity capable of superseding national authority in the name of public health.[9]

## Opposition to Johnson in the 119th Speaker vote

Ahead of the January 3, 2025 vote to seat the Speaker of the 119th Congress, Massie vowed to vote on the floor for someone other than incumbent Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]].[10] Because all Democrats were expected to be present and voting for [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]], Massie's defection meant a single additional Republican no vote would deny Johnson the simple majority required.[10] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that while Massie was loudly critical of Johnson, there did not appear to be sufficient coordinated opposition of the kind Matt Gaetz had assembled against Kevin McCarthy in 2023, and she did not expect "Massie & Co" to work with Democrats to engineer an upset.[10] See [[2025-speaker-of-the-house-election|2025 Speaker of the House election]].[10]

## March 2023 banking-crisis call

On Sunday afternoon, March 12, 2023, Massie participated in a Zoom meeting with members of [[us-congress|Congress]], the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury]] and the [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|FDIC]], convened after [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] crashed and was taken over by the State of California the previous Friday.[11] Massie reported afterwards that during the call one Democratic senator asked whether there was a program in place to censor free speech on social media at that time — a question conservative outlets characterised as an immediate reflex to strip Americans of [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] protections and restrict the free flow of information amid the banking crisis.[11] Badlands contributor [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] wrote that the episode showed a "blood-thirst for complete control of the narrative," with officials seeking to censor posts they judged capable of provoking a run on the banks.[11]

## March 2025: Trump's primary threat and the shutdown fight

On the night of Monday, March 10, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] lashed out at Massie in a Truth Social post declaring that the congressman "SHOULD BE PRIMARIED" and pledging to "lead the charge against him."[12] Trump wrote that Massie "is an automatic 'NO' vote on just about everything, despite the fact that he has always voted for Continuing Resolutions in the past," called him "just another GRANDSTANDER, who's too much trouble, and not worth the fight," likened him to former Rep. [[liz-cheney|Liz Cheney]] "before her historic, record breaking fall," and asked, "DO I HAVE ANY TAKERS???"[12] Massie fired back on Tuesday, leveraging the incoming fire as a fundraising opportunity while declaring that he would seek re-election.[12] The comparison invoked Cheney's 2022 defeat by Trump-endorsed challenger Harriet Hageman in Wyoming's at-large district; the exchange was reported by [[fox-news|Fox News]].[12]

On Tuesday, March 11, 2025, Massie was the only Republican to vote against the House stopgap funding bill, which passed 217-213 with a single Democratic supporter, Rep. Jared Golden of Maine.[12] Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]]'s [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]] would fund federal agencies through September 30 while reducing Justice Department state and local law enforcement assistance, and had to clear the [[us-senate|Senate]] by March 14 to avert what would have been the first shutdown of Trump's term.[12]

Badlands contributor Jon Herold urged consistency, writing that people who had attacked him for criticizing Massie in the past now hated Massie "because Trump said so," and that changing standards to await "permission" from Trump's social media meant having no standards at all; he added that he very much wanted a government shutdown.[12] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that "elections are fake" and "primaries are the fakest of fake elections," making a call to primary someone meaningless, and suggested Massie might be helping to shut down what he called the corrupt and illegitimate government in a way that could not be blamed on Trump.[12]


## Speech-or-debate strategy on Epstein accusers (September 2025)

In September 2025, Massie publicized survivors' fear of being sued for naming [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s alleged abusers and proposed instead to name the accused himself on the House floor, where the Constitution's speech and debate clause confers immunity from legal liability.[13] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] sharply criticized the plan, arguing that victims naming their abusers is one of the most legally protected circumstances in America and that a lawsuit would in fact be "a gift," opening discovery and a genuine path to justice.[13]

She argued that Massie — reportedly joined by [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] — would be airing what amounts to inadmissible hearsay with complete immunity and no regard for the rights of the accused, comparing the approach to a soap opera or influencer event rather than a legal proceeding.[13] Because there are consequences for false allegations in court but none for statements made on the House floor, she contended the stunt could sow further distrust in the Epstein story, make a fair trial impossible for anyone named, and ultimately preclude justice for the survivors who had called on [[congress|Congress]] to legislate the release of the remaining files.[13]
## Opposition to the 2025 House rules package

At the opening of the 119th Congress in January 2025, Massie was the lone Republican dissenter in the extended first round of voting that reelected Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]], after [[ralph-norman|Ralph Norman]] (R-SC) and Keith Self (R-TX) switched their votes to Johnson at the end.[15] He and [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] then objected to the [[2025-house-rules-package|House rules package]], which the chamber adopted 215-209 and which raised the [[motion-to-vacate|motion-to-vacate]] threshold from one member to nine Republicans.[15] Massie's specific complaint concerned language queuing up a bill to impose sanctions over any [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] action against people such as Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], writing on social media: "How did a bill to protect Netanyahu make it into the House rules package to be voted on immediately after the speaker vote? Where are our priorities?!"[15]

## Interview with Tucker Carlson on Ukraine funding (December 2023)

In December 2023 Massie sat down with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] to discuss the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration's $106 billion aid package for Ukraine and Israel, which the two framed as funding for a "war they cannot win."[16] Asked why establishment lawmakers pressed for more Ukraine money, Massie said the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]] thrives on the sale of "deadly munitions," enriching shareholders "some of whom are congressmen."[16] He noted that the head of the [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]] had written to Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] imploring him to spend more in Ukraine in order to "revitalize our defense industrial base," attaching a list of states — Alabama, Ohio, Texas — that would receive manufacturing money.[16] Massie concluded that officials were "saying the quiet part out loud," since federal spending routed to Ukraine is "laundered back to the military-industrial complex" and enriches districts and stockholders.[16]

## Opposition to the 2024 TikTok divestment bill

Ahead of the House vote on the TikTok divestment bill on March 13, 2024, Massie took to the floor to argue against the measure, saying "there's some of us who feel that — intentionally, or unintentionally, this legislation to ban TikTok is actually a trojan horse."[17] He added that "some of us are concerned that there are First Amendment implications here. Americans have the right to view information, and don't need to be protected by the government from information."[17] The bill nevertheless passed 352-65-1, with fifteen Republicans and fifty Democrats voting against it.[17]

## September 2023 Garland hearing

At the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]]'s September 20, 2023 oversight hearing, Massie confronted Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] in what [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described as a fiery exchange, stating that Garland appeared to perjure himself relative to his prior statements.[18] The moment was among several viral confrontations generated by Republican members during the [[september-2023-garland-house-judiciary-hearing|five-hour session]].[18]

## Criticism of the Venezuela oil rationale, December 2025

Amid President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s December 2025 blockade of [[venezuela|Venezuela]], Massie disputed the claim that Venezuela had stolen American oil, writing that Trump was "talking about projects by Oil Companies that were nationalized by the government of Venezuela two decades ago" and that "no one campaigned on using our soldiers to overthrow Venezuela to make these Oil Companies whole again."[19] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] cited the remark approvingly, saying "even Thomas Massie is smarter than the Maduro haters."[19]

## First bill passed (November 2025)

Massie's [[epstein-files-transparency-act|Epstein Files Transparency Act]], carried to the floor by [[discharge-petition|discharge petition]] and passed by the [[us-house-of-representatives|House]] on November 18, 2025, was widely noted as the first bill he had succeeded in passing in thirteen years in [[congress|Congress]].[20] Critics circulated the claim that he was "the 1st member of congress to pass a bill that unmasks 1000's of child abuse victims" and releases hundreds of hours of abuse material to the public.[20] Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] amplified that framing, joking about the elapsed time relative to Massie's remarriage after his wife's death, and argued that "fake Epstein declas" is a red herring rather than genuine transparency — real transparency would mean opening every election's votes to public inspection and publishing a list of Americans who have received foreign money.[20]
