# Infowhyse EdiVote100 Clickers

The Infowhyse EdiVote100 is a handheld electronic audience-response device — commonly called a "clicker" — used to record and tally votes at meetings and conventions.[1] The [[colorado-republican-party|Colorado Republican Party]] used EdiVote100 units to conduct delegate voting at its [[2022-colorado-republican-state-assembly|2022 state assembly and convention]], a choice that became the central controversy of the event.[1]

## Discontinued status

According to information available on the manufacturer's website at the time, the devices were unsupported, having been discontinued in July 2021.[1] Delegates attempting to raise this point were unable to do so: only ten minutes total had been allotted for motions from 3,772 credentialed delegates, and that time was consumed by the failed effort to substitute hand-marked paper ballots.[1]

## Reported operational failures

Reporting by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who shifted from covering the votes to documenting clicker complaints as the day progressed, catalogued several categories of problem.[1]

Unequal handling by county: delegates from Routt County had to sign out their clickers and were reprimanded for swapping or appearing to swap them, implying serial numbers were tied to individuals, while El Paso, Douglas, and Adams county delegates received clickers with no tracking at all.[1] [[el-paso-county|El Paso County]] chair Vickie Tonkins confirmed that in her county the clickers were neither assigned by delegate nor tracked.[1] Some units carried serial numbers or barcodes and some did not; party officials said the difference did not matter because the barcodes were unimportant.[1]

Ineffective training: verifying a vote after the fact required only a three-digit label, usually on the front of the device, and a "channel number" displayed by pressing the Link button, which the Teller Committee said formed a unique combination.[1] A PowerPoint training delivered by Weld County Clerk and state Teller Committee co-chair Carly Koppes added that the screen also had to read "COUNTED."[1] Throughout the day delegates reported that their devices did not display "COUNTED" after voting, and there appeared to be no help desk, recourse, or support process for affected delegates.[1]

Shortages: multiple counties reported missing units. Fremont County was short one, Adams County reported ten missing before attributing the gap to a delegate-list problem, and the official party position was that it was three clickers short overall because none remained.[1] El Paso County, the largest in the state, received its clickers last in a process integrity-focused delegates described as "chaotic and insecure," delaying the start of voting for every county.[1]

Absence of security controls: Ashe in America reported witnessing people wearing guest badges rather than delegate badges voting with clickers, finding clickers lying unattended, and overhearing a delegate confirm voting choices with a nearby person before handing over the device, hugging them goodbye, and leaving the arena.[1] She concluded that there were zero controls governing the process by which Republican candidates reached the primary ballot.[1]

## Consequences

The very first clicker vote, for Governor, produced a result that shocked the room, leaving [[danielle-neuschwanger|Danielle Neuschwanger]] roughly 112 votes short of the 30% ballot-access threshold; her volunteers were immediately inundated with reports that the clickers had malfunctioned.[1] Clicker problems continued through the day and affected every race.[1] Ashe in America doubted Colorado would attempt the clickers again, but warned that mobile voting was "just around the corner."[1]
