# Asif William Rahman

Asif William Rahman is a United States government employee charged in November 2024 with leaking classified documents assessing Israel's plans to strike Iran.[1]

## Arrest and charges

Court papers filed on Wednesday, November 13, 2024 disclosed that Rahman had been charged with leaking classified information relating to Israel's earlier plans to attack Iran.[1] He was arrested by the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] that week in Cambodia and was due to make his first court appearance in Guam.[1] Rahman had been indicted the previous week in federal court in Virginia on two counts of willful transmission of national defense information, felony charges carrying the possibility of significant prison sentences.[1] It was not immediately clear whether he had retained counsel or which federal agency employed him, though officials said he held top secret security clearance.[1]

## The leaked documents

The charges stem from documents attributed to the [[national-geospatial-intelligence-agency|National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency]] and the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]] that appeared the previous month on a channel of the Telegram messaging application.[1] The documents indicated that Israel was still moving military assets into place to conduct a strike in response to Iran's ballistic missile attack of October 1, 2024; Israel carried out a retaliatory attack on multiple sites in Iran in late October.[1] The material was sharable within the [[five-eyes|Five Eyes]] intelligence partnership comprising the United States, Britain, Canada, New Zealand and Australia.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] News Brief, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used the indictment to revisit earlier speculation that Pentagon adviser Ariane Tabatabai had been the source of the leak, noting that Tabatabai had said no agency had contacted her about any investigation.[1] He cautioned that Rahman's charging neither exonerated Tabatabai nor established Rahman's guilt, raising the possibility that Rahman was a patsy, and framed the episode as an artifact of an ongoing fifth-generation [[information-war|information war]] in which readers should guard their psyches.[1]
