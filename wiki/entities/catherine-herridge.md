# Catherine Herridge

Catherine Herridge is an American investigative journalist covering national security and intelligence matters, frequently cited by Badlands writers for her reporting on declassified documents.[1]

## Reporting on the Gabbard document release

In April 2026 Herridge reported that the document dump by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] had uncovered allegations against Representative [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] of leaking state secrets.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] relayed the report in the Badlands Brief alongside news of Swalwell's resignation from [[congress|Congress]], connecting the leak allegations to his earlier association with a suspected Chinese intelligence operative.[1]

## Havana Syndrome whistleblower interview (2024)

In late December 2024, Herridge published an interview with a former [[cia]] officer using the pseudonym "Alice," who alleged that the CIA and other intelligence agencies had continually downplayed or outright denied the causes and effects of "Anomalous Health Incidents," commonly known as [[havana-syndrome|Havana Syndrome]].[2] "Alice" described how she and many other intelligence agents and government officials were targeted by energy weapons that caused serious neurological harm, and said the CIA denied suggestions that at least some of the attacks were carried out by Russia.[2] The interview was carried as a bonus item in the Badlands News Brief of January 2, 2025, under the framing of intelligence-community gaslighting.[2]

## "Black swan" prediction for 2024

In a roundtable discussion aired in late December 2023, Herridge was asked by host Margaret Brennan what to expect in the coming year and offered what she called a "dark" forecast.[3] "I just feel a lot of concern that 2024 may be the year of a black swan event," she said. "This is a national security event with high impact that's very hard to predict."[3] The remark, reported by Modernity News, was carried as a bonus item in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[3]

## CBS layoff and seizure of her files (2024)

In February 2024 Herridge was included in a round of layoffs at [[cbs-news|CBS]], a move that shocked many colleagues given her record of breaking news and original reporting.[4] Concern intensified after CBS officials took the unusual step of seizing her files, computers and records, including information on privileged sources.[4] The network's position alarmed many observers, including the union, as an attack on free press principles by one of the nation's most esteemed press organizations.[4]

Law professor Jonathan Turley, writing in The Hill, reported that current and former CBS employees he spoke with confidentially could not recall the company ever taking such a step before; one former CBS journalist said employees "are confused why [Herridge] was laid off, as one of the correspondents who broke news regularly and did a lot of original reporting."[4] The same source said he had never seen a seizure of records from a departing journalist and that the move had sent a "chilling signal" in the ranks of CBS, raising questions about the source of the pressure.[4] Turley opened the column by quoting CBS icon [[edward-r-murrow|Edward R. Murrow]]: "Anyone who isn't confused really doesn't understand the situation."[4]

In the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] news brief of February 23, 2024, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the alleged confiscation of Herridge's personal files "one of the worse violations of freedom of the press in modern history," argued that a genuine journalistic organization would fight such an overreach "to the point of imprisonment," and urged unions to sue, journalists to walk off and sources to refuse to collaborate with the network.[4]
