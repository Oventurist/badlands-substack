# Gary Sick

Gary Sick was a Middle East specialist and White House staffer in the [[jimmy-carter|Carter]] administration who became the principal public documenter of the alleged 1980 "October Surprise" deal.[1]

## "October Surprise"

Sick wrote a 1991 book titled *October Surprise* after discovering evidence that [[william-casey|William Casey]] had made a deal with the Iranians to prevent the release of the American hostages before the 1980 election, thereby preventing Carter's re-election.[1] Reporting on his findings, the New York Times noted that the allegation of meetings between Casey — Reagan's campaign chairman, who went on to be Director of Central Intelligence — and Hojatolislam [[mehdi-karrubi|Mehdi Karrubi]], a representative of Ayatollah [[ruhollah-khomeini|Ruhollah Khomeini]], had been reported for the first time by Sick.[1]

Sick said he had become convinced there were two meetings between Casey and Karrubi at the Ritz Hotel in Madrid in late July 1980.[1] His principal source for the Madrid meetings was Jamshid Hashemi, an Iranian arms dealer who said that he and his brother Cyrus had helped arrange them; attending, they said, were Casey, the Hashemi brothers and an unnamed American intelligence officer.[1]

## Significance in the article

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] uses Sick's work, together with the later testimony of [[ben-barnes|Ben Barnes]], as the evidentiary basis for arguing that [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] and Casey opened a working relationship with Iran in 1980 that would later produce the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra]] scandal.[1]
