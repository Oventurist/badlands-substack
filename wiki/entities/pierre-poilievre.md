# Pierre Poilievre

Pierre Poilievre is the leader of the Conservative Party of Canada and, as of the 2025 federal campaign, the principal opposition rival to Liberal leader [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]].[1]

## 2025 federal election campaign

When Carney asked the Governor General to call a snap federal election for April 28, 2025, Poilievre launched his campaign from Gatineau, across the Ottawa River from the capital.[1] He publicly acknowledged that the political ground in [[canada|Canada]] had shifted and that a new political rival had emerged in the form of U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], whose on-again, off-again tariffs and annexation rhetoric came to dominate the race.[1] Poilievre campaigned on cutting taxes and boosting natural-resource production, seeking to relate to voters' economic anxieties — and anger — and promising to convert those emotions into action, before taking his campaign to Toronto.[1]

## Defeat in the 2025 election

On 28 April 2025, Poilievre lost the federal election to [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]], whose Liberals were projected by CBC/Radio-Canada to form government.[2] NPR described the outcome as a stunning reversal of fortunes for the 45-year-old career politician, who for more than a year had ridden high in the polls — at one point with his Conservatives up 27 points over the Liberals — before Trump's annexation threats and tariffs triggered a wave of Canadian nationalism that reframed the race as a referendum on who could best handle the U.S. president.[2]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary treated the contest between Poilievre, Carney, and the New Democratic Party's Jagmeet Singh as a managed exercise rather than a genuine choice, arguing that both major parties were campaigning on continuing what the author characterized as trade arrangements unfavourable to the United States.[1]
