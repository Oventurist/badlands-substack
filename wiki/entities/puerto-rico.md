# Puerto Rico

Puerto Rico is a United States territory whose electronic voting infrastructure became the subject of a federal intelligence and law-enforcement examination disclosed publicly in February 2026.[1]

## 2025 voting equipment examination

In the spring of 2025, the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] (ODNI), then led by [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], coordinated with the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] to obtain and examine electronic voting machines and related data used in Puerto Rico's elections.[1] The operation was not publicly reported until [[reuters|Reuters]] broke the story on February 4, 2026, at which point ODNI confirmed the action in a written statement that was quoted only in part and never released in full.[1]

ODNI said it had "found extremely concerning cyber security and operational deployment practices that pose a significant risk to U.S. elections," and justified the examination by reference to its "broad statutory authority to coordinate, integrate, and analyze intelligence related to election security."[1] The agency characterized the seizure of machines and data as "standard practice in forensic analysis," and indicated that some of the identified security gaps stemmed from the machines' reliance on vulnerable cellular technology.[1]

Anonymous officials cited by Reuters said the objective had been to investigate claims that [[venezuela|Venezuela]] had hacked voting machines in the territory, but that the probe produced no clear evidence of Venezuelan interference.[1]

## Local political response

Puerto Rico's non-voting member of the U.S. House, Pablo Jose Hernandez Rivera (D-PR), disputed the foreign-interference framing, saying: "We have had widely reported problems in election administration. But they are all attributable to incompetence and corruption, not foreign interference."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Badlands' own sources described the Reuters story as "a hit job to fud the election investigations," while confirming that the voting machine analysis in the territory did in fact occur.[1] According to those sources, the investigation was not about Venezuela — "it's likely about the Americans."[1] The commentary emphasized two features of the disclosure: the analysis occurred nearly a year before it surfaced, and it had not previously leaked.[1]

## Harris visit protest (March 2024)

In March 2024, Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] visited the Goyoco community center in the Santurce neighborhood of San Juan, where her motorcade was confronted by dozens of demonstrators, including one holding a sign reading "Kamala Harris war criminal."[2] Musicians at the site performed a song protesting her presence, asking what she had come for, invoking "Free Palestine and Haiti," and demanding to know what she thought "of the colony" — a reference to Puerto Rico's territorial status.[2] Harris clapped along before an aide explained the song's content.[2]

## June 2024 primary voting-machine discrepancies

Following Puerto Rico's heated June 2, 2024 primaries, the island's elections commission announced that it was reviewing its contract with [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] after hundreds of discrepancies were discovered in the results.[3] Interim commission president Jessika Padilla Rivera attributed the discrepancies to a software issue that caused the machines to incorrectly calculate vote totals; machine-reported counts came in lower than paper counts in some cases, while other machines reversed totals or reported zero votes for particular candidates, though no party contested the outcome.[3] Padilla stressed that with general elections due in November the island needed assurance both that the machines produce a correct result and that the reported result matches it.[3] More than 6,000 Dominion machines were used in the primaries, and the company said the software problems stemmed from the digital files used to export results.[3]
