# Kate Starbird

Kate Starbird is an American academic and misinformation researcher who, in Badlands Media's account, has become one of the most visible practitioners of the newly minted profession of "disinformation expert."[1] She is described as a member of the [[cisa|Cybersecurity & Infrastructure Security Agency]] (CISA), and the article situates her alongside [[nina-jankowicz|Nina Jankowicz]] as an individual who has ascended to what it calls the "lofty perch" of arbiter of online truth.[1]

## Media profile

Starbird was the featured subject of a segment on CBS's [[60-minutes|60 Minutes]] devoted to disinformation, a program the article characterizes as having a long history of delivering establishment propaganda; the same broadcast, it notes, also promoted claims about Russia using directed-frequency weapons against NATO officials that the author regards as debunked.[1] The segment framed the dispute over online truth and falsity as a liberal-versus-conservative conflict, presenting concern about the relationship between Big Tech, the federal government and fact-checking organizations as an exclusively conservative preoccupation.[1] Introduced as an academic from Stanford, Starbird was portrayed as a virtuous defender against disinformation and as a victim of online harassment.[1]

Asked by the host about death threats against researchers, Starbird replied that she had received one, adding that "sometimes they're threats with something behind them, and sometimes they're just there to make you nervous."[1] The article argues that a single alleged death threat is a slender basis for the sympathy the broadcast solicited, contrasting her treatment with journalists such as [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] and [[glenn-greenwald|Glenn Greenwald]], whose reporting on state secrets has exposed them and their families to sustained security risks.[1] When the host suggested that a campaign against her was designed to discredit her, Starbird agreed and observed that "the people that pushed voter fraud lies are some of the same people that are trying to discredit researchers."[1] The article responds that criticism of a researcher's decisions is a legitimate exercise of speech rather than evidence of a conspiracy.[1]

Starbird also appeared in a January 2024 article in [[the-guardian|The Guardian]] titled "'Stakes are Really High': misinformation researcher changes tack for 2024 US election," in which she said she had herself become the subject of an ongoing misinformation campaign and noted that researchers across the country had received subpoenas, letters and criticism seeking to frame misinformation research as partisan censorship.[1] The article contends that the pattern of both the outlets and the researchers is to target the populist right while never pushing back against falsehoods originating with the Washington establishment.[1]

## Political statements

The article disputes the claim that Starbird is politically neutral, citing a Facebook post she made on 31 October 2018, shortly before that year's midterm elections.[1] In it she urged readers to vote for a Democratic majority in the House and Senate so that "we can begin to hold this President and his friends/family accountable for things like corruption and collusion," argued that the word "nationalism" in the mouth of the president carried undertones of white supremacy and ethno-nationalism, criticised tax cuts for the wealthy, warned that GOP health-care rhetoric threatened people with pre-existing conditions such as her wife, and described a global rise of fascism and white nationalism.[1] She closed by saying the moment was not one for old party divisions and offered to point conservative friends toward longtime conservatives advocating a similar voting strategy.[1]

Badlands treats the "corruption and collusion" line as a reference to the allegation that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] colluded with the Russian government to win the 2016 election — an allegation the article regards as thoroughly debunked and as itself a product of misinformation contained in the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]].[1] The objection raised is one of epistemic posture: Starbird did not write that Trump "may be" guilty or "has been accused," but treated the charge as settled fact, and she has never retracted the post, apologised, or acknowledged the findings of [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]].[1] Her characterisation of nationalism is likewise described as opinion presented as fact.[1]

## Content-removal requests

Among the grievances aired in the 60 Minutes segment was that [[twitter|Twitter]] acted on Starbird's requests to remove information only about 30 percent of the time.[1] The article reads this complaint as evidence that the mainstream media has its own stake in the censorship question, and as a sign that the public is no longer willing to let researchers dictate which opinions may be expressed.[1]
