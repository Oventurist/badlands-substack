# Matt Gaetz

Matt Gaetz is an American Republican politician who served in the United States House of Representatives and became one of the most combative populist-aligned members of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]].[1]

## Confrontation with John Durham (June 2023)

At the [[durham-house-judiciary-testimony-2023|House Judiciary Committee hearing of June 21, 2023]], Gaetz sharply attacked Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] over the limited results of the [[durham-report|Durham Report]], a performance that [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentators described as uncharacteristic and seemingly unfair given Gaetz's general alignment with critics of the [[russiagate-hoax|Russia collusion narrative]].[1] Video of the exchange circulated widely on Twitter.[1]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that, whether or not Gaetz is himself a "Con, Inc." asset, his performance carried a benefit in the narrative war: by raging at Durham for not producing substantive results, Gaetz reinforced the perception that the real crimes remain unexposed, countering the mainstream framing that the Durham Report exonerated the officials [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had accused.[1]

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] grouped Gaetz with [[peter-navarro|Steve Navarro]] and [[steve-bannon|Steven Bannon]] as proponents of the view that Durham was "more rotten, spineless fruit from the [[bill-barr|Bill Barr]] tree."[1]

## Role in the 2023 speakership fight

Gaetz led the motion to oust Rep. [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] (R-Calif.) as Speaker of the House, an action that opened a three-week leadership void in the chamber.[2] After the GOP conference eventually settled on Rep. [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] (R-La.), who won 220 votes on the first floor ballot, Gaetz declared the outcome a demonstration of the strength of the "MAGA movement" in the Republican Party and of the waning power of the "swamp."[2]

Speaking on [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]]'s "War Room" podcast, Gaetz said: "The swamp is on the run. MAGA is ascendant. If you don't think that moving from Kevin McCarthy to MAGA Mike Johnson shows the ascendance of this movement and where the power in the Republican Party truly lies, then you're not paying attention."[2] He also described the internal party deliberations that produced Johnson's elevation after the short-lived nomination of Rep. [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]] (R-Minn.), who withdrew hours after winning the conference nod, and the earlier failures of Reps. [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]] (R-La.) and [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] (R-Ohio) to assemble sufficient support.[2] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] credited Gaetz with correctly summarizing Johnson's ascendance as a proxy for the [[america-first-movement|America First]] and Trump-inspired movement.[2] See [[2023-speaker-of-the-house-election]].

The removal vote itself, on October 3, 2023, was the first successful ouster of a sitting Speaker in U.S. history, forced by a contingent of hard-right conservatives that Gaetz led and joined by Democrats who piled on.[3] Badlands commentary placed Gaetz alongside [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the America First movement as the public antithesis of the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] establishment that McCarthy was taken to represent in the [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]], and read the resulting chaos as a designed feature of an exposure operation rather than a governance failure.[3] See [[ouster-of-kevin-mccarthy]].

## Syria withdrawal resolution (2023)

In March 2023 Gaetz sponsored a war powers resolution that would have forced the withdrawal of U.S. troops from Syria within six months; it failed in the House 103–321, with backing from Reps. [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] and [[andy-biggs|Andy Biggs]] among others.[4] On the House floor Gaetz described American troops in Syria as trapped in a "hellscape" of war and foreign meddling with no end in sight for counter-terrorism operations, and argued that [[isis|ISIS]] forces there posed no serious threat to the United States.[4] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that the vote exposed the members of Congress "beholden to the establishment, specifically the war machine."[4] See [[gaetz-syria-troop-withdrawal-resolution|the resolution]] for details.[4]

## October 2023 move against Speaker McCarthy

On October 1, 2023, Gaetz appeared on ABC's "This Week" and said he would file a [[motion-to-vacate|motion to vacate]] against Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] that week, following reporting that McCarthy had struck a Ukraine funding arrangement with Democrats without informing his own caucus until after the 45-day [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]] cleared the House.[5] McCarthy, speaking on CBS's "Face the Nation," dismissed the threat as "personal with Matt" and said Gaetz had opposed "the most conservative bill to protect our border."[5]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] sided with Gaetz, recalling that Gaetz and about twenty allies had forced fifteen ballots in January 2023 to extract rules changes for the 118th Congress — including an end to omnibus bills and continuing resolutions — which McCarthy accepted in exchange for the gavel and then, in her view, violated.[5] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the standoff differently, calling Gaetz versus McCarthy "the most obvious bit of [[kayfabe|Kayfabe]]" in the information war, with both men allegedly serving roles chosen by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[5]

## Weaponization Subcommittee questioning (February 2023)

At the first hearing of the [[house-weaponization-committee|Select Subcommittee on the Weaponization of the Federal Government]] in February 2023, Gaetz questioned the Democrat-called witness [[elliot-williams|Elliot Williams]], who was defending the [[fbi|FBI]]'s collusion with Big Tech to censor conservatives.[6] Gaetz left Williams stammering as he pressed him to acknowledge that he is a partner at the [[raben-group|Raben Group]], a lobbying firm representing [[google|Google]], [[george-soros|George Soros]]'s [[open-society-foundations|Open Society]], and [[pfizer|Pfizer]] — exposing, in the account's framing, the union between the DC establishment and private institutions targeting Americans' rights.[6] Gaetz posted the exchange to X on the evening of February 9, 2023, where it drew about 3,150 reposts and 9,140 likes.[6]

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that Gaetz was again showing the rest of the House GOP how to serve constituents, rejecting claims that the hearings were "just theatrics" on the grounds that although the legislature lacks prosecutorial powers, the hearings place the record before the public.[6]

## September 2023 warning to McCarthy

In early September 2023, amid the fight over the Biden administration's $40 billion supplemental spending request — including roughly $24 billion in additional Ukraine funding — Gaetz delivered a public warning that Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] needed to launch an impeachment inquiry into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] over his family's foreign business dealings or risk his job.[7] Speaking to conservative radio host Todd Starnes, Gaetz said, "I worked very hard in January to develop a toolkit for House Republicans to use in a productive and positive way. I don't believe we've used those tools as effectively as we should have," adding: "That means forcing votes on impeachment. And if Speaker McCarthy stands in our way, he may not have the job long."[7] Freedom Caucus members simultaneously pressed to separate the Ukraine money from the rest of the package.[7] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the feuding accelerated the collapse of the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] establishment in public perception while elevating the Freedom Caucus and Trump, and that it usefully highlighted alleged money laundering between the U.S. deep state and Ukraine.[7] See [[september-2023-ukraine-supplemental-spending-request]].[7]

## Aftermath of the withdrawn attorney general nomination

Gaetz's selection on 13 November 2024 left Senate Republicans shocked, and his withdrawal just over a week later came equally suddenly; the episode was read as a dramatic affirmation that the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] still maintained its constitutional "advice and consent" power over a president's Cabinet, even as observers expected further struggles over that authority.[8] Within hours [[donald-trump|Trump]] had moved on, naming [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] to run the Justice Department.[8] Commentary in the Truth Community described the episode as the "Gaetz rug pull" and argued that the shockwaves it sent through Washington had raised the stakes for the still-unannounced [[fbi|FBI]] leadership decision, with [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]]'s possible elevation described as capable of sending "Gaetz-like shockwaves" through the capital.[8]

## June 2023 rule-vote revolt

On June 6, 2023, Gaetz was among eleven Republicans, led by [[house-freedom-caucus|House Freedom Caucus]] members, who broke with their party on a routine procedural rule vote in retaliation for Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s handling of the [[debt-ceiling-agreement-2023|debt ceiling]] deal, the first such failed rule vote in nearly two decades.[9] "We're frustrated with the way this place is operating," Gaetz said. "We're not going to live in the era of the imperial speaker anymore."[9]
