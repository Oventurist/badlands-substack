# Sergey Ryabkov

Sergey Ryabkov is a Russian Deputy Foreign Minister who serves as one of Moscow's principal public voices on arms control, nuclear policy and relations with the United States.[1]

## Statements on Russian nuclear doctrine

In remarks given to the Russian state news agency TASS in September 2024, Ryabkov said that Russia would revise its nuclear doctrine on the basis of an analysis of recent conflicts and of Western conduct toward what Moscow calls its "special military operation" in Ukraine.[1] He stated that "the work is in the advanced stage, and there is a clear intent to introduce a correction" to the doctrine, attributing the revision in part to "our Western adversaries' escalation course."[1]

Ryabkov added that the document was being finalized but declined to give a timeframe, noting that "the timeframe for its completion is a rather complicated issue, considering that we are talking about the most important aspect of our national security."[1] His comments followed earlier statements by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] that Russian nuclear doctrine is a "live document" subject to amendment, while maintaining that Moscow assumes a nuclear exchange will never occur.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read Ryabkov's announcement, alongside remarks by Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergei Lavrov]], as Russia "posturing to threaten to nuke the United States" — while maintaining the Badlands editorial position that Russia and the United States would not enter an actual kinetic conflict, and that the narrative of imminent war would nonetheless accelerate.[1] See [[russian-nuclear-doctrine]].

## Warning to Israel over Syria (December 2024)

After Israeli troops launched an incursion into Syria and Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] declared his intent to expand Israeli holdings in the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]], Ryabkov — as Russian Deputy Foreign Minister — issued the Kremlin's response, advising "hotheads" in West Jerusalem to refrain from trying to exploit the situation in Syria.[2] He warned that Israel should avoid being "intoxicated by opportunities" presented by the ongoing crisis.[2] Badlands read the remarks as evidence that Russia and Israel were on a collision course over the post-Assad settlement.[2]
