# Olaf Scholz

Olaf Scholz served as Chancellor of [[germany|Germany]], leading the government that in September 2024 announced temporary controls at all German land borders in a crackdown on irregular migration.[1]

The measures, announced by Interior Minister [[nancy-faeser|Nancy Faeser]], were to begin on September 16 and last an initial six months, and included a scheme allowing authorities to reject more migrants directly at the border.[1] Reporting characterized the move as an attempt by Scholz's government to seize back the political initiative from far-right and conservative opposition parties whose support had risen as they tapped into voter concerns about stretched public services, integration, and security.[1]

The border controls represented part of a multi-year hardening of German asylum and migration policy following a surge in arrivals from the Middle East, and were notable for suspending normal free movement within the European Schengen zone.[1]

## Role in the Gershkovich exchange

German Chancellor Scholz was a necessary participant in the arrangements to free Wall Street Journal reporter [[evan-gershkovich|Evan Gershkovich]] from Russian custody.[2] At the White House Correspondents' Dinner, Gershkovich's mother Ella asked President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to telephone Scholz, at the direction of hostage-affairs envoy [[roger-carstens|Roger Carstens]]; Biden claimed he had already called, Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] corrected him, and Biden ultimately sent a formal signed letter to Scholz instead, which was necessary to advance the deal.[2]

## Loss of confidence vote (December 2024)

### Firing of Lindner and coalition implosion (November 2024)

On the evening of Wednesday, November 6, 2024, Scholz announced that he would fire Finance Minister [[christian-lindner|Christian Lindner]] over persistent rifts on spending and economic reforms, imploding the three-party coalition and paving the way for a snap election at the end of March.[4] The firing ejected Lindner's fiscally conservative [[free-democratic-party|Free Democratic Party]] from the coalition and forced Scholz to call a confidence vote he said would be held on January 15.[4] Scholz, described in the coverage as the weakest German chancellor in decades, said at the chancellery: "Dear fellow citizens, I would have liked to have spared you this difficult decision, especially in times like these, when uncertainty is growing."[4] The collapse came just hours after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s clear U.S. election win, a result that stunned German leaders dependent on American military might and fearful that Trump's tariffs would hobble German industry.[4] See [[2024-german-government-collapse|2024 German government collapse]].[4]

On Monday, December 16, 2024, Scholz lost a vote of no confidence in the Bundestag, clearing the way for early elections in February and capping a turbulent year for Europe's leaders and governing parties.[3] He had called the vote expecting to lose it, hoping the outcome would boost the fortunes of his center-left Social Democratic Party; he needed 367 votes but only 207 members backed him, while 394 voted against and 116 abstained.[3]

Scholz's fractious coalition had collapsed the previous month after he fired Finance Minister Christian Lindner of the pro-market Free Democrats, with which the SPD governed alongside the environmentalist Green Party.[3] Scholz accused Lindner of violating his trust by publicly backing an economic policy different from the one they had agreed, and subsequently announced he would call the confidence vote.[3] President [[frank-walter-steinmeier|Frank-Walter Steinmeier]] then had 21 days to decide whether to dissolve parliament and call an election — expected after Christmas — with new elections to be held within 60 days of that decision.[3] It was only the sixth time a no-confidence vote had been called in the country of 83 million, Europe's most populous and largest economy; the mechanism is required because post-World War II Germany's constitution does not allow the Bundestag to dissolve itself.[3]

## Luftwaffe wiretap affair, March 2024

In March 2024 Scholz promised a full inquiry after Russia published a recording of a phone call between senior German air force officers in which they appeared to discuss supplying missiles to Ukraine.[5] Speaking on the sidelines of a meeting with Pope Francis in Rome, he described the incident as a "very serious affair," saying: "It will be investigated very carefully, intensively and quickly. It is also necessary to do so."[5]

Politicians from parties in Scholz's coalition expressed concern that Russia may have eavesdropped on other sensitive conversations, and that German government communications might no longer be adequately protected.[5] The recording, posted on Telegram by [[margarita-simonyan|Margarita Simonyan]], head of Russian state broadcaster RT, captured the head of the Luftwaffe, [[ingo-gerhartz|Ingo Gerhartz]], discussing the possible deployment of German Taurus missiles in Ukraine, whether Ukraine could attack Russian targets without German soldiers' participation, and how many missiles could be delivered.[5] Scholz had to that point refused to deliver Taurus missiles to Kyiv.[5]

## June 2024 European elections

Scholz was identified in Badlands commentary, together with France's [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]], as among the leaders most clearly rattled by the [[2024-european-parliament-elections|June 2024 European Parliament elections]], in which both were rejected by double-digit deficits.[6] The commentary treated the outcome as a bellwether of European sentiment shifting toward strong borders and anti-interventionism rather than as an immediate change to German leadership.[6]

## Eastern state election setbacks (September 2024)

Newsweek reported that the outcomes of the September 2024 eastern German state elections underscored deepening discontent with Scholz's government, which had been mired in unpopularity and internal conflicts.[7] In the [[2024-thuringia-state-election|Thuringia state election]] the [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany]] won 32 to 33 percent, its first state election victory since the Nazi era, far outpacing the [[christian-democratic-union|Christian Democratic Union]]'s roughly 24 percent, while the [[2024-saxony-state-election|Saxony contest]] was projected as a near tie between the two parties.[7]
