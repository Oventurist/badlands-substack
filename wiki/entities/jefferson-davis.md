# Jefferson Davis

Jefferson Davis was the President of the Confederacy during the American Civil War.[1] In 2023 his name resurfaced in American political argument as the historical reference point for Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|14th Amendment]], the disqualification clause adopted after the war to bar former Confederates from holding office.[1]

## Role in the 2023 Trump disqualification litigation

During the Colorado trial that led the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] to disqualify [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the 2024 ballot, a significant portion of the discussion was dedicated to Davis, and the court's ruling reflected the view that Davis was an apt comparison for Trump.[1] The 14th Amendment challenges brought across the nation similarly used Davis as the benchmark for what the amendment's framers intended to prevent.[1]

[[new-york-times|New York Times]] editorial board member [[mara-gay|Mara Gay]] extended the analogy on [[msnbc|MSNBC]], describing critics of the Colorado ruling as displaying "the spirit of those confederates" and invoking the "traitors" the amendment was written to exclude.[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized this rhetorical move as implying that January 6 had been a civil war, and called the framing insane but predictable given the establishment's failure to stop Trump ahead of 2024.[1]

## Cited in Anderson v. Griswold (2023)

Confederate president Jefferson Davis featured prominently in the December 6, 2023 oral argument before the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] in [[anderson-v-griswold|Anderson v. Griswold]], where counsel and justices discussed at length the parallels between the Civil War era that produced Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]] and the January 6 events used to challenge [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s ballot eligibility.[2]
