# Society of the Cincinnati

The Society of the Cincinnati — rendered in Badlands Media commentary as the International Society of Cincinnati or the Society of the Cincinnatus — is described by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as an international republican organisation founded around [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]] which, together with the Philosophical Society founded by [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]], operated as a globally coordinating body for republican politics.[1]

## Membership and reach

The article states that the two societies worked closely as a coordinating body stretching through Europe, Germany and Spain to the United States, with members across Russia, Europe and the new republic, and that leading figures within Russia, Prussia, Poland, Ireland and England took part.[1] The [[marquis-de-lafayette|Marquis de Lafayette]] was a leading figure within the society and served as president of its European branch, so that if there was to be a second chance at an American-style revolution in France, Lafayette was expected to lead it.[1]

Named American members include [[james-fenimore-cooper|James Fenimore Cooper]] and [[edgar-allan-poe|Edgar Allan Poe]], the latter described as a third-generation member who was in France in 1832 alongside a network of fellow members who had followed Lafayette back across the Atlantic.[1] Also situated within this milieu were Washington Irving, then US ambassador to Spain, the Canadian republican leader William Lyon Mackenzie, and the physician Samuel Gridley Howe, founder of American schools for the blind whose lineage the article connects to Helen Keller and Anne Sullivan.[1]
