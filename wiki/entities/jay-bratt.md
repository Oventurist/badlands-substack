# Jay Bratt

Jay Bratt is a career U.S. Justice Department prosecutor who joined Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s investigative team in November 2022 and became a central figure in the classified-documents case brought against former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] Reporting compiled in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of August 29, 2023 identified Bratt, then 63 years old, as one of Smith's principal aides on the prosecution.[1]

## White House meetings

Citing a *New York Post* report relayed by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], the brief reported that Bratt met at the White House on March 31, 2023 with Caroline Saba, deputy chief of staff for the White House counsel's office, in a 10 a.m. meeting also attended by [[fbi|FBI]] agent Danielle Ray.[1] Trump was indicted by Smith's office nine weeks later, a sequence the brief presented as evidence that the prosecution was coordinated with the incumbent administration rather than insulated from it.[1]

The brief further noted that Bratt had met with Saba at the White House once before, in November 2021, at a time when Trump's legal team was negotiating with the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]] over the return of presidential records from Mar-a-Lago and before any formal criminal investigation had been opened.[1]

## Badlands framing

Badlands Media contributors treated the Bratt meetings as documentary support for the claim that the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] had been weaponized against President Biden's leading political opponent ahead of the 2024 election, contradicting Biden's repeated assertions of departmental autonomy.[1] Commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the exposure of Smith's team by the *New York Post* was itself part of a broader dynamic in which aggressive prosecution forced disclosure of [[2020-election-fraud-claims|election fraud claims]] onto the national stage.[1]
