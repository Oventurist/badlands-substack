# Yevgeny Prigozhin

Yevgeny Prigozhin was the Russian financier and paramilitary leader who headed the mercenary organization [[wagner-group|Wagner]], described in reporting as a "warlord" and the "Wagner chief."[1] He was reported killed in a plane crash in Russia's Tver region in August 2023, weeks after Wagner's abortive armed march on Moscow.[1]

## Claims of a faked death

In late August 2023, Russian political analyst [[valery-solovey|Dr Valery Solovey]] asserted that Prigozhin was in fact "alive, well, and free" in an unnamed country, and that the man killed in the crash was a body double whom the Wagner leader was known to employ.[1] According to this account, Prigozhin survived an assassination attempt sanctioned by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and drawn up by Putin's security council, and was subsequently plotting revenge.[1] Solovey further accused the Russian authorities of lying about the recovery of Prigozhin's DNA at the crash site while knowing the assassination attempt had failed.[1]

The claims were reported by [[daily-mail|The Daily Mail]] as Russia staged a funeral for Prigozhin that Putin declined to attend.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] treated the story skeptically, noting that Solovey — who claims "sources inside the Kremlin" — has a long record of unsupported assertions about Putin's health and intentions, including repeated claims that Putin was terminally ill, was in "very poor physiological health," and intended to demonstrate tactical nuclear weapons.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that the "Actual" of Prigozhin's fate could not be gauged but that the narrative possibilities were "tantalizing," imagining the media simultaneously advancing stories about Prigozhin faking his death to stage a coup, a returning COVID variant, and the denial of election fraud — asking "Who are the conspiracy theorists, again?"[1]

## Appearance at the 2023 Russia-Africa summit

Prigozhin was photographed at the [[russia-africa-summit-2023|Russia-Africa summit]] in St. Petersburg on July 27, 2023, appearing on the sidelines of the event hosted by President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] just one month after launching his failed [[wagner-group-mutiny|mutiny]].[2] Two pictures circulated on social media showed him in casual jeans and a white shirt, smiling as he pressed the flesh with visiting officials.[2] In the first, posted to Facebook by an Africa-based aide, he shook hands with a suited African delegate identified by the watchdog All Eyes on Wagner as chief of protocol to [[faustin-touadera|Faustin Touadéra]], president of the [[central-african-republic|Central African Republic]]; in the second he greeted the "director of Afrique media" according to a Wagner-branded Telegram channel.[2] The Orchestra Wagner channel subsequently claimed credit for the [[2023-niger-coup|coup in Niger]] staged on the eve of the summit, a claim that could not be independently verified.[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the reappearance vindicated anons who had read the June revolt as military deception, noting that Prigozhin and Putin had publicly engaged in obvious MILDEC one month prior during the defense of Bakhmut.[2]

## Bakhmut claims, May 2023

Late on Saturday, May 13, 2023, Prigozhin issued an audio statement clarifying that although regular Russian forces had "retreated" from parts of northern Bakhmut, his own [[wagner-group|Wagner]] fighters were still advancing in the [[battle-of-bakhmut|battle for the city]].[3] The statement extended his running power struggle with the military chain of command and the defence ministry, as he persisted in the controversial narrative that only his company was spearheading gains and engaged in heavy fighting, comparable to the earlier battle for Soledar.[3] He asserted that [[ukraine|Ukraine]] controlled only one square kilometre of Bakhmut and remained in control of just twenty-nine or so tall buildings.[3]

Prigozhin had described the regular Russian forces as "fleeing" — a characterization denied by the Kremlin — even though the defence ministry had itself acknowledged a limited pullback in northern sectors of the city the day before.[3] Russian state agency TASS separately quoted Akhmat commando commander Apty Alaudinov saying that Ukrainian activity had increased "only in some sectors" and that the situation in his sector was "completely under control," a framing at odds with claims of a broad Ukrainian surge.[3] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the affair as the "soft mutiny" of Prigozhin's mercenary group and cautioned that Western coverage of it was unreliable.[3]
