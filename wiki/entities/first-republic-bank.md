# First Republic Bank

First Republic Bank (FRC) was a U.S. lender catering heavily to wealthy clients that failed at the end of April 2023, becoming the third major American bank casualty of that year's banking turmoil.[1]

## Seizure and sale

The Wall Street Journal reported late on Friday, 28 April 2023, that large banks including [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan]] and [[pnc-financial-services-group|PNC]] were set to buy First Republic — not through a private, market-arranged transaction, but in a deal following a government seizure of the troubled lender.[1] A seizure and sale, which would wipe out First Republic's equity and potentially impose losses on creditors, was expected as soon as that weekend.[1] The outcome was that JPMorgan, already the largest U.S. bank, stood to become larger still by acquiring First Republic's sound assets while the [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|FDIC]] and, ultimately, taxpayers absorbed the toxic remainder.[1]

## The $30 billion deposit question

It was not immediately clear whether the $30 billion in deposits that JPMorgan and other large banks had earlier funneled into First Republic to stabilize it would be treated as insured funds.[1] Nor was it clear how a wipeout of that capital could be absorbed without sparking a systemic crisis, given that the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] was, in ZeroHedge's characterization, running a policy of "monetary tightening through bank collapse" after failing to contain inflation by conventional means.[1]

## Commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that the framing across alternative financial media was that "another boutique bank for the 'Elites' is folding" with the government — meaning taxpayers — left holding the bag.[1] He drew a comparison between the First Republic auction and the government-brokered [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]]–[[ubs|UBS]] merger, suggesting that large banks might be not only absorbing toxic assets from failing peers but also serving as custodians for the rest, and noting that many such institutions have been implicated in financing entities involved in human rights abuses.[1] He concluded that the ongoing exposure of the fiat central banking system was, on balance, a positive development even if the road proved bumpy.[1]

## March 2023 default risk

In coverage of the March 13, 2023 selloff, First Republic Bank (FRC) was identified — alongside Western Alliance (WAL) and MYFW — as one of the highest default-risk banks in the Russell 3000 Banks Subsector according to Bloomberg data.[2] Regional bank shares fell dramatically that session despite the emergency backstop announced by the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], Treasury and [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|FDIC]] the previous night, with multiple trading halts through the day following the failures of [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] and [[signature-bank|Signature Bank]].[2]
