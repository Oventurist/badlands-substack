# Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency

The **Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)** is the component of the U.S. Department of Homeland Security responsible for defending federal networks and coordinating protection of designated critical infrastructure, a category that since 2017 has included election systems. Its first director was [[chris-krebs|Chris Krebs]].

CISA's election role has been among the most contested in American politics. Following the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 presidential election]], the agency joined coordinating councils in declaring the vote "the most secure in U.S. history," a statement used to rebut claims of irregularities and to justify platform action against contrary speech.

The 2026 declassification by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] of a January 2020 [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] memo sharpened criticism of that posture. The memo, written by [[christopher-porter|Christopher Porter]], had warned that internet-connected voter registration databases, pollbooks, and election websites were vulnerable to foreign compromise, and evidence emerged after the election that [[china|China]] had obtained voter registration data in several states and [[iran|Iran]] had breached election-related systems. Badlands writers treat the gap between the classified warning and the public reassurance as an instance of institutional narrative management rather than security practice, a theme they connect to the broader [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship-industrial complex]].
