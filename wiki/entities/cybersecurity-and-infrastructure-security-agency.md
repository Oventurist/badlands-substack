# Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency

The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) is the U.S. federal agency charged with protecting critical infrastructure, including election systems. In Badlands coverage it appears chiefly as the institution whose 2020 public assurances are alleged to have conflicted with classified intelligence.[1]

Under director [[chris-krebs|Chris Krebs]], CISA and allied officials publicly declared the November 2020 election "the most secure in U.S. history," despite a January 2020 [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] memo warning that centralized voter registration databases, pollbooks, and election websites were internet-connected and vulnerable to foreign manipulation.[1] The declassification of that memo by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] and the ensuing inspector general investigation reframed CISA's election-security messaging as part of an alleged multi-year suppression of known vulnerabilities.[1]

## Krebs memorandum and ordered review

On April 9, 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed the memorandum [[addressing-risks-from-chris-krebs-and-government-censorship|"Addressing Risks from Chris Krebs and Government Censorship"]], which asserted that CISA under [[chris-krebs|Chris Krebs]] promoted the censorship of election information, including known risks associated with certain voting practices, and "falsely and baselessly denied that the 2020 election was rigged and stolen."[2] The memorandum ordered a review of Krebs' activities as a government employee, "including his leadership of CISA," together with recommendations for remedial or preventative actions, and directed revocation of his security clearance and suspension of clearances at associated entities such as [[sentinelone|SentinelOne]].[2] Badlands commentators cast the agency, and Krebs personally, as the origin point of the official 2020 election narrative they had long sought to see investigated.[2]

## Resumed platform contacts ahead of the 2024 election

In May 2024, Senator [[mark-warner|Mark Warner]], chairman of the [[senate-intelligence-committee|Senate Intelligence Committee]], said that CISA and the [[fbi|FBI]] had resumed discussions with social media companies about removing disinformation as the November presidential election approached — a reversal after the Biden administration froze such communications for months during the pending First Amendment case [[murthy-v-missouri|Murthy v. Missouri]].[3] That case was fueled by allegations that federal agencies including CISA had coerced platforms into removing content related to vaccine safety and 2020 presidential election results.[3] According to Warner, contacts resumed roughly when multiple Supreme Court justices appeared to favor the executive branch's position at oral argument.[3]

## Removal of election security staff (February 2025)

In February 2025, several CISA employees responsible for election security were placed on administrative leave, a move the Associated Press reported would affect critical support for state and local election offices across the country.[4] Seventeen CISA employees who worked with election officials on cybersecurity, ransomware threats, and physical security were placed on leave pending a review; ten of them were regional election security specialists hired to strengthen election security ahead of the 2024 election.[4] The review was to examine efforts to counter foreign interference in US elections, work that was reassigned to other staff members.[4]

The affected employees, all former state or local election officials, had been brought in to build relationships across all 50 states and more than 8,000 local election jurisdictions, spending the previous year working with officials, attending conferences, and providing guidance on cybersecurity and physical security.[4] Democracy Docket, the outlet founded by [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]], warned that the move put the security and integrity of US elections at risk, particularly at the state level where local officials rely on CISA resources.[4]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the opposite: that the staff "failed in that mission by every conceivable metric," that elections are not secure — let alone immune to known hardware vulnerabilities and admitted critical software vulnerabilities — and that CISA's 2020 election narrative "was always vaporware nonsense."[4] She wrote that removing the actors who propagated what she called a technologically illiterate story was expected and welcomed.[4]

## Medusa ransomware advisory (March 2025)

CISA issued a joint cybersecurity advisory on March 12, 2025 with the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] and the Multi-State Information Sharing and Analysis Center warning about [[medusa-ransomware|Medusa]], a ransomware-as-a-service variant credited with over 300 known victims across critical infrastructure sectors as of February 2025.[5] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the warning skeptically, asking whether it came from "the cyber experts at CISA or from environmental lawyers cosplaying as cyber experts at CISA," and concluding that the agency had earned the scrutiny.[5]


## Baltimore bridge collapse role

Following the [[francis-scott-key-bridge-collapse|Francis Scott Key Bridge collapse]], an unclassified CISA memo reported that federal officials were monitoring roughly 1.8 million gallons of fuel aboard the container ship [[dali|Dali]] for its "spill potential."[6] A separate memo obtained by ABC News showed that the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] had deemed the water near the crash site unsafe for divers.[6] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] closed her commentary on the pollution question by noting sardonically that with CISA involved, the episode was "sure to be the safest and most secure boat-crash-turned-fuel-spill in history."[6]
