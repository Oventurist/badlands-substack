# Mike Bost

Mike Bost is a United States congressman from Illinois who became the lead plaintiff in a long-running federal lawsuit challenging his home state's practice of counting mail ballots received after Election Day.[1]

## Bost v. Illinois State Board of Elections

The suit was filed on May 25, 2022 by [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] on behalf of Bost and two presidential electors, and targeted an Illinois law extending Election Day by fourteen days beyond the date established by federal law.[1] Federal law defines Election Day as the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November of every even-numbered year, and the plaintiffs argued that the Illinois receipt deadline conflicted with that statutory date.[1]

The lower courts, including the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, denied that Bost had standing to bring the challenge, disposing of the case without reaching the merits of the state law itself.[1] In June 2025 the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] agreed to hear Bost's appeal of the Seventh Circuit decision, a development [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] announced publicly and framed as a test of whether federal candidates may challenge election provisions at all.[1]

## Significance in Badlands coverage

Judicial Watch president [[tom-fitton|Tom Fitton]] called it "an injustice that the courts would deny a federal candidate the ability to challenge an election provision that could lead to illegal votes being cast and counted for two weeks AFTER Election Day," and described the grant of certiorari as a critical opportunity to uphold federal law and protect election integrity.[1] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the Court's decision to take the case as "encouraging" and as potentially producing a precedent that would reinforce a key [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaign promise on elections, writing that "Election Day should be Election Day."[1]
