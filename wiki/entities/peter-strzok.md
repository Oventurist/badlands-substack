# Peter Strzok

Peter Strzok is a former [[fbi|FBI]] agent whose name resurfaced in declassified [[intelligence-community-inspector-general|Intelligence Community Inspector General]] memos released in 2026 concerning the whistleblower complaint that triggered the [[first-impeachment-of-donald-trump|first impeachment]] of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

According to the August 2019 memos declassified at the request of [[just-the-news|Just the News]] by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], a second intelligence official identified only as "Witness 2" reviewed the Trump–[[ukraine|Ukraine]] call transcript "for situational awareness" and assisted the whistleblower, citing a "moral and patriotic duty."[1] The memo states that this official had previously worked with Strzok.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the Strzok connection as evidence that the impeachment complaint was part of a continuous operation against Trump beginning with RussiaGate in 2016, arguing that the documents implicate multiple intelligence agencies as well as both major political parties.[1]

## Privacy Act settlement (2024)

In July 2024, Strzok and former FBI lawyer [[lisa-page|Lisa Page]] reached settlements with the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] under which the government agreed to pay a combined $2 million over the department's 2018 release of text messages the two had exchanged.[2] Strzok received $1.2 million and Page $800,000 under agreements obtained by [[cnn|CNN]], finalized in court filings on Friday, July 26, 2024.[2] The pair had alleged that the department violated the Privacy Act by releasing to the media texts in which they criticized [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during the 2016 campaign.[2]

Strzok had played a senior role on special counsel [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]]'s team until he was removed after an internal investigation first surfaced the texts with Page, with whom he was having an extramarital relationship, that could be read as exhibiting political bias.[2] The release of the messages became political fodder for Trump in opposing the Russia investigation during his presidency.[2] Strzok retained other pending claims in the lawsuit relating to his termination during the Trump administration, and the Justice Department declined to comment on the settlements.[2]

## Dismissal of the First Amendment lawsuit (2025)

On Tuesday, September 23, 2025, a federal judge dismissed Strzok's long-running lawsuit claiming that he had been illegally fired and that the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] and FBI had violated his free speech and privacy rights.[3] The FBI had terminated his employment in 2018 after a review of thousands of his text messages with bureau lawyer [[lisa-page|Lisa Page]], with whom he was having an affair, showed bias against then-GOP presidential nominee [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[3]

U.S. District Judge Amy Berman Jackson, an Obama appointee, ruled that Strzok and his legal team had failed to prove how his rights were violated, while emphasizing that she was not ruling on whether the firing was the right course of action.[3] She wrote that "the sole question to be determined here, then, is whether the FBI's imposition of the sanction of termination comported with the Constitution," finding no genuine dispute of material fact precluding summary judgment for the defendants.[3] Jackson said she would keep her full opinion under seal until September 30, 2025, because some materials and depositions had been filed under seal, but indicated she did not believe continued sealing was necessary and would lift it absent objection.[3] Strzok did not immediately comment publicly and retained the right to appeal to the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the Biden Justice Department had paid Strzok $1.2 million in a settlement finalized a few months before Joe Biden left office, over his claim that his privacy was violated when his government-phone text messages were published — calling it a boondoggle paid for by the public.[3] She characterized the court loss as a reminder that Strzok's moment of accountability had not yet come, observing pointedly that he never received a pardon.[3]

## Texts about an Obama presidential daily briefing

The August 2023 Badlands article "Clowns in America," citing PJ Media, reported that text messages between Strzok and FBI lawyer [[lisa-page|Lisa Page]] show that Strzok and then-FBI Assistant Director William Priestap were concerned about sharing sensitive evidence with the departing [[barack-obama|Obama]] White House.[4] Other texts from the same day show the pair discussing apparent drama involving one of Obama's [[presidents-daily-brief|presidential daily briefings]] — the same day in April 2014 that Obama fired [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] as head of the [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]] over his opposition to the Iran nuclear deal.[4] These messages were not released by the FBI but were made available to John Solomon in September 2018, who reported that investigators were examining whether Obama's disdain for Flynn influenced FBI leadership's decision to reject its own agent's recommendation to close the Flynn probe in January 2017 and instead pursue an interview at which agents might catch him in a lie.[4] Badlands writer [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] rejected the coincidence, arguing Flynn had trapped them.[4]

## Role in the Flynn entrapment (Badlands account)

"[[the-biggest-boomerang-in-history|The Biggest Boomerang In History]]" identifies Strzok as one of the [[fbi|FBI]] agents who interviewed [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] in order to trap him, and notes that he was also friends with the judge in the Flynn case, which [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] raises as a conflict of interest.[5]

Boston Herald reporting quoted in the article describes an email exchange in which former FBI lawyer [[lisa-page|Lisa Page]] and Strzok discussed reminding Flynn that lying to the bureau is a crime, with Page recommending that Strzok "slip it in" at some point in the conversation.[5] Other released documents show agents unsure whether the purpose of the interview was to catch Flynn in a lie or to elicit a violation of the [[logan-act|Logan Act]], and mulling whether the objective was to create conditions under which he would be fired.[5]

Lange argues the declassified Strzok–Page text messages put Strzok on centre stage before a public that had never heard of him, and shifted the narrative from Trump–Russia collusion to bias within the FBI — but that the real bombshells were the emails Attorney General [[william-barr|William Barr]] released and used to exonerate Flynn.[5] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
