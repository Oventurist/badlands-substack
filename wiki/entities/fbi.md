# Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI)

The Federal Bureau of Investigation appears in Erik Carlson's *A Journey of Ever-changing Truths* as one of the two agencies alleged to have engineered the emergence of [[gangster-rap|gangster rap]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Role in the article

Carlson states that the late-1980s shift in rap music — from lyrics about positivity, unity and ending violence to lyrics about shooting cops and selling drugs — was not organic but was spurred on by the FBI and the [[cia|CIA]], acting because crime rates were down and the newly privatized prison system needed to be filled.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) He asserts that the two agencies were by that point running record labels, and that artists who did not rap about crime and killing police found no place in the genre.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

The FBI's role is thus the cultural component of a wider scheme in the essay that also includes [[prison-privatization|prison privatization]], the [[crime-bill-1994|1994 Crime Bill]], and CIA drug importation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Brennan investigation and Section 702 (2026)

In April 2026 the FBI was preparing to question several witnesses in the criminal investigation into former [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]], focusing on the 2017 [[intelligence-community-assessment|Intelligence Community Assessment]] and how it was drafted.[2] The probe, led by the US Attorney's Office in Miami, had been underway for months, had already included initial witness interviews, and centered in part on Brennan's 2023 congressional testimony; prosecutors had told Brennan he was a target of the inquiry.[2]

The FBI is also among the agencies empowered by [[section-702|Section 702]] of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act, which permits warrantless surveillance targeting foreign individuals abroad while incidentally collecting Americans' communications — a central point in the 2026 renewal fight in [[congress|Congress]].[2]

## 2020 election investigation in Fulton County

According to court documents filed on February 10, 2026, in federal court by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], the FBI is actively investigating potential improprieties in [[fulton-county|Fulton County]]'s handling of ballots from the 2020 presidential election.[3] The probe is detailed in search warrant affidavits authored by FBI Special Agent [[hugh-evans|Hugh Evans]] of the Atlanta Field Office, and focuses on alleged deficiencies in vote counting and record-keeping — missing ballot images, inconsistent totals across recounts, chain-of-custody documentation issues, duplicate ballot images, missing SHA hash files used to verify image integrity, potential tabulator tape manipulation, broken security seals, and absentee ballots lacking the creases expected from being folded into envelopes.[3]

The affidavits explain that access to the physical ballots is required to determine whether these issues stemmed from administrative errors or intentional acts, and Agent Evans emphasized that deliberate interference with election records would violate federal law even if it did not alter the outcome of Georgia's 2020 race.[3] The investigation followed a referral from [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]], a Trump-appointed director of election security and integrity.[3] In January 2026, FBI agents executed court-authorized search warrants at the Fulton County Election Hub and Operation Center, with Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] present at the president's request.[3]

## Minneapolis ICE shooting investigation (2026)

The FBI assumed control of the investigation into the January 7, 2026 fatal shooting of 37-year-old Renee Nicole Good by federal immigration agents in south Minneapolis, limiting access by state investigators.[4] Minnesota investigators claimed the FBI was blocking their access to information about the case.[4] The takeover occurred as protests escalated in Minneapolis and solidarity actions spread to other US cities.[4]

Former FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] also featured in January 2026 coverage: the court-ordered dismissal of US Attorney [[lindsey-halligan|Lindsey Halligan]] in Virginia led to the invalidation of indictments against both Comey and New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]].[4]

## Mar-a-Lago raid and internal DOJ doubts

The FBI executed the August 8, 2022 search of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]] residence, a raid personally approved by Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]].[5] Two days later, senior [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] official [[patty-stemler|Patty Stemler]] emailed National Security Division attorney [[sophia-brill|Sophia Brill]] questioning whether Trump had declassified the seized documents and noting that the president as Commander in Chief is not necessarily bound by standard declassification procedures.[5] The email, obtained by Just the News and surfaced during the Department's review of federal law enforcement actions, was presented as evidence of early internal doubts about the raid's legal footing.[5]

## Las Vegas biolab search (2026)

The FBI, working with local police, searched an east Las Vegas rental home in late January and early February 2026 and discovered the [[las-vegas-biolab|illegal biolab]] there, recovering lab equipment, test kits, vaccines, influenza samples and other biological materials from a locked garage.[5] Property manager [[ori-solomon|Ori Solomon]] was arrested in February on a federal firearms charge later dismissed, and the Bureau also served a new search warrant on the associated California laboratory tied to [[jia-bei-zhu|Jia Bei Zhu]].[5]

## Arctic Frost phone records

In late February 2026, Reuters reported that the Bureau had subpoenaed the phone records of [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] and [[susie-wiles|Susie Wiles]] during the Biden administration, while both were private citizens, as part of the [[arctic-frost|Arctic Frost]] investigation; the records were found in files labeled "Prohibited," according to statements by Patel, now FBI Director.[6] Congressional oversight disclosures indicated that hundreds of Trump-aligned individuals and groups, including sitting members of [[congress|Congress]], were targeted with subpoenas and data requests under the same investigation, drawing scrutiny over its scope and methods.[6]

## SCIF records cache (2026)

In March 2026 the FBI disclosed, in response to a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit, that it had identified as many as 1.9 million pages of records stored inside a secured room at its headquarters.[7] A Department of Justice filing stated the documents are housed in a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility where multiple safes contain between roughly 950,000 and 1.9 million pages, and that the bureau would require 10 to 12 months to digitize and review the materials before determining responsiveness.[7]

The cache reportedly includes legacy records tied largely to two closed investigations plus more recent files connected to active probes and prior Special Counsel matters, with much of the legacy material subject to review in consultation with the [[department-of-justice-office-of-inspector-general|Department of Justice Office of Inspector General]].[7] The underlying August 2025 lawsuit also seeks internal FBI communications involving Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] and former Deputy Director [[dan-bongino|Dan Bongino]] about the discovery of the records.[7] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the timeline as the bureau buying itself another year of obstruction.[7] See [[fbi-scif-records-cache|FBI SCIF records cache]].[7]

## First FBI office in Ecuador (March 2026)

In March 2026, the United States established its first FBI office in [[ecuador|Ecuador]], intended to strengthen cooperation with Ecuadorian authorities on investigations involving drug and weapons trafficking, money laundering, and the financing of terrorism.[8] Ecuador's interior minister, [[john-reimberg|John Reimberg]], said the key change was the permanent presence of FBI agents working directly with a newly formed unit inside Ecuador's national police.[8] Acting Chargé d'Affaires [[lawrence-petroni|Lawrence Petroni]] said the arrangement would expand the ability of both governments to "identify, dismantle, and bring to justice those who traffic drugs, launder money, smuggle weapons, and finance terrorism," and the US embassy called the opening "another strategic and operational milestone in security."[8]

Badlands writer Ashe in America situated the office within a network that began in 1940 as part of the Special Intelligence Service, was modernized in the 1980s through the Legal Attaché program placing FBI agents inside US embassies, and expanded sharply in the 2010s as part of the bureau's global investigative liaison network.[8] She noted the FBI coordinates with intelligence services and the State Department but does not exercise law-enforcement powers inside foreign countries, working instead through host-nation authorities — a constraint she likened to the rules governing CIA activity domestically.[8]

The bureau also featured in the March 2026 dispute over [[abc-news|ABC News]] reporting that the FBI had warned of a potential Iranian drone attack on California, which press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] demanded be retracted.[8]

## Maricopa County election investigation (2026)

In March 2026 the FBI obtained election-related records tied to [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]], Arizona, as part of a federal criminal investigation into election administration spanning 2020 through 2024.[9] The records were secured through a grand jury subpoena seeking "terabytes of data" and other materials connected to voting systems and election procedures; the subpoena's scope and the specific materials obtained were not publicly disclosed.[9]

The investigation followed concerns raised by House Administration Committee observers during the 2024 election at the third-party ballot processing facility operated by [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]], where completed and blank mail ballots were reportedly stored together, no state officials were present during handling, and some pallets of blank ballots had torn wrapping with ballots missing.[9] Rep. [[abe-hamadeh|Abe Hamadeh]] had earlier asked the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] to investigate the vendor, and the Arizona action was described as part of a broader multi-state federal inquiry.[9] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] shared the report on Truth Social with the comment "Great!!!"[9]

## ISIS plot in New York (2026)

FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] announced the Bureau's role in the March 2026 arrest of two [[isis|ISIS]] supporters charged with attempting to detonate explosive devices during a protest, saying the FBI and its partners "have no tolerance for terrorist organizations or those inspired by them" and commending NYPD officers who intervened.[9]

## April 2026: Atlantic report on Kash Patel

In April 2026 [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] published a report alleging that FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] had a serious drinking problem that left him unreachable at moments and intoxicated while on the job.[10] A [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] opinion piece disputed the account, citing attorney [[jesse-binnall|Jesse Binnall]] and arguing the magazine could face legal consequences.[10] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the story as an attempt to muddy Patel and stall the justice phase, and cited Rasmussen polling in which 64% of voters said the FBI had been weaponized and 53% called it Biden's "personal Gestapo."[10]

## Skid Row voter fraud operation (June 2026)

In June 2026 the FBI announced a raid on Los Angeles' Skid Row, with more than twenty agents investigating alleged voter fraud in the city's mayoral election; Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] amplified the operation and shared the Department of Justice's May 2026 press release on a related indictment.[11] Personnel from [[homeland-security-investigations|Homeland Security Investigations]] participated in the canvass, interviewing dozens of homeless residents.[11] The operation is documented in detail at [[skid-row-voter-fraud-investigation|Skid Row voter fraud investigation]], and coincided with a federal subpoena to [[america-votes|America Votes]] in a widening probe tied to the [[ohio-organizing-collaborative|Ohio Organizing Collaborative]].[11]

## Milwaukee 2020 election probe (2026)

In May 2026 the FBI was reportedly preparing to interview multiple [[milwaukee-police-department|Milwaukee Police Department]] officers as part of its investigation into Wisconsin's 2020 election, according to sources cited by the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel.[12] The interviews were expected to focus on officers stationed at Milwaukee's central count facility and at polling places on Election Night.[12]

Investigators were also examining an incident involving former [[milwaukee-election-commission|Milwaukee Election Commission]] Executive Director [[claire-woodall|Claire Woodall]], who briefly left a flash drive inside a voting machine.[12] Federal agents spoke with [[wisconsin-elections-commission|Wisconsin Elections Commission]] Deputy Administrator Robert Kehoe, attempted to question a top Milwaukee County election official at her home, and contacted at least two other individuals involved in administering the city's 2020 election.[12] Milwaukee's 2020 ballots still exist despite standard retention timelines, partly due to ongoing litigation brought by a New London man who sued state and local election officials.[12]

Separately, former FBI cyber official [[bryan-vorndran|Bryan A. Vorndran]] was named in the [[american-accountability-foundation|American Accountability Foundation]]'s referral over the revolving door between federal cyber enforcement and [[microsoft|Microsoft]].[12]

## February–March 2026: California drone-attack bulletin

At the end of February 2026, the FBI warned law-enforcement agencies in California that [[iran|Iran]] had aspired to conduct a surprise drone attack on the US West Coast if the United States carried out strikes against Iran.[13] The alert said intelligence indicated Iran had considered launching unmanned aerial vehicles from an unidentified vessel off the US coast against unspecified locations in California.[13]

The bulletin stated that authorities had no additional information on the timing, method, targets, or perpetrators associated with the potential attack, and it was circulated before hostilities escalated in the US–[[israel|Israel]] conflict with Iran.[13] California officials, including Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]], said there was no known imminent threat but that state authorities remained in close coordination with federal agencies.[13]

## UFC Freedom 250 plot (June 2026)

The FBI said it disrupted an alleged domestic plot to attack the [[ufc-freedom-250|UFC Freedom 250]] event held on the [[white-house|White House]] South Lawn on June 14, 2026, during [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]]'s 80th birthday weekend.[14] The bureau learned of the threat around June 10 and, working with law enforcement partners including the [[united-states-secret-service|U.S. Secret Service]], conducted a multi-state operation that produced arrests and secured the gathering without incident.[14] Investigators described a scheme centered on explosive-laden drones striking buildings near the event to trigger a mass evacuation funneling crowds toward a pre-positioned sniper team, with a planned "second wave" storming the White House gate.[14] Organizers allegedly coordinated over encrypted Signal chats involving as many as 23 individuals and discussed targeting "capitalist elites," billionaires and politicians tied to the [[american-israel-public-affairs-committee|American Israel Public Affairs Committee]].[14] At least five American citizens were in custody across multiple states by June 16 on charges including conspiracy to commit murder, and Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] credited the rapid interagency response.[14] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] questioned the speed of the case, noting that a four-day path from tip to arrests accompanied by a complete cache of digital evidence resembled a "digital magic suitcase. All wrapped up in a bow."[14]

## Puerto Rico voting equipment examination (2025)

In the spring of 2025 the FBI worked with the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]], then led by [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], to obtain and examine electronic voting machines and related data from [[puerto-rico|Puerto Rico]], an operation not disclosed publicly until Reuters reported it on February 4, 2026.[15] Anonymous officials said the joint goal had been to investigate claims that [[venezuela|Venezuela]] had hacked voting machines in the territory, though the probe produced no clear evidence of Venezuelan interference; ODNI separately described the removal of machines and data as "standard practice in forensic analysis."[15]

## Search of a Washington Post reporter's home (January 2026)

On January 14, 2026, FBI agents executed a search warrant at the Virginia home of [[washington-post|Washington Post]] reporter [[hannah-natanson|Hannah Natanson]], seizing her phone, two laptops — one personal and one issued by the Post — and a smartwatch.[16] The action formed part of a federal investigation into [[pentagon|Pentagon]] contractor [[aurelio-perez-lugones|Aurelio Perez-Lugones]], accused of unlawfully retaining classified national defense information, including allegedly taking home and printing sensitive intelligence; authorities said classified information appeared in communications between Natanson and the contractor.[16] Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] said the raid was conducted at the Pentagon's request and stressed a zero-tolerance approach to leaks, while FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] echoed the national security rationale on social media.[16] Press freedom groups and journalists widely condemned the search as "highly unusual" and "aggressive," arguing it could chill investigative journalism and undermine [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] protections.[16]

## Joe Kent leak probe and the nonprofit terror-funding task force (March 2026)

In March 2026 the FBI opened an investigation into former [[national-counterterrorism-center|National Counterterrorism Center]] Director [[joe-kent|Joe Kent]] over allegations that he improperly disclosed classified information, a probe that sources cited by Semafor said had been underway for months before his resignation.[17] FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] appeared alongside DNI [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] and [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] at the [[senate-intelligence-committee|Senate Intelligence Committee]]'s annual hearing that same week, which was dominated by Kent's departure.[17]

The same month, FBI and [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] agents began assembling a joint task force to investigate nonprofits suspected of funding [[domestic-terrorism|domestic terrorism]] or political violence, described to CBS News as a "mission control command center" to be based at the FBI with IRS Criminal Investigations agents on temporary one-year assignments.[17] The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] was separately assembling a task force to probe [[antifa|antifa]]-linked funding, and US attorney offices were directed to assign domestic terrorism coordinators.[17] The initiative followed a December memo from Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] directing federal agencies to prioritize investigations of antifa and other extremist groups and to compile intelligence on such groups for the FBI, which itself built on a September executive order from [[donald-trump|President Trump]] on domestic terrorism issued after the 2025 assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[17]

## Wisconsin 2020 election inquiry (2026)

In May 2026, Townhall reported — citing unnamed sources and the Wisconsin-based Heartland Post — that the FBI was preparing interviews with Milwaukee police officers as part of an investigation into the administration of the 2020 presidential election in Wisconsin, with as many as 30 officers potentially to be interviewed about activities at Milwaukee's [[milwaukee-central-count-facility|Central Count facility]] during election-night ballot processing.[18] The officers were characterized as potential witnesses rather than investigative targets, and investigators were reported to be examining whether police assigned to Central Count had been instructed to avoid certain areas while election operations were underway.[18] The reporting touched on the flash drive episode involving former [[milwaukee-election-commission|Milwaukee Election Commission]] director [[claire-woodall-vogg|Claire Woodall-Vogg]] and on a sworn affidavit attributed to county election commission employee [[david-j-bolter|David J. Bolter]].[18] Milwaukee County Executive [[david-crowley|David Crowley]] and Mayor [[cavalier-johnson|Cavalier Johnson]] criticized the inquiry as "an act of intimidation," and the report referenced remarks by FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] in an April Fox News interview concerning allegations tied to the 2020 election.[18]

## Denial of targeting Trump supporters (2023)

In October 2023 the FBI denied a [[newsweek|Newsweek]] report that it was targeting supporters of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] ahead of the 2024 presidential election through its [[anti-government-anti-authority-violent-extremism|anti-government or anti-authority violent extremism]] (AGAAVE) threat category.[19] "Any allegation that the FBI targets individuals solely for their political beliefs is categorically false," a spokesperson said, adding that the Bureau "investigates those who commit acts of violence or threaten violence, and we do not take action based on political belief or any First Amendment protected activity."[19] The statement described AGAAVE as one of the Bureau's top threat priorities, encompassing anarchist, militia and sovereign citizen violent extremists "and other violent extremists—some of whom are motivated by a desire to harm those with a real or perceived association with a political party or faction."[19]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the qualifier "solely" amounted to an admission that political ideology is a contributing — possibly primary — factor in the Bureau's targeting formula, and that the statement therefore practically confirmed the weaponization it purported to deny.[19]

## Durham's 2023 congressional criticism

Testifying before the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] on June 21, 2023, Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] said the FBI did not "sufficiently scrutinize information it received," nor "apply the same standards to allegations it received about the Clinton and Trump campaigns."[20] He said the Bureau "was too willing to accept and use politically funded and uncorroborated opposition research, such as the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]]," and had relied on the dossier in FISA applications knowing the material likely originated with a political opponent.[20] Durham added that it would "take time to rebuild the public's confidence in the institution" following its mishandling of the Russia collusion probe, and said Director [[james-comey|Jim Comey]] had withheld a [[john-brennan|Brennan]] referral memo from [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] agents.[20]


## 2024 election censorship coordination

Reporting in August 2024 described the FBI as ramping up censorship efforts ahead of the 2024 presidential election by increasing coordination with social media companies, following a quiet operation begun in February of that year.[21] A Department of Justice memo dated July 12, 2024 from Associate Deputy Attorney General George D. Turner stated that after the Supreme Court's decision in [[murthy-v-missouri|Murthy v. Missouri]], the Biden administration sought to censor information it deemed "foreign malign influence" (FMI).[21] Following the Court's October 2023 stay of a Fifth Circuit injunction restricting FBI collaboration with Big Tech, the DOJ "began developing a standardized approach for sharing FMI information with social media companies that continued to appropriately account for First Amendment considerations."[21] The FBI began using the standard operating procedure in "early February 2024" and was "actively sharing FMI threat information with social media companies on a continuing basis," with plans to resume regular meetings with the platforms to brief them on potential FMI threats.[21]

## Subpoenaed document production (March 2025)

In March 2025 the FBI produced hundreds of pages of subpoenaed material to the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] related to past Republican investigations, records members said they had never received from the Biden administration.[22] An FBI assistant director wrote in a letter obtained by the Washington Examiner that the tranche covered the bureau's inquiries into threats to school administrators, the pipe bombs discovered near the Capitol on January 6, 2021, and violent extremists' alleged ties to a faction of Catholicism, along with material on the FBI's engagement with social media companies about foreign interference in the 2020 election.[22] Republicans had accused former Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] of withholding the documents.[22] See [[fbi-subpoenaed-document-production-to-the-house-judiciary-committee|FBI subpoenaed document production to the House Judiciary Committee]].[22]

## Patel nomination fight (December 2024)

On December 1, 2024, incoming California Senator [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] demanded that the Senate reject [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]]'s nomination as FBI director, continuing a long personal conflict dating to Patel's 2017 role in exposing the abuse of "unmasking" authority by outgoing Obama administration officials — a practice believed to have contributed to the firing and prosecution of National Security Advisor [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]].[23] The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] noted Patel's repeated promises of extensive declassification if confirmed, and cited [[mike-davis|Mike Davis]], former Chief Counsel for the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]], as having nearly guaranteed his confirmation.[23]


## Lewiston shooting and handler allegations

Badlands commentary on the October 2023 [[lewiston-maine-mass-shooting|Lewiston, Maine mass shooting]] emphasized that the gunman, [[robert-card|Robert Card]], was known to the FBI before the attack.[24] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Card's death by suicide meant he could never be questioned about "whose voices he was hearing or what his FBI handler's instructions were," framing the episode within a broader pattern of suspicion toward bureau involvement in mass-casualty events.[24]

## Clinton annex disclosures (July 2025)

A newly declassified watchdog report released in July 2025 revealed that the FBI under the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration failed to fully examine several thumb drives containing hacked [[state-department|State Department]] data, including messages linked to Obama, during its 2016 investigation of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s private email server.[25] The drives were supplied by a confidential source and were deemed too sensitive to review fully because of concerns about privileged victim data; a draft internal memo recommended analyzing the material to "assess the national security risks" tied to the private server, a step the bureau declined to take.[25] Sen. [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] said the document "shows an extreme lack of effort and due diligence in the FBI's investigation of former Secretary Clinton's email usage and mishandling of highly classified information."[25] The material formed part of the [[clinton-annex|Clinton annex]], an appendix to the Justice Department Office of Inspector General's 2018 report, declassified at Grassley's request by Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] and Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]].[25] Badlands commentary argued the bureau under [[james-comey|James Comey]] prioritized protection over justice, citing intercepted Russian intelligence suggesting Obama used Attorney General [[loretta-lynch|Loretta Lynch]] to pressure Comey into burying the scandal.[25]

## Wei espionage case and the Comey memos (2025)

The probe of [[vadim-mikerin|Vadim Mikerin]] began under Director [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] in 2009, was supervised by then-Assistant Director [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] with U.S. Attorney [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]], and ended in late 2015 under Director [[james-comey|James Comey]].[103] The bureau declined to say whether Mueller had alerted senior [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration officials to the corruption before the deal was approved, and it took little public credit when the defendants were arrested in 2014.[103] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues the muted handling was deliberate concealment.[103]

## Source handling exposed at the Danchenko trial

The third day of the [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] trial produced a detailed picture of how the Bureau ran, paid and protected the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]]'s primary sub-source.[106] Special Agent [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]] of the Washington Field Office, a twenty-year veteran assigned to a Russia squad, was given the Danchenko assignment by the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] team without being brought onto it, and testified he had been asked to join and declined.[106] Danchenko was paid roughly $3,000 in cash at his second meeting and later $10,000 justified by useful information across at least twenty-five reports in his first year, and was retained as a confidential human source with annual reports describing his inconsistencies as "minor."[106]

Helson testified that although he was unaware of Danchenko's immunity agreement, a confidential human source is obligated to tell the truth and not to withhold or falsify information.[106] He nonetheless never received from Danchenko the emails with [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]], the [[olga-galkina|Galkina]] messages or the Facebook records, and the Crossfire Hurricane team never sought basic access to Danchenko's accounts or communications; Helson said such material would have been very useful and would have changed the investigation's course.[106] He also advised Danchenko to scrub his phone of anything leading back to [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]] without first memorialising the contents, and the Bureau worked Danchenko and Steele against each other.[106]

Internal warnings went unheeded. A member of the Bureau's human validation unit — a former army counterintelligence officer with nineteen years' service in Europe — told Helson she believed Danchenko was a GRU officer and wanted him polygraphed and closed as a source; Helson did not act and she lacked the authority to compel him.[106] Danchenko's 2009 solicitation of [[brookings-institution|Brookings Institution]] figures moving into the Obama administration for classified information he could sell was likewise never revisited.[106] The article characterised the dossier's success as depending on willing or gullible accomplices inside the FBI, the corporate media and both political parties, and noted that Helson himself had been interviewed by the Office of the Inspector General.[106]

## The Mar-a-Lago search (August 2022)

On August 8, 2022 the Bureau executed a search warrant at [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]] estate in [[palm-beach-florida|Palm Beach, Florida]], acting on a warrant authorised by Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] and approved by Magistrate Judge [[bruce-reinhart|Bruce Reinhart]] after a referral from the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives and Records Administration]].[107] [[alpha-warrior|Alpha Warrior]] described the scene as reminiscent of a thrilling action novel and the operation as an unprecedented step against a former commander-in-chief.[107]

The unsealed warrant revealed a multi-faceted investigation into potential violations of the [[espionage-act|Espionage Act]], unauthorised retention of national defense information, activities intended to destroy or conceal records so as to impede or obstruct the federal government, and illegal removal or destruction of federal records.[107] The redacted affidavit released afterwards described the National Archives' 2021 recovery attempts, boxes moving in and out of the estate, and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s hawk-like observation, supporting the Bureau's conclusion that classified documents had been deliberately concealed and removed.[107] Badlands commentary treated the [[mar-a-lago-raid|raid]] as evidence that political bias can seep into law enforcement and distort accountability.[107]

## In Lange's counterinsurgency account (December 2022)

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s December 28, 2022 essay "What's Taking So Long?" treats the FBI as one of the justice institutions whose corruption had to be exposed before reform could proceed, citing the [[counterinsurgency-guide|Counterinsurgency Guide]]'s discussion of security sector reform.[108] He noted that oversight of the bureau falls to [[congress|Congress]], which he said was preparing fresh investigations in 2023.[108] He also described the [[mar-a-lago-raid|Mar-a-Lago raid]] as an operation in which the FBI gathered evidence while [[donald-trump|Trump]], who in Lange's telling announced the raid himself, was not the target of the investigation.[108]
