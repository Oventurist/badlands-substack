# Hashim ibn Abd Manaf

Hashim, born with the name Amr, was the Bedouin warrior and Meccan leader from whom the [[hashemites|Banu Hashim]] take their name, and the grandfather of the Prophet [[muhammad|Muhammad]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Life

Over 1,500 years ago a Bedouin warrior emerged from the desert and entered the town of Mecca.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) He was a ḥanīf — one who practiced the monotheistic religion of [[abraham|Abraham]] — and was also a direct descendant of Abraham.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Many years earlier his grandfather, Qusayy ibn Kilab, had gathered many [[bedouin|Bedouin]] clans and led them to retake the Kaaba, the stone sanctuary built by Abraham and his son Ishmael, from a syndicate of godless criminals who had seized control of Mecca; the success of that mission bound the clans by oath into the tribe of [[quraysh|Quraysh]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

Like his grandfather, Hashim was a strong and just man.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) He used his scimitar not to rape or pillage but to protect the villagers of Mecca from nomadic raiders.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) He organized the Bedouin into bi-annual merchant caravans to the Mediterranean, giving them new-found wealth and enabling trade for food and supplies that carried the sedentary ḥāḍir villagers through seasonal famines.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) He developed a broth that, mixed with crumbled bread, was hearty and filling, and distributed it to weary pilgrims crossing the desert to pray at the Kaaba — a tradition of hospitality still practiced in Mecca today and adopted by all great desert sheikhs.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Name and legacy

The people came to call him "Hashim," an Arabic kunya (nickname) meaning both "he who feeds the starving" and "the pulverizer of evil."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Hashim, along with all Islamic scholars since, held up Abraham's warm hospitality toward the three angels as exemplary, urging all followers to practice the same kindness toward visitors in their home.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

Through his strength Hashim stabilized the region and civilization began to restore itself — but it was his grandson Muhammad who would forever change the world.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The author counts Hashim among the region's key historical figures who were men of honor and reason rather than the ruthless ideologues Western media portrays.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)
