# Hama

Hama is a city in central Syria, situated roughly halfway between [[aleppo|Aleppo]] to the north and the capital [[damascus|Damascus]] to the south.[1] It was one of the few cities to remain mostly under government control throughout the conflict that broke out in March 2011 following a popular uprising.[1]

## Capture by insurgents, December 2024

On Thursday, December 5, 2024, Syrian insurgents swept into Hama and government forces withdrew, dealing another major blow to President [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar Assad]] only days after the fall of much of Aleppo.[1] The weeklong offensive was led by the jihadist group [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|HTS]] together with the Syrian National Army, an umbrella of Turkish-backed Syrian militias.[1] By sunset, dozens of fighters were seen in live footage celebrating and firing into the air in Hama's Assi Square — the same square that had been the site of mass anti-government protests in the early days of the 2011 uprising, before security forces stormed it and restored control of the city.[1]

Reporting at the time indicated the offensive was likely to continue, with the insurgents setting their sights on Homs, Syria's third-largest city, roughly 40 kilometers (25 miles) south of Hama.[1] Homs was described as the gateway to Damascus, Assad's seat of power, and to the coastal region that forms his base of support.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the sequence as "a tale of two cities," noting that Hama's position between Aleppo and Damascus made its capture a strategic wedge rather than a local setback.[1] In that reading, forces characterized as [[cia|CIA]] mercenaries had taken the city between Aleppo and Damascus while Russian special forces worked to secure the capital.[1]
