# Christopher Steele

Christopher Steele is a former British intelligence officer whose opposition-research dossier, produced through [[fusion-gps|Fusion GPS]], became a central artifact of the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]]/[[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate]] investigation.[1]

## Durham records litigation

In January 2026, [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] announced a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit against the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] seeking unredacted records from former Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s probe into the origins of Crossfire Hurricane.[1] The request explicitly covers communications involving Steele, along with [[british-intelligence|British Intelligence]] head Sir [[richard-dearlove|Richard Dearlove]], U.S. intelligence officials and [[stefan-halper|Stefan Halper]] — grouping Steele among the foreign-linked figures whose contacts with American agencies remain incompletely disclosed.[1]

## July 5, 2016 first report

Badlands commentary places Steele's delivery of his first report to the FBI on July 5, 2016, the same day as the closure of the Clinton email investigation and a private Obama–Clinton meeting aboard a plane.[2]

## The dossier and the FBI corroboration offer

Evidence placed on the record by Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] at the October 2022 [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]] trial showed that the [[fbi|FBI]] had verified none of the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]] allegations it used in the FISA warrant on [[carter-page|Carter Page]].[3] With the 2016 election approaching and no corroboration in hand, the Bureau in October 2016 offered Steele payment of up to $1 million to substantiate the reporting; Steele was unable to verify a single allegation.[3] At the time the Page warrant issued, Steele had still not identified his primary sub-source to the Bureau, which supposedly did not learn until late December that the sub-source was Danchenko, a man previously investigated for suspected espionage.[3] The trial also revealed that longtime Democratic operative [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles H. "Chuck" Dolan]] was among the dossier's sources.[3]

## Testimony at the Danchenko trial

Evidence at the opening day of the 2022 trial of [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] established that Steele, a former MI6 intelligence officer, hired Danchenko in March 2016 to work for [[orbis-business-intelligence|Orbis Business Intelligence]] and to investigate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], [[paul-manafort|Paul Manafort]], [[carter-page|Carter Page]], [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] and others connected to the Trump campaign, with the aim of establishing a basis for allegations of Russian collusion; Danchenko's material was placed into the Steele or "Company" reports.[4]

FBI Supervisory Intelligence Analyst [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]] testified that the Bureau received the [[steele-dossier|dossier]] on 19 September 2016 and that he travelled with other agents to interview Steele in early October 2016, both to test the reporting and to identify its sourcing.[4] Steele was offered as much as $1,000,000 to corroborate the allegations in his own reports and could provide nothing, and at that point had not identified his primary sub-source, although he did give the FBI the names of some people to look into.[4] Millian's name surfaced through Steele in that October interview, before Danchenko was identified as the source in December.[4] The [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|FISA warrant on Carter Page]] was nevertheless taken to the surveillance court weeks after that meeting, a sequence Badlands contributor Just Human characterised as proof of institutional corruption in the Bureau's use of the reports.[4]

## The FBI's effort to turn Danchenko against Steele

Testimony on the third day of the [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]] trial disclosed that the FBI worked its own primary sub-source against Steele.[5] Handling agent [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]] confirmed under cross-examination that he had advised Danchenko to scrub his phone of anything that could lead back to Steele, and an October 2018 Office of the Inspector General interview of Helson showed that the Bureau was working Danchenko and Steele against each other — evidence the Badlands author read as the FBI having flipped, or attempted to flip, Danchenko against Steele.[5]

Steele's firm [[orbis-business-intelligence|Orbis Business Intelligence]] also figured in the documentary record. An April 29, 2016 email from Danchenko to [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]] transmitted material about the public relations firm [[kglobal|kglobal]] in connection with a possible Orbis business relationship; Dolan testified that he never met Steele or his partner Christopher Burrows and that no relationship was established.[5] The defense established that Danchenko never saw the assembled [[steele-dossier|dossier]] until BuzzFeed published it in January 2017, because Steele — not Danchenko — compiled the document, even though Danchenko claimed to have supplied 80 percent of its raw intelligence and half its analysis.[5] When the defense objected that Steele's words were coming in through Helson repeating what Danchenko had told him, the judge sustained the objection.[5]
