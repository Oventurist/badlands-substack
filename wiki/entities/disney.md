# Disney

Disney appears in Badlands Media commentary both as one of the brands most associated with American culture and as a target of consumer boycotts over "woke" content and policy.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] includes Disney on his list of the brands that most say "Americana" to him, alongside Coca-Cola, Levi's, Hershey's, [[budweiser|Budweiser]] and [[mcdonalds|McDonald's]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) He judges that, with the possible exception of McDonald's, all of these companies "have gone out of their way to upset their customer base through 'wokeness'."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

Disney is named again in Carlson's closing qualification on boycotts: he is not telling people to stop boycotting Budweiser, [[target|Target]], Disney "or any other company that is pushing wokeness, Satanism or the grooming of children," only asking that they consider whether the campaign serves the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) The underlying claim — that such companies are forced into these positions by investment-portfolio and financing pressure — is set out under [[woke-capital|woke capital]], and the possibility that the boycotts are themselves engineered comes from [[andy-frisella|Andy Frisella]] as part of the [[communist-takeover-of-america|Communist takeover]] thesis.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## Seedance 2.0 copyright dispute (2026)

In February 2026 Disney, together with [[paramount-global|Paramount]], sent cease-and-desist letters to [[bytedance|ByteDance]] alleging that the AI video-generation model [[seedance-2-0|Seedance 2.0]] reproduced Disney characters and copyrighted material without authorization.[2] Disney's attorneys wrote that "over Disney's well-publicized objections, ByteDance is hijacking Disney's characters by reproducing, distributing and creating derivative works featuring those characters," describing the conduct as a "virtual smash-and-grab of Disney's IP" that is "willful, pervasive and totally unacceptable" and warning that it was "just the tip of the iceberg."[2]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the studios were repeating the record industry's Napster-era mistake — standing on legal authorities their customers had already rejected — and that franchise democratization would outrun any court ruling.[2]

## Live-action remake slump (2025)

Disney's live-action remake of *Snow White* was a box office and critical failure in 2025, extending a string of underperforming live-action adaptations that included *The Lion King*, *Mulan*, and *The Little Mermaid*.[3] Commentary noted the trend had started strongly with *Cinderella* and *The Jungle Book* before audiences soured on the format.[3] The studio's next remake, *Lilo & Stitch*, was scheduled for release on May 23, 2025, and was regarded as an unusual choice because most prior remakes had drawn on fairy tales or Disney Renaissance films rather than a comparatively recent, comedic 2002 title.[3]

Badlands contributor Brad Cologero Getz argued that "movie execs know exactly what people want to see, but don't make these movies because Hollywood isn't about making hits" — concluding that "it's about social reform."[3]

## Florida special district fight and DEI (2023)

Since the creation of the [[reedy-creek-improvement-district|Reedy Creek Improvement District]] in 1967, Disney had enjoyed a unique self-governing status in Florida that placed Disney World outside county or city government, allowed the company to appoint the district's board, and conferred various tax exemptions.[4] After Disney's leaders publicly opposed Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]]'s [[parental-rights-in-education-act|Parental Rights in Education]] bill, DeSantis signed legislation in February 2023 abolishing that board and creating the [[central-florida-tourism-oversight-district|Central Florida Tourism Oversight District]], whose members he appointed, arguing that no other business in Florida had such a sweetheart deal.[4]

On August 1, 2023, district administrator [[glenton-gilzean|Glenton Gilzean]] announced the abolition of all [[diversity-equity-and-inclusion|diversity, equity, and inclusion]] programs at the district, citing an internal investigation and prior hiring and contracting practices said to have discriminated on the basis of gender and race.[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted the decision issued from the new oversight board rather than from Disney proper, applying only to Florida businesses under the board's purview, and that legal ambiguity remained over its reach into the company's own practices.[4] Former federal prosecutor [[michael-mcauliffe|Michael McAuliffe]] told [[newsweek|Newsweek]] the pronouncement was "a mixture of political posturing and legal implementation," with Disney likely revamping DEI initiatives to avoid using race as an explicit remedial factor.[4]

## Firing of Victoria Alonso (March 2023)

In late March 2023 Disney abruptly fired [[victoria-alonso|Victoria Alonso]], President of Physical, Post Production, VFX and Animation at [[marvel-studios|Marvel Studios]], "for cause," in a decision reached by a committee of Disney executives that studio head [[kevin-feige|Kevin Feige]] chose not to intervene in.[5] The company said the sole reason was her work on and promotion of the non-Disney Argentine film *Argentina, 1985*, which it characterized as a contract breach; Alonso's team said she had permission.[5] Her lawyers countered that she had been fired for being one of the loudest internal voices urging Disney to fight Florida's "Don't Say Gay" legislation, including publicly calling then-CEO Bob Chapek out by name before GLAAD, after which she was reportedly "benched" from press duties.[5] Alonso called Disney's stated reasons "absolutely ridiculous," said she had been "silenced," and was reported to be weighing legal action.[5]

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] tied the episode to Disney's wider troubles, citing hundreds of millions in losses on recent Marvel films, increasing net losses at the Disney+ streaming service, and the arrest of [[jonathan-majors|Jonathan Majors]] as a pattern of "escalation and acceleration hitting some of the biggest woke entertainment brands in the world."[5]

## 2023 layoffs

In April 2023 Disney began the second and largest wave of a restructuring that the company projected would eliminate 7,000 jobs, expecting to reach 4,000 cuts within a single week and to complete a final round before the start of summer.[6] A staff memo from Disney Entertainment Co-Chairmen [[alan-bergman|Alan Bergman]] and [[dana-walden|Dana Walden]] delivered the news, telling employees that "these are hard decisions and not ones we take lightly" and that the process would be conducted "with respect and compassion."[6] Workers had anticipated the round for weeks, calling it "the big one" or a "bloodbath"; the initial wave had begun on March 27.[6]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] used the layoffs to extend the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] framing beyond news outlets to Hollywood and the entertainment industry, which he argued is as influential — if not more so — on the American zeitgeist than the news media.[6] He contended that this system of control was "beginning to fray and fracture" and invoked [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s line that everything woke turns to ruin.[6]

## 2024 shareholder and legal crisis

In March 2024, Disney faced accusations that it had misled its shareholders by prioritizing a "woke political and social agenda" over profits, thereby violating its fiduciary duty.[7] A letter sent to the company on March 27 by [[america-first-legal|America First Legal]] (AFL), the organization run by former Trump senior adviser [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]], accused Disney of unlawful discrimination and of forcing political messages that produced "damage to Disney's brand, properties, and commercial reputation by management's manufactured misalignment between its woke political and social agenda and the vast majority of the Company's customers."[7] AFL claimed Disney's upper management had "intentionally manufactured misalignment between the Company and its core customers," contributing to a loss of more than $100 billion in market capitalization since February 2021.[7]

In a press release, AFL said Disney had "displayed an inexplicable disregard for its customers and shareholders, forcing radical gender-expansive, anti-White, and anti-police content on families while providing warnings about harmful content on uncontroversial content," citing examples across the company's television and film output.[7] The previous month, AFL had filed a civil rights complaint against Disney with the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission alleging a "patently illegal" hiring program that overwhelmingly favored "underrepresented" groups.[7] AFL Executive Director [[gene-hamilton|Gene Hamilton]] argued that as a publicly held corporation Disney's leadership was "gambling with–and losing–shareholder money and appears to be violating federal law in the process."[7]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the week's developments as a "Narrative Crisis Cascade" for the world's biggest entertainment company, pointing to The Hollywood Reporter's headline "[[bob-iger|Bob Iger]]'s Invincible Era Is Over," billionaire [[nelson-peltz|Nelson Peltz]]'s bid for a Disney board seat following his endorsement of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Disney's settlement with the State of Florida over its special status disputes, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s backing of Gina Carano's lawsuit against the company, and AFL's legal campaign.[7] "Disney WAS a distraction," he wrote. "Now, it's demise has become the very entertainment it used to peddle."[7]


## Jimmy Kimmel suspension and reinstatement

In September 2025, Disney-owned ABC took [[jimmy-kimmel|Jimmy Kimmel]]'s late-night program *Jimmy Kimmel Live!* off the air under pressure from the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]], station owners and conservative critics over comments Kimmel made after the killing of activist [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[8] Nearly a week later, The Walt Disney Co. announced his return for Tuesday, September 23, saying in a statement: "Last Wednesday, we made the decision to suspend production on the show to avoid further inflaming a tense situation at an emotional moment for our country. It is a decision we made because we felt some of the comments were ill-timed and thus insensitive."[8] The company said that after "thoughtful conversations with Jimmy," it had reached the decision to return the show.[8]

## September 2024 corporate layoffs

In late September 2024, Disney began a new wave of job cuts described by the company as part of a "cost-saving initiative," with roughly 300 people affected by layoffs that began mid-week and continued over following days.[9] All of the eliminated positions were based in the United States and spanned Disney's corporate operations, including legal, human resources, finance and communications; the Parks, ESPN and Disney Entertainment divisions were not affected in that round.[9] A company spokesperson said Disney "continually evaluate[s] ways to invest in our businesses and more effectively manage our resources and costs," and that a review of the cost structure for corporate-level functions had identified ways for them to operate more efficiently.[9]

The cuts followed a July 31, 2024 round at Disney Entertainment Television in which about 140 people — roughly 2% of that workforce — were let go, and a 14% staff reduction at the animation studio Pixar in May of the same year.[9] They also coincided with a second round of layoffs at [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]], which was aiming to cut 15% of its US workforce with Paramount+ bearing the brunt.[9]

## 2023 SEC filing and the "woke" pivot

In its fiscal 2023 annual filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, the Walt Disney Company acknowledged that it faces "risks relating to misalignment with public and consumer tastes and preferences for entertainment, travel, and consumer products," and that "revenues and profitability are adversely impacted" when its "entertainment offerings and products" fail to "achieve sufficient consumer acceptance."[10] The same filing described the company as "a diversified worldwide entertainment company" with Diversity, Equity and Inclusion objectives that include "building teams that reflect the life experiences of our audiences, while employing and supporting a diverse array of voices in our creative and production teams."[10] Commentators read the disclosure as a tacit admission of the maxim coined by Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]], "Go Woke. Go Broke."[10]

The filing reported fiscal 2023 revenues of $88.9 billion, a seven percent increase over fiscal 2022, even as The Hollywood Reporter noted Disney had cut television and film content spending from $29.8 billion to $27.2 billion.[10] Business Insider had reported in May 2023 that the company entered a third round of layoffs, releasing around 7,000 employees, and scrapped plans for a $900 million corporate campus in Florida.[10]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the "Get Woke, Go Broke" trend was accelerating, with Disney in the midst of a "Crisis Cascade" of box-office failures across both male- and female-dominated brands.[10] He added that a key informational trend of the culture war was the subconscious training of otherwise-disengaged Americans in sourcing primary documents and parsing corporate speak, since financial filings now proved the company knew exactly what was happening.[10]

## The 2024 boardroom "mutiny"

Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] cited Disney as the leading example of shareholders using the annual [[shareholder-proxy-voting|proxy vote]] to push back against woke corporate policy.[12] Investors attempted what he called a mutiny, seeking to seat directors focused on growing the share price and potentially to remove chief executive [[bob-iger|Bob Iger]].[12] Carlson attributed Disney's performance to its [[diversity-equity-and-inclusion|DEI]]-driven policies, noting the stock was down 15% from its level five years earlier while the S&P 500 had risen 74% over the same period.[12]

## 2023 layoffs and the stock reaction

In February 2023, Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] cited Disney's announcement that it was laying off 7,000 employees, after which its stock rose 6% in after-hours trading.[13] Carlson argued the market's positive reaction was not a sign of an improving economy but evidence that the company was cutting costs in anticipation of a rougher economic period, and he used the episode as a case study in why headline [[unemployment-statistics|unemployment statistics]] and share-price moves can diverge from real economic conditions.[13]
