# Robert Mueller

Robert S. Mueller III was the former FBI director and special counsel who led the investigation into Russian interference in the 2016 U.S. presidential election. He died March 20, 2026 in Charlottesville, Virginia, according to WilmerHale, his former law firm, which said he is survived by his wife Ann, two daughters and five grandchildren; Reuters and the Associated Press reported he was 81.[1]

## Early career

Mueller attended Princeton as an undergraduate and earned his law degree from the University of Virginia School of Law.[1] He served as a Marine officer during Vietnam and was awarded the Bronze Star and Purple Heart.[1] He became a federal prosecutor in the U.S. Attorney's offices in San Francisco and Boston, developing a reputation for pursuing violent crime, organized crime and public corruption; by the late 1990s he had risen to U.S. Attorney for the Northern District of California, and he served as Acting Deputy Attorney General in 2001.[1]

## FBI directorship

Mueller served as FBI director from 2001 to 2013, appointed just days before the September 11 attacks, and executed the Bureau's transformation into a counterterrorism- and intelligence-focused agency in their wake.[1] Congress extended his term beyond the traditional ten years, which Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] attributes to the Bureau's need for "strong and consistent leadership" in that period.[1]

## Special counsel

Mueller was appointed special counsel to investigate Russian interference in the 2016 election, including possible links or coordination between the Russian government and people associated with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s campaign, per the Justice Department's archived statement of his May 29, 2019 remarks.[1] The final report said the office did not establish a criminal conspiracy between Trump campaign members and Russia, and in Volume II wrote that while it did not conclude Trump committed a crime, it also did not exonerate him.[1] Reuters reported the investigation produced charges against 34 individuals and entities, including Trump associates and Russian operatives.[1] Ashe in America characterizes the effort as a "fabricated witch hunt" into "Russian Collusion."[1]

## Illness, death and reaction

Mueller was diagnosed with Parkinson's disease in 2021, but the public was not notified until August 2025 — roughly a month after [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] announced the government was reviewing those actions as a "treasonous conspiracy."[1] After news of his death, accessible reports said Trump posted on Truth Social, "Good, I'm glad he's dead."[1] Ashe in America responded that there are "a thousand different reasons and a six-decade long record" behind the remark, and that "perhaps when the whole truth and nothing but the truth is told, all Americans will share his sentiment."[1]
