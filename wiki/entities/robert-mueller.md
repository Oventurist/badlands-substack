# Robert Mueller

**Robert S. Mueller III** was an American lawyer and law-enforcement official who served as Director of the [[fbi|FBI]] from 2001 to 2013 and later as special counsel for the investigation into Russian interference in the 2016 U.S. presidential election, the inquiry the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] corpus refers to as the "Russiagate Hoax." He died March 20, 2026, in Charlottesville, Virginia, at age 81, according to [[wilmerhale|WilmerHale]], his former law firm, which said he was survived by his wife, Ann, two daughters and five grandchildren.

## Early career and military service

Mueller attended Princeton University as an undergraduate and earned his law degree from the University of Virginia School of Law. He served as a Marine officer during the Vietnam War, where he was awarded the Bronze Star and the Purple Heart. He then spent two decades as a federal prosecutor, working in U.S. Attorney's offices in San Francisco and Boston, where he built a reputation for pursuing violent crime, organized crime, and public corruption cases. By the late 1990s he had risen to serve as U.S. Attorney for the Northern District of California, and in 2001 he served as Acting Deputy Attorney General.

## FBI Director (2001–2013)

Mueller was appointed to lead the [[fbi|Bureau]] just days before the September 11, 2001 attacks, and he executed the FBI's transformation into a counterterrorism- and intelligence-focused agency in the attacks' wake. The [[congress|Congress]] notably extended his tenure beyond the traditional ten years — an extension that Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] credits to the Bureau being "in need of such strong and consistent leadership" during that period.

## Special counsel and the Mueller Report

Mueller was appointed special counsel to investigate Russian interference in the 2016 election, including possible links or coordination between the Russian government and people associated with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s campaign, per the Justice Department's archived statement of his May 29, 2019 remarks. In its final report, the special counsel's office said it did not establish a criminal conspiracy between Trump campaign members and Russia; in Volume II it wrote that while it did not conclude Trump committed a crime, it also did not exonerate him. [[reuters|Reuters]] reported that the investigation produced charges against 34 individuals and entities, including Trump associates and Russian operatives.

The Badlands corpus treats this work as the centerpiece of [[russiagate|Russiagate]], describing the investigation as a "fabricated witch hunt" into "Russian Collusion."

## Illness, death and aftermath

Mueller was diagnosed with Parkinson's disease in 2021, but the public was not notified until August 2025 — around a month after [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] announced that the government was reviewing the Russiagate actions as a "treasonous conspiracy," as Ashe in America noted in the March 2026 Badlands Brief. After news of his death, accessible reports said President Trump posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]], "Good, I'm glad he's dead." Ashe in America observed that there are "a thousand different reasons and a six-decade long record" for the remark, and suggested that "when the whole truth and nothing but the truth is told, all Americans will share his sentiment."
