# Heterodox Academy

Heterodox Academy is a network of academics, post-secondary professors, other faculty members and students who work to counteract what they see as a lack of viewpoint diversity on campuses across North America.[1]

## Role in the campus debate

The organization's Toronto chapter hosted a screening of the documentary *The Coddling of the American Mind*, attended by a Badlands writer who reported on the event in April 2024 amid the wave of pro-Palestinian campus encampments.[1] The film follows separate stories of college students who were, in the writer's framing, infected by ideological conformity and subsequently recovered.[1]

More significant than the film, according to the account, were the university professors present at the screening, who described in detail watching students at their institutions descend into what they viewed as a form of ideological madness.[1] A recurring example cited by every professor in attendance was a sudden spike in students presenting "accommodation letters" demanding additional time to complete assignments relative to their peers.[1] The professors connected this to what the filmmakers call the Three Great Untruths: that what doesn't kill you makes you weaker; that one should always trust one's feelings; and that life is a battle between good people and evil people.[1]

## Significance

The Badlands commentary argued that the existence and growth of Heterodox Academy reflects academics across North America putting skin in the game to fight for free thought, and predicted that such academically-minded organizations would receive an influx of new members as the scale of student ideological capture became apparent.[1] One professor was reported to have written directly to say he felt "something bigger is going on."[1] The framing situated the campus unrest as a product of sophisticated fifth-generation psychological operations targeting youth, with the awakening of faculty as the counter-movement.[1]

## Mission statement and the Toronto chapter

Heterodox Academy describes its mission as advancing "the principles of open inquiry, viewpoint diversity, and constructive disagreement to improve higher education and academic research."[2] Its Toronto chapter organised a screening of [[the-coddling-of-the-american-mind|The Coddling of the American Mind]] attended by [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]], who described it as the first Toronto-based Heterodox event and as symbolic of the local academic class finally beginning to catch up with what he argued had long been evident to the wider public.[2]

Esler situated the organisation within what he called an educational revolution, in which new universities and colleges are being developed in response to the psychological warfare that he says passes for modern post-secondary education.[2] Using an immune-system metaphor, he described the academic collective as identifying a sickness and developing the capacity to defend itself, with Heterodox representing a crystallisation of that process.[2] He attributed the academic class's slowness to recognise the problem to cognitive entrenchment, a theme of his earlier essay on Western cognition and [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]].[2]

## Faculty testimony at the Toronto screening

[[leigh-rivers|Leigh Rivers]], a [[university-of-toronto|University of Toronto]] instructor sanctioned for a [[spectrum-street-epistemology|Spectrum Street epistemology]] exercise, spoke at the screening and was the first professor present to raise the wave of student "accommodation letters"; his account was echoed by others, including a former instructor at the [[ontario-college-of-art-and-design|Ontario College of Art and Design]] who said the phenomenon had appeared earlier at art schools.[2] Another attendee was writing a master's thesis on the penetration of the same ideology into kindergarten-through-grade-12 education, work Esler argued would materially disrupt the adversary's use of academia.[2]
