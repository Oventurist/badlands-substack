# Laure Beccuau

Laure Beccuau is the Paris prosecutor who directed the French criminal case against [[pavel-durov|Pavel Durov]], the founder and chief executive of [[telegram|Telegram]], following his arrest at Le Bourget Airport on August 24, 2024.[1]

## Role in the Durov prosecution

Durov's arrest rested on a warrant issued by the French judicial police as part of a preliminary investigation into twelve suspected violations regarding crime on the Telegram platform.[1] After an initially vague press release from the prosecutors, Beccuau's office released the charges, which Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterized as sweeping and deliberately dangerous.[1]

Beccuau stated that Durov had been ordered to pay bail of five million euros — roughly $5.5 million — and was released but required to check in at a police station twice a week; she also said that if convicted, Durov could face up to ten years in prison.[1] The charges included complicity in managing an online platform to enable illegal transactions, complicity in crimes such as enabling the distribution of child sexual abuse material, drug trafficking and fraud, and refusal to cooperate with law enforcement.[1] Two further counts concerned providing cryptology services aiming to ensure confidentiality without certified declaration, and providing a cryptology tool not solely ensuring authentication or integrity monitoring without prior declaration.[1]

## Criticism

DeLarme argued that the charges rested on the theory that the operator of a social media platform can be criminally liable for the criminal acts of anybody who uses his platform — a theory that investigative journalist [[glenn-greenwald|Glenn Greenwald]] likened to "arresting AT&T executives and charging them with the crimes of allowing people who use the telephone to commit and plan criminal acts."[1] Durov's lawyer David-Olivier Kaminski responded that "Telegram is in conformity with every aspect of European norms on digital matters" and that "It is absurd to think that the head of a social network is being charged."[1] DeLarme wrote that the prosecution sought to establish a precedent under which figures such as [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] or [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] could likewise be held criminally liable for speech on their platforms.[1]
