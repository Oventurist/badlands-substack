# Andrew Jackson

Andrew Jackson was a President of the United States who opposed the [[second-bank-of-the-united-states|Second Bank of the United States]] and championed [[hard-money|hard money]] over the bankers' paper money.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

When the Second Bank was established, Jackson concluded that the bankers were overreaching and acting as a fourth branch of government run by the elite.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) He believed that if the bankers succeeded in their ultimate plan they would enslave every citizen in the country, and he therefore sought to root them out.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) Jackson also argued that bankers would extend their loans to prominent people rather than the common citizen and would use their financial power to enable further corruption.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

Jackson was, according to the article, the only U.S. President in history to completely pay off the national debt.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) The [[cabal]] nonetheless later obtained what it wanted in the form of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], and, in the article's telling, added insult to injury by placing Jackson's portrait on the $20 bill.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

Jackson was [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s favorite U.S. President because he took on the cabal bankers and won, a posture Trump is said to have adopted since taking office.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## A contrasting assessment

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] offers a sharply opposed reading of Jackson, calling the man celebrated for having "killed the bank" and "paid America's debts" a force of pure destruction.[2] In Ehret's account Jackson paid the debt by cutting all infrastructure projects and unleashing mass speculation, producing the devastating 1837 bank panic that drove the nation into discord and depression.[2] After the 2nd National Bank was killed in 1836 following a mass propaganda campaign that convinced a duped mob it was an instrument of tyranny, the only five presidents over the next six decades who made serious efforts to revive America's nationalist system all died in office — [[william-henry-harrison|Harrison]] in 1841, [[zachary-taylor|Taylor]] in 1850, [[abraham-lincoln|Lincoln]] in 1865, Garfield in 1880 and McKinley in 1901.[2]

Ehret describes Jackson as an unrepentant racist who gave enormous assistance to the slavocracy by emptying the southern lands of Cherokee in the genocidal [[trail-of-tears|Trail of Tears]] and handing the land to cotton-planting oligarchs loyal only to their profits, their "way of life" and the British Empire.[2] Jackson's 1830 Indian Removal Act belonged to what Ehret calls the southern perversion of [[manifest-destiny|Manifest Destiny]], shared with Jefferson Davis and Albert Pike, which envisioned expanded black slavery and Native Americans confined to plantations or reservations.[2] The degeneration of the American system proceeded slowly until the presidencies of Jackson and his handler Martin van Buren, after which cotton exports rose to 4 million bales per year by 1860 and the slave power grew immensely under the Kansas-Nebraska Act of 1854.[2] This story is told in detail in historian Michael Kirsch's 2012 study "How Andrew Jackson Destroyed the United States."[2]

## Consequences of the 1836 bank killing

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that Jackson's 1836 killing of the national bank, combined with the [[independent-treasury-act-of-1846|Independent Treasury Act of 1846]], produced a highly de-regulated system in which every private bank could issue currency with no federal authority.[3] By the [[american-civil-war|Civil War]] there were over 7,000 recognized bank notes issued by more than 1,496 banks, no national projects were possible, international investment was scarce, manufacturing collapsed, and the slavocracy grew in influence between the bank panic of 1837 and 1860.[3] [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]]'s 1840 campaign speeches for Harrison's Whig presidency were aimed at restoring the bank Jackson had cancelled four years earlier.[3]

## War against the banking cabal

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] writes that Jackson was elected president vowing to get rid of the central bank and did exactly that, waging war against the banking cabal — the reason, Lange suggests, that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] focused on him during his first term.[5] After dissolving the [[second-bank-of-the-united-states|Second Bank of the United States]], Jackson took credit for paying off the national debt completely in 1835; the following year a record $25 million in land sales accounted for about half of all federal receipts, leaving the U.S. Treasury with a budget surplus and no public debt at a time long before any federal income tax.[5] He achieved this using import duties (tariffs) and public land sales, freeing the government from control by foreign private bankers.[5]

In 1835 a would-be assassin tried to shoot Jackson in what was the first attempted assassination of a president in American history; the attempt stemmed from his refusal to reauthorize a charter for the Second Bank.[5] Senator Thomas Benton remarked on the "extraordinary case of two pistols in succession—so well loaded, so coolly handled" that both misfired when levelled eight feet at the president's heart, and much of the public thought Jackson's life had been saved by divine intervention.[5]

Jackson then pressed for the surplus to be distributed to state banks across the country in gold and silver rather than fiat money. The [[deposit-act-of-1836|Deposit Act of 1836]] moved precious metals from the coast to the interior, cutting New York City deposit-bank reserves from $7.2 million in September 1836 to $1.5 million in May 1837 and removing the concentration of wealth in New York.[5] His [[specie-circular|Specie Circular]], an executive order of the same year, required all public land sales over 320 acres to be purchased in specie, ending large purchases of government land with fiat money and forcing British buyers to surrender gold and silver.[5] Lange argues these measures drained the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]]'s reserves, provoked British retaliation, and culminated in the [[panic-of-1837|Panic of 1837]] — and he speculates that Trump may issue a comparable specie requirement to block foreign purchases of American land and corporations.[5]

## Product of the Burr machine

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents a sharply contrary reading, describing Jackson as a "racist puppet" raised to the presidency by the political machine of [[aaron-burr|Aaron Burr]].[6] In an 1815 letter to his son-in-law Joseph Alston, Burr laid out the earliest plan for the project, writing that "if, then, there be a man in the United States of firmness and decision, and having standing enough to afford even a hope of success, it is your duty to hold him up to public view: that man is Andrew Jackson," adding that "nothing is wanting but a respectable nomination, made before the proclamation of the Virginia caucus, and Jackson's success is inevitable."[6]

The eventual federal takeover of the Burr machine under the combined presidencies of Jackson and [[martin-van-buren|Martin Van Buren]] from 1828 to 1840 represented, in this account, a massive defeat of the Hamiltonian networks then led by [[john-quincy-adams|John Quincy Adams]], William Harrison, Matthew Carey and [[henry-clay|Henry Clay]].[6] Under Jackson and Van Buren protectionism was dismantled in favour of British free trade, speculation grew rampantly along with the southern slave power, and the south was cleansed of Cherokee under the [[trail-of-tears|Trail of Tears]] and given over to racist oligarchs.[6] The National Bank was destroyed in Jackson's last year in office, cutting the USA off from its only means of generating sovereign credit for development and producing the panic and depression of 1837, while all major infrastructure projects were cancelled under the banner of "paying the debt."[6] Ehret calls the narrative of a heroic Jackson defeating the Bank a "sick joke," arguing that those who killed [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]] and then the national bank in 1836 were the true agents of the British Empire; he cites Michael Kirsch's 2012 article "How Andrew Jackson Destroyed the United States" as documenting the story.[6]

## The 1835 repayment of the national debt

A February 2023 Badlands Media feature on [[fractional-reserve-banking|fractional reserve banking]] cited Jackson as the author of the only complete repayment of the United States national debt, achieved in 1835 after he shut down the central bank of his time.[8] The article quoted his warning that "the bold efforts the present bank has made to control the Government … are but premonitions of the fate that awaits the American people should they be deluded into a perpetuation of this institution, or the establishment of another like it."[8] It noted that Jackson's fight predated the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], which was installed in 1913.[8]

## "Jackson And No Bank!"

Ryan DeLarme's "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 6" recounts that Jackson ran his 1832 re-election campaign under the slogan "Jackson And No Bank!", a reference to his plan to return control of the American money system to the American people rather than to the profiteering of the [[rothschild-family|Rothschilds]].[9] The following year he began removing government deposits from the Rothschild-controlled [[second-bank-of-the-united-states|Second Bank of the United States]] and placing them in banks run by Democratic bankers, prompting the bankers, in DeLarme's account, to contract the money supply and cause a depression.[9] Jackson is quoted denouncing them as "a den of thieves vipers, and I intend to rout you out, and by the Eternal God, I will rout you out."[9]

On January 30 of the following year an assassin attempted to shoot Jackson, but both of the assailant's pistols misfired.[9] Jackson ultimately succeeded in expelling the central bank from America when its charter was not renewed, and DeLarme writes that it was not until 1913 that the Rothschilds established their third American central bank, the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[9] Asked before his death what he regarded as his greatest achievement, Jackson replied without hesitation, "I Killed The Bank."[9]
