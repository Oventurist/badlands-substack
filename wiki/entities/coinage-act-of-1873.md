# Coinage Act of 1873

The Coinage Act of 1873 ended the coinage of silver and placed the American economy on the gold standard for the first time. According to the article, this shift caused deflation, which increased the buying power of America's currency. The source frames the Act as the beginning of a golden age of prosperity and freedom that the Founding Fathers wanted for every US citizen.

The article claims that bankers hated the gold standard so intensely that by 1873 they began calling for the government to permanently adopt a fiat currency issued by a central bank. It asserts that the gold standard "forced the bankers into a siloed depression while citizens flourished." The source cites [[Murray Rothbard]]'s argument that the so-called "Long Depression" following the Act was "only a misunderstood recession," since real wages and production were increasing throughout the period.

The Coinage Act is presented in the article as the high-water mark of the hard-money era and the standard against which the cabal's later fiat system is judged. The source claims the gold standard spanned from 1873 to 1933 and represents the monetary system that [[Donald Trump]] and [[Andrew Jackson]] allegedly sought to restore.
