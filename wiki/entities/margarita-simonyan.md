# Margarita Simonyan

**Margarita Simonyan** is the head of the Russian state broadcaster RT and a prominent figure in Russian information operations.[1]

## Publication of the Luftwaffe recording

On Friday, March 1, 2024, Simonyan posted on her Telegram channel a recording of a phone call among senior German air force officers.[1] In the 30-minute conversation, the head of the Luftwaffe, [[ingo-gerhartz|Ingo Gerhartz]], discussed with colleagues the possibility of deploying German Taurus missiles in Ukraine, whether Ukraine could strike Russian targets without the participation of German soldiers, and how many missiles could be delivered.[1]

The publication forced German chancellor [[olaf-scholz|Olaf Scholz]] to promise a full and rapid investigation into what he called a "very serious affair," and prompted politicians in his coalition to warn that Russia may have intercepted other sensitive conversations and that government communications might no longer be adequately protected.[1]

## Alleged assassination plot (July 2023)

In July 2023, Russian media widely reported that Simonyan, as editor-in-chief of RT, had been targeted in an assassination plot uncovered and thwarted by the country's Federal Security Service ([[fsb|FSB]]).[2] According to RIA Novosti, the FSB announced on a Saturday in mid-July that members of a neo-Nazi group calling itself [[paragraph-88|Paragraph 88]] had been arrested in connection with the alleged plot, which was said to have been disrupted before any attempt was made.[2] Authorities stated that the group was also preparing to target the prominent Russian journalist and former presidential candidate [[ksenia-sobchak|Ksenia Sobchak]], and that its members had "carried out intelligence at the addresses of work and residence of Simonyan and Sobchak."[2] A report in TASS cited by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] said six people had been detained and were expected to appear in a Moscow court.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that while Russian wartime media must be treated as propaganda-laden, the claim was credible on its face: the killings of [[darya-dugina|Darya Dugina]] and war blogger [[vladlen-tatarsky|Vladlen Tatarsky]] demonstrated that assassination plots against high-profile Russian media figures were real, and neo-Nazi formations operating on behalf of the Ukrainian cause — most notably the [[azov-battalion|Azov Battalion]] — were well documented, including by the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] before the war.[2]
