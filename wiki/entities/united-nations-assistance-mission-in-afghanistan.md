# United Nations Assistance Mission in Afghanistan

The United Nations Assistance Mission in Afghanistan (UNAMA) is the [[united-nations|United Nations]] political mission responsible for coordinating international assistance in Afghanistan, and features in November 2023 oversight reporting on U.S. aid flows to the country after the 2021 collapse of the Afghan government.[1]

## Role in post-withdrawal cash shipments

A report issued by [[john-sopko|John Sopko]], the [[special-inspector-general-for-afghanistan-reconstruction|Special Inspector General for Afghanistan Reconstruction]], found that [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] had received roughly $11 billion in U.S. foreign aid since August 2021, delivered in part through cash shipments of about $80 million every 10 to 14 days.[1] Sopko said the United Nations had assured him that all of the money was "placed in designated U.N. accounts in a private bank," and was not being deposited in the central bank or provided to the [[taliban|Taliban]].[1]

UNAMA itself claimed that all of the cash shipments were "carefully monitored, audited, inspected, and vetted in accordance with U.N. financial rules and processes."[1] Sopko's report nonetheless noted that the Taliban had stolen foreign aid before and had blocked the poorest segments of the population from receiving assistance intended for them, using methods including siphoning cash from U.N. shipments, collecting royalties, and charging fees on cash shipments.[1]

## Commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] cited the continuing flow of funds — and the possibility that they reach Taliban coffers — as supporting [[devolution|Devolution]] and "Sovereign Alliance–Regions of Responsibility" interpretations of the Kabul withdrawal and the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]].[1]
