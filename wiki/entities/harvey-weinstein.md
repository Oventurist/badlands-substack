# Harvey Weinstein

Harvey Weinstein is a former American film producer whose name surfaced in court filings connected to the [[us-virgin-islands|United States Virgin Islands]] lawsuit against [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] over the bank's relationship with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[1]

## Appearance in the JPMorgan filing

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] highlighted portions of the filing describing a "production company" that allegedly facilitated Weinstein's sexual conduct, going so far as to supply him with "medications and other paraphernalia to perform sex acts."[1] DeLarme characterized the filing as containing substantially more material than mainstream coverage of the case reported, and cautioned readers about its contents.[1]

The inclusion of Weinstein in a filing nominally concerned with banking conduct reinforced the Badlands framing that elite sexual misconduct networks were institutionally supported rather than isolated, spanning finance, entertainment, and philanthropy.[1]

## Los Angeles sentencing (February 2023)

On the morning of Thursday, February 23, 2023, Weinstein was sentenced in downtown Los Angeles to 16 years in prison, a term that effectively ensured the former Hollywood mogul and convicted rapist would spend the rest of his life behind bars.[3] The sentencing came nearly two months after his Los Angeles trial concluded with a jury convicting him on three counts of rape and sexual assault.[3] Weinstein was already serving a 23-year sentence in New York following his 2020 conviction there for rape and sexual assault, and Judge [[lisa-b-lench|Lisa B. Lench]] ordered that the Los Angeles term be served consecutively after the New York sentence.[3] Addressing the court before sentence was pronounced, Weinstein made a final plea, saying, "I maintain that I'm innocent."[3]

Badlands Media contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] responded to the sentencing with the comment, "Good. Next!"[3] Contributor [[absolutetruth1776]] paired it with the same-day federal sentencing of [[r-kelly|R. Kelly]], writing that "yesterday was a bad day to be a celebrity pedophile" and predicting that further disclosures, including an anticipated Epstein client list, would bring similar outcomes for other prominent names.[3]

## 2025 retrial and mistrial

In June 2025, jurors in Weinstein's New York retrial spoke publicly about the deliberations that produced a partial verdict and, ultimately, a mistrial on one of the charges declared by the judge on Thursday, June 12.[2] Members of the 12-person panel who spoke to the New York Post denied Juror No. 1's claim that he had been "threatened" by a fellow juror — the accusation that led to the mistrial declaration — and alleged instead that a "sneaky" foreman had set a deadline for deliberations.[2] Juror No. 10, Chantan Holmes-Clayborn, said outside Manhattan Supreme Court that "everything he did was sneaky," expressing remorse at the panel's inability to reach a verdict on the final count.[2] Juror No. 7, who had earlier alerted the court to "playground" bullying among the jurors, claimed that "there were accusations being thrown (by) the jurors that they were bought out by Weinstein."[2]

## Overturning of the 2020 New York conviction (April 2024)

On Thursday, April 25, 2024, the [[new-york-court-of-appeals|New York Court of Appeals]], the state's highest court, threw out Weinstein's 2020 rape conviction, finding that the trial judge had unfairly allowed testimony against him based on allegations that were not part of the case, and ordering a new trial.[4] The ruling shocked and disappointed women who had celebrated historic gains during the [[metoo-movement|#MeToo]] era and left those who testified bracing for a retrial against the former movie mogul.[4] Weinstein, then 72, remained in prison because of his 2022 conviction in Los Angeles for another rape.[4] The New York ruling was described as reopening a painful chapter in America's reckoning with sexual misconduct by powerful figures — an era that began in 2017 with a flood of allegations against Weinstein — while #MeToo advocates noted that the decision rested on legal technicalities rather than exoneration of his behavior.[4]

Badlands Media commentary treated the decision as an object lesson in [[judicial-abuse-of-discretion|judicial abuse of discretion]] in New York, arguing that Weinstein is a powerful man with powerful friends who has been held up as proof that "no one is above the law" while remaining, in the writer's view, quietly favored by elites.[4] The commentary rejected the framing that the chapter on Hollywood's systemic sexual abuse had ever closed, pointing to the allegations against [[sean-combs|Sean "Diddy" Combs]], and drew a parallel to the New York appellate cases involving [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]].[4] It concluded that Weinstein was "a monster" who deserves prison and will remain there on the separate conviction, but that even monsters retain due process rights that should not be violated because of how much the public hates the accused.[4]
