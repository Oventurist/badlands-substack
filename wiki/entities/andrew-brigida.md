# Andrew Brigida

Andrew Brigida is the lead plaintiff in a long-running class-action lawsuit against the [[federal-aviation-administration|Federal Aviation Administration]] alleging that the agency turned away roughly 1,000 qualified would-be air traffic controllers on the basis of race in pursuit of diversity hiring targets.[1] The suit was originally filed in 2015 and remained in litigation for nearly a decade before returning to public attention in early 2025.[1]

## The lawsuit

The complaint alleges that the FAA denied employment to some 1,000 applicants for air traffic control positions because of diversity hiring goals, notwithstanding their qualifications.[1] Brigida has argued publicly that the agency's focus on diversity, equity, and inclusion in hiring made an eventual aviation accident more likely by degrading the quality and depth of the controller workforce.[1]

## Return to prominence in 2025

Details of the litigation resurfaced after the January 2025 midair collision over Washington, DC, between an [[american-airlines|American Airlines]] passenger jet and a Black Hawk helicopter, which killed 67 people in what was described as the deadliest US aviation disaster in nearly a quarter century.[1] Reporting at the time indicated that controller staffing levels were "not normal" when the collision occurred, which sharpened scrutiny of the FAA's hiring practices and of Brigida's claims.[1]

Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the case as evidence that the burden of justifying DEI hiring policy had shifted onto its defenders, arguing that competence must be valued ahead of compassion when staffing safety-critical roles.[1]
