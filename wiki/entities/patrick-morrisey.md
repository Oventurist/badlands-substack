# Patrick Morrisey

Patrick Morrisey is the Republican governor of West Virginia.[1]

## Food dye ban

On March 24, 2025, Morrisey signed into law legislation barring the sale in West Virginia of food products containing any of seven listed food dyes or two preservatives, effective January 1, 2028.[1] The same law bans schools from using the additives in meals beginning in the fall of 2025.[1]

"West Virginia ranks at the bottom of many public health metrics, which is why there's no better place to lead the Make America Healthy Again mission," Morrisey said in a statement after signing the bill.[1] He added that "by eliminating harmful chemicals from our food, we're taking steps toward improving the health of our residents and protecting our children from significant long-term health and learning challenges."[1]

The [[make-america-healthy-again|Make America Healthy Again]] movement Morrisey invoked has been championed by the Trump administration and Health and Human Services Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], a long-time critic of substances commonly added to processed foods and drinks.[1]

## Attorney general: transgender athlete litigation

As West Virginia Attorney General, Morrisey filed a lawsuit against the [[harrison-county-board-of-education|Harrison County Board of Education]] in April 2024 on behalf of five Lincoln Middle School girls who had been barred from future track meets after protesting the participation of transgender athlete [[becky-pepper-jackson|Becky Pepper-Jackson]] at an April 18 shot put competition.[2] The action prompted Morrisey to ask the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] to weigh in on transgender student-athlete bans for a second time, after a federal appeals court held that West Virginia's law barring transgender girls from girls' teams could not lawfully be applied to the eighth-grader.[2] Badlands commentary credited the attorney general for "having these girls' backs."[2]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary praised the measure as "a good start" that should be extended nationwide, noting that the United States has the highest rate of people with multiple chronic conditions in the world and an obesity rate nearly twice the OECD average, and arguing that such outcomes in the country that subsidizes the rest of the world appear intentional.[1]
