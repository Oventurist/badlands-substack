# Hannah Natanson

Hannah Natanson is a reporter for [[washington-post|The Washington Post]] whose Virginia home was searched by [[fbi|FBI]] agents on January 14, 2026, as part of a federal classified-information leak investigation.[1] Agents executing the search warrant seized her phone and two laptops — one personal and one issued by the Post — along with a smartwatch.[1]

## The January 2026 search

The search of Natanson's residence formed part of a federal investigation into a [[pentagon|Pentagon]] contractor identified in court documents as [[aurelio-perez-lugones|Aurelio Perez-Lugones]], who is accused of unlawfully retaining classified national defense information, including allegedly taking home and printing sensitive intelligence.[1] Authorities stated that classified information appeared in communications between Natanson and the contractor.[1]

Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] said the raid had been conducted at the Pentagon's request and stressed a zero-tolerance approach to leaks of classified information, asserting that the individual suspected of the leak was "behind bars."[1] FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] echoed the national security rationale in social media posts.[1]

## Reaction

Press freedom groups and working journalists widely condemned the search as "highly unusual" and "aggressive," arguing that it could chill investigative journalism and undermine [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] protections.[1] The Washington Post editorial board framed the raid as a serious threat to constitutional press protections while emphasizing the continuing importance of robust reporting despite government pressure.[1]

Commentary at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] was divided in emphasis. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that she placed First Amendment freedoms at the top of the list of rights to protect and was "genuinely and vehemently opposed" to violating the rights of journalists in the name of national security, while simultaneously arguing that the constitutional oath's phrase "all enemies, foreign and domestic" contains no carve-out for those holding press credentials — invoking the principle that "all means all."[1]
