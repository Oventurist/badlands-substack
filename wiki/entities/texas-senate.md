# Texas Senate

The Texas Senate is the upper chamber of the Texas Legislature. Under the Texas Constitution it sits as the court of impeachment for officials impeached by the Texas House of Representatives.[1]

## 2023 Paxton impeachment trial

In September 2023 the Senate sat as the court of impeachment in the trial of Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]]. On Saturday, September 16, 2023, state senators voted to acquit Paxton on all sixteen articles brought against him, including "disregard of official duty," "misapplication of public resources," "constitutional bribery," "false statements in official records," "conspiracy and attempted conspiracy," "dereliction of duty," "unfitness for office," and "abuse of public trust."[1] Four original articles had been held in abeyance and were dismissed in a separate vote taken after the acquittal, dissolving the court of impeachment and formally restoring Paxton to office.[1]

Paxton was not present for the verdict, and his wife, Senator [[angela-paxton|Angela Paxton]], was barred from voting.[1] Following the trial, the presiding member urged legislators to amend the impeachment statute in the next session to prevent a similarly flawed and politicized process from recurring.[1] Badlands commentary characterized the proceeding as a show trial and "a comedy of errors" for the House managers, led by Speaker [[dade-phelan|Dade Phelan]], and cited it as an example of the principle that when the law is weaponized, the process itself becomes the punishment.[1]

## 2023 border enforcement bills

During the third special legislative session of 2023, the Texas Senate advanced two immigration-related bills on Thursday evening, 12 October 2023.[2] Senate Bill 11, sponsored by [[brian-birdwell|Brian Birdwell]], R-Granbury, would create a new state crime for illegally entering Texas from Mexico and authorize state police to arrest violators, with a first offense punishable as a misdemeanor and repeat entry by a person with a criminal record punishable as a felony; the chamber gave initial approval by a 19-12 vote, with a final vote required before the bill could move to the Texas House.[2] Senate Bill 4, sponsored by [[pete-flores|Pete Flores]], R-Pleasanton, would raise the minimum sentence for smuggling immigrants or operating a stash house from two years to ten, and passed with bipartisan support 29-2.[2]

The session had been convened by Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]], who asked lawmakers to take up school vouchers and immigration enforcement after earlier sessions adjourned without an enforcement bill; Abbott has for years blamed the Biden administration for record Border Patrol apprehensions at the southern border.[2] Badlands commentator [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] framed the votes as Texas hitting back against the border crisis without federal help, following the chamber's earlier acquittal of [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]].[2]
