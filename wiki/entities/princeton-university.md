# Princeton University

**Princeton University** is one of the eight [[ivy-league|Ivy League]] institutions and, in the Badlands Media essay "The Ivy Dominion," is characterized as the "sanctum of central banking orthodoxy" within the alleged [[ivy-dominion|Ivy Dominion]] architecture.[1]

## Alleged role

The article describes Princeton as the node that shapes the language of monetary policy and trains the architects of institutions including the [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]], the [[bank-for-international-settlements|Bank for International Settlements]] and the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[1] It is called "the temple of central banking," producing Federal Reserve governors, IMF and BIS strategists and [[group-of-thirty|Group of Thirty]] economists; in the author's phrasing, the university "doesn't just study monetary policy — it scripts it."[1]

## Societies and pathways

Princeton's grooming apparatus is identified as the [[ivy-club|Ivy Club]], said to handle the "financial aristocracy," and the [[whig-cliosophic-society|Whig-Cliosophic Society]], described as training rhetorical masters and global NGO liaisons.[1] The Ivy Club is credited with producing generations of central bankers including former Federal Reserve Chair [[ben-bernanke|Ben Bernanke]], with onward pathways to the Group of Thirty, the [[brookings-institution|Brookings Institution]] and the IMF.[1] The article concludes that Princeton functions as "the philosophical core of financial globalization."[1]
