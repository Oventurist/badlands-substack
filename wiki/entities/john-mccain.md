## Benghazi, April 2011

"A Conflict of Interest" details McCain's April 2011 visit to [[benghazi|Benghazi]], where — as the most senior U.S. official to reach the rebel-held east — he praised the Obama administration's military action, urged NATO allies to do more, and called on every nation, "especially the United States," to recognise the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]] as "the legitimate voice of the Libyan people."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) He argued the rebels should receive "every appropriate means of assistance," including command and control support, battlefield intelligence, training and weapons.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) He described the fighters opposing [[muammar-gaddafi|Gaddafi]] as "his heroes" and asserted that "they are not Al-Qaeda" but "Libyan patriots who want to liberate their nation."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

The article argues this was a knowing falsehood: the men McCain met were leaders of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]], whose emir was [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]], and the group had been designated by the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] in October 2008 as an [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] affiliate, listed by the [[state-department|State Department]], and identified by the Combating Terrorism Center at West Point as having joined Al-Qaeda in 2007 while sending fighters to kill American soldiers in Iraq.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) As the senior member of the Senate Armed Services Committee, McCain could not plausibly have been unaware of those designations.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Feud with Donald Trump

The [[trump-mccain-feud|feud between McCain and Donald Trump]] began on June 30, 2015, two weeks after Trump announced his candidacy, when McCain distanced himself from Trump over his remarks about Mexican immigrants, telling *The Arizona Republic*, "I just disagree with his comments about the, quote, Mexicans."[6] On MSNBC he called the remarks "offensive to not only Hispanic citizenry, but other citizenry."[6] Trump responded at a July 11, 2015 Phoenix Convention Center rally, telling the crowd, "We have incompetent politicians, not only the president. I mean, right here, in your own state, you have John McCain," and afterward saying McCain was "very weak on immigration" and would lose if the right person ran against him.[6] On July 18, 2015 Trump said, "He's not a war hero. He was a war hero because he was captured. I like people who weren't captured," a remark widely expected at the time to end his campaign because of McCain's five years as a prisoner of war in Vietnam.[6] On March 19, 2019, more than six months after McCain's death, Trump said, "I was never a fan of John McCain and I never will be."[6]

Badlands commentary argues McCain opened the fight by deploying a coordinated "racist" narrative against Trump as a supposed fellow Republican, an instance of what [[qanon|Q]] called "All Assets Deployed" in drop 4645, and that his value to the enemy lay in his cultivated image as a war hero and bipartisan statesman signalled by phrases such as "bipartisanship," "comprehensive" legislation and working "across the aisle."[6] His media allies nicknamed him "Maverick" and cast him as "the voice of reason" whenever he broke with the [[republican-party|Republican Party]].[6]

## Immigration record

The Republican Party of Arizona censured McCain in 2014 for being too liberal on issues including immigration, and his Senate primary challenger [[kelli-ward|Kelli Ward]] attacked his support for "comprehensive immigration reform" as a code word for amnesty.[6] McCain was a principal architect of the 2013 [[gang-of-eight-immigration-bill|Gang of Eight immigration bill]], which according to the reporting cited by Badlands would have granted amnesty to 11.5 million illegal immigrants at a cost of trillions of dollars while requiring only that [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] submit a fencing "strategy" to Congress.[6] In August 2017 he told *The Arizona Republic* that he and [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] agreed on reviving the bill, updated for "new challenges, like opioids," even as Congress considered the Trump-backed [[raise-act|RAISE Act]].[6] McCain publicly disparaged the wall itself, saying "to think that a wall is going to stop illegal immigration or drugs is crazy," and the Badlands account casts him as the biggest obstacle to Trump's border wall.[6]

## The Obamacare repeal vote

McCain cast the decisive vote against the July 2017 [[skinny-repeal-vote|"skinny repeal"]] of the [[affordable-care-act|Affordable Care Act]], after [[susan-collins|Susan Collins]] and [[lisa-murkowski|Lisa Murkowski]] had already committed to oppose it, despite having campaigned for re-election on repeal.[6] The Badlands account attributes the vote to money and to his activation by a puppet master, and notes that he had undergone surgery following a brain cancer diagnosis shortly beforehand.[6] Q drop 52 asked where McCain obtained his surgery, "what surgery did he supposedly have," how quickly he returned to Congress and the Armed Services Committee, and what his net worth was and how it reconciled; Q drop 5 suggested the diagnosis might be cover "for a future out if needed against prosecution."[6]

Jon Kyl, [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]], then-CIA Director [[david-petraeus|David Petraeus]], former NATO ambassador Kurt Volker and the actor Ben Affleck — and the institute was created in December 2012 with an $8.7 million donation of unused funds from his 2008 presidential campaign.[6] He regularly attended its events and fundraisers, his wife [[cindy-mccain|Cindy McCain]] sat on its Human Trafficking Advisory Council, longtime allies sat on its board, and a longtime McCain fundraiser, Carly Eudy, maintained its financial records.[6] The Post awarded the claim two Pinocchios; his spokeswoman Rachael Dean said he had "intended to convey" only that he had nothing to do with soliciting the Saudi donation.[6] The Badlands account connects the arrangement to [[campaign-finance-reform|campaign finance reform]], noting that foreign contributions to campaigns are banned while donations to politicians' nonprofits are not, and to Q's question, "What is the John M INSTITUTE? Notice any patterns relating to the CF?"[6]
