# John McCain

John McCain was a U.S. Senator from Arizona, serving from 1987 until his death in 2018, and the Republican presidential nominee in 2008. In the Badlands Media article "A Conflict of Interest," McCain is portrayed as a central figure in a scheme to divert U.S. taxpayer funds to terrorist organizations under the cover of military intervention in Libya and Syria.

According to the article, McCain served as the primary political legitimizer for rebel groups in both countries, using his seniority, war-hero image, and position as ranking Republican on the Senate Foreign Relations Committee and later the Senate Armed Services Committee to grant credibility to groups the source identifies as terrorists. The article cites a 2011 Politico report describing McCain's April 2011 visit to [[benghazi|Benghazi]], where he praised the anti-Gaddafi rebels as "heroes" and urged the U.S. and NATO to recognize the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]] as Libya's legitimate government. The source claims that McCain's advocacy was designed to facilitate the flow of taxpayer money to these groups.

The article asserts that the rebels McCain supported were members of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] (LIFG), an organization designated as an [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] affiliate by the U.S. military, the U.S. Treasury, and the State Department. It quotes McCain's declaration that the fighters he met were "not Al-Qaeda" and describes this as a knowing lie, noting that the U.S. military had evidence that LIFG had officially joined Al-Qaeda in 2007 and was sending fighters to kill American soldiers in Iraq.

The source further claims that McCain traveled secretly to Syria in 2013 to meet with [[isis|ISIS]] leaders, and that [[mouza-moustafa|Mouza Moustafa]] served as the "go-between" for McCain and ISIS leader [[abu-bakr-al-baghdadi|Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]]. It asserts that [[barack-obama|President Obama]] and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] gave these operations their "full approval and blessing," and that McCain's value to [[prince-alwaleed|Prince Alwaleed]]—whom the source describes as a Saudi royal with deep ties to the Bush family and the [[cia|CIA]]—was his ability to provide political legitimacy to rebel groups that were, in the source's view, fronts for terrorist organizations.

The article also claims that McCain had previously led the [[libyan-emergency-task-force|Libyan Emergency Task Force]] before moving on to the [[syrian-emergency-task-force|Syrian Emergency Task Force]], and that both groups were "set up by our enemies to fund the very groups we are at war with." It frames McCain as a key node in what it describes as a decades-long operation to steal taxpayer money and arm U.S. enemies, with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] positioned as the outsider who ended the wars and threatened this system.
