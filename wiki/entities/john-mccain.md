# John McCain

Senator John McCain is named in the article as one of the three American figures — with [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] — who promoted the overthrow of Muammar Gaddafi and who installed the successor Libyan government.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The article states that Obama, Clinton and McCain promised that removing Gaddafi would bring peace and freedom to the Libyan people, and that the opposite followed.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) It asserts that the government they installed was filled with Al Qaeda terrorists whom the Obama administration funded and supplied with weapons, and that all three knew both the new government and the terrorists who killed the four Americans in [[benghazi|Benghazi]] were affiliated with Al Qaeda.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

McCain is further implicated in the smuggling of [[shoulder-fired-missiles|shoulder-fired missiles]] out of Libya to arm [[isis|ISIS]] against Bashar al-Assad, and in the weapons-sales relationship in which Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|Christopher Stevens]] served as liaison.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Benghazi, April 2011

"A Conflict of Interest" details McCain's April 2011 visit to [[benghazi|Benghazi]], where — as the most senior U.S. official to reach the rebel-held east — he praised the Obama administration's military action, urged NATO allies to do more, and called on every nation, "especially the United States," to recognise the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]] as "the legitimate voice of the Libyan people."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) He argued the rebels should receive "every appropriate means of assistance," including command and control support, battlefield intelligence, training and weapons.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) He described the fighters opposing [[muammar-gaddafi|Gaddafi]] as "his heroes" and asserted that "they are not Al-Qaeda" but "Libyan patriots who want to liberate their nation."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

The article argues this was a knowing falsehood: the men McCain met were leaders of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]], whose emir was [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]], and the group had been designated by the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] in October 2008 as an [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] affiliate, listed by the [[state-department|State Department]], and identified by the Combating Terrorism Center at West Point as having joined Al-Qaeda in 2007 while sending fighters to kill American soldiers in Iraq.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) As the senior member of the Senate Armed Services Committee, McCain could not plausibly have been unaware of those designations.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Syria, 2013, and the lobbying network

Two years later McCain travelled secretly into Syria, a country with which the United States had no diplomatic relations, escorted by the lobbyist [[mouaz-moustafa|Mouaz Moustafa]], to meet rebel leaders the article identifies as ISIS.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) Moustafa, who ran both the [[libyan-emergency-task-force|Libyan Emergency Task Force]] and the [[syrian-emergency-task-force|Syrian Emergency Task Force]], acted as the go-between connecting McCain to ISIS leader Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article characterises McCain's recurring function as the supply of legitimacy: he used his seniority in [[congress|Congress]] and his "war hero" image to make hardline Islamist factions acceptable in Washington, which it says was his value to [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed]], who funded the McCain Institute.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) Consortium News is quoted describing McCain's Senate office as "a clubhouse for arms industry lobbyists and neocon operatives."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## "No-name" and the sixteen year plan

The closing section of "Bad Actors" names McCain as the next major figure in Joe Lange's series, describing him as "a traitor who portrayed himself as a war hero and a patriot" and as one of the biggest traitors in the war.[3] Lange notes that Q gave McCain a specific moniker — "No-name" — for a reason, and that the Q drop in question mentioned him alongside Nancy Pelosi because he too had taken a secret trip to a foreign country that would reveal his part in the [[sixteen-year-plan|sixteen year plan]].[3] He states that McCain was "removed from the chessboard for a reason" and promises to tie him to Libya and "The Sum of All Fears" in a subsequent article.[3]

## Legacy of the "McCain wing" (2026)

Reporting on the July 2026 death of Sen. [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] described the traditional "McCain wing" of the Republican Party — the interventionist faction that had pushed assertive U.S. engagement since McCain's death in 2018 — as further eroded by Graham's passing and by [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]]'s health-related absence.[4] [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] framed the moment as a "mortal blow" to that wing, which he labeled the "America Last" party.[4]

## Campaign against the UFC

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] recounted that McCain attempted to shut the [[ufc|UFC]] down in the early 2000s, successfully pressuring New York State into waging a campaign against the league.[5] The episode is contrasted in Badlands commentary with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s decision to offer the promotion a free venue and transportation to save a last-minute event whose cancellation could have bankrupted the company and the emerging sport with it.[5]
