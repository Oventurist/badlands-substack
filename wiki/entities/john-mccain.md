# John McCain

John McCain was a United States Senator from Arizona and, in the account given by the Badlands Media article "A Conflict of Interest," a central political enabler of the flow of American taxpayer money to armed groups that the United States was simultaneously fighting.[1]

## Libya, 2011

In April 2011 McCain traveled to [[benghazi|Benghazi]], the stronghold of the Libyan rebellion against [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]], becoming the most senior U.S. official to visit the rebel-held eastern city.[1] Politico reported that McCain praised the Obama administration's military action, urged NATO allies to do more, and called on every nation — "especially the United States" — to recognize the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]] as "the legitimate voice of the Libyan people."[1] He called for "every appropriate means of assistance," including command and control support, battlefield intelligence, training and weapons.[1] The Guardian reported that McCain described the rebels fighting Gaddafi's troops as "his heroes."[1]

The article argues that McCain's push for recognition of the TNC had a financial motive: formal recognition would make it far easier for taxpayer money to flow directly to the rebel groups.[1]

## "They are not Al-Qaeda"

Activist Post quoted McCain saying of the Benghazi fighters, "I have met with these brave fighters, and they are not Al-Qaeda. To the contrary: They are Libyan patriots who want to liberate their nation."[1] The article contends this was a knowing falsehood, since the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] — the main insurgent outfit McCain met with — had been designated a Specially Designated Global Terrorist in 2001, sanctioned by the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Treasury]] in 2008 as an al-Qaeda affiliate, identified by the Combating Terrorism Center at West Point as having joined [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] in 2007, and designated by the [[us-state-department|State Department]] as well.[1] As the ranking Republican on the Senate Armed Services Committee, the article argues, McCain could not plausibly have been unaware.[1]

## Syria and ISIS

Two years after Libya, McCain made a secret 2013 trip into Syria to meet rebel leaders opposing Bashar al-Assad — men the article identifies as [[isis|ISIS]] terrorists — chaperoned by lobbyist [[mouaz-moustafa|Mouaz Moustafa]] of the [[syrian-emergency-task-force|Syrian Emergency Task Force]].[1] Moustafa had previously run the [[libyan-emergency-task-force|Libyan Emergency Task Force]], making Libya and Syria "the same playbook" run by the same pair.[1]

## Value to the network

The article frames McCain's principal utility to the network around [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] as the conferral of legitimacy: McCain used his seniority, Senate rank and "war hero" image to make hardline Islamist factions acceptable to Washington, and Alwaleed in turn funded the McCain Institute.[1] Consortium News described McCain's Senate office as functioning "as a clubhouse for arms industry lobbyists and neocon operatives."[1] The article calls him "the worst kind of traitor."[1]
