# Elizabeth O'Bagy

Elizabeth O'Bagy is an American Syria analyst who, in 2013, served simultaneously as a researcher at the [[institute-for-the-study-of-war|Institute for the Study of War]] and as Political Director of the [[syrian-emergency-task-force|Syrian Emergency Task Force]] (SETF), the Washington, D.C. advocacy group that lobbied Congress on behalf of the Syrian rebels.[1] In the Badlands Media feature "Transparency and Prosecution," author [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presents O'Bagy as a deployed "asset" whose sudden elevation to expert status was engineered to manufacture congressional support for intervention in Syria.[1]

## Role in the 2013 Syria intervention push

O'Bagy accompanied the May 2013 delegation into Syria organized by SETF executive director [[mouaz-moustafa|Mouaz Moustafa]], and appears in the background of photographs from Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]]'s meeting with self-described Syrian rebel leaders.[1] On August 30, 2013 the Wall Street Journal published an op-ed under her byline arguing that moderate elements dominated the Syrian opposition; McCain cited that op-ed during a Senate hearing as evidence supporting military action and funding for the rebels, referring to her as "Doctor" O'Bagy.[1] Secretary of State [[john-kerry|John Kerry]] cited the same piece in congressional testimony.[1]

The article argues that the claim advanced by O'Bagy, McCain and Kerry — that "moderate" rebel fighters were cleanly separated from terrorist extremists and did not commingle — was false, pointing to photographs showing McCain meeting both the ostensibly moderate General Salem Idris and figures the author identifies as [[isis|ISIS]] leaders in the same room.[1]

## Credentials controversy

Almost nobody had heard of O'Bagy before 2013, and she was abruptly presented across BBC, CNN, Fox and PBS as an expert analyst on Syria at the age of twenty-six.[1] Days after the Wall Street Journal op-ed was cited in Congress, the Institute for the Study of War announced that, contrary to her representations, she did not in fact hold a Ph.D. from [[georgetown-university|Georgetown University]], and terminated her employment effective immediately.[1] She had received a Master of Arts in Arab Studies from Georgetown in May of that year and was said to be working on a dissertation.[1] CNN stated she would not appear again as an analyst on its networks.[1] Lange characterizes the timing of the exposure as a "ghosts in the machine" intervention by the military that helped derail the congressional effort to commit U.S. forces against [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]].[1]

## Alleged Alwaleed connection

Citing the Shoebat Foundation, the article notes that SETF's press release announcing her hiring emphasized her Master's in Arab Studies from Georgetown, an institution described as heavily influenced by Saudi [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] following his $20 million 2005 gift to what became the Prince Alwaleed Bin Talal Center for Muslim-Christian Understanding.[1] The center's director, John Esposito, is listed on the faculty of the department in which O'Bagy studied.[1] On this basis Lange concludes that both O'Bagy and McCain functioned as Alwaleed puppets pushing for war in Syria while lobbying for money that would ultimately fund ISIS.[1]
