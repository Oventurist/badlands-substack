# Jordan Fuchs

Jordan Fuchs is a Georgia state election official who served as deputy secretary of state under Secretary of State [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]] and who figures in disputes over the reporting of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s post-2020 election telephone contacts with Georgia officials.[1]

## Role in the Georgia call reporting dispute

In February 2021, Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] opened an investigation into Trump based on his call to Raffensperger following the 2020 election — a call that, according to commentary published by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] and aggregated in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], was misrepresented to [[washington-post|The Washington Post]].[1] The Post later corrected its report after determining it had been given inaccurate information, but by then, the account holds, the damage was done.[1]

In this telling, Willis's investigation into Trump rested entirely on a falsehood attributed to Fuchs and Raffensperger, and Willis and her team were aware that Fuchs had lied when they nonetheless indicted Trump and eighteen associates in August 2023 in the case that became [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|State of Georgia v. Trump]].[1]

## Statements on election security litigation (2023)

As deputy secretary of state, Fuchs defended Georgia's electronic voting systems against the plaintiffs in [[curling-v-raffensperger|*Curling et al v. Raffensperger et al*]], the suit the state had been fighting since 2017.[2] Speaking to the [[atlanta-journal-constitution|Atlanta Journal-Constitution]] as the case was set for a January 2024 trial, Fuchs said the plaintiffs had never offered an election security solution that did not include hand-marked paper ballots, and declared: "We don't negotiate with election deniers. If they have an idea that wouldn't take Georgia back to the days of hanging chads and stuffed ballot boxes, they should offer it."[2]

Critics writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] responded that hanging chads were the 2000 Bush v. Gore controversy that produced the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]] and the modern black-box election industry, and asked whether Fuchs feared hand-marked paper ballots or was invested in the status quo.[2] Her remarks came as the state declined to patch vulnerabilities identified by [[j-alex-halderman|J. Alex Halderman]] ahead of the 2024 election and amid the [[2023-georgia-municipal-election-discrepancies|2023 municipal election discrepancies]] in [[fulton-county|Fulton]] and [[spalding-county|Spalding]] counties.[2]

## Assessment

The allegations concerning Fuchs are presented here as they appear in the source material, which is commentary from alternative-media outlets rather than adjudicated findings; the underlying correction by The Washington Post is a matter of record, while the characterization of intent is contested.[1]
