# MSNBC

MSNBC is an American cable news network presented by Badlands Media as the archetype of partisan mainstream punditry aimed at establishment Democratic audiences.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

Like [[cnn|CNN]], MSNBC was hemorrhaging viewers before the rise of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and both networks were revived temporarily as previously disinterested liberals became "maniacally obsessed" with politics during his presidency.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Badlands describes this as a sugar high that would inevitably wane.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

The article singles out the programs of Joy Reid and Jen Psaki as examples of content it characterizes as substance-free affirmations of liberal cult-of-personality talking points, inviting readers to "try to sit through five minutes" of either.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The emphasis of such programming, Badlands argues, is on highlighting differences and asserting why "our team" is better than "their team" — the same criticism it levels at most Fox News hosts.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Coverage of Trump's Colorado disqualification

MSNBC's morning program *Morning Joe* provided the venue for one of the most widely criticized reactions to the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]]'s December 2023 decision disqualifying [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the 2024 ballot.[2] [[new-york-times|New York Times]] editorial board member [[mara-gay|Mara Gay]], asked on the program how she would answer those questioning the constitutional basis for using the [[fourteenth-amendment|14th Amendment]] to bar Trump from ballots, replied: "Why are you standing with confederates who betrayed this country?"[2] She argued that objectors embodied "the spirit of those confederates" rather than the Americans who reunified the country after the Civil War and amended the Constitution to keep ex-Confederates out of office.[2]

Law professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] characterized the exchange as emblematic of "unhinged coverage" of the ruling, noting that the underlying 14th Amendment theory had previously been rejected by jurists in various states, including many Democrats.[2] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the segment as escalating state-aligned rhetoric that recast January 6 as a civil war, and predicted such hyperbole would intensify through 2024 as establishment efforts to stop Trump faltered.[2]

## Comcast cable spinoff (2024)

In November 2024, [[comcast|Comcast]] announced it would proceed with a spinoff of its [[nbcuniversal|NBCUniversal]] cable television networks, placing MSNBC alongside [[cnbc|CNBC]], USA, Oxygen, E!, Syfy and the Golf Channel into a separate company.[3] The divested assets generated roughly $7 billion in revenue in the twelve months ended September 30, while Comcast retained Bravo, the Peacock streaming service and the NBC broadcast network on the expectation that broadcast, sports, movies and theme parks were better positioned for growth.[3] Commentary tied the timing to expected pressure on pharmaceutical advertising, a major source of cable revenue.[3]

## 2023 call for a post-9/11 style crackdown

In July 2023, MSNBC commentator [[paul-rieckhoff|Paul Rieckhoff]] — founder of [[iraq-and-afghanistan-veterans-of-america|Iraq and Afghanistan Veterans of America]] and Righteous Media — used a network appearance to call for a post-9/11 style government crackdown as a means of winning "a battle for hearts and minds" among Americans he framed as at risk of becoming overly patriotic.[4] He compared that prospect to the "number one threat" after 9/11 and urged programs to draw fence-sitters into community organizing and non-profits and away from [[patriot-front|Patriot Front]] and the [[oath-keepers|Oath Keepers]].[4]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the segment as an establishment call for another [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]] aimed at political speech, and argued that such demands reflected a narrative battle the establishment had already lost.[4]

## DOGE opinion piece (December 2024)

In December 2024, MSNBC published an opinion column arguing that [[doge|DOGE]] had exposed the hypocrisy of wealthy Americans who complain that the federal government is full of unelected decision-makers operating without authorization from Congress and the Constitution, while seeking such positions themselves.[5] The piece cited billionaire investor [[marc-andreessen|Marc Andreessen]], reported by the Financial Times to be helping identify and interview candidates for President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s advisory commission on cutting government spending, despite his recent complaints about the [[consumer-financial-protection-bureau|Consumer Financial Protection Bureau]].[5]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded that the outlet had gone through "a full character arc" demonstrating zero remaining self-awareness, since the writer spent more than 800 words casting [[elon-musk|Musk]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Ramaswamy]] as hypocrites before conceding in the 18th paragraph that "there are plenty of problems with DOGE, but how it's set up is not one of them," and that "presidents have long had the authority to set up advisory committees overseen by outsiders not confirmed by Congress."[5] She argued the writer knew most readers would never reach that paragraph, and that the outlet's real objections were about power, MSNBC having been among the regime's most dedicated participants in the decades-long capture of institutions.[5]
