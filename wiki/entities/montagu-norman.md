# Montagu Norman

Montagu Norman was Governor of the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]] from 1920 to 1944 and, in Matthew Ehret's account, the single most important central-banking figure behind the financing of European fascism.[1]

## Positions and networks

Norman led the Anglo-German Fellowship Trust and served as the controller of Germany's [[hjalmar-schacht|Hjalmar Schacht]], who was Reichsbank president from 1923 to 1930 and Minister of Economy from 1934 to 1937.[1] He was also the primary controller of the [[bank-for-international-settlements|Bank for International Settlements]] from its creation in 1930 throughout the entirety of the Second World War.[1]

His family bank, Brown Brothers, merged in 1931 with Harriman, Bush and Co. to form [[brown-brothers-harriman|Brown Brothers Harriman]], the institution through which [[prescott-bush|Prescott Bush]] and the Harrimans financed the Nazi party.[1]

## Assessment by contemporaries

Dutch central banker Johan Beyen described Norman's dominance of the BIS in these terms: "Norman's prestige was overwhelming. As the apostle of central bank cooperation, he made the central banker into a kind of arch-priest of monetary religion. The BIS was, in fact, his creation."[1]
