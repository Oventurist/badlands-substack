# Sonya Jaquez Lewis

Sonya Jaquez Lewis is a former Colorado state senator and retired pharmacist whose felony sentencing in early 2026 became the comparative benchmark in the debate over clemency for [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]].[1]

## Conviction and sentence

Jaquez Lewis was convicted of four felonies — three counts of forgery and one count of attempting to influence a public official — and was sentenced in late February 2026 to probation and community service, including 150 hours of public service and a fine.[1] Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]], who described her as a personal friend of many years, said she had "made a horrible mistake" but expressed relief that a 68-year-old retired pharmacist would not go to prison.[1]

## Sentencing disparity argument

Polis publicly observed that Jaquez Lewis had been convicted of the same felony charge — attempting to influence a public official — as Peters, who as a non-violent first-time offender received a nine-year sentence.[1] He cited the disparity as context for extending Colorado's clemency application deadline to April 3, 2026.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] analyzed the two prosecutions in detail, writing that in Jaquez Lewis's case prosecutors used a proceeding-based theory for the influence charge and minimized multiplicity by treating the committee as a single official, whereas in Peters' case they used a recipient-based theory and maximized multiplicity by treating each communication as a separate statutory violation.[1] Both approaches, she noted, are legally permissible under existing Colorado doctrine, making the exercise of prosecutorial discretion the operative question.[1] She argued that beyond claimed differences in repentance, the salient distinction was partisan: Jaquez Lewis was a Democrat whose alleged conduct served to keep her seat within a system controlled by Democrats, while Peters was a Republican whose alleged conduct served an investigation into how officials attain power.[1]
