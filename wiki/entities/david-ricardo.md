# David Ricardo

David Ricardo is described in Badlands Media reporting as one of the [[british-east-india-company|British East India Company]] economists who advanced the pseudo-scientific [[law-of-diminishing-returns|law of diminishing returns]], a doctrine refuted by [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]]'s economic advisor [[henry-c-carey|Henry C. Carey]].[1]

In *Unity of Law* (1872), Carey wrote that after [[thomas-malthus|Malthus]] assumed cultivation had always begun on the richest soils and that rising population forced recourse to inferior soils with diminishing returns to labor, "Mr. Ricardo followed the theory up by assuming that constantly diminishing production had been, and must be, attended with power on the landlord's part to take to himself a constantly increasing share, of the diminished product, leaving to the poor laborer a steadily diminishing share of a constantly declining quantity".[1] Carey noted that the growing inequality of the people of England was thereby presented as the result of "a great law established by the Creator for government of the human race."[1]

Ricardo's rent theory, together with the writings of [[john-stuart-mill|J. S. Mill]], is treated as part of the closed-system imperial economics that later underpinned Marxist class-struggle theory and the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]]'s [[limits-to-growth|limits-to-growth]] modelling.[1]
