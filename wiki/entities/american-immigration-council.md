# American Immigration Council

The American Immigration Council (AIC) is a U.S. immigration advocacy organization whose cost estimates for mass deportation became a focal point of debate ahead of the second Trump administration.[1]

## Mass deportation cost estimates

In testimony before the U.S. Senate Judiciary Committee in late December 2024, AIC senior fellow [[aaron-reichlin-melnick|Aaron Reichlin-Melnick]] stated that mass deportations would cost "at a minimum" $316 billion.[1] The council estimated an annual cost of $88 billion to deport one million illegal aliens each year, broken down as $7 billion for arrests, $66 billion for detentions, $12.6 billion for legal processing, and $2.1 billion for transporting deportees out of the country — roughly $88,000 per deportee, which the council characterized as a "highly conservative estimate," concluding a total cost of $967.9 billion over more than a decade.[1]

Critics countered that per-deportee costs in recent years were less than one-sixth of the AIC figure: comparing Enforcement and Removal Operations budgets from 2015 to 2022 with the Immigration Enforcement Actions Annual Flow Reports yields per capita deportation costs in 2024 dollars averaging $12,124, ranging from $9,767 under Obama to $11,637 under Trump and $15,499 under Biden.[1] Advocacy organizations such as the AIC were accused of producing shock-value numbers.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands commentary argued that mass deportations would cost Americans less than continuing to subsidize the lives of tens of millions of illegal migrants, and that for many immigration-focused NGOs "their existence depends upon the continuation of the crisis."[1] Reichlin-Melnick's testimony was said to illuminate how NGOs estimate resource needs and justify costs — for example, the AIC assumed each detention facility holds 500 beds, requiring 83 new facilities at $35.91 million each, or about $3 billion, a fraction of the $66 billion detention figure.[1] Writers suggested that any federal funding application seeking $66 billion for a need actually costing $3 billion would clearly be fraud, and proposed that a [[department-of-government-efficiency|DOGE]] team review government funding of immigration NGOs and refer cases to the [[pam-bondi|Bondi]] Justice Department for prosecution and restitution.[1]
