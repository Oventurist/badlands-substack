# Stacey Plaskett

Stacey E. Plaskett is the Democratic Delegate to the U.S. House of Representatives from the U.S. Virgin Islands and served as Ranking Member of the [[house-weaponization-committee|House Select Subcommittee on the Weaponization of the Federal Government]].[1]

## March 2023 weaponization hearing

At the subcommittee's March 9, 2023 hearing featuring [[matt-taibbi|Matt Taibbi]] and [[michael-shellenberger|Michael Shellenberger]] as witnesses, Plaskett opened by labeling the two writers "so-called journalists" and describing them as dangerous and a "threat" to former Twitter employees.[1] She contended that Republicans had invited "two of Elon Musk's 'public scribes'" in order "to release cherry-picked out-of-context emails and screenshots designed to promote his chosen narrative — [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s chosen narrative — that is now being parroted by the Republicans" for political advantage.[1]

Taibbi rejected the framing on the record, citing his National Magazine Award, the I.F. Stone Award for Independent Journalism, and ten published books.[1] The exchange, clipped and shared by commentator Greg Price, became the most widely circulated moment of the hearing and was read in independent media as evidence that the [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship-industrial complex]]'s defenders were resorting to credential-policing rather than rebutting the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] substance.[1]

## 2024 Republican caucuses in her district

Plaskett's district served as the setting for the [[2024-us-virgin-islands-republican-caucuses|2024 US Virgin Islands Republican caucuses]] on February 8, 2024, in which 182 voters cast ballots for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], giving him 74 percent of the vote against 64 votes and 26 percent for [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]].[2] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] invoked Plaskett's name sarcastically when criticizing the territory's use of [[ranked-choice-voting|ranked-choice voting]], writing "you too can have representation like Stacey Plaskett."[2]

## January 2025 speaker-election protest

At the opening of the 119th Congress on Friday, January 3, 2025, Plaskett protested from the House floor over the non-voting status of delegates from the U.S. Virgin Islands and other territories during the election of the [[mike-johnson|speaker of the House]].[3] Her microphone was cut off for failing to follow House rules, and the House clerk answered her demand to know why territorial delegates had not been called by stating flatly that "delegates-elect and the resident commissioner-elect are not qualified to vote. Representatives-elect are the only individuals qualified to vote in the election of a speaker."[3] Coverage characterized the episode as a bid for viral attention that fell flat.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the incident as the other main source of excitement at the opening of the 119th Congress and cast Plaskett as a territorial delegate confused about basic American civics.[3] She noted that the stunt may cost Plaskett her limited-power seat, since her own constituents disagreed with it: nearly 82 percent of USVI voters want the islands to remain a territory, meaning Plaskett was arguing against the expressed will of the voters about their fundamental form of government.[3] The Brief predicted more such performances from "out-of-power uniparty loyalists" over the following four years.[3]

## Failed censure resolution over Epstein texts (November 2025)

On the evening of Tuesday, November 18, 2025, the [[us-house-of-representatives|House]] rejected a [[house-freedom-caucus|House Freedom Caucus]]-backed resolution that would have formally censured Plaskett and removed her from the [[house-intelligence-committee|House Intelligence Committee]] over recently released documents showing she had consulted with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] by text during a 2019 hearing.[4] The vote was 209-214-3, with three Republicans — Don Bacon of Nebraska, Lance Gooden of Texas and David Joyce of Ohio — joining every Democrat in voting no and three Republicans voting present.[4] The resolution was introduced by Rep. [[ralph-norman|Ralph Norman]] of South Carolina, and had it passed, Democrats were expected to immediately bring a retaliatory censure resolution against Rep. [[cory-mills|Cory Mills]] of Florida, whose ex-girlfriend had been granted a restraining order against him the previous month.[4] The rejection came just hours after the House passed, and the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] approved, the bill directing the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] to release its [[epstein-files|Epstein files]].[4]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Plaskett allegedly conspired with Epstein in the middle of a committee hearing in 2019 and that, despite all the congressional "wailing about Epstein files," Congress decided to do nothing about it — as if allowing a prolific pedophile to influence congressional work "isn't an actual concern for the Congress."[4] She also recalled an earlier viral clip in which Plaskett's aide appeared to mouth an error before Plaskett repeated it and then seemed to correct her, and predicted that the promised Epstein files release would end in "a lot of bruised hips and egos following the imminent rug."[4]
