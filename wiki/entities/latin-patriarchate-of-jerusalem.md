# Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem

The Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem is the Roman Catholic diocesan structure responsible for Latin-rite Catholics in Israel, the Palestinian territories, Jordan and Cyprus. It is headed by the Latin Patriarch, an office held in 2026 by Cardinal [[pierbattista-pizzaballa|Pierbattista Pizzaballa]], and works alongside the Custody of the Holy Land, the Franciscan body charged with guardianship of the Christian shrines. Together these institutions hold recognized rights of worship at the [[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre|Church of the Holy Sepulchre]] under the centuries-old Status Quo arrangement governing the shared administration of the site.

## March 2026 dispute with Israeli authorities

On March 29, 2026, Israeli police prevented the Patriarch and other senior Catholic clergy from entering the Holy Sepulchre for a private [[palm-sunday|Palm Sunday]] Mass. The Patriarchate issued a public rebuke, characterizing the police action as "a manifestly unreasonable and grossly disproportionate measure" and stating that it impeded freedom of worship. Church officials said the exclusion of the Patriarchate's leadership from the site on Palm Sunday was unprecedented in centuries of the institution's history.

Israeli officials attributed the closure to security conditions arising from the war with [[iran|Iran]] — missile threats, insufficient shelter capacity and constrained emergency access in the Old City. Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] subsequently ordered that access be restored after international criticism, including from Italian Prime Minister [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] and U.S. Ambassador [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]].

## Broader pattern of friction

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the Palm Sunday incident as the latest stage in an escalating conflict between the Israeli state and the Christian churches of Jerusalem: the imposition of a property tax on church properties for the first time in the institutions' history, the freezing of church bank accounts when the churches refused to pay, progressive restrictions on access to the Holy Sepulchre, and finally the barring of clergy outright. He contrasted this with the centuries of Muslim rule over Jerusalem, during which, he argued, no comparable tax or exclusion was attempted. The Patriarchate's protests have accordingly become a focal point for arguments within Western Christianity over [[christian-zionism|Christian Zionism]] and the treatment of Holy Land Christians.
