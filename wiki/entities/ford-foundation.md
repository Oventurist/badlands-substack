# Ford Foundation

The **Ford Foundation** is a major American philanthropic institution described in the Badlands Media essay "The Ivy Dominion" as one of the foundations that has been "repurposed" into an instrument of psychological operations.[1]

The article names Eric Doppstadt of the Ford Foundation, alongside [[rick-slocum|Rick Slocum]] of the [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller Foundation]], as personnel through whom [[harvard-university|Harvard]]'s "deeper ties" run, managing funds it links to intelligence-adjacent activities.[1] It further cites [[mcgeorge-bundy|McGeorge Bundy]] — a [[skull-and-bones|Skull & Bones]] alumnus who moved from National Security Advisor to the Ford Foundation presidency — as evidence of "covert foreign influence through academia."[1]

More broadly, the piece asserts that foundations like Ford and Rockefeller "serve as laundromats for psychological operations," funding ideologically compliant research, incentivizing activist scholarship and enforcing loyalty through grant dependency, producing what the author calls total capture of media, medicine, education and policy within the [[ivy-dominion|Ivy Dominion]].[1]

## Norman Dodd testimony

In *The Men Behind the Curtain — Part 7*, Badlands Media cites the Ford Foundation as the central illustration of the political capture of [[tax-exempt-foundations|tax-exempt foundations]].[2] [[norman-dodd|Norman Dodd]], director of research for the House Select Committee to Investigate Foundations and Comparable Organizations, reported in 1952 that the president of the Ford Foundation told him bluntly that the foundation was "operating under a directive from the White House" to "use our grant-making power so as to alter our life in the United States that we can be comfortably merged with the Soviet Union."[2]

## Constitutional revision movement

The same article notes that a long-time president of the Lord Foundation was [[mcgeorge-bundy|McGeorge Bundy]] — a [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] member, Bonesman, and the National Security Advisor who presided over the Gulf of Tonkin incident that precipitated the Vietnam War.[2] It further records that in the mid-1980s a movement to rewrite the U.S. Constitution gained momentum in part through the work of the Center for the Study of Democratic Institutions, an entity established with Ford Foundation money; the movement eventually floundered in the face of widespread opposition.[2] Ford is also named in the article's broader catalogue of nonprofits said to advance globalist aims.[2]
