# Martin Armstrong

Martin Armstrong is an economic analyst associated with Armstrong Economics whose commentary is frequently republished by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] and similar outlets.[1]

## Commentary on European politics

In November 2023, Armstrong argued that a "shift to the middle" was coming in European politics and that the media had become so hostile to common sense that anyone taking a contrary view was branded "far-right," as though they were Nazis.[1] In his framing, simply wanting to return to normal life had become grounds for the label, while acceptable opinion required supporting a wider war, high taxes, lifestyle sacrifice for climate change, and gender ideology in primary schooling.[1] His analysis was cited alongside mapping of European populist strength published after the [[2023-dutch-general-election|2023 Dutch general election]] won by [[geert-wilders|Geert Wilders]]' [[party-for-freedom|PVV]].[1]

The name also appears in this context attached to Statista chart work on right-wing populism's potential across Europe, which the coverage credited to a Martin Armstrong at Statista.[1]

## Views on money and the gold standard

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] describes Armstrong as a market analyst and forecaster who is famous for developing "the most successful market prediction software in history," an artificial intelligence named Socrates.[2] Armstrong is cited as one of several historians and economists who hold that the [[gold-standard|gold standard]], as understood in modern times, is not an ultimate solution to over-printing of money by the banking cartels; Armstrong himself regards the gold standard as "garbage."[2]

In December 2020, Armstrong appeared on the Palisades Gold Radio show to discuss the money cycle and the failure of the gold standard, addressing what Deschamps calls a side of history suspiciously ignored by modern financial experts and economists.[2] The key claim attributed to Armstrong is that [[money|money]] is intimately connected with human behavior: rather than bankers deciding everything about money including its value, citizens exert an incredible influence over it, particularly if they become active players in the governance of money itself.[2]
