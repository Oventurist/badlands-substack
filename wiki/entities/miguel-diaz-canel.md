# Miguel Diaz-Canel

Miguel Diaz-Canel is the president of [[cuba|Cuba]] and the first leader of the country from outside the Castro family since Fidel Castro took power in 1959.[1]

## March 2026 announcement

On a Thursday evening in March 2026, Diaz-Canel announced that he would hold an emergency press conference the next morning to disclose something important.[1] He appeared the following morning alongside Raulito Castro — a grandson of Raul Castro, known by the nickname "El Cangrejo," the Crab — and stated that Cuba was in open negotiations with the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]].[1] No further details were provided.[1]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the announcement all but confirmed Axios reporter Marc Caputo's earlier reporting that Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and Castro had been engaged in secret negotiations over Castro potentially taking over as Cuba's leader.[1] He noted the irony that neoconservatives had long demanded the Castro family be expelled from Cuba or arrested for war crimes, while [[donald-trump|President Trump]] now appeared poised to install one of them as America's preferred Cuban leader, following a template already seen in other countries such as Venezuela.[1]

## 2026 talks with the United States

In mid-March 2026 Díaz-Canel confirmed the existence of largely undisclosed talks between [[cuba|Cuba]] and the Trump administration, in which Havana sought relief from sanctions including eased embargo restrictions, a waiver of Title III of the [[helms-burton-act|Helms-Burton Act]], and removal from the state sponsors of terrorism list.[2] The negotiations, led on the American side by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], followed a US oil blockade imposed roughly two months earlier, and Cuba's senior diplomat in Washington, [[lianys-torres-rivera|Lianys Torres Rivera]], publicly offered "mutually beneficial discussions" on economic cooperation.[2]

## Response to the 2024 Santiago protests

In March 2024 Díaz-Canel responded to a rare public protest by hundreds of residents in Santiago, [[cuba|Cuba]]'s second-largest city, where demonstrators chanted "power and food" amid blackouts lasting 18 hours or more a day and severe food shortages.[3] He confirmed the protest on X shortly after the rally concluded and called for dialogue in an "atmosphere of tranquility and peace," while also alleging that "terrorists" from the United States were seeking to foment further uprisings.[3] "This context will be taken advantage of by the enemies of the Revolution, for destabilizing purposes," he wrote.[3] See [[2024-santiago-de-cuba-protests|2024 Santiago de Cuba protests]].[3]

## Rise to power and the 2021 protests

Díaz-Canel rose through the ranks of the communist party before assuming the position of head of state, taking over after the tenure of [[raul-castro|Raúl Castro]], which the Badlands essay "Glimpses into Hell — Part 1" says ended roughly halfway through [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s presidency.[4] The essay argues that hopes of a return to the pre-revolutionary "golden age" after the Castros were "a naive fairy tale," since the Cuban state is a monolithic system in which "business continues as usual" regardless of who occupies the seat of power.[4]

During the [[2021-cuban-protests|2021 protests]], Díaz-Canel openly called for violence, instructing his followers that "the order to combat has been given. Revolutionaries need to be on the streets."[4] The party deployed [[rapid-reaction-brigades|Rapid Reaction Brigades]] to suppress dissent, and the state cut internet access nationwide.[4]
