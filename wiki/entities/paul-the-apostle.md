# Paul the Apostle

Paul the Apostle, formerly Saul, is invoked in a July 11, 2024 Badlands Media essay on the exploitation of trust as the scriptural model for trust that has been verified rather than merely extended.[1]

## The Damascus road as verification

In 1 Corinthians 16:13 — a verse Q cites and which the essay treats as pointing to the solution — Paul insists that the church stand firm in the faith in Jesus Christ.[1] The essay argues that Paul was confident in this trust because of his encounter with Jesus on the road to Damascus, recounted in Acts 9.[1] Saul, a Jew, a Roman citizen and a Pharisee, had been persecuting the followers of Jesus after His death and resurrection; the encounter left him blind until he met a man sent by the Lord to heal him.[1] In the essay's reading, the truth was verified for Paul, and he developed faith as a result, knowing his trust was perfectly placed.[1]

The example is doing argumentative work: the essay's whole case is that trust extended without evidence or investigation invites exploitation, and Paul is offered as the counter-model in which conviction follows verification. The same logic underlies its treatment of "[[trust-but-verify|trust, but verify]]" and its endorsement of trust-less systems such as [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]].[1] See also [[trust-the-plan|"TRUST the plan"]].[1]
