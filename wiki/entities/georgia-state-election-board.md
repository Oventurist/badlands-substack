# Georgia State Election Board

The Georgia State Election Board (SEB) is the state body responsible for adjudicating election-related complaints in Georgia and for making referrals to the state's attorney general where it finds evidence of violations.[1]

## January 2026 Fulton County hearing

On Wednesday, January 21, 2026, the SEB held an eleven-hour hearing to address outstanding election cases and to determine which matters would be referred to the Georgia attorney general.[1] Badlands Media characterized the session as the first occasion on which the board "finally heard rebuttal" to the election narrative advanced by the office of Georgia Secretary of State [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]].[1]

During the hearing, local investigator [[joe-rossi|Joe Rossi]] presented what he described as evidence of intentional human intervention in the 2020 election in Fulton County, called on the SEB chairman to resign, and asked the board to commit to criminal referrals.[1] Rossi's presentation focused on a report generated at 12:06 p.m. on Thursday, December 3, 2020, which was emailed seven minutes later to [[ryan-macias|Ryan Macias]], described by Rossi as a consultant associated with The Election Group.[1] Rossi tied that sequence to testimony by Fulton County elections director [[richard-barron|Richard Barron]], who had said during certification week that officials "knew we were short" and had "called the state."[1]

Badlands writers summarized the claim as follows: officials knew on November 19, 2020 that the counts did not match, and on December 3, 2020 "it appears that, they made them match."[1]

## Coverage and aftermath

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] and [[canncon|CannCon]] recapped the hearing's key moments in a Badlands Media special broadcast and carried the subsequent Georgia State Senate Ethics Committee meeting live.[1] The following day, [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] testified before Congress and, according to Badlands commentary, repeatedly relied on Raffensperger's authority in justifying his treatment of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — a juxtaposition Badlands writers considered significant given that Raffensperger had been implicated at the SEB hearing the day before.[1]

## Related legislative action

On Thursday, January 22, 2026, the Georgia State Senate Ethics Committee passed Resolution 563, urging the Georgia Secretary of State to fully comply with a Justice Department request for Georgia voter registration information in order to meet obligations under the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act]], the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]], and the [[civil-rights-act-of-1960|Civil Rights Act of 1960]].[1]

## October 2024 hand-count rule blocked

In September 2024 the board's pro-Trump majority passed a rule that would have required poll workers to hand-count ballots; it had been introduced by Sharlene Alexander, a Trump supporter appointed to the Fayette County board of elections in February 2024, described in press accounts as one of twelve election deniers who had introduced more than thirty rules to the board since May.[2] On Tuesday, October 15, 2024, Judge [[robert-mcburney|Robert McBurney]] ruled against the rule, holding that with early voting already underway mandatory hand-counting would create chaos and delay results; he did not determine whether the rule was illegal but paused it pending further review.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the ruling exposed the hollowness of the election ecosystem's stated controls: if state election boards are the statutory authority over election integrity yet may not unilaterally determine that additional security is required, then certification is a rubber stamp and the boards are ceremonial.[2] She framed this as a paradoxical win for those seeking to reveal the theater of U.S. elections, concluding that "a façade of security is no security at all."[2]

## December 2025 access to Fulton County 2020 ballots

The Republican-led board had reopened an investigation in 2024 into how [[fulton-county|Fulton County]] handled the 2020 election, and in December 2025 a Georgia Superior Court judge granted it access to the county's 2020 presidential ballots and documents.[3] Fulton County Superior Court Judge [[robert-mcburney|Robert McBurney]] ruled that the board would need to cover the costs of the production, estimated at about $400,000.[3] County Chairman [[robb-pitts|Robb Pitts]] maintained that the elections had been open and transparent and that county taxpayers would not pay for the production.[3] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] welcomed the ruling as a step forward while questioning the cost and the county's record of obstruction.[3]

## 2025 Fulton County subpoena and DOJ referral

The board's subpoena to the Fulton County Board of Registration and Elections became the basis for a November 2025 demand letter from the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]]'s Civil Rights Division, signed by Assistant Attorney General [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]], ordering [[fulton-county|Fulton County]] to produce all responsive election records within 15 days.[4] Dhillon's letter cited a July 30, 2025 resolution of the State Election Board that invited the Justice Department's assistance in ensuring transparency, and a November 7, 2024 board letter referencing "unexplained anomalies in vote tabulation and storage related to the 2020 election."[4]

## "Reasonable inquiry" certification rule (May 2024)

On Wednesday, May 8, 2024, the State Election Board began writing a new rule requiring a "reasonable inquiry" before the certification of Georgia elections, granting a request from a Republican county election board member who had refused to sign off on that year's presidential primary.[5] Board member [[sara-tindall-ghazal|Sara Tindall Ghazal]], the Democratic Party representative, warned that if certification rules were changed, local election board members could use them as justification for voting against approving results in upcoming elections.[5] "The risk is using pretextual reasons to fail to certify when folks are not pleased with the results," she said. "That is my concern — using excuses to fail to certify."[5]

Three Republican board members from Fulton and Gwinnett counties had voted against certifying the presidential primary results, saying they wanted more detailed documentation beyond a broad summary of votes cast, even though [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had each easily won their respective primaries.[5]

## January 2024 oversight bill

A bill sponsored by Republican State Sen. [[max-burns|Max Burns]] to remove Secretary of State [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]] from his seat on the board — and to permit the board to investigate him — passed the [[georgia-senate|Georgia Senate]] Ethics Committee on January 23, 2024.[6] Raffensperger's lawyer [[charlene-mcgowan|Charlene McGowan]] objected that there was no precedent for removing the secretary of state from the board or for establishing oversight over him, and warned that "giving a board of unelected bureaucrats unchecked power over the state's executive branch is a dangerous policy proposal."[6] Supporters denied the proposal was unconstitutional, with Burns saying they were "trying to provide reasonable oversight of a process that affects every citizen of Georgia."[6]

Badlands commentary observed the irony that McGowan's characterization reduced the board — the entity meant to bestow trust and confidence in Georgia's elections — to a "meaningless group of unelected bureaucrats," and urged that reform prioritize decentralizing both oversight and administration toward the counties.[6]
