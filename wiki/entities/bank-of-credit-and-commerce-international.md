# Bank of Credit and Commerce International

The **Bank of Credit and Commerce International** (BCCI), also rendered as the Bank of Commerce and Credit International, was an international bank widely described as a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] proprietary and known for pervasive corruption.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] writes that the Cold War drug-running operation underpinning U.S. proxy armies eventually resulted in BCCI's collapse.[1]

## Reputation

The Justice Department referred to BCCI as the "world's sleaziest bank," and Time Magazine as the "dirtiest bank of all."[1]

## Intelligence and criminal ties

A July 29, 1991 Time Magazine report quoted at length in the article stated that BCCI's more conventional departments handled services such as laundering money for the drug trade and helping dictators loot their national treasuries, while its "black network" operated a lucrative arms-trade business and transported drugs and gold, often working with Western and Middle Eastern intelligence agencies.[1] The account described the bank's ties to the intelligence agencies of several countries as so pervasive that even the White House became entangled: the National Security Council used BCCI to funnel money for the Iran-Contra deals, the CIA maintained accounts at the bank for covert operations, and investigators said the Defense Intelligence Agency kept a slush-fund account there apparently to pay for clandestine activities.[1]

Time further reported that the CIA may have used BCCI as more than an undercover banker, with U.S. agents collaborating with the black network in several operations according to a former BCCI black-network "officer" turned secret U.S. government witness; that BCCI worked closely with Israel's spy agencies and other Western intelligence groups, especially in arms deals; and that investigators discovered suspected terrorist accounts for Libya, Syria and the Palestine Liberation Organization at the bank's London offices.[1]

## Significance in the drug-funded covert economy

DeLarme presents BCCI as a demonstrable example of the long-standing claim that the drug trade funds CIA and Department of Defense covert operations and black projects.[1] In his account, the black-market drug economy was set up during the Cold War to fund proxy armies such as the [[taliban|Taliban]]'s Mujahedeen predecessors and the Contras, and proved so lucrative that it outlived the Cold War — after which, he argues, the messaging apparatus of the [[united-states-institute-of-peace|United States Institute of Peace]] supplied a palatable public rationale for continued opium production in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]].[1]

## Bear Stearns clearing and the Epstein connection

During the late 1970s, while [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] handled complex trades in the special products division of [[bear-stearns|Bear Stearns]], the firm processed substantial transactions for BCCI, an institution later exposed as a conduit for laundering funds in support of covert operations.[2] Court records and investigations — including the UK's "Sandstorm Report," unredacted in 2011 — confirm Bear Stearns' role as a broker for BCCI, clearing transactions that disguised illicit cash movements.[2]

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterized BCCI as an important and deeply corrupt piece of the deep political engine — a financial organization protected and used by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]].[2] Researcher Kevin Ryan summarized it as a "CIA-linked terrorist financing network," and former acting CIA Director Richard Kerr told a Senate committee "that the CIA had used BCCI for certain intelligence-gathering operations."[2] Investigative journalist Lucy Komisar, writing in "A Game as Old as Empire," described BCCI as a dirty offshore bank that Ronald Reagan's CIA used to run guns to Saddam Hussein, finance Osama bin Laden, move money in the illegal [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra]] operation and carry out other black operations, adding that one of the bank's largest Saudi investors helped bail out George W. Bush's troubled oil investments.[2] Komisar further reported that the CIA used BCCI's Islamabad and other Pakistani branches to funnel part of the $2 billion Washington sent to bin Laden's [[mujahideen|Mujahideen]] against the Soviets in Afghanistan, moving cash skimmed by Pakistani military and government officials as well as money required by Saudi intelligence.[2]

BCCI facilitated billions in flows for entities including the CIA, Saudi intelligence and arms networks supporting anti-Soviet efforts in Afghanistan and other proxy conflicts, and [[adnan-khashoggi|Adnan Khashoggi]] borrowed heavily from the bank to finance arms purchases backed by Saudi and U.S. elements.[2]

## The Bush family connection

"Profiting From War" describes BCCI as one of the biggest [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] money streams ever disrupted — a CIA bank that turned into a $20 billion fraud funding both agency operations and individuals.[3] Quoting a 2004 [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] opinion piece, it identifies BCCI as Abu Dhabi-based and known by the nickname "Bank of Crooks and Criminals International," and links [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]]'s persistent bent toward the Middle East, covert operations and rogue banks to his combined oil and intelligence background.[3] Bush worked closely with [[kamal-adham|Kamal Adham]], head of Saudi intelligence and an early BCCI insider, after taking the CIA helm in January 1976.[3]

Some of the clandestine arms shipments to Iran in the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra affair]] were BCCI-financed, and [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues Bush used the bank to fund CIA operations during both Iran-Contra and [[iraqgate]].[3] The article contends the BCCI scandal was more dangerous to Bush than either affair because it implicated the whole [[bush-family|family]]: all four sons — [[george-w-bush|George W.]], [[jeb-bush|Jeb]], [[neil-bush|Neil]] and [[marvin-bush|Marvin]] — lined up deals with Saudi, Kuwaiti and Bahraini moneymen and "cozied up" to the bank, making the Middle East a "family money spigot."[3]

Specific links cited include Texas businessman [[james-bath|James Bath]], North American representative for billionaire [[salem-bin-laden|Salem bin Laden]] and BCCI insider [[khalid-bin-mahfouz|Khalid bin Mahfouz]], whose $50,000 went into George W. Bush's 1979 [[arbusto-energy|Arbusto]] oil partnership; the "mosaic of BCCI connections" surrounding [[harken-energy|Harken Energy]] noted by the Wall Street Journal in 1991; and the conclusion by Time reporters Jonathan Beaty and S.C. Gwynne in *The Outlaw Bank* that Mahfouz or other BCCI players steered Harken's 1990 Bahrain drilling contract to the president's son.[3] Jeb Bush's business associates Guillermo Hernandez-Cartaya and Camilo Padreda were longtime associates of BCCI investor and arms dealer [[adnan-khashoggi|Adnan Khashoggi]], and Jeb also socialized with Adbur Sakhia, the Miami BCCI branch chief and later its top U.S. official.[3] The article argues that congressional and criminal investigations into BCCI drove Bush to start the [[gulf-war|Gulf War]] as a distraction.[3]
