# Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States

The Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS) is the interagency body that reviews foreign acquisitions of American assets for national security implications. It is the panel whose approval permitted Russian state ownership of American uranium through the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]].[1]

As Secretary of State, [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] sat on the committee alongside then-Attorney General [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]] at the time the transaction was approved, a fact emphasised in reporting cited by Badlands Media because the FBI was simultaneously running an undercover investigation into bribery by officials of [[rosatom|Rosatom]] subsidiaries seeking that very approval.[1] A congressional probe later examined whether the FBI, then under [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]], had alerted senior [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration officials to the corruption before the committee rendered its favourable decision; the FBI declined to comment.[1]

Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats the committee's approval, granted despite the pending criminal case, as the pivotal official act in what he calls the biggest cover-up in American history — arguing that the members who signed off, including Clinton and Holder, must have known of the investigation.[1]
