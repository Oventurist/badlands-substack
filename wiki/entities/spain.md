# Spain

Spain is a European country whose men's national football team won the [[2026-fifa-world-cup|2026 FIFA World Cup]], defeating [[argentina|Argentina]] 1-0 after extra time at MetLife Stadium to capture the country's second men's world championship.[1] Substitute Ferran Torres scored the only goal in the 106th minute, and Spain dominated possession and shot volume across the match.[1]

## World Cup final

Spain had two further goals disallowed during the match — one ruled offside after the winner, and one earlier disqualified when an Argentine player flopped and a foul was called while the game was scoreless.[1] After the final whistle, Spanish players were involved in a confrontation with Argentina midfielder Leandro Paredes, and Argentine defender Nahuel Molina reportedly punched the Spanish captain, triggering a brawl.[1]

## Political context

The Badlands Brief characterized Spain as an underdog favored by online audiences in part for political reasons, describing Spain as having "led the trend of European countries defecting from the longstanding tradition of supporting [[israel|Israel]] no matter what," in contrast to Argentina under [[javier-milei|Javier Milei]].[1]

## March 2026: expulsion of the Israeli ambassador

In March 2026 the government of Spain expelled the Israeli ambassador and announced the termination of diplomatic relations with [[israel|Israel]] amid escalating tensions over the regional conflict.[2] Spanish officials said the decision included withdrawing Spain's own ambassador from Israel and ordering the Israeli ambassador to leave Spain, effectively suspending formal diplomatic ties between the two countries.[2] Such expulsions and ambassador withdrawals are a common escalation step in international disputes, signaling a breakdown in formal relations without necessarily closing embassies permanently.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the move owed less to the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] crisis than to the pattern of Israeli government conduct since the October 7, 2023 attack, and predicted that the psychological effect of both [[iran|Iran]] and Spain defying "the Hegemony" would encourage other states to follow.[2] He also noted, without a firm read on Spanish politics, that numerous corrupt politicians and their associates had fled to Spain in recent years to evade prosecution.[2]

## Clash with Trump at the 2026 Ankara NATO summit

### Trump's suggestion of expulsion from NATO (October 2025)

Speaking during an Oval Office meeting with Finnish President [[alexander-stubb|Alexander Stubb]] on Thursday, October 9, 2025, US President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said Spain should be thrown out of [[nato|NATO]] for failing to meet the alliance's new 5% defense spending target.[7] Trump, who had spearheaded the increase, boasted that he secured the commitment "virtually unanimously" at the June NATO summit, adding: "We had one laggard. It was Spain," and that "they have no excuse not to do this."[7] He concluded: "Maybe you should throw them out of NATO, frankly."[7]

Trump had repeatedly accused NATO members of failing to shoulder the military spending burden equitably even during his first term, and since returning to office in January 2025 intensified demands that European members spend more on defense; the push culminated at the June summit in The Hague, where members committed to spending 5% of GDP annually on defense by 2035, a meeting Trump called "the most unified and productive in history."[7] Badlands commentary read the remark as an example of Trump using the alliance's own logic against it — arguing, in effect, for NATO's dismantling by proposing to expel every member unwilling to fight Russia.[7]

### Tariff threat over NATO spending (October 2025)

Nearly a year before the Ankara summit, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened to impose punitive tariffs on Spain over its failure to meet [[nato|NATO's]] increased defense spending requirements.[5] Speaking to journalists at the White House on October 14, 2025, Trump accused Spain of being the only member nation that had not raised military spending to 5% of GDP, said "I'm very unhappy with Spain," and claimed Madrid had been "unbelievably disrespectful" of the alliance.[5] "I was thinking about giving them trade punishment through tariffs because of what they did. I may do that," he said, without detailing the potential measures.[5] Earlier that month, during an Oval Office meeting with Finnish President Alexander Stubb, Trump had suggested expelling Spain from NATO altogether, saying "they have no excuse not to do this."[5] Madrid had previously dismissed the 5% target as impossible.[5]

At the [[2026-ankara-nato-summit|NATO summit in Ankara]] in July 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] sharply criticized Spain over its opposition to the US military campaign against [[iran|Iran]] and over its defense spending, calling the country "bad people" and ordering a halt to US trade with Spain.[3] Trump also accused Spain of refusing to support US operations against Iran, while Prime Minister [[pedro-sanchez|Pedro Sánchez]] maintained his government's opposition to the conflict.[3] Despite the public dispute, Trump later said the summit concluded with "a lot of unity" among NATO allies.[3]

## "Make Spain Great Again" parade moment (July 2026)

During Spain's victory parade in Madrid following its 1-0 extra-time win over Argentina in the World Cup final, match hero [[ferran-torres|Ferran Torres]] drew attention by wearing a red hat reading "Make Spain Great Again," a play on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "Make America Great Again" slogan.[4] Photos went viral, prompting debate over whether the hat was a political statement or simply a celebratory slogan; Torres did not publicly comment.[4] Badlands commentary presented the moment as a rebuttal to media framing of a supposed rift between Trump and the Spanish players arising from geopolitical disagreements with the Spanish government.[4]

## Cancellation of Israeli arms contracts (September 2025)

In September 2025 Spain scrapped nearly €1 billion ($1.18 billion) in defense contracts with Israeli firms, its strongest move yet under Prime Minister [[pedro-sanchez|Pedro Sánchez]]'s newly announced measures against Israel over the Gaza offensive.[6] The Defense Ministry reportedly canceled a €700 million ($826 million) deal for twelve SILAM rocket launchers and a €287.5 million ($339 million) contract for 168 Spike L.R. anti-tank missiles; the SILAM systems, based on [[elbit-systems|Elbit]]'s PULS platform, were to have been built by a Spanish consortium.[6] Madrid was reported to be preparing to disengage militarily and technologically from [[israel|Israel]] while seeking alternative suppliers, following Sánchez's pledge to enshrine in law a ban on arms sales to and purchases from Israel.[6] The move is documented at [[spain-israel-arms-deal-cancellations-of-2025|Spain–Israel Arms Deal Cancellations of 2025]].[6]
