# Synergy Marine Group

Synergy Marine Group is a Singapore-based ship management company that managed the container vessel [[dali|Dali]], the 984-foot cargo ship that struck and brought down the Francis Scott Key Bridge in Baltimore in March 2024.[1]

## Role in the Key Bridge collapse

The Dali experienced a "complete blackout" minutes before colliding with the bridge, an event that placed the vessel's management and maintenance practices at the center of the ensuing inquiries.[1] In April 2024 the [[fbi|FBI]] opened a criminal investigation into the ship, with investigators examining whether crew members knew of problems with the vessel before it left port.[1] That criminal inquiry proceeded separately from the [[national-transportation-safety-board|National Transportation Safety Board]] investigation into the cause of the crash.[1]

## Liability filing

Earlier in April 2024 the Singaporean company filed a preemptive petition in the U.S. district court in Baltimore seeking to limit its liability.[1] The filing asked the court to "issue an order enjoining the commencement of or further prosecution of any claims or causes of action against Petitioners except in this action" and to "determine that Petitioners are not liable for any loss or damage arising out of the Casualty."[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the filing as an attempt by Synergy Marine to get ahead of liability findings as federal criminal exposure mounted, noting that the FBI had boarded the Dali just after dawn on a Monday and claimed jurisdiction from the Coast Guard.[1] She observed that private law firms had simultaneously been retained to represent the Baltimore mayor's office and some of the construction workers on the bridge at the time of the collapse — six of the eight of whom died — and concluded that the government was "looking for accountability, and they have many scapegoats to choose from."[1]

## Statements on the Dali crew, May 2024

Seven weeks after the [[dali|Dali]] destroyed the Francis Scott Key Bridge, Synergy Marine said the crew of twenty Indian sailors and one Sri Lankan remained aboard the marooned vessel because "nobody knows the ship better than they do" and they were integral to its functioning.[2] Critics observed that the men were nonetheless not being treated as integral helpers: their phones had been confiscated, they had no contact with the outside world and no freedom of movement, with visa and immigration requirements and the parallel [[national-transportation-safety-board|NTSB]] and [[fbi|FBI]] investigations cited as the reasons for their confinement.[2]
