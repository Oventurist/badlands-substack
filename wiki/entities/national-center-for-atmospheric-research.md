# National Center for Atmospheric Research

The National Center for Atmospheric Research (NCAR) is a federally funded research center in Boulder, Colorado, and one of the leading government-funded institutions for atmospheric and climate research in the United States.[1]

## 2025 breakup announcement

In December 2025, Office of Management and Budget director [[russ-vought|Russ Vought]] announced that the [[national-science-foundation|National Science Foundation]] would be "breaking up" NCAR, describing the facility as "one of the largest sources of climate alarmism in the country."[1] Vought said a comprehensive review was underway and that any vital activities, such as weather research, would be moved to another entity or location.[1]

## Political fallout

The announcement became the flashpoint for a renewed federal funding fight. Senate Democrats walked away from a potential deal to fund roughly two-thirds of the discretionary budget — including the departments of Defense, Labor, Education, and Health and Human Services — before [[congress|Congress]] adjourned for the Christmas recess, citing President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s threat to dismantle NCAR as the reason they could not advance a five-bill spending package.[1] Had the package passed, it would have funded an estimated 85 to 90 percent of the federal government through September of the following year and removed the threat of another shutdown; instead the shutdown threat remained alive into late January.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing from Colorado, noted that local opponents of the defunding had protested over the weekend and were seeking allies in other states to force a shutdown, and welcomed both the defunding and the prospect of a shutdown.[1]
