# Rob Bauer

Admiral Rob Bauer is a Dutch naval officer who serves as chairman of [[nato|NATO]]'s Military Committee, the alliance's senior military body.[1]

## Warnings of war with Russia

Speaking to reporters in January 2024 after a meeting of NATO defence chiefs in Brussels, Bauer said that Europeans should prepare for the possibility of a full-scale war with [[russia|Russia]] at some point over the following two decades. He argued that governments needed to begin putting preparations in place immediately and that civilian populations would have to be mobilised on a large scale.[1]

"We have to realise it's not a given that we are in peace. And that's why we [NATO forces] are preparing for a conflict with Russia," Bauer said. He added that the discussion extended well beyond uniformed forces: "It is also the industrial base and also the people that have to understand they play a role." He framed the point as one of public expectation management, saying the realisation had to take hold that "not everything is planable and not everything is going to be hunky dory in the next 20 years."[1]

Bauer also praised Sweden for having told its population to brace for war, a stance reported at the time by The Telegraph.[1]

Commentary in the Truth Community treated remarks of this kind as part of a broader pattern of Western institutions conditioning publics for prolonged confrontation with Russia rather than as a neutral assessment of military risk.[1]

## Support for Ukrainian long-range strikes (September 2024)

At the close of the NATO Military Committee's annual meeting in September 2024, Bauer said Ukraine had the solid legal and military right to strike deep inside Russia to gain combat advantage, reflecting the beliefs of a number of U.S. allies even as the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration balked at allowing Kyiv to do so with American-made weapons.[2] "Every nation that is attacked has the right to defend itself. And that right doesn't stop at the border of your own nation," Bauer said at the meeting, which was also attended by U.S. General CQ Brown, chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff.[2] Bauer added that nations retain the sovereign right to place limits on the weapons they send to Ukraine, while Lieutenant General Karel Řehka, chief of the General Staff of the Czech Armed Forces, said standing beside him that his country imposes no such restrictions: "We believe that the Ukrainians should decide themselves how to use it."[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] mocked Bauer personally and argued that the appearance of such headlines indicated a public relations campaign — that NATO figures were lobbying for public support precisely because they lacked permission to use the missiles, and hoped to use public sentiment to subvert whatever authority was withholding them.[2] He paired the commentary with a call to abolish NATO immediately and to investigate and prosecute its leaders for crimes against humanity.[2]

## Call for businesses to prepare for a "wartime scenario" (November 2024)

Speaking at a European Policy Center think-tank event in Brussels on 25 November 2024, in what was described as one of his final interventions as outgoing chief of the alliance's military committee, Bauer urged businesses in NATO countries to prepare themselves for a "wartime scenario" and to adjust their production lines and supply chains so as to be less vulnerable to blackmail by nations such as Russia and China.[3] "If we can make sure that all crucial services and goods can be delivered no matter what, then that is a key part of our deterrence," he argued, adding that "businesses need to be prepared for a wartime scenario and adjust their production and distribution lines accordingly. Because while it may be the military who wins battles, it's the economies that win wars."[3] Referring to the collapse in Russian gas supplies to the European Union after the escalation of the Ukraine conflict in 2022, Bauer said, "We thought we had a deal with Gazprom, but we actually had a deal with [Russian President Vladimir] Putin."[3]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] dismissed the admiral as "such a blowhard" and linked the timing of the remarks to the abrupt exit of Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen, closing with the rhetorical question of whether readers had bought any Bitcoin yet.[3]

## Warning against a Ukraine deal favoring Putin (November 2024)

In a wide-ranging interview on the sidelines of a European defense summit in Prague on Saturday, November 9, 2024, Bauer — described as the Dutch chairman of NATO's Military Committee — suggested that any peace deal negotiated by President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] that allowed Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to claim victory in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] would undermine the interests of the United States.[4] "If you allow a nation like Russia to win, to come out of this as the victor, then what does it mean for other autocratic states in the world where the U.S. has also interests?" he asked, adding: "It's important enough to talk about Ukraine on its own, but there is more at stake than just Ukraine."[4] The remarks came as Trump repeatedly said he could end the war in a day without explaining how, and as a settlement outlined by Vice President-elect [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] in September echoed what people close to the Kremlin said Putin wanted: Russia keeping captured territory and a guarantee that Ukraine would not join NATO.[4] Transition spokeswoman [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] responded that Trump had been re-elected because Americans "trust him to lead our country and restore peace through strength around the world."[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] answered the admiral bluntly, recommending that "Admiral Bobby Bauer sit down and shut the gaping noise-hole in the middle of his face" and reminding him "who works for whom."[4] He argued that NATO was close to being tossed onto the ash heap of history and that the American people were giddy at the prospect of dismantling the bureaucratic ecosystem surrounding it, deferring to Professor [[jeffrey-sachs|Jeffrey Sachs]] and a clip of his appearance on Judge [[andrew-napolitano|Napolitano]]'s show.[4]
