# Ma'aden (Saudi Arabian Mining Company)

Ma'aden, the Saudi Arabian Mining Company, is the state-owned mining champion through which the kingdom pursues the minerals pillar of its economic diversification program.[1] By January 2025 the company had become the central vehicle for Saudi Arabia's ambition to position itself as a global exploration hub and a significant supplier of critical minerals.[1]

## Exploration Results (January 2025)

At the Future Minerals Forum 2025, Ma'aden announced significant updates in its efforts to explore and unearth Saudi Arabia's mineral endowment.[1] The company reported promising gold and copper intercepts in the Arabian Shield, including a brand-new gold discovery at Wadi Al Jaww and further success at Jabal Shayban, in a first set of drill results the company said indicated an exciting new potential gold and copper district.[1] Ma'aden also released new drilling results from its flagship Mansourah–Massarah gold mine showing strong gold mineralisation, building on the exploration program's earlier success.[1]

## Lithium Joint Venture with Aramco

In the same period Saudi Arabia announced a joint venture between [[saudi-aramco|Aramco]], the world's largest oil company, and Ma'aden, described as a significant step in the kingdom's push to dominate critical minerals.[1] The partnership aims to extract lithium — an essential component in electric vehicles and electronic devices — from high-concentration deposits while exploring cost-effective direct extraction technologies, with commercial production expected to commence by 2027.[1] Aramco's president of exploration and production, Nasser al-Naimi, said the partnership would leverage "the world's leading upstream enterprise . . . with a view to meeting the kingdom and potentially the world's projected lithium demand."[1] The announcement was framed as aligning with [[vision-2030|Vision 2030]], the initiative to reduce Saudi dependence on oil by developing mining and other industries.[1]

Badlands commentary connected the announcement to speculation that a recent conversation between Brazilian President Lula da Silva and Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] had concerned lithium extraction from Brazil's oil fields.[1]
