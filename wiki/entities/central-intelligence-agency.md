# Central Intelligence Agency

The Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) is the organizing subject of the Badlands series to which "A Family Affair" belongs — a set of articles on the "seedy origins and consequences" of the agency, following "Clowns in America," "School of Assassins and Dictators," "The Shadow Game," "Shadow Government Exposed," "Follow the Money" and "The Legacy of George H.W. Bush."[1] The article glosses the acronym as "Criminals In Action."[1]

## Front companies and banks

In the article's account the agency operates through commercial cover: oil firms such as [[zapata-offshore|Zapata Offshore]], banks such as [[texas-national-bank|Texas National Bank]] and [[texas-commerce-bank|Texas Commerce Bank]], and corporations such as [[enron|Enron]] and Howard Hughes's Summa Corporation.[1] Officers were placed in the international divisions of banks and investment firms as "non-official cover" (NOC) operatives, the route by which [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] entered Texas Commerce Bank.[1] Oil revenues provided the laundering channel for drug proceeds, which the article says explains the agency's close ties to the petroleum industry.[1]

## Operations and personnel

The CIA's abortive 1961 [[bay-of-pigs-invasion|Bay of Pigs invasion]], code-named OPERATION ZAPATA, used ships supplied by Bush's firm.[1] The agency recruited and maintained a large Cuban exile force trained in paramilitary work, terrorism, assassination and money laundering, of which [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] is described as a leader; Bush became director in 1976 under President Gerald Ford, and the agency later named its headquarters after him.[1] Agency personnel trained the accountants and private bankers of the [[medellin-cartel|Medellín cartel]] to launder its billions, and its 1980s covert wars against Nicaragua and Salvadoran guerrillas were financed in part by cartel money exchanged for CIA-supplied weapons.[1] Former officers also appear in the article's Florida material: [[richard-lawless|Richard Lawless]], a fifteen-year veteran, won state contracts under commerce secretary Jeb Bush, and CIA operative Robert Gambino trained Jeb Bush in overseas banking.[1]
