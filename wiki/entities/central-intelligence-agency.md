# Central Intelligence Agency

The Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) is the civilian foreign intelligence service of the United States. In [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary it is treated less as a conventional intelligence agency than as the operational arm of what the outlet calls the [[deep-state|Deep State]], and is frequently discussed interchangeably with the [[state-department|State Department]].[1]

## The "State Department" convention

In the News Brief of June 28, 2024, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] proposed that writers should "always refer to the CIA as the State Department, to gaslight the naive public into understanding how sinister and duplicitous that agency is."[1] The rhetorical device was applied immediately: reporting that the Biden administration was moving toward lifting its de facto ban on American military contractors deploying to Ukraine was recast as the State Department "using their mercenaries to attack Russia and destabilize America."[1]

## Claims about ISIS and drug trafficking

The same take advanced the claim that [[isis|ISIS]] is not a genuinely Islamist organization but a mercenary force. The author reported interviewing two veterans of High-Value Target teams in Afghanistan and Iraq who said that in ten years of raiding ISIS safe houses they never found a Quran or Islamic prayer materials, but did find western pornography, video game consoles and DVDs that would be difficult to obtain in remote parts of the Middle East without western friends.[1] [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] was cited as having said on multiple occasions that ISIS fighters are highly paid mercenaries rather than religious zealots.[1]

A companion item on the collapse of Afghan opium production extended the argument to narcotics, asserting that the agency invaded Vietnam to protect a cartel drug trade and, after 9/11, took Afghanistan to seize the opium fields and drive up production — a chain the Brief linked to the opioid crisis and the [[sackler-family|Sackler Family]].[1] The piece concluded that the agency had waged kinetic war against the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] for years and now appeared prepared to wage it against the American people on U.S. soil.[1]

## Flynn collusion allegation (December 2025)

On December 21, 2025, former National Security Adviser [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] alleged that the CIA was colluding with European intelligence services to undermine President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s efforts to broker peace in Ukraine, writing that the agency is "in cahoots with [[mi6|MI6]] and others in the EU intelligence community."[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that major voices in the movement had long ignored that the CIA "represents a clear and present danger not only to world peace, but to the security of the American People."[2] He argued the CIA, working with foreign partners most likely including MI6 and [[mossad|Mossad]], was the most likely culprit behind the failed assassination attempt against Trump, named the intelligence community as the most likely suspect in the Charlie Kirk assassination, and asserted that the agencies control the mainstream media and use it as a propaganda tool.[2] He framed the emergence of Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] and Flynn as public critics as a turning point, alleging that the agencies want wars with Russia, Venezuela and Iran.[2]

## Authorization of covert action in Venezuela (November 2025)

In November 2025 The New York Times reported that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had signed off on C.I.A. plans for covert measures inside [[venezuela|Venezuela]], operations described by people briefed on the matter as potentially meant to prepare a battlefield for further action.[3] The move came with the largest U.S. aircraft carrier positioned in the Caribbean and coincided with authorization of back-channel negotiations in which President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] reportedly offered a delayed departure, an offer the [[white-house|White House]] rejected.[3] It was not clear what the covert actions might be or when they might be carried out; with ground combat forces not yet authorized, the next phase of the pressure campaign was expected to involve sabotage or cyber, psychological or information operations.[3]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] questioned the premise that a president would publicly announce authorization of covert C.I.A. operations and still expect them to succeed, proposing instead that Trump may have been openly warning Maduro that an agency operation was in the works.[3]

## Assange visitors' surveillance lawsuit

In December 2023, U.S. District Judge [[john-koeltl|John Koeltl]] rejected the agency's bid to dismiss a lawsuit brought by journalists and allies of [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]] founder [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]], allowing four Americans who visited Assange at the Ecuadorian Embassy in London to press claims that a security contractor supplied the CIA with data copied from their phones.[4] Koeltl dismissed allegations concerning eavesdropping on embassy conversations and the copying of passports, but found that accessing the contents of the visitors' devices would violate their Fourth Amendment expectation of privacy.[4] He barred money damages against former Director [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]] while permitting plaintiffs to seek an order compelling the agency to destroy any records obtained from the phones; spokespeople for the CIA and the U.S. Attorney's Office in Manhattan declined to comment.[4]

## Badlands claims on Al Qaeda and ISIS

Badlands' July 2024 commentary asserted that both [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] and [[isis|ISIS]] are creations of the CIA.[6] Al Qaeda was traced to [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]], the 1979 plan by [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]] to arm the Afghan mujahideen against the Soviets, through which [[osama-bin-laden|Osama bin Laden]] was recruited, trained and resourced — the group's name, meaning "The Base," said to derive from the CIA base where its fighters trained.[6] ISIS was traced to the agency's operation to overthrow [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] and to redirect Libyan weapons to Syrian fighters opposing [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]].[6] The same piece speculated that [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]] had been working for the CIA and [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[6]


## Project Stargate Mars Remote-Viewing Document

A December 2024 Daily Mail report highlighted a CIA document, 'Mars Exploration May 22, 1984,' which describes the agency using astral projection to transport a 'subject' to Mars approximately one million years BC.[7] The subject reported an "oblique view of a pyramid" and a "very large road" with a monument resembling those of ancient Egypt, and described a population of "very large people" searching for "a new place to live because their environment was corrupted."[7] The study was part of [[project-stargate|Project Stargate]], the secret US Army unit established in 1977 at [[fort-meade|Fort Meade]], Maryland, that studied remote viewing, telepathy, and psychokinesis.[7] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry asked whether the CIA was "pulling our leg" or whether the disclosure represented signal from Ghosts in the Machine.[7]

## Alleged role in the narcotics trade

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argues that the [[war-on-drugs|War on Drugs]] was "birthed in the shadowy bowels of Langley" and designed not to stop drugs but to harness their economic and destructive power, naming the CIA alongside the [[state-department|State Department]] and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] as an "unholy trinity" that exploited trafficking to fund covert agendas.[13]

The pattern he traces begins before the agency existed, with the [[office-of-strategic-services|OSS]] and [[operation-underworld|Operation Underworld]] during the Second World War.[13] After the CIA's creation in 1947 it cut deals with the Corsican mob in Marseille — the [[french-connection|French Connection]] — to keep the docks clear of law enforcement, and by the 1950s was arming Chiang Kai-shek's defeated generals in the [[golden-triangle|Golden Triangle]], trading guns for opium intelligence.[13] In Laos during the Vietnam War the agency allied with the warlord [[vang-pao|Vang Pao]], and historians such as [[alfred-mccoy|Alfred McCoy]] argue that its airline [[air-america|Air America]] became a conduit for opium and heroin precursors whose profits sustained anti-communist operations.[13] Former [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]] agent [[hector-berrellez|Hector Berrellez]] has claimed that CIA contract pilots knowingly flew drugs into the United States, landing on military bases with impunity.[13]

In [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] under [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]] the agency backed [[mujahideen|Mujahedeen]] commanders including the drug lord [[gulbuddin-hekmatyar|Gulbuddin Hekmatyar]], with declassified records showing awareness of the expanding opium trade but a decision to prioritise the anti-Soviet fight.[13] In Central America the agency's support for the [[nicaraguan-contras|Nicaraguan Contras]] after the [[boland-amendment|Boland Amendment]] cut off official funding produced the findings of the 1989 [[kerry-committee-report|Kerry Committee report]] and the allegations of [[gary-webb|Gary Webb]]'s "Dark Alliance" series.[13] The pilot [[barry-seal|Barry Seal]] was on the agency's payroll by 1984, flying a camera-equipped C-123 for a Nicaragua sting, an episode documented in the 1996 CIA Inspector General report on [[mena-arkansas|Mena, Arkansas]] released via [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] in 2020.[13]

## The February 2017 Alfa Bank approach

Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s [[michael-sussmann|Sussmann]] prosecution established that the CIA was the second federal agency approached with the [[alfa-bank-hoax|Alfa Bank Hoax]] material generated by [[hillary-clinton|Clinton]] campaign contractors.[14] After the [[fbi|FBI]] closed its inquiry into the original allegation around the time [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took office, the operatives revised and expanded the package with data freshly taken from [[executive-office-of-the-president|Executive Office of the President]] servers and brought it to the CIA in February 2017, seeking to induce the agency to open its own counterintelligence investigation of the sitting President.[14] Badlands contributor [[brian-cates|Brian Cates]] argued that officials in the intelligence and law enforcement agencies who knowingly continued illegally predicated surveillance of Trump after his inauguration could, on the evidence Durham had surfaced, be charged with treason.[14]

Badlands contributor Joe Lange describes the CIA as the most dangerous weapon in the world and as the instrument by which a global cabal kept the planet in a permanent state of war, using the threat of nuclear conflict to manipulate populations while sustaining both military spending and drug trafficking revenue streams.[15] In this account the agency operated a worldwide network of proxy states and terrorist organizations: [[pakistan|Pakistan]] as a haven for heroin trafficking and nuclear proliferation, [[hamas|Hamas]] as a manufactured terrorist group used to generate recurring wars with Israel, Ukraine as its most corrupt client state, and [[armenia|Armenia]], [[north-korea|North Korea]] and [[iran|Iran]] as "rogue nuclear states" whose sanctioned status conveniently removed them from the global financial system so the agency could operate in cash beyond congressional oversight.[15] Lange argues the agency deliberately fomented chaos in the post-Soviet Caucasus in order to establish drug routes, and that it maintained seven [[snow-white-supercomputers|supercomputers]] named for the seven dwarfs — three in North Korea and four in Armenia — used to wage a narrative war against [[qanon|Q]] and the anon movement.[15] He credits [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]] with cleansing the agency's leadership and taking those machines offline, and casts [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as being at war with the CIA in order to dismantle the cabal's control.[15]

## Steering of activist movements

The second installment of Badlands Media's "How to Rent a Riot" series argues that the CIA, the [[pentagon|Department of Defense]], [[nato|NATO]] and their surrogate institutions have long since perfected the art of infiltration, radicalization and steering of activist groups.[19] It documents [[gene-sharp|Gene Sharp]]'s links to the agency and his recruitment into Harvard's [[weatherhead-center-for-international-affairs|Center for International Affairs]], nicknamed "The CIA at Harvard," which also nurtured [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]], McGeorge Bundy and future CIA director Robert Bowie.[19]

In the Balkans, the agency "helped to train" the separatist Albanian [[kosovo-liberation-army|Kosovo Liberation Army]] — previously designated a terrorist group by Washington — and, per the Guardian, encouraged it to launch a rebellion in southern Serbia to undermine [[slobodan-milosevic|Slobodan Milosevic]].[19] The article characterizes the [[international-republican-institute|IRI]] and [[national-democratic-institute|NDI]] as vehicles for CIA influence over the Republican and Democratic parties respectively, and states that the wider NGO network — including the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|NED]], created in 1983–84 — was erected to carry out functions ascribed to the CIA before its "dirty laundry was made public."[19] Contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s conclusion draws a line from historical operations such as [[cointelpro|COINTELPRO]] and Operation Ajax to these modern proxies.[19]
