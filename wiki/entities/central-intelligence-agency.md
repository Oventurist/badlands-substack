# Central Intelligence Agency

The Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) is the civilian foreign intelligence service of the United States. In [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary it is treated less as a conventional intelligence agency than as the operational arm of what the outlet calls the [[deep-state|Deep State]], and is frequently discussed interchangeably with the [[state-department|State Department]].[1]

## The "State Department" convention

In the News Brief of June 28, 2024, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] proposed that writers should "always refer to the CIA as the State Department, to gaslight the naive public into understanding how sinister and duplicitous that agency is."[1] The rhetorical device was applied immediately: reporting that the Biden administration was moving toward lifting its de facto ban on American military contractors deploying to Ukraine was recast as the State Department "using their mercenaries to attack Russia and destabilize America."[1]

## Claims about ISIS and drug trafficking

The same take advanced the claim that [[isis|ISIS]] is not a genuinely Islamist organization but a mercenary force. The author reported interviewing two veterans of High-Value Target teams in Afghanistan and Iraq who said that in ten years of raiding ISIS safe houses they never found a Quran or Islamic prayer materials, but did find western pornography, video game consoles and DVDs that would be difficult to obtain in remote parts of the Middle East without western friends.[1] [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] was cited as having said on multiple occasions that ISIS fighters are highly paid mercenaries rather than religious zealots.[1]

A companion item on the collapse of Afghan opium production extended the argument to narcotics, asserting that the agency invaded Vietnam to protect a cartel drug trade and, after 9/11, took Afghanistan to seize the opium fields and drive up production — a chain the Brief linked to the opioid crisis and the [[sackler-family|Sackler Family]].[1] The piece concluded that the agency had waged kinetic war against the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] for years and now appeared prepared to wage it against the American people on U.S. soil.[1]

## Flynn collusion allegation (December 2025)

On December 21, 2025, former National Security Adviser [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] alleged that the CIA was colluding with European intelligence services to undermine President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s efforts to broker peace in Ukraine, writing that the agency is "in cahoots with [[mi6|MI6]] and others in the EU intelligence community."[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that major voices in the movement had long ignored that the CIA "represents a clear and present danger not only to world peace, but to the security of the American People."[2] He argued the CIA, working with foreign partners most likely including MI6 and [[mossad|Mossad]], was the most likely culprit behind the failed assassination attempt against Trump, named the intelligence community as the most likely suspect in the Charlie Kirk assassination, and asserted that the agencies control the mainstream media and use it as a propaganda tool.[2] He framed the emergence of Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] and Flynn as public critics as a turning point, alleging that the agencies want wars with Russia, Venezuela and Iran.[2]

## Authorization of covert action in Venezuela (November 2025)

In November 2025 The New York Times reported that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had signed off on C.I.A. plans for covert measures inside [[venezuela|Venezuela]], operations described by people briefed on the matter as potentially meant to prepare a battlefield for further action.[3] The move came with the largest U.S. aircraft carrier positioned in the Caribbean and coincided with authorization of back-channel negotiations in which President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] reportedly offered a delayed departure, an offer the [[white-house|White House]] rejected.[3] It was not clear what the covert actions might be or when they might be carried out; with ground combat forces not yet authorized, the next phase of the pressure campaign was expected to involve sabotage or cyber, psychological or information operations.[3]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] questioned the premise that a president would publicly announce authorization of covert C.I.A. operations and still expect them to succeed, proposing instead that Trump may have been openly warning Maduro that an agency operation was in the works.[3]
