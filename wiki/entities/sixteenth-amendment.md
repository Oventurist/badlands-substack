# Sixteenth Amendment

The Sixteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution was ratified in 1913. According to the article, the amendment was part of the cabal's master plan to permanently consolidate control over the American people. The source states that after the ratification of the Sixteenth Amendment, Democratic leaders agreed to seek passage of the Revenue Act of 1913, which would dramatically lower tariffs and implement an income tax.

The article links the Sixteenth Amendment directly to the Federal Reserve Act, noting that both were passed in 1913 as complementary components of the cabal's plan. It claims that Representative Oscar Underwood quickly moved the Revenue Act through the House of Representatives, with Senate approval following extensive lobbying by the Wilson administration.

The source presents the Sixteenth Amendment as one of the two great consolidations of 1913, alongside the Federal Reserve Act, that "successfully enslaved every American to the fiat monetary system." It asserts that after 1913, every American had to pay income tax and faced continued devaluation of their money.

## In "A Week to Remember"

The essay [[a-week-to-remember|A Week to Remember]] refers to the 1913 amendment as the mechanism by which income taxation was imposed against the founders' intent: "Our founding fathers viewed income tax as being unconstitutional. Politicians had to later amend the Constitution to start taxing Americans' incomes in 1913."

The article treats the amendment as one leg of a tripod erected in a single year, alongside the creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] and the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]], and argues that even setting the constitutional question aside, the resulting regime cannot justify taxing "the same dollar being taxed multiple times" through income, property, sales, and capital gains levies.
