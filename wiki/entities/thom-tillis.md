# Thom Tillis

Thom Tillis is a Republican United States Senator from North Carolina and a member of the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]].[1]

## Confrontation with Kristi Noem

At the committee's March 3, 2026 oversight hearing, Tillis delivered the most fiery Republican remarks against Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]], telling her that he was giving her a performance review and that she was not permitted to speak in response.[1] He called for her resignation, citing misplaced priorities at the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] amid criticism of its $220 million advertising campaign and a $143 million no-bid contract.[1]

Badlands contributor Ashe in America observed that Tillis, whose Senate career is ending, was "going all out," and that his remarks repeatedly included criticism directed at [[donald-trump|Trump]] allies such as Stephen Miller and Corey Lewandowski. The author attributed some of the animus to Tillis's earlier public falling-out with the President.[1]

## Resistance to the 2026 agenda pace

In June 2026, Tillis was among the Senate Republicans reported to be in tension with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over the pace and strategy of advancing the administration's 2026 agenda.[2] Alongside [[john-cornyn|John Cornyn]] and [[bill-cassidy|Bill Cassidy]], he pushed back on expectations that senators approve proposals without changes or extended debate, citing Senate procedures and narrow majorities, while Majority Leader [[john-thune|John Thune]] emphasized working through the legislative process.[2] The senators involved were nonetheless described as generally supportive of many of Trump's policy goals.[2]

## Role in the Warsh confirmation standoff

In March 2026, Tillis was reported by [[axios|Axios]] to be involved in discussions about the nomination and confirmation dynamics surrounding [[kevin-warsh|Kevin Warsh]], whom President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was expected to nominate as Chair of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] in succession to [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]].[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described Tillis as functioning as Powell's political "security detail," refusing to confirm Powell's successor in the Senate unless the administration dropped its criminal case against the outgoing chair — a posture she framed as suspicious in itself.[3] She linked this to unsealed court filings indicating Powell might remain on the Fed's board of governors past the expiration of his chairmanship while the investigation stayed open, raising the possibility of coordination between Powell and certain senators.[3]

## Opposition to the Powell criminal probe

In January 2026, after the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] opened a criminal investigation into [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] over his congressional testimony concerning renovations to the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] headquarters, Tillis publicly criticized the probe.[4] He said he would oppose confirmation of new Federal Reserve nominees for as long as the investigation remained ongoing, vowing to withhold consent "until this legal matter is fully resolved."[4]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted the irony that Tillis's threat would, if carried out, leave the Fed "rudderless and incapacitated," an outcome congenial to advocates of ending the central bank altogether.[4] She further argued that Tillis was undermining his own credibility, since Powell was alleged to have lied under oath to Congress and Tillis — a member of the very branch said to have been deceived — was treating the alleged deception as immaterial.[4] Her posed question was whether the stance reflected [[stockholm-syndrome|Stockholm Syndrome]] or "brazen corruption."[4]


## Opposition to Ed Martin (2025)

In May 2025 Tillis said he had informed the [[white-house|White House]] that he would oppose [[ed-martin|Ed Martin]]'s nomination as U.S. attorney for the District of Columbia, a potentially fatal blow to Martin's confirmation prospects.[5] The North Carolinian, who was seeking reelection the following year, had previously signaled concerns about Martin's comments related to the January 6, 2021 Capitol attack and his work defending those who took part in the riot.[5] "I have no tolerance for anybody that entered the building on Jan. 6, and that's probably where most of the friction was," Tillis told reporters after meeting with Martin.[5]
