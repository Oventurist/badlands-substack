# Sergei Shoigu

Sergei Shoigu is the Secretary of the Russian Security Council and a senior figure in the defense establishment of [[russia|Russia]]. In 2026 he served as Moscow's principal interlocutor with the government of [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] on security and reconstruction matters.[1]

## Role in Russia–Afghanistan military cooperation

On May 27, 2026, Shoigu met Afghan Defense Minister [[mohammad-yaqub|Mohammad Yaqub]] at an international security forum in the Moscow region, a meeting that preceded the signing of a military-technical cooperation agreement between the two governments, as reported by [[interfax|Interfax]].[1] Following the meeting, Shoigu argued that Western countries should unfreeze Afghan assets and accept responsibility for post-conflict reconstruction in Afghanistan.[1]

Interfax also reported that on May 14, 2026, Shoigu said Russia had established a working dialogue with the [[taliban|Taliban]] and was developing what he described as a full partnership with Kabul.[1] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read this as the formalization of security ties that began with the Taliban's high-profile 2024 appearance at the St. Petersburg International Economic Forum under President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[1]

## Tehran mission, August 2024

In August 2024, Shoigu — described as a senior ally of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] — travelled to Tehran to deliver a message from the Russian president to Iran's supreme leader [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]] urging a restrained response to Israel's suspected killing of [[hamas|Hamas]] leader [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]], and advising against attacks on Israeli civilians.[2] Two senior Iranian sources described the message, delivered on a Monday in meetings with top Iranian officials, to Reuters.[2]

During the same visit, Tehran pressed Moscow for delivery of Russian-made Sukhoi Su-35 fighter jets.[2] The Kremlin did not respond to a request for comment, though state-run RIA reported that Shoigu said he had discussed Haniyeh's killing while in Tehran.[2] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the mission as advice to [[iran|Iran]] to stand down and wait, on the theory that an Iranian strike was precisely what [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] needed to rally collapsing domestic support.[2]

## August 2023 Moscow security conference remarks

Speaking on August 15, 2023 to a security conference in Moscow attended by China's defence minister Li Shangfu, Russian Defence Minister Shoigu, then 68, said Ukraine's "military resources are almost exhausted" and that the war had exposed vulnerabilities in Western weapons systems that Moscow would soon share.[3] "In the special military operation, the Russian army has debunked many myths about the superiority of Western military standards," he said in a rare public speech, offering no detailed evidence for either claim.[3] Shoigu, described as one of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s most powerful allies, called the conflict a serious test for Russia; Reuters was refused accreditation to attend the conference without explanation.[3]

## May 2024 move to the Security Council

In a cabinet reshuffle announced in May 2024, Putin moved Shoigu out of the Defense Ministry and installed him as head of Russia's Security Council, displacing [[nikolai-patrushev|Nikolai Patrushev]], while economist [[andrey-belousov|Andrey Belousov]] took over as defense minister.[4] The shuffle was unexpected and registered as a surprise in European capitals and Washington, where the [[state-department|State Department]] spokesman [[vedant-patel|Vedant Patel]] framed it as "further indication of Putin's desperation to sustain his war of aggression against Ukraine."[4] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] disputed that reading, arguing Shoigu had in effect been promoted into a post closer to Putin rather than demoted, and that Belousov's elevation signaled a Russian doctrine in which economic defense is national defense after two years of Western sanctions.[4]

## ICC arrest warrant

In 2024 the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] issued arrest warrants for senior Russian military figures including Shoigu, then the former Defense Minister, and General [[valery-gerasimov|Valery Gerasimov]], accusing them of attacking civilian infrastructure in Ukraine.[5] The warrants followed the court's 2023 warrants against President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Children's Rights Commissioner [[maria-lvova-belova|Maria Lvova-Belova]].[5] Moscow does not recognize the court's jurisdiction and has declared the warrants null and void.[5]

## Federation Council confirmation of the 2024 replacement

On Sunday evening, 12 May 2024, the [[federation-council|Federation Council]] announced that President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had proposed replacing Shoigu as Russian defense minister with acting First Deputy Prime Minister [[andrey-belousov|Andrey Belousov]], with Shoigu appointed secretary of the [[russian-security-council|Russian Security Council]].[8] Senators were scheduled to consult on the president's nominees in committee sessions on 13 May and at a Federation Council meeting on 14 May, according to the upper house of the Russian parliament.[8] No further alterations were made to Putin's slate of cabinet candidates, which included [[vladimir-kolokoltsev|Vladimir Kolokoltsev]] for interior minister, Alexander Kurenkov for minister of emergency situations, [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] for foreign minister and Konstantin Chuichenko for justice minister, while [[denis-manturov|Denis Manturov]] was nominated for first deputy prime minister.[8]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] dismissed Western predictions that the swap of Putin's long-time friend would destabilize Russia, stressing instead that Belousov had virtually no defense experience and was an economist with a long record in the Russian banking sector.[8] From an asymmetric-warfare perspective, the commentary argued that Russia was in firm control of the kinetic situation in the Donbas and Ukraine and that Putin was signaling he regarded the Russian economy itself as a matter of national defense, preparing for the next leg of asymmetric conflict.[8]

## March 2023 deconfliction call with Lloyd Austin

On March 15, 2023, Shoigu took a phone call from U.S. Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] regarding the previous day's crash of a U.S. [[mq-9-reaper|MQ-9 Reaper]] drone over the Black Sea — the first such contact between the two defense chiefs since October.[6] Austin publicly characterized the exchange as an effort at deconfliction in which both sides sought to underscore that they were not seeking a fight, while restating that the United States "will continue to fly and to operate wherever international law allows."[6] See [[2023-black-sea-drone-incident|the 2023 Black Sea drone incident]].[6]

## August 2024 mission to Tehran

On Monday, August 5, 2024, as the Middle East approached what was described as the "zero hour" of [[iran|Iran]]'s expected major retaliation against [[israel|Israel]], Shoigu led a Russian defence delegation in Tehran while a United States defence delegation was in Tel Aviv.[7] In his capacity as secretary of Russia's national Security Council, and as the country's former defence minister, he met senior Iranian military and security officials as well as the newly sworn-in president [[masoud-pezeshkian|Masoud Pezeshkian]].[7] The RIA Novosti news agency described the talks as focused on "strengthening bilateral cooperation in a wide range of spheres including security," and the visit coincided with speculation that Russia was actively helping the Islamic Republic thwart potential Israeli attacks.[7] Russia had strongly condemned the killing of [[hamas|Hamas]] leader [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]] in Tehran the previous Wednesday, an act Iran treated as tantamount to assassinating a head of state on an official visit, and Pezeshkian hailed Moscow as a "valued strategic ally."[7]

Badlands Media commentary observed that Shoigu had served as [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s defence minister for twelve years before being replaced in May 2024, and argued the change reflected not incompetence but a shift in strategy — bringing in an economist to wage irregular warfare against [[nato|NATO]] — which would explain Shoigu's continuing prominent role in what the outlet calls the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]]'s operations.[7] With Shoigu advising the Iranians in Tehran and General [[michael-kurilla|Michael Kurilla]] of [[centcom|CENTCOM]] advising the Israelis in Tel Aviv, the commentary concluded that the Ukrainian and Israeli wars had become one and the same.[7] It also questioned whether Shoigu would encourage Iran to engage U.S. forces directly, noting a reported drone attack on a U.S.-occupied base in Iraq and Kremlin-released video of [[isis|ISIS]] recruiters arrested inside Russia who had allegedly recruited at least nineteen Russians to fight in Syria.[7]
