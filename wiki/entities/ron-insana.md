# Ron Insana

**Ron Insana** is an American financial journalist and commentator who writes and appears for [[cnbc|CNBC]].[1]

## Commentary on Fed rate cuts, February 2024

In a February 5, 2024 CNBC column, Insana argued that the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] might take a slow approach to cutting interest rates but that the United States still had "a good economy."[1] He wrote that financial markets, especially in the US, behave like "voracious beasts" that never appear satisfied even when they get what they want.[1]

Insana noted that Fed chairman [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]], in his *60 Minutes* interview aired that Sunday, had confirmed what markets already knew: the Fed was pivoting toward cutting rates — not in March but later in the year — with roughly three reductions expected in 2024.[1] Powell told correspondent Scott Pelley that "all but a couple of our participants do believe it will be appropriate to, for us to begin to dial back the restrictive stance by cutting rates this year," calling it the base case while the committee picked the right time.[1] Insana pointed to continued labor-market strength, solid consumer spending, and a beginning rebound in residential real estate, arguing that both Wall Street and Main Street should be celebrating the Fed all but declaring victory in the post-pandemic war on inflation, despite Monday's jump in interest rates and the S&P 500's pullback from record levels.[1]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the same coverage as evidence that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was executing a "bluff charge" on the central bank, arguing that a near-term cut would produce a temporary surface-level boom followed by severe inflation, while delay would invite accusations of election interference and holding or raising rates would squeeze the banking system — a "pincer move."[1]
