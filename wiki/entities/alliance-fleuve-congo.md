# Alliance Fleuve Congo

The Alliance Fleuve Congo (AFC), also rendered in English as the Congo River Alliance, is a rebel coalition fighting the government of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Its membership includes the powerful [[m23|M23]] armed force, and its president is Bertrand Bisimwa.[1]

## Withdrawal from the Luanda talks

In March 2025 the AFC withdrew from peace talks that had been scheduled to take place in Angola, blaming the [[european-union|European Union]] for the collapse of the process.[1] Brussels had announced sanctions against five senior rebel leaders — including Bisimwa — for "serious human rights violations and abuses," with the measures enacted on the eve of the Luanda discussions.[1]

In a statement, the coalition said: "Successive sanctions imposed on our members, including those enacted on the eve of the Luanda discussions, severely undermine direct dialogue and make any progress impossible. Under these circumstances, the talks have become impracticable. Consequently, our organization can no longer continue to participate in the discussions."[1] The AFC accused the EU of deliberately attempting to undermine peace efforts in the DR Congo and to obstruct "much-anticipated talks."[1]

The M23 component of the coalition had planned direct negotiations with the Congolese government to end the conflict in the country's east.[1] Alongside the withdrawal, [[rwanda|Rwanda]] announced it was cutting diplomatic ties with its former colonial ruler Belgium, with Kigali accusing Brussels of attempting to "sustain its neo-colonial delusions."[1]

## Casualties and stakes

According to official figures cited in reporting, at least 8,500 people — including children and international peacekeepers — had been killed in the Central African nation since clashes broke out between the rebels and Congolese forces at the beginning of 2025, escalating a decades-old conflict.[1]

Badlands commentary argued that Rwandan President [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]] was using the EU sanctions as a pretext to walk away from talks, while Rwanda itself funded the M23 forces seizing border towns containing the processing plants for rare earth minerals mined in the region.[1] Those minerals are ultimately purchased by major corporations including Apple, Sony and Lockheed Martin, and the unmined rare earth deposits of the eastern DRC were estimated to be worth in excess of $24 trillion.[1] The commentary linked the loss of a globalist monopoly over these resources to the freezing of Israeli oligarch [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]]'s assets under Executive Order 13818 in December 2017.[1]
