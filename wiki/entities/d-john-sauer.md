# D. John Sauer

D. John Sauer is the Solicitor General of the United States in the second Trump administration, representing the federal government before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[1]

## Independent board removal cases

In April 2025 Sauer filed an emergency application asking the Supreme Court to block district court decisions that had restored [[cathy-harris|Cathy Harris]] to the [[merit-systems-protection-board|Merit Systems Protection Board]] and [[gwynne-wilcox|Gwynne Wilcox]] to the [[national-labor-relations-board|National Labor Relations Board]], contending that the orders harmed the president and the separation of powers.[1] He wrote that "the president should not be forced to delegate his executive power to agency heads who are demonstrably at odds with the administration's policy objectives for a single day — much less for the months that it would likely take for the courts to resolve this litigation."[1] Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]] granted an administrative stay the same day.[1]

## TikTok filing before the Supreme Court

In late December 2024, as one of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s personal appellate attorneys, Sauer authored the president-elect's filing asking the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] to delay the deadline for the potential [[tiktok-ban|TikTok ban]], which was set for the day before the inauguration.[2] He wrote that "President Trump alone possesses the consummate dealmaking expertise, the electoral mandate, and the political will to negotiate a resolution to save the platform while addressing the national security concerns," and that Trump "opposes banning TikTok in the United States at this juncture" and seeks to resolve the matter through political means once in office.[2] As Trump's nominee for U.S. solicitor general, Sauer would take over managing the government's defense of the ban from the Biden administration.[2]

## Border crossings case (2025)

As U.S. Solicitor General, Sauer asked the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] in July 2025 to review a [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-ninth-circuit|Ninth Circuit]] ruling on the processing of asylum seekers, and the justices granted review on November 17, 2025.[3] Echoing Judge Bress's dissent, Sauer told the Court that the Ninth Circuit's decision "defies the plain text of the governing statutes," adding that "in ordinary English, a person 'arrives in' a country only when he comes within its borders. An alien thus does not 'arrive in' the United States while he is still in Mexico."[3]

## Testimony before the weaponization subcommittee (2023)

As special assistant attorney general for Louisiana and an attorney for the plaintiffs in [[missouri-v-biden|Missouri v. Biden]], Sauer testified in July 2023 before the House [[house-weaponization-committee|Select Subcommittee on the Weaponization of the Federal Government]] at a hearing on federal censorship of Americans.[4]

## Venezuelan Temporary Protected Status application (2025)

In May 2025, as Solicitor General, Sauer asked the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] to end the legal [[temporary-protected-status|temporary protected status]] of more than 300,000 Venezuelan migrants, a step that would make it easier for the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] to deport them.[5] The request followed Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]]'s earlier announcement that she would not renew the protection, which allows certain migrants to remain in the country legally when they cannot return home because of natural disasters, armed conflicts, or other extraordinary events.[5]

## Birthright citizenship oral argument (May 2025)

As Solicitor General, Sauer argued the Trump administration's case before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] in May 2025 on the power of lower courts to issue [[nationwide-injunctions|universal injunctions]], a dispute arising from three national injunctions blocking [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s executive order on [[birthright-citizenship|birthright citizenship]].[6] Justice [[elena-kagan|Elena Kagan]] pressed him on how the high court would manage lower-court challenges without universal injunctions and told him the administration had been "losing uniformly" on the substantive question, remarking, "If I were in your shoes, there's no way I'd approach the court with this case!"[6]
