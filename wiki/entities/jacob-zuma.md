# Jacob Zuma

Jacob Zuma is a former president of South Africa and former leader of the [[african-national-congress|African National Congress]] (ANC) who, at age 82, returned to electoral politics at the head of a new party ahead of the May 29, 2024 South African general election.[1]

## Return to politics and the MKP

After evading prison by claiming terminal illness, Zuma formed the Umkhonto we Sizwe party (MKP).[1] Badlands commentary characterized his re-entry as a successful insurgency against [[cyril-ramaphosa|Cyril Ramaphosa]], who now leads Zuma's former party, the ANC.[1] The MKP appeared to have formed an alliance with another newly emerged party, the Economic Freedom Fighters (EFF), led by ANC defector [[julius-malema|Julius Malema]].[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Writing as [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], the Badlands contributor had earlier described the "Sovereign Alliance" expelling what he terms the Globalist Empire from South Africa, and the Empire's effort to retake control through its agent Zuma; following the 2024 election result he assessed that, for the time being, the Empire had succeeded.[1] Zuma's corrupt past had been detailed in previous briefs, and he was described as presenting himself as a white-collar criminal in contrast to Malema's more overtly warlord-like archetype.[1] The commentary noted that the MKP and EFF branded themselves as pro-multipolar and aligned with Beijing and Moscow while simultaneously advocating seizure of white-owned land and nationalization of mines and banks — positions read as a subversion of the Sovereign Alliance's years-long effort to legally usurp mining rights from the Empire.[1]

## Corruption record and the arms deal

In 2002, while serving as deputy president of the [[african-national-congress|African National Congress]], Zuma was identified by South African authorities as a target of a corruption investigation into the high-profile 1999 Strategic Defense Procurement Package that equipped [[south-africa|South Africa]] with weaponry.[2] Investigators alleged the deal ended up costing upward of 70 billion rand despite a contract amount of only 30 billion.[2] Zuma was dismissed from his position in 2005 after a close associate who had wired money to him was convicted in the case, and was subsequently indicted, though those charges were later dropped.[2] He was indicted again in 2007 — one week after being elected president of the ANC — on 12 counts of racketeering and money-laundering, but a high court judge ruled the charges invalid on the grounds that Zuma had not been consulted before the indictments were issued.[2] As a result he served simultaneously as executive president of South Africa and as president of the ANC from 2008 until 2018, resigning upon the creation of the [[zondo-commission|Zondo Commission]] as public dissent over his corruption reached a fever pitch.[2]

The commission's inquiry centred on Zuma's "state capture" of the South African government, which some 300 witnesses testified had been facilitated in large part by the [[gupta-brothers|Gupta brothers]] through their media companies and illegally procured state contracts.[2] Zuma evaded his prison sentence by claiming terminal illness.[2] The United States Treasury's October 2019 sanctioning of the Gupta family under [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]], announced by Secretary of State [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]], specifically cited the family's corrupt dealings with Zuma.[2]

## 2024 comeback

Ahead of South Africa's May 2024 election, the 82-year-old Zuma leveraged the anti-apartheid past to rally support against the ANC through the [[umkhonto-we-sizwe-party|MK party]], named after the ANC's old armed wing — a party he built despite once claiming the ANC would rule until "Jesus comes back."[2] In the KwaZulu-Natal heartland of Zuma support, longtime ANC voters such as cab driver Dumisani Ndlovu switched allegiance, telling the Associated Press, "They think they have finished him, but we are with him all the way. The ANC will pay."[2] Even being barred from the election as a parliamentary candidate over a recent conviction did not blunt his influence.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the comeback as the Hegemony's attempt to retake control of South Africa, noting that Zuma was challenging [[cyril-ramaphosa|Cyril Ramaphosa]], the man who assumed the presidency on his departure and who has since worked with the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[2] He argued that the Associated Press feature's failure to mention the Gupta brothers even once was "a major indicator that Zuma is the hegemony's insurgent."[2] He further noted that the day after Pompeo announced the Gupta sanctions in October 2019, an article appeared detailing [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s campaign to establish economic and military ties with African nations in coordination with [[china|China]] and the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]], citing [[john-bolton|John Bolton]]'s alarm that Putin had become Africa's number one military supplier while acquiring mining rights long held by Western oligarchs.[2]
