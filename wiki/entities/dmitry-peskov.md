# Dmitry Peskov

Dmitry Peskov is the Kremlin spokesman for the Russian government.[1]

## July 2026 pipeline strike response

After Ukrainian drones targeted the Krasnodarskaya compressor station serving the [[blue-stream-pipeline|Blue Stream]] pipeline, Peskov condemned the attack as a threat to critical international energy infrastructure.[1] He said [[russia|Russia]] expected countries affected by the pipeline, including [[turkey|Türkiye]], to respond to the strike.[1] The operator, [[gazprom|Gazprom]], reported only minor damage and uninterrupted deliveries.[1]

Peskov's statement came amid an escalating exchange in which [[ukraine|Ukraine]] expanded long-range attacks on Russian energy infrastructure while Russia continued missile and drone strikes on Ukrainian cities and energy facilities.[1]

## Statement on Iran's new Supreme Leader (2026)

In July 2026, Russian state media reported Peskov saying that Vladimir Putin currently had no plans to speak by telephone with [[iran|Iran]]'s Ayatollah, despite no world leader having yet spoken with the new Supreme Leader.[2] The Badlands Brief paired that statement with Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]]'s admission that he had never met [[mojtaba-khamenei|Mojtaba Khamenei]] in person, using the two to question whether the figure exists at all.[2]

## Warning over Bushehr strikes (April 2026)

In April 2026 Peskov warned that Israeli attacks on the [[bushehr-nuclear-power-plant|Bushehr nuclear power plant]] — a facility constructed and maintained by [[russia|Russia]] — could have irrevocable consequences.[3] The statement was one of several from senior Russian officials warning [[israel|Israel]] during the [[iran-war|Iran war]], alongside [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]]'s criticism of the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] and [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]]'s demand that Israel halt its attacks on [[iran|Iran]] immediately.[3]


## Response to Trump Talks Overture (January 2025)

Speaking to Russian media on January 10, 2025, Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said the Kremlin "welcomed" President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "readiness" for talks on the war in Ukraine.[4] He stated that "no conditions are required" for a meeting between Trump and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], adding that "(only) a mutual desire and political will to conduct a dialogue and resolve existing problems through dialogue is required."[4] Peskov noted that no concrete plans for a meeting yet existed and that preparations would not be drawn up until after Trump's inauguration later that month.[4]

## Response to reports of withheld US intelligence (March 2024)

After The New York Times reported in March 2024 that the United States had not shared all of the information it possessed about a terrorist plot in Russia ahead of the [[crocus-city-hall-attack|Crocus City Hall attack]] outside Moscow, Peskov said he was unaware of information about Washington withholding intelligence and cast doubt on the report.[5] "The information of The New York Times, citing sources, is information that should be treated with great caution," he said.[5]

## Possible Putin–Trump meeting in Beijing (July 2025)

On Monday, July 21, 2025, Peskov stated that Russia is ready to discuss holding talks between Presidents [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] should Trump attend commemorative events in Beijing in September.[6] The Chinese capital was scheduled to host a military parade on September 3 marking the 80th anniversary of the end of the Second World War, an event Putin had already accepted an invitation to attend.[6] Speculation grew over a possible meeting involving Putin, Trump and Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]]; Japan's Kyodo News reported that the Chinese government had decided to invite Trump, while The Times suggested Beijing was positioning itself to host a trilateral summit, though Chinese officials did not confirm the reports.[6] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that in the span of exactly two days Peskov moved from full denial of any meeting to all but announcing one.[6]

## Denial of Ukraine Peace-Feeler Reports (January 2024)

On Friday, January 26, 2024, Peskov responded to a Bloomberg report claiming that officials working for [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had used diplomatic back-channels in December to "put out feelers" to senior Biden administration officials about ending the war in Ukraine.[7] Speaking at a press briefing, Peskov said of the report: "That's false information, it's completely untrue."[7] The denial, issued on behalf of the [[kremlin|Kremlin]], also implicitly rejected earlier New York Times reporting from December 2023 that Putin was "quietly signaling" a desire for a ceasefire.[7]

## Comparison of the EU to a criminal gang (October 2025)

Commenting on deliberations at the informal [[european-union|EU]] summit in Copenhagen over a €140 billion loan to Ukraine backed by Russia's immobilized central bank assets, Peskov said the countries debating ways they could "steal" Russian assets were behaving like a criminal "gang," likening the bloc to a group of mobsters planning their next heist.[8] His remarks followed warnings from Belgium's [[bart-de-wever|Bart De Wever]] — whose country hosts [[euroclear|Euroclear]] — that "there's no free money" and that participating states should sign on to shared responsibility "if it goes wrong."[8]

## Statement on US-Iran talks (March 2025)

On March 4, 2025, TASS reported Peskov telling Bloomberg that Moscow believes Washington and Tehran should settle all their differences through talks and is ready to contribute to that end.[9] "Russia believes that the United States and Iran should resolve all problems through negotiations," he said, adding that Moscow "is ready to do everything in its power to achieve this."[9] The statement appeared to corroborate reporting that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had agreed to a mediating role; see [[russian-mediation-of-us-iran-nuclear-talks|Russian mediation of US–Iran nuclear talks]].[9]

## Response to Trump's Ukraine ultimatum (January 2025)

On Thursday, January 23, 2025, Peskov dismissed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s threats of sanctions and tariffs over a Ukraine peace deal as "nothing new," after Trump had issued a thinly veiled ultimatum the previous day calling on Moscow to end the conflict and strike a "deal" or face new sanctions.[10] Peskov told reporters that Russia remained open to "equal and respectful dialogue" with the United States, noting that Trump had engaged in that kind of exchange with President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] during his first term, and that Russia was waiting for similar signals but had not seen any so far.[10] "Trump in his first term was the US president most frequently resorting to sanctions. He likes this method," Peskov added.[10]

Addressing the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] by video link the same day, Trump said he would "very much like" to meet his Russian counterpart "soon" to end the conflict; he had campaigned on resolving it swiftly and, in the weeks before his inauguration, adjusted the timeline to hoping to negotiate peace within six months.[10] Russia repeatedly stated throughout the conflict that it was ready for peace negotiations at any time while accusing Kiev of refusing to talk.[10] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the exchange as Trump rapidly expanding the Overton window around the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] concept.[10]

## Call for caution on nuclear rhetoric, August 2025

On 4 August 2025 Peskov urged caution in the use of "nuclear rhetoric" amid inflammatory language from former Russian president [[dmitry-medvedev|Dmitry Medvedev]] and pushback from U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[11] "Russia is very attentive to the topic of nuclear non-proliferation. And we believe that everyone should be very, very cautious with nuclear rhetoric," he told Russian state media.[11]

The remarks followed Medvedev's suggestion that greater U.S. involvement in ending Russia's war against Ukraine could bring Washington and Moscow closer to direct conflict, and Trump's response ordering two U.S. nuclear-powered submarines repositioned to "appropriate regions."[11] Trump had set an 8 August deadline for Russia to reach a ceasefire with Ukraine, failing which Moscow would face U.S. secondary sanctions potentially imposing tariffs on its trading partners including Brazil, China and India.[11]

## Black Sea tanker attacks (December 2025)

Speaking to journalists on Monday, December 1, 2025, Peskov called Ukraine's attacks on commercial tankers in the Black Sea an "outrageous" infringement of Turkish sovereignty.[12] His comments followed strikes by explosives-laden sea drones on two Gambian-flagged tankers, the Kairos and the Virat, which were sailing off the Turkish coast en route to the Russian port of Novorossiysk, and a further drone attack on a crude hub on Russia's Black Sea coast belonging to the [[caspian-pipeline-consortium|Caspian Pipeline Consortium]].[12] Peskov said the tanker attacks were a direct violation of the rights of the vessels' owners and an encroachment on Turkish sovereignty, and noted that the Kremlin viewed the incidents as serious with possible implications for ongoing diplomatic efforts.[12]

## April 2025 remarks on Putin and Trump

In an interview with the French magazine Le Point published in April 2025, Peskov said [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] respects [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] because he sees him as a strong personality who is nonetheless open to productive dialogue.[13] He stressed that while the two leaders share positions on some matters, differences remain on many others, as "each defends his country's interests above all else," but that both understand the importance of diplomacy.[13] "What unites them is that they both understand the absurdity of refusing dialogue. What unites them is their willingness to resolve the most difficult issues through discussion. Exactly what was missing with the previous Biden administration," Peskov said.[13] Reflecting on Trump's first term, including two impeachments, the spokesman attributed his comeback to his being "a powerful political leader" with "a very strong personality."[13]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the remarks as signal from the [[kremlin|Kremlin]] that Trump would side with [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] in an unfolding feud against [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]], [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and the [[neoconservatives|NeoCons]], noting that critics accused the businessmen-turned-envoys Witkoff and [[adam-boehler|Adam Boehler]] of "legitimizing" adversaries by meeting them face to face, while the envoys maintained they could not negotiate without looking counterparts in the eye.[13]
