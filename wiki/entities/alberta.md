# Alberta

Alberta is the western Canadian province whose political identity, energy resources and autonomy movement made it a recurring reference point in U.S.–Canada tensions during January 2026.[1]

## "Natural partner" remarks at Davos

Speaking at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in January 2026, U.S. Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] suggested that Alberta could serve as a "natural partner" for the United States, referencing ongoing discussions about the province's political identity and autonomy.[1] The remark reverberated well beyond Europe, coming amid a broader dispute over tariffs, [[greenland|Greenland]] and Canada's trade posture toward China.[1]

## Badlands framing

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited Alberta as one of the causes she has long championed, writing that she has advocated "for the truckers and for Alberta and for liberation" in Canada.[1] In her framing, Canada functions as a colony of the British Crown and the face of globalism in North America, and liberating it from that arrangement serves both American and Canadian interests — with Alberta's autonomy movement forming part of that project.[1]

## Alaska–Alberta rail advocacy

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that the 2,570 km [[alaska-canada-railway|Alaska–Canada rail connection]] would move freight, oil, grains and other goods from Anchorage to the Yukon, the Northwest Territories and northern Alberta.[2] In 2015 the Province of Alberta funded a feasibility study while, in his description, the province sat on the edge of total economic despair under the weight of decarbonization initiatives being pushed into law by Ottawa technocrats.[2] That same year the [[alaska-alberta-railway-development-corporation|Alaska-Alberta Railway Development Corporation]] (A2A) was created as a private initiative to advance the plan.[2]
