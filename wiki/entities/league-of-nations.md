# League of Nations

The **League of Nations** is cited by Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as one in a long series of post-war attempts to construct an international framework capable of preventing another plunge into war.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Place in the sequence of peace settlements

Ehret situates the League within a lineage of conferences that, in the pre-nuclear epoch, were typically arranged only after years of devastating war and bloodshed had become insufferable.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) That lineage runs from the 1648 [[peace-of-westphalia|Peace of Westphalia]], which emerged from the Thirty Years' War and established the framework of the modern sovereign nation-state system at the heart of international law, through the [[congress-of-vienna|Congress of Vienna]] of 1815 after two decades of Napoleonic wars, to the League itself, which arose in the wake of what he calls the meat grinder of the First World War.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) The sequence continues with the [[united-nations|United Nations]] and the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods System]] brought online after the Second World War.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Assessment

Ehret observes that such conferences were sometimes shaped by honest agendas and sometimes not, but that in all instances the parties agreed a new system was necessary to avoid war.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) His broader caution is that human memory is weak, and that the Byzantine web of power politics in which the Anglo-American deep state operates is not easily transformed by conferences or agreements to play nicely with neighbours.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Origins in the Versailles settlement and the entente system

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that anyone attending the League's opening conference on January 10, 1920 would not have had in mind the intrigue that produced the war, since the world was told the cause had been German imperial ambition and the nation state system itself rather than the encirclement strategy of the [[entente-cordiale|Entente Cordiale]].[2] Amid what he calls a frenzy of looting under the [[treaty-of-versailles|Versailles Treaty]], the League's acolytes demanded that the world learn that if nation states were permitted to exist such wars would plague humanity forever, and that the solution was the dissolution of sovereign nation states, enforced through Articles 10 and 16 of the League Covenant — described as precursors to NATO's Article 5 collective security pact.[2]

## Resistance and collapse

A return to sanity under [[warren-harding|Warren Harding]] (1921–23) brought the United States into a hostile relationship with the League and its Round Table affiliates within the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] and Wall Street, and Harding pursued bilateral agreements with Austria, Germany, Hungary, Russia and China outside the League's authority.[2] Many nations in the 1920s shared this mistrust of the new supranational organization, seeing it clearly as cover for a new [[british-empire|British Empire]], and the League was never permitted to acquire the teeth one-world government advocates desired.[2] From 1921 to 1932 the body fell into disarray, and its last serious battle against nationalism died in June 1933 when [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] torpedoed the [[london-economic-conference|London Conference]] on finance and trade.[2] The League was formally dissolved just as the [[united-nations|United Nations]] came online, a coincidence of timing Ehret says has been used to induce credulous people into the provably false belief that the UN is a continuity of the League.[2]
