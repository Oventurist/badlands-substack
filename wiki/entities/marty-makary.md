# Marty Makary

Marty Makary is the Commissioner of the U.S. [[food-and-drug-administration|Food and Drug Administration]] in the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1]

## Real-time adverse event reporting

In August 2025 Makary announced that the FDA had begun "real-time reporting of adverse event data," describing the change on his X account as "radical transparency."[1] The agency characterized the move as a "significant step forward in modernizing" the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS).[1]

"Adverse event reporting should be fast, seamless and transparent," Makary said in a statement, adding that people who navigate the government's clunky adverse event reporting websites "should not have to wait months for that information to become public. We're closing that waiting period and will continue to streamline the process from start to finish."[1] The FDA operates under the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]], led by Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], and defines an adverse event as "any undesirable experience associated with the use of a medical product in a patient."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that it would be a revelation for ordinary Americans to learn that FDA adverse event data was not already reported in real time, questioning how an agency costing Americans billions annually — around $60 billion over the previous decade by conservative estimates — could lack such data while the public is told vaccines are known safe and effective because of the FDA.[1] She argued that accurate health data would accelerate the [[make-america-healthy-again|MAHA]] movement.[1]

## Nomination to lead the FDA (November 2024)

President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced late on Friday, November 22, 2024 that he had selected Makary, a Johns Hopkins gastrointestinal surgeon who had criticized the Biden administration's COVID-19 response, to lead the [[food-and-drug-administration|Food and Drug Administration]].[2] "FDA has lost the trust of Americans, and has lost sight of its primary goal as a regulator," Trump said, adding that Makary would "course-correct" the agency if confirmed.[2] Trump specified that Makary would work under the leadership of [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] to "properly evaluate harmful chemicals poisoning our Nation's food supply and drugs and biologics being given to our Nation's youth, so that we can finally address the Childhood Chronic Disease Epidemic."[2]

Makary had emerged during the pandemic as a critic of the FDA — first over how long the agency took to review the data leading to approval of the Pfizer-BioNTech vaccine, and then for declining to revisit recommendations for children in light of the risk of a rare heart condition in young males linked to the shot.[2] His suggestion that the agency slow-walked the first COVID vaccines in order to undermine Trump's first term drew fierce pushback from agency leaders; four years later he stood to be rewarded for his vocal support of the president-elect's pandemic response.[2] He had also advised the first Trump White House.[2]

Politico assessed that while Makary's record might not endear him to all Senate Democrats, his selection could quell fears of an anti-vaccine cloud over the agency, since he had advocated nuanced federal recommendations — focusing on older and high-risk individuals and considering a single-dose mRNA regimen for healthy children.[2] "I'm pro-vaccine. But the issue of the appropriate clinical indication of the [Covid] vaccine is not an all-or-nothing phenomenon, as we frequently see in American culture and politics," he wrote in an August 2021 op-ed for U.S. News & World Report.[2]
