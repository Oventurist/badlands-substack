# Michelle Lujan Grisham

Michelle Lujan Grisham is a Democratic politician serving as Governor of New Mexico.[1]

In September 2023 she issued an emergency health order that banned legal gun owners from carrying weapons in and around Albuquerque and throughout Bernalillo County for at least 30 days, saying she acted in response to recent shootings including the death of an 11-year-old boy.[1] The order drew a lawsuit challenging its constitutionality and was publicly refused by Bernalillo County District Attorney [[sam-bregman|Sam Bregman]], Albuquerque Mayor Tim Keller and Police Chief Harold Medina.[1] Commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] labelled her a "tyrant governor" whose Second Amendment restrictions were obviously unconstitutional.[1] The episode is treated in detail at [[new-mexico-public-health-order-suspending-concealed-carry]].[1]

## Impeachment calls and refusal to enforce (September 2023)

AP News reported that Lujan Grisham issued the emergency order on Friday, September 8, 2023, suspending the right to carry firearms in public across Albuquerque and the surrounding county for at least 30 days in response to a spate of gun violence, including the death of an 11-year-old boy outside a minor league baseball stadium that week.[2] She said she expected legal challenges but felt compelled to act, and specified that state police would enforce what amounted to civil violations.[2] Albuquerque police chief [[harold-medina|Harold Medina]] said he would not enforce the order, and Bernalillo County Sheriff [[john-allen-sheriff|John Allen]] said he was uneasy about it because it raised too many questions about constitutional rights.[2] New Mexico lawmakers responded by calling for the governor's impeachment over [[second-amendment|Second Amendment]] overreach.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the order was void under [[marbury-v-madison|Marbury v. Madison]], since all laws repugnant to the Constitution are null and void and states cannot separate citizens from their constitutional rights.[2]
