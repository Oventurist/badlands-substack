# Menendez Brothers

Erik and Lyle Menendez are brothers serving double life sentences without the possibility of parole for the 1989 murder of their parents at the family's Beverly Hills home.[1]

## Case history

The brothers have long maintained that they shot their parents in an act of self-defense stemming from years of sexual abuse at the hands of their father, José Menendez.[1] Beginning in 2023, they launched several legal avenues to regain their freedom after new and, in the words of contemporaneous reporting, compelling evidence lent significant weight to that long-standing defense.[1]

## 2025 parole hearing

California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] announced in March 2025 that Erik and Lyle Menendez would appear before the California Parole Board for a hearing on June 13, 2025.[1] Following the hearing, a three-month investigation into their conduct over decades in prison and any risk they would pose if released was to be submitted to the governor's office and to a Superior Court judge, both of which would then decide whether the brothers should be resentenced and released, granted clemency, or kept in prison.[1]

## May 2025 resentencing

On Tuesday, May 13, 2025, a Los Angeles judge resentenced the Menendez brothers to 50 years to life in prison for the 1989 murders of their parents, making them eligible for parole and revisiting a case that had captivated the public for decades.[2] Los Angeles County Superior Court Judge Michael Jesic reduced Erik and Lyle Menendez's sentences from life without parole to 50 years to life.[2] Jesic said he had given the matter "long thought" and emphasized that the two had committed an "absolutely horrific crime, and there's no way around it," but said he was moved by the brothers' community-driven works while in prison.[2] "Life without parole gives an inmate no hope, no reason to do anything good. And I give them a lot of credit. It's remarkable what they did when they had no hope of getting out," he said.[2] The brothers have admitted to killing their parents, Kitty and Jose Menendez, but claimed at the time that the killings were done in "self-defense" after years of sexual abuse.[2]
