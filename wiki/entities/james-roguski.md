# James Roguski

**James Roguski** is an American author and researcher known for his sustained analysis and criticism of the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]]'s proposed pandemic instruments — the [[who-pandemic-accord|pandemic treaty]] and the amendments to the [[international-health-regulations|International Health Regulations]].[1]

## Analysis of the WHO proposals

According to Roguski, the two proposals taken together would morph the WHO from an advisory organization into a de facto world government whose edicts would become legally binding on member states.[1] He has published extensive documentation of the proposed texts, including a widely circulated list of reasons to oppose them.[1]

Roguski's reading aligns with warnings from other analysts that ratification of either instrument, let alone both, would devastate or strip away sovereignty from nation-states and concentrate public health decision-making power in the hands of the WHO and its director-general.[1] The proposals, in this account, would greatly expand the scope and reach of the IHR, institute a system of global health certificates and "passports," and allow the WHO to mandate medical examinations, quarantine, and treatment.[1]

## Detailed documentation in "Behold, a Trojan Horse" (May 2023)

In a May 2023 Badlands Media article, "Behold, a Trojan Horse," Roguski was cited as a central analyst of what the piece termed a "Trojan Horse" for global health authority.[2] Beyond characterizing the WHO's transformation into a de facto world government, Roguski documented that the proposed documents would give the WHO power over the means of production during a declared pandemic, call for IHR infrastructure at "points of entry" such as national borders, redirect billions of dollars to what he termed the "Pharmaceutical Hospital Emergency Industrial Complex," and remove mention of "respect for dignity, human rights, and fundamental freedoms of people."[2] The article reported that Roguski had compiled a list of 307 proposed IHR amendments and a "top 10 reasons to oppose the amendments," including the shift of the WHO from advisory to governing body, the removal of human-rights language, the grant of authority to require medical examinations and vaccine proof, the institution of global health certificates, and empowerment of the WHO Emergency Committee to override sovereign nations.[2]

## Reception in the Truth Community

Roguski's research has been cited in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] coverage of the WHO negotiations, where contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the treaty and amendments would create a legal framework elevating the WHO to a de facto world-governing body in the event of another pandemic, while cautioning that the organization remains "like a paper tiger" without an enforcement arm of its own.[1]

## "The WHO's Last Stand" (2023)

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described Roguski as an activist, author and researcher who "has been the leading voice on SubStack raising awareness of the WHOs lightly veiled machinations," and interviewed him for Vigilant News ahead of the December 2023 deadline.[3] Roguski's tracking of the amendment process is the source for the figure of 307 proposed amendments to the [[international-health-regulations|International Health Regulations]] cited in the article.[3]

In June 2023, Roguski attended an Office of Global Affairs listening session at which the public was given an opportunity to speak directly to the delegates appointed to represent them in the negotiations, and voiced concerns and criticisms held by the greater public.[3] DeLarme cautioned that the listening session created the appearance of public input while most of what was being planned and discussed occurred in secret.[3]
