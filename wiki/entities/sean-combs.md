# Sean "Diddy" Combs

Sean "Diddy" Combs is an American music mogul who became the subject of a
cascade of physical abuse, rape and sexual trafficking allegations, and whose
homes in Miami and Los Angeles were raided by the
[[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] in March
2024, during which federal agents seized computers and other electronic
devices.[1]

## Blackmail tape allegations

Combs' former bodyguard [[gene-deal|Gene Deal]] claimed publicly that the music
mogul held blackmail tapes of politicians, princes and other prominent
individuals who had attended his sex parties.[1] Speaking to the YouTube
channel "The Art of Dialogue," Deal said, "I don't think it's only celebrities
gonna be shook. He had politicians in there, he had princes in there. He also
had a couple of preachers in there," adding, "Can you imagine, he had every
room bugged."[1] Asked why Combs' media department had remained silent about
the allegations, Deal answered that they had either "took part in some of the
stuff that happened, or they're scared that it may mess up their brand."[1]

## Commentary in the Truth Community

Badlands Media contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that the Combs

## The Buzbee accusers (October 2024)

On October 1, 2024, Houston attorney [[tony-buzbee|Tony Buzbee]] announced that he represented 120 accusers alleging sexual misconduct by Combs, who was then awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges.[2] Buzbee said he expected lawsuits to be filed within the following month, most of them in New York and Los Angeles, and described the victims as 60 males and 60 females, with 25 who were minors at the time of the alleged misconduct — including one individual who said he was nine years old when abused.[2] The allegations covered a period from 1991 to 2024.[2] "This type of sexual assault, sexual abuse, sexual exploitation should never happen in the United States or anywhere else," Buzbee said at a news conference, adding that the conduct "created a mass of individuals who are injured, scared and scarred."[2] An attorney for Combs responded that the performer "cannot address every meritless allegation in what has become a reckless media circus."[2]

## Badlands interpretation (2024)

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the Combs affair was neither a distraction psyop nor the Deep State burning one of its own, but rather the takedown of the entertainment arm of what he called the transnational cartel that effectively controls the world.[2] He connected it to singer [[jaguar-wright|Jaguar Wright]]'s claims about [[jay-z|Shawn "Jay-Z" Carter]] and Beyoncé Knowles, and noted that Combs had aggressively pursued [[kanye-west|Kanye West]] by text message after West's November 2016 endorsement of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], messages West later posted to social media.[2] Writing separately, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] pointed to the [[southern-district-of-new-york|SDNY]]'s parallel activity in the [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] corruption case, remarking that the district had "been very busy lately — just ask Diddy."[2]

## Verdict in the federal sex-trafficking trial (July 2025)

On Wednesday, July 2, 2025, the 55-year-old record executive was acquitted of racketeering and sex-trafficking charges but found guilty on two lesser counts of transportation to engage in prostitution.[3] According to Vulture, he still faced 66 civil lawsuits connected to a slew of sexual impropriety allegations.[3] Longtime collaborator [[tony-dofat|Tony Dofat]], who worked with Combs on several projects from the early 1990s to the late 2000s, told the New York Post shortly after the verdict that he would "definitely work together with him again."[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] disputed press framing of the split verdict as a defeat for prosecutor [[maureen-comey|Maureen Comey]], daughter of former FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]], writing that many had speculated Comey was "landing the plane" for Combs and his network and that on that view "she did her job perfectly."[3] Late in the trial, prosecutors downgraded or abandoned certain claims — specifically those relating to attempted kidnapping and attempted arson within the racketeering charge — and chose not to proceed with the theory of aiding and abetting sex trafficking as a predicate act, a move reportedly made to "streamline" jury instructions as deliberations approached and leaving the jury to focus on other aspects of the racketeering conspiracy such as sex trafficking and forced labor, of which Combs was acquitted.[3]


## Federal trial jury selection (2025)

Combs' [[sean-combs-federal-trial|federal trial]] began with jury selection on Monday, May 5, 2025, in Manhattan on charges of sex trafficking, racketeering, and transportation to engage in prostitution, to which he pleaded not guilty after being indicted in the Southern District of New York in September.[4] Prosecutors allege that Combs organized so-called "freak offs," described as "elaborate and produced sex performances."[4] On the second day of jury selection a prospective juror was excused after admitting he smoked marijuana every night and that abstaining for the roughly eight-week trial would be difficult.[4]


## Civil-to-criminal pattern
Badlands cited Combs in October 2024 as one instance of a recurring sequence in elite trafficking prosecutions: like Jeffrey Epstein, he was first sued in civil court before later being indicted on criminal charges, a pattern the site also identified in the case of former Abercrombie & Fitch chief executive Mike Jeffries.[5] The commentary rejected claims that the Combs storyline was a psyop intended to distract from political events, arguing instead that the sex-trafficking narrative is the thread that ties Washington to Corporate America and Hollywood.[5]

## Detention at MDC Brooklyn (September 2024)

In September 2024, Business Insider reported that Combs and convicted crypto fraudster [[sam-bankman-fried|Sam Bankman-Fried]] were bunking in the same dormitory-style room at the [[metropolitan-detention-center|Metropolitan Detention Center]] in Brooklyn.[6] Combs had been held there for almost a week after being denied bail on federal sex trafficking and racketeering charges, while Bankman-Fried had been imprisoned at the facility since the previous year.[6]

## March 2024 federal raids

On March 25, 2024, agents of the U.S. Department of Homeland Security opened an investigation of Combs and searched his properties in Los Angeles and the Miami area, agency officials said.[7] The inquiry was led by [[homeland-security-investigations|Homeland Security Investigations]] agents in New York, where a former girlfriend had filed a federal lawsuit against Combs in November accusing him of engaging in sex trafficking.[7] Combs, then 54, maintained that he was innocent and that his accusers were seeking "a quick payday"; his New York-based lawyer, Ben Brafman, did not immediately respond to requests for comment.[7]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the raids through the [[shark-fin-template|Shark Fin Template]], describing the case as part of the "Darkest Path in the Info War" — the continued exposure of coordinated, systemic sex trafficking by elites — and noting that while truthers can be frustrated by the slow rollout, the theme returns to the Mind War "with a vengeance."[7] He observed that normies engaged with the "P Diddy Saga" more intensely than with most other alleged elite debauchery, consistent with the maxim that politics is downstream of culture.[7] In his assessment the episode seeds four propositions: that elite predators are coordinated and protected, that they have hidden in plain sight in American culture and politics, that they are not self-made, and that they are no longer safe — concluding that "it's not about Diddy. It's not about Epstein. It's about who's behind them. And why."[7]

## SNL and the 2006 "Diddy Kiddies" sketch

In October 2024, Badlands contributor Ashe in America connected Combs to Saturday Night Live while examining the show's programming shift in the context of the legal landscape in the Southern District of New York, asking what overlap existed between Jeffrey Epstein, Diddy, and SNL.[8] She noted that the prior week's "Weekend Update" had tied all three together.[8]

Researching how many times Combs had appeared on SNL, she surfaced a 2006 sketch titled "Diddy Kiddies," described as three multi-ethnic middle-schoolers "solving mysteries, doing the bad thing, they out there going for the brass ring," whose running mystery was what exactly Diddy does for a living.[8] She reported that the video had been wiped and that the Internet Archive was down at the time, and asked readers to share copies.[8]

## Fifth bail denial, August 2025

On Monday, 4 August 2025 federal judge [[arun-subramanian|Arun Subramanian]] denied Combs' bail, the fifth time the 55-year-old had been refused release from the Brooklyn Metropolitan Detention Center, where he had been held since his arrest in September 2024.[9] In court documents obtained by People, Subramanian wrote that the court must "find by clear and convincing evidence that the person is not likely to flee or pose a danger to the safety of any other person or the community if released," and found that Combs posed a risk of "flight or danger."[9]

The judge described the conditions that would warrant release as "'exceptional reasons' warranting release where it was undisputed that the defendant posed no risk of flight or danger, and where 'unique circumstances' counseled release, including the defendant's advanced 'age and serious medical conditions, which require care that the MDC may not be able to provide.'"[9]

## Pardon request, October 2025

On Friday, October 3, 2025, Sean "Diddy" Combs was sentenced to more than four years in prison following his conviction on prostitution-related charges.[10] On Monday, October 6, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told reporters that Combs had requested a pardon.[10]

## Tiffany Red lawsuit, March 2024

In early March 2024, veteran songwriter [[tiffany-red|Tiffany Red]] was reported to be preparing a lawsuit against Combs, adding to the series of legal problems the mogul was fighting.[11] Red discussed her plans during an Instagram Live appearance in which she was visibly distressed and outlined her reasons for suing.[11] Known in the industry for her songwriting and her close association with the singer Cassie, Red said: "I am suing Sean Combs, I'm suing Interscope Records, I'm suing Bad Boy Entertainment, I'm suing Epic Records, and I don't know who else… for tortious interference, for all of the work that I have done that has been [expletive] because of all of this [expletive]. My work was [expletive]; it was derailed. I spent years working with Cassie… the music is incredible, it's all [expletive] now, it's got all this nastiness all over it."[11] Her suit centered on accusations of alleged manipulation and coercion in the environment surrounding Combs.[11]

Also in March 2024, author and journalist [[liz-crokin|Liz Crokin]] suggested that Combs may have been part of [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s sex trafficking ring and that Britney Spears may have been one of their victims, a claim amplified by Evie Magazine, which argued such theories were not far-fetched given that Epstein's circle was full of rich and powerful people and that a crime syndicate within Hollywood would explain why so many abuse cases go unreported.[11]
