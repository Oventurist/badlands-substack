# Alexandre de Moraes

Alexandre de Moraes is a justice of Brazil's Supreme Federal Court who, according to an interim staff report released by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], leads a censorship system that issues global takedown orders, coordinates with censorship efforts in other countries, and strips legal protections from online platforms.[1]

## Role in the Brazilian censorship regime

The Judiciary Committee report, which drew on newly obtained nonpublic documents, characterized the apparatus directed by de Moraes as reaching beyond Brazil's borders: takedown orders issued under his authority are described as global in scope rather than confined to Brazilian users or Brazilian territory.[1] The committee said the system also removes the legal protections that ordinarily shield platforms from liability for user content, increasing the pressure on companies to comply with removal demands rather than litigate them.[1]

## Alleged targeting of US speech

The committee alleged that orders flowing from this regime have directly targeted speech inside the United States, including posts by Brazilian journalists and political commentators who live in the US, as well as content praising President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and content critical of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] Because the targets included US residents posting from US soil, the report framed the practice as a threat to [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] protections, warning that compelling American companies to censor Americans on behalf of a foreign court effectively exports a foreign speech standard into the United States.[1]

## Enforcement against platforms

When platforms such as [[x-corp|X]] and [[rumble|Rumble]] declined to comply fully with orders, Brazilian authorities responded with fines and with threats or orders to cease operations in the country entirely.[1] The report treats those escalations as evidence that the regime is willing to use market access as leverage against American firms.[1]

## Foreign coordination

The report further stated that Brazil's censorship network coordinates with similar foreign censorship regimes and with [[stanford-university|Stanford University]], which the committee alleges previously played a central role in "laundering US government censorship requests" to social media companies during the 2020 US election cycle before shifting toward the support of foreign censorship efforts.[1] The committee said it would continue oversight of the matter and pursue legislative responses aimed at countering foreign censorship threats.[1]

### 2025 lawsuit by Trump Media and Rumble
In February 2025, Trump Media and Technology Group sued de Moraes in U.S. federal court in Florida, with the video-sharing platform Rumble joining as a co-plaintiff.[2] The suit, first reported by the New York Times and relayed by Reuters, accused the justice of illegally censoring political discourse.[2] Badlands commentary framed the case as a potential vehicle for exposing the extent to which the Biden administration supported Brazilian government censorship.[2]

## Escalation against X (August 2024)

In late August 2024 de Moraes moved against [[x-corp|X]] after the platform refused to comply with orders it characterized as illegal demands to censor his political opponents, prompting X to warn on August 29 that its service in [[brazil|Brazil]] might be shut down imminently.[3] X said the judge had threatened its Brazilian legal representative with imprisonment and froze her bank accounts even after she resigned, and that its legal challenges were either dismissed or ignored while his Supreme Court colleagues were "either unwilling or unable to stand up to him."[3] The company announced it would publish all of his demands and the related court filings, and reporting at the time also noted his blocking of Starlink bank accounts as the confrontation with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] escalated.[3]
