# Norway

Norway is a Nordic [[nato|NATO]] member state that became a central antagonist in the January 2026 transatlantic crisis over President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s campaign to bring [[greenland|Greenland]] under U.S. control.[1]

## Nobel Peace Prize dispute and Greenland

The Nobel Committee in Oslo declined to award Trump the Nobel Peace Prize despite his claim to have stopped eight wars.[1] After Norway condemned what it viewed as his belligerence over the annexation of Greenland, Trump formally responded that he "no longer feel[s] an obligation to think purely of peace," a line he repeated in a letter to Prime Minister [[jonas-gahr-store|Jonas Gahr Støre]] that was shared with other NATO leaders.[1] Støre replied firmly, affirming Norway's support for [[denmark|Denmark]]'s sovereignty over Greenland.[1]

Norway was among eight countries — with Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, the Netherlands, Sweden and the United Kingdom — that deployed a small military contingent to Greenland to bolster Arctic security and demonstrate solidarity, and that Trump then threatened with additional 10–15% tariffs effective February 1 unless they supported a U.S. purchase deal.[1] The eight issued a joint statement condemning the threats and pledging full solidarity with Denmark and Greenlandic self-determination, while the [[european-union|European Union]] convened an emergency meeting of its 27 ambassadors to coordinate potential countermeasures worth €93 billion.[1]

## Epstein Files fallout and royal hospitalization (February 2026)

In February 2026 Norwegian authorities charged former prime minister [[thorbjorn-jagland|Thorbjørn Jagland]] with gross corruption, reportedly in connection with information identified in the latest [[epstein-files|Epstein Files]] released by the U.S. [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]]; Jagland was hospitalized following reports of an alleged suicide attempt, and the investigation remained ongoing.[2] Norwegian officials did not publicly confirm specifics regarding his condition, and his attorney disputed some initial media characterizations.[2]

In the same week, [[harald-v-of-norway|King Harald V]], 89, was admitted to a hospital on Spain's Canary Island of Tenerife during a private winter vacation with Queen Sonja; the royal palace confirmed he was being treated for an unspecified infection and dehydration while in good condition.[2]

## May 2024: readiness to arrest Israeli leaders

On Tuesday, 21 May 2024, Norway became the first European country to publicly announce that it would arrest Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] if the Hague Tribunal issued arrest warrants against them and they landed on Norwegian territory.[3] Foreign Minister [[espen-barth-eide|Espen Barth Eide]] made the statement a day after [[international-criminal-court|ICC]] prosecutor [[karim-khan|Karim Khan]]'s announcement, saying that if warrants were issued 'we will be obliged to arrest them,' and that all signatory countries must act on a court decision.[3] Israeli coverage treated the pledge as an idle threat, since no Israeli prime minister or defense minister was likely to visit Norway — a country then considered, along with Spain and Ireland, among the most hostile to Israel in Europe, with bilateral relations at an unprecedented low.[3] Britain, by contrast, criticized Khan's decision, particularly the equivalence it drew between the Israeli leaders and the [[hamas|Hamas]] leaders [[yahya-sinwar|Yahya Sinwar]], Muhammad Deif and [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]].[3]
