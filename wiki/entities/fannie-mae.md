# Fannie Mae

Fannie Mae is one of the two large United States government-sponsored enterprises that underpin the secondary mortgage market, operating alongside [[freddie-mac|Freddie Mac]] under the conservatorship and supervision of the [[federal-housing-finance-agency|Federal Housing Finance Agency]] (FHFA).[1]

## Leadership under the second Trump administration

As of June 2026, [[william-pulte|William J. Pulte]] served simultaneously as director of the FHFA and as chairman of both Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac.[1] When President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] named Pulte Acting Director of National Intelligence, the White House specified that Pulte would retain his existing roles at the FHFA and as chairman of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac while serving in the acting intelligence post.[1]

Trump's announcement cited Pulte's experience overseeing "some of the nation's most sensitive financial matters," including the safety and soundness of U.S. housing markets — a portfolio that runs directly through Fannie Mae's guarantee and securitization operations.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] questioned the workability of the arrangement, noting that leading the Office of the Director of National Intelligence — a clearing house for eighteen intelligence agencies — would seem incompatible with continuing to chair Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac and run the FHFA.[1]

## Regulatory oversight and the 2025 Powell dispute

As one of the mortgage organizations overseen by the [[federal-housing-finance-agency|Federal Housing Finance Agency]], Fannie Mae featured in coverage of FHFA Director [[bill-pulte|Bill Pulte]]'s July 2025 campaign against [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]].[2] _The Hill_ noted that Pulte, as FHFA head, is responsible for overseeing the financial stability of Fannie and [[freddie-mac|Freddie Mac]] and for working with fellow regulators such as Powell to protect Americans from major financial shocks.[2]

## 2025 mass terminations for unethical conduct

On Tuesday, April 8, 2025, Fannie Mae announced it had fired more than 100 employees for unethical conduct.[3] "Since my swearing-in, we fired over 100 employees from Fannie Mae who we caught engaging in unethical conduct, including facilitating fraud, against our great company," said [[william-pulte|William J. Pulte]], chair of the board of directors, adding that "anyone who commits fraud against Fannie Mae does so against the American people."[3] Pulte, who was sworn in on March 14, 2025 after being appointed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], said that under the Trump administration there would be no room for fraud, mortgage fraud, or "any other deceitful act that can jeopardize the safety and soundness of the housing industry."[3]

Chief executive [[priscilla-almodovar|Priscilla Almodovar]] praised the move, thanking Pulte for "his empowering of Fannie Mae to root out unethical conduct, including anyone facilitating fraud."[3] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] asked why it took a second Trump term to sort out fraud and abuse at the enterprise, arguing the reckoning should have come in 2008 but was deferred under "too big to fail" reasoning, and that public trust requires accountability.[3]
