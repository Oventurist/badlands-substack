# Arthur Koestler

**Arthur Koestler** is the writer credited in Part 4 of the [[structures-of-control-part-1-series|Structures of Control]] series with formulating the concept of the [[holon|holon]], which the essay uses to explain self-reinforcing control networks.[1]

## The holon concept

The article states that Koestler's holon describes a structure in which every component is simultaneously a whole unto itself and a part of a larger structure.[1] Its illustrative example is a university, which houses its own administration, curriculum, and faculty and therefore appears independent, yet adheres to national accreditation boards, funding sources, and ideological trends shaped by think tanks and international policy bodies — making it both self-contained and systemically entangled.[1]

## Application to control systems

Koestler's framework underpins the essay's description of what it calls the fifth dimension, where the system no longer requires direct enforcement because it has become recursively self-reinforcing.[1] Control in that layer is said to embed itself in language, incentive, culture, and unconscious permission: a professor self-censors to protect status with a funding body, a CEO promotes ESG and DEI standards because markets reward compliance, and a parent enforces school attendance because the alternative feels unsafe.[1] The article summarizes the result with the line "This is not a conspiracy. It is a design."[1]

The same recursion is presented as the route out: because what can be encoded can be rewritten, holons are said to be capable of serving sovereignty as well as control, with [[decentralized-autonomous-organization|DAOs]], Earthship communities, regenerative eco-villages, and homeschooling co-ops offered as existing sovereign holons.[1]
