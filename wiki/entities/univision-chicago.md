# Univision Chicago

Univision Chicago is the Chicago affiliate of the Spanish-language television network Univision.[1] The station drew national attention in late August 2023 when one of its news crews was robbed at gunpoint while reporting on a wave of robberies in the city.[1]

## 2023 armed robbery of a news crew

Shortly before 5 a.m. on Monday, August 28, 2023, a Univision Chicago reporter and photographer were filming in Chicago's West Town neighborhood when three masked men brandishing firearms accosted and robbed them.[1] The assailants took a television camera and other personal items. "They were approached with guns and robbed. Mainly it was personal items, and they took a camera," Luis Godinez, vice president of news at Univision Chicago, told the Chicago Tribune.[1]

Godinez said the crew had been filming a story about robberies in the West Town community that was slated to air on the morning news; because the footage was inside the stolen camera, the story never made it to air.[1] Chicago police identified the victims as a 28-year-old man and a 42-year-old man, and said the three robbers had arrived in a gray sedan and a black SUV before fleeing in those vehicles.[1] No injuries were reported and no arrests had been made at the time of reporting.[1] The incident was covered by ABC News.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] Badlands News Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the robbery as an illustration of the consequences of decriminalization policy, citing figures attributed to SOS Private Security showing Chicago averaged 3,926 crimes per 100,000 people in 2022, with robbery up 26 percent, burglary up 11 percent, theft up 24 percent, aggravated battery up 31 percent, criminal sexual assault up 12 percent and motor vehicle theft up 165 percent in early 2023 relative to 2022 — the last figure being 349 percent above 2019.[1] Homicide and murder rates had fallen 9 percent versus 2022 but remained 78 percent above 2019.[1] She also observed that the reporters' union had begun issuing warnings about field safety while the broader media establishment had spent years advocating defunding the police.[1]
