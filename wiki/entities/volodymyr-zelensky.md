# Volodymyr Zelensky

Volodymyr Zelensky is the president of Ukraine, described in "A Bright Red Line" as the leader of a country whose war effort depends on continued Western funding, arms and, ultimately, manpower.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The article characterizes Ukraine as having "an installed leader who is subservient to the West," which it offers as an explanation for Kyiv's drive to join [[nato|NATO]] while at war with a global superpower.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Manpower crisis

Badlands argues it was well known to anyone not relying on major networks that this was a war Ukraine could not hope to win, a judgment resting on the disparity in soldiers available to each side and on the gap between Russian and Western artillery production capabilities.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) For that reason Ukraine began, as early as 27 February 2022, to plead with Western supporters to enlist and fight rather than merely virtue-signal with Ukrainian flags in their profile pictures — a call remarkably few Americans heeded.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

The resulting desperation for manpower led Zelensky, already working with a vanishingly small conscripted army, to sign legislation lowering the draft age to 25 in April 2024.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The article contends that even a lowered draft age will not sustain the war effort much longer or beat back the Russians, and that Ukraine "pretty much has to" join NATO to keep the one-sided war of attrition going — an outcome that could see the United States military shipped to the front lines as the cannon fodder Zelensky requires.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Marketing battle-tested military technology (2026)

By 2026 Zelenskyy was positioning [[ukraine|Ukraine]] as a global exporter of combat-tested military technology, particularly drones and robotic systems refined through battlefield use against Russia, pitching these systems to foreign buyers and turning wartime innovation into a defense export strategy.[2] Commentary framed the move alongside the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]'s outreach to civilian manufacturers as evidence of an emerging global [[war-economy|war economy]], in which frontline states monetize rapid battlefield innovation while Western nations retool industrial capacity for long-term geopolitical competition.[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Zelenskyy as openly pitching Ukraine's battlefield evolution to the highest bidders, noting that what began as a supposed human meat grinder had pivoted with startling speed toward fully autonomous systems — ground robotic platforms, drone swarms, and unmanned vehicles — taking over the war narrative without infantry crossing the wire.[2]

## Call for a Maduro-style raid on Kadyrov (2026)

In January 2026 Zelensky said the United States should intensify pressure on Russia and suggested Washington could pursue "some sort of operation" to remove Chechen leader [[ramzan-kadyrov|Ramzan Kadyrov]], drawing an explicit parallel to the recent US operation that captured Venezuela's [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[3] He portrayed the idea as a way to raise the cost for Moscow and push President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to reconsider continuing the war in Ukraine.[3] Kadyrov responded by calling Zelensky a coward for refusing to attempt the operation himself.[3]

The remarks came as European leaders warned that US actions were straining the postwar international system, a cluster of developments Badlands treated as part of the broader [[erosion-of-the-postwar-international-order|erosion of the postwar international order]].[3]

## Maritime strike campaign (July 2026)

In late July 2026 Zelensky said [[ukraine|Ukraine]] had conducted long-range strikes against vessels involved in military cargo shipments linked to [[iran|Iran]], as well as against a warship.[4] Iran accused Ukraine of attacking an Iranian commercial vessel in the Caspian Sea, saying the strike killed one sailor and wounded another, and summoned Ukraine's chargé d'affaires in Tehran to protest.[4] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the Caspian Sea lies on the far side of [[russia|Russia]] from Ukraine, implying Ukrainian drones had to overfly Russian territory to reach the target.[4]

Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] said Ukraine had acted "at the behest of Israel" to draw Europe deeper into the Middle East war.[4] Badlands counted strikes in the preceding week on 13 ships in the Black Sea — at least one Turkish, with another sailor killed — and predicted the attacks across the Black, Mediterranean and Caspian Seas would push Russia, [[turkey|Turkey]] and Iran toward a naval alliance that would complicate [[nato|NATO]] positioning in the Persian Gulf and Strait of Hormuz.[4]

## Melnyk reburial and the rift with Israel (May 2026)

In May 2026 Zelenskyy and senior officials attended a state reburial ceremony in Kyiv honoring [[andriy-melnyk|Andriy Melnyk]], a leader of the [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists]] who collaborated with Nazi Germany during World War II; Melnyk was described as a national hero and his remains were repatriated from Luxembourg.[5] [[israel|Israel]]'s Foreign Ministry condemned the ceremony, saying there is "no place for ignoring historical truth and the memory of the victims murdered by the Nazis and their collaborators," and [[yad-vashem|Yad Vashem]] warned that the honors undermine Holocaust remembrance.[5] The episode reopened a longstanding fault line: Israel and Poland had jointly condemned state glorification of Melnyk and [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]] as early as 2020, with Ukrainian officials then calling it an internal matter.[5]


## Response to Putin's 2026 peace signals

After Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] suggested during Victory Day events in May 2026 that the [[ukraine|Ukraine]] war might be "coming to an end," Zelenskyy rejected the suggestion that Russia was genuinely seeking peace.[6] The exchange took place amid a fragile US-brokered ceasefire that both Kyiv and Moscow accused the other of violating.[6]

## July 2026 government reshuffle
In July 2026 Zelensky reshaped his wartime government, and Ukraine's parliament voted 289–22 to confirm former [[naftogaz|Naftogaz]] chief executive [[sergii-koretskyi|Sergii Koretskyi]] as prime minister in place of [[yulia-svyrydenko|Yulia Svyrydenko]].[7] Zelensky said Koretskyi's energy-sector experience made him the best choice as Ukraine prepared for another winter under continued Russian attacks, and described the wider overhaul — which also included planned changes at the Defense Ministry and other senior posts — as a leadership "renewal."[7] Opposition lawmakers questioned the purpose of replacing officials without broader structural reforms, while RT and some Ukrainian critics highlighted unsubstantiated reports linking businessman [[timur-mindich|Timur Mindich]] to members of the new government.[7] Mindich is a co-owner of [[kvartal-95-studio|Kvartal 95 Studio]], the production company Zelensky founded in 2003 that made *Servant of the People*.[7]

## Drone production and the June 2026 Russian strikes

Following the [[starobelsk-dormitory-attack|Starobelsk dormitory attack]] of May 22, 2026, Russian forces conducted a large-scale overnight missile and drone strike whose targets included a drone production plant linked to Zelensky.[8] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted reporting that a drone film-scouting company connected to Zelensky's friends had become Ukraine's "miracle" factory producing all of its long-range drones, and asked how many other Ukrainian film production companies had been repurposed for the war effort.[8] He argued that Ukrainian leadership under Zelensky had grown "increasingly unhinged," citing the drone strike on the [[zaporozhye-nuclear-power-plant|Zaporozhye Nuclear Power Plant]] and a drone incident in Romania that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] publicly attributed to a Western or Ukrainian false flag.[8]

## Final meeting with Lindsey Graham (July 2026)

Shortly before his death on July 11, 2026, Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] returned from a trip to Ukraine during which he met Zelenskyy.[9] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Graham had spent several days in Ukraine on his way back from the [[nato|NATO]] summit in Turkey, meeting Zelensky and touring drone factories.[9] The visit became a focal point of speculation after Graham's death, with commentators aligned with Israel blaming Russia and Iran for an alleged assassination — a framing Badlands read as an attempt to merge the Ukraine and Iran wars into a single conflict.[9]

## Washington visit (July 2026)

On 28 July 2026, Zelenskyy was hosted by [[donald-trump|President Trump]] for an Oval Office meeting, held separately from a meeting the same day with Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and timed to coincide with the funeral of Sen. [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]], a longtime advocate for both countries.[10] Zelenskyy focused on advancing licenses for Ukrainian production of Patriot interceptor missiles — announced earlier in Ankara — and other ideas to support Kyiv, with special envoys [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] and [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] expected to visit Ukraine soon to reinvigorate diplomacy.[10] White House press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] described both meetings as "positive and productive."[10]

The visit continued Ukraine's deliberate outreach to MAGA and [[america-first-movement|America First]] circles that have long been wary of open-ended support.[10] Recent Ukrainian battlefield gains and a high-profile meeting with activist [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] — who declared full support after interviewing him — created limited openings, including positive comments from Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], though many hard-line Republicans remained skeptical and Trump responded cautiously to Zelenskyy's claims of Russian-Iranian collaboration.[10]

## Badlands commentary: the "Prussian Proxy Prince"

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that he has referred to Zelenskyy as the "Prussian Proxy Prince" since 2022, when the Ukrainian leader was still popular enough on a near-bipartisan basis to be compared more often to Luke Skywalker than to Gríma Wormtongue.[10] He argued that the "American Mindscape" has since turned, leaving Zelenskyy as unpopular as any foreign leader among the MAGA core while he takes reputational damage on a lag among liberal-minded observers.[10] Burning Bright paired him with Netanyahu as "twin Proxy Princes" arriving in Washington with "hegemonic hats held firmly in hand," describing the convergence as a humiliation ritual staged in the Western mind and predicting that the resulting headlines would be glowing but unheeded because "the proxies' wicks have run."[10]

## Conflict with Kiev's mayor (2026)

In February 2026, Kiev Mayor [[vitali-klitschko|Vitali Klitschko]] accused Zelensky of infringing on municipal authority by appointing military-civilian administrations in the capital, telling the *Financial Times* that the city was "teetering on the brink of catastrophe" amid large-scale power outages.[11] Zelensky had claimed the previous month that Kiev was falling behind other Ukrainian cities in responding to the outages, criticism Klitschko dismissed on the grounds that electricity generation falls within the central government's purview.[11]

Zelensky's chief of staff [[andriy-yermak|Andrey Yermak]] resigned in the wake of the November 2025 disclosure of a $100 million corruption scheme at Ukraine's state-owned nuclear energy operator, alongside ministers [[german-galushchenko|German Galushchenko]] and [[svetlana-grinchuk|Svetlana Grinchuk]].[11]

## EU accession timeline demand (February 2026)

Amid regional tensions in February 2026, Zelenskyy called on the [[european-union|European Union]] to provide Ukraine with a concrete accession timeline, having repeatedly urged accelerated membership negotiations as the war with Russia continued.[12] The EU granted Ukraine candidate status in 2022; full membership requires approval from all EU member states and completion of reform benchmarks.[12]

## New Year 2026 address

In a roughly 20-minute New Year address delivered Wednesday, December 31, 2025, Zelenskyy said Ukraine was 90 percent ready for a peace agreement while stressing that the remaining share was decisive: "Ten percent remains…those 10 percent contain, in fact, everything…Those are the 10 percent that will determine the fate of peace, the fate of Ukraine and Europe, how people will live."[13] Asked implicitly whether Kyiv's arguments had landed, he said, "Have our arguments been heard? We very much hope so. Have they agreed with us? Not fully. Not yet. That is precisely why, for now, we speak of 90 percent, not the full 100 percent, readiness of a peace agreement."[13] He warned that a "Budapest-style piece of paper will not satisfy Ukraine," and framed the country's position as: "What does Ukraine want? Peace? Yes. At any cost? No. We want the end of the war — not the end of Ukraine."[13]

## Long-range weapons request (September 2024)

In September 2024 Ukrainian officials, including Zelenskyy, repeatedly pressed visiting Western diplomats for permission to use Western-supplied weaponry for long-range strikes inside Russia.[14] U.S. Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] and British Foreign Secretary [[david-lammy|David Lammy]] spent a day in Kyiv and pledged to bring the requests to their leaders.[14] U.S. Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] had met Zelenskyy the previous week and pushed back on the ask, noting that Ukraine had already struck inside Russia using its own internally produced systems, including drones.[14]

President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had allowed Ukraine to fire U.S.-provided missiles across the border in self-defense but largely limited the distance.[14] President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] warned that granting the request would mean [[nato|NATO]] countries were "at war" with Russia.[14] Badlands additionally argued that the 31 Abrams tanks supplied to Zelenskyy had failed because Ukrainian crews used them as stationary pillboxes rather than in maneuver warfare, having not been properly trained in the doctrine.[14]

## October 2024 complaints of a double standard on air defense

In October 2024, Kyiv objected publicly to what it called a double standard in Western military support after the United States deployed an advanced air defense system and dozens of troops to protect [[israel|Israel]] from Iranian ballistic missiles, while offering nothing comparable to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] despite daily Russian drone, missile and bomb attacks.[15] Zelensky had asked the previous month: "If the allies shoot down missiles together in the sky of the Middle East, why is there still no decision to shoot down drones and missiles over Ukraine?"[15]

After U.S. and British air defenses and fighter aircraft helped destroy hundreds of Iranian missiles on October 1 — following a similar intervention in April — Ukraine's foreign ministry called on allies "to defend Ukrainian airspace with the same determination and without hesitation from Russian missile and drone attacks, recognizing that human life is equally precious in any part of the world."[15] Reporting attributed the asymmetry to nuclear risk: a senior U.S. Senate aide working on Ukraine policy said the allies could accept the risk of shooting down Iranian missiles over Israel without triggering a nuclear war with Tehran, but that "there's a lot more risk in trying that with Russia."[15] Badlands commentary rejected that explanation, arguing instead that U.S. behavior reflected the influence of the [[israel-lobby|Israel lobby]] over American policy.[15]

## February 2025 rejection of a "quick win" ceasefire

In an interview broadcast on Monday, February 17, 2025, Zelenskyy said the United States was trying to "please" [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] as it aimed for a "quick win" by rushing toward a ceasefire in the Russia-Ukraine war.[16] "The US is now saying things that are very favourable to Putin … because they want to please him. They want to meet quickly and have a quick win. But what they want – just a ceasefire – is not a win," he said, adding that Ukraine "will not sign just anything in order to be applauded … the fate of our state for generations to come [is at stake]."[16]

He warned that Europe was in a weak position if it could not rely on U.S. security assistance: although "readiness has increased" in recent years, "in terms of troop strength, the number of combat troops, the fleet, the air force, the drones … I honestly think that Europe is weak today."[16] Zelenskyy said Ukraine had grown more resilient over three years of war and that "Putin wouldn't be able to occupy us the way he wanted to," while conceding that "there will definitely not be a Ukrainian victory without US support."[16] He said he and [[donald-trump|Trump]] had discussed deploying foreign troops to police a future ceasefire.[16]

The remarks coincided with reports that the Trump administration had offered key concessions to Putin ahead of talks in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], ended the Russian president's international isolation, and excluded European and Ukrainian diplomats from the Riyadh meeting — prompting France to call an emergency leaders' meeting in Paris.[16] Separately, his predecessor [[petro-poroshenko|Petro Poroshenko]] claimed Ukrainian authorities were preparing presidential elections for October 26, 2025.[16] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] returned to Trump's "perfect phone call" with Zelenskyy as an origin point of Info War narrative whiplash, arguing that "it takes two to make a perfect phone call."[16]

## Rejection of Crimea recognition, April 2025

In April 2025 Zelensky rejected a fast-moving United States proposal that Washington formally recognize [[crimea|Crimea]] as Russian territory as part of a settlement freezing the war's front lines.[17] Speaking to reporters on 22 April, he said Ukraine would not recognize Crimea as Russian because doing so would violate the country's constitution, and argued that discussing Crimea and other Ukrainian territory aided Moscow by allowing it to continue the war, since "it will not be possible to agree on everything quickly."[17] Ukraine's allies, meeting in London, sought security guarantees and reconstruction programs in exchange for any territorial concessions.[17] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that both Zelensky and [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had their backs against the wall, fighting wars that had lost public support yet which neither leader could afford to stop.[17]

## April 2025 ceasefire posture

On April 23, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told reporters in the Oval Office that he believed a deal to end Russia's war had been reached, saying "I think we have a deal with both, I hope they do it," while adding, "I think we have a deal with Russia. We have to get a deal with Zelensky."[18] Moscow had shown signs it was unwilling to move forward, listing maximalist demands in ceasefire negotiations with Ukraine and the United States.[18] Ukraine had already agreed to a U.S.-proposed full 30-day ceasefire, saying on March 11 that Kyiv was ready if Russia also agreed to the terms, which Moscow refused; on April 23 Zelensky reaffirmed that Ukraine insists on an "immediate, full, and unconditional ceasefire."[18]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the deal Ukraine ultimately signs with Russia will be indistinguishable from the one Russia offered at the outset, and that Trump is not intervening to stop an unjust war but recognizing Russia's victory.[18] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] asked what it means that the deal ending the war would match the arrangement in place before 2022, given that the war actually began in 2014.[18] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called Trump's comment "pure signal," noted that rumors of a completed deal appeared false and that Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] had dismissed rumors the U.S. was considering lifting sanctions on Russia, criticized [[ben-shapiro|Ben Shapiro]] for helping Zelensky maintain his media branding, and predicted that Zelensky would not make a deal while Trump and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] would meet in Riyadh the following month.[18]

## November 2023 emergency visits from Washington

In November 2023 Zelensky publicly complained that deliveries of artillery shells to Ukraine had decreased since the outbreak of the Gaza war, with stocks being redirected to Israel.[19] Against that backdrop, U.S. Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] arrived in Kiev on November 20, 2023 in an unannounced visit — his second since the Russian invasion — and met Zelensky to "reaffirm the United States' steadfast support for Ukraine," pledging support for "urgent battlefield needs and long-term defense requirements."[19] Days earlier, [[cia|CIA]] Director [[william-burns|William Burns]] had also made a secretive, unannounced trip to hold urgent meetings with Zelensky and top Ukrainian military and intelligence officials.[19] Commentary in Asia Times attributed the flurry of visits to deteriorating battlefield conditions, while [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Ukraine's "kinetic and narrative collapse" was exposing the concepts of proxy presidents and proxy wars to an awakening public.[19]

## Prisoner mobilisation bill (May 2024)

On 8 May 2024 the [[verkhovna-rada|Verkhovna Rada]] passed legislation allowing certain categories of prisoners to volunteer for service in the armed forces, a measure requiring Zelensky's signature — alongside that of the parliament's chairperson — before it could take effect.[20] MP Olena Shuliak, head of Zelensky's party, announced the vote and said the draft law "opens the possibility for certain categories of prisoners who expressed a desire to defend their country to join the Defence Forces."[20] The reversal followed Kyiv's long-standing opposition to prisoner mobilisation and its criticism of Moscow for the same practice, and came amid fresh Russian battlefield advances and a critical manpower shortage.[20] Badlands commentary used the occasion to restate criticisms of Zelensky's government over corruption, religious persecution and the cancellation of elections.[20]

## Postponed Israel visit (November 2023)

Zelensky was reported on November 4, 2023 to be in advanced planning for a visit to Israel, then embroiled in war with [[hamas|Hamas]] in the Gaza Strip, but he did not travel on November 7 as initially planned after news of his arrival leaked.[21] The Times of Israel, citing undisclosed Israeli diplomatic sources, reported the postponement and said Zelensky was still expected to make the trip, though no dates had been set; an Israeli official said that "If President Zelensky comes, he will be welcomed with open arms."[21] An unnamed Ukrainian diplomat told the outlet that the plans were thrown into doubt by the leak, explaining that Zelensky "wanted the trip to be public when he stepped on Israeli soil" and was "very disappointed."[21] It was not the first time a Zelensky visit to Israel had reportedly been delayed.[21]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], rejected the leak explanation, arguing instead that Zelensky cancelled because of the "Narrative Whiplash" the leaked plans caused within an awakening American population, and framing the episode as an example of the [[truth-community|Truth Community]] and alternative media pre-empting establishment narrative deployments before they could take hold in the [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]].[21] He and [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] had earlier discussed how the "Covid-23 Scariant" was blunted by the same decentralized counter-narrative dynamic, and he argued the process was now being applied to defunding one proxy war as globalists attempted to spin up another.[21]

## Considered dismissal of Zaluzhnyi, 2024

On Sunday, February 4, 2024, Zelensky publicly confirmed that he was considering dismissing Ukraine's top military official, commander in chief General [[valerii-zaluzhnyi|Valerii Zaluzhnyi]], after more than a week of speculation about a rift between the two men.[22] Asked by Italian outlet RAI TV to confirm the reports, Zelensky said the Ukrainian people wanted "a reset" and that "a new beginning is necessary" following more than a year of brutal stalemate with Russian forces in eastern Ukraine.[22]

Zelensky framed the move as broader than a single personnel decision, saying, "I have something serious in mind, which does not concern a single person but the direction of the country's leadership," and clarifying that he meant a "replacement of a series of state leaders, not just in a single sector like the military."[22] Ukrainian and American media had reported that Zelensky already informed the popular general of his imminent firing, and some accounts said Zaluzhnyi had been asked to resign and refused.[22]

### "We waited too long" (September 2023)

In an interview with CNN's Fareed Zakaria broadcast in September 2023, Zelensky conceded that Ukraine had "waited too long" to launch its counteroffensive against Russia, attributing the delay to the pace of Western weapons deliveries.[23] He said Russia had used the interval to lay mines and dig in its defences while Kyiv waited for equipment, and stressed that an announcement of aid such as Bradley Fighting Vehicles "doesn't mean the result," since promised weapons did not arrive immediately.[23] Zelensky nonetheless thanked the United States, the European Union and other partners, singling out President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and Congress, and reiterated that only victory would restore Ukraine's territory, warning that failure would allow Russia to attack other countries.[23]

## Lex Fridman interview and criticism of Biden (January 2025)

## Energy-strike moratorium proposal (October 2024)

Speaking to journalists in Kyiv in October 2024, Zelensky said that an end to Russian aerial attacks on Ukrainian energy targets and cargo ships could pave the way for negotiations to end the war, arguing that "when it comes to energy and freedom of navigation, getting a result on these points would be a signal that Russia may be ready to end the war."[48] Ukraine was bracing for a painful winter after Russian missile strikes on thermal power plants destroyed almost half of its generating capacity, leaving the country largely dependent on its nuclear plants and on energy imports from European countries.[48] Referring to Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian oil refineries, he proposed a mutual halt: "We saw during the first [peace] summit that there could be a decision on energy security. Separately, in the same period Zelensky warned that at least 10,000 North Korean troops would soon join [[russia|Russia]] in its war against his country, a claim partially borne out when Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] confirmed the presence of DPRK personnel in Russia on 23 October 2024.[48]

### February 2025 approval-rating dispute and Trump's call for elections
On February 18, 2025, President Donald Trump told reporters — without citing evidence — that Zelensky's approval rating was "down at four percent," and called for Ukraine to hold presidential elections, which are banned under martial law by the country's constitution.[59] The following day a telephone poll of 1,000 people by the Kyiv International Institute of Sociology found that 57 percent of respondents trusted Zelensky while 37 percent did not, with the remainder undecided; trust had risen five percent since the previous poll in December despite Ukraine suffering military setbacks.[59]

Zelensky accused Trump of being influenced by Russian disinformation as a result of the U.S. president's discussions with the Kremlin.[59] The exchange came amid a rapid shift in U.S. foreign policy toward Russia: the two countries had held talks in Saudi Arabia on February 18 and agreed to strengthen diplomatic and economic ties and begin peace talks to end the war, with no Ukrainian officials invited — a thaw that alarmed European allies.[59] Trump also suggested that Kyiv bore responsibility for the war, then approaching its fourth year.[59] Badlands commentary speculated that Washington and Moscow were sequencing a ceasefire, Ukrainian elections, and only then a peace deal, and asked what would happen if Vladimir Putin were to run for president of Ukraine and win.[59]
