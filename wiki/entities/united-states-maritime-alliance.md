# United States Maritime Alliance

The United States Maritime Alliance (USMX) is the employer bargaining group representing ocean carriers and marine terminal operators in contract negotiations with dockworkers at East Coast and Gulf Coast ports.[1]

## 2024 negotiations

In September 2024 USMX was negotiating with the [[international-longshoremens-association|International Longshoremen's Association]] as a strike deadline neared, with authorities preparing for a work stoppage that could paralyze East and Gulf Coast ports just weeks before the presidential election.[1] The [[port-authority-of-new-york-and-new-jersey|Port Authority of New York and New Jersey]] publicly urged both sides to reach agreement for the sake of the national economy.[1]

## 2024 negotiations with the ILA

In late September 2024, USMX was negotiating with the [[international-longshoremens-association|International Longshoremen's Association]] as a strike deadline approached at East Coast and Gulf Coast ports.[2] The [[port-authority-of-new-york-and-new-jersey|Port Authority of New York and New Jersey]] publicly urged both sides "to find common ground and keep the cargo flowing for the good of the national economy," while logistics operator [[flexport|Flexport]] warned that a shutdown 36 days before the presidential election would be the campaign's biggest overlooked wild card.[2]

## Contract expiry, September 30, 2024

The contract between USMX — described as a coalition of port operators and carriers — and the ILA was set to expire at midnight on September 30, 2024, with thousands of longshoremen at three dozen facilities across fourteen Gulf and East Coast ports poised to walk out at 12:01 a.m. EST absent a deal.[3] [[goldman-sachs]] analyst Jordan Alliger warned clients that upwards of $4.9 billion per day in international trade was at risk along those coasts, with emergent congestion likely to trigger a re-emergence of transport price inflation.[3] See [[2024-east-and-gulf-coast-dockworker-strike]].[3]
