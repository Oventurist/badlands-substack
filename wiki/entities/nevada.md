# Nevada

Nevada is a western U.S. state and a recurring presidential battleground, whose Democratic statewide fortunes have historically depended on large vote margins out of [[clark-county|Clark County]], the home of Las Vegas — a cushion widely referred to as the "Clark firewall."[1]

## 2024 early voting

During the [[2024-early-voting|2024 early-voting period]], reported turnout in Nevada showed Republican voters outnumbering Democrats, a reversal of the state's normal pattern in which Democrats dominate early balloting.[1] Democrats led statewide mail-in voting by 17,298, while Republicans led in-person early voting by 25,173, out-polling Democrats even in Clark County and Washoe County (Reno).[1] Republicans made up 52 percent of in-person early voters, Democrats 28 percent, and other voters 20 percent.[1]

Veteran Nevada reporter [[jon-ralston|Jon Ralston]] described the in-person numbers as "startling" and noted that the Clark firewall had shrunk to roughly 6,500 — about one-seventh of its 2020 size — while the Republican turnout advantage in rural Nevada exceeded the Democratic advantage in urban Nevada by more than two to one.[1] Ralston framed the shift as the emergence of a Republican "rural firewall."[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would likely carry Nevada and might even win Clark County, while cautioning that early-voting turnout by party registration is not a vote count and should be treated only as a leading indicator, consistent with the analytical approach of [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]].[1] The commentary situated Nevada's numbers within a broader claimed national trend also visible in Georgia, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin.[1]
