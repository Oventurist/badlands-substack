# Main Directorate of Intelligence (GUR)

The Main Directorate of Intelligence, commonly abbreviated GUR, is Ukraine's military intelligence agency.[1] In 2024 it became the focus of an African diplomatic dispute after its spokesman, [[andrey-yusov|Andrey Yusov]], said publicly that GUR agents had helped plan an ambush on a convoy carrying [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] contractors and Malian soldiers in northern [[mali|Mali]] in late July, an attack that killed dozens.[1]

Yusov described the operation as directed "against Russian war criminals" and vowed that "there will be more" such actions.[1] Ukraine's embassy in [[senegal|Senegal]] shared the interview on Facebook with a caption by Ambassador [[yury-pivovarov|Yury Pivovarov]] signaling further actions against Wagner; the video was later deleted.[1]

The Foreign Ministry of [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] condemned the remarks as praise for terrorism, said Kiev should not set itself against a global community that unanimously condemns terrorism, and called on Ukraine to refrain from interfering in other states' internal affairs and to "come to its senses."[1] Badlands commentary treated the episode as evidence that US aid to Ukraine was indirectly financing jihadist violence in Africa, and as a marker of a proxy conflict between Russia and [[nato|NATO]].[1]

## Alleged Kaliningrad kidnapping operation (October 2024)

On October 3, 2024, Russia's [[fsb|Federal Security Service]] claimed it had detained HUR agents illegally crossing from Lithuania into Russia's Kaliningrad exclave, and alleged that an HUR officer had tried to use the agency's resources to kidnap his own son from Russia.[2] According to the FSB statement, "a secondary goal of the operation was the kidnapping and cross-border smuggling of an underage Russian citizen, who is the son of an HUR staff member."[2] Video released by the FSB showed the arrest of a driver and the removal of a boy in his early teens from a vehicle, with participants confirming on camera that they had been tasked with a kidnapping on Russian soil.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the incident as emblematic of Western institutional collapse and of what Washington had come to represent.[2]
