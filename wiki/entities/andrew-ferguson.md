# Andrew Ferguson

Andrew Ferguson is the Chairman of the [[federal-trade-commission|Federal Trade Commission]] (FTC) under the second Trump administration, where he has combined conventional antitrust enforcement with an explicit focus on politically motivated collusion in the advertising market.[1]

## Omnicom–IPG merger conditions

In June 2025 Ferguson announced that, as a condition of the FTC's approval of the merger between [[omnicom|Omnicom]] and [[interpublic-group|Interpublic Group]] — two of the world's "Big Six" advertising firms — the merging parties agreed not to collude or discriminate against media outlets on the basis of political or ideological viewpoint.[1] The FTC noted that both companies had been founding members of [[global-alliance-for-responsible-media|GARM]], a cross-industry initiative through which center-right outlets such as Breitbart News, Fox News, and the Daily Wire were allegedly blacklisted from advertising revenue.[1]

Ferguson wrote that a documented history of "collusion in the [advertising] market for media-buying services, and the increased potential for collusion post-merger" made the transaction "a rare instance where the imposition of a behavioral remedy is appropriate."[1]

## Reception

Badlands Media commentary treated the settlement as a potentially transformative win for independent media, comparing its significance for the information economy to what overturning Citizens United would mean for elections, while cautioning that the remedy is only meaningful if the merged firm actually abides by it.[1] Commentators tied the underlying advertiser boycotts to outlets that had challenged official narratives on COVID-19 vaccine efficacy and election integrity, arguing those outlets were "bled dry" of quality advertising regardless of the factual accuracy of their reporting.[1]
