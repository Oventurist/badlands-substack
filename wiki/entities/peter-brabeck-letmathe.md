# Peter Brabeck-Letmathe

Peter Brabeck-Letmathe is the former Nestlé chief executive named as the successor to [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]] as chairman of the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] following Schwab's April 2025 departure as executive chairman.[1]

## Role at Nestlé

Brabeck-Letmathe oversaw Nestlé's participation in what critics describe as a "global water grab," including the extraction of groundwater in vulnerable regions.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterized him as a figure who "might actually outclass" Schwab "in terms of outward malevolence."[1]

## Position on water as a commodity

Brabeck-Letmathe is best known publicly for arguing that water should be treated as a "foodstuff" commodity with a market value rather than recognized as a human right. In remarks quoted in the Badlands coverage, he described the position of NGOs "who bang on about declaring water a public right" as "an extreme solution," and said he personally believed "it's better to give a foodstuff a value so that we're all aware it has its price," with separate targeted measures for populations lacking access.[1]

## Significance in the globalist–populist framing

The Badlands treatment situates Brabeck-Letmathe within a broader analysis of the "globalist" faction — the international billionaires, think tanks and private-political operatives associated with bodies such as the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] and the World Economic Forum — which the article argues has lost favor with ordinary people across many nations and has historically maintained its position through dominance in the information war.[1] His elevation is presented as evidence that the ethos of the WEF is continuous rather than reformed.[1]
