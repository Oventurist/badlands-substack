# Barry Loudermilk

Barry Loudermilk is a Republican member of the United States House of Representatives from Georgia who chairs the House Administration Oversight Subcommittee.[1] In that role he has led an investigation into security lapses connected to the January 6, 2021 Capitol riot and into the potential ramifications of those lapses for criminal trials arising from the events of that day.[1]

## Missing January 6 Committee videotapes

In December 2023 Loudermilk publicly stated that the videotaped records of witness depositions taken by the Democratic-led House Select Committee on the January 6 Attack had vanished.[1] Speaking on the "Just the News, No Noise" television program, he said flatly that "all of the videotapes of all depositions are gone."[1]

Loudermilk described how the absence was discovered: early in his subcommittee's investigation he received a call from someone seeking information contained on one of the tapes, and a search of the transferred records turned up nothing.[1] He said he then wrote to [[bennie-thompson|Bennie Thompson]], the Mississippi Democrat who chaired the now-defunct [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]], to request the recordings, and that Thompson confirmed the tapes had not been preserved because he did not believe the committee was required to keep them.[1]

Loudermilk argued that the recordings met the definition of congressional evidence under House rules, in part because segments of the depositions had been played publicly at the committee's hearings, and that the select committee therefore should have retained all of the video material.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized Thompson's stated rationale as implausible given the central role the "insurrection" narrative plays in contemporary American politics, and argued that the failure to preserve the tapes suggests the underlying material contained information damaging to that narrative.[1]

## Deleted encrypted files

In January 2024 Loudermilk's Republican-led House Oversight subcommittee, investigating the January 6 committee and the security failures of that day, revealed that evidence from the earlier probe had been destroyed.[2] Sources close to Loudermilk told Fox News Digital that House rules required the former select committee, chaired by [[bennie-thompson|Bennie Thompson]], to turn over all documents from its investigation, and that Thompson had reportedly said he would transmit four terabytes of archived data.[2] What arrived amounted to less than three terabytes, prompting Loudermilk's committee to hire a digital forensics team to scrape hard drives and determine what was missing.[2]

The forensics team determined that 117 files had been both encrypted and deleted on January 1, 2023 — days before Thompson's team was required to transmit the data to the successor committee and before Republicans retook the House majority.[2] The team recovered all 117 deleted files, and Loudermilk sought answers as to why they had been removed.[2]
