# Shanghai Five

The Shanghai Five was a Chinese-led security compact set up in 1996 comprising China, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Russia and Tajikistan.[1] Its purpose was to secure China's borders in an attempt to reduce the chaos of a new age of radical Islamist warfare across Central Asia.[1]

## Relationship to the SCO

The grouping is the direct institutional ancestor of the [[shanghai-cooperation-organization|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]], founded in 2001.[1] The decisive step was taken by Russian strategist [[yevgeny-primakov|Yevgeny Primakov]], who brought Russia into deeper alliance with the compact as part of a broader doctrine of multipolarity built on a Russian–Chinese–Iranian core.[1] Primakov and his allies read the coming Eurasian instability as the intended outcome of a balkanization and asymmetric-warfare strategy of the sort outlined in [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]]'s writings and pursued by [[nato|NATO]].[1]
