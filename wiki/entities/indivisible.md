# Indivisible

Indivisible is a left-wing American activist organization known for organizing mass protest mobilizations. In January 2026 it was identified as the lead organizer, together with allied groups, of a nationwide wave of demonstrations against U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement.[1]

## "ICE Out For Good" mobilization

The protests were staged under the banner "ICE Out For Good" following the fatal shooting of [[renee-nicole-good|Renee Nicole Good]] during a confrontation with an ICE agent in Minneapolis.[1] Organizers claimed more than 1,000 protests took place nationwide, with the largest gatherings in Washington, D.C. and Philadelphia.[1] In Minneapolis, thousands rallied, including Representative [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]].[1]

Smaller actions were reported elsewhere: roughly 500 people demonstrated outside a Home Depot in Boca Raton, Florida, where police arrested one individual on battery charges after an altercation with a demonstrator, and protests in Los Angeles, Austin, and Houston also produced several arrests.[1]

## Badlands framing

Badlands writers treated the mobilization as an astroturfed narrative operation rather than a spontaneous uprising, drawing an explicit comparison to the 2020 George Floyd protests and characterizing the 2026 wave as "like that but more shrill and unhinged."[1] The rapid, coordinated scale of the response — more than a thousand simultaneous events — was cited as evidence of pre-existing organizational infrastructure rather than organic outrage.[1]

## Texas data-center opposition (2026)

A May 2026 report described Indivisible chapters and allied organizations — groups connected to the broader funding network associated with [[george-soros|George Soros]] — as participating in local campaigns opposing major AI and data-center expansion projects in Texas.[2] The resistance efforts centered on energy use, water consumption, land acquisition and grid strain, with critics arguing the campaigns could slow Texas' bid to become a national AI infrastructure hub while organizers said their focus was community and environmental concerns.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] remarked that Indivisible's opposition instinctively made her want to support the projects, while cautioning that this reaction could itself be the point of the operation and that withholding belief remains critical.[2] See [[golden-age-infrastructure]].

## Origins and network ties

The Indivisible Project is a left-of-center 501(c)(4) tax-exempt organization headquartered in Washington, D.C., created in late 2016 as a response to the election of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]; it grew out of a widely circulated Google doc published in 2017.[3] Its most prominent allies include the [[democratic-socialists-of-america|Democratic Socialists of America]], the [[american-civil-liberties-union|American Civil Liberties Union]], [[moveon-org|MoveOn.org]], [[planned-parenthood|Planned Parenthood]], the [[working-families-party|Working Families Party]] and the [[tides-foundation|Tides Foundation]].[3]

Although Indivisible claims not to be part of the Democratic Party establishment, the Tides Foundation — founded in 1976 and described as a pass-through organization and pillar of the Left — is a funding partner for the Indivisible Fund, now Indivisible Civics, the group's 501(c)(3) nonprofit arm.[3] Its policy director, [[angel-padilla|Angel Padilla]], previously worked as an analyst at the [[national-immigration-law-center|National Immigration Law Center]], funded by grants from George Soros's [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]], and as an immigration policy consultant at [[unidosus|UnidosUS]], formerly the National Council of La Raza.[3]

## Role in Resistance 2.0

Indivisible was among the sponsor organizations that, in the lead-up to the 2024 election, joined with the Working Families Party, MoveOn, Public Citizen and the ACLU to prepare a response to a second Trump term.[3] The coalition ultimately aligned more than 100 groups plus Representative [[pramila-jayapal|Pramila Jayapal]], and scheduled eight roughly weekly "Mass Calls" beginning November 7, 2024 — an effort branded [[resistance-2-0|"Resistance 2.0"]].[3] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued these groups could identify no concrete policy objection beyond [[project-2025|Project 2025]], and predicted the NGO network and its lawyers would pursue [[lawfare|lawfare]] as the main vector of attack.[3]
