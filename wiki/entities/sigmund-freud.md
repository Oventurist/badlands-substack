# Sigmund Freud

Sigmund Freud was an Austrian neurologist described in Badlands Media commentary as the originator of psychoanalysis and, indirectly, of the modern techniques of mass persuasion that spread through American institutions in the 1910s.[1]

## Origins of psychoanalysis

Freud was introduced to hypnosis in 1885, an encounter that led him toward what he considered more consistent techniques for treating ailments. Out of that work came [[psychoanalysis]], a clinical method for evaluating and treating pathologies understood as arising from conflicts within the psyche and addressed through dialogue between patient and analyst — in effect, treating people for ailments occurring at an unconscious level.[1]

## Transmission to the United States

Freud's work eventually reached [[james-jackson-putnam|James Jackson Putnam]], Professor of Diseases of the Nervous System at [[harvard-university|Harvard]], whose public endorsement is credited as a significant breakthrough for the psychoanalytic cause in the United States. In 1911, drawing on Freud's work, Putnam and fellow neurologist [[ernest-jones|Ernest Jones]] organized the founding of the [[american-psychoanalytic-association|American Psychoanalytic Association]], with the two elected president and secretary respectively.[1]

## Legacy through Bernays

Freud's American nephew [[edward-bernays|Edward Bernays]] graduated from Cornell University in 1912 and applied his uncle's understanding of the unconscious mind to journalism and commerce, becoming the father of public relations and modern advertising.[1] In the Badlands framing, this lineage made the 1910s the decade in which Americans first began to be altered, affected and controlled at an unconscious level.[1]
