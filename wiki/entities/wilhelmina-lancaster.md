# Wilhelmina Lancaster

Sister Wilhelmina Lancaster was an American nun, foundress-associated with the [[benedictines-of-mary-queen-of-apostles|Benedictines of Mary, Queen of Apostles]] in Gower, Missouri, whose exhumed body drew mass pilgrimage in May 2023 after it was found to have barely decomposed since her death.[1]

## Exhumation and pilgrimage

Lancaster died on May 29, 2019, at the age of 95, and was exhumed in April 2023, nearly four years later, at the community's monastery in Gower, roughly 45 minutes north of Kansas City.[1] Thousands travelled to the small Missouri town over the following weekend to view the remains, which were set to be placed in a glass shrine in the church.[1] CBS News quoted Samuel Dawson, 63, who visited with his son and was raised Sicilian Catholic, saying: "With all the negativity in the world, it was beautiful to see people from all races from around the country come together in peace."[1]

## Question of incorruptibility

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported that the exhumation was prompted by construction, according to nuns close to the matter, and that Lancaster had not been embalmed before her 2019 burial in a wooden coffin set in "very moist clay."[1] The local diocese began investigating the case as a possible instance of [[incorruptibility|incorruptibility]], which Catholic tradition holds "indicates a divine intervention that stops or slows decomposition as a sign of holiness."[1]

Ashe in America declined to adjudicate whether natural or divine causes were at work, but noted that since embalming is meant to preserve, an unembalmed body would ordinarily be expected to decompose more quickly, making the outcome "both intriguing and counterintuitive."[1] She reported that 10,000–15,000 people per day were travelling from around the world to see Lancaster, that Canon Law permits inspection of an "incorruptible saint," and that visitors were reminded not to touch the remains.[1]
