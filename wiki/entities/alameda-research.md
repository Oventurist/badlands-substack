# Alameda Research

Alameda Research was the hedge fund and sister company of the cryptocurrency exchange [[ftx|FTX]], both founded by [[sam-bankman-fried|Sam Bankman-Fried]].[1]

## Role in the FTX fraud case

Alameda Research was central to the criminal fraud case against Bankman-Fried tried in Manhattan federal court before Judge [[lewis-kaplan|Lewis Kaplan]].[1] Prosecutors argued that Bankman-Fried used FTX as a personal piggy bank, taking customer money to enrich himself and his family, buy luxury beachfront property in the Bahamas, and funnel millions into U.S. political campaigns.[1]

Testifying on the fourth day of his time on the stand, Bankman-Fried said he knew as early as 2020 that FTX customer funds were being held in a bank account controlled by Alameda Research, and that he did not recall giving any directions to Alameda employees to safeguard those funds.[1] The government countered that he was well aware he was misrepresenting key aspects of his business to investors, customers, and lawmakers in Congress.[1]

## Caroline Ellison's cooperation

Alameda's former co-chief executive [[caroline-ellison|Caroline Ellison]] reportedly cut a deal with federal prosecutors in 2022 in the aftermath of the [[ftx|FTX]] collapse.[2] Writing in May 2023, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] objected to media efforts to portray Ellison as a victim — including arguments that she had been underpaid because she is a woman — and insisted that she and the wider network that enabled the fraud be held accountable alongside [[sam-bankman-fried|Sam Bankman-Fried]].[2]

## Caroline Ellison's release from prison, 2025

[[caroline-ellison|Caroline Ellison]], the former chief executive of the Alameda Research cryptocurrency hedge fund founded by [[sam-bankman-fried|Sam Bankman-Fried]], was quietly moved out of federal lockup after serving roughly 11 months of her two-year sentence, Business Insider reported in December 2025.[3] She was transferred on October 16 from the Danbury Federal Correctional Institution in Connecticut to community confinement — either home confinement or a halfway house — remaining in federal custody.[3]
