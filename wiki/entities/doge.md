# DOGE (Department of Government Efficiency)

DOGE appears in Erik Carlson's essay through an extended analogy to the film *Office Space*.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## The analogy

Carlson invites readers to think of DOGE as the two "Bobs" brought into a company to improve efficiency: they interview employees, asking each what it is they actually do, and discover that many within the organization literally do nothing or are redundant — resulting in mass layoffs and a company living in fear.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) He asks whether this sounds familiar.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

The comparison sits alongside his framing of Israel, Iran and China as the *Office Space* manager who circulates with a coffee mug asking staff to work Saturdays — the recognizable face who is not making the decisions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths) Behind the scenes, Carlson argues, the real work is being done by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] against the [[city-of-london|City of London]], while those in charge of the [[deep-state-cabal|Cabal]] are flushed out.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths)

## Legacy and the accountability phase

Writing in June 2026, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] credited DOGE with exposing waste, fraud, and abuse and with deploying modern tooling inside government to monitor and flag it in what she called "the golden age government."[2] She argued, however, that no golden age is possible without accountability for the preceding era, pointing to [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]]'s criminal referrals over green energy grants as the kind of follow-through required, and warning that absent transparency and prosecution "the next corrupt scheme will be emboldened to go even harder."[2]

## DOGE as tooling

In February 2026 [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that DOGE is best understood as a technology transformation program running the standard phases of plan, design, test, deploy, review, and launch, with planning likely completed before Inauguration Day and later phases dependent on systems and data access.[3] She noted that the executive order creating DOGE explicitly framed it as a technology endeavor, directing the USDS Administrator to commence a Software Modernization Initiative covering government-wide software, network infrastructure, and IT systems, and requiring agency heads to ensure USDS had "full and prompt access to all unclassified agency records, software systems, and IT systems" under rigorous data protection standards.[3]

"DOGE was tooling," she concluded, contrasting corporate environments where employees are tracked to the keystroke with a federal government built on plausible deniability.[3] Findings attributed to [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and his team included Treasury payment approval officers instructed to always approve payments — zero denials across entire careers, even for known fraudulent or terrorist-linked recipients; budget codes optional or blank for nearly 70% of federal spending, about $4.7 trillion; agencies buying 37,000 software licenses for 13,000 employees; 47 redundant financial systems across 24 major agencies and 111 instances of 59 different HR products; retirements hand-processed in a Pennsylvania limestone mine by over 700 workers using manila envelopes and cardboard boxes at roughly 10,000 applications a month; and an incomplete website modernization project that burned over $5 million with no output.[3] Musk described most government systems as "shockingly primitive."[3]

With Musk departed from government service in May 2025 and the temporary DOGE organization shut down in November, Ashe held that the installed tooling was likely doing its job but required human follow-through — supplied by Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] through the [[war-on-fraud|War on Fraud]].[3] She asked openly whether the DOGE tooling came from [[palantir|Palantir]].[3]

## July 4, 2026 wind-down of the central office

The [[white-house|White House]] marked July 4, 2026 as the formal conclusion of DOGE's initial mission, closing the temporary initiative created by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to identify waste, fraud, and inefficiency throughout the federal government.[4] While [[politico|Politico]] characterized the move as the end of DOGE, Trump administration officials said the effort was continuing under a different structure rather than being dismantled, with one official describing DOGE as remaining "alive in spirit" and its recommendations now carried out directly by individual departments and agencies.[4]

According to administration officials, many DOGE personnel transitioned into permanent roles across the federal government to oversee implementation of the initiative's reforms.[4] Agencies were expected to continue reviewing contracts, reducing unnecessary spending, modernizing technology systems, streamlining operations, and pursuing workforce efficiency measures first developed under the centralized DOGE office.[4] The administration said it would keep tracking and publicizing savings generated through these reforms, portraying the July 4 transition as the beginning of a long-term government-wide management effort.[4]

[[office-of-personnel-management|OPM]] Director [[scott-kupor|Scott Kupor]] told [[reuters|Reuters]] that DOGE "doesn't exist," then clarified on X that it "may not have centralized leadership" but that "the principles of DOGE remain alive and well."[4]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] restated her view that DOGE was a technology project within a broader transformation program, positioned that way in the executive order temporarily establishing it, with the popular narrative of DOGE and its promises externally generated by the media and third-party actors such as [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]].[4] On that reading, DOGE's internal success metric was not money saved but something closer to a comprehensive install with active reporting across agencies and departments.[4] She conceded the theory might be wrong — DOGE could have been a shiny object distracting from expansion of the techno-surveillance state — and suggested both could be true at once.[4] Comparing modern workplace tracking software, which employees fear and employers covet, she argued that citizens as the employers of government should want federal staff to fear being caught defrauding or subverting the public agenda, and that if the DOGE principles endure, additional prosecutions for waste, fraud, and abuse should follow along with a continued shrinking of government's size and expense.[4]

## Fraud investigation wave (2026)

In April 2026, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] linked the simultaneous surfacing of fraud investigations, indictments, and convictions across US states and industries to DOGE, writing that it was "almost like the data dump phase of DOGE yielded actionable information for local, state, and federal investigators."[5] The framing casts DOGE's disclosure of federal spending data as an upstream input to downstream prosecutions by local, state, and federal authorities in the [[2026-fraud-investigations|2026 fraud investigation wave]].[5]

## January 2026: reports of DOGE's demise

In mid-January 2026, Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] discussed DOGE and its status in an interview with [[christopher-rufo|Chris Rufo]], stating that roughly 10% of the US federal budget is lost annually to fraud.[6] According to the DOGE website — tracked daily by [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] on The Daily Herold — the initiative had delivered $215 billion in estimated savings through a combination of asset sales, contract and lease cancellations and renegotiations, fraud and improper payment deletion, grant cancellations, interest savings, programmatic changes, regulatory savings, and workforce reductions.[6] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] commented publicly on DOGE and government efficiency efforts in a [[truth-social|Truth Social]] post that neither announced the dissolution of DOGE nor formally ended the initiative.[6]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that reports of DOGE's demise had been greatly exaggerated, and that what the public perceived as a scaling back or quiet burial during the 2025–2026 transition was in fact a deliberate narrative pivot layered over continuing work on the "Actual" rails.[6] In this reading, DOGE was from the inception of the Trump 2.0 era a dual-front mission statement running along both Actual and Narrative rails rather than a conventional bureaucratic deployment.[6] He cited a five-day surge in which federal agencies under DOGE coordination terminated or descoped 42 contracts totaling $1.5 billion in ceiling value, yielding hundreds of millions in immediate savings as part of the broader $215 billion reclaimed to date, and predicted a 2026 acceleration accompanied by "organic" calls to supercharge DOGE's mandate.[6] He further characterized the Trump–[[elon-musk|Musk]] feud as a "Narrative Pincer" now resolving into public reconciliation, citing Mar-a-Lago dinners, viral photo ops and Musk's declaration that "2026 is going to be amazing."[6]

## February 2025: litigation, the Pentagon, and Fort Knox

In February 2025, Democrat-led states sought an immediate court order barring [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and his allies from accessing data or causing firings across a broad swath of the federal government.[7] At a February 17 hearing, U.S. District Judge [[tanya-chutkan|Tanya Chutkan]] appeared poised to reject that request for lack of concrete evidence, while agreeing that DOGE's operations were taking place in troubling secrecy and moving so swiftly that quick legal conclusions were difficult.[7] "DOGE appears to be moving in no sort of predictable and orderly fashion and plaintiffs are obviously scrambling to find out what's next," she said, adding, "I don't know if that's deliberate or not."[7]

Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] welcomed DOGE to the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], where Musk faced an accounting system that has never passed an audit and decades of rampant price gouging by defense contractors.[7] Defense policy experts said the Pentagon's $850 billion budget was in sore need of oversight, with investigators regularly uncovering billions of dollars of waste.[7] President [[donald-trump|Trump]] floated cutting the defense budget in half after things "settle down" with Russia and China — a sharp turn from Hegseth's same-day comment that "nobody can or should test the extent of America's willingness to invest in national security."[7]

Musk also signaled that DOGE would examine the gold reserve at [[fort-knox|Fort Knox]], amplifying Senator [[mike-lee|Mike Lee]]'s complaint that he had been denied access and drawing support from Senator [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]].[7] Separately, LexisNexis CEO [[haywood-talcove|Haywood Talcove]] testified at a DOGE hearing that verifying recipient identities could save $1 trillion per year, and Musk posted that 400 million Americans appeared eligible for [[social-security-administration|Social Security]] in a nation of roughly 340 million.[7]

### Badlands framing

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the DOGE deployments as accelerated "speed runs" of the [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian Dialectic]] run in reverse: asserting problems with the administrative state, fomenting reactions across the American mindscape and the exposed media protectorate, and then letting Trump and the American people lend mandate to long-term solutions.[7] [[canncon|CannCon]] argued that DOGE functions as a precursor to a larger exposure of the 2020 election, making election-fraud claims legible to moderates by first proving how bloated and inefficient the government is.[7]

## Credit for USAID cancellations (March 2025)

Announcing the cancellation of 83% of US foreign aid contracts in March 2025, Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] thanked DOGE — described as the Department of Government Efficiency headed by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] — for implementing "overdue and historic reform."[8] The 5,200 canceled contracts had, in Rubio's account, spent tens of billions of dollars in ways that did not serve and in some cases harmed core US national interests.[8] Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] rejected celebration of the result, arguing the remaining roughly 1,000 contracts should also have been terminated.[8]

## H-1B dispute (December 2024)

At the end of December 2024, the "twin heads" of DOGE — [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] — appeared to call for expansion of the [[h-1b-visa-program|H-1B visa program]], setting off a week of public conflict with [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] and America First figures who sought to end the skilled foreign worker program.[9] Columnist David Marcus dubbed the episode MAGA's H-1B "civil war" and argued it was exactly how politics is supposed to work, with tensions easing after President-elect [[donald-trump|Trump]] said he likes the program without endorsing its expansion.[9]

## Musk's departure and the spending-bill clash

[[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] stepped down as head of DOGE in mid-2025 and then repeatedly criticized President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "Big Beautiful" tax and spending bill, arguing it undermined his efforts to trim the federal budget.[10] The feud escalated in June 2025, with Musk accusing Trump of "ingratitude," backing calls for his impeachment and threatening to ground the Dragon spacecraft; Trump replied that Musk had "gone crazy" and blamed the split on the end of the "EV mandate."[10] Badlands commentary sided partly with Musk, calling it "laugh out loud funny" that federal spending was rising after everything DOGE had exposed, while noting that a $36 trillion national debt cannot realistically be retired through conventional budgeting.[10]

## Backlash and litigation (March 2025)

DOGE's cost-cutting drew both legal challenge and street protest in early 2025. The [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] prevailed in a federal court case challenging the [[elon-musk|Musk]]-led department's cost-cutting actions, clearing the way for the [[state-department|State Department]] to formally announce the closure of [[usaid|USAID]] on March 28, 2025.[11]

Simultaneously, more than 200 coordinated [[tesla-takedown|#TeslaTakedown]] protests took place across the United States opposing Musk's influence over federal policy through DOGE.[11] Demonstrators objected to the sweeping budget cuts and federal workforce reductions the department had spearheaded, and raised concerns about effects on public health, scientific research, and foreign aid — citing DOGE's attempts to dismantle the [[united-states-institute-of-peace|United States Institute of Peace]] and to cut [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]] staffing by 20%.[11]

## Founding mandate and the 2024 Wall Street Journal op-ed

In November 2024, tech entrepreneurs [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] — named co-chairs of the new government efficiency panel the week before — said their body would identify "thousands" of regulations for President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to eliminate, which they argued would justify "mass head-count reductions" across the federal government.[12] The pair laid out the plan for the "Department of Government Efficiency" in a Wall Street Journal opinion piece, writing that they would advise DOGE "at every step to pursue three major kinds of reform: regulatory rescissions, administrative reductions and cost savings," with a focus on "driving change through executive action based on existing legislation rather than by passing new laws."[12]

Musk and Ramaswamy pointed to recent Supreme Court decisions curbing the power of the administrative state, arguing that a "plethora of current federal regulations" exceeded agency authority and could be rescinded, and that slashing regulations should permit at least proportional cuts to the government workforce: "Not only are fewer employees required to enforce fewer regulations, but the agency would produce fewer regulations once its scope of authority is properly limited."[12] They also preemptively addressed civil service protections that could block the firing of federal workers.[12]

Badlands writer Ashe in America highlighted the pair's declaration that "We are entrepreneurs, not politicians. We will serve as outside volunteers, not federal officials or employees," and noted that their mandate was to deliver results by July 4, 2026 rather than produce an analysis or report.[12] The commentary described the expected outcome as a mass reduction in force (RIF) across the federal government, guided by DOGE's stated aim to "identify the minimum number of employees required at an agency for it to perform its constitutionally permissible and statutorily mandated functions," and argued that applying cuts across every agency would make claims of retaliation difficult to sustain.[12] It cited DOGE's stated goal of helping affected workers transition into the private sector, and quoted the op-ed's closing line: "We are prepared for the onslaught from entrenched interests in Washington. We expect to prevail."[12]

## Role in the December 2024 spending fight

Ahead of the second Trump administration, DOGE existed as an advisory panel focused on cutting spending and the size of the federal government, co-led by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]].[13] In December 2024 the two co-leaders helped drive the wave of Republican opposition that killed a bipartisan spending deal, an effort that ended with President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Vice President-elect [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] urging Republicans to oppose the measure; see the [[2024-december-continuing-resolution-fight|December 2024 continuing resolution fight]].[13] Rep. [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]], responding to Sen. [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]]'s suggestion that Musk be made Speaker of the House, wrote that "DOGE can only truly be accomplished by reigning in Congress to enact real government efficiency."[13]

## USAID injunction (March 2025)

On March 18, 2025, U.S. District Judge [[theodore-chuang|Theodore Chuang]] in Maryland indefinitely blocked DOGE from making further cuts to [[usaid|USAID]], ruling that its dismantling of the agency likely violated the Constitution.[14] The order required the administration to restore email and computer access to all USAID employees, including those on administrative leave, though it did not reverse firings or fully restore the agency.[14]

The case was described as one of the first DOGE lawsuits brought against [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] personally.[14] Chuang rejected the administration's position that Musk was merely an adviser to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], finding that his public statements and social media posts — notably one in which he said he had "fed USAID into the wood chipper" — demonstrated "firm control over DOGE."[14] Badlands commentary dismissed the ruling's authority, with Chris Paul writing that judges "have no say in any of these matters" and describing the situation as a "collision course."[14]

Separately, the [[dogequest|Dogequest]] doxxing site published the personal information of DOGE employees amid a wave of attacks on Tesla property, including an arson and shooting incident at a Las Vegas service center.[14]

## Departure of Edward Coristine (June 2025)

[[edward-coristine|Edward Coristine]], the 19-year-old DOGE staffer nicknamed "Big Balls" who had been hired by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] for the team, left the White House in June 2025 and joined the [[social-security-administration|Social Security Administration]] as a special government employee focused on improving the agency's website.[15]

## 2025 federal property disposal list

On March 4, 2025, as part of the administration's continued downsizing of the federal government, the [[general-services-administration|General Services Administration]] identified 443 federal properties that might be put up for sale, including the headquarters of the FBI and of the Labor and Justice departments.[16] The "non-core" properties span 47 states, Washington, D.C., and Puerto Rico, and total some 80 million square feet; the GSA said a sale could save more than $430 million in annual operating costs.[16] Badlands commentary also raised the prospect of applying a DOGE-style approach to corporations, suggesting [[larry-fink|Larry Fink]] could accelerate such a process.[16]

## Young engineering team (February 2025)

Reporting in early February 2025 profiled the cohort of young software engineers recruited into DOGE and tasked with "modernizing federal technology and software to maximize governmental efficiency and productivity."[17] Six young men between the ages of 19 and 24 — Akash Bobba, Edward Coristine, Luke Farritor, Gautier Cole Killian, Gavin Kliger and Ethan Shaotran — had taken up various roles furthering the DOGE agenda, according to a Wired report relayed by Fox News.[17] Bobba was part of the Management, Entrepreneurship, and Technology program at UC Berkeley and had held internships at the hedge fund Bridgewater Associates, at Meta, and at Palantir.[17] Grata AI chief executive Charis Zhang publicly vouched for Bobba on X, recounting that after accidentally deleting an entire codebase two days before a Berkeley deadline, Bobba rewrote everything from scratch in one night, better than before, and the team submitted early and placed first in the class.[17]

Badlands contributor Jordan Sather argued that Washington's anger at DOGE stemmed from twenty-year-olds "kid-splaining" bureaucrats twice their age and proving them incompetent, calling the group heroes helping to save the country.[17] Opposition to DOGE also surfaced in street politics: Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer attended a rally with members of Congress opposing Trump giving Elon Musk access to the federal government, and specifically the U.S. Treasury.[17]

## "What did you do last week?" directive (February 2025)

On Saturday 22 February 2025, federal workers received an email from the [[office-of-personnel-management|Office of Personnel Management]] with the subject line "What did you do last week?", instructing recipients to reply with five examples of what they had accomplished in the past week, excluding classified information, and to copy their supervisor.[18] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], who oversaw the Department of Government Efficiency, posted on X that "failure to respond will be taken as a resignation," describing the directive as "consistent with President [Trump's] instructions," though the email itself contained no such threat.[18]

The stated deadline for replies was Monday at midnight.[18] At least three agencies told their workers to ignore the request and not to respond, raising doubts about the enforceability of Musk's threat, since federal workers outside OPM do not report to that agency and it cannot dismiss employees directly.[18]

Badlands commentator Ashe in America compared the exercise to the "standup" reporting used in manufacturing, engineering and technology development, noting that most professionals routinely report progress and barriers on a daily basis, and argued that officials who complained about producing five bullet points for a week's work had "a character problem" as well as a culture problem.[18] Separately, a New York Post opinion piece argued that DOGE's scrutiny of [[usaid|USAID]] spending was warranted given the agency's record in Haiti and elsewhere.[18]

## First Cabinet meeting and CIA backlash (February 2025)

At the first Cabinet meeting of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second term on February 26, 2025, Trump ceded the top of the meeting to [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], who summarized DOGE's efforts amid tensions between him and several senior administration officials.[19] Seated in a row of chairs behind the Cabinet table in a black MAGA hat and a black T-shirt reading "Tech Support" — which he called a "literal description" of the U.S. DOGE Service's work — Musk outlined how far-reaching DOGE's work had become and argued it was necessary to the nation's economic health: "We simply cannot sustain as a country $2 trillion deficits. Just the interest on the national debt now exceeds the Defense Department's spending. If this continues, the country will become de facto bankrupt."[19] Trump offered full-throated support for the mission.[19]

Separately, [[cnn|CNN]] reported that senior [[cia|CIA]] officers unhappy with DOGE-driven dismissals were "quietly discussing" the risk of disgruntled former employees taking what they know to a foreign intelligence service, prompting Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] to accuse them of using the network as a propaganda arm to make indirect threats.[19] Badlands commentator CannCon also linked the collapse of Democratic donor funding to DOGE "cleaning house," arguing a large chunk of those donations were never real.[19]

## Backlash and tariff-era criticism (April 2025)

By April 2025 [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s leadership of DOGE's cuts to federal spending had become one of the central grievances animating the nationwide [[hands-off-protests|"Hands Off!" protests]] of 5 April, alongside the mass firing of federal workers and immigration raids.[20] White House trade adviser [[peter-navarro|Peter Navarro]] drew a boundary around Musk's remit after the Tesla chief criticized the administration's tariffs, saying "Elon when he's in his DOGE lane is great, but we understand what's going on here. Elon sells cars."[20]

## Access to the Treasury payment system (2025)

Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] gave DOGE representatives access to the federal payment system late on Friday, January 31, 2025, handing [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and the team he was leading a powerful tool to monitor and potentially limit government spending.[21] The move followed a standoff with [[david-lebryk|David Lebryk]], a career civil servant at the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] who had resisted allowing DOGE into the system; Lebryk was placed on leave and then abruptly retired.[21] Reporting noted the system could give the administration another mechanism to attempt to unilaterally restrict disbursement of money approved by Congress for specific purposes, a push that had faced legal roadblocks.[21]

Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] disputed the framing that Musk personally runs DOGE outside constitutional authority, noting that DOGE was established via executive order and reports to White House Chief of Staff [[susie-wiles|Susie Wiles]].[21] The piece argued that DOGE's vocal disclosures of waste, fraud, and abuse were angering the public and panicking entrenched interests, and that the bipartisan reaction was itself telling.[21]

## Origins: the 2024 government efficiency commission proposal

The initiative traces to September 5, 2024, when [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] unveiled a slate of economic plans at the Economic Club of New York and announced that [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] had agreed to lead a proposed [[government-efficiency-commission|government efficiency commission]] if Trump were elected in November.[22] "At the suggestion of Elon Musk, who has given me his complete and total endorsement … I will create a government efficiency commission tasked with conducting a complete financial and performance audit of the entire federal government and making recommendations for drastic reforms," Trump said.[22] The proposal followed a conversation between the two men on X in which the Tesla CEO suggested creating such a group to tackle inflation and appoint him to it, to which Trump replied, "I'd love it."[22]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] wrote that Musk's early references to DOGE — which he rendered as "Director of Government Efficiency" — combined with Musk's status as the largest [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] contractor under Trump and operator of the world's most powerful decentralized communications infrastructure, proved rather than merely suggested "the convergence of something that was planned long ago."[22]

## March 2025 federal layoff data

Data released by the outplacement firm [[challenger-gray-and-christmas|Challenger, Gray & Christmas]] on April 3, 2025 showed a 60 percent surge in announced US job cuts, totalling 275,240 positions in March — the highest monthly figure since May 2020 and the third-largest on record — driven largely by reductions among federal workers and contractors.[23] The cuts were heavily concentrated in Washington, D.C., with roughly 280,253 planned federal worker and contractor layoffs across 27 agencies over the preceding two months as the administration sought to eliminate waste and inefficiency and bring federal spending under control.[23] Terminated federal aid and contracts produced a further 4,429 job cuts, notably at non-profits and health-oriented organizations, which The National Pulse linked to the administration's efforts to defund abortion and transgender programming.[23]

## DOGE as a transformational change program

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized DOGE in April 2025 as "an 18-month transformational change program to optimize America and bring about the Golden Age," arguing that any such program must first get costs under control.[23] On that framing, government is a cost center and every job "created" in government cuts into the profits of the American people as shareholders, since the fully loaded costs of federal employment — salary, benefits, real estate, and retirement — are borne by taxpayers.[23] She drew on [[mckinsey-and-company|McKinsey & Company]] research finding that about 70 percent of business transformations fail, mapping McKinsey's identified pitfalls onto the administration's approach and concluding that firing federal employees from jobs that should not exist is good for the health of the nation.[23]

### DOGE dividend proposal and the EPA $2 billion discovery
Speaking in Miami Beach on February 19, 2025, President Donald Trump said he was weighing a "DOGE dividend": "There's even under consideration a new concept where we give 20% of the DOGE savings to American citizens and 20% goes to paying down debt."[24] The idea originated with investor James Fishback and gained traction after Elon Musk posted that he would "check with the President." While the exact amount DOGE had saved was unclear, Musk had stated a goal of $1 trillion in annual cost cuts.[24] Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick told Fox News that Musk, in his capacity as a senior White House adviser leading DOGE, was "going to cut" $1 trillion, after which the administration would "get rid of all these tax scams that hammer against America" and raise a trillion dollars of revenue — part of a stated goal of abolishing the Internal Revenue Service.[24]

DOGE was also credited with uncovering the $2 billion the Biden administration's Environmental Protection Agency had parked at Citibank for Power Forward Communities, a climate nonprofit linked to Stacey Abrams.[24]

## New York Times savings analysis (December 2025)

A December 2025 New York Times analysis found that DOGE said it had made more than 29,000 cuts to the federal government — slashing billion-dollar contracts, canceling thousands of grants and pushing out civil servants — but did not do what [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] said it would: reduce federal spending by $1 trillion before October.[25] On DOGE's watch, federal spending did not go down at all; it went up.[25] According to the analysis, many of the largest savings DOGE claimed turned out to be wrong, and in DOGE's published list of canceled contracts and grants the 13 largest were all incorrect; the thousands of smaller cuts that were real jolted foreign aid recipients, American small businesses and local service providers but amounted to little in the scale of the federal budget.[25]

## State lawsuit over DOGE's authority (February 2025)
