# Edgar Allan Poe

Edgar Allan Poe was an American writer who, in Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], is presented not merely as a literary figure but as a third-generation member of the [[society-of-the-cincinnati|Society of the Cincinnatus]] active within the transatlantic republican networks of the early 1830s.[1]

## Republican networks

As a young man Poe met with the [[marquis-de-lafayette|Marquis de Lafayette]] during the latter's American tour.[1] Ehret argues that in 1832, on the evidence of letters written to the head of West Point, General Thayer, and of letters by Alexandre Dumas, Poe was himself in France alongside a vast network of fellow Cincinnatus members who had followed Lafayette back from America.[1] The article cites a letter discovered in 1929 by the rare book dealer David Wells, in which Dumas recalls that "it was about the year 1832" when an American arrived at his house with an introduction from [[james-fenimore-cooper|James Fenimore Cooper]]: "His name was Edgar Poe. From the outset, I realized that I had to deal with a remarkable man."[1] Poe is listed among the American literary figures — with Cooper, Washington Irving and the physician Samuel Gridley Howe — who were coordinating a republican movement in Europe at the time of Lafayette's second chance.[1]
