# Southwest Airlines

Southwest Airlines is a United States discount carrier historically known for an unconventional business model built around open seating and free checked bags.[1] During 2024 and 2025 the airline dismantled several of those hallmarks following pressure from an activist investor, a shift that Badlands commentators framed as the deliberate destruction of the company's brand identity.[1]

## End of open seating and free bags

Southwest announced in 2024 that it would end its decades-old open-seating policy, long a hallmark of the carrier's model, in favor of assigned seating taking effect the following year.[1] The company also said in early 2025 that it would end its "bags fly free" policy beginning May 28.[1] Commentary in the Badlands News Brief argued that open seating's removal was the first indication the airline was abandoning its brand promises, and that "bags fly free" had made Southwest "a hero in these economic times."[1]

## New fare bundles

Ahead of the assigned-seating rollout, Southwest introduced new fare bundles combining seating, checked bag and boarding options, available for booking sometime in the third quarter for flights departing in 2026.[1] Tony Roach, executive vice president for customer and brand, said the enhanced bundles let customers "customize their trip while still enjoying our industry-leading reliability and hospitality."[1] The airline launched the announcement with a promotion offering up to 30% off all base fares.[1]

## Elliott Investment Management board takeover

Southwest appointed six new independent directors effective November 1, five of them recommended by activist investor [[elliott-investment-management|Elliott Investment Management]].[1] Executive chairman and former CEO Gary Kelly stepped down from the board on that date, earlier than planned.[1] The appointments came as part of "cooperation and information sharing agreements" reached with Elliott, which in June had disclosed a $1.9 billion stake in the carrier and had been seeking to oust CEO Bob Jordan and Kelly and replace much of the board.[1]

## Competitive context

[[united-airlines|United Airlines]] simultaneously expanded at Denver International Airport in direct response to competition from Southwest, which maintains a strong presence at that airport.[1] Badlands commentary found it "humorous" that United was combatting Southwest's competitive pressure at the same moment Southwest was dismantling the distinctive features of its own model, concluding that "United has nothing to worry about."[1]
