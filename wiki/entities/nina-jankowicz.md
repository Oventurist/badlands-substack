# Nina Jankowicz

Nina Jankowicz is an American disinformation researcher who was tapped to lead the [[disinformation-governance-board|Disinformation Governance Board]], a short-lived body created under the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]].[1] Although the board never held a meeting, Jankowicz became one of the most recognizable figures in the American debate over government involvement in policing speech.[1]

## Disinformation Governance Board

The stated purpose of the board was to coordinate disinformation efforts across DHS on topics ranging from migration to countering influence plots attributed to Russia and Iran.[1] The board drew immediate and sustained criticism from Republicans, and the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] dubbed it the "Ministry of Truth" in a reference to George Orwell's novel *1984*, characterizing its remit as targeting "any speech we don't like."[1]

## Defamation suit against Fox News

Jankowicz launched a defamation suit against [[fox-news|Fox News]], alleging that the network published false reports about her that endangered her life and threatened her safety.[1] According to the complaint's underlying premise, Fox News mentioned Jankowicz and her role leading the board more than 300 times in its coverage, with hosts and commentators asserting that she planned to censor speech and applying nicknames including "disinfo overlord," "disinformation czaress," and "minister of truth."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that because the Disinformation Governance Board was an entity under DHS, Jankowicz as its head was a representative of the government, and that criticizing the government is among the most profoundly protected rights under the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[1] The commentary described a government official seeking exemption from criticism by one of the nation's largest news networks as "stunning," particularly given that the same official was tasked with policing speech and acting as "the arbiter of American truth."[1]

Ashe in America warned that if Jankowicz prevailed, it would demonstrate that constitutional protections for both media entities and individuals were "no longer in place," and noted that in the prevailing judicial climate, with what the author called the questionable posture of the courts, she could well succeed.[1] The case was framed as one that could set a historic precedent for free speech.[1] The commentary referred to Jankowicz by the nickname "Scary Poppins" and concluded that she should be kept away from American governance.[1]

## Fundraising for the Fox News suit

On March 3, 2023, [[jack-posobiec|Jack Posobiec]] posted that Jankowicz was soliciting donations to fund her lawsuit, noting that it came one year after he claimed credit for her removal from the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]]; the post drew roughly 1,740 reposts and 9,680 likes.[2] Coverage in the Western Journal characterized the announcement as instantly backfiring.[2]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] called Jankowicz "the wicked witch of censorship" and "Scary Poppins," argued that her suit against [[fox-news|Fox News]] was most likely baseless, and read the mocking public reaction as good news.[2] Deschamps framed the episode within a broader thesis that globalist attempts at mass persuasion had failed and had given way to a "Plan B" of forced compliance and the silencing of dissenters as a display to deter others, concluding that human beings "were designed for freedom and autonomy."[2]

## "Scary Poppins" and the disinformation profession

An April 2024 Badlands article by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] revisited Jankowicz as the emblematic case against the profession of [[disinformation-expert|"disinformation expert"]], recalling the nickname "Scary Poppins" and describing her sardonically as "the stable, unbiased individual" tasked with heading the department's internal "Ministry of Truth."[3] The article characterized her as a quintessential "resistance liberal" who is vocal about her political position, arguing that a brief glimpse at her social-media output shows she is "essentially Taylor Lorenz on crack" and that she is therefore a case study in why such experts are not the unbiased arbiters of truth they are presented as.[3]

The article contended that the "Scary Poppins" public-relations disaster caused the [[disinformation-governance-board|Disinformation Governance Board]] to be shut down but that the underlying effort had only temporarily receded.[3] It further argued that the pattern established in the Jankowicz fallout — treating public distrust of a disinformation official as proof that such officials are needed more than ever — was repeated in the media defence of [[kate-starbird|Kate Starbird]].[3]
