# Gilbert Chagoury

Gilbert Chagoury is a Nigerian-Lebanese businessman and construction magnate described in the Badlands Media essay [[america-for-sale-part-3|America for Sale — Part 3]] as a close ally and business partner of the commodities trader [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]] and one of the largest private benefactors of the Clinton network.

Quoting the New York Post, the article notes that Nigerian media declared in 1999 that the "Gilbert Chagoury-Marc Rich alliance remains a formidable foe," and that the two sold oil together on international markets. In 2000 Chagoury was convicted in Geneva of money laundering and of aiding a criminal organization in connection with the billions of dollars stolen from Nigeria during the rule of dictator Gen. Sani Abacha. The conviction was subsequently expunged in a plea deal — an outcome the Badlands author suggests [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] or [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] may have had a hand in.

In the years after Rich's pardon, the New York Post reported, Chagoury organised a 2003 event at which Bill Clinton was paid $100,000 to speak, donated millions to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]], and in 2009 pledged $1 billion to the [[clinton-global-initiative|Clinton Global Initiative]]. His family was active in Hillary Clinton's 2008 presidential campaign: a Los Angeles relative, Michel Chagoury, served as a bundler and campaign staffer, and numerous other relatives gave the maximum $4,600 each.

The relationship extended into ceremony and honours. In 2009 CGI gave Chagoury's company an award for sustainable development; in 2013 Bill Clinton appeared in Nigeria at a public ceremony for one of Chagoury's projects; Chagoury was an invited guest at Clinton's 60th birthday party and attended the wedding of Clinton aide [[doug-band|Doug Band]], through whom, the article states, almost all CGI money flowed. The author summarises the episode with the observation that CGI "put the whole world up for sale," since Chagoury's billion-dollar pledge was made possible by billions stolen from Nigeria.
