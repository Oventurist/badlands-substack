# National Hurricane Center

The National Hurricane Center is the United States government body responsible for tracking, forecasting, and issuing advisories on tropical cyclones in the Atlantic and eastern Pacific basins. Its bulletins are the reference point for state and federal emergency planning during hurricane season.[1]

## 2024 Atlantic hurricane season

In early October 2024, as communities across Appalachia and the southeastern United States were still reeling from [[hurricane-helene|Hurricane Helene]], the National Hurricane Center was simultaneously tracking multiple developing systems. [[hurricane-milton|Milton]] intensified into a hurricane on Sunday, October 6, and was heading for the west coast of Florida, where the warm waters of the Gulf of Mexico were expected to strengthen it into a Category 3 storm before landfall.[1]

At the same time, the Center was tracking Kirk and Leslie, two tropical depressions in the Atlantic. Commentators noted that these storms carried the potential to compound the already troubled recovery situation in Helene-impacted states, straining the response capacity of [[fema|FEMA]] and state agencies further.[1]
