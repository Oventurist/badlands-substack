# Republicans for the Rule of Law

Republicans for the Rule of Law is the principal initiative of [[defending-democracy-together|Defending Democracy Together]], the conservative anti-[[donald-trump|Trump]] group founded in 2019 by [[william-kristol|Bill Kristol]], Mona Charen, Linda Chavez, Sarah Longwell and Andy Zwick.[1] The project built an advertising campaign pressing Republican members of [[congress|Congress]] to "demand the facts" about the Trump–Ukraine affair during the [[first-impeachment-of-donald-trump|impeachment inquiry]].[1]

## Composition and campaigns

The group describes itself as composed of "life-long Republicans dedicated to defending the institutions of our republic and upholding the rule of law," and consists primarily of traditionally Republican lawyers.[1] Its legal advisory board has included Charles Fried, Solicitor General under [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]]; Wendell Willkie II, grandson of the 1940 Republican presidential nominee; and spokesman Chris Truax, with former Senator Slade Gorton of Washington also serving on the board.[1]

As the impeachment effort advanced, the group spent more than $1 million on cable television advertising on [[fox-news|Fox News]] and [[msnbc|MSNBC]] urging Republicans to "demand the facts" about Trump and Ukraine.[1] Its campaigns further accused Trump of abusing his office to enrich himself, citing his attempt to host the 2020 G7 summit at his own Doral resort, and encouraged Republicans to publicly oppose what the group called his solicitation of foreign interference in U.S. elections; the ads were placed on programming that draws Republican viewers.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Erik Carlson's "The Sabotage of Trump's Administration" (December 27, 2023) presents the group's Ukraine advertising as a case of the [[deep-state|Deep State]] accusing Trump of its own conduct, arguing that establishment figures were themselves enriching themselves through Ukraine while attempting to impeach Trump for asking [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] to investigate corruption there.[1] He notes that [[olivia-troye|Olivia Troye]] became an adviser to the parent organisation after her 2020 departure from the White House.[1]
