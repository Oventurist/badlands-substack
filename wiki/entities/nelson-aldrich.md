# Nelson W. Aldrich

Nelson W. Aldrich was a United States Senator and, at the time, the leader of the Republican Party, who played the central legislative role in the creation of American central banking.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

After the [[panic-of-1907|Panic of 1907]], Aldrich set up the [[national-monetary-commission|National Monetary Commission]] to investigate a better economic system for the United States.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) The Commission concluded that the European model, including the Central Bank of England, was superior, and its reports and recommendations became one of the principal bases for enactment of the [[federal-reserve-act-of-1913|Federal Reserve Act of 1913]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

In 1910, the leaders of the [[populist-party|Populist Party]] convinced Aldrich to transform the entire monetary system of the United States, and the resulting master plan was hatched at the [[jekyll-island-club|Jekyll Island Club]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) Among the attendees, Aldrich is noted for having a daughter who married a Rockefeller.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Jekyll Island, 1910

Badlands Media commentary lists Nelson Aldrich first among the six men who met at the [[jekyll-island-club|Jekyll Island Club]] off the coast of Georgia in November 1910 — alongside A. Piatt Andrew, Henry Davison, Arthur Shelton, Frank Vanderlip and [[paul-warburg|Paul Warburg]] — to draft in secret a plan to reform the nation's banking system.[3] The men are described as representing wealthy bankers such as [[jp-morgan|J.P. Morgan]], the Rockefellers and the [[rothschild-family|Rothschilds]], and the resulting plan laid the foundation for the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve System]].[3]

## Chairman of the National Monetary Commission

Nelson Aldrich, a senator from Rhode Island, chaired the [[national-monetary-commission|National Monetary Commission]] created after the [[panic-of-1907|Panic of 1907]].[4] He represented Newport, Rhode Island, home of America's richest banking families, and was therefore exceptionally well connected.[4] The article notes that the Aldrich family would merge with the Rockefellers to create one of America's most deeply ingrained elite bloodlines: his daughter married John D. Rockefeller Jr., and their five sons included [[nelson-rockefeller|Nelson Rockefeller]], who became Vice President in 1974, and [[david-rockefeller|David Rockefeller]], who became head of the Council on Foreign Relations.[4]

## European Tour and the Aldrich Bill

After the commission was established, Aldrich immediately embarked on a two-year fact-finding tour of Europe, consulting at length with private central bankers in England, France and Germany — all of which the article asserts were controlled by the [[rothschild-family|Rothschilds]].[4] He returned on the 22nd of November, after which America's wealthiest men boarded his private railcar in strict secrecy for the [[jekyll-island-meeting|Jekyll Island meeting]].[4] The resulting [[aldrich-plan|Aldrich bill]] was presented to Congress but was quickly identified as a bill to benefit bankers, and Republican leadership never brought it to a vote.[4]

Aldrich subsequently publicly opposed the successor Glass-Owen Bill in order to make the public believe it was radically different from his own plan, a posture the article calls sufficient to quell suspicion.[4] Aldrich also pushed through the bill legalizing direct income taxation that became the [[sixteenth-amendment|16th Amendment]], passed weeks before the Federal Reserve Act.[4]
