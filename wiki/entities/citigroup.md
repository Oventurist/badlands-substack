# Citigroup

Citigroup is a major American global bank.[1] It was one of three lenders — with [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] and [[bank-of-america|Bank of America]] — slated to provide a $20 billion bank-led debt facility to [[argentina|Argentina]] as part of the [[argentina-bailout-package-2025|2025 Argentina bailout package]] backed by the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1]

That facility was shelved in November 2025 in favor of a smaller, short-term loan package, after Argentina's October congressional elections delivered a resounding victory for President [[javier-milei|Javier Milei]]'s party and sent Argentine bonds and currency rallying.[1]

## Origins and history

Citigroup traces its lineage to National City Bank, later First National City Bank and [[citibank|Citibank]], which Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] calls the most corrupt bank in American history.[2] Under heavy pressure from [[national-city-bank|National City Bank]], the United States displaced France as the dominant power in [[haiti|Haiti]], dissolving its parliament at gunpoint and controlling its finances for more than 30 years, though Haiti is barely mentioned in Citigroup's official timeline and the company declined to open its archives to New York Times reporters.[2]

The 1999 repeal of the [[glass-steagall|Glass-Steagall Act]] allowed the merger of Citibank with Travelers, owner of Salomon Brothers and Smith Barney, creating Citigroup itself.[2] Lange argues this merger led directly to the [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 financial crisis]], in which Citigroup received what he describes as the largest bailout in world history.[2] The book *Borrowed Time* by James Freeman and Vern McKinley put the total rescue at more than $517 billion, roughly $40 billion above the $476 billion in cash and guarantees described in a 2011 congressional report, and argued that across the bank's 206-year history it has repeatedly been saved by "friends in high places."[2]
