# Intel

Intel is a United States semiconductor manufacturer whose processors were targeted by Chinese government procurement restrictions in March 2024.[1]

## Exclusion from Chinese government procurement

New guidelines introduced by Beijing in March 2024 provided for US chips from Intel and [[amd|AMD]] to be phased out of government PCs and servers, as [[china|China]] ramped up a campaign to replace foreign technology with homegrown solutions, according to Financial Times reporting.[1] The same procurement guidance sought to sideline Microsoft's Windows operating system and foreign-made database software in favour of domestic options, and ran alongside a parallel localization drive under way in state-owned enterprises.[1] The Financial Times described the rules as China's most significant step yet to build up domestic substitutes for foreign technology.[1]

## Context in the US-China chip war

The restrictions were characterized as retaliation after the United States took several steps to prevent Chinese companies from acquiring the latest [[nvidia|Nvidia]] artificial intelligence chips and to stop the Dutch equipment maker ASML from sending advanced chipmaking machines to Beijing, measures that produced a one-time flood of Chinese orders ahead of the sales ban.[1] Commentators linked the episode to questions about whether the [[chips-and-science-act|CHIPS and Science Act]] had built comparable domestic substitution capacity in the United States.[1]

## US government 10% equity stake (August 2025)

On August 22, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that the United States government had taken a 10% stake in Intel, which he described as a struggling American chipmaker.[2] "It is my Great Honor to report that the United States of America now fully owns and controls 10% of INTEL, a Great American Company that has an even more incredible future," Trump wrote on Truth Social, adding that he had negotiated the deal with chief executive Lip-Bu Tan.[2] At various points that day Trump valued the shares at $10 billion and $11 billion without providing details, writing that "the United States paid nothing for these Shares."[2]

[[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] connected the transaction to the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] becoming the largest shareholder of [[mp-materials|MP Materials]] with a 40% stake, arguing that with the Intel deal the United States had federalized both rare earth minerals and the products those minerals are used to make.[2]

## Secure Enclave and Pentagon chip production (2024)

In September 2024, Intel officially qualified for as much as $3.5 billion in federal grants to make semiconductors for the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], after reaching a binding agreement with U.S. officials.[3] The money came through the secretive [[secure-enclave|Secure Enclave]] program, which seeks to establish production of advanced chips with military and intelligence applications across multiple states, including a manufacturing facility in Arizona.[3] Although Intel had always been the frontrunner, the award drew pushback from rival chipmakers, concern in Washington about relying on a single firm, and a funding fight across multiple agencies and Capitol Hill that threatened to reduce Intel's total.[3] The award would add to a possible $8.5 billion in grants and $11 billion in loans Intel received in March 2024 under the [[chips-and-science-act|Chips and Science Act]], the 2022 law intended to revitalize U.S. semiconductor manufacturing and reduce reliance on Asia.[3] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the news as strong signal of impending U.S. semiconductor supremacy and as evidence for his "Switch" theory of a delayed economic turn-on timed to a second Trump administration.[3]
