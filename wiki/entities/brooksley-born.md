# Brooksley Born

Brooksley Born was an American financial regulator whose attempt to regulate the derivatives market in the 1990s was blocked, an episode [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] cites as evidence that no level of regulation could function under the post-deregulation financial structure.[1]

## Attempt to regulate derivatives

Ehret writes that under the cartelized financial structure produced by universal banking, [[nafta|NAFTA]], Euro integration and the [[derivatives|derivative]] economy, the degree of conflict of interest and private political power was uncontrollable, as evidenced in the United States by the shutdown of Born's attempt to fight the derivative cancer at its early stages.[1] (Ehret identifies her as head of the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]]; she in fact chaired the Commodity Futures Trading Commission.)[1]

## Aftermath

Her defeat was followed in 1999 by the [[gramm-leach-bliley-act|Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act]], which removed the [[glass-steagall|Glass-Steagall]] separation and opened the way for over-the-counter derivatives to grow from $60 trillion in 2000 to $600 trillion by 2008.[1]
