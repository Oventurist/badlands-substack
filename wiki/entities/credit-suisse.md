# Credit Suisse

Credit Suisse was a Swiss global bank that collapsed in 2023 and was absorbed by its rival [[ubs|UBS]].[1]

In the Badlands account, Credit Suisse was among the financial institutions hit by massive asset forfeitures stemming from the cooperation of banker [[mathias-krull|Mathias Krull]], who laundered billions embezzled from Venezuela's state oil company [[pdvsa|PDVSA]] as well as funds for drug cartels.[1] The bank faced insolvency in 2023 and had to be purchased by fellow Swiss bank UBS in an acquisition described as ostensibly forced by the Swiss government, with the money at issue owed to the government of Venezuela.[1]

## Nazi-era accounts investigation

In February 2026 Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]], chair of the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]], announced that an independent investigation had uncovered 890 previously undisclosed Credit Suisse accounts with potential links to the Nazi regime during World War II.[2] The accounts were held by entities including the German Foreign Office, the SS paramilitary organization, and a German arms-manufacturing company, with evidence that the SS's economic arm maintained an account at the bank — revealing far more extensive ties than previously known.[2] The probe also found that Credit Suisse expropriated funds from accounts belonging to Jewish individuals during the Nazi era and transferred them to Nazi clients, and produced additional detail on schemes assisting Nazis fleeing to [[argentina|Argentina]] after the war.[2] No specifics were released on the amounts held in the accounts or their current status.[2]

The congressional inquiry was initiated by Democratic Senator [[sheldon-whitehouse|Sheldon Whitehouse]], who was first put up to the task in March 2020 by the [[simon-wiesenthal-center|Simon Wiesenthal Center]].[2] Credit Suisse hired former federal prosecutor [[neil-barofsky|Neil Barofsky]] as an independent ombudsman in 2021, pressured him a year later to narrow his inquiry, and fired him in December 2022 — months before the bank's insolvency.[2] Whitehouse and Grassley, believing the firing was intended to conceal the scandal's depth, demanded his reinstatement, which UBS carried out after completing the acquisition in 2023.[2] UBS and Credit Suisse had previously reached a global settlement in 1999 that resolved all Nazi-era claims, including future ones, with both banks issuing apologies.[2] The probe was expected to conclude by early summer 2026, with a final report anticipated by year's end.[2]

## PDVSA accounts and cartel finance

Badlands analysis holds that Credit Suisse's insolvency was most likely triggered by the asset forfeiture conducted under the DOJ's [[operation-money-flight|Operation Money Flight]], which arrested Krull in July 2018 for laundering $1.2 billion embezzled from PDVSA.[2] Argentine press reporting cited by Badlands links Credit Suisse to PDVSA while alluding to the bank's Nazi-era history, and reports that the PDVSA-related accounts used to conceal embezzled money were all opened between 2004 and 2015 — the same corruption timeline cited in [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]]'s 2018 RICO lawsuit, which itself named no bank.[2] That reporting also indicated Credit Suisse held over 18,000 accounts belonging to white-collar criminals and cartel bosses totaling more than $100 billion in assets, and named former PDVSA official [[nervis-villalobos|Nervis Villalobos]], arrested in Spain in 2018.[2]

Badlands frames the convergence of the Wiesenthal-initiated congressional inquiry and the Maduro-derived criminal investigations as evidence that "the global banking system is being exposed—through multiple different tracks—for the fact that it is the actual cartel."[2]

## PROMIS software and CIA laundering allegations

In April 2026 [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that "everything keeps coming up Credit Suisse," observing that a Swiss bank account had become an elitist status symbol in the later twentieth century.[3] Citing research by author Gordon Thomas, he claimed that [[robert-maxwell|Robert Maxwell]] — father of [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]] — was dispatched by [[mossad|Mossad]] to sell the [[promis-software|PROMIS software]] to entities around the world, including Credit Suisse.[3] The backdoored software allegedly allowed Mossad to monitor governments and to track money laundering by organized-crime syndicates through the banking system as well as arms deals through weapons manufacturers.[3]

Through that surveillance, the account holds, Mossad learned that the [[cia|CIA]] had been using Credit Suisse to launder revenue from off-the-books endeavors, and Maxwell's study of those transactions made him the most prolific money launderer of the late 1980s by moving funds between Western and Soviet banks.[3] The same commentary asserts that [[ubs|UBS]], having absorbed Credit Suisse in 2023, was replicating its predecessor's conduct by blocking investigator [[neil-barofsky|Neil Barofsky]] from obtaining internal documents.[3]

Also in April 2026, a US court rejected UBS's bid to limit liability over Nazi-linked Credit Suisse accounts, keeping alive claims that the bank failed to disclose or investigate accounts connected to assets stolen during the Holocaust.[3]

## March 2023 crisis

On Tuesday, March 14, 2023, Credit Suisse Group AG disclosed in its annual report that it had identified "material weaknesses" in its reporting procedures for the financial years 2022 and 2021 and was adopting a remediation plan.[4] The bank stated that for those two years "the group's internal control over financial reporting was not effective," and management concluded that its disclosure controls and procedures were likewise not effective.[4] The weaknesses were said to relate to a failure to design and maintain effective risk assessments in its financial statements.[4]

The annual report had been delayed from the prior week after United States regulators raised last-minute queries, and Credit Suisse did not specify whether those queries had been resolved.[4] In the same period, the cost of insuring the bank's bonds against default climbed to the highest level on record as the collapse of [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] sparked concern about broader contagion in the banking industry.[4]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] described Credit Suisse as "the International Swamp's favorite bank," noting it had been embroiled in scandals for years, leading to multiple investigations with some still ongoing.[4] He observed that shares had hit an all-time low of $2.44 that week, a striking figure against assets under management of $1.29 trillion as of 2022, and warned that if Credit Suisse fell it "will be catastrophic to [their] systems."[4]

## Record default risk, March 13, 2023

Market commentary compiled on March 13, 2023 reported that Credit Suisse credit-default-swap spreads had never closed higher, standing at more than double the level of risk implied at the peak of the 2008 financial crisis.[5] The move came as CDS spreads widened across all of the global banking majors, not merely at the distressed United States regionals, prompting the question of why systemic signals were worsening if the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] backstop announced after the [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] failure was as effective as advertised.[5]

## Parliamentary inquiry sealed for 50 years

Reuters reported in July 2023 that the Swiss parliamentary investigation into the collapse of Credit Suisse would keep its files closed for 50 years, a level of secrecy that triggered concern among Swiss historians.[6] The period, specified in a parliamentary committee document, is two decades longer than the 30 years standard in comparable cases, and the moratorium was justified as ensuring "high levels of confidentiality apply to the investigation."[6] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that banks are fundamentally in the business of trust and that sealing the record of a failure for half a century removes the vetting on which trust depends.[6] See [[credit-suisse-collapse-inquiry|Credit Suisse collapse inquiry]].[6]

## Collapse and Sale to UBS (March 2023)

The failure of [[silicon-valley-bank|SVB]] earlier in March 2023 triggered a chain of dominoes that culminated in the collapse of Credit Suisse, described by ZeroHedge as a systemically important bank with $600 billion in assets.[7] On March 19, 2023, [[ubs|UBS]] agreed to acquire Credit Suisse in a deal whose price rose from an initial CHF 1 billion to CHF 2 billion and finally to CHF 3 billion (about US$3.25 billion), or 0.76 francs per share; Credit Suisse shareholders were to receive one UBS share for every 22.48 Credit Suisse shares.[7] As part of the arrangement, the Swiss National Bank offered UBS 100 billion francs in liquidity assistance while the Swiss government granted a 9 billion-franc guarantee against potential losses on assets UBS was absorbing, which ZeroHedge characterized as a taxpayer-backed bailout.[7]

## Job losses following the UBS takeover

The takeover of Credit Suisse by [[ubs|UBS]] had by the end of 2023 already resulted in at least 13,000 fewer roles at the combined bank, with further large redundancy rounds expected in the year ahead.[8] The reduction formed a substantial part of the [[2023-global-bank-job-cuts|more than 60,000 jobs cut by global banks in 2023]].[8]

## March 15, 2023 central bank liquidity facility

On Wednesday, March 15, 2023, Credit Suisse disclosed that it had uncovered "material weaknesses" relating to its financial reporting and announced late in the day that it would borrow up to $53.7 billion from the [[swiss-national-bank|Swiss National Bank]], a move that raised fears for the stability of the global financial system in the wake of the collapse of [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]].[9] The disclosure sent the bank's shares plunging, drove European banking stocks sharply lower, contributed to a roughly 3 percent fall in the pan-European Stoxx 600 index, and coincided with a nearly 1 percent decline in the Dow Jones industrial average.[9] Compounding the bank's problems, its largest investor, the [[saudi-national-bank|Saudi National Bank]], signaled the same day that it would not rush in with more cash to buttress the firm.[9] Badlands contributor [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] read the Swiss National Bank's public statement as a signal from insiders and predicted Credit Suisse would be "the next big domino to fall in the current ongoing banking crisis."[9]
