# Maurice Joly

Maurice Joly was a 19th-century French writer and lawyer whose 1864 book *Dialogue in Hell Between Machiavelli and Montesquieu* was plagiarized to manufacture [[protocols-of-the-elders-of-zion|*The Protocols of the Learned Elders of Zion*]].[1] Historian [[david-livingstone|David Livingstone]] describes Joly as a protégé of [[adolphe-cremieux|Adolphe Crémieux]], head of the [[alliance-israelite-universelle|Alliance Israelite Universelle]]; Joly was himself a Jew, a lifelong Freemason, and a member of the Rite of Misraïm.[1]

Joly's work contained nothing about a Jewish plot. It was an attack on the political ambitions of Napoleon III, who — represented in the dialogue by Machiavelli — plots to rule the world.[1]

The scale of the borrowing is documented in *Warrant for Genocide*: more than 160 passages in the Protocols, two fifths of the entire text, derive clearly from Joly; in nine chapters the borrowings exceed half the text, in some three quarters, and in Protocol VII nearly the whole. The order of the borrowed passages matches Joly's with fewer than a dozen exceptions, and the Protocols' twenty-four chapters correspond roughly to the *Dialogue*'s twenty-five — described as "as clear a case of plagiarism — and of faking — as one could well desire."[1]
