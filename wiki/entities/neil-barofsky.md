# Neil Barofsky

Neil Barofsky is an American lawyer and former federal prosecutor who has served as the independent investigator examining Nazi-era accounts held at [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]] and, following the bank's collapse, at [[ubs|UBS]].[1] His career has repeatedly placed him at the center of the largest financial-corruption inquiries of the past two decades, a pattern that Badlands writers treat as significant in itself.[1]

## Earlier career

Before his banking-investigation work, Barofsky served as an assistant U.S. district attorney in New York, where he prosecuted fifty leaders of the Colombian militia group FARC on narcotics charges and successfully argued what has been described as one of the largest accounting fraud cases in history.[1] He was subsequently appointed Special Inspector General for the Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) at the heart of the 2008 financial crisis, and was also involved in the Southern District of New York's mortgage-fraud investigation touching the credit-default-swap market.[1] Testifying before a Senate hearing on the roughly $3 trillion bank bailout, Barofsky said that "it is extremely unlikely that the taxpayer will see a full return on its TARP investment."[1]

## Credit Suisse ombudsman

In 2021 Credit Suisse hired Barofsky as an independent ombudsman to conduct an internal investigation into the bank's Nazi-era accounts.[1] A year into the inquiry the bank pressured him to narrow its scope, and in December 2022 he was fired outright — months before Credit Suisse faced insolvency and was forced by the Swiss government into acquisition by UBS.[1] Senators [[sheldon-whitehouse|Sheldon Whitehouse]] and [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]], concluding that the firing was an attempt to conceal the depth of the scandal, intervened and demanded Barofsky's reinstatement; UBS rehired him after completing the acquisition in 2023 and continued the review on a voluntary basis.[1]

## Findings

Barofsky's investigation uncovered 890 previously undisclosed accounts with potential links to the Nazi regime, held by entities including the German Foreign Office, the SS paramilitary organization, and a German arms manufacturer, with evidence that the SS's economic arm banked at Credit Suisse.[1] The probe also found that the bank expropriated funds from accounts belonging to Jewish individuals during the Nazi era and transferred them to Nazi clients, and produced additional detail on schemes assisting Nazis fleeing to [[argentina|Argentina]] after the war.[1] The investigation was expected to conclude by early summer 2026, with a final report anticipated by the end of that year.[1]

Badlands commentary emphasizes that Barofsky "understands the corruption of the banking and financial systems as well as anybody," while also carrying the unusual distinction of having prosecuted Colombian cartel figures on narcotics charges — a combination read as connecting the Swiss banking inquiry to parallel cartel-finance investigations.[1]

## Obstruction allegations at UBS (2026)

In April 2026 [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that [[ubs|UBS]] was blocking Barofsky from acquiring internal documents that could reveal the depth of alleged money-laundering schemes running through [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]], describing the conduct as an imitation of Credit Suisse's own behavior in the final months before its collapse.[2] The commentary noted that Barofsky's report was awaited and would be delivered to Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] in the Senate sometime that year.[2] The allegations surfaced alongside a US court ruling rejecting UBS's attempt to limit liability over Nazi-linked accounts.[2]
