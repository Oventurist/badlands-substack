# Neil Barofsky

Neil Barofsky is an American lawyer and former federal prosecutor who serves as the independent investigator examining Nazi-era accounts at [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]]. His work is the basis of the February 2026 announcement by Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] that 890 previously undisclosed accounts with potential links to the Nazi regime had been uncovered, as described in the Badlands Brief of February 4, 2026.

## Career before the Credit Suisse probe

Barofsky served as an assistant U.S. attorney in New York, where, according to the reporting cited in the Brief, he "prosecuted 50 leaders of the Colombian militia group [[farc|FARC]] on narcotics charges and successfully argued one of the largest accounting fraud cases in history." He subsequently became Special Inspector General for the [[troubled-asset-relief-program|Troubled Asset Relief Program]], the watchdog role at the heart of the 2008 financial crisis response, and was also involved in the mortgage fraud investigation in the Southern District of New York associated with the credit default swap market, described in the source as a $55 trillion exposure. In Senate testimony he stated that "It is extremely unlikely that the taxpayer will see a full return on its TARP investment," referring to the roughly $3 trillion bank bailout.

## The Credit Suisse ombudsman role

Credit Suisse hired Barofsky in 2021 as an independent ombudsman to conduct an internal investigation into the bank's Nazi-era accounts, following a congressional inquiry initiated by Senator [[sheldon-whitehouse|Sheldon Whitehouse]] at the prompting of the [[simon-wiesenthal-center|Simon Wiesenthal Center]]. A year into the investigation the bank pressured him to limit the scope of his inquiry and ultimately fired him in December 2022 — months before Credit Suisse faced insolvency and was forced by the Swiss government into acquisition by [[ubs|UBS]]. Whitehouse and Grassley, believing the firing was intended to conceal the depth of the scandal, demanded his reinstatement; UBS rehired him after the 2023 acquisition closed and continued the review as a voluntary initiative.

## Findings

Barofsky's probe found that Credit Suisse held accounts for entities including the [[german-foreign-office|German Foreign Office]], the [[schutzstaffel|SS]] and a German arms-manufacturing company, that the SS's economic arm maintained an account at the bank, that the bank expropriated funds from Jewish account holders and transferred them to Nazi clients, and that it was involved in schemes to help Nazis flee to [[argentina|Argentina]] after the war. The investigation is expected to conclude by early summer 2026 with a final report by year's end.

The Badlands commentary by [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] emphasizes that Barofsky's combined background in banking corruption and narcotics prosecution makes him unusually well positioned to connect the Swiss banking system to cartel finance.
