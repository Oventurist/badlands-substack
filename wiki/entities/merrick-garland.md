# Merrick Garland

Merrick Garland served as U.S. Attorney General under President Joe Biden and headed the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] during the federal investigations into [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Mar-a-Lago raid

Garland personally approved the August 8, 2022 [[fbi|FBI]] search of Trump's [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]] residence.[1] Two days later, senior DOJ official [[patty-stemler|Patty Stemler]] — described as a longtime department veteran and a Garland ally who had been consulted on Trump-related matters — emailed National Security Division attorney [[sophia-brill|Sophia Brill]] questioning whether Trump had declassified the seized documents and noting that the president as Commander in Chief is not necessarily bound by standard declassification procedures.[1]

Badlands commentary presented the email, unearthed during the Department's own review of federal law enforcement actions, as evidence that Garland's Justice Department harbored early internal doubts about the raid's legal basis even while projecting public confidence.[1] The episode is treated within the broader [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of government]] framework as an instance of policy-level rather than rogue misconduct.[1]

## IRS whistleblower allegations (2023)

In June 2023, testimony from two [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] whistleblowers was characterized by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] as bombshell evidence against the Bidens, the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and Attorney General Garland.[2] The disclosures alleged that Garland's Justice Department buried evidence of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s tax crimes, blocked U.S. Attorney [[david-weiss|David Weiss]] from bringing charges in two jurisdictions, denied Weiss special counsel status, and reduced recommended felony charges to two misdemeanors.[2]


## Release of the Jack Smith Report (January 2025)

Following [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s resignation from the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] on January 10, 2025, department officials argued in a filing to Judge [[aileen-cannon|Aileen Cannon]] that she had no power to block Attorney General Merrick Garland from releasing Smith's final report.[3] Garland said he planned to release publicly only the portion of the report covering Smith's investigation into [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s effort to subvert the 2020 election.[3]

## Public distrust in June 2023

A Harvard/Harris poll released in June 2023 was widely characterized as bad news for Garland and the Justice Department, finding that 55 percent of Americans believed the indictment of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was politically motivated and 56 percent believed it constituted election interference.[4] Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] wrote that the poll captured the level of distrust for the Justice Department and further demonstrated what he had described as the failure of Merrick Garland at the midpoint of his tenure as Attorney General.[4] Turley concluded that the department and the media had "lost the room" with the American people and were appealing primarily to Democrats, about 80 percent of whom supported the indictment.[4] See [[harvard-harris-poll-on-the-trump-indictment|Harvard/Harris poll on the Trump indictment]].[4]

## Partial release of the Jack Smith report

In January 2025 Garland decided to release only the volume of special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s report dealing with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s alleged plans to subvert the transfer of power after his loss in the 2020 election, holding back the Mar-a-Lago classified documents volume while the president-elect's two co-defendants still faced trial.[5] Federal prosecutors made his plans clear in a Wednesday morning filing urging an appeals court to reject a bid by Trump valet [[walt-nauta|Walt Nauta]] and property manager [[carlos-de-oliveira|Carlos De Oliveira]] to bar the attorney general from releasing the report in its entirety.[5] Garland made the determination at Smith's recommendation "to avoid any risk of prejudice," and prosecutors wrote that "the Attorney General determined that he will not make a public release of Volume Two while defendants' cases remain pending. That should be the end of the matter."[5] The decision all but assured that the public would never see Smith's review of Trump's handling of classified records at his Palm Beach resort.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the nuance of the report's release called for "a scalpel, when the situation really necessitates a chainsaw," describing everything done to Trump and his associates by the [[fbi|FBI]] as a farce beyond a mere miscarriage of justice.[5] He questioned whether the country had the luxury of a prolonged [[lawfare|lawfare]] campaign to establish legal precedents, and said he would rather see the corrupt oligarchy exposed for the assassination of President Kennedy, [[september-11-attacks|9/11]], [[october-7-attacks|October 7th]], COVID-19 and the 2020 election — the kind of revelations he believed would catalyze systemic change.[5]

## Criticism over Biden-related referrals (2023)

Badlands commentary in July 2023 predicted that any criminal referrals against [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] arising from the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] investigation would "die in Merrick Garland's DOJ," describing the department under his leadership as a corrupted institution that persecutes political opposition and silences dissent.[6] Law professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] was cited in the same brief for his remark that the FBI and DOJ appeared to be where investigations go to die.[6]

## Contempt of Congress vote, June 2024

On Wednesday, June 12, 2024, House Republicans voted to hold Garland in contempt of Congress for refusing to turn over the audio recordings of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s interviews with former special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]], who had investigated Biden's handling of classified material and declined to bring charges.[7] The vote followed a monthslong dispute over the recordings between House Republicans and the executive branch, escalating after Biden asserted executive privilege over the files, and passed 216 to 207 with a single Republican, Rep. Dave Joyce of Ohio, voting against.[7] Following certification of the report by House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] to the United States attorney for the District of Columbia, the law required the US attorney to bring the matter before a grand jury, though the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] retained its own prosecutorial discretion.[7]

Garland responded that it was "deeply disappointing that this House of Representatives has turned a serious congressional authority into a partisan weapon," adding that the vote disregarded the constitutional separation of powers, the Justice Department's need to protect its investigations, and the substantial information already provided to the committees.[7]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright framed the episode through "patriot control" theory, arguing bicamerally that if Garland belonged to the establishment then a Politico headline declaring "everyone hates him" showed he had failed to do their bidding despite being a Biden appointee, while if Garland were aligned with the patriot side his pariah status supplied narrative shielding for investigations conducted under his DOJ to be used against the Deep State under a second Trump administration.[7] The commentary judged that the media establishment did not appear to know how to handle the contempt vote, consistent with either scenario.[7]

Garland also featured in the June 2024 [[america-first-legal|America First Legal]] complaint to the [[federal-election-commission|Federal Election Commission]], having declined at a congressional hearing to deny the existence of communications between the Biden Justice Department and the office of Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]], while refusing to disclose them.[7]

## Live Nation antitrust suit

On May 23, 2024, Garland announced a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] antitrust lawsuit against [[live-nation-entertainment|Live Nation Entertainment]], the parent company of [[ticketmaster|Ticketmaster]].[8] At the press conference he said: "We are not here today because Live Nation-Ticketmaster's conduct is inconvenient or frustrating. We are here because, as we allege, that conduct is anti-competitive and illegal. It is time to break it up."[8] The suit followed the collapse of Ticketmaster's 2022 presale for [[taylor-swift|Taylor Swift]]'s Eras Tour.[8]

## Grassley-Johnson letter on blocked FBI arrests

Garland was among the recipients — alongside Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] and FBI Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] — of May 2024 letters from Sens. [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] and [[ron-johnson|Ron Johnson]] conveying whistleblower claims that the Obama-Biden [[state-department|State Department]] had actively interfered to stop the [[fbi|FBI]] from executing arrest warrants against people illegally in the United States who were allegedly supporting Iranian weapons-of-mass-destruction financing.[8]

## School board memo and 2023 subpoena

Garland signed the [[doj-school-board-memo-2021|2021 school board memorandum]] in October 2021, citing a "disturbing spike in harassment, intimidation, and threats of violence against school administrators, board members, teachers, and staff" amid broader disputes over COVID-19 policies and how race and gender were addressed in schools.[9] In early February 2023 he was subpoenaed over that memo by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] under Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], in the committee's first subpoenas of the Congress, alongside [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] and Education Secretary [[miguel-cardona|Miguel Cardona]].[9]

## House lawsuit over the Hur interview audio (July 2024)

On Monday, July 1, 2024, House Republicans filed suit against Garland seeking the audio recording of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s interview with the special counsel in his classified documents case, asking the courts to enforce their subpoena and reject the White House's effort to withhold the materials from Congress.[10] The suit, brought by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], followed the White House's assertion of executive privilege to block release of the recording; House Republicans had responded by voting to make Garland the third attorney general in U.S. history held in contempt of Congress, but the Justice Department declined to take up the referral, citing its longstanding practice of not prosecuting officials who withhold materials under a presidential privilege claim.[10] According to the filing, Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] made a "last-ditch effort" to resolve the matter with Garland, who referred the Republicans to the White House; the White House rebuffed the attempt.[10]

In a concurrence to the Supreme Court's presidential-immunity decision issued the same day, Justice [[clarence-thomas|Clarence Thomas]] questioned whether Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], appointed under Garland's authority, had lawful power to prosecute [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — a point Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized as Thomas "dunking on" both men.[10]

## Special counsel report fight (January 2025)

In January 2025, Attorney General Merrick Garland pressed for the public release of [[jack-smith|Special Counsel Jack Smith]]'s final report on the two federal prosecutions of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], drawing accusations that he was trampling the due process rights of Americans named in the document.[11] A brief filed in a Florida federal court in the weekend before January 13, 2025 argued that release of the report by the attorney general would severely prejudice the due process rights of the individuals identified in it, and that it would be outrageous for Garland to continue pushing for disclosure of Volume I given the substantial injury it would cause to [[jeff-clark|Jeffrey Clark]] and potentially dozens of other Americans.[11] Critics tied the effort to the broader pattern of [[lawfare|lawfare]] that they said Garland had permitted under his watch at the [[justice-department|Justice Department]].[11]

## September 2023 House Judiciary oversight hearing

On Wednesday, September 20, 2023, Garland testified before the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] in a roughly five-hour hearing in which Republican lawmakers lambasted his department's handling of criminal probes into [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], the events of January 6 and other high-profile investigations.[12] Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] opened by declaring "the fix is in" and repeatedly accused Garland of "slow walking" the Hunter Biden investigation to benefit President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[12] Garland used his opening statement to argue that Republican efforts to target career officials were "dangerous" amid rising threats against public servants, saying "we will not be intimidated" and "we will not back down from defending our democracy."[12]

Under questioning from Rep. [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] on the Biden family art-dealing arrangement, Garland confirmed that he had neither asked questions about nor investigated the matter.[12] Rep. [[kevin-kiley|Kevin Kiley]] pressed him on the inception of the [[doj-school-board-memo-2021|school board memo]], and Garland cited mainstream media coverage as the impetus while confirming that he had not spoken with [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] before issuing it.[12] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Garland fumbled his words and appeared to perjure himself relative to prior statements, a charge Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] made directly in a fiery exchange, and concluded that although the hearing was theater it was a reminder that Garland never reached the Supreme Court — a slight about which she argued he remained bitter.[12] The session is treated in detail at [[september-2023-garland-house-judiciary-hearing|September 2023 Garland House Judiciary Hearing]].[12]

## June 2023 impeachment threat over the Weiss testimony

On June 26, 2023, House Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] said Republicans would begin an impeachment inquiry into Garland by July 6 if IRS whistleblower [[gary-shapley|Gary Shapley]]'s allegations checked out — specifically the claim that Garland had interfered in the criminal investigation of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]].[13] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the implication of the whistleblower allegations was that Garland lied under oath in his March 1, 2023 testimony before Congress.[13]

In that testimony Garland said the U.S. attorney "has been advised that he has full authority to make those referrals you're talking about or to bring cases in other districts if he needs to do that," adding, "I have not heard anything from that office that suggests they are not able to do anything that the U.S. attorney wants them to do."[13] Representative [[jason-smith|Jason Smith]], chairman of the [[house-ways-and-means-committee|House Ways and Means Committee]], countered that [[david-weiss|David Weiss]] tried to bring charges in the District of Columbia around March 2022 and was denied, and sought to charge in the Central District of California in the fall of 2022 only to have that request denied in January 2023.[13] Ashe in America concluded that if Weiss was in fact denied, Garland either did not know — raising the question of who possessed authority to deny him — or, more likely, lied, and that "there is no universe in which Garland didn't know."[13] Garland doubled down the following Friday, saying Weiss "was given complete authority to make all decisions on his own."[13]

## July 2025 disclosures on the October 2021 memo

On Friday, July 18, 2025, [[america-first-legal|America First Legal]] released documents it said conclusively proved that Garland's October 4, 2021 memo labeling concerned parents as "domestic terrorists" was a political operation of weaponized government rather than lawful enforcement of federal law by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[14] The records, obtained after three and a half years of effort, were said to expose the Biden [[white-house|White House]]'s involvement in pushing a weaponized DOJ, as well as dissent from career attorneys in the Civil Rights Division who warned there was no federal authority or legal basis to target parents whose speech was protected by the First Amendment.[14]

The disclosures allowed the group to complete the timeline between the [[national-school-boards-association|National School Boards Association]] memo — which had urged the DOJ to use the Patriot Act against parents objecting to mask mandates and Critical Race Theory — and the publication of the [[garland-memo|October 4 memo]], confirming AFL's October 2021 prediction that political leadership had circumvented and disregarded the advice of department lawyers.[14]

## The June 2023 Trump indictment

Commenting on the June 8, 2023 [[2023-trump-classified-documents-indictment|indictment of Donald Trump]], legal writer [[technofog|TechnoFog]] said that Attorney General Garland had "done what his predecessors - Comey, McCabe, Mueller - could not," framing the charge as the culmination of years of what he described as prosecutorial and investigative abuses at the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[15] TechnoFog wrote that the department, which under Garland had instigated the investigation and appointed Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], had thereby inserted itself into the 2024 presidential election.[15]

## Contempt referral declined (June 2024)

Late on Friday, June 14, 2024, the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] refused to act on the congressional contempt referral against Garland, declining to prosecute the sitting Attorney General.[16] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the refusal in questioning the value of Congress's investigative campaigns, including the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] probe of [[newsguard|NewsGuard]] announced the day before.[16] Garland's name also headed the caption of [[garland-v-cargill|Garland v. Cargill]], the Supreme Court case decided that same day invalidating the [[bump-stock-ban|bump stock rule]] his department had defended.[16]

## 2024 investigation of Kremlin-linked US commentators

As Attorney General, Garland oversaw a Justice Department push, reported in August 2024, to investigate Americans with ties to [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s state television networks as part of a broad effort to quash potential Kremlin interference in the 2024 presidential election.[20] The [[fbi|FBI]] had earlier that month searched the homes of former UN weapons inspector [[scott-ritter|Scott Ritter]] and of Russian-born [[dimitri-simes|Dimitri Simes]], a former adviser to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2016 campaign, and officials indicated further searches were likely with criminal charges not ruled out.[20] Badlands framed the initiative as Garland's DOJ "going after Putin's mouthpieces" and warned it risked sweeping in ordinary commentators who shared Russian material online.[20]

## Hur recordings and contempt effort (July 2024)

In July 2024 the Justice Department under Garland disclosed that it had located transcripts of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s interviews with a biographer that it had previously denied possessing, records tied to special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s classified-documents investigation.[22] The department argued that releasing audio of Biden's interviews would violate the president's privacy, invite abuse such as deepfakes, and deter future witnesses, while Biden asserted executive privilege over the recordings in a bid to head off House Republicans' effort to hold Garland in contempt of Congress for refusing to release them.[22]

## Contempt proceedings over the Hur recordings, May 2024

On May 16, 2024, House Republicans prepared to meet to consider resolutions holding Attorney General Garland in contempt of Congress for failing to turn over audio recordings of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s interviews with special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]], which had been subpoenaed by the House Judiciary and Oversight committees.[26] Hours before the meeting, Biden invoked executive privilege over the recordings, with [[white-house|White House]] counsel [[ed-siskel|Ed Siskel]] citing the president's commitment to the independence of the Department of Justice and questioning Republicans' motives.[26] The resulting [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] hearing descended into open acrimony between members.[26]
