# Merrick Garland

Merrick Garland served as U.S. Attorney General under President Joe Biden and headed the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] during the federal investigations into [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Mar-a-Lago raid

Garland personally approved the August 8, 2022 [[fbi|FBI]] search of Trump's [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]] residence.[1] Two days later, senior DOJ official [[patty-stemler|Patty Stemler]] — described as a longtime department veteran and a Garland ally who had been consulted on Trump-related matters — emailed National Security Division attorney [[sophia-brill|Sophia Brill]] questioning whether Trump had declassified the seized documents and noting that the president as Commander in Chief is not necessarily bound by standard declassification procedures.[1]

Badlands commentary presented the email, unearthed during the Department's own review of federal law enforcement actions, as evidence that Garland's Justice Department harbored early internal doubts about the raid's legal basis even while projecting public confidence.[1] The episode is treated within the broader [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of government]] framework as an instance of policy-level rather than rogue misconduct.[1]

## IRS whistleblower allegations (2023)

In June 2023, testimony from two [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] whistleblowers was characterized by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] as bombshell evidence against the Bidens, the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and Attorney General Garland.[2] The disclosures alleged that Garland's Justice Department buried evidence of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s tax crimes, blocked U.S. Attorney [[david-weiss|David Weiss]] from bringing charges in two jurisdictions, denied Weiss special counsel status, and reduced recommended felony charges to two misdemeanors.[2]


## Release of the Jack Smith Report (January 2025)

Following [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s resignation from the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] on January 10, 2025, department officials argued in a filing to Judge [[aileen-cannon|Aileen Cannon]] that she had no power to block Attorney General Merrick Garland from releasing Smith's final report.[3] Garland said he planned to release publicly only the portion of the report covering Smith's investigation into [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s effort to subvert the 2020 election.[3]

## Public distrust in June 2023

A Harvard/Harris poll released in June 2023 was widely characterized as bad news for Garland and the Justice Department, finding that 55 percent of Americans believed the indictment of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was politically motivated and 56 percent believed it constituted election interference.[4] Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] wrote that the poll captured the level of distrust for the Justice Department and further demonstrated what he had described as the failure of Merrick Garland at the midpoint of his tenure as Attorney General.[4] Turley concluded that the department and the media had "lost the room" with the American people and were appealing primarily to Democrats, about 80 percent of whom supported the indictment.[4] See [[harvard-harris-poll-on-the-trump-indictment|Harvard/Harris poll on the Trump indictment]].[4]

## Partial release of the Jack Smith report

In January 2025 Garland decided to release only the volume of special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s report dealing with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s alleged plans to subvert the transfer of power after his loss in the 2020 election, holding back the Mar-a-Lago classified documents volume while the president-elect's two co-defendants still faced trial.[5] Federal prosecutors made his plans clear in a Wednesday morning filing urging an appeals court to reject a bid by Trump valet [[walt-nauta|Walt Nauta]] and property manager [[carlos-de-oliveira|Carlos De Oliveira]] to bar the attorney general from releasing the report in its entirety.[5] Garland made the determination at Smith's recommendation "to avoid any risk of prejudice," and prosecutors wrote that "the Attorney General determined that he will not make a public release of Volume Two while defendants' cases remain pending. That should be the end of the matter."[5] The decision all but assured that the public would never see Smith's review of Trump's handling of classified records at his Palm Beach resort.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the nuance of the report's release called for "a scalpel, when the situation really necessitates a chainsaw," describing everything done to Trump and his associates by the [[fbi|FBI]] as a farce beyond a mere miscarriage of justice.[5] He questioned whether the country had the luxury of a prolonged [[lawfare|lawfare]] campaign to establish legal precedents, and said he would rather see the corrupt oligarchy exposed for the assassination of President Kennedy, [[september-11-attacks|9/11]], [[october-7-attacks|October 7th]], COVID-19 and the 2020 election — the kind of revelations he believed would catalyze systemic change.[5]

## Criticism over Biden-related referrals (2023)

Badlands commentary in July 2023 predicted that any criminal referrals against [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] arising from the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] investigation would "die in Merrick Garland's DOJ," describing the department under his leadership as a corrupted institution that persecutes political opposition and silences dissent.[6] Law professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] was cited in the same brief for his remark that the FBI and DOJ appeared to be where investigations go to die.[6]

## Contempt of Congress vote, June 2024

On Wednesday, June 12, 2024, House Republicans voted to hold Garland in contempt of Congress for refusing to turn over the audio recordings of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s interviews with former special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]], who had investigated Biden's handling of classified material and declined to bring charges.[7] The vote followed a monthslong dispute over the recordings between House Republicans and the executive branch, escalating after Biden asserted executive privilege over the files, and passed 216 to 207 with a single Republican, Rep. Dave Joyce of Ohio, voting against.[7] Following certification of the report by House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] to the United States attorney for the District of Columbia, the law required the US attorney to bring the matter before a grand jury, though the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] retained its own prosecutorial discretion.[7]

Garland responded that it was "deeply disappointing that this House of Representatives has turned a serious congressional authority into a partisan weapon," adding that the vote disregarded the constitutional separation of powers, the Justice Department's need to protect its investigations, and the substantial information already provided to the committees.[7]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright framed the episode through "patriot control" theory, arguing bicamerally that if Garland belonged to the establishment then a Politico headline declaring "everyone hates him" showed he had failed to do their bidding despite being a Biden appointee, while if Garland were aligned with the patriot side his pariah status supplied narrative shielding for investigations conducted under his DOJ to be used against the Deep State under a second Trump administration.[7] The commentary judged that the media establishment did not appear to know how to handle the contempt vote, consistent with either scenario.[7]

Garland also featured in the June 2024 [[america-first-legal|America First Legal]] complaint to the [[federal-election-commission|Federal Election Commission]], having declined at a congressional hearing to deny the existence of communications between the Biden Justice Department and the office of Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]], while refusing to disclose them.[7]

## Live Nation antitrust suit

On May 23, 2024, Garland announced a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] antitrust lawsuit against [[live-nation-entertainment|Live Nation Entertainment]], the parent company of [[ticketmaster|Ticketmaster]].[8] At the press conference he said: "We are not here today because Live Nation-Ticketmaster's conduct is inconvenient or frustrating. We are here because, as we allege, that conduct is anti-competitive and illegal. It is time to break it up."[8] The suit followed the collapse of Ticketmaster's 2022 presale for [[taylor-swift|Taylor Swift]]'s Eras Tour.[8]

## Grassley-Johnson letter on blocked FBI arrests

Garland was among the recipients — alongside Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] and FBI Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] — of May 2024 letters from Sens. [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] and [[ron-johnson|Ron Johnson]] conveying whistleblower claims that the Obama-Biden [[state-department|State Department]] had actively interfered to stop the [[fbi|FBI]] from executing arrest warrants against people illegally in the United States who were allegedly supporting Iranian weapons-of-mass-destruction financing.[8]

## School board memo and 2023 subpoena

Garland signed the [[doj-school-board-memo-2021|2021 school board memorandum]] in October 2021, citing a "disturbing spike in harassment, intimidation, and threats of violence against school administrators, board members, teachers, and staff" amid broader disputes over COVID-19 policies and how race and gender were addressed in schools.[9] In early February 2023 he was subpoenaed over that memo by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] under Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], in the committee's first subpoenas of the Congress, alongside [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] and Education Secretary [[miguel-cardona|Miguel Cardona]].[9]
