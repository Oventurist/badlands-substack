# Pfizer

**Pfizer** is an American pharmaceutical company that ranked among the five largest U.S. companies by market capitalization in 2000.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Rise in 2000

*America Has a CIA-Run Economy* attributes Pfizer's size at the turn of the millennium to two developments: pharmaceutical advertising being permitted on broadcast television beginning in 1997, and the public release of Viagra in 1998.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] adds his general characterization of the industry — that Big Pharma pumps the public full of toxins and then sells drugs to fix what it caused.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

Pfizer's presence in that year's top five is cited as evidence of how much more diversified the American corporate landscape was in 2000, when the leaders spanned energy, industrials, technology, pharmaceuticals and retail, compared with the all-AI-big-tech leadership of the [[sp-500-index|S&P 500 Index]] in 2025.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## 2026 blocked safety studies

Studies supporting the safety of Pfizer's COVID-19 vaccine were among the research the FDA blocked from publication in 2026, alongside work on Moderna's COVID-19 vaccine and GSK's Shingrix.[2] See [[fda-blocked-vaccine-safety-studies|FDA Blocked Vaccine Safety Studies]].[2]

## 2023 updated formulation

On September 11, 2023 the U.S. [[food-and-drug-administration|Food and Drug Administration]] authorized an updated Pfizer COVID-19 vaccine alongside [[moderna|Moderna]]'s, approved for people aged 12 and older with emergency authorization for children from six months to 11 years.[3] The reformulated shot targeted the XBB.1.5 Omicron subvariant, already largely displaced by newer strains according to the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]].[3] See [[2023-fda-authorization-of-updated-covid-19-vaccines]].[3]


## Post-pandemic writedowns

In October 2023, as COVID-weary Americans sparked billions of dollars in corporate writedowns across the health-care industry, Pfizer cut $9 billion from its annual sales forecast because of declining demand for its COVID shots and the Paxlovid treatment — one of the most dramatic signs of the turnabout for a company described as among the biggest winners of the pandemic boom.[4]

## Truncated mRNA allegations (2023)

In February 2023 The Epoch Times reported that Pfizer knowingly allowed dangerous components in its vaccines, centering on the presence of [[truncated-mrna|truncated mRNA]] in the COVID-19 product.[5] The [[european-medicines-agency|European Medicines Agency]] had flagged truncated mRNA as the basis of a "major objection" indicating a preclusion of approval, and Pfizer was said not to have investigated its detrimental outcomes.[5] The report further alleged that Pfizer submitted Western blot figures to the [[food-and-drug-administration|Food and Drug Administration]] and the EMA that were digitally generated rather than produced by actual experiments, and that health authorities had taken alarmingly little action.[5] Truncated mRNA was said to potentially contribute to misfolded spike protein-induced fibrous blood clots, autoimmune disorders and cancer, and to have produced drastic batch-to-batch quality variation that could explain differences in adverse events among recipients.[5] Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] summarized the episode as "They knew and they did it anyway," arguing that fines and resignations amount to a cost of doing business and calling for citizen-led peer review to expose scientific fraud.[5]

## Myocarditis documents (March 2023)

On March 16, 2023, [[project-veritas|Project Veritas]] released confidential Pfizer documents which it said revealed that the company possessed "evidence" suggesting an "increased risk of myocarditis" following COVID-19 vaccination as early as 2022.[6] The quoted internal language stated that "there is evidence that suggests patients who receive a COVID-19 vaccine are at an increased risk," and the disclosure spread rapidly on X, accumulating roughly 7,150 reposts and 14,300 likes.[6] Badlands contributor [[absolute-1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] argued the documents established that Pfizer "knew the risk of myocarditis was elevated with the vaccine and they still released it."[6]

## House Judiciary investigation into vaccine trial timing (May 2025)

The [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] launched an investigation into allegations that Pfizer executives deliberately slowed COVID-19 vaccine clinical trials to influence the outcome of the 2020 presidential election.[7] The claims center on comments by [[philip-dormitzer|Philip Dormitzer]], Pfizer's former head of vaccine research and development, who said the company's top research executives intentionally delayed completion of clinical testing so that results would not be available before the election.[7] Dormitzer reportedly told colleagues at his subsequent employer, GlaxoSmithKline, that the timing of the vaccine's release was "not a coincidence," and GSK later passed the information to federal prosecutors in New York.[7] The committee, chaired by Rep. [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], demanded all relevant documents on Pfizer's vaccine trials from March 1, 2020 onward and asked Dormitzer to schedule a transcribed interview by May 29, 2025.[7]

## Reset of 2024 COVID-19 expectations

On December 13, 2023, Pfizer forecast 2024 sales as much as $5 billion below Wall Street expectations, driving its shares to a ten-year low.[8] Revenue from the company's COVID-19 vaccine and treatment, which peaked at $57 billion in 2022, was projected at $8 billion for 2024 — below both the $13 billion analyst consensus and Pfizer's own lowered $12.5 billion view for 2023.[8] Chief Executive Albert Bourla told investors, "We want to be conservative. We want to be reliable so we won't create uncertainty (again), which was the case, unfortunately, this year."[8]

Pfizer had deployed part of its COVID windfall on acquisitions, including a $43 billion purchase of cancer drugmaker Seagen expected to close that week, and had launched a new RSV vaccine whose rollout trailed a rival's shot; the company's shares had fallen 44% over the course of 2023.[8] U.S. COVID vaccination uptake also dropped sharply, with only about 17% of the eligible population taking the most recent updated booster amid declining concern about the virus and vaccine fatigue.[8]

## Texas deceptive-practices investigation (2023)

In May 2023 Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] announced an [[texas-investigation-of-covid-19-vaccine-manufacturers|investigation]] of Pfizer alongside [[moderna|Moderna]] and [[johnson-and-johnson|Johnson & Johnson]], alleging that the companies had misrepresented the safety and efficacy of their COVID-19 vaccines and pledging to examine whether "profit motive or political pressure" shaped pandemic interventions.[9] Paxton stated that the development of the COVID-19 vaccine and "the representations made by and knowledge of Pfizer, Moderna, and Johnson & Johnson" were of profound interest to the public's health and welfare, and that the investigation aimed "to discover the truth."[9]

## Myocarditis study deadline extension

As a condition of approving the Pfizer-BioNTech COVID-19 vaccine, the [[food-and-drug-administration|Food and Drug Administration]] imposed a list of postmarketing requirements that included a study of subclinical myocarditis, or heart inflammation, following receipt of the vaccine.[10] The FDA listed the completion date in 2021 as June 20, 2022, with results due to the agency by the end of 2022.[10] After the deadline passed, the FDA quietly altered the entry, stating that the study had "an original expected completion date of June 30, 2023."[10]

Badlands Media contributor [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] contrasted the delay with Pfizer's statement, issued in response to the [[project-veritas|Project Veritas]] video leak, that the company is "committed to full transparency" about its vaccine production, calling the change a case of moving the goalposts and questioning whether a pharmaceutical company should be conducting a safety study on its own product.[10]

## Texas lawsuit over vaccine efficacy claims (2023)

On November 30, 2023, Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] sued Pfizer, alleging the company "misrepresented" the effectiveness of its COVID-19 vaccine and violated the [[texas-deceptive-trade-practices-act|Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act]] in five separate respects.[11] The complaint alleged that Pfizer gave the impression the vaccine would end the pandemic and that its 95 percent efficacy claim — drawn from a November 2020 analysis measuring protection 28 days after the first dose — was misleading and achieved in a "technical, and artificial way."[11] More than 366 million doses of Pfizer's original coronavirus vaccine had been administered in the United States according to federal data.[11] The case is covered in detail at [[texas-v-pfizer-covid-19-vaccine-lawsuit|Texas v. Pfizer]].[11]

## Link to reported new-onset psychosis

A peer-reviewed systematic review published in Frontiers in Psychiatry on April 12, 2024 examined 21 articles describing 24 cases of new-onset psychosis following COVID-19 vaccination and found that in 33.3 percent of those cases the Pfizer mRNA vaccine "potentially induced adverse psychiatric events."[12] The viral vector [[astrazeneca|AstraZeneca]] shot was linked to psychotic symptoms in 25 percent of cases, and the authors concluded that the data suggested a potential link between young age, mRNA, and viral vector vaccines and new-onset psychosis within seven days post-vaccination.[12] See [[covid-19-vaccine-associated-psychosis|COVID-19 vaccine-associated psychosis]].[12]

## October 2023 guidance collapse

On Friday, 13 October 2023, after the market close, Pfizer slashed its revenue and earnings forecasts for the year after agreeing to accept US returns of its COVID antiviral [[paxlovid|Paxlovid]] amid collapsing demand.[13] The company cut projected 2023 revenue to $58-61 billion, down $9 billion from a prior estimate of $67-70 billion, and reduced adjusted earnings per share guidance to $1.45-$1.65 from $3.25-$3.45 — a cut of more than half.[13] Pfizer simultaneously launched a multi-year, enterprise-wide "cost realignment program" targeting at least $3.5 billion in savings, $1.0 billion of it in 2023 and $2.5 billion in 2024, which ZeroHedge read as a signal of mass layoffs.[13]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the stock had trended downward all year amid public backlash over the shots, that the late-Friday timing meant the news had yet to hit the market, and that an investor call was scheduled for the following Monday at 8:00 a.m. ET to soften the impact.[13] A separate item in the same brief reported a UK National Health Service-led review finding that almost 57 percent of patients who developed rheumatic immune-mediated inflammatory diseases after COVID-19 vaccination had received the Pfizer product.[13]

## Biohaven kickback settlement (2025)

In late January 2025, Pfizer agreed to pay nearly $60 million to resolve Justice Department charges that [[biohaven|Biohaven]], a company it acquired in October 2022, had paid kickbacks to induce physicians to prescribe the migraine drug Nurtec ODT, thereby defrauding Medicare and other federal health care programs.[14] The improper remuneration included speaker honoraria and meals at high-end restaurants, and the scheme ran from March 1, 2020 through September 30, 2022, in violation of the federal anti-kickback statute.[14] The drug had once been promoted by Lady Gaga.[14] Badlands commentary contrasted the government's stated commitment to hold violators accountable "regardless of their status or prestige" with the absence of criminal charges, argued that a $60 million fine is negligible for a company of Pfizer's size, and maintained that predatory behavior will continue until Big Pharma executives are imprisoned.[14]

## 2023 booster demand estimate

In September 2023 Pfizer said only about 24 percent of eligible Americans were likely to receive a COVID-19 booster shot, implying a maximum market of roughly 82 million people — far below earlier projections.[15] The disclosure knocked more than 9 percent off [[moderna|Moderna]]'s market capitalization at one point in Monday afternoon trading and is treated at [[2023-covid-19-vaccine-market-collapse|2023 COVID-19 vaccine market collapse]].[15]

## 2025 nongovernmental vaccine coalition

Pharmaceutical companies, pharmacists, and medical associations with longstanding ties to vaccine manufacturers including Pfizer, Merck, and Sanofi joined a new private coalition in 2025 to bypass the CDC's Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices, following Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s dismissal of all ACIP members over conflicts of interest.[16] The coalition worked with the University of Minnesota's Vaccine Integrity Project to develop a "nongovernmental vaccine system" under which vaccines would be ordered directly from manufacturers.[16]

## 2023 "directed evolution" undercover video

In January 2023 [[project-veritas|Project Veritas]] published undercover footage of a man identified as Pfizer executive [[jordan-trishton-walker|Jordan Trishton Walker]], who purportedly described a company "[[directed-evolution|directed evolution]]" plan to mutate COVID-19 in-house and keep the disease a "cash cow."[17] Confronted by Project Veritas founder [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]], Walker recanted, calling himself a "liar" who had been "trying to impress a person on a date," requested police involvement, and physically attacked O'Keefe and his film crew.[17] The video attracted congressional curiosity but also intermittent Big Tech throttling and a partial media blackout, with the [[daily-mail|Daily Mail]] removing its article on the sting; critics read the episode as an attempt to rebrand [[gain-of-function-research|gain-of-function research]] before public exposure was intended.[17]

## IgG4 immune-exhaustion research (2023)

A study reported in June 2023 argued that repeated COVID-19 vaccination weakens the immune system, potentially leaving recipients susceptible to life-threatening conditions such as cancer.[18] Researchers noted that multiple doses of the Pfizer or [[moderna|Moderna]] vaccines produce higher levels of the antibody subclass IgG4, and that a growing body of evidence indicated these "abnormally high levels" render the immune system more susceptible to the spike protein contained in the vaccines.[18] The paper cited mouse experiments in which boosters on top of the initial vaccination "significantly decreased" protection against both the Delta and Omicron variants, and testing showing a spike in IgG4 levels after repeat Pfizer vaccination that suggested immune exhaustion.[18] Separate studies had detected higher IgG4 levels in people who died with COVID-19 than in those who recovered.[18]
