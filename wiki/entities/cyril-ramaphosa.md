# Cyril Ramaphosa

Cyril Ramaphosa is the President of [[south-africa|South Africa]] and, in Badlands coverage, the counterpart confronted by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] during a White House meeting on Wednesday, May 21, 2025.[1]

## May 2025 White House meeting

The meeting was initially intended to focus on trade and bilateral relations, but shifted to the treatment of South Africa's white minority after a journalist asked what would convince Trump that there is "no white genocide in South Africa."[1] Ramaphosa interjected, emphasizing the need to listen to the voices of US "friends" in order to obtain a full perspective on the issue, prompting Trump to respond, "We have thousands of stories talking about it... I could show you a couple of things."[1] Trump then instructed his staff to "Turn the lights down, and just put this on," and played a five-minute video montage that included clips of South African opposition figures making inflammatory remarks and images purportedly showing the graves of white farmers.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the presentation as a calculated move, arguing that Trump remained fully engaged throughout and began narrating the images roughly three and a half minutes into the montage.[1] In this reading, Trump orchestrated the moment to draw attention to alleged violence against white farmers by "tricking the media into reporting it," while also creating a basis for comparison between the situations in Gaza and South Africa.[1]

The commentary further suggested that figures in Trump's orbit would have been eager to humiliate Ramaphosa, because it was Ramaphosa's government that filed the original petition at the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]] seeking war crimes charges against [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and the Israeli government over the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] offensive in Gaza that followed the October 7, 2023 attack by [[hamas|Hamas]].[1] South Africa's simultaneous status as ICJ petitioner and as a state accused of its own alleged atrocities was described as an "interesting dynamic that feels like bait" for the mainstream media.[1]

The analysis also noted that South Africa is a member of [[brics|BRICS]], giving it a deepening alignment with Vladimir Putin and Russia, while having historically been a hub of regime and regime-change activity — a combination the author called an enigma under Ramaphosa's leadership.[1]
