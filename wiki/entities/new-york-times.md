# The New York Times

The New York Times is an American newspaper of record, described by Badlands Media as one of a group of "mainstream, historically spook-friendly outlets" alongside [[washington-post|The Washington Post]] and [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Hiring and orthodoxy

Badlands argues that with corruption widespread in the West, the mainstream press cannot afford to employ journalists worthy of the title, because "effective journalists of integrity are a literal security risk for the security state."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Outlets such as the Times therefore tend to hire people already naturally inclined to support the narratives handed down from above — writers who "live and die by the modern liberal orthodoxy" and whose primary skillset is identifying and punishing internet users who deviate from it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Taylor Lorenz

The article's principal example of this hiring pattern is [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]], a former New York Times and Washington Post writer who covered the social media beat and became known for what Badlands calls "tattletale journalism."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)


## Coverage of the fall of Assad

Badlands Media singled out the *New York Times*, alongside the *[[washington-post|Washington Post]]*, as a publication that asks and purports to answer all the wrong questions about the ousting of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]].[2] It quoted at length from the paper's account of the first dawn in more than five decades without an al-Assad in the presidential palace, describing celebratory gunfire in Damascus and 13 years of bloodshed since Syrians first "hoped to follow revolutions in Tunisia, Egypt and Libya."[2]

The article criticized that framing on two grounds: that it implies Syrians were unanimously ecstatic, ignoring Alawite and Christian constituencies that supported Assad, and that the invoked precedents of Tunisia, Egypt, and Libya were themselves countries the United States "helped" under identical media treatment.[2]

## March 2026: Trump attack over Iran coverage

On March 12, 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly rebuked the New York Times over its coverage of the [[iran-war|Iran conflict]], posting on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that "if you read the Failing New York Times, you would incorrectly think that we are not winning."[3] He countered the paper's framing with a list of claimed results — that Iran's navy was gone, its air force no longer existed, its missiles and drones were being decimated, and its leaders "wiped from the face of the earth."[3] Badlands Media treated the exchange as an example of narrative warfare between the administration and legacy outlets over the state of the war.[3]

## The Israeli espionage story

In June 2026 the Times, together with NBC News, reported that the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]'s [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]] had elevated its assessment of the threat from Israeli espionage to a "critical" level, describing a seven-page document and accompanying chart and naming [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], [[elbridge-colby|Elbridge Colby]] and [[michael-dimino|Michael DiMino]] as alleged collection targets.[4] A [[white-house|White House]] official told NBC News that "the entire story is false."[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the outlets' involvement as itself evidentiary, writing that because the report came from NBC News and the Times it was "possible—if not likely—to be Fake News," while allowing that the substance was "more than plausible."[4] He argued the more important question was not veracity but placement: the story had entered the public zeitgeist at a moment of accelerating hostility toward [[israel|Israel]], making it read as a deliberate narrative deployment rather than an accident.[4]

## Trump classified map story (April 2023)

In April 2023 the Times reported that federal investigators were asking witnesses whether [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had shown aides and visitors a map containing sensitive intelligence information taken from the White House, citing four people with knowledge of the matter.[5] The report described the map as one focus of the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] inquiry overseen by special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]].[5]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] questioned the story's timing, arguing it coincided awkwardly with the [[discord-classified-documents-leak|Discord leak]] of more than 100 actually classified documents and maps, and suggesting the piece had been intended to run two or three weeks earlier as part of a narrative synchronization that misfired.[5]

## December 2023 coverage

On December 21, 2023 the *Times* published a side-by-side analysis, "Excerpts From Dr. Claudine Gay's Work," presenting five examples in which the [[harvard-university|Harvard]] president [[claudine-gay|Claudine Gay]] was accused of lifting words, phrases and sentences from other scholars without proper attribution.[6] The paper noted that most of the disputed passages were written in technical academic jargon rather than conveying sweeping or original ideas, but that Gay's papers sometimes lifted text verbatim and at other times made minor adjustments, such as changing "adage" to "popular saying" or "Black male children" to "young black athletes."[6] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the paper's decision to cover rather than bury the scandal as itself significant, asking "why has *The NY Times* started covering this scandal instead of covering it up?"[6]

The same day the *Times* reported the mass shooting at [[charles-university-shooting|Charles University in Prague]], in which at least 15 people were killed.[6]

Separately, *Times* editorial board member [[mara-gay|Mara Gay]] became the focus of criticism after telling MSNBC's *[[morning-joe|Morning Joe]]* that critics of the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]]'s disqualification of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were "standing with confederates who betrayed this country," remarks law professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] cited as emblematic of unhinged coverage of the ruling.[6]

## Durham coverage, January 2023

On January 26, 2023 the Times published "How Barr's Quest to Find Flaws in the Russia Inquiry Unraveled," a long retrospective on the [[john-durham|Durham]] special counsel investigation and its origins under Attorney General [[bill-barr|Bill Barr]].[7] Badlands contributor Just Human described the piece as a "hit and spin" article resting on "current and former officials" who spoke on condition of anonymity "because of the legal, political and intelligence sensitivities surrounding the topic," along with defense lawyers for clients Durham had interviewed.[7] He read it as an attempt to establish that the Trump-Russia investigations were well founded while Durham's was not, that Durham had been captured politically, and that his work was over - and speculated that the paper's timing indicated it was trying to get ahead of a coming development.[7]

## Durham coverage and the RealClear rebuttal (February 2023)

A Jan. 26, 2023 Times article by Charlie Savage, Adam Goldman and Katie Benner reported that Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] had gone "unsuccessfully down one path after another" and "failed to find wrongdoing in the origins of the Russia inquiry," a conclusion drawn from a monthslong review and interviews with more than a dozen current and former officials.[8] In a February 15, 2023 [[realclear-investigations|RealClear Investigations]] piece, [[aaron-mate|Aaron Maté]] argued the paper was still engaging in the journalistic behavior that had made it a reliable disseminator of discredited innuendo about a conspiracy between [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Russia, and that by omitting countervailing information and distorting facts the Times sought to write off the Durham probe in advance while whitewashing key [[fbi|FBI]] and Clinton campaign actors.[8]

Badlands commentary added that the paper was amplifying op-eds by former Obama administration Solicitor General [[neal-katyal|Neal Katyal]] calling for Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] to "discipline and fire" Durham and to withhold his report from the public, which contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] read as an effort to damage Durham's legitimacy and credibility ahead of his next move.[8]

## Coverage of the Gaza troop withdrawal

The New York Times reported the Israeli military's January 1, 2024 announcement that it would begin withdrawing several thousand troops from the [[gaza|Gaza Strip]], the most significant publicly announced reduction since the war with [[hamas|Hamas]] began, attributing it to the growing toll on the Israeli economy and to U.S. pressure.[9] The report carried the local health authority figure of more than 20,000 killed in Gaza and the [[united-nations|United Nations]] assessment that half the territory's 2.2 million people were at risk of starvation, along with Rear Admiral [[daniel-hagari|Daniel Hagari]]'s insistence that the [[israeli-troop-withdrawal-from-gaza-2024|drawdown]] signalled no change in war aims.[9]

## Coverage of Ukraine and Biden's age (June 2023)

On June 5, 2023 the Times published a report on Nazi-associated symbols worn by Ukrainian service members, noting that Ukraine's government and [[nato|NATO]] allies had posted and quietly deleted photographs showing such patches.[10] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the piece as an admission that Western media had ignored [[nazi-symbols-in-ukrainian-military|Nazi ideology within Ukrainian forces]] and as evidence of a narrative "turn" on Ukraine coverage.[10]

A day earlier the paper ran a four-byline feature on the complicated reality of being America's oldest president, describing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] as both sharp at critical moments and prone to lapses of memory.[10] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the piece an advertorial-style exercise in carrying water for the president, singling out the passages describing him as "a president in aviator shades commanding the world stage" and quoting advisers who use "sharp as a tack" as a mantra.[10] She argued that real journalists should scrutinize the relationship between the [[white-house|White House]] and the Times.[10]

## 2024 editorial calling on Biden to withdraw

On June 28, 2024, the day after the [[2024-presidential-debate|presidential debate]], the Times editorial board published a piece declaring that "the greatest public service Mr. Biden can now perform is to announce that he will not continue to run for re-election."[11] While repeatedly emphasizing what it called the "enormous danger" posed by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], the board wrote that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was "engaged in a reckless gamble" with America's future and that "it's too big a bet to simply hope Americans will overlook or discount Mr. Biden's age and infirmity that they see with their own eyes."[11] Commentators observed that a cornerstone liberal media institution declaring an incumbent Democratic president mentally incapable of seeking re-election would embolden other outlets and elected officials to follow.[11]

Separately, Times reporter Peter Baker reported that Biden engaged in no organized process outside his family in deciding to run for a second term — reporting cited by Axios in its account of the "Biden oligarchy."[11]

## The 2023 anti-elections op-ed

In August 2023 the Times published an opinion essay by [[adam-grant|Adam Grant]] arguing that public office would have more integrity if elections were abolished in favor of [[sortition|sortition]] — selection by random lottery, as practiced in ancient Athens and in American jury selection.[12] Badlands noted that the piece originally ran under the headline "Elections are Bad for Democracy" before the paper changed it, and treated the substitution as telling.[12] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the essay amounted to open advocacy for "selections," offered no mechanism beyond an unspecified vetting process, and thereby implied the end of consent as a requirement for governance.[12] She further charged that the Times, having peddled divisive narratives for years, was citing the resulting national division as grounds for eliminating elections — an instance of an establishment outlet manufacturing a problem and selling the regime's preferred solution — and proposed watching whether the idea propagated across other outlets, going [[operation-mockingbird|mockingbird]], as the measure of intent.[12]

## Nord Stream pipeline reporting, March 2023

On March 7, 2023, the Times reported that new intelligence reviewed by U.S. officials suggested a pro-Ukrainian group had carried out the attack on the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream pipelines]], describing the finding as a step toward determining responsibility for a sabotage operation that had confounded investigators on both sides of the Atlantic.[13] The paper said U.S. officials had no evidence that President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] or his top lieutenants were involved, or that the perpetrators acted at the direction of any Ukrainian government officials, and characterized the attack as one of the most consequential unsolved mysteries of the war in Ukraine.[13]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] treated the article as a significant narrative shift, calling it remarkable that the Times was receiving intelligence-official leaks and moving from "Russia and Putin did it!" toward Ukrainian attribution, and noting that the story cited [[seymour-hersh|Seymour Hersh]]'s Substack account.[13] He argued the piece was "ripe with factoids and indicators of note" for readers applying a "fake news filter," and paired it with the parallel [[zeit-online|Zeit Online]] investigation as complementary inputs into a fuller picture.[13]
