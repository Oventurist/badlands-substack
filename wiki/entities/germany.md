# Germany

Germany is the largest economy in the [[european-union|European Union]] and, in April 2026, a central casualty of the energy disruptions surrounding the [[druzhba-pipeline|Druzhba pipeline]] and the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] blockade.[1]

## Energy exposure (2026)

[[russia|Russia]] announced that it would halt Kazakh crude oil shipments to Germany through the northern branch of the Druzhba pipeline beginning 1 May, citing technical issues.[1] The suspension affected the PCK Schwedt refinery, which supplies most of the fuel, jet fuel, and heating oil used in Berlin; Kazakh oil had covered about 17% of the refinery's needs, with volumes reaching nearly 60,000 barrels per day in the first quarter of 2026.[1]

Germany's Economy Ministry responded that the suspension did not jeopardize overall petroleum product supply security, because northern pipeline sections and increased deliveries through Poland's Gdansk port could cover any shortfall even if Schwedt ran at reduced capacity.[1]

## Broader context

Badlands commentary placed Germany within a wider pattern in which Hormuz disruptions hammered importers across Europe, Asia, and Australia while the United States absorbed the shock through domestic energy abundance.[1] In that framing, populations "from Berlin to Canberra" began questioning why their sovereignty had been outsourced to distant chokepoints and foreign entanglements, with the European Green Agenda described as lying in ruins.[1]

## 2026 conscription legislation

Germany is preparing legislation that could restore mandatory military service in 2027 if voluntary recruitment falls short, a step reported alongside [[poland|Poland]]'s continued strengthening of security measures on its border with Germany.[2] Both moves were presented as part of European governments adjusting defense and border policies amid the ongoing war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[2]

## 2026 industrial crisis

In July 2026 Germany's manufacturing sector entered an acute contraction described as a [[german-industrial-crisis-2026|competitiveness crisis]].[3] The [[federation-of-german-industries|Federation of German Industries]] reported the sector was shedding roughly 15,000 jobs per month, blaming high energy costs, weak investment, geopolitical pressures, and increased competition from [[china|China]], while the second quarter of 2026 brought the highest number of corporate bankruptcies in 21 years.[3] [[bmw|BMW]] announced several thousand German job cuts by the end of 2027 and a global workforce reduction of about 8,000, joining Volkswagen, Mercedes-Benz, and Porsche in major cost-cutting.[3]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Germany as the backbone of the European economy, holding the continent's largest manufacturing base at roughly 28% of GDP alongside an engineering tradition legendary for centuries; he noted that many great American inventions of the Industrial Revolution were developed by first-generation German immigrants.[3] He attributed the collapse not to the [[iran-war|Iran war]] but to Europe's elimination of serious domestic energy production and its resulting dependence on [[russia|Russia]], asking whether the pattern reflects "stupidity, or narcissistic sociopathy."[3]

## AfD gains and the establishment firewall (March 2026)

By March 2026 the [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany (AfD)]] party had posted gains in recent state elections, particularly in eastern regions of the country.[4] Despite those gains the AfD remained excluded from governing coalitions, as mainstream parties continued to enforce a longstanding refusal to cooperate with it, and German domestic intelligence authorities classified the party as "right-wing extremists."[4] The mainstream parties' "firewall" policy barred coalitions or governing alliances with the AfD despite its growing popularity, yet the party's vote share continued to increase.[4]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] compared the German dynamic to the American experience of MAGA, arguing that labeling opposition as radical works only until it doesn't and that "far right" is the globalists' pejorative for nationalists.[4]

## US troop presence dispute (2026)

In April 2026 [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signalled that the United States could reconsider its military presence in Germany following criticism of US policy in the [[iran-war|Iran conflict]] by Chancellor [[friedrich-merz|Friedrich Merz]].[5] Trump tied continued deployments to allied support and burden-sharing, restating long-standing doubts about the value of US commitments within [[nato|NATO]].[5] Germany hosts tens of thousands of US troops and serves as a key component of NATO's European defense posture and logistics network, so any reduction would mark a significant shift in US force positioning on the continent.[5]

## 2026 U.S. troop withdrawal

President Donald Trump announced the withdrawal of thousands of American troops stationed in Germany, a move European leaders said surprised them in its timing and interpreted as a signal that Europe must assume greater responsibility for its own security.[6] The decision is described in [[us-troop-withdrawal-from-germany-2026|the 2026 U.S. troop withdrawal from Germany]].[6]

## Commercial bankruptcies and NATO capacity (January 2026)

In January 2026 Germany announced the highest number of commercial bankruptcies in over a decade, concentrated in transportation, storage, hospitality and construction — sectors whose distress Badlands analyst [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read as a signal that the German economy was entering a severe recession, compounded by a 74% rise in natural gas prices between 2022 and 2025.[7] He argued that a Germany in recession cannot sustain a war effort, and that without the German economy Europe as a continental military power is incapable of prosecuting a war, leaving the continent "basically a tourism economy."[7] The same commentary noted that Germany's contribution to the reinforcement of [[greenland|Greenland]] amounted to 13 personnel assigned to reconnaissance, a figure [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited as evidence of the weakness of an America-less [[nato|NATO]] power projection.[7]

## Border controls and migration crackdown (September 2024)

In September 2024 the German government announced tighter controls at all of the country's land borders as part of an effort to tackle irregular migration and protect the public from threats such as Islamist extremism.[8] Interior Minister [[nancy-faeser|Nancy Faeser]] said the controls — within what is normally the free-movement European Schengen zone — would begin on September 16 and initially last six months, and that authorities had designed a scheme enabling them to reject more migrants directly at the border.[8]

"We are strengthening internal security and continuing our hard line against irregular migration," Faeser said, noting that the European Commission and neighboring countries had been notified.[8] The measures extended years of tightening following a surge in arrivals from the Middle East, and were read as Chancellor [[olaf-scholz|Olaf Scholz]]'s attempt to reclaim initiative from far-right and conservative opposition parties gaining support on public services, integration, and security.[8]

## Arrest of Hamas suspects (October 2025)

In early October 2025, German authorities detained three suspected members of [[hamas|Hamas]] on charges of planning terror attacks in the country.[9] The three men were detained on a Wednesday in Berlin during a police operation targeting a weapons handover, according to security sources cited by *Der Spiegel*, and Germany's Federal Prosecutor General confirmed the provisional arrests.[9] Prosecutors said the men were accused of Hamas membership and of preparing serious acts of violence endangering the state, alleging they had been tasked by Hamas leaders with acquiring firearms and ammunition in Germany for use against Jewish and Israeli targets.[9] "During today's arrests, various weapons, including an AK-47 assault rifle and several pistols, and a significant amount of ammunition were found," the prosecutor's office said, and law enforcement also searched the towns of Leipzig and Oberhausen.[9]


## September 2024 land border controls

On Monday, September 16, 2024, Germany began new controls at all of its land borders as part of a crackdown on migration, restricting movement within the [[schengen-area|Schengen Zone]] and angering its European neighbors.[10] In addition to existing controls with Austria, Switzerland, the Czech Republic and [[poland|Poland]], Berlin extended internal border controls to [[france|France]], Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Belgium and Denmark, and gained the power to reject people at all land borders under rules set to last six months initially, according to the Interior Ministry.[10] [[cnn|CNN]] described the move as a measure of how far Germany had shifted on migration since the government of [[angela-merkel|Angela Merkel]] welcomed more than one million arrivals during the 2015–2016 migrant crisis, with Berlin now toughening rules amid a surging far-right opposition.[10] Days earlier, Germany had struck a controlled migration deal with [[kenya|Kenya]] to admit skilled and semi-skilled Kenyan workers.[10]

Badlands argued that neighboring criticism reflected unwillingness to absorb Germany's share of the migration burden, noting Poland's pushback and warnings from both Greece and Austria that they would not accept migrants rejected by Germany.[10] The commentary rejected media framing of the shift as racism, contrasted the German move with [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]]'s results in [[italy|Italy]], and predicted continued U.S. media attacks on [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany (AfD)]] in the wake of the decision.[10]

## Nuclear phase-out and gas pivot

Germany completed its [[german-nuclear-phase-out|nuclear phase-out]] in April 2023, taking its last three nuclear power plants — including the reactor at Neckarwestheim — offline, with no apparent intention of restarting them.[11] The decision was unusual for a country that positions itself as a leading proponent of the green energy transition, since it removed one of its most reliable sources of carbon-free generation while prioritizing that step over abandoning coal.[11] It also came amid acute energy-security concerns: Germany relied on Russia for roughly 50 percent of its natural gas supplies at the time of the February 2022 invasion of Ukraine, and the sanctions that followed helped plunge Europe into an energy crisis that hit the German economy hard.[11] Rather than extending the nuclear fleet's life, Berlin opted to spend billions on new domestic natural gas plants alongside renewable expansion, falling back on coal when supplies run short — a course many energy and climate experts found baffling.[11] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] predicted catastrophic consequences for the German people, arguing that solar and wind cannot handle grid loads, and that the EU risks being left behind as the United States, Russia, China and Global South nations pursue a nuclear renaissance.[11]

## Free expression survey, 2025

An INSA survey reported in August 2025 found that 84% of German respondents believe people withhold opinions out of fear of consequences, with only 9% disagreeing.[12] A significant 54% said they had personally felt unable to express their opinions freely, a perception rising by 6 percentage points over the eight months since the previous survey.[12]

Younger respondents, especially those under 30, reported feeling stifled more than older ones, and among party supporters [[alternative-for-germany|AfD]] voters felt the most restricted at 76%.[12] The report suggested free speech could become as pivotal an issue as migration and the pandemic, and emphasised the need to foster a culture of open dialogue and diverse opinion.[12]

Badlands commentator Chris Paul said Constantin Von Hoffmeister had expressed the same sentiment in a recent interview, arguing that threats against speech in Germany have proven very effective and that the German people seem unwilling to defy what he called the Regime.[12]

Quoting retired Lt. Col. [[tony-shaffer|Tony Shaffer]] in The Daily Caller, the article calls it especially egregious that Germany claimed to need protection from Putin while purchasing large amounts of gas from Russia and thereby helping to fund the very adversary it named — "in a word, insane."[13] Shaffer noted that the United States alone accounted for 71.7 percent of [[nato|NATO]] defense spending, more than Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Canada and the United Kingdom combined.[13] Lange argues that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] ended the arrangement, leaving European central banks exposed to the financial squeeze the article describes.[13]
