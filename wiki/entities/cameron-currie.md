# Cameron Currie

Cameron Currie is a US District Judge who, on Monday, November 24, 2025, dismissed the federal criminal indictments against former FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] and New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]].[1]

## The Halligan ruling

Currie concluded that the appointment of [[lindsey-halligan|Lindsey Halligan]] by Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] to lead the federal prosecutors' office in the Eastern District of Virginia was illegal.[1] Because Halligan was the only prosecutor to sign off on the Comey and James prosecutions, Currie ruled that both cases had to be dismissed.[1]

The dismissals pre-empted separate proceedings in which judges in each case were weighing "vindictive prosecution" claims — both defendants argued they had been targeted by Halligan, [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s former personal lawyer, to carry out the president's personal vendetta against them.[1]

## Window left open

Currie's ruling explicitly left a window for prosecutors to try again, and did not foreclose re-indictment.[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that 18 U.S. Code § 3288 permits a new indictment within six calendar months of a dismissal even where the statute of limitations has otherwise expired — or within 60 days of a dismissal decided on appeal — concluding that "there is a path" to a renewed prosecution.[1]

## Appeal of her dismissal ruling (December 2025)

The [[justice-department|Justice Department]] appealed Currie's dismissal of the criminal cases against [[james-comey|James Comey]] and [[letitia-james|Letitia James]] on Friday night, December 19, 2025.[2] Currie had ruled the previous month that prosecutor [[lindsey-halligan|Lindsey Halligan]], a former White House aide, was ineligible for her post, so that "all actions flowing from" the appointment, including the indictments, "constitute unlawful exercises of executive power and must be set aside."[2] Badlands commentary questioned whether a district judge may override a presidential appointment and framed the appeal as a chance to settle the question.[2]
