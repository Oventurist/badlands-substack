# Robert Birchum

Robert Birchum is a retired United States Air Force lieutenant colonel and former intelligence officer who pleaded guilty in January 2023 to unlawfully retaining national defense information at his home in Tampa, Florida.[1]

## Guilty plea

According to a federal plea agreement signed in late January 2023 and reported by the New York Post, Birchum, then 54, admitted to "willfully" keeping top secret documents on a thumb drive and a computer at his Tampa residence.[1] Investigators determined that he had retained 135 top secret, secret and otherwise classified files on the thumb drive and computer hard drive as of 2017.[1] The charge of unlawful retention of national defense information was filed in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Florida and carries a maximum sentence of ten years in prison and a fine of up to $250,000.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[brad-getz|Brad Getz]] treated the Birchum plea as part of a wider pattern of classified-document cases surfacing in early 2023, alongside the discovery of classified materials in the possession of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]].[1] Getz argued that the retention of classified material had been presented months earlier as "the crime of the century" when it involved [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], but was recast as a minor offense once other figures were implicated, and noted that of the four individuals then under scrutiny only Trump had held declassification authority as president.[1] He framed the contrast as useful material in what he called a "war of redpills" and a battle for the minds of the American people.[1]
