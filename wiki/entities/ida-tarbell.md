# Ida Tarbell

**Ida Minerva Tarbell** was the American investigative journalist whose exposé of [[standard-oil|Standard Oil]] delivered [[john-d-rockefeller|John D. Rockefeller]] his first sustained spell of hostile press.[1]

## Background

Tarbell was the daughter of a Pennsylvania oil producer who had been run out of business by Rockefeller.[1] The author [[eustace-mullins|Eustace Mullins]], quoted in Badlands Media's account, identifies that father as "a Mr. Tarbell," one of the competitors ruined after [[john-d-rockefeller|Rockefeller]] obtained shipping rebates through his holding company, South Improvement Co.[1]

## The History of Standard Oil Company

Based on five years of research, her series was published beginning in 1902 in *McClure's Magazine* under the title "The History of Standard Oil Company."[1] One reviewer called the work a "fearless unmasking of moral criminality masquerading under the robes of respectability and Christianity."[1] Tarbell herself wrote that the investigation had taught that "our present law-makers, as a body, are ignorant, corrupt, and unprincipled; that the majority of them are, directly or indirectly, under the control of the very monopolies against whose acts we have been seeking relief."[1]

President [[theodore-roosevelt|Theodore Roosevelt]] labelled her a "muckraker," a term that promptly entered the language.[1]

## Consequences

Her exposé led to governmental and court actions that at least appeared to break up Standard Oil's monopoly.[1] Badlands Media notes, however, that as early as 1882 Rockefeller had already moved to mask his dealings through the Standard Oil Trust, and that the eventual 1911 dissolution only increased his wealth.[1]
