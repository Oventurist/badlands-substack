# John Poindexter

Vice Admiral John M. Poindexter was President [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]]'s National Security Adviser during the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra affair]] and the direct superior of [[oliver-north|Oliver North]] at the [[national-security-council|National Security Council]].[1]

The [[justice-department|Justice Department]] admitted that it held back its probe of [[southern-air-transport|Southern Air Transport]] for ten days in late October and early November 1986 at Poindexter's request.[1] A department spokesman said Poindexter feared the investigation would interfere with arms shipments to Iran and the possible release of American hostages in Lebanon; the department's internal inspector subsequently examined the delay.[1] Joe Lange argues that the hostage rationale was a deliberate red herring, an excuse the [[shadow-government]] would reuse for years both to garner public sympathy and to distract from the real question of independent funding.[1]

## Immunity and overturned conviction

[[congress|Congress]] granted immunity to both Poindexter and North in order to secure their testimony, over the objections of the Special Counsel, who begged legislators not to do so.[1] The grants spoiled the criminal investigations that followed: although the Special Counsel won convictions against both men, those convictions were overturned on appeal because of the immunity deals.[1] Lange notes that the pair admitted to shredding hundreds of documents recording their crimes and to laundering money through Swiss bank accounts, yet walked away "scot-free" — evidence, in his view, that the proceedings were a show trial.[1]

## Modernization of the White House crisis apparatus

Poindexter arrived at the White House in 1981 as a military assistant and, as an engineer by training, was given the task of fixing the early warning and crisis management system of the national security apparatus, including the upgrade of a Situation Room that was a technological backwater lacking many of the basics for keeping the president in touch with the world.[2] He outfitted the Situation Room with modern communications equipment and built a new $14 million [[crisis-management-center|Crisis Management Center]] in the Old Executive Office Building, installing video conferencing systems, large wall screens, links to diplomatic, military and intelligence cable traffic, and even the first early versions of "e-mail" in the White House — a generational leap for the executive branch.[2]

On the night of October 23, 1983, as deputy national security adviser, Poindexter was woken at his suburban Maryland home by a secure phone call from a watch officer in the White House Situation Room reporting the [[1983-beirut-barracks-bombings|bombing of the Marine compound in Beirut]] and, minutes later, the near-simultaneous attack on the French regiment; the final death count reached 241 Americans.[2]

In reaction to the Beirut attack and a subsequent embassy bombing nearly a year later, Poindexter led a massive reorganization of the government's crisis management teams and formed an alliance with his friend [[william-casey|Bill Casey]], the CIA Director, one of the few senior officials he felt he could speak to frankly.[2] The [[cia|CIA]] then set up a secure hotline connecting the [[state-department|State Department]], the [[national-security-agency|NSA]], the Defense Intelligence Agency and the [[national-security-council|NSC]] staff with the agency's photographic intelligence center.[2] Poindexter also took over the NSC's Crisis-Pre Planning Group and turned it into the engine of the government's antiterrorism campaign, powered for the first time by modern information technologies.[2]

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that this reorganization gave the CIA control over all information flowing to the entire executive branch, not merely to the president through the [[presidents-daily-brief|Presidential Daily Brief]], and that Poindexter was thereby planting the seeds of a broader and ultimately global system of information gathering.[2] Two decades later, after the [[september-11-attacks|9/11 attacks]], Poindexter would help lead the Defense Department's [[total-information-awareness|Total Information Awareness]] program, which bore a striking resemblance to modern NSA global surveillance.[2] Lange describes him as a man with long-standing ties to [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] who stood at the center of Iran-Contra, walked away unpunished, later played a key role in another power grab during the presidency of George W. Bush, and remains a free man still involved in helping the [[shadow-government|shadow government]].[2]
