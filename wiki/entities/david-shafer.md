# David Shafer

David Shafer is a Georgia Republican figure and a co-defendant alongside [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the Fulton County racketeering prosecution over the 2020 presidential election, [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|State of Georgia v. Trump, et al.]][1]

## Pretrial motions

At a March 28, 2024 hearing before Fulton County Superior Court Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]], two of the pretrial motions argued were filed by Shafer, heard the same morning as Trump's First Amendment motion to dismiss.[1] The hearing was described as a return to normalcy after the case was rocked by allegations that District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] improperly benefited from her romantic relationship with special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]].[1]

Shafer's attorneys argued two motions concerning the alternate slate of presidential electors.[1] The first contended that Shafer acted on the advice of counsel and engaged in conduct that was lawful at the time — that is, before the 116th Congress passed the Electoral Count Act reform.[1] The second sought to strike language from the indictment that characterized guilt versus innocence, such as the words "fake," "false" and "lawful."[1] Prosecutors responded that such characterizations were matters to be determined at trial, while Shafer argued the language would taint the jury pool.[1]

## Badlands coverage

Badlands Media covered the Trump and Shafer motions hearing live, with [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] describing the proceedings as "a master class in RICO" that would prove useful in the future.[1]

## Motion to disqualify Fani Willis

On Tuesday, February 13, 2024, Shafer — described as a former chairman of the Georgia Republican Party charged with racketeering in connection with efforts to help [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] overturn the 2020 election — submitted additional court filings to the motion seeking to disqualify Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] and her boyfriend, special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]], from prosecuting Trump and 18 co-defendants.[2] The filing accused Wade of lying in his divorce proceedings, presenting evidence that he had updated his answers to two interrogatories about sexual relations outside his marriage.[2] The documents showed Wade answered "none" to both questions on May 30, 2023, then changed his response the previous month — after allegations of an "improper relationship" with Willis emerged — to assert privilege under O.C.G.A. Sec. 24-5-505, a provision protecting parties from testimony that would incriminate or "bring infamy, disgrace, or public contempt upon" them or their family.[2] The underlying prosecution is covered at [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|State of Georgia v. Trump]].[2]
