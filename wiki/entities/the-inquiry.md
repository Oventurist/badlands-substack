# The Inquiry

The Inquiry was the name adopted by a group of American foreign-policy elites convened during the First World War to plan the shape of the postwar world, and is identified by Badlands Media as the direct ancestor of the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]].[1]

## Formation

According to [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 1," the group grew out of a series of meetings conducted during World War I, when [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]]'s confidential advisor [[edward-mandell-house|Edward Mandell House]] gathered roughly 100 of the most prominent men in the country to discuss the postwar settlement.[1] These men styled themselves "the Inquiry."[1]

## The Fourteen Points

The Inquiry helped concoct Wilson's [[fourteen-points|Fourteen Points]], presented to Congress in January 1918.[1] DeLarme reads the points as a globalist wish list, calling for the removal of "all economic barriers" between nations, "equality of trade conditions," and the formation of "a general association of nations."[1]

## Paris and after

Almost two dozen members of the Inquiry accompanied Wilson, House and international bankers [[paul-warburg|Paul Warburg]] and [[bernard-baruch|Bernard Baruch]] to the [[paris-peace-conference|Paris Peace Conference]] of 1919.[1] When the U.S. Senate refused to ratify the covenant establishing the [[league-of-nations|League of Nations]], House and his associates resolved instead to form an "Institute of International Affairs," whose branches became [[chatham-house|Chatham House]] in England and the Council on Foreign Relations in the United States.[1]
