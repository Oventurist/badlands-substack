# False Memory Syndrome Foundation

The False Memory Syndrome Foundation is described in the Badlands Media article "Gazing Into the Abyss pt.2: a Therapist's Tale" as an organized political group that, during the [[memory-wars|"memory wars"]] of the 1990s, took up the task of informing the media that unethical therapists were suggesting to helpless clients that they had been sexually abused, or worse, by family members.[1]

## Role in the memory wars

The article presents the foundation and similar groups as a principal source of the isolation and peer pressure experienced by therapists treating [[ritual-abuse|ritual abuse]] and [[dissociative-identity-disorder|dissociative identity disorder]], at a time when the dissociative disorder diagnosis was not widely accepted.[1] It records that a leading journal, *Treating Abuse Today*, was forced out of business by picketers representing supposedly innocent accused parents.[1]

Leading therapists in the field of dissociative disorders, such as Bennett Braun and Judith Peterson, were targeted for lawsuits and, in Peterson's case, criminal charges; insurance companies chose to settle most lawsuits out of court, which gave the impression that the therapists were guilty.[1] The article treats this campaign as part of the broader risk environment facing clinicians such as [[alison-miller|Alison Miller]], alongside direct threats from perpetrator groups.[1] Related Badlands coverage frames such memory-discrediting narratives under the heading of [[false-memory-syndrome|False Memory Syndrome]].[1]
