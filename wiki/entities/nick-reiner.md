# Nick Reiner

Nick Reiner is the son of Hollywood filmmaker [[rob-reiner|Rob Reiner]] and, as of February 2026, the defendant in a double-homicide prosecution arising from the fatal stabbings of his parents.[1]

## Charges

Reiner was charged with two counts of first-degree murder in the deaths of his parents. Prosecutors filed special-circumstance allegations, including multiple murders and use of a knife, making the case death-penalty eligible; if convicted, Reiner faces life without the possibility of parole or the death penalty.[1] Prosecutors allege the victims were killed in fatal stabbings and that Reiner used a knife, though detailed forensic evidence — including any DNA results, fingerprint analysis, autopsy specifics beyond stabbing, and chain-of-custody details — had not been publicly released.[1]

## Arraignment and representation

At his February 23, 2026 arraignment, Reiner entered a not guilty plea to both first-degree murder counts and was held without bail.[1] TMZ reported that Reiner's siblings were not paying and had no plans to pay for a private attorney; he proceeded with public defender representation, and as of the arraignment was represented by Deputy Public Defender Kimberly Greene.[1]

## Evidentiary picture

Reiner was reportedly seen on gas station surveillance footage prior to his arrest, placing him outside the home after the reported time of the deaths. No publicly reported surveillance footage captures the killings, and no in-home camera evidence has been publicly confirmed. A family member reportedly discovered the victims and contacted authorities, and there are no publicly identified eyewitnesses to the killings. The probable cause affidavit and broader evidentiary record had not been fully disclosed publicly. The case was proceeding through pretrial stages.[1]

## Arrest and initial reporting (December 2025)

Nick Reiner, 32, was arrested late Sunday, December 14, 2025 and booked on suspicion of murder after the bodies of his parents were found at their Brentwood home; the bodies were reportedly discovered by his 28-year-old sister, Romy.[2] He was booked on a felony charge early Monday morning, and the LAPD told CNN he was not being held on bail despite a slew of news reports announcing a $4 million bond.[2] In an earlier interview with *PEOPLE*, Reiner had discussed a long battle with drug addiction that eventually left him homeless after numerous rehab stints — experiences that became the foundation for the semi-autobiographical film *Being Charlie*, which he co-wrote with his father.[2] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] noted that his mother, [[michele-singer-reiner|Michele Singer Reiner]], was killed alongside his father, and speculated about outcomes ranging from Reiner testifying under oath at trial to jury nullification.[2]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] devoted a segment of the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] to the case, noting that it was "consuming the normies" in her life and that she had catalogued what is actually publicly known versus assumed. She argued that Reiner may have done it, may be mentally ill, may be justified, or may have been set up, but that in the court of public opinion he was already treated as guilty — a posture she called unacceptable. She characterized the case as a double homicide of "Hollywood royalty that threatens to open a cavern of family—and, potentially, industry—secrets," and insisted the defendant deserves a trial and an impartial jury.[1]
