# Susan Bradley

Susan Bradley is a Canadian psychiatrist and an early pioneer in the clinical treatment of childhood gender dysphoria who, in March 2023, publicly repudiated the affirmation-and-puberty-blocker model of care she had once helped establish.[1]

## Career and the pediatric gender clinic

Bradley started a pediatric gender clinic in 1975 aimed at treating children with gender dysphoria — described as a deep sense of discomfort with one's body and biological sex.[1] Her original approach was therapy-focused, and she has said that most patients under that model outgrew their feelings of being transgender over time.[1] Around 2000, the clinic began prescribing puberty blockers to gender-dysphoric children as a means of alleviating their distress, a model that has since been widely adopted by medical establishments around the world, including in the United States.[1]

## Reversal and public criticism

In an interview with the Daily Caller News Foundation, published by the [[daily-caller|Daily Caller]] on March 11, 2023, Bradley — then in her early eighties — said "we were wrong" and expressed regret that her clinic had participated in administering puberty blockers for gender dysphoria.[1] She argued that the drugs can cement a child's sense of confusion out of which the child would likely otherwise grow, and voiced concern about the side effects of the medications.[1] Her comments were widely circulated as a rare recantation from a founding figure in the field.

## Reception in the Badlands corpus

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], contributor [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] treated Bradley's reversal as confirmation of a narrative the outlet had long advanced, comparing it to disputes over COVID-19 policy and January 6 and asserting that such procedures are irreversible and that patients are not given adequate information beforehand.[1] The commentary predicted that parental backlash over pediatric gender medicine would soon "boil over."[1]

## Toronto desistance study

Susan J. Bradley is identified in Badlands Media coverage as one of three researchers — with Devita Singh and [[kenneth-zucker|Kenneth J. Zucker]] — who conducted the [[toronto-desistance-study|Toronto desistance study]] out of the [[centre-for-addiction-and-mental-health|Centre for Addiction and Mental Health]], beginning in Toronto in 1975.[2] The study followed 139 birth-assigned males referred for [[gender-dysphoria|gender dysphoria]] between the ages of three and twelve, and reported follow-up data described by the authors as the largest sample to date of boys clinic-referred for gender dysphoria.[2] Its published results classified 17 participants (12.2%) as persisters and the remaining 122 (87.8%) as desisters.[2]
