# Michelle Obama

Michelle Obama, former First Lady of the United States and wife of [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], featured repeatedly in early-2024 speculation about a replacement nominee for President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1]

Following the release of the [[hur-report|Hur report]], Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued against [[nigel-farage|Nigel Farage]]'s prediction that Democrats would simply drop Biden before the convention, forecasting instead a contested [[2024-democratic-national-convention|Democratic National Convention]] that could produce a [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]], Michelle Obama, or [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] candidacy.[1] Obama has never held elected office, and her recurring appearance in such lists reflects name recognition and establishment appeal rather than any declared candidacy.[1]

## Absence from the Carter funeral

All living former first ladies attended the funeral of former President [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]] on Thursday, January 9, 2025, except Michelle Obama, who reportedly had "scheduling conflicts."[2] Journalist Jeff Zeleny told CNN that "she's still in Hawaii," underscoring the lack of an open seat next to her husband, while her spokesperson told reporters that "Mrs. Obama sends her thoughts and prayers to the Carter family, and everyone who loved and learned from the remarkable former President."[2] Because of her absence, [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] sat between President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and former first lady Laura Bush in the second row of the Washington National Cathedral, and video footage showed Obama and Trump smiling and laughing before the service — images that commentators suggested might not have been captured had Michelle Obama attended.[2]

## Skipping the 2025 Inauguration

In January 2025 the former first lady announced she would not attend [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second inauguration at the Capitol, leaving former President Barack Obama to attend alone.[3] Reporting characterized the decision as a signal that she was "done" with Trump — a pointed gesture from the figure who had once urged Democrats to "go high" when Republicans go low — with longtime allies saying she was making a powerful statement by skipping the event.[3]

## "Terrified" comments (January 2024)

In January 2024, Obama said she was kept awake at night by the prospect of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] winning that year's election, remarks that immediately fueled speculation she could replace [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] as the Democratic nominee.[4] Asked on a podcast what keeps her up at night, she answered, "What's gonna happen in this next election," adding: "I am terrified about what could possibly happen, because our leaders matter. Who we select. Who speaks for us. Who holds that bully pulpit."[4] She said leadership "affects us in ways that I think sometimes people take for granted," claimed that government does "everything for us," and warned, "We cannot take this democracy for granted and sometimes I worry that we do."[4] Later in the interview she said, "We see childish leadership right before us, what that looks like and how that feels. Where someone is just base and vulgar and cynical. In a leadership position it doesn't trickle down well."[4]

## Roger Stone’s 2024 nomination prediction

Speaking at the Turning Point Action Conference in West Palm Beach, Florida, in July 2023, political consultant [[roger-stone|Roger Stone]] predicted that Michelle Obama, rather than incumbent [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], would secure the 2024 Democratic presidential nomination, and that the vice-presidential slot would be "bought."[5] Badlands commentary judged her the most logical Democratic candidate on the grounds that she carried the most political capital and the fewest public scandals, while arguing that an Obama run would be welcomed by opponents as a visible continuation of what it called the DNC shadow presidency and "Liberal Royalty."[5]

## Post-debate betting surge (July 2024)

After Biden's widely criticized June 27, 2024 debate performance against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], bookmakers sharply shortened the odds on Michelle Obama winning the 2024 election, despite her having previously ruled out running and endorsed the incumbent.[6] According to data compiled by Odds Checker, Bet 365, Sky Bet and William Hill had offered 16/1 (5.9 percent), 16/1 (5.9 percent) and 20/1 (4.8 percent) respectively immediately before the debate; by 8 a.m. ET on Tuesday, July 2 these had moved to 12/1 (7.7 percent), 14/1 (6.7 percent) and 10/1 (9.1 percent).[6]

Badlands commentary listed her position among the contradictions being pushed into public consciousness by what it called a "Deep State Archipelago": "Michelle Obama is NOT going to seek candidacy ... unless she does," alongside claims that [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] publicly backed Biden while their aides and consultants signalled a later move.[6]
