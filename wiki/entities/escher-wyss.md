# Escher-Wyss

Escher-Wyss & Cie. was a Swiss engineering firm whose Ravensburg branch, managed by [[eugen-schwab|Eugen Schwab]], father of [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]], became entangled with Nazi Germany's war effort.[1]

## Wartime role

The company was a leader in large turbine technology for hydroelectric dams and power plants, but it also manufactured parts for German fighter planes and, according to Badlands Media, was involved in the Nazi atom-bomb project — work that, if completed, could have changed the outcome of World War II.[1] Under Eugen Schwab's management, the Ravensburg branch was awarded the title of "National Socialist Model Company" by [[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]]'s party, which some interpret as an attempt to woo the Swiss company into wartime cooperation.[1]

A rumored agreement involving Escher-Wyss and other firms, together with the presence of the Red Cross, is credited with allied forces publicly agreeing not to bomb Ravensburg, which was never treated as a significant military target and retains many original features today.[1]

## Forced labor

Escher-Wyss used forced labor at its Ravensburg works.[1] Nearly 3,600 forced laborers worked in Ravensburg during the war; city archivist Andrea Schmuder recorded that the Escher-Wyss machine factory employed between 198 and 203 civil workers and prisoners of war.[1] Historian Karl Schweizer states that the firm "maintained a small, special camp for forced laborers on the factory premises."[1]
