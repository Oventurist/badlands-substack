# Clay Higgins

Clay Higgins is a US Representative associated with proposed federal mail ballot legislation referenced in Badlands commentary on the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]]'s June 2026 absentee ballot ruling.[1]

## Mail ballot act

Following the Court's decision upholding [[mississippi|Mississippi]]'s late-arriving ballot law, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that because the judiciary had declined to legislate from the bench, pressure should instead be applied to [[congress|Congress]] to pass Higgins' mail ballot act, alongside the SAVE America Act.[1] The same commentary held that within the states, the people should have the power to compel state legislatures to run what she termed real elections.[1]

## Push for lifetime social media bans (2025)

On Thursday, September 11, 2025, Higgins (R-La.) said he would seek to have social media companies impose lifetime bans on users who celebrated the [[assassination-of-charlie-kirk|assassination of Charlie Kirk]], the conservative activist shot the previous day at [[utah-valley-university|Utah Valley University]].[2] Writing on X, Higgins said: "I'm going to use Congressional authority and every influence with big tech platforms to mandate immediate ban for life of every post or commenter that belittled the assassination of Charlie Kirk."[2] He added that those who "ran their mouth" with hatred celebrating "the heinous murder of that beautiful young man who dedicated his whole life to delivering respectful conservative truth into the hearts of liberal enclave universities, armed only with a Bible and a microphone and a Constitution" must have their profiles taken down.[2] The Louisiana lawmaker said he would lobby Big Tech to have "zero tolerance for violent political hate content," at a time when most social media companies had scaled back content policing while maintaining rules against direct threats.[2]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded that Higgins was threatening to use state power to silence speech in direct violation of the First Amendment, and that a self-described conservative was flatly encouraging state censorship and cancellation from life.[2]
