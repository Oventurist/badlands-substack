# Ron DeSantis

Ron DeSantis is the Governor of Florida and was, at the time of the October 7, 2023 attacks, still competing — in Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s description, rather unsuccessfully — against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for the Republican presidential nomination.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Ban on Students for Justice in Palestine

DeLarme identifies DeSantis as the first politician to propose censorship measures after October 7.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) Within days of the attack, DeSantis forced the deactivation of the [[national-students-for-justice-in-palestine|National Students for Justice in Palestine]] chapter at the University of South Florida.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) Reuters reported that Florida's university system, working with DeSantis, ordered colleges to shut down the pro-Palestinian student organization, "marking the first U.S. state to outlaw the group whose national leadership backed Hamas' attack on Israel."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

DeLarme notes that the Florida chapter itself had not made the contested claims; the national chapter had stated that Hamas's actions were justified, citing decades of brutality from the Israeli government and military.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) He argues that however reprehensible the stance, it remains protected speech under the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Criticism

DeLarme places DeSantis in direct competition with [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] over who could make the most fervently pro-Israel statements, and asks whether both had not been railing months earlier against placing specific minorities on a pedestal when liberals did so with BIPOC and LGBTQ+ groups.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) The episode serves as his leading example of Republican-sponsored efforts to limit speech.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## The "DeSantis over Trump" faction (2022)

Following the [[2022-midterm-elections|2022 midterm elections]], Badlands Media commentary identified a "DeSantis over Trump" crowd within the conservative base which, alongside [[con-inc|Con Inc.]], was expected to smell blood in the water from a demoralized MAGA base and to widen hairline fractures into open division.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/all-assets-deployed) [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s November 2022 candidacy announcement at [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]] was read partly as a move to undercut political ambitions being entertained by other influential figures — DeSantis foremost among them — who were gathering directed political energy from the conservative base in an attempt to sweep it out from under Trump's leadership.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/all-assets-deployed)

## Florida HB 269 and the Jerusalem signing

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] identifies DeSantis as a central figure in what he characterizes as the legislative expansion of speech restrictions built on the [[ihra-definition-of-antisemitism|IHRA definition of antisemitism]].[3] Then-state senator [[randy-fine|Randy Fine]] sponsored [[florida-hb-269|HB 269]], which created new offenses related to harassment, threats or intimidation targeting religious or ethnic groups and, in this account, criminalized flyers and pamphlets critical of Israel while imposing harsh prison sentences.[3] Fine and DeSantis made what the commentary describes as a "secret trip" to Jerusalem, where the governor signed the bill into law — a detail the author flags as strange, a governor and state senator traveling to a foreign country to enact state legislation.[3]

## AI oversight push (2026)

In his final year in office DeSantis made artificial intelligence oversight a priority, supporting state-level regulation and warning that unchecked AI development could harm workers and strain energy infrastructure.[4] Republican allies in the Florida Senate introduced an Artificial Intelligence Bill of Rights at the start of the 2026 legislative session.[4] Politico grouped DeSantis with Senator [[josh-hawley|Josh Hawley]] and Utah Governor [[spencer-cox|Spencer Cox]] as Republicans breaking from [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s deregulatory approach, framing AI as a potentially defining 2028 issue.[4]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the Politico piece as a [[media-protectorate|Media Protectorate]] narrative that casts DeSantis and Hawley as defenders of workers and families in order to position Trump against those values, and argued that while the underlying concerns are real, the resistance risks slowing the accelerationist momentum Trump has pursued.[4]

## Cayo Falcones fallout (February 2026)

After Cuban border guards killed four people aboard a Florida-registered speedboat in the [[cayo-falcones-boat-incident|Cayo Falcones boat incident]], Florida Attorney General [[james-uthmeier|James Uthmeier]] ordered prosecutors to open an investigation and declared that "the Cuban government cannot be trusted."[5] Badlands writer [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] framed Uthmeier as "Ron! DeeSantis' servant" and asked why he was making definitive statements and talking tough while US agencies had confirmed nothing about the incident.[5]

## Haitian migrant boat interdiction (March 2024)

In March 2024, DeSantis announced that Florida state law enforcement had intercepted a vessel bound for the Florida coast carrying dozens of Haitian migrants along with firearms, drugs and night vision gear.[6] He said Florida Fish and Wildlife officers "interdicted a vessel that had 25 illegal immigrants, potential illegal immigrants from Haiti in their boat," and that the occupants were boating recklessly in a manner that could endanger others.[6] The interdiction occurred near the Sebastian Inlet within the preceding few weeks, and those aboard were turned over to the [[us-coast-guard|Coast Guard]] for deportation.[6]

## Shipment of weapons and gear to Israel (October 2023)

As a 2024 Republican presidential candidate, DeSantis pursued the pro-[[israel|Israel]] vote to what [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] called "a new extreme" by facilitating the shipment of weapons, ammunition and military gear.[7] The outlet described an ongoing spectacle "that would repel Washington and Jefferson," with DeSantis and fellow presidential hopeful [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] locked in a contest over who could demonstrate the most intense loyalty to a foreign country.[7] Whereas Haley was limited to warmongering, interventionist rhetoric, DeSantis's status as a sitting governor gave him opportunities for executive action.[7]

Having already arranged flights to evacuate 700 Americans from war- and terrorism-shaken Israel, DeSantis announced on a Thursday that the state of Florida had used cargo planes to send drones, body armor and helmets to Israel, and that he had also facilitated the shipment of privately purchased guns and ammunition.[7] Details of the announcement were immediately challenged by an Israeli official: the governor's office claimed DeSantis had acted in accordance with a request from Israel's Miami consulate, but Major Elbaz-Starinsky, the consul general in Miami, said that was false.[7]

## Pressure to ban mRNA vaccines (2023)

In 2023 Republican executive committees in nine Florida counties adopted the [[ban-the-jab-resolution|"Ban the Jab" resolution]], pressuring DeSantis, county sheriffs and the Florida Legislature to prohibit the sale and distribution of [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]] and all other mRNA vaccines in the state.[8] The 83-page measure, written by psychotherapist Joseph Sansone, also asked state Attorney General Ashley Moody to seize remaining vaccine supplies for forensic analysis, and had spread from Lee County to Collier, Lake, Santa Rosa, Seminole, St. Johns, Hillsborough, Brevard and finally Franklin County by August 19, 2023.[8]

## End of the 2024 campaign

DeSantis ended his fading presidential campaign on Sunday, January 21, 2024, and endorsed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] two days before the pivotal New Hampshire primary, leaving former U.N. Ambassador [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] as Trump's last long-shot challenger for the Republican nomination.[9] The decision came less than a week after his deflating loss to Trump in Iowa despite an enormous investment there, capping what Reuters described as a stunning fall from grace for a governor widely seen as Republicans' most promising alternative to Trump ahead of the November general election.[9]

His departure produced the one-on-one contest Haley had coveted against Trump, who had maintained an iron grip on the Republican electorate despite facing four criminal prosecutions in all of which he pleaded not guilty.[9] DeSantis's supporters nonetheless appeared more likely to migrate to Trump than to the more moderate Haley: in New Hampshire, about two-thirds of DeSantis backers named Trump as their second choice, according to Andrew Smith, director of the University of New Hampshire Survey Center.[9]

In a video posted on X, DeSantis endorsed Trump while delivering a parting shot at Haley: "He has my endorsement because we can't go back to the old Republican guard of yesteryear: a re-packaged form of warmed-over corporatism that Nikki Haley represents."[9]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] wrote that it was "always inevitable" that both DeSantis and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] would endorse Trump, arguing that whether by design or by accident they had helped divert establishment funding and expose uniparty sleepers, and that Trump "has been the signal in the noise, and remains so."[9] He predicted the "DeSimps, Vivekians and Con Inc. Doom Brigade" would crawl back into their shells and reapply the MAGA war paint they so easily wash away when the story turns against the movement, adding: "These people showed you who they are, emphatically. Believe them."[9]

## 2023 debate attacks on Trump

Through the first two Republican primary debates of the 2024 cycle, DeSantis appeared confident and relatively unscathed and acted like Trump's leading challenger for the nomination — with only the frontrunner himself missing.[10] After Trump skipped the first two debates and his campaign announced he would also be a no-show at a third, DeSantis accused him of being "missing in action" and, worse in the eyes of many Republicans, of following the example of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[10] "With all due respect to Donald Trump," DeSantis told Maria Bartiromo on Fox News, "we're not going to beat the Democrats by adopting Joe Biden's basement strategy."[10] The governor was deliberately repurposing one of Trump's own favorite attacks — that Biden hid from voters and the press during the pandemic — echoing Trump's September 2020 line in Virginia: "I got a guy who stays in his damn basement all day long, and I'm doing this."[10] The DeSantis campaign concluded that Trump might no longer be the debate brawler who bullied the previous GOP field, hoped to prod him into a showdown, and argued that voters deserved to hear him defend his record directly.[10] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] dismissed the line of attack, writing that it merely confirmed what the country already knew — that campaigning against "Uniparty shills & RINO cast-offs" for a presidency Trump never lost was beneath his notice.[10]

## 2024 primary expectations (March 2023)

In March 2023, Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] observed that the "inflated opinion" that DeSantis would win the Republican presidential primary was "haunting social media and comment sections across the internet," despite DeSantis trailing in every poll DeLarme had seen.[11] He associated the sentiment with anonymous accounts that simultaneously argued [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had admitted Deep State figures into his White House.[11]

## Defense of Trump after the August 2023 indictment

After the [[trump-january-6-indictment|third federal indictment]] of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on August 1, 2023, DeSantis was among the more notable defenders, saying that as president he would "end the weaponization of government, replace the FBI Director, and ensure a single standard of justice for all Americans."[12] He described Washington, D.C. as a "swamp" and said it was "unfair to have to stand trial before a jury that is reflective of the swamp mentality."[12] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that whether the Trump–DeSantis feud was genuine or narrative, DeSantis's rush to Trump's defense showed that the powers behind him sought to wear the [[america-first-movement|America First movement]] "as a skin suit" and co-opt its messaging.[12]

## Eroding clout and the Hope Florida scandal (2025)

By April 2025, a year after the collapse of his presidential ambitions, DeSantis and his wife [[casey-desantis|Casey DeSantis]] could no longer claim the future of the Republican Party, and their standing in Florida itself was in jeopardy.[13] Sunshine State Republicans who had once been firmly in his corner turned on him, stymieing his agenda.[13] At the same time Casey DeSantis met quiet but firm resistance as she laid the groundwork for a run to succeed her husband — a campaign that would pit her against [[byron-donalds|Byron Donalds]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s handpicked choice to lead the state.[13]

A funding scandal involving [[hope-florida|Hope Florida]], a state assistance program that was one of Casey's signature initiatives, cast a shadow over the governor's legacy.[13] Lawmakers spent the spring investigating why $10 million from a state Medicaid settlement was routed to a charity connected to Hope Florida, which then transferred the same amount to two groups that financially backed a DeSantis-led campaign against legalizing recreational marijuana; key lawmakers publicly suggested the flow of money appeared illegal.[13] The couple denied wrongdoing, with DeSantis calling the criticism "all political" and Casey describing the program as "a philosophy" that "shows we can help people in need."[13] That the investigation was conducted by Republicans rather than Democrats was read as a striking sign of his diminished authority in Tallahassee.[13]

## Immigration enforcement cooperation (April 2025)

DeSantis publicly praised [[operation-tidal-wave|Operation Tidal Wave]], the joint ICE–Florida enforcement operation that produced nearly 800 arrests in April 2025, calling it "an example of FL and [the Department of Homeland Security] partnering to deliver big results on immigration enforcement and deportations" and asserting that "Florida is leading the nation in active cooperation with the Trump administration for immigration enforcement and deportation operations!"[13]

## Deportation Legislative Blueprint (January 2025)

On January 15, 2025, DeSantis unveiled a package of legislative proposals designed to expedite the incoming Trump administration's planned deportation of illegal aliens.[14] According to a press release from his office, the proposed legislation would establish "a state crime for entering the U.S. illegally and a process for self-deportation," expand "the legal definition of gang-related activities to include more groups of dangerous illegal aliens," and repeal "in-state college tuition for illegal immigrant students."[14] The blueprint also called for "maximum participation in the 287(g) deportation program, with penalties for non-compliance, including suspension of officials."[14]

## The 2023 feud with Trump

By May 2023 the relationship between DeSantis and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had deteriorated into open hostility, prompting a New York Times opinion piece asking whether either man remembered a time when they had liked one another.[15] The column contrasted the two on personal conduct, noting that only Trump had just lost a $5 million jury verdict finding that he sexually abused a woman in a department store dressing room, while Trump for his part insinuated that DeSantis had engaged in shady conduct with high school girls in his distant past and ran an advertisement drawing attention to the governor's reported habit of eating pudding with his fingers.[15]

On policy the column found Trump harder to pin down, observing that he had been running to DeSantis's left by accusing the Florida governor of wanting to slash Social Security and Medicare benefits — proposals DeSantis had indeed voted for while in [[congress|Congress]], though he had since adopted a no-changes position.[15] On abortion the contrast was sharper: DeSantis, described as a devout Catholic consistent throughout his political career, had signed a bill barring abortions in Florida after six weeks, while Trump was said to adapt.[15]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] responded that whether the feud was real did not matter for the moment, arguing that Trump was "the past, present and immediate future of the America First movement," that there would be other [[maga-coalition|MAGA]] leaders in time, and that anyone attempting to move supporters off the Trump train was seeking to divide rather than unify.[15] He added that [[con-inc|Con Inc.]] had fallen into the same quicksand as the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]].[15]

## "Slitting throats" rhetoric and the Disney district (2023)

Campaigning in New Hampshire in early August 2023, DeSantis said he would begin "slitting throats" of deep state personnel on his first day as president: "We're going to have all these deep state people. You know, we're going to start slitting throats on Day One."[16] The remark followed a vow to crack down on Mexican drug cartels, of whose operatives at the border he said "they're going to be shot stone, cold dead," and was paired with a pledge that "we're going to make sure that the woke agenda ends up in the dustbin of history."[16] At the time he trailed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] by a significant margin in polling, and the language turned off at least one attendee — Norm Olsen, a self-described "Sununu Republican" exploring alternatives to Trump, told New Hampshire Public Radio that as DeSantis's PR adviser he "would have said, 'Don't use that terminology.'"[16]

His Florida record was cited alongside the rhetoric: the [[stop-woke-act|Stop WOKE Act]], which bars schools and businesses from teaching material that could cause guilt or psychological distress based on race, national origin or sex, and the [[parental-rights-in-education-act|Parental Rights in Education Act]], the so-called "don't say gay" law later extended through the twelfth grade.[16] In February 2023 he signed legislation abolishing the [[reedy-creek-improvement-district|Reedy Creek Improvement District]] — under which [[disney]] had appointed its own regulator since 1967 and enjoyed tax exemptions — and creating the [[central-florida-tourism-oversight-district|Central Florida Tourism Oversight District]] with members he appointed, an act arising from his war with Disney over the education bill.[16] That board eliminated all of its [[diversity-equity-and-inclusion|diversity, equity, and inclusion]] programs on August 1, 2023.[16]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that although DeSantis's remarks on the [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] indictment seemed strong on the surface, they drew resounding backlash from the base he was courting, whose members saw a wolf in sheep's clothing willing to say the right things while withholding an unqualified endorsement of Trump.[16] He maintained that the net effect of the DeSantis campaign had been to add "rocket fuel" to Trump's, and speculated about a later shift in tune once DeSantis had absorbed Establishment GOP funding.[16] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cautioned that the DEI decision came from the DeSantis-created board rather than Disney itself and might deliver little real change.[16]

## Turning Point Action invitation (July 2023)

In July 2023 DeSantis was reported to have declined an invitation to speak at a [[turning-point-action|Turning Point Action]] event in West Palm Beach, Florida — a decision that perplexed conservatives, since Turning Point had invited every presidential candidate and DeSantis had spoken at its events before.[17] [[jack-posobiec|Jack Posobiec]] broke the story, [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] responded "Too bad!", and TPA Chief Operating Officer [[tyler-bowyer|Tyler Bowyer]] questioned the campaign's strategy.[17]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that a serious candidate would be campaigning in overdrive rather than passing on a friendly appearance in his own state, and argued the snub was one more misstep in a campaign that had no chance in the primary against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — though it might affect his prospects for a cabinet post.[17]

## 2023 debate with Newsom

DeSantis was scheduled to debate California governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] on November 30, 2023, in a televised matchup previewed as an event where neither candidate was really debating the other but auditioning for the nomination within his own party.[18] Any slights or barbs were understood to be aimed at impressing voters in the taunter's own party rather than winning the general electorate, with DeSantis in particular targeting the first crucial states — Iowa, with caucuses on January 15, and New Hampshire, with its January 23 primary.[18] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterised the debate as a contest over "who wears the skin of false reality projection more unconvincingly within their respective dying political parties," and expected it to damage both men.[18]

## Senior staff departures (July 2023)

In July 2023, two senior advisers on DeSantis' presidential campaign — communications adviser and media director [[dave-abrams|Dave Abrams]] and external affairs lead [[tucker-obenshain|Tucker Obenshain]] — departed to help a pro-DeSantis outside political nonprofit, a move that came as the governor struggled to gain traction against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[19] Obenshain was to lead the group, which was expected to host events as part of what the campaign called a "DeSantis is everywhere strategy" targeting Iowa, New Hampshire and South Carolina.[19] Campaign spokesman [[andrew-romeo|Andrew Romeo]] said that "the more people hear and see Gov. DeSantis and his forward-thinking vision to fix Joe Biden's failures, the better our path to victory."[19]

The reshuffle came amid broader questions about the campaign's strength, even as DeSantis and his allied super PAC [[never-back-down|Never Back Down]] self-reported large fundraising hauls, including a claimed $20 million in the second quarter of 2023.[19] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the departures as "rats fleeing a sinking ship" and the campaign as "a farcical operation covered by a thin veneer of False Trumpism," noting that DeSantis donors claimed to be shocked at his inability to cut into Trump's lead among the GOP base.[19]

## Social media ban for minors

On March 25, 2024, DeSantis signed a law giving Florida one of the most restrictive social media regimes for minors in the United States, banning accounts for children under 14 and requiring parental permission for 14- and 15-year-olds.[20] The measure — the top legislative priority of Republican state house speaker [[paul-renner|Paul Renner]] — was slightly watered down from a proposal DeSantis had vetoed earlier in March, a week before the annual legislative session ended, and takes effect on January 1, though it must still withstand expected legal challenges.[20]

## Turning Point USA partnership (October 2025)

On Monday, October 27, 2025, DeSantis announced that the state of Florida was partnering with [[turning-point-usa|Turning Point USA]] to ensure that all "Club America" chapters on high school campuses could launch without impediments or holdups.[21] He warned that districts or schools opposing the launch of these clubs would receive a warning before the state would "drop the hammer."[21]

The remarks came at a press conference at the College of Florida, where DeSantis also highlighted a new Florida debate and civics initiative featuring a Ronald Reagan Presidential Debate, with a championship prize of $50,000 and a trophy named in honor of the late Turning Point USA president [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[21] Badlands commentary dismissed the announcement as "so gay and predictable," suggesting the storyline had been written long in advance on the assumption that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would not be in the picture.[21]

## Trump's "globalist" attack, January 2023

Shortly after midnight on January 30, 2023, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted on TruthSocial calling the [[club-for-growth|Club for Growth]] a "GLOBALIST group" and asserting that DeSantis — whom he again nicknamed "Ron DeSanctimonious" — "is also a Globalist, & so are his donors," adding that [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] had been beside him the previous week.[22] Trump claimed credit for making DeSantis governor "in BOTH the Primary & the General."[22]

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] suggested the exchange might be a ploy to occupy the media with a non-story about DeSantis's presumed 2024 ambitions, noting the governor had made no statement about running.[22] He argued the effect was to attract centrists who dislike Trump but favor the America First agenda, citing figures such as [[tim-pool|Tim Pool]], and described DeSantis as effectively a policy copy of Trump playing "good cop" — speaking against China, globalists, critical race theory and the queer agenda without invoking 2020 election fraud or the Russiagate hoax.[22] Deschamps also noted DeSantis's popularity during the COVID-19 pandemic for his anti-vaccine-mandate stance and refusal to impose lengthy closures, in contrast to Trump's exposure over [[operation-warp-speed|Operation Warp Speed]], and speculated that if the feud were staged the two would "make up" once a GOP nominee emerged.[22]


## Ending Florida vaccine mandates (September 2025)

On September 3, 2025, DeSantis appeared alongside Florida Surgeon General [[joseph-ladapo|Joseph A. Ladapo]] as the state announced plans to become the first in the nation to end all vaccine mandates, including those for schoolchildren — rejecting a practice public health experts have credited for decades with limiting the spread of infectious diseases.[23] Ladapo told an applauding audience in Valrico, near Tampa: "Who am I to tell you what your child should put in their body? Your body is a gift from God," and said the administration would be "working to end" all vaccine mandates.[23] The New York Times noted that DeSantis, who rose to national prominence during the coronavirus pandemic, had over time espoused increasingly anti-vaccine views.[23]
## 2024 campaign launch with Elon Musk (May 2023)

NBC News reported on May 23, 2023 that DeSantis would announce his presidential candidacy during a discussion with Twitter chief executive [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], according to three sources familiar with the plans.[24] The two were to host an event on Twitter Spaces, the platform's audio-chat service, on Wednesday at 6 p.m. ET, moderated by tech entrepreneur [[david-sacks|David Sacks]], a Musk confidant and DeSantis supporter.[24] That same evening the campaign planned to release a launch video, with DeSantis beginning visits to several early states after Memorial Day.[24]

The arrangement was described as a potentially significant boost, introducing DeSantis to Musk's massive following — including his 140 million [[twitter|Twitter]] followers — while also risking distraction from the tycoon's many controversial comments.[24] Musk, who like DeSantis regularly chides corporate media, had said the previous year that he would support the governor if he ran for president.[24]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the launch as "The Ron DeSantis Deployment," predicting that whether or not DeSantis was "in on it," the episode would spell the death of [[con-inc|Con Inc.]], described as the most powerful and subversive narrative enemy remaining inside the MAGA and America First movements.[24] He argued these were the same "wolves in sheep's clothing" exposed during COVID and in earnest during the [[2022-midterm-elections|2022 midterm cycle]], and that they were about to publicly choose a side — just as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] wanted.[24] In his reading the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] deployed the DeSantis card far too early, before the 2024 cycle had even begun, because Trump baited them by appearing weak on the eve of the midterms and by announcing his own 2024 run in a move the establishment read as premature; the media then activated Con Inc. from "the Daily Liar on down the line," spending its ammunition and falling into "the Trump quicksand."[24]

## Red vs. Blue State Debate (November 2023)

On November 30, 2023, DeSantis faced California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] in [[great-red-vs-blue-state-debate|"The Great Red vs. Blue State Debate"]], a 90-minute [[fox-news|Fox News]] program moderated by [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]] in Alpharetta, Georgia — described in coverage as possibly the most unorthodox match-up of the 2024 primary campaign.[25] Asked to grade President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], DeSantis gave a "fail" and charged that Newsom was "joined at the hip with Biden and Harris."[25]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] judged DeSantis "completely detached from the conservative base, independents, and thinking people," arguing his attacks on California were headline talking points without substance and that Hannity raised the most salient points of the night.[25] She faulted him for ignoring Newsom's jab about pardoning J6 defendants rather than defending them, and read his production of a gender book on stage as evidence he mistook the GOP base for the Daily Wire audience.[25] She also argued the format itself was a trap, forcing a presidential candidate to attack the state with the most electoral votes, and concluded the appearance proved his campaign "has not yet found rock bottom."[25]

## August 2023 collapse in the polls

An [[emerson-college-polling|Emerson College]] national poll released in August 2023 showed DeSantis had fallen from 21 percent in June to 10 percent, leaving him tied for second place with [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]], who had risen from 2 percent over the same period; [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] led with 56 percent.[26] The poll coincided with a leaked memo from his super PAC [[never-back-down|Never Back Down]] urging the governor to "take a sledgehammer" to Ramaswamy at the coming debate, which Ramaswamy mocked as a pre-programmed attack from "Robot Ron."[26]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] wrote that DeSantis was "tainted" — that he had betrayed Trump by saying he would not run and then running, and had betrayed the trust of Floridians — while conceding he was fairly authentic and more trustworthy than the average establishment politician.[26] Deschamps cited the campaign's use of AI-generated images of Trump with [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] as an example of its "shady" conduct.[26] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the DeSantis campaign had "run out of juice before it really left the starting blocks" and that [[con-inc|Con Inc.]] was already pivoting to Ramaswamy as its next vehicle.[26]

## May 2023 Iowa polling

Shortly after formally declaring his presidential run on Wednesday, May 24, 2023 in a Twitter livestream with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], DeSantis trailed badly in Iowa.[27] An [[emerson-college|Emerson College]] poll released the following morning found 62 percent of Iowa Republicans backing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] against 20 percent for DeSantis — a three-to-one margin — with [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]] and [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] tied for third at 5 percent.[27] Despite two widely covered Iowa visits earlier that month, no corresponding polling boost had materialized, and his Iowa number was roughly level with his RealClearPolitics national average.[27] The survey found DeSantis ran closest to Trump among Republicans with postgraduate degrees, while Trump dominated among voters under 35 and those without college degrees; in a general-election test DeSantis led President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in Iowa 45 percent to 38 percent, a narrower margin than Trump's 49-38 lead.[27]

## 2028 polling (December 2025)

## Hurricane Milton and the Harris feud (October 2024)

Ahead of [[hurricane-milton|Hurricane Milton]]'s approach to Florida's Gulf Coast in October 2024, DeSantis came under attack from Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], who accused him of "political gamesmanship" and called him selfish and "utterly responsible" after reports that he had not taken her calls.[29] In a Monday evening interview with Fox News, DeSantis said he was "leveraging all resources available, including from the federal government," that he had been in touch with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and with [[fema|Federal Emergency Management Agency]] officials, and that "for Kamala Harris to try to say that my sole focus on the people of Florida is somehow selfish, is delusional."[29]

The dispute produced unusual cross-partisan support for the governor: CNN senior political commentator [[scott-jennings|Scott Jennings]] said Harris was "attacking the governor of a state that's about to get lashed with one of the worst storms" and asked "Is she crazy?," while Biden said on Tuesday that DeSantis was doing a great job and had been given his personal phone number.[29] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read Biden's siding with DeSantis as part of a wider pattern of establishment figures hedging against the Harris campaign.[29]

## April 2024 pledge to fundraise for Trump

In early April 2024 DeSantis reportedly told donors and supporters at a private gathering that he planned to help raise money for the presidential campaign of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[30] The former Republican presidential candidate made the announcement on a Saturday at South Florida's Hard Rock Hotel and Casino, and a DeSantis adviser confirmed to NBC News, "He did say it in front of a group of people at the Hard Rock."[30]

Texas businessman Roy Bailey, a co-chairman of the DeSantis national finance advisory board who attended the retreat, said the governor was "committed to helping Trump in any and every way" and pledged to raise money for Trump himself as he had in 2020.[30] Bailey added that he knew of DeSantis supporters "all over Texas and all over the country" who would want to help.[30] The news followed Trump's largest fundraiser of the cycle, which had raised $50.5 million.[30]

## The "De-Sanctimonious" nickname

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] devoted part of a June 2023 essay to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s nickname for DeSantis, "De-Sanctimonious," calling it a strange insult that he had rarely if ever heard used against anyone.[49] Noting that "sanctimonious" means "making a show of being morally superior to other people," he argued the phrase "to make a show" implies only that the person presents himself differently from how he really is — not really as conservative, not really as America First, or not really as moral as claimed — while leaving wiggle room for mere exaggeration rather than outright opposition.[49]

Carlson's purpose was to connect the nickname to Senator [[joseph-mccarthy|Joseph McCarthy]], who used the same unusual word as an insult, referring to his critic [[stuart-symington|Stuart Symington]] as "Sanctimonious Stu" even in official Senate hearings during his 1950s investigations of suspected Communists.[49] He conceded the parallel could be dismissed as thin coincidence on its own, but presented it alongside the fact that Trump's mentor [[roy-cohn|Roy Cohn]] had served as McCarthy's chief counsel, arguing that the accumulated connections were too numerous to explain away.[49]

## Deschamps's "double agent" theory (December 2023)

In "Vivek, DeSantis, and Trump's Master Move," published December 20, 2023, [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] advanced the theory that DeSantis was playing a role for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] while simultaneously appearing to play one for the [[deep-state|Deep State]] — in his words, "a double agent, working for Trump, while at the same time, seemingly working for the Deep State."[50] Deschamps argued that DeSantis was meant to look like the uninteresting, non-charismatic, "let-the-wind-take-me" candidate while still seeming like a plausible next-best-thing to many Trump supporters and Trump-hating Republicans.[50]

On his account, Trump anticipated that the Deep State would look for a candidate with some of his freedom- and America-first qualities who could be pushed around, either for lack of firm positions or through blackmail.[50] Deschamps proposed that the Deep State intended Florida to serve as the "bastion of freedom" during the pandemic specifically to make DeSantis look good in preparation for a 2024 [[nikki-haley|Haley]] play, believing that the theatre of DeSantis "fighting back against COVID-tyrants" would win over patriots who did not perceive the larger game, even though the operation's architects knew DeSantis never had a chance of beating Trump.[50]

Deschamps argued that Trump played into this by propping DeSantis up from the beginning and then apparently breaking ties with him, attacking DeSantis seemingly out of nowhere before he had even begun campaigning — a move Deschamps called brilliant because it fed the media image of Trump as an ego-driven "brat prince" who could not share the limelight, and because it forced the Deep State to commit its card.[50] The plan he attributed to the Deep State was to have DeSantis draw support away from Trump, then make DeSantis look ridiculous — Deschamps cited reporting on DeSantis's heel-lifted boots and the possibility of a campaign staffed with aggressive online defenders whose white-knighting made him more unpalatable — before sacrificing him at the right moment, so that his embittered supporters would migrate to Haley as their only remaining hope.[50] Deschamps also grouped DeSantis with [[chris-christie|Chris Christie]] and [[tim-scott|Tim Scott]] as props deployed to make Haley look "strong" and "presidential."[50]

He further noted that [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]]'s December 2023 pledge to withdraw from the Colorado GOP primary ballot explicitly demanded that DeSantis, Christie and Haley do the same, on pain of tacitly endorsing what Ramaswamy called an illegal maneuver.[50]
