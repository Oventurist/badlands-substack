# Wisconsin Elections Commission

The Wisconsin Elections Commission is the state agency that administers and oversees elections in Wisconsin.[1]

In May 2026 the [[fbi|FBI]], as part of its investigation into Wisconsin's 2020 election, spoke with the commission's Deputy Administrator, Robert Kehoe.[1] The bureau's inquiry also encompassed the [[milwaukee-election-commission|Milwaukee Election Commission]], former director [[claire-woodall|Claire Woodall]]'s handling of a flash drive left inside a voting machine, and planned interviews with [[milwaukee-police-department|Milwaukee Police Department]] officers assigned to the central count facility and polling places on Election Night.[1]

## Musk criminal referral (2026)

In July 2026 a commission panel found probable cause that [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] likely violated Wisconsin's election bribery law by offering $1 million to voters during the 2025 Wisconsin Supreme Court race.[2] In a 5-1 vote, the commission referred two confidential complaints to the Brown County District Attorney's Office, which will decide whether to pursue criminal charges and has 40 days to report back.[2]

The referral centers on a social media post in which Musk offered $1 million to people who voted in the election, which the commission said was intended to induce voting.[2] Three Wisconsin voters ultimately received $1 million checks, including two who were presented the money by Musk at a Green Bay rally days before the election.[2] Musk and affiliated groups spent at least $20 million supporting Republican-backed candidate [[brad-schimel|Brad Schimel]], and his [[america-pac|America PAC]] earlier offered $100 to voters who signed a petition opposing "activist judges" or referred others to sign it.[2]

## Cerny v. WEC ruling, October 2025

In a decision issued Friday, October 3, 2025, in [[cerny-v-wec|Cerny v. WEC]], a Wisconsin judge ruled that the Wisconsin Elections Commission must verify US citizenship for every voter registration and audit the state's entire voter roll.[3] The court issued a writ of mandamus directing the commission to comply with state law and the constitution: it must verify citizenship for every new registration, cross-check current rolls against Department of Transportation and other lawful records to identify non-citizen registrants, refuse registration requests lacking proof of citizenship, and report progress on the audit to the court within 60 days.[3] Judge Maxwell criticized the commission for having previously claimed no duty to prevent non-citizens from registering, citing both the US Constitution and the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]].[3]
