# Accountability Review Board

The Accountability Review Board (ARB) is the [[state-department|State Department]] mechanism convened to review security failures at U.S. diplomatic facilities; the board convened after the September 11, 2012 [[benghazi-attack|attack on the U.S. mission in Benghazi]] became one of the ten official inquiries into the incident.[1]

## Findings and the "29 recommendations"

The board's review focused on security lapses at the Benghazi facilities, and Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] responded by agreeing to implement all 29 of its recommendations so that such an event could never happen again.[1] Most of the media treated that acceptance as closing the matter.[1] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that this pattern — admitting "mistakes," announcing reforms with great fanfare, and moving on without any individual accountability — is precisely how genuine wrongdoing is buried.[1]

## Criticism as a sham review

Citing National Review's coverage of the Benghazi chapter of Clinton's memoir, the article notes that Clinton pointed to having ordered an investigation nine days after the attacks — a delay the columnist treated as a strangely tardy discharge of an obvious duty rather than as exculpatory — and to having agreed with and implemented all 29 recommendations of a review board that had so thoroughly and preemptively rejected the notion of her responsibility that it never bothered to interview her.[1] Because Clinton sat at the center of the scandal yet was never interviewed, the Badlands account calls the ARB investigation "a complete sham" and part of the cover-up rather than a genuine accountability exercise.[1]

## The withheld documents

Whistleblower [[raymond-maxwell|Raymond Maxwell]], a Deputy Assistant Secretary in the department's Bureau of Near Eastern Affairs, later alleged that Clinton confidants — he named [[cheryl-mills|Cheryl Mills]] and [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] — ran an after-hours weekend session in a basement operations center at State Department headquarters to "separate" damaging documents before they were turned over to the board.[1] If accurate, the allegation means the ARB never received the complete record it had requested.[1] The claim surfaced publicly as the [[house-select-committee-on-benghazi|House Select Committee on Benghazi]] under [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]] prepared for its first hearing in September 2014.[1]

These characterizations reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
