# Chad

Chad is a Central African state that in July 2026 became the latest nation to announce its withdrawal from the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]].[1]

## ICC withdrawal

Chad announced on a Monday in late July 2026 that it was withdrawing from the ICC, accusing the Hague-based body of ineffectiveness and of bias against African countries. The government submitted a formal letter to the United Nations to begin the process, which under the [[rome-statute|Rome Statute]] takes at least one year to complete.[1]

Chad joined a growing wave of exits. Niger, Mali, and [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] all began withdrawing the previous year, and [[venezuela|Venezuela]] announced its formal withdrawal days before Chad's move, accusing the court of "geographical bias" that disproportionately targets countries in Africa and Latin America.[1]

## US response

US Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] said earlier in July 2026 that the United States was launching a campaign to dismantle the ICC's threat to national sovereignty. A February 2025 executive order signed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had imposed sanctions on the court over its investigations of [[israel|Israel]]. The [[state-department|State Department]]'s Bureau of African Affairs welcomed Chad's move on X, calling on more of the court's 125 member states to withdraw and "reclaim their sovereignty from this flawed institution."[1]

## French military withdrawal (December 2024)

Two French fighter jets left Chad in December 2024, signalling the start of Paris's withdrawal from the central African nation and a dramatic reduction in French military involvement in the wider Sahel region.[2] The Mirage warplanes departed the Chadian capital N'Djamena and returned to their base in eastern France; France still had more jets and around 1,000 soldiers in the country, and military spokesman Colonel Guillaume Vernet said the move marked "the beginning of the return of French equipment stationed in N'Djamena."[2]

A French colony since the turn of the twentieth century, Chad was granted independence in 1960 but remained tied to its former ruler through Paris's control of the Central African CFA Franc and the presence of French troops from the 1980s onward.[2] The French base in N'Djamena served as headquarters for France's anti-terrorism operation across the Sahel, but the perceived ineffectiveness of French forces against jihadism led to that operation ending in 2022 and to multiple countries terminating military agreements with Paris.[2]

Badlands commentary framed the withdrawal as the erasure of the last bastion of European imperialism - military occupation - from the African continent, dating the story arc to a 2015 leaked UN whistleblower report on the trafficking and rape of children in Nigeria by French peacekeeping troops.[2] It cautioned that much work remained, citing reports that around 30% of Burkina Faso under Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traore]] remains under some form of control or influence by what the writer called NATO-cartel groups such as ISIS, and concluded that sovereignty is not a privilege but a responsibility.[2]

## US and French troop presence (2024)

In September 2024 the Prime Minister of Chad said that the US military, which had left the country in May 2024, was not coming back.[3] Badlands Media noted, citing Sputnik, that the government of Chad has no quarrel with the US military; it is the French military that the people of Chad consider a hostile adversary and are demanding be expelled.[3] If Chad does expel French forces, it would mean that for the first time since 1659 — when the French first built a port in what is now Senegal — [[france|France]] would have no military presence on the continent of Africa, making Chad "the last bastion of French imperialism."[3] The commentary situated this within a broader African realignment in which the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] and the Russian military have trained local security forces to counter forces trained by NATO and the EU, and rejected French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]]'s stated ambition of "building bridges" with Africa.[3]
