# Omer Maxim Neutra

Omer Maxim Neutra was an American-Israeli citizen who served as a tank platoon commander in the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] and was long believed to be alive and held hostage in Gaza before the Israeli military announced on Monday, December 2, 2024, that he had been killed during the Hamas-led attack of [[october-7-attacks|October 7, 2023]].[1] He was 21 years old.[1]

## Announcement and circumstances

The IDF said Neutra's body was still being held in Gaza, and it was unclear what prompted the announcement; in previous cases a hostage had been declared dead on the basis of new intelligence and evidence.[1] Israeli Foreign Minister Israel Katz said Neutra "fell in a heroic battle near Nir Oz on October 7."[1] Nir Oz, a small kibbutz near Gaza in southern Israel, was among the communities worst affected by the attacks, with roughly one in four residents murdered or kidnapped.[1]

The Hostages and Missing Families Forum described Neutra in a statement as a young man who "loved sports, playing soccer, basketball, and volleyball," served as captain of his school's sports teams, and was remembered by family and friends as "a warm, optimistic, and caring individual who 'lights up the room the moment he enters.'"[1]

## Family advocacy

Neutra's parents, Ronen and Orna Neutra, had spoken to CNN the previous week about marking a second Thanksgiving without their son.[1] Believing him alive, they hoped the ceasefire between Israel and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] in Lebanon could serve as "a time to refocus" on securing his release.[1] Ronen Neutra said attention in Israel and abroad had shifted from Gaza to Lebanon over the preceding four or five months, and expressed disappointment that the truce was not combined with a hostage deal, calling it "a missed opportunity."[1] Neutra was one of seven American citizens held in Gaza, four of whom, including him, had been pronounced dead.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the announcement showed the family had been led for thirteen months to believe their son was alive and had been publicly presented as part of what he characterized as a psychological operation.[1] He questioned the plausibility of [[hamas|Hamas]] preserving a body through more than a year of bombardment, and connected the episode to the IDF's reported use of the [[hannibal-directive|Hannibal Directive]] on October 7.[1]
