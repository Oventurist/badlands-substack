# Minor Cooper Keith

**Minor Cooper Keith** was an American businessman whose Central American railroad and banana ventures gave rise to the [[united-fruit-company|United Fruit Company]].[1]

## Costa Rican railroad and the banana trade

According to a historical account relayed by Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] on [[breaking-history|Breaking History]] and sourced to researcher War Hamster, Keith moved to Costa Rica in 1880 at the age of 21 to work for his uncle's railroad company, which was ceded to him two years later.[1] The company was contracted with the Costa Rican government, which defaulted on its payments; Keith raised capital from London banks and bought the government out.[1] He completed the railroad in 1890, but Central America lacked passenger traffic, so he entered the banana business instead.[1]

Bananas are not indigenous to the Americas — they originate in Southeast Asia — but Keith had been experimenting with planting them since 1873 and eventually got them to take in the tropical climate.[1] Partnering with M.T. Snyder and the Tropical Trading and Transport Company, he expanded the banana industry from Costa Rica into Panama and Colombia, and by the turn of the century bananas were the second-most consumed food in America.[1]

## Formation of United Fruit

In 1899, following financial setbacks, Keith merged his company with the Boston Fruit Company, which already held an established empire in the West Indies, forming the United Fruit Company.[1] The combined firm immediately dominated the fruit trade and, through it, the logistics and transport of all freight in the region — earning the nickname *El Pulpo*, "The Octopus."[1] That control conferred political power the company exerted over developing nations, propping up dictators and giving rise to the term "banana republic."[1] The modern successor to Keith's enterprise is [[chiquita-brands-international|Chiquita Brands International]].[1]
