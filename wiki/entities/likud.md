# Likud

Likud is the Israeli political party led by Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and the dominant force in the governing coalition described in Badlands Media coverage of the 2026 Israel–[[iran|Iran]] war.[1]

## Role in the Sde Teiman debate

Likud member of the [[knesset|Knesset]] [[hanoch-milwidsky|Hanoch Milwidsky]] became the most-quoted figure in the parliamentary debate over whether Israeli soldiers should be permitted to sodomize male Palestinian detainees with objects, telling colleagues, "Shut up! If he is a Nukbha, then everything is legitimate to do!"[1] The debate followed the arrest — and later the dropping of charges against — soldiers accused of assaulting a detainee at [[sde-teiman|Sde Teiman]].[1]

## Electoral context

Badlands analysis framed Likud's political fortunes as tied directly to Netanyahu's personal legal exposure: with an election scheduled for October and the prime minister standing trial on bribery, fraud, and breach-of-trust charges, commentators argued that Netanyahu had strong incentives to press forward with the [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] agenda and the war against Iran rather than pursue a more gradual political strategy.[1]

## Alleged organized-crime infiltration

Badlands commentary published in July 2024 cited [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]]' 2010 release of U.S. diplomatic communications in which the American Embassy in Israel expressed "grave concern" over the election of Inbal Gavrieli to the [[knesset|Knesset]] as a Likud member, she being the niece of the head of the Gavrieli crime family.[2] The cables were described as revealing serious concern that organized crime syndicates had infiltrated Likud's central committee and effectively controlled it, a claim Badlands ties to the broader [[israeli-mafia|Israeli mafia]] thesis.[2]

## Absorption of Yisrael BaAliyah

The same commentary notes that [[natan-sharansky|Natan Sharansky]]'s [[yisrael-baaliyah|Yisrael BaAliyah]] party — whose 1996 campaign strategy and polling were produced by [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] — was later integrated into [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s Likud.[2]

## West Bank annexation letter (July 2025)

In early July 2025, 15 members of the Likud party who serve in Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s cabinet signed a letter demanding that he immediately annex the West Bank and make Palestine part of Israel.[3] Badlands commentary by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] held that the disclosure was notable precisely because the ministers making the request were not drawn from the factions of radicalized zealots serving in the Security Cabinet — the highest ranking officials in the Israeli government — and yet sounded just as unhinged.[3] He predicted the Likud ministers would find strong allies among the Ultra-Orthodox zealots in the security cabinet, concluding that a war with Palestine had become the ambition of many top Israeli political parties and that removing Netanyahu would not resolve the underlying wars, because these other factions also want wars of imperialism.[3]

## Kahane controversy (November 2025)

On Wednesday, November 12, 2025, Likud MK [[nissim-vaturi|Nissim Vaturi]] told the [[knesset|Knesset]] plenum that "Kahane was right" and that the party had erred in ostracizing [[meir-kahane|Meir Kahane]], insisting the rabbi "was not a terrorist" despite his convictions for terrorist activity in Israel and the United States.[4] The remarks came during a debate on a bill to approve tax benefits for communities in the West Bank and the Gaza border area, in which MK Yorai Lahav Hertzanu of Yesh Atid told Likud that "from a proud liberal nationalist movement, you've turned into a pathetic branch of Kahane Chai."[4] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] argued that the episode reflects the broader ascendancy of [[kahanism|Kahanism]] within [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s Security Cabinet.[4]

## 2025 West Bank annexation letter

In 2025, fifteen cabinet ministers from Likud, together with Knesset speaker [[amir-ohana|Amir Ohana]], signed a letter calling for [[west-bank-annexation|annexation of the West Bank]] before the end of the parliamentary summer session, arguing that the creation of a Palestinian state in the territory would pose an "existential threat" to Israel and its settlement policy.[5] The signatories wrote that "the October 7 massacre proved that the doctrine of settlement blocs and the establishment of a Palestinian state in the remaining territory is an existential danger to Israel. It's time for sovereignty."[5] Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]], not a Likud member, praised the initiative.[5]

Badlands commentary highlighted that the demand came from Likud ministers holding smaller portfolios rather than from the hardliners of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s Security Cabinet, the only Likud member of which was Deputy Prime Minister and Justice Minister [[yariv-levin|Yariv Levin]].[5]

## Strains in June 2024

By late June 2024 Likud was "beginning to crack" as [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s government showed signs of heading toward an early election, with coalition party leaders already openly campaigning, senior military and civil-service figures clashing publicly with the prime minister, and Netanyahu himself broadly unpopular despite general public support for the war.[6]
