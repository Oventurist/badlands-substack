# H. R. McMaster

Herbert Raymond "H. R." McMaster is a retired United States Army lieutenant general who served as National Security Adviser during the first term of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], a post from which he was removed after thirteen months.[1] Since leaving government he has been a frequent commentator on national security matters and a critic of the president he once served.[1]

## The 2025 "accidental" phone call

In April 2025 CBS News reported, on the basis of four sources, that roughly a month earlier McMaster had received an unexpected call on his personal cell phone from the [[white-house|White House]], with the voice on the line instructing him to stand by for the president.[1] McMaster was surprised to be contacted at all: only a day earlier, on 2 March, Trump had publicly attacked him on social media as a "weak and totally ineffective loser."[1]

According to two sources cited by CBS News who were not authorized to discuss private conversations, Trump opened the call with "Henry..." — a name McMaster does not use, since H. R. stands for Herbert Raymond — leading McMaster to conclude that the commander-in-chief had not meant to call him at all.[1] After McMaster identified himself, Trump reportedly responded, "Why the f*** would I talk to H. R. McMaster?" and delivered a scathing critique of his former aide before the brief call ended.[1]

## Badlands commentary

The Badlands News Brief treated the episode as comic rather than damaging to Trump, noting that the leak appeared to originate from McMaster's own camp under the apparent belief that it embarrassed the president.[1] The commentary questioned the premise that a call placed to a man Trump had just insulted, and who had recently appeared on 60 Minutes to criticize him, was genuinely accidental.[1]

## National Security Advisor tenure and the Purple Revolution

Badlands Media's [[purple-revolution|Purple Revolution]] analysis treats McMaster as a case study in the infiltration of the [[donald-trump|Trump]] White House by compromised military figures.[2] He was appointed National Security Advisor on February 20, 2017, replacing General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]], whom the article describes as a target of the Deep State and too great a liability for Trump to keep in the Oval Office at that time, and was fired thirteen months later.[2] During his tenure McMaster is said to have ordered surveillance on Trump's family and on [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]].[2]

After [[january-6-capitol-protest|January 6]], McMaster wrote in a series of tweets that "President Trump and other officials have repeatedly compromised our principles in pursuit of partisan advantage and personal gain," and that those who engaged in "disinformation and demagoguery in pursuit of self-interest abdicated their responsibility to the American people" in what was "in every sense of the phrase, a dereliction of duty."[2] The article contrasts this with Bannon and Flynn, both also removed early, who spent the following years defending Trump — the distinction the author uses to separate saboteurs placed inside the administration from allies pushed outside it.[2]
