# Kevin Morley

Kevin Morley is the Federal Relations Manager for the [[american-water-works-association|American Water Works Association]] (AWWA) and one of four witnesses at the February 6, 2024 hearing of the [[house-committee-on-homeland-security|House Committee on Homeland Security]]'s Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Protection Subcommittee on securing [[operational-technology|operational technology]] in the water sector.[1]

## Background

Morley has twenty years of experience working across the water sector on security and preparedness.[1] He has supported national policy discussion on risk and resilience as a Disaster Resilience Fellow for the National Institute of Standards and Technology, as a member of the President's National Infrastructure Advisory Council, and as a member of the [[water-sector-coordinating-council|Water Sector Coordinating Council]].[1] He holds a PhD from [[george-mason-university|George Mason University]].[1]

## Testimony

Asked by Representative [[rob-menendez|Rob Menendez]] what results the [[biden-administration|Biden administration]]'s water sector cybersecurity "sprint" had produced, Morley answered that "some of the resources and focus was on some very specific technology solutions that, honestly, were a bit beyond the reach of many utilities in terms of maturity."[1]

On solutions, Morley advocated devolving resources to the community level, arguing that "if there was more direct resourcing to the local communities and the water companies that actually deal with their local integrators, the local contractors, etc., you would not only achieve more efficiency, but then you wouldn't have to worry about trying to make awareness available to 50,000 people or 50,000 entities," and that local jobs and resources would follow.[1] Representative [[august-pfluger|August Pfluger]] summarized the point as "taking some of that national and making it more local based and regional based."[1]

Morley's nightmare scenario echoed [[charles-clancy|Charles Clancy]]'s: cascading degradation of drinking water or wastewater services and the community consequences of those services being unavailable.[1] Responding to Representative [[troy-carter|Troy Carter]] on saltwater intrusion in Louisiana, Morley said planning for alternative water supply is "a critical need" requiring collaboration among the [[environmental-protection-agency|EPA]], the Army Corps of Engineers, and [[cisa|CISA]], and noted that the Water Sector Coordinating Council had made emergency alternative water supply a critical priority.[1] The article's author found this in tension with the panel's simultaneous position that building non-digital redundancy against cyber attack was inefficient and unnecessary.[1]
