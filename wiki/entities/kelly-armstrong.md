# Kelly Armstrong

Kelly Armstrong is a Republican member of the United States House of Representatives from North Dakota (R-ND) who, in December 2023, took a central procedural role in formalizing the House inquiry into President Joe Biden.[1]

## Impeachment inquiry resolution

In anticipation of a vote scheduled for the following week that would officially commence the Biden impeachment investigation, Armstrong introduced a fourteen-page resolution endorsing the inquiry.[1] The measure was scheduled for markup on Tuesday, December 12, placing it on track for a floor vote on Wednesday.[1] Its operative text directed "certain committees to continue their ongoing investigations as part of the existing House of Representatives inquiry into whether sufficient grounds exist for the House of Representatives to exercise its Constitutional power to impeach Joseph Biden, President of the United States of America, and for other purposes."[1]

In a statement accompanying the resolution, Armstrong argued that it was "time for the House to take the next step in the Biden impeachment investigation and adopt an impeachment inquiry resolution," noting that the White House and multiple witnesses had "repeatedly refused to cooperate with the investigation and have rejected subpoenas."[1] Despite that refusal, he said, the investigation had "uncovered alarming details that demand further scrutiny."[1]

## Allegations cited

Armstrong's statement summarized the financial allegations underpinning the inquiry: that the Biden family and associates had received more than $24 million from foreign nationals; that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] received $200,000 from his brother [[james-biden|James Biden]] on the same day James received a $200,000 loan from a failing rural hospital operator; and that Joe Biden also received $40,000 in laundered Chinese money from his brother and sister-in-law.[1] He concluded that the Biden family "sold influence around the world using Joe Biden's name as the product," that "an investigation in any jurisdiction around the country would move forward if it had these facts," and that a formal inquiry vote would put the House "in the best position to prevail in court and uncover the truth."[1]

Badlands Media commentary was skeptical that the effort would culminate in removal, arguing that more than 100 "weak, capitulating" Republicans routinely vote as the political establishment dictates, and speculating instead that Biden would be displaced as the 2024 nominee by other means.[1]
