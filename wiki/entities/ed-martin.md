# Ed Martin

Ed Martin is a Justice Department figure in the second [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] who became the subject of an intense narrative episode in early February 2026.[1] Rumors circulated that Martin had been stripped of his authorities, demoted, and sidelined, prompting what Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described as a full meltdown across the MAGA movement.[1]

Ashe urged caution, noting that the same accounts amplifying the story are known for what she calls "gayotic narrative deployments" recurring since the second Trump administration began.[1] She also observed that Martin himself appeared unbothered, posting memes about lawfare and presidential handshakes while online commentators cast him as a victim and cited the episode as proof of presidential incompetence.[1] The "Martin madness" ran in parallel with the concurrent controversy over [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]]'s election-integrity role, and Ashe dismissed both as poorly executed information operations: "We need better psyops."[1]

## On the bar associations

In June 2026 Martin argued that the legal profession's governing bodies were destroying themselves, declaring: "The Bar Associations are ending themselves. Soon, they will be like the Guilds of Florence."[2] The reference was to the Florentine guilds, abolished by decree in 1770 after the people rose up against them.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] endorsed the framing, writing that professional associations are "entirely unaccountable to anyone or anything" while enjoying enormous powers to affect change — a shorthand for what she calls the expert class.[2] She argued this is nowhere more apparent than in the bar associations, citing the treatment of Jeff Clark, John Eastman, Rachel Alexander and Brian Blehm, and contended that in practice the bodies act like unions when attorney or law firm conduct confirms their political biases.[2] She invoked [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]]'s Justice Department letter stating that while the [[american-bar-association|ABA]] "is free to comment on judicial nominations along with other activist organizations, there is no justification for treating the ABA differently from such other activist organizations and the Department of Justice will not do so."[2] Martin's remarks came in the context of an ethics complaint filed against Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] after the dismissal of the case against [[kilmar-abrego-garcia|Kilmar Abrego Garcia]].[2]

## Weaponization Working Group role

Commenting on reporting that seizures of phone records had become a pattern at the center of a "grand conspiracy" weaponization case, [[canncon|CannCon]] wrote that Martin had been deemed qualified for "any other district" but not the District of Columbia, and said "I think we all know why Martin couldn't be the USA of DC."[3] He argued the outcome backfired on Martin's opponents: describing him as "a pitbull," CannCon said Martin went from having to take on an elephant as an assistant U.S. attorney to a lapdog as head of the Weaponization Group, and called for giving him the resources needed to clean the matter up.[3] The underlying reporting concerned a Trump [[justice-department|Justice Department]] probe, opened quietly in the summer of 2025, treating a decade of political "weaponization" of law enforcement and intelligence agencies as an ongoing criminal conspiracy — including the Trump-Russia collusion narrative and special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s subpoenas for phone records of GOP members of Congress.[3]


## Collapse of the D.C. U.S. attorney nomination (May 2025)

Republican Senator [[thom-tillis|Thom Tillis]] said in May 2025 that he had told the [[white-house|White House]] he would oppose Martin's nomination to be the top federal prosecutor for the District of Columbia, a potentially fatal blow to his already dicey confirmation chances.[4] Tillis, who spoke to reporters after meeting with Martin the previous night, had previously signaled concerns over Martin's comments about the January 6, 2021 Capitol attack and his work defending participants in the riot, saying "I have no tolerance for anybody that entered the building on Jan. 6, and that's probably where most of the friction was."[4]

Senate Majority Leader [[john-thune|John Thune]] told reporters that Martin was now unlikely to emerge from the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]], and Illinois Senator [[dick-durbin|Dick Durbin]], the panel's top Democrat, said that "in practical terms" the nomination was dead.[4] Badlands contributor The AlphaWarrior Show urged readers to make calls and apply political pressure in response.[4]


## Ethics probe disclosure (May 2025)

On his last day as interim U.S. attorney for the District of Columbia before moving to the [[justice-department|Justice Department]], Martin disclosed in an office-wide goodbye email that he was under investigation by the [[office-of-disciplinary-counsel|Office of Disciplinary Counsel]], the ethics office that handles attorney discipline in Washington.[5] He alerted the office's roughly 350 attorneys and 400 staff members to the matter while claiming that his confidentiality had been violated, alleging the ethics office had inappropriately notified a unit of his own U.S. attorney's office of the probe.[5] Martin identified the case number, 2025-D047, but not the specific allegations against him.[5]

Democratic lawmakers and outside groups, in complaints lodged with disciplinary chief [[hamilton-p-fox|Hamilton P. "Phil" Fox]], accused Martin of abusing his power and sought suspension of his law license or other penalties, alleging he had used the threat of prosecution to intimidate and chill the speech of perceived adversaries — lawmakers, protesters, journalists, medical journals and others he said opposed the agendas of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] or adviser [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] — even where they had committed no crime but "acted simply unethically."[5]

[[canncon|CannCon]] wrote that he had been a big fan of Martin's nomination and would welcome the chance to interview him, said he would "forever speak out against Sen. [[thom-tillis|Thom Tillis]] for punishing a lawyer for providing Sixth Amendment-protected Right to Counsel," and noted that Fox was the same official who had gone after [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]] and a slew of other lawyers who brought elections cases.[5]

## On-air spitting incident (May 2025)

In May 2025, a woman was arrested and accused of spitting in Martin's face during a live television interview, an attack she allegedly bragged about afterward in taunting social media posts.[6] Investigators identified [[emily-gabriella-sommer|Emily Gabriella Sommer]] after locating an X account using the name "lefttits" that replied to Martin's posts, "That was me that spit in your face today," and followed up with further mocking comments.[6] The [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] charged her with one count of assaulting, resisting, or impeding a government official.[6]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that there is "something so vile and animalistic about spitting," emphasized that spitting constitutes assault, and criticized the accused as a privileged woman who publicly boasted about attacking Martin.[6]

## Biden pardon investigation (May 2025)

As newly appointed Pardon Attorney, Martin confirmed in May 2025 that pardons issued by former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] at the end of his term were under investigation.[7] "The integrity of the American Pardon system requires that we examine the Biden pardons and who did what," Martin said, adding, "We will get to the bottom of it. Count on us."[7] The pardons at issue went to figures including Biden's son [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] and members of the January 6 Committee such as Liz Cheney.[7]

A prominent America First voice, Martin had initially served President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as Interim U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia but could not continue after Senator Thom Tillis (R-NC) blocked his confirmation; Trump instead appointed him Pardon Attorney and leader of a Weaponization Working Group in the Department of Justice.[7] Martin's confirmation came as House Oversight Committee Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] (R-KY) said investigators had found that not only the pardons but many of Biden's executive orders being used by courts to "Trump-proof" policy "were all signed with the autopen."[7] See [[autopen-scandal|autopen scandal]].[7]
