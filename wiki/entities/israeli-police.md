# Israeli Police

The Israel Police is the national civil law-enforcement body of [[israel|Israel]], responsible among other duties for security and access control in Jerusalem's Old City and at its contested religious sites.[1]

## Palm Sunday 2026 access denial

On March 29, 2026, Israeli police blocked senior Catholic leaders — including Latin Patriarch [[pierbattista-pizzaballa|Pierbattista Pizzaballa]] — from entering [[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre|the Church of the Holy Sepulchre]] to celebrate a private [[palm-sunday|Palm Sunday]] Mass.[1] Israeli authorities justified the decision on security grounds tied to the conflict with [[iran|Iran]], citing missile threats, limited shelter availability and restricted emergency access inside the Old City.[1] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said holy sites of all three faiths had been "repeatedly targeted" by Iranian missile strikes, that fragments had landed meters from the church, and that there was "no malicious intent whatsoever" in the temporary request that worshippers of all faiths avoid Old City holy sites.[1]

Badlands writers noted that in prior years — including the COVID-era closures of 2020 and 2021 and the crowd-control regimes of 2022 through 2024 — police restrictions had constrained pilgrim access but had never denied clergy themselves entry to hold Mass.[1] The 2026 denial was therefore treated as an unprecedented escalation.[1]
