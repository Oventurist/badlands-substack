# Los Angeles Times

The Los Angeles Times is a major American daily newspaper, cited by Badlands Media as one of the clearest illustrations of the financial collapse of the legacy press.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## 2024 layoffs

In January 2024 the paper laid off 25% of what the article calls an "already-decimated newsroom" in a single day — 115 employees — only months after cutting 13% of its workforce.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) According to [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]], the round wiped out the paper's entire Washington DC bureau in an election year, eliminated essentially all of its sports coverage, killed the entire tech and business section, and removed breaking-news writers and social media editors.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Significance in the Badlands account

The Times is grouped with [[time-magazine|Time Magazine]], NBC News, [[sports-illustrated|Sports Illustrated]], National Geographic and Business Insider as widely recognized organizations that had either suffered significant workforce reductions or been pushed to the verge of extinction within the space of a few weeks.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Badlands presents this cluster of failures as evidence that the [[centralized-media|centralized media]] model is terminal, and that the vacuum is being filled by [[citizen-journalism|citizen journalists]] in the [[decentralized-media|decentralized media]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## January 2024 layoffs

In the week of January 22, 2024, the Los Angeles Times laid off 120 employees, roughly 20% of its newsroom, an event that became the proximate trigger for [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]]'s widely circulated claim that "the entire journalism industry is basically in a free-fall."[2] The cuts were reported alongside layoffs at [[time-magazine|Time Magazine]] and [[business-insider|Business Insider]] and asset sales at [[buzzfeed|BuzzFeed]] and [[vice-media|Vice]].[2]

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] used the occasion to argue that what was being lost was not journalism but narrative management, citing as representative the paper's headline "How white and affluent drivers are polluting the air breathed by L.A.'s people of color."[2]

## 2024 presidential non-endorsement

On Wednesday, October 23, 2024, the Los Angeles Times announced that it would not endorse either candidate in the 2024 presidential election, a decision that surprised observers who had assumed the paper would back [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[3] Editorials editor [[mariel-garza|Mariel Garza]] resigned in protest, telling the [[columbia-journalism-review|Columbia Journalism Review]] that "I want to make it clear that I am not OK with us being silent. In dangerous times, honest people need to stand up."[3] Garza said the editorial board had planned to endorse Harris before billionaire owner [[patrick-soon-shiong|Patrick Soon-Shiong]] decided against any endorsement, and the paper gave readers no explanation for the reversal.[3]

The owner's daughter, [[nika-soon-shiong|Nika Soon-Shiong]], supplied the missing rationale in a social media thread reported by the [[hollywood-reporter|Hollywood Reporter]], attributing the decision to opposition to Harris's position on the war in Gaza and invoking her father's experience as an emergency surgeon at Baragwanath Hospital in Soweto under apartheid: "For my family, Apartheid is not a vague concept."[3] She added that the choice was "not a vote for Donald Trump" but "a refusal to ENDORSE a candidate that is overseeing a war on children."[3] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that this substantive reason went unmentioned by liberal critics because it conflicted with the narrative that the [[2024-newspaper-non-endorsements|non-endorsements]] were driven by fear of a second [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] presidency.[3]
