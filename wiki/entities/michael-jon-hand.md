# Michael Jon Hand

Michael Jon Hand was the American co-founder of the [[nugan-hand-bank]] and, for thirty-five years, one of the world's more successful fugitives.[1] Born in New York City, he served as a Green Beret in Vietnam and then as a CIA operative in Laos before moving to Australia, where in 1973 he and [[frank-nugan]] established the bank with $80.[1]

Hand fled Australia in 1980, seven years after the bank's founding, after Nugan was found dead inside his Mercedes-Benz with a .30-calibre rifle positioned as if in suicide.[1] During the inquest into Nugan's death Hand testified that the bank was insolvent, owing investors — large and small — some $50 million.[1] He then escaped the country in disguise using a phony passport; according to a declassified document he surfaced in Central America a few years later, helping to fight the Sandinistas in Nicaragua.[1]

ProPublica later reported that Hand had been tracked to a small town in Idaho, where he had been living under the alias Michael Jon Fuller and running a business called G.M.I. Manufacturing, which makes tactical weapons for US Special Forces, special operations groups and hunters.[1] Joe Lange notes that despite the alias he continued using the same Social Security number — the thread that eventually led investigators to him — and argues that this carelessness reflects his membership in a protected class of "untouchables," while his partner Nugan proved expendable.[1]

Lange frames Hand's biography as the connective tissue of the story: a CIA operative from the [[secret-war-in-laos]], where the agency was running a massive opium operation, who then founded a complicit bank to launder the proceeds.[1] The 1982 New York Times account noted that Hand and fellow bank figure [[bernie-houghton]] both had backgrounds in American intelligence before arriving in Sydney in 1967.[1]
