# Edward Mandell House

Edward Mandell House, generally known as Colonel House, was an adviser to President [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]]. Badlands Media reporting characterizes him as a "handler" assigned to Wilson by international bankers in order to secure passage of the [[federal-reserve-act-of-1913|Federal Reserve Act]].[1]

## Role in the Federal Reserve Act

According to the Badlands account, the Federal Reserve Act faced substantial and well-informed opposition when proposed, because it was widely understood that the legislation was intended to benefit international bankers at the expense of the public.[1] To overcome that opposition, the article states, the bankers placed House beside Wilson in the guise of an adviser, where he exerted what the author calls a hypnotic influence over the president — an outcome Wilson is said to have bitterly regretted.[1]

## The McFadden citation

The article's principal documentary support is a passage from Congressman [[louis-t-mcfadden|Louis T. McFadden]] entered in the Congressional Record at pages 12596–12693 on June 10, 1932.[1] McFadden recounts that on December 23, 1913, the night the Federal Reserve bill became law, House wrote to "his hidden master in Wall Street" congratulating him on the "silent but no doubt effective work" done in the interest of currency legislation, adding that the bill was "good enough to start with" and could be perfected by experience in due time.[1]

McFadden characterized the letter as striking evidence of how predatory interests sought to control the government by surrounding the executive with the personality and influence of what he called a financial Judas, and argued that Congress would not have passed the Act without pressure from the president.[1] By House's own report, McFadden contended, the Act passed because Wilson stood firm under the guidance of New York usurers acting through their hireling.[1] McFadden further alleged that shortly after the law took effect the German international banking house [[kuhn-loeb-and-co|Kuhn, Loeb & Co.]] sent one of its partners to run the new system.[1]

## Sleepy Hollow Club membership

House, described as President Wilson's handler, was a leading member of the [[sleepy-hollow-club|Sleepy Hollow Club]] alongside Senator [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson Aldrich]], both of whom [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] credits with a key role in establishing the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] and the income tax in 1913.[2] House was also the subject of [[george-sylvester-viereck|George Sylvester Viereck]]'s 1932 bestseller The Strangest Friendship in History: Woodrow Wilson and Colonel House, which Ehret describes as possibly the first exposé of the "Jewish bankers conspiracy" behind the Federal Reserve and as the template for ninety years of American conspiracy research.[2]
