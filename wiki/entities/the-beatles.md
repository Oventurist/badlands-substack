# The Beatles

The Beatles were the British rock band whose emergence in the early 1960s reshaped popular music worldwide. In Badlands Media commentary they appear chiefly as an alleged product of intelligence-directed culture creation rather than as a musical subject.[1]

## The manufactured-band claim

In his September 17, 2024 essay "The Media is the Enemy of the People," [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] wrote that "God created [[elvis-presley|Elvis Presley]]. The [[mi6|MI6]]/[[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] created the Beatles and [[the-rolling-stones|Rolling Stones]]."[1] Anticipating that the statement would ruffle feathers, Carlson grounded it in a theological premise — that only God can create and the Devil can only imitate — and characterized the Beatles as an imitation of Elvis.[1] He cited John Lennon's remark, "without Elvis, there would be no Beatles," suggesting Lennon might have added that without MI6 there would be no Beatles either.[1]

Carlson contrasted the two acts on religious grounds, noting that Presley spent his evenings singing Gospel music into the late hours while he could not recall a Beatle ever mentioning the Judeo-Christian God professed by the majority of Americans.[1]

## Relationship to later manufactured acts

The band functions in Carlson's argument as the template for subsequent engineered phenomena. He called [[taylor-swift|Taylor Swift]] "Gen Z's Beatles" — not a claim about comparable music, but about acts created in a similar fashion, for similar reasons, by similar people.[1] The essay situates all of this within a broader thesis that popular culture is funded and directed as an instrument of population control.[1]
