# Kari Lake

Kari Lake is a former Phoenix television news anchor who ran as the Republican candidate for Governor of Arizona in the [[2022-arizona-general-election|2022 general election]] and became a leading voice contesting the conduct of that election in [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

Badlands Media contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] had published an article on November 5, 2022 titled "Why Kari Lake Will Lose in Arizona," warning that the outcome would require flipping the entire Republican down-ballot ticket for the governor's race result to appear believable.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## Post-election posture

In the weeks after the vote, Lake's campaign and its volunteers pressed the county for records; the Kari Lake War Room account publicized a dismissive reply from [[tom-liddy|Tom Liddy]], civil division chief of the [[maricopa-county-attorneys-office|Maricopa County Attorney's Office]], to one such volunteer, posting on November 18, 2022 that "We've asked Maricopa County for answers. This is what we get in return."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

On November 21, 2022, Lake released a message to "the People of Arizona & the United States" stating that "Attorneys are working diligently to gather information. Whistleblowers are coming forward, and the curtain is being lifted. Whether done accidentally or intentionally, it is clear that this election was a debacle that destroyed any trust in our elections … I will continue fighting until we restore confidence and faith in our elections."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Gillespie endorsed the statement directly: "I believe you, Kari. And I'm with you."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Attorneys working on election-integrity litigation in the state at that time included [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## The December 2022 election contest

After Secretary of State [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]] certified the general election on Monday, December 5, 2022, Arizona law gave candidates five days to contest the result. Lake used that window, filing a 70-page lawsuit on the afternoon of Friday, December 9, 2022 alleging election fraud in the midterm.[2] She was the sole contestant and plaintiff; the defendants were Hobbs personally and in her official capacity, County Recorder [[stephen-richer|Stephen Richer]], the five members of the [[maricopa-county-board-of-supervisors|Maricopa County Board of Supervisors]], elections director [[scott-jarrett|Scott Jarrett]], and the Board itself.[2]

The [[kari-lake-election-contest-2022|contest]] argued that the number of illegal votes far exceeded the 17,117-vote margin, that printer and tabulator failures at 59 percent of Maricopa County's 223 vote centers suppressed Lake's Election Day vote by between 15,603 and 29,257 ballots, that more than 300,000 mail-in ballots lacked any chain of custody, and that signature verification had collapsed.[2] [[liz-harrington|Liz Harrington]] publicized the filing in a detailed Twitter thread the same night, which [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] reproduced for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]].[2] The complaint concluded that maladministration and illegal votes caused Arizona wrongfully to name Hobbs the winner of the governor's race.[2]

Lake's suit was one of three filed by Republican candidates that Friday, alongside those of [[abe-hamadeh|Abe Hamadeh]] and of [[mark-finchem|Mark Finchem]] with [[jeff-zink|Jeff Zink]]; Gillespie observed that Finchem could only succeed to the line of succession she envisioned for him if Lake won her contest and was sworn in as governor.[2]

## Mail-in ballot signature lawsuit (2023)

Nearly a year after the November 2022 midterm election, Lake pursued a lawsuit in Arizona seeking access to mail-in ballot affidavit signatures from [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]].[3] In late August 2023 she announced on X that "we are scheduled for a 2-day trial set for September 21 & 25th," adding that she would "never stop fighting for Honest & Transparent Elections."[3] In a statement to [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] she called the court order "a huge victory for election transparency," saying "we're moving forward."[3]

Superior Court Judge [[john-r-hannah-jr|John R. Hannah Jr.]] wrote on Aug. 24, 2023 that a two-day trial would begin Sept. 21 at 9 a.m., with exhibits due by Sept. 14.[3] Earlier in the year a Maricopa County Superior Court judge had ruled that Lake's request to access affidavit envelopes should not be dismissed; Hannah wrote in June that county recorders generally include ballot affidavit envelopes in voter registration records and that the court is "not required to defer to the elections officials in how they have historically interpreted" the law.[3] He rejected Maricopa County lawyers' arguments that the signatures were part of the confidential voter registration record.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that many had assumed the system was too corrupt for the case ever to get a fair hearing, praised Lake and her team for their "tremendous follow-through" in seeking to prove election fraud, and speculated about the Deep State pressure she and her lawyers were likely enduring.[3]

## Commentary on the 2022 Arizona race

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote in May 2023 that establishment interests had strong motives to prevent Lake from becoming governor, describing her as an "overtly America first" candidate and arguing that Arizona's 2022 midterm was "probably the most critical for them to win" because of the state's emerging semiconductor industry and its role as a corridor for illegal immigration and trafficking.[4]

### On third-party candidacies and 2024 election integrity

In comments on "The Clay Travis & Buck Sexton Show" broadcast 9 September 2023, Lake argued that a third-party presidential run by [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] would make it harder for Democrats to "cheat" in the 2024 election.[4] Her reasoning was that with three parties competing in any given race — whether the Arizona Senate seat or the presidency — operators could not predict "what's going to be on the inside of the ballot," since a registered Democrat might cast a ballot for an independent.[4] She added that election-integrity litigation would supplement those dynamics: "we're going to work through the court system to also make it harder for them to cheat."[4] Badlands Media commentary emphasised the significance of the messenger as much as the message, describing Lake as widely seen as a future president and one of the few figures in American politics consistently backed by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and reading her remarks as evidence that the death of the two-party system could mark "the rebirth of the Republic."[4]

## Dismissal of Lake v. Hobbs and the May 2023 press conference

Judge Thompson dismissed [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]] on the evening of Monday, May 22, 2023.[5] At trial Lake attorney [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]] had told the court that "11 of the signature verification workers approved 170k signatures at a rate of less than 0 and 2.99 seconds with a 99.97% approval rating," and in closing argument said roughly 274,000 ballot signatures were compared and verified in under three seconds each.[5] [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] summarized the ruling as a finding that "anything goes" and that "laws are suggestions."[5]

Lake held a press conference the following day at which she made two announcements: a new get-out-the-vote effort described as "chasing ballots," which [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted leftists on social media were already misconstruing as an admission that Lake was chasing fraudulent ballots — characterized as disinformation — and her intention to appeal further, with her team exploring multiple undisclosed paths.[5]

The event became better known for the conduct of the press corps. Far-left 12 News reporter [[brahm-resnick|Brahm Resnick]] asked what The Gateway Pundit called a nasty question about Lake's attorneys; Lake responded by telling the crowd Resnick had been wrong on every major story and had wanted masks on kids, prompting him to shout "You make up sh*t!" and drawing loud boos from the audience.[5] Ashe in America wrote that reporters yelling profanities at their subject is bad form, noted a separate moment when a journalist called The Gateway Pundit's Jordan Conradsen Lake's "PR guy," and observed that Lake's ability to trigger the press is second only to that of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], concluding that "legacy media is dead. We are the news now."[5]

## Day two of the 2023 election challenge trial

On Thursday, May 18, 2023, Lake's legal team spent the second day of [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]] using [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]]'s own data to argue that [[signature-verification|signature verification]] had not been carried out in accordance with Arizona law.[6] Testimony indicated that 779,330 signatures were compared in under six seconds each, 512,597 in under four seconds, and 321,495 in under three seconds, with faster review speeds correlating to higher approval rates.[6] Plaintiffs' expert [[erich-speckin|Erich Speckin]] testified that 45,670 signatures approved by two reviewers could not conceivably have been examined by a human.[6]

After Lake's team rested, the defense moved unsuccessfully for summary judgment and then called a single witness, [[ray-valenzuela|Ray Valenzuela]], whom Lake's attorneys were set to cross-examine the following day.[6] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] carried live coverage of the proceedings throughout, with running commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]].[6]

## Alleged bribery attempt over the 2024 Senate race

In January 2024 DailyMail.com published an audio recording that it said captured [[jeff-dewit|Jeff DeWit]], chair of the [[arizona-republican-party|Arizona Republican Party]], attempting to bribe Lake into not running in the state's U.S. Senate race.[7] In the conversation, recorded at the start of March of the previous year, DeWit asked Lake — then 54 and described as a populist firebrand, a close ally of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and one of the most public faces of the MAGA movement — to name her price to stay out of politics for two years.[7] "There are very powerful people who want to keep you out," DeWit told her, adding that he believed Trump would lose and that it was time to make way for someone else.[7] Lake was at the time frequently discussed as a potential 2024 vice-presidential pick.[7]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that the episode illustrated how openly the organized-crime dimensions of the [[deep-state|Deep State]] were being exposed to the wider [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]], and that the striking feature of such disclosures was how "cartoonish" they had become.[7]

## USAGM testimony, June 2025

As a senior adviser to President Donald Trump, Lake told the House Foreign Affairs Committee in June 2025 that the U.S. Agency for Global Media, which oversees Voice of America, is "rotten to the core" and should be reshaped to align with the administration's 'America First' agenda.[8] Her testimony came the same day Trump publicly urged Republicans to back his campaign to dismantle VOA as partisan and wasteful.[8]

## 2024 Arizona Republican Senate primary

Lake won Arizona's Republican Senate primary on Tuesday, July 30, 2024, as projected by the Associated Press.[9] She clinched the nomination after defeating Pinal County Sheriff Mark Lamb, another Trump supporter, and neuroscientist Elizabeth Reye.[9] The victory set up a general-election contest against Democratic Rep. [[ruben-gallego|Ruben Gallego]], who ran unopposed in his primary, in the race to replace Sen. [[kyrsten-sinema|Kyrsten Sinema]] (I-Ariz.) — a contest reported as potentially decisive for control of the U.S. Senate.[9]

Lake, an ally of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] who echoed his claims about the 2020 election being stolen, had outraised Gallego and attacked him on crime and immigration.[9] Arizona was described as a critical battleground state in the 2024 presidential race whose Senate outcome could tilt the balance of power in November.[9]

## DeWit resignation (January 2024)

On January 24, 2024, [[arizona-republican-party|Arizona GOP]] chairman [[jeff-dewit|Jeff DeWit]] resigned a day after Lake's camp released the leaked audio in which he appeared to offer her money to exit the 2024 Senate race.[10] DeWit said he had received "an ultimatum from Lake's team: resign today or face the release of a new, more damaging recording," and that he was resigning "as Lake requested," hoping she would honor a commitment to cease her attacks.[10] The recording dated from March 2023, and the state party had been split over Lake's continued insistence, pressed through repeated lawsuits, that she lost the 2022 governor's race because of election irregularities.[10]

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that the episode vindicated so-called conspiracy theorists, noting that the released call named "powerful people … from back east" desperate to remove Lake from politics entirely.[10] He held that Lake's record of political independence combined with her support for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] made her a force in exposing the [[uniparty|NWO Uniparty]], and questioned why DeWit appeared so unfazed at being exposed — asking whether his landing spot in the business world had been secured for him as payment.[10]

## Second trial on remand (May 2023)

Maricopa County Superior Court Judge [[peter-thompson|Peter Thompson]] reaffirmed a minute entry setting trial dates of May 17, 18 and 19, 2023 for Lake's remanded fraudulent signature verification claim in [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs]].[12] Thompson found the motions of [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]] and [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] to dismiss Count III "not persuasive," but dismissed Count II regarding intentional election day tabulator failures and refused to reconsider it despite newly discovered evidence that Lake's attorneys said showed "clear misconduct and intent" behind the failure of 260 of 446 tabulators — evidence including technical analysis and whistleblower testimony about a secret logic and accuracy test the county had allegedly concealed.[12] Lake's team argued that her voters had turned out three to one on election day and were deliberately disadvantaged.[12] To prevail on the surviving count she was required to show that the county's higher level signature reviewers performed no [[signature-verification|signature verification]] or curing and systematically failed to materially comply with the law, and to "prove a competent mathematical basis to win."[12] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] carried live coverage of the trial from noon EDT on May 17.[12]
