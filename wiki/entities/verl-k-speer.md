# Verl K. Speer

Verl K. Speer is described in Badlands Media reporting as an obscure but brilliant scholar of law whose writing supplied the conceptual foundation for the outlet's series on the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[1]

## Work

Speer is the author of [[pied-pipers-of-babylon|The Pied Pipers of Babylon]], a book available through the Internet Archive that the Badlands author credits as the direct inspiration for the Federal Reserve series.[1] The passage quoted from the book, at page 168, sets out the rule that the word "trust" need not be used to create a trust, and that where the language of an arrangement fairly interpreted means the recipient of property is legally bound to use it for the benefit of others, a trust arises.[1] Speer also describes trusts implied in fact, or resulting trusts, which are inferred from the acts of the parties and surrounding circumstances rather than from express words, summarizing the principle as "actions speak louder than words."[1]

## Influence

The Badlands essay treats Speer's formulation as the most succinct explanation available of how implied trusts function, and builds on it to argue that Americans may occupy trustor, trustee or beneficiary roles with respect to the Federal Reserve system without ever having signed anything so labelled.[1] Speer's framing of [[trust-law|trust law]] as the underlying grammar of legal relationships is used throughout the article as an analytical method for penetrating legal complexity.[1]
