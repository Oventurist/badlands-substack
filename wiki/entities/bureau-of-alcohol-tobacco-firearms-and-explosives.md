# Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives

The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) is a United States federal law enforcement agency responsible for investigating firearms and explosives offenses.[1]

## Akron crackdown (September 2025)

In September 2025, the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Northern District of Ohio announced the unsealing of 17 federal indictments charging more than 30 people in connection with firearms offenses and illegal drug violations, with ten additional defendants facing state charges.[1] The ATF, assisted by numerous federal, state, and local law enforcement agencies, led the investigation and the coordinated arrests, one federal defendant remaining at large at the time of the announcement.[1]

The initiative ran over a 60-day period and focused on Akron's most violent areas.[1] Law enforcement seized 32 pistols, one revolver, six rifles, two suppressors, and one machinegun conversion device, along with 417.6 grams of powdered cocaine, 376 grams of crack cocaine, 94 grams of heroin, 4,663.4 grams of methamphetamine, 443.5 grams of marijuana, and 134.6 grams of fentanyl.[1]

## Akron Crime Gun Intelligence Center

The charges coincided with the ATF's announcement of a new Akron Crime Gun Intelligence Center (CGIC), intended to serve as an intelligence hub and coordination center for local, state, and federal responses to firearms crime.[1] CGICs use forensic science and evidence to support the investigation and prosecution of violent criminals.[1]

## Stabilizing brace rule (2023)

In January 2023 the ATF issued a rule reclassifying pistols fitted with stabilizing braces as short-barreled rifles, a category regulated since the 1930s and treated in law much like a sawed-off shotgun.[2] The agency grounded the rule in the [[gun-control-act-of-1968|Gun Control Act of 1968]] and the [[national-firearms-act-of-1934|National Firearms Act of 1934]], holding that a braced firearm counts as a rifle "if other factors indicate that the weapon is designed, made, and intended to be fired from the shoulder."[2] The rule took effect June 1, 2023, and was one of several firearms actions announced by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in 2021 after a gunman using a brace killed ten people at a Boulder, Colorado grocery store.[2]

The [[us-house-of-representatives|House]] voted 219–210 on June 13, 2023 to repeal the rule under a resolution sponsored by Rep. [[andrew-clyde|Andrew Clyde]].[2] Critics in the independent media, including [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], argued the rule burdened disabled veterans and other lawful users who rely on braces for accuracy and recoil control while doing little to affect the small number of mass shooters it targeted.[2]

## DC patrol surge (August 2025)

ATF agents were among the federal personnel assigned to the seven-day patrol of Washington, D.C. announced by the [[white-house|White House]] on August 7, 2025 and led by the [[united-states-park-police|U.S. Park Police]], alongside the [[fbi|FBI]], the [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]] and divisions of [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]].[3]
