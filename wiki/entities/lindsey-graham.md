# Lindsey Graham

**Lindsey Graham** is a Republican United States Senator from South Carolina, long associated in Badlands commentary with the hawkish, interventionist wing of the party.[1]

## 2026 primary and the Iran reversal

The June 12, 2026 [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] noted that Graham had been "radio silent" on the Iran–Israel conflict for the preceding weeks because he faced a primary election that Tuesday, which he won after receiving an endorsement from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] According to the Brief, once his primary was safely behind him and Trump announced plans to conclude a peace deal with Tehran, "the knives came out."[1]

Badlands reproduced a Graham post implying that Trump was behaving as a chickenhawk comparable to [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], and observed that this line of attack tracked an argument circulating the previous day among commentators the Brief labeled "Mossad Media."[1] The sequencing — endorsement sought, primary won, criticism launched — was presented as evidence that Graham's alignment with Trump was transactional rather than ideological.[1]

## Death and the Saudi nuclear deal

Graham was vehemently opposed to [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] receiving nuclear technology.[2] In July 2026, reporting on the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's decision to send the U.S.–Saudi [[123-agreement|123 Agreement]] to [[congress|Congress]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the timing of the deal — "coming on the heels of his death" — was not lost on him.[2] The remark places Graham among the congressional obstacles whose removal, in the Badlands reading, cleared the path for U.S. participation in a Saudi civil nuclear program.[2]

## Iran deal and remarks on Pakistan (2026)

Graham was identified in May 2026 as one of the figures most heavily invested in the outcome of the [[2026-iran-deal-negotiations|Iran negotiations]], and he publicly attacked Pakistani Defence Minister [[khawaja-asif|Khawaja Asif]] and Pakistanis generally in a post on X.[3] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] contrasted Graham's hostility with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s consistently high praise for Pakistan's leadership, and predicted that most of the GOP would turn on Trump once an Iran deal was formally announced because those members "actually work for the State of [[israel|Israel]], not the American People."[3]

## February 2026 taunt over Iran

In February 2026 Badlands reported Graham calling [[donald-trump|President Trump]] a "wuss" by comparing him to [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] should Trump decline to attack [[iran|Iran]].[4] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the taunt as evidence that Graham and allied figures such as [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] were not earnestly seeking peace but were "bloodthirsty sociopaths fawning for war," and predicted that no war with Iran would occur because Trump had given his word to his Muslim partners to bring stability to the region.[4]

## March 2026 Middle East meetings

In March 2026 Graham met with Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] amid discussions about [[iran|Iran]] and regional security, urging stronger action against Tehran including the possibility of military strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities.[5] The meetings with Netanyahu drew attention during a Fox News segment in which host [[laura-ingraham|Laura Ingraham]] questioned Senator [[ted-cruz|Ted Cruz]] about what she described as "secret meetings" between the South Carolina senator and the Israeli leader; Cruz replied that members of [[congress|Congress]] routinely meet with foreign officials.[5] The episode unfolded while President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was simultaneously pursuing negotiations to limit Iran's nuclear program and authorizing US strikes as part of joint operations.[5]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Graham had misread his audience, noting that the Saudi royal family prefers to conduct diplomacy behind closed doors, so presenting a proposal via Fox News and social media was unlikely to secure Saudi agreement.[5] He characterized the behavior as reckless desperation, "dangerous" and "likely doomed to fail," and dismissed Graham as "a big fat loser."[5] Citing Graham's claim that Trump had been emboldened by his operation against Maduro and Venezuela, the commentary advanced the view that Trump had deliberately baited the neoconservative bloc onto the Forever War narrative before stepping off it, leaving figures such as Graham boasting publicly of collusion with foreign leaders.[5]

## Reaction to the June 2026 Iran peace deal

Graham was among the figures Badlands highlighted as reacting angrily to the announced U.S.–[[iran|Iran]] peace framework in June 2026.[6] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] presented Graham's reaction alongside those of [[ben-shapiro|Ben Shapiro]], [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]], and [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] as evidence that the [[israel-lobby|Israel Lobby]] intended to use its influence over [[congress|Congress]] to block the deal from being finalized — the point at which, he predicted, the GOP would turn on [[donald-trump|President Trump]].[6]

## Iran and Saudi Arabia (2026)

During the January 2026 crisis over possible US strikes on [[iran|Iran]], Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] pointed to Graham threatening both Iran and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] in a single social media post, citing it as evidence of Republican hostility toward governments the commentator argued were being pushed into [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] protocols.[7] He also recalled that in the week before the 2024 US presidential election, as Saudi Arabia announced joint naval exercises with Iran, Graham and other Republicans were calling on Joe Biden and Benjamin Netanyahu to launch missile strikes against Iran.[7] See [[us-precision-strike-planning-against-iran-2026|US precision strike planning against Iran (2026)]].[7]

## Death (July 11, 2026)

Graham died on July 11, 2026, at the age of 71, after more than two decades in the [[united-states-senate|U.S. Senate]]; his office attributed the death to a "brief and sudden illness."[8] Preliminary findings released by his office indicated that he had suffered an aortic dissection resulting from arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease, with final findings pending additional testing.[8] He had represented South Carolina since 2003 and was campaigning for another term at the time of his death.[8] Shortly before dying he had returned from a trip to Ukraine, where he met Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] and toured drone factories, travelling on from the [[nato|NATO]] summit in Turkey the previous week.[8]

## Badlands reaction to the death

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] declined to "dance on Lindsey Graham's grave" while calling the circumstances extremely weird and fascinating, noting first that the wider movement had been expecting an imminent Senate death announcement — but not Graham's — and second that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s public tribute, though succinct and broadly respectful, struck an odd tone for a supposed close friend and ally.[8] He assessed Graham as "as establishment as establishment gets," acknowledged the theories surrounding Graham's questioning of [[brett-kavanaugh|Brett Kavanaugh]] about treason and the death penalty during the Supreme Court confirmation hearings as adding "fifth-gen color," and described Graham as a prime example of [[uniparty|Uniparty]] politics who appeared to moonlight as the de facto point of contact between U.S. proxy states — Ukraine and [[israel|Israel]] chief among them — and the Senate.[8] He closed by extending his trust in Trump's navigation of the operation to no other member of [[congress|Congress]], and wishing Graham rest in peace subject to God's judgment.[8]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] grouped Graham's death with those of former Qatari Emir [[hamad-bin-khalifa-al-thani|Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani]] and actor [[sam-neill|Sam Neill]], and above all with the same-day rumors that Graham's longtime colleague [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]] had also died — calling the pairing of the two most notorious NeoCon Republican senators one of the most bizarre coincidences of recent memory.[8] He reported that Zionist commentators immediately blamed Putin and Russia, accusing them of assassinating Graham in a conspiracy with Iran in what he read as an attempt to merge the Ukraine and Iran wars into a single conflict, while noting that it is [[mossad|Mossad]] that is notorious for undetectable poisons, maintaining laboratories dedicated to developing them and ordering its assassins to observe autopsies.[8] He urged readers not to rush to a conclusion given the limited facts available.[8]

## Posthumous invocation in the Iran talks debate

In February 2026 coverage of the second round of indirect US–[[iran|Iran]] talks in Geneva, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] recirculated footage of Graham speaking from [[israel|Israel]] after being asked whether he was concerned about Iran's public pledge to target US military bases in the region if attacked, quoting his framing that such risk was "a small price to pay to keep Iran at bay."[9] The commentator read the remark as treating the lives of American service members as expendable for the sake of Israeli objectives, called it treason on the premise that Graham was "wholly owned by the State of Israel," and said the life of every US soldier was worth a thousand Lindsey Grahams.[9] He credited [[donald-trump|President Trump]] with keeping such figures in power long enough to get their positions on the record, predicting history would not be kind to Graham and his allies.[9]

As fighting inside [[iran|Iran]] subsided amid [[backchannel-diplomacy|backchannel]] messaging in January 2026, Graham publicly urged stronger action against Tehran, claiming the regime was still "killing protesters at an alarming rate" despite the recent developments.[10] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described him as "scurrying over to Tel Aviv to grovel at the feet of his masters," defying President [[donald-trump|Trump]] and flagrantly contradicting Trump's assertion that the reported hangings were not occurring, and mocked his on-camera demeanor as that of a "dejected man."[10]

## Saudi troop withdrawal framing (July 2026)

Amid reports of a rift between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] over the [[iran|Iran]] war, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that warmongers such as Graham characterised a potential US troop withdrawal from [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] as a punishment for the kingdom.[11] The commentary argued the opposite — that the Iran war had shown the American military presence to be a liability rather than a deterrent, since US bases were specifically targeted while Iran said it had no intention of attacking Saudi Arabia.[11]

## Funeral and the SkyFall drone

Graham's funeral was held in Washington, with [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] in attendance; [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s separate Oval Office meetings with Zelenskyy and [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] on 28 July 2026 were timed to coincide with the service, reflecting Graham's role as a longtime advocate for both countries.[12]

Activist [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] stated that an FPV drone presented to Graham as a birthday gift by the Ukrainian manufacturer [[skyfall|SkyFall]] during his final visit to Kyiv would be deployed on the front lines "to kill Russians."[12] SkyFall's subsequent advertisement featuring Loomer at its factory reminded viewers that Graham had visited that same facility days before his death, a juxtaposition Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called morbid.[12]

## The "hawkish void" after his death

Politico reported in July 2026 that Graham's sudden death had created a perceived "hawkish void" in Republican foreign policy, which [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s [[america-first-movement|America First]] allies viewed as an opportunity to advance a more restrained U.S. role abroad.[13] Long one of the GOP's most influential advocates for assertive engagement, Graham had strongly backed aid to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and [[israel|Israel]], NATO commitments, and measures such as supplying Tomahawk missiles to Ukraine and potential strikes on [[iran|Iran]]; the day before his death he helped broker a bipartisan agreement on new Russian sanctions with the [[white-house|White House]].[13]

His passing, alongside [[mitch-mcconnell|Sen. Mitch McConnell]]'s health-related absence, was seen as further eroding the traditional "McCain wing" of the party that had pushed interventionist policies since [[john-mccain|John McCain]]'s death in 2018.[13] [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] called it a "mortal blow" to the "McCain wing of the 'America Last' party," arguing that remaining hawks such as [[tom-cotton|Sen. Tom Cotton]] lacked Graham's gravitas and political skill.[13] Trump praised Graham as "a great politician and a kind man," adding that Israel and Ukraine had "lost somebody who was very special."[13]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] seized on that phrasing, observing that the President had said out loud what the movement already believed — that Israel and Ukraine were the parties who "really lost" — and expressing hope that these moments would one day be reviewed "with post-Justice eyes."[13] She paired the remark with [[fred-fleitz|Fred Fleitz]]'s account of Graham constantly calling Trump to press "responsible national security policy" and to relay his trips abroad and vast international contacts, dryly noting that this made Graham sound like an "asset" rather than a friend, and remarked that no funeral had yet been announced.[13]

## Calls for action against Iran (January 2026)

Amid the January 2026 crackdown on spreading protests in [[iran|Iran]], Senator Lindsey Graham publicly called for consideration of stronger US action against the Iranian government.[14] His remarks coincided with Iranian accusations that the United States and [[israel|Israel]] were interfering in the country's internal affairs, and with de-escalation discussions between Iran's foreign minister and US envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]].[14]

## Cuba regime-change advocacy after the Maduro capture

## February 2026 pressure over Iran

In early February 2026, as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[iran|Iran]] signaled they were talking and likely to reach a deal, Graham publicly bared his teeth at the president, accusing him of acting like Barack Obama because he had not followed through and started a war.[16] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the outburst as a leading example of NeoCons and Zionists being "MAD" that Trump had again pulled the rug out from under the war they had long been promised.[16]

## 2026 re-election endorsement

On the night of March 26, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] endorsed Graham for a fifth Senate term in 2026 in a Truth Social post, writing that "Senator Lindsey Graham is working incredibly hard for the Great People of South Carolina" and granting him his "Complete and Total Endorsement for Re-Election."[17] The endorsement was widely read as all but locking up the Republican nomination in South Carolina for Graham, who faced rumored primary challenges from Representatives [[nancy-mace|Nancy Mace]] and Ralph Norman.[17] Graham already held the endorsements of Governor Henry McMaster and fellow Senator Tim Scott, and was considered unlikely to lose the general election.[17]

Badlands commentary treated the endorsement as a litmus test of Q literacy, arguing that while non-Q observers were shocked, readers familiar with the material understood that Graham had "rolled" and had since followed orders — citing the Kavanaugh hearings as an example, and reading his occasional public breaks with Trump on issues such as abortion as theater intended to make Trump appear more centrist.[17]

## August 2024 Iran resolution

On July 31, 2024, Graham introduced a Senate resolution asserting that any escalation by [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] against Israel leading to a major confrontation should be viewed as an attack carried out and executed by [[iran|Iran]].[18] The resolution stated that the Senate "asserts that efforts to deter Hezbollah and the Islamic Republic of Iran are most credible when the President keeps all options on the table, including military force," held Iran and Hezbollah responsible for "any adverse impacts on the people of Lebanon" resulting from an attack on Israel, and urged [[congress|Congress]] and the President to use "all diplomatic tools and power projection capabilities" against both.[18] Graham separately introduced an Authorization for Use of Military Force permitting the use of U.S. armed forces against Iran over its development of nuclear weapons.[18]

Badlands commentary responded that "some things never change. Like Lindsey Graham, and his thirst for blood," and advanced the claim that Graham helped found ISIS alongside [[john-mccain|John McCain]].[18] It cited the June 2011 trip that McCain, Graham and Richard Blumenthal took to Libya to meet Abdelhakim Belhaj, leader of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]], roughly four months before [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] was killed.[18] According to that account, the LIFG and its allies subsequently raided Libyan armories and worked with the CIA to traffic weapons to fighters in Syria arrayed against Bashar al-Assad, seeding a conflict that ran through Ukraine and Gaza to the Lebanese border.[18]

## Criticism over Gaza war advocacy (May 2024)

Badlands commentary in May 2024 singled out Graham, alongside [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]], as an emblem of what it called an "Age of Cowards," describing both as elitists who had never seen combat or served in the military yet agitated loudly for war in [[gaza|Gaza]].[19] The writer argued that strong, capable men — and especially combat veterans — do not call for violence, and that it is invariably "petulant cowards" who demand that better men do the dirty work on their behalf.[19] The remarks were made in the context of the [[rafah|Rafah]] operation and President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s threat to withhold offensive weapons from [[israel|Israel]].[19]

## Sanctioning Russia Act (2025)

In June 2025 Graham promoted what he called a "bone crushing" sanctions bill that would impose 500 percent tariffs on any country buying Russian energy, co-sponsored with Senator [[richard-blumenthal|Richard Blumenthal]] and signed by 82 senators.[20] Analysts warned the measure amounted to a hard decoupling of the United States from major economies including China, India and European allies, and Graham proposed a broad carve-out for countries providing aid to Ukraine that would spare the European Union.[20] See [[sanctioning-russia-act|the Sanctioning Russia Act]].[20]

## Senate delegation to Israel (October 2023)

Graham led a bipartisan delegation of five Republicans and five Democrats to Israel in October 2023, telling a press conference in Tel Aviv on Sunday, October 22 that "destroying [[hamas|Hamas]] is non-negotiable" and threatening to open a war front on [[iran|Iran]]'s borders if Tehran moved to broaden the war in Gaza.[21] Asked to define victory, Graham said: "For the terrorist organization to be destroyed, for the people of Israel to have the sense of peace they've lost, for the Palestinian people to have hope they don't have, and for the Arabs and Israelis to end a conflict that would make the world better."[21] He also framed the endgame as "Israel thrives, they survive, Hamas is destroyed, the Palestinian people have a better life."[21]

The delegation had originally planned the trip to promote Israeli-Saudi normalization, and stressed a positive regional vision after the destruction of Hamas.[21] The senators met Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] on Saturday and urged him to provide funds to sustain the Gazan population until Hamas was destroyed, and also met families of hostages taken during the October 7 assault, in which more than 1,400 people were killed and over 200 taken captive.[21]

Badlands contributor Ashe in America described the visit as evidence of a "reboot of the war on terror," with US senators antagonizing Iran while the USS Eisenhower carrier group was re-routed into the Persian Gulf, and mocked Graham as "Lindsey Grambo," writing that he had "never heard of a region in the world where he wasn't keen to send Americans to die."[21] The commentary also questioned the cost of dispatching legislators abroad "to advertise for war" while Americans struggled economically.[21]

## "Blow it off the map" (December 2023)

Amid the [[houthis|Houthi]] attacks on Red Sea shipping, Graham urged the Biden administration to blow parts of [[iran|Iran]] "off the map."[22] The South Carolina Republican said he had told Secretary of Defense [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] not to show "weakness" and to make clear what the US red lines for a retaliatory attack were.[22] "Without Iran there are no Houthis," Graham told Fox News, adding that he had been saying for six months that the United States should hit Iran, which "have oil fields out in the open" and "the Revolutionary Guard headquarters you can see from space."[22] He continued: "If you really want to protect American soldiers, make it real to the ayatollah [that if] you attack a solider through a proxy, we're coming after you."[22]


## MBS, Khashoggi, and the 2024 Atlantic profile

A September 2024 Atlantic essay reported that after the 2018 murder of Washington Post contributor [[jamal-khashoggi|Jamal Khashoggi]], Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] became a voracious student of American politics and spoke frequently with Graham, a close ally of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], conversations said to have informed a fresh Saudi analysis of its interests; the piece added that in Middle Eastern capitals Graham is viewed as a potential secretary of state in a second Trump administration, so his opinions carry weight.[24] Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry ridiculed the notion of a Secretary of State Graham and contrasted the Atlantic's account with Graham's October 2018 posture, when he introduced a Senate resolution holding MBS responsible for Khashoggi's murder — in Badlands' telling, on the strength of anonymous CIA sourcing rather than evidence.[24] Graham said at the time: "Saudi Arabia, if you're listening, there are a lot of good people you can choose. But MBS has tainted your country and tainted yourself."[24]

## Push for ATACMS transfers to Ukraine (2023)

In September 2023 Graham joined Senators [[tom-cotton|Tom Cotton]], [[roger-wicker|Roger Wicker]] and [[susan-collins|Susan Collins]] in a letter urging President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to provide Ukraine with [[atacms|MGM-140 Army Tactical Missile Systems]], arguing that not doing so would "only prolong the war" with Russia.[25] The letter came on the heels of the [[senate-armed-services-committee|Senate Armed Services Committee]]'s unanimous approval for the Department of Defense to send long-range missiles.[25] Badlands commentary characterized the four signatories as neoconservative holdovers of the Bush-era War on Terror who had regained influence through anti-Trump rhetoric and a well-marketed war in Ukraine.[25]

## Criticism of Lloyd Austin (December 2023)

After Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] warned at the Reagan National Defense Forum that Israel risked a "strategic defeat" if it did not reduce civilian casualties in Gaza, Graham was among the Republican hawks who seized on the comments as evidence that the Biden administration's commitment to Israel was weak.[27] He blasted Austin's words as "naïve" and said, "I've just lost all confidence in this guy."[27]


## Hiroshima comparison (May 2024)

On Sunday, May 12, 2024, Graham compared Israel's war against [[hamas|Hamas]] to the U.S. decision to drop atomic bombs on Japan in World War II during an interview on NBC News' "Meet the Press."[28] "When we were faced with destruction as a nation after Pearl Harbor, fighting the Germans and the Japanese, we decided to end the war by the bombing [of] Hiroshima [and] Nagasaki with nuclear weapons," Graham said. "That was the right decision."[28] He added, "Give Israel the bombs they need to end the war. They can't afford to lose."[28] Graham used the analogy multiple times while condemning President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] for threatening to withhold certain weapons from [[israel|Israel]] if it launched a military operation in [[rafah|Rafah]], where over a million civilians were sheltering.[28]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that "Crisis always exposes the weaklings in the room," calling Graham "among the biggest weaklings in DC," but argued that "Ladybug Lindsey isn't the story here" — the real story being Israel's undeclared nuclear program and the history of the [[negev-nuclear-research-center|Negev Nuclear Research Center]], [[david-ben-gurion|David Ben-Gurion]]'s pursuit of the bomb, and the suppression of scrutiny by figures including [[charles-de-gaulle|Charles de Gaulle]] and [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]].[28] He closed by asking whether Israel illegally possesses the very weapons of mass destruction that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] has for decades accused his Muslim neighbours of holding.[28]

## Syria posture after the fall of Assad (December 2024)

In December 2024, following the collapse of the [[bashar-al-assad|Assad]] government and Israel's advance into the Syrian buffer zone, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] contrasted Graham's public statements on Syria with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s instruction not to get involved.[31] The commentary, which referred to the senator as "Lady Lindsay," noted that Graham had in 2018 called Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] a "thug" and demanded his ouster, and alleged that Graham had helped Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] form [[isis|ISIS]] out of the collection of [[cia|CIA]] proxy groups operating across North Africa and the Middle East, with the assistance of [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[31] These are claims advanced in Badlands commentary rather than established findings.[31]


## March 2025 call to replace Zelensky

After the [[2025-oval-office-clash|February 2025 Oval Office clash]] between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], Graham said the Ukrainian leader would have to "fundamentally change or go."[32] Despite having been among the most outspoken congressional supporters of Ukraine, he told reporters he doubted Zelensky could recover in American public opinion: "I can't believe most Americans after what they saw today would want to be partners with Zelensky," while still calling Ukraine "an important ally."[32] He added, "They have fought like tigers. I don't want Putin to win. I want to help Ukraine, but it's hard to help people who seem unable to realize the moment they're in."[32]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded by cataloguing Graham's history of regime-change advocacy — Iraq from 2003, Libya in 2011, Syria from 2011, Iran from 2015, North Korea from 2017, Russia from 2022 including calls for [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s assassination — and adding Ukraine in 2025 as the newest entry.[32] She noted that the pivot was a more complicated script than usual given that Graham had been "wearing Zelensky as a backpack up to the moment of the Oval Office smackdown."[32]

## Pressure on Ukraine's draft age (2024)

Amid the [[ukrainian-desertion-crisis|Ukrainian desertion crisis]] of 2024, in which Ukrainian prosecutors opened 51,000 desertion cases in nine months, Graham was among American politicians pressing Kyiv to lower its conscription age to 18 after Ukraine had already cut it from 27 to 25.[33] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] singled Graham out for scorn over the push, mocking him as "Lady Lindsey" and predicting he would ultimately answer for what the writer called a century of accumulated sins.[33]


## Silver Elephant Gala remarks on Israel (August 2025)

At the 58th annual Silver Elephant Gala, a major Republican fundraiser held in South Carolina over the weekend of August 9–10, 2025, Graham spoke against any weakening of American aid to [[israel|Israel]] and suggested that reducing support would bring divine punishment upon the United States; he shared the speech with a broader audience on social media on Wednesday, August 13.[36] He praised Israel for purportedly abstaining from committing "genocide" in Gaza despite being in full capacity to do so, and said, "Israel is our friend. They are the most reliable friend we have in the Middle East. They are a democracy, surrounded by people who would cut their throats if they could."[36] The remarks followed Israel's announcement of a plan to occupy Gaza City, one of the few areas of the Strip its military does not control, a scheme touted as a roadmap to concluding the war with [[hamas|Hamas]] that drew overwhelmingly negative reaction in the West.[36]
