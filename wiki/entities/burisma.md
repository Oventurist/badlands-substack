# Burisma

Burisma is a Ukrainian energy company that became a central reference point in American political controversy because of the presence of foreign political figures on its board of directors during and after the 2014 Ukrainian political crisis.[1]

## Role in the Biden family controversy

Badlands Media's coverage of the December 2024 pardon of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] emphasized that the clemency grant reached back to the period immediately preceding Hunter Biden's appointment to Burisma's board, a position reported to pay approximately $1 million per year.[1] The Brief characterized this timing as evidence that the pardon was designed to insulate the family's foreign business dealings from scrutiny rather than to correct a narrow prosecutorial injustice.[1]

A widely circulated timeline reproduced in the Brief laid out a sequence of 2014 events: on April 16, then-Vice President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] met [[devon-archer|Devon Archer]] at the White House; on April 21 Biden visited Ukraine and became the public face of U.S. policy there; on April 22 Archer joined Burisma's board; on April 28 British officials seized $23 million from Burisma's owner; and on May 12 Hunter Biden joined the board, with both men reportedly paid millions.[1] The same account asserted that a 2019 investigation found the Obama administration was aware that Hunter Biden's board seat was problematic and that it interfered with U.S. policy toward Ukraine.[1]

The Brief framed these events against the backdrop of the 2013–2014 upheaval that led to the ousting of Ukrainian President [[viktor-yanukovych|Viktor Yanukovych]], which the cited commentary described as a U.S.-orchestrated coup.[1]

## Related allegations

The same commentary linked the Burisma arrangement to a broader pattern of foreign-linked transactions, including approvals said to have allowed a Chinese state-owned company and an investment firm with Chinese ties to acquire Henniges, a U.S. military technology firm, through the Bohai Harvest RST (BHR) vehicle associated with Hunter Biden and Chris Heinz.[1]

### Central to the 2023 impeachment inquiry rationale

Burisma featured directly in House Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s stated rationale for opening an impeachment inquiry into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on 12 September 2023, when he alleged that Biden "used his official office to coordinate with [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s business partners, about Hunter's role in Burisma — the Ukrainian energy company."[2]

## Board payments in the 2023 bank records

Bank records released by [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight]] Republicans in August 2023 detailed $1 million in yearly payments to [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] and [[devon-archer|Devon Archer]] for their positions on Burisma's board of directors.[3] Those payments formed part of a wider accounting of roughly $20 million routed to the Biden family and their partners from foreign actors in Russia, Ukraine and Kazakhstan during [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s vice presidency, much of it, according to the committee, moved through shell companies.[3]

## Italy energy project lobbying (2016)

Records released in 2024, which the Biden administration had withheld for years, indicated that [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] wrote at least one letter to the U.S. ambassador to Italy in 2016 seeking assistance for Burisma in connection with a potentially lucrative energy project in Italy, at a time when he sat on the company's board and his father was vice president.[4] Embassy officials appeared uneasy with the request from the son of the sitting vice president on behalf of a foreign company; a Commerce Department official based at the U.S. Embassy in Rome who was assigned to respond wrote, "I want to be careful about promising too much."[4]

## The December 2015 Dubai board meeting

Testimony expected from [[devon-archer|Devon Archer]] before the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] in July 2023 centred in part on a Burisma board meeting held in Dubai on Friday, December 4, 2015.[5] At the time the company was paying [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] $83,000 a month to serve as a director, with Archer also on the board.[5] After a board dinner at the Burj Al Arab Hotel, Archer and Hunter moved to the Four Seasons Resort Dubai at Jumeirah Beach, where senior Burisma executive [[vadym-pozharskyi|Vadym Pozharskyi]] called to locate them because the company's owner, [[mykola-zlochevsky|Mykola Zlochevsky]], urgently needed to speak with Hunter.[5] The episode was cited as part of the broader question of whether Hunter Biden's Burisma role afforded the company access to his father, then Vice President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[5]

## The 2015 prosecutor firing

A May 2023 Badlands Media deep dive by [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] titled "[[quid-pro-joe|Quid Pro Joe]]" revisited then–Vice President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s December 9, 2015 visit to Ukraine, during which he threatened to withhold $1 billion in loan guarantees unless Kyiv fired the prosecutor investigating Burisma — the company then paying [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] astronomical sums to serve as a "board member."[6] Badlands contrasted that leverage with the speech Biden gave to the Ukrainian Rada on the same visit, in which he declared that "sovereign states have the right to make their own decisions and choose their own alliances," calling the juxtaposition a brazen display of hypocrisy.[6] The piece extended the story with reporting that Hunter Biden had served for two years on a national security advisory board with [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]].[6]

## 2023 informant disclosure

In July 2023 Sen. [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] released an unclassified FBI document in which a confidential human source described an alleged bribery scheme in which Burisma, a Ukrainian gas giant, hired [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] in order to gain access to his father, then–Vice President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[7] Details of the alleged $10 million scheme were made public for the first time that week, with the informant claiming that both Joe and Hunter Biden were involved.[7]

## The 2023 European Commission disclosure

In September 2023 the New York Post published a December 2015 [[european-commission|European Commission]] progress report praising Ukrainian Prosecutor-General [[viktor-shokin|Viktor Shokin]] — the prosecutor investigating Burisma — for anti-corruption progress, dated nine days after then-Vice President Joe Biden demanded his removal.[8] Badlands commentary paired the report with [[devon-archer|Devon Archer]]'s congressional testimony that Burisma had pressured the Bidens to have Shokin fired, and reiterated that the company paid [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] about $83,000 per month nominally for "the brand" but in substance to secure favorable foreign policy decisions from his vice-presidential father.[8]

## The 2015 interagency loan-guarantee memos

Government memos obtained by [[just-the-news|Just the News]] and reported in August 2023 showed that, weeks before then–Vice President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] threatened to withhold a $1 billion U.S. loan guarantee, an Interagency Policy Committee task force of State, Treasury and Justice Department officials had concluded that "Ukraine has made sufficient progress on its reform agenda to justify a third guarantee," in an October 1, 2015 memo.[9] At the time Biden made the December 2015 threat to force the firing of Prosecutor General [[viktor-shokin|Viktor Shokin]], Shokin's office was conducting an increasingly aggressive corruption investigation into Burisma, an energy firm the [[state-department|State Department]] deemed to have engaged in bribery and which employed [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] and paid him millions while his father was vice president.[9] Badlands commentary argued the memos showed Burisma was using Hunter Biden to pressure his father into removing Shokin, that senior U.S. officials knew it and escalated the warning to Ambassador [[marie-yovanovitch|Marie Yovanovitch]], and that the resulting record was suppressed through the first impeachment.[9]

## Smirnov false-statement allegations

Federal prosecutors alleged that former FBI informant [[alexander-smirnov|Alexander Smirnov]] falsely told agents that executives associated with Burisma — where [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] had worked — had paid the Bidens $5 million each, and that a Burisma official said it would take investigators ten years to locate "illicit payments" to President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[13] Smirnov was charged in February 2024 with making false statements and creating a false and fictitious record, pleaded not guilty, and was ordered held until an April 23, 2024 trial.[13]

## The December 2015 pressure campaign as described in Archer's testimony

In his July 31, 2023 transcribed interview with the [[house-committee-on-oversight-and-accountability|House Oversight and Accountability Committee]], [[devon-archer|Devon Archer]] testified that Burisma Holdings pressured [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] in December 2015 to deal with the Ukrainian prosecutor then investigating the firm for corruption, shortly before then-Vice President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] moved to force that prosecutor's removal.[12] Archer said Hunter Biden had been placed on the board in 2014 because the family "brand" carried value at a time when the company faced corruption allegations from the United States, Great Britain and Ukraine's own prosecutor general's office, and that Burisma "would have gone under if not for 'the brand.'"[12] Committee chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] released a statement confirming that Archer identified Joe Biden as the source of most of the brand's value.[12] Badlands commentary noted the company was paying Hunter Biden $83,000 per month and argued that the value purchased was favourable U.S. foreign policy toward the firm.[12]

## Shokin's 2023 remarks

In August 2023 [[viktor-shokin|Viktor Shokin]], the Ukrainian prosecutor fired while investigating Burisma, spoke publicly for the first time since 2019 and told Fox News it was his "firm personal conviction" that the Bidens had been bribed.[14] A 2020 U.S. Senate committee report confirmed that Shokin had an active and ongoing investigation into Burisma and its owner [[mykola-zlochevsky|Mykola Zlochevsky]], who hired [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] to sit on the company's board and who granted Burisma permits to drill for oil and gas in Ukraine while he was serving as Minister of Ecology and Natural Resources.[14] Shokin stated in a 2019 deposition that there were five criminal cases against Zlochevsky, including money laundering, corruption, illegal funds transfers, and profiteering through shell corporations while a sitting minister.[14]
