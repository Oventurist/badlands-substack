# Francis Dashwood

Sir Francis Dashwood, Chancellor of the Exchequer of England, is presented in the Badlands Media essay "In Defense of Benjamin Franklin" as the leading organiser of the eighteenth-century [[hellfire-club|Hell-Fire Club]].[1]

## Medmenham Abbey and the underground rites

Dashwood leased [[medmenham-abbey|Medmenham Abbey]], beneath which lay the caverns where the ceremonies of the [[eleusinian-mysteries|Eleusinian Mysteries]] were performed by leading figures of London's satanic Hellfire Club.[1] The Abbey had formerly housed a Cistercian Order in the thirteenth century and, the article argues, like all Mithraic cults its location was selected on account of the vast caverns below, which had been Mithraic altars built during Roman times.[1]

In 1751 Dashwood restored the decrepit St. Lawrence Church above the caverns, taking as his inspiration the Sol Invictus (Mithraic) Temple recently discovered at Palmyra.[1] He dubbed his occult society "The Order of Knights of St. Francis," sometimes called "The Medmenham Monks," which the article reads as a triple reference to his own name, to the Abbey above the caverns, and to St. Francis of Assisi, whose order is described as a bridge between the Cistercian elite who created the Knights Templar and the later Jesuits.[1]

After redesigning the abbey in the gothic style, Dashwood placed a banner above the entrance reading "Do What thou Wilt" — a motto later adopted by the arch Satanist Aleister Crowley as the foundation of his theology of Thelema.[1]

## Depiction by Hogarth

The satirical painter [[william-hogarth|William Hogarth]] captured the irony of the enterprise in his rendition of Dashwood dressed as St Francis of Assisi, but holding an erotic novel instead of a bible, contemplating a naked woman instead of Jesus, accompanied by a Venetian masque, and with the figure of fellow Hellfire leader Lord Sandwich standing in as a lunar god in place of a halo — an indication of the Dionysian orgiastic ceremonies performed at night by the initiated.[1]
