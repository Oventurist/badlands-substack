# Jeff Zucker

Jeff Zucker is a former president of CNN who, after leaving the network, became a central figure in the protracted effort to acquire the British newspaper [[the-telegraph|The Telegraph]].[1]

## RedBird IMI and The Telegraph

Zucker served as chief executive of RedBird IMI, a vehicle combining the U.S. private investment firm [[redbird-capital-partners|RedBird Capital Partners]] with the United Arab Emirates' [[international-media-investments|International Media Investments]] (IMI).[1] RedBird IMI's 2023 attempt to buy The Telegraph was derailed after the then-governing Conservative Party passed legislation curbing foreign state ownership of British media, IMI being owned primarily by the Emirati royal and UAE vice president [[mansour-bin-zayed-al-nahyan|Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed Al Nahyan]].[1]

In May 2025 a transatlantic consortium led by RedBird Capital Partners agreed to acquire the newspaper, founded in 1855, for £500 million (about $675 million), with IMI reduced to a minority stakeholder contingent on the [[labour-party|Labour Party]] government finalizing rules allowing foreign state investors to hold up to 15 percent of British newspaper publishers.[1] Because Zucker is also an operating partner at RedBird Capital Partners, commentators noted that his influence would survive the restructuring — [[the-national-pulse|The National Pulse]] characterizing the transaction as a UAE-linked Zucker firm selling the paper to a China-linked Zucker firm.[1]
