# China Institute of Atomic Energy

The China Institute of Atomic Energy (CIAE) is the Chinese state nuclear research institution whose scientists produced what was described in May 2025 as the world's first artificial-intelligence system for nuclear arms-control verification.[1]

## The AI nuclear warhead inspector

According to the *South China Morning Post*, Chinese scientists developed an AI system capable of distinguishing real nuclear warheads from decoys, marking the first AI-driven solution for arms-control verification.[1] The technology was disclosed in a peer-reviewed paper published in April 2025 by researchers with the CIAE.[1] Coverage suggested the system could bolster Beijing's position in stalled international disarmament talks while fuelling debate over the role of AI in managing weapons of mass destruction.[1]

The project was built on a verification protocol jointly proposed by Chinese and American scientists more than a decade earlier.[1] It faced three described hurdles: training and testing the AI using sensitive nuclear data, including real warhead specifications; convincing Chinese military leaders that the system would not leak technological secrets; and persuading sceptical nations — particularly the United States — to abandon Cold War-era verification methods.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that on the same day the Chinese state-run coverage appeared, the U.S. [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] posted a message linking AI to the nuclear framing, and argued this confirmed a prediction she had made two years earlier.[1] In her 2023 commentary on the film *Oppenheimer*, she had written that "the primary AI metaphor used by the regime to drive hysteria and push for global governance is the nuclear arms race," and that Christopher Nolan's film served to refresh public memory of world-ending capabilities "at the precise moment the global regime needs the public to remember."[1] The argument holds that the nuclear analogy is deployed to generate fear-driven compliance and to justify global governance of AI development, in the same way earlier arms-control advocacy urged sharing information rather than keeping national secrets.[1]
