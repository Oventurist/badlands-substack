# Muhammad Zia-ul-Haq

General Muhammad Zia-ul-Haq was the president of [[pakistan|Pakistan]] during the [[soviet-afghan-war|Soviet–Afghan War]] and a central partner of the United States intelligence apparatus in that conflict.[1]

## Relationship with the CIA and BCCI

[[agha-hasan-abedi|Agha Hasan Abedi]], the head of the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|Bank of Credit and Commerce International]], was a close adviser to Zia and arranged repeated secret meetings between Zia and [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] Director [[william-casey|William Casey]] in Pakistan. Saudi intelligence chiefs [[kamal-adham|Kamal Adham]] and [[turki-al-faisal|Prince Turki al-Faisal]], both BCCI shareholders, also participated in this circuit of meetings.[1]

Badlands Media describes these arrangements as taking place outside normal government channels as part of the [[shadow-government|shadow government]] that [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] was building across the executive branch during the Reagan years.[1]

## Protection of the heroin trade

According to WhoWhatWhy material quoted in the article, one of the lasting historical consequences of Casey's decision to work with BCCI was that the bank's drug clients in Pakistan and Afghanistan — notably [[gulbuddin-hekmatyar|Gulbuddin Hekmatyar]] — emerged in the 1980s under Zia's protection as dominant figures in an expanded Afghan heroin traffic that continues to afflict the world. Historian [[alfred-mccoy|Alfred McCoy]] is quoted as stating that BCCI played a critical role in facilitating the movement of Pakistani heroin money, which reached $4 billion by 1989 — more than the country's legal exports.[1]

Cash for the Afghan operation moved through Zia's state: bags of dollar bills were flown into Pakistan and handed to Lieutenant General [[akhtar-abdur-rahman|Akhtar Abdur Rahman]], the [[inter-services-intelligence|ISI]] director, who banked them in ISI accounts at the National Bank of Pakistan, BCCI, and the Bank of Oman.[1]
