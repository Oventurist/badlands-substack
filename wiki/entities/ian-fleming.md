# Ian Fleming

Ian Fleming was the British author who created the fictional intelligence officer James Bond. In Badlands Media commentary by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]], Fleming is cited not primarily as a novelist but as an example of British wartime influence operations directed at the United States: Fleming and children's book author [[roald-dahl|Roald Dahl]] are described as having been "sent west" to spy on the United States, promote England, and help draw the Americans into the Second World War.[1]

## The Bond image versus real tradecraft

Carlson uses the Bond archetype as a foil for his argument about how [[british-intelligence|British intelligence]] actually operates. If James Bond were a real British intelligence officer, the article contends, he would be a pudgy, middle-aged, balding man in ordinary clothes driving an ordinary car — someone nobody would notice was in the room.[1] The glamorous Bond persona is treated as a cover story that obscures the genuine British method of going unseen, which the article identifies with [[mi6|MI6]] as the "gold standard of clandestine operators."[1] This ties into the article's broader thesis that the [[british-cabal|British Cabal]] draws attention to others while controlling events from the shadows.[1]
