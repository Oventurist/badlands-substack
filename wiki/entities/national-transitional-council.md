# National Transitional Council

The National Transitional Council (TNC), also rendered as the Libyan Transitional National Council, was the rebel political body that claimed to govern Libya during and after the 2011 uprising against [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]].[1]

## Recognition and funding

[[john-mccain|Senator John McCain]] pressed the United States and its allies to recognize the TNC as Libya's legitimate government, and the group was the organization with which McCain associate Mouaz Moustafa was affiliated.[1] On July 15, 2011 — one day after [[sidney-blumenthal|Sidney Blumenthal]] emailed [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] promoting an [[osprey-global-solutions|Osprey Global Solutions]] contract to supply anti-Gadhafi forces — the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration recognized the TNC as Libya's government, giving it access to $30 billion held in U.S. accounts previously controlled by Gaddafi.[1] The Badlands account argues this release of funds is what allowed [[abdelhakim-belhadj|Abdelhakim Belhadj]] to become a billionaire able to purchase airlines and shipping companies used to move fighters trained in Benghazi to Syria.[1]

## Arming the TNC

A sworn declaration filed in the District Court of Arizona on May 5, 2015 by career CIA officer David Manners stated: "It was then, and remains now, my opinion that the United States did participate, directly or indirectly, in the supply of weapons to the Libyan Transitional National Council."[1] The declaration was made in the prosecution of arms dealer [[marc-turi|Marc Turi]], and supports the claim that weapons flowed to the TNC while [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] led the [[state-department|State Department]] and before the opposition was formally recognized or direct arms supply authorized.[1] During that period the CIA director was [[david-petraeus|David Petraeus]].[1]

Clinton and [[christopher-stevens|Chris Stevens]], then special U.S. representative to Libya, met with TNC leadership in March 2011 while Turi's arms applications were pending and as Obama authorized air attacks on Gaddafi's military.[1]
