# David Brock

David Brock is an American political operative and the founder of [[media-matters|Media Matters for America]].[1] Time magazine has described him as "one of the most influential operatives in the Democratic Party," and his career is characterized by an astounding and confounding personal transformation.[1]

## Conservative period

In his earlier and lesser-known years, Brock wrote op-eds about "campus Marxists," defended Clarence Thomas during the sexual harassment allegations brought by Anita Hill, and instigated the "Troopergate" story, while acting as a harsh critic of both Bill Clinton and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[1]

## Conversion

As the Clinton years drew to a close, Brock abruptly became a steadfast supporter of the Clinton political machine.[1] The turn came while he was writing *The Seduction of Hillary Rodham*, a book expected to be a hit piece in the style of his work on Anita Hill and Bill Clinton but which instead proved largely sympathetic to Mrs. Clinton.[1] In his subsequent book *Blinded by the Right* (2002), Brock claimed he had reached "a turning point," saying he had examined the charges against the Clintons, found no evidence of wrongdoing, and did not wish to make further misleading claims.[1]

Observers across the political spectrum remained skeptical. The conservative-libertarian magazine National Review proposed that because "no liberal source in the world would talk to Brock," he could not gather the material needed for a proper takedown of Hillary Clinton, and further suggested that Brock had been "seduced" while writing the book by the political operative Sidney Blumenthal, a senior Clinton advisor.[1] Journalist [[glenn-greenwald|Glenn Greenwald]] remarked that he did not know what had happened to Brock, but that "overnight he went from one of the most psychotic Hillary haters to someone who treats Hillary Clinton like a deity."[1]

## Media Matters and Correct the Record

A theory circulating among independent journalists holds that Brock founded Media Matters as a vehicle to funnel money into the Democratic establishment and to build an effective media pressure group.[1] The organization proved a powerful instrument for the Clinton operation, fighting for Hillary Clinton's victory in both the 2008 primary against Barack Obama and the 2016 campaign, and attacking anyone even slightly critical of her.[1] Brock also spent millions establishing the super PAC Correct the Record, which the Los Angeles Times reported in May 2016 was spending roughly $1 million to find and confront social media users posting unflattering messages about Clinton — an effort the paper said effectively amounted to paying to increase online trolling.[1]

## Legal exposure

The lawsuit filed by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] against Media Matters, the statement by Rumble's [[chris-pavlovski|Chris Pavlovski]], and the investigation opened by Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] are described as evidence that the boomerang is returning to Brock and his organization.[1]
