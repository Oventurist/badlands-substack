# Sarah Wallace

Sarah Wallace is a Colorado district court judge who presided over the state lawsuit seeking to bar [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the 2024 Colorado primary ballot under Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]].[1] In a ruling issued on Friday, November 17, 2023, Wallace found that Trump "engaged in insurrection" in connection with the events of January 6, 2021, but nonetheless held that he could remain on the state's ballot because the constitutional ban on insurrectionists holding office was not clearly intended to apply to the presidency.[1]

## The November 2023 ruling

Wallace's decision was reported as the latest in a series of defeats for the nationwide effort to end Trump's candidacy through Section 3 of the Fourteenth Amendment, a provision used only a handful of times since the years after the Civil War and originally created to prevent former Confederates from returning to government positions.[1] Because her ruling split the difference — an adverse factual finding paired with a favorable legal outcome — both sides appealed to the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] on Monday night, November 20, 2023.[1]

Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington, filing on behalf of a group of Republican and unaffiliated Colorado voters, argued that Wallace was wrong to rule that it was unclear whether the provision applied to presidents.[1] Trump, for his part, appealed Wallace's insurrection finding itself and questioned whether a state trial judge, rather than Congress, was the proper body to settle the question.[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized Wallace's ruling as one that "rubber stamped the J6 Committee Report and affirmed everything the petitioners said as true," calling it "a horrible ruling for Truth" even though it preserved Trump's ballot access.[1] She noted that under the Colorado election timeline the appeal would move quickly, since the primary ballot had to be finalized by December 29, 2023.[1]

## The 102-page opinion

Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] described Wallace as "the latest jurist to reject the effort to bar former president Donald Trump from the ballot under the novel 14th Amendment theory," a theory he has long criticized as historically and legally unfounded and as arguably the most dangerous to arise in decades.[2] While he judged that Wallace reached the right conclusion, he faulted her for what he saw as fundamental errors in her analysis of the free speech elements of the case.[2] Turley also characterized the underlying suit as a chilling effort by Democratic Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]] to use her office to prevent voters from casting ballots for Trump, one of the leading candidates for the presidency, at a moment when polling showed Trump and Joe Biden in a statistical dead heat in Colorado at 42 percent to 38 percent, within the margin of error.[2]

In her 102-page ruling Wallace declared that "[t]he Court holds there is scant direct evidence regarding whether the presidency is one of the positions subject to disqualification," and concluded that "[a]fter considering the arguments on both sides, the Court is persuaded that 'officers of the United States' did not include the President of the United States. It appears to the Court that for whatever reason the drafters of Section 3 did not intend to include a person who had only taken the Presidential Oath."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who dissected the full ruling the morning after it dropped on a Friday evening, argued that the case "wasn't about President Trump accessing the ballot" but about [[lawfare|lawfare]] and its consequences for ordinary citizens, especially Coloradans.[2] She contended that the first 95 pages affirm "the central narrative, the J6 report, the pseudo-science of far right extremism, thought crimes, speech crimes, and the Fourteenth Amendment being superior to the First," with the ballot-access holding confined to pages 96 through 102 after the court had "rubber stamp[ed] everything the petitioners said as true."[2] Because the outcome was technically a win for Trump, she expected no appeal, though she hoped a motion to intervene by [[treniss-evans|Treniss Evans]] might be granted if the petitioners did appeal, allowing the insurrection narrative to be contested on the full record.[2] She also noted that the case would matter little for Trump himself unless [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] or [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] sought to build upon it.[2]

Ashe further observed that the [[january-6-capitol-security-footage-release|release of January 6 security footage]] a few hours before the Colorado ruling made the timing "curious," since the videos directly undercut Wallace's legal conclusion that January 6 was an insurrection incited by Trump.[2]
