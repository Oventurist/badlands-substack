# John Durham

John Durham is the former US attorney appointed Special Counsel to investigate the origins of the FBI's 2016 counterintelligence investigation into the [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaign.[1]

## Investigation and final report

Durham's probe examined how the FBI's [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] investigation began and how Russia-related intelligence was handled.[1] His final report, released in 2023, criticized aspects of the FBI's handling of intelligence but did not establish a criminal conspiracy involving senior officials.[1]

## Durham-era fallout

Scrutiny of the Russia assessments continued after the report. In May 2026 a newly disclosed witness, [[gavin-wilde|Gavin Wilde]] — a co-author of the January 2017 [[intelligence-community-assessment|Intelligence Community Assessment]] — came forward to congressional investigators, extending the inquiry into the origins and communication of Russian interference findings that Durham had first reviewed.[1]

## Judicial Watch FOIA lawsuit (January 2026)

On January 21, 2026, [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] announced it had filed a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit against the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] seeking fully unredacted records from Durham's probe into the origins of the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]]/[[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate]] investigation.[2] The suit targets previously withheld or missing materials, including [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]]'s 2016 White House briefing notes, the DOJ Inspector General report critiquing the [[fbi|FBI]]'s handling of Crossfire Hurricane, the 14 missing pages of the [[durham-report|Durham Report]] appendix, and all unredacted transcripts or notes from Durham's interviews with key figures including [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], Brennan, [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] and [[john-podesta|John Podesta]].[2]

The request also seeks unredacted records of Durham's referrals to other federal agencies, emails between [[fusion-gps|Fusion GPS]] and major media outlets, the Clinton campaign or the [[democratic-national-committee|DNC]], and communications involving [[british-intelligence|British Intelligence]] head Sir [[richard-dearlove|Richard Dearlove]], U.S. intelligence officials, [[stefan-halper|Stefan Halper]] and [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]].[2]

## June 2023 House Judiciary testimony

On June 21, 2023, Durham testified before the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] about his report, telling members that the [[cia|CIA]] knew [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] had approved a plan to smear then-candidate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with Russia allegations to distract from her classified email scandal.[3] He said the [[fbi|FBI]] had failed to "sufficiently scrutinize information it received" and had not applied the same standards to allegations about the Clinton and Trump campaigns, and that the Bureau "was too willing to accept and use politically funded and uncorroborated opposition research, such as the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]]."[3] Durham added that it was "going to take time to rebuild the public's confidence in the institution."[3]

Under questioning from Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], Durham said former FBI Director [[james-comey|Jim Comey]] had withheld a referral memorandum — containing intelligence from a high-level briefing with CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] on Clinton's plan to tie Trump to Russia — from the agents working [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]].[3] Democratic members pressed Durham on why his investigation had cost more than $6 million and produced fewer indictments than the [[robert-mueller|Mueller]] probe, while Republican [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] unexpectedly attacked him for producing too little.[3] Truth Community commentators were divided over whether Durham was controlled opposition tied to [[bill-barr|Bill Barr]], a well-intentioned prosecutor defeated by a corrupt system, or a participant in a longer plan whose findings would matter later.[3] Full detail is covered at [[durham-house-judiciary-testimony-2023|the hearing page]].[3]


## Elias grand jury connection

Commentary in the April 2023 Badlands News Brief noted that Durham had interviewed Democratic election attorney [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]] and placed him before a grand jury.[4] [[just-human|Just Human]] cited that exposure as the likeliest explanation for the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]]'s abrupt decision to sever ties with Elias, suggesting the party was separating itself before the trouble became public.[4]

## Anticipation of the June 2023 testimony

Ahead of his appearances on Capitol Hill, Durham was scheduled to testify before two committees of the House of Representatives more than a month after releasing the findings of his inquiry into the FBI's Crossfire Hurricane investigation.[5] The [[house-intelligence-committee|House Intelligence Committee]] hearing was set for Tuesday at 3 p.m., with the House Judiciary Committee hearing following Wednesday at 9 a.m.[5] Intelligence Committee Chairman [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]] had said shortly after the report's release that it outlined "serious errors and shortcomings" in the FBI's handling of the investigation.[5]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] previewed the hearings by asking whether Durham would speak openly about his findings or deliver "oral redactions and 'no comments'," whether legislators would press him on his failure to recommend charges, and whether there would ever be accountability for four years of baseless Russia claims that obstructed President Trump's ability to govern.[5] She contrasted the prosecution of [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] for "obstruction of government" with the absence of any constitutional protection for the bureaucratic state to obstruct a duly elected president, arguing that the latter is closer to treason and that the Durham report proves it occurred during Trump's first term.[5] Badlands Media announced it would stream the public-facing testimony with exclusive analysis from [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] and [[just-human|Just Human]].[5]

## New York Times retrospective, January 2023

On January 26, 2023 [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] published a lengthy retrospective titled "How Barr's Quest to Find Flaws in the Russia Inquiry Unraveled," examining the origins and trajectory of Durham's special counsel investigation.[6] Badlands contributor Just Human characterized the piece as a "hit and spin" article built almost entirely on "current and former officials" who spoke anonymously "because of the legal, political and intelligence sensitivities surrounding the topic," supplemented by comments from defense lawyers representing clients Durham's office had interviewed.[6]

He argued the article was structured to program readers into accepting five propositions: that the Trump-Russia collusion investigations had a basis while Durham's did not; that [[bill-barr|Barr]] and Durham were Trump's attack dogs; that Durham had once been fair and respectable before drinking "the Trump Kool-Aid"; that Durham had failed to find anything meaningful; and that the investigation was finished.[6] Just Human contended that anyone who had followed Durham's work closely knew each claim was "180 degrees out of sync," and asked why the Times would reframe the story in January 2023 - suggesting the paper was "trying to get ahead of something. Perhaps something huge."[6]

## New York Times pre-emptive coverage (February 2023)

By mid-February 2023 Durham, leading a multi-year probe of how U.S. intelligence officials conducted the Russia investigation, had still not issued his final report, yet the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] had already declared that he had come up empty.[7] A widely circulated Jan. 26, 2023 Times article by Charlie Savage, Adam Goldman and Katie Benner, based on a monthslong review and interviews with more than a dozen current and former officials, said Durham's team had gone "unsuccessfully down one path after another" and had "failed to find wrongdoing in the origins of the Russia inquiry."[7] Writing for [[realclear-investigations|RealClear Investigations]], [[aaron-mate|Aaron Maté]] argued the Times omitted countervailing information and distorted the available facts, attempting to write off the Durham probe before its findings were released.[7]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] described "Durham panic" in the air "like never before," noting that the Times was not only smearing Attorney General Barr and Durham but running op-eds by former Obama administration Solicitor General [[neal-katyal|Neal Katyal]] urging Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] to "discipline and fire" Durham and to refuse to make his report public.[7] Just Human speculated that the paper's urgency reflected unique awareness of what move Durham might make next.[7]


## Family connection to Joint Task Force Vulcan

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] noted in February 2023 that the director of [[joint-task-force-vulcan|Joint Task Force Vulcan]], the Justice Department task force created in August 2019 to prosecute the leadership of [[ms-13|MS-13]], is [[john-j-durham|John J. Durham]], a son of Special Counsel John H. Durham.[8] The observation surfaced in coverage of a four-count indictment unsealed in Central Islip against thirteen of MS-13's highest-ranking leaders.[8]
