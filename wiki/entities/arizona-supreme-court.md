# Arizona Supreme Court

The Arizona Supreme Court is the highest appellate court in the U.S. state of Arizona. In Badlands Media coverage it appears principally as the tribunal of last resort in the state-level prosecution of allies of [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] over the 2020 alternate-elector slates, a case brought by Arizona Attorney General [[kris-mayes|Kris Mayes]].[1]

## Role in the 2020 electors prosecution

On June 4, 2026 the Arizona Supreme Court rejected an appeal in the 2020 electors case involving Trump allies.[1] The court's refusal left standing a lower-court ruling that had dismissed the original indictment after a judge found that prosecutors failed to present the grand jury with the precise statutory language tied to the alleged conduct.[1] Rather than ending the matter, the decision prompted Mayes to announce that her office would return the case to a grand jury and seek a fresh indictment, effectively restarting the prosecution.[1] The mechanics of the dispute — a procedural defect before the grand jury, an unsuccessful appeal, and a decision to re-charge — made the court a pivotal but largely passive actor in what Badlands writers characterize as an ongoing exercise in weaponized government.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the sequence less as a legal event than as a political self-exposure, arguing that pressing forward after the appellate setback amounted to "a regime puppet outing themselves."[1] In that framing the court's rejection of the appeal simply cleared the way for a prosecutor to double down at a moment when public skepticism of institutional impartiality — and of the certified 2020 result itself — was, in the author's view, higher than ever.[1]
