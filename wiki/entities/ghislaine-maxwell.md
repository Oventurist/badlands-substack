# Ghislaine Maxwell

Ghislaine Maxwell is a longtime associate of [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] who figures in the February 2026 [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] document release.[1]

## Trump's 2006 characterization

In a 2006 phone call to former Palm Beach police chief [[michael-reiter|Michael Reiter]], recounted in a 2019 [[fbi|FBI]] interview disclosed in the release, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] described Maxwell as "evil" and as his "operative."[1]

## Clemency offer

Maxwell's legal team indicated her willingness to testify if granted clemency by President Trump.[1]

## Congressional deposition (February 2026)

Maxwell, serving a federal prison sentence following her conviction on sex-trafficking-related charges, declined to answer questions during a congressional deposition on Monday, February 9, 2026, invoking her Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination.[2] She stated that she would testify only if granted clemency or immunity, and as of press time no agreement had been reached; congressional investigations remained ongoing.[2] Her silence was the proximate cause of the stalled US congressional track of the [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]] matter, even as the parallel fallout in the United Kingdom cost Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] senior aides.[2]


## Family background

Badlands commentary published in April 2026 describes her father [[robert-maxwell|Robert Maxwell]] as having been dispatched by [[mossad|Mossad]] to sell the [[promis-software|PROMIS software]] to entities across the world, including [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]], and as becoming the most prolific money launderer of the late 1980s by moving funds between Western and Soviet banks.[3]

## UBS banking relationship

U.S. Justice Department documents reported in February 2026 show that Swiss banking giant [[ubs|UBS]] maintained a financial relationship with Maxwell beginning in 2014, shortly after [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] cut ties with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[4] UBS opened both personal and business accounts for her and managed up to about $19 million in her assets in the years before her 2020 arrest and 2021 conviction on sex-trafficking charges.[4]

The bank assigned Maxwell two relationship managers and processed significant transactions on her behalf, including a $130,000 transfer on July 22, 2019 — days after Epstein's arrest — to help pay a credit card bill.[4] JPMorgan had previously flagged Maxwell as a "high risk client" because of her Epstein links and closed Epstein's accounts in 2013.[4] In August 2019 UBS received a grand jury subpoena for her records and complied, providing wire transfer information to the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]]; Reuters reported no evidence that the bank or its advisers engaged in wrongdoing.[4]

## "Going down with the ship"

In commentary accompanying the April 2023 leak of [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s private calendar, Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] described Maxwell as Epstein's handler — or boss, depending on who is asked — and said she was "going down with the ship."[5] He argued that her strategy of blaming everything on Epstein was likely exactly what she had been trained to do, and what Epstein himself willingly embraced before his death.[5]

Deschamps placed the pair within a lineage of [[blackmail-operations|blackmail operations]] in which practitioners know how to obtain leverage on anyone and how to absorb blame in order to protect what he called their dynastic, blood-pacted benefactors.[5]

## Reported willingness to testify before Congress (July 2025)

In July 2025 sources told the Daily Mail that Maxwell, then 63 and serving a 20-year sentence on child sex trafficking charges, was willing to speak before [[congress|Congress]] about the [[epstein-files|Epstein files]].[6] She remained the only person imprisoned in connection with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s activities, despite allegations that Epstein controlled a web of underage girls whose victims said they were passed to his wealthy friends and billionaire business associates at his homes, including his private island, Little Saint James.[6] A source said that "despite the rumors, Ghislaine was never offered any kind of plea deal," that no one from the government had ever asked her to share what she knows, and that she would welcome the chance to tell the American public the truth.[6]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] paired the report with [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s autopen clemency admission as twin illustrations of [[state-capture|State Capture]], arguing that the failure to seek Maxwell's testimony pointed to a government usurped by a transnational cartel.[6]

## July 2025 DOJ interview

On July 24, 2025, an attorney representing Maxwell said his client had answered every question the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] asked her without invoking the Fifth Amendment.[7] The meeting took place at the low-security federal prison in Florida where Maxwell is incarcerated, serving a 20-year sentence for child sex trafficking and related offenses.[7] Deputy Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] conducted the meeting, which had been teased two days earlier, and it came as the Trump administration faced backlash for shutting down investigations into [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]] despite lingering questions about a supposed client list.[7] See [[doj-fbi-epstein-memo]].

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] dismissed the framing of the story, noting that it would have been strange for Maxwell to invoke the Fifth Amendment during a meeting she was not compelled to attend in the first place.[7]

## $10 million appeal (February 2023)

In February 2023 Maxwell was reported to be mounting a $10 million appeal against her twenty-year prison sentence, financed from funds secured through a recent divorce settlement.[8] The [[daily-mail|Daily Mail]] reported exclusively, citing an unnamed source close to the family, that Maxwell had settled with her ex-husband Scott Borgerson, who had previously refused to release money from her $20 million trust.[8] She had sought those funds to pay an outstanding legal fee of $878,302 and to hire [[harvey-weinstein|Harvey Weinstein]]'s lawyers to work on her appeal.[8]

After the settlement was reached, the source described the then 60-year-old Maxwell as "ready to fight back," saying "her lawyers are confident that she has a strong case for appeal."[8] She had been sentenced in June 2022 for, as the Justice Department put it, "her role in a scheme to sexual [sic] exploit and abuse multiple minor girls with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] over the course of a decade," and was serving her term at Florida's low-security federal prison [[fci-tallahassee|FCI Tallahassee]].[8] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] welcomed the renewed attention, arguing that "more Maxwell in the news means more exposure of her crimes and [theirs]."[8]

## Supreme Court denial and pardon speculation, October 2025

On Monday, October 6, 2025, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|US Supreme Court]] declined to hear Ghislaine Maxwell's bid to overturn her conviction for helping [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] sexually abuse teenage girls.[9] The same day, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] left the door open to a pardon, saying he would speak to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] about setting Maxwell free.[9] Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[canncon|CannCon]] said he was glad she would not be freed but warned the outcome raised questions about the authority of a US Attorney to make agreements with defendants and about the government's ability to renege on them; he also asked whether the close of Maxwell's litigation would finally bring movement on the [[epstein-files|Epstein Files]].[9]
