# Vision Banc Savings

Vision Banc Savings was the Texas thrift created in March 1986 when Houston developer [[robert-corson|Robert Corson]] bought the Kleberg County Savings and Loan for $6 million and renamed it.[1] Corson reportedly obtained the institution using a letter of recommendation from a judge who in 1988 would head [[george-hw-bush|George Bush]]'s campaign in Harris County.[1]

At the time of purchase the thrift had assets of $70 million; four months later it was insolvent.[1] Journalist Pete Brewton attributed the failure partly to a $20 million loan to help finance a Florida land deal, of which Vision Banc lost $17 million, with [[hill-financial-savings|Hill Financial Savings]] of Pennsylvania putting up a further $80 million and losing $40 million.[1] Brewton described Corson as a known money launderer who transported cash for the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], and said the Pennsylvania thrift and the deal's Miami organizer [[lawrence-freeman|Lawrence Freeman]] both had CIA and organised crime ties.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presents Vision Banc as an emblem of the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan scandal]]: a CIA money launderer buying a $70 million thrift that collapses in four months, in what he calls perhaps the fastest bankruptcy in history.[1]
