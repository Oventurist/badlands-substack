# X.com

X.com was an online bank founded by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] in 1999, before the company's transformation into [[paypal|PayPal]].[1] Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats X.com as the earliest concrete expression of Musk's ambition to build a consumer-facing digital bank capable of competing with the legacy banking industry.[1]

## Concept and launch

Contemporary reporting by CBS Market Watch, quoted at length in Lange's article, described Musk as taking advantage of new reforms in the banking industry and of innovations offered through the internet to develop a financial services concern that married banking with mutual fund management, an effort that took just six months.[1] Besides offering what was billed as the first seamless integration of banking and mutual fund purchases on the internet, Musk sought to make X.com the low-cost leader in the mutual fund industry, offering a no-load, no-cost S&P 500 index fund free of fees of any kind and even paying shareholders a small amount to participate.[1]

In the same interview Musk described the genesis of the venture: after selling his previous company to Compaq in April 1999, he judged that the internet had passed through a stage in which people trusted it for information and a second stage in which they trusted it for purchases, and had reached a "third stage" in which "people are ready to use the internet as their main financial repository."[1]

## Merger and loss of the name

X.com merged with Confinity Inc., which had built a well-regarded online payment system.[1] Musk wanted the combined digital bank to challenge the legacy banks, but other company leaders preferred to concentrate on payments; Musk was removed as CEO and the company was renamed PayPal.[1] Musk has since said that if PayPal had executed his plan it would likely be the most valuable company in the world and would be called "X."[1]

## Revival

Musk later quietly purchased the X.com domain name back from PayPal, which Lange presents alongside Musk's acquisition of [[twitter|Twitter]] and Twitter's payment-processor registration with the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] as signs that the original digital-bank plan is being revived.[1]
