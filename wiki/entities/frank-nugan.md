# Frank Nugan

Frank Nugan was the Australian co-founder of the [[nugan-hand-bank]], described by ProPublica as a wealthy playboy, who with the American CIA operative [[michael-jon-hand]] established the Sydney-based merchant bank in 1973 with $80 in capital.[1]

Nugan manufactured the appearance of solvency at the outset: he wrote the bank a check for $980,000 and then covered it by writing a bank check to himself for the same amount, an elementary accounting fraud through which, as Alfred W. McCoy wrote in "The Politics of Heroin," he could claim the company's paid-up capital was a million dollars.[1]

In 1980 Nugan was found dead inside his Mercedes-Benz, his left hand holding the barrel of a .30-calibre rifle a few inches from his head and his right hand near the trigger. The inquest ruled the death a suicide, a finding many Australians found dubious.[1] Documents found on his body bore the name of former CIA director [[william-colby]], who served as the bank's legal adviser.[1] His death triggered the bank's collapse and multiple Australian government investigations.[1]

Joe Lange contrasts Nugan's fate with that of his partner: Hand was protected by a [[shadow-government]] and remained untouchable, escaping Australia and living for decades under an alias, while Nugan was clearly expendable.[1]
