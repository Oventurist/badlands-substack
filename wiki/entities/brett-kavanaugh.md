# Brett Kavanaugh

Brett Kavanaugh is an Associate Justice of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] who received a rare public apology from Justice [[sonia-sotomayor|Sonia Sotomayor]] in April 2026 after she criticized him in personal terms in an immigration dissent.[1]

## The Sotomayor dissent and apology

The underlying case allowed the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] to proceed with contested immigration enforcement actions.[1] Sotomayor's dissent accused the majority — implicitly including Kavanaugh — of enabling harmful outcomes, and she publicly remarked that the colleague who had described certain detentions as "only temporary stops" was "a man whose parents were professionals" who "probably doesn't really know any person who works by the hour."[1] Sotomayor later apologized for the personal tone while maintaining her legal objections, and Kavanaugh reportedly accepted the apology.[1]

## Montgomery County religious liberty case, 2025

During oral argument on 22 April 2025 in the [[montgomery-county-lgbtq-books-case|Montgomery County LGBTQ books case]], Kavanaugh said he was "surprised" by a dispute arising from his lifelong home of Montgomery County, Maryland.[2] Kavanaugh, his wife Ashley and their two daughters live in Chevy Chase Section Five, a small village within the county; a former U.S. Court of Appeals judge, he was nominated to the Supreme Court by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2018 to replace the retiring Justice Anthony Kennedy.[2] He described the county as a longstanding "beacon" for religious freedom and told the courtroom: "I guess I'm surprised that this is the hill we're going to die on, in terms of not respecting religious liberty."[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read significance into his use of "we," arguing that he was surprised not by American parents generally but by his own community.[2]

## Commentary on his confirmation

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Kavanaugh had endured a confirmation crucible arguably worse than that of Justice Clarence Thomas, and that the uniparty left continued to regard him as a rapist despite an accuser who could not recall the what, when, where, why, or how of the alleged incident while being certain of the who, and who did not report the allegations until decades later during his confirmation hearings.[1] In this account, the widely covered apology story was a non-event amplified across networks while the Court had major merits cases still pending.[1]

## Tennessee gender-care arguments (December 2024)

At the December 4, 2024 oral arguments over Tennessee's ban on gender-affirming care for minors, Kavanaugh — described alongside Chief Justice [[john-roberts]] as one of the Court's two most important votes — repeatedly suggested that such medical questions should be left to legislatures rather than courts.[3] He also raised concerns about the impact any ruling might have on girls' sports, pressing Solicitor General [[elizabeth-prelogar]]: "If you prevail here, what would that mean for women's and girls' sports in particular?"[3] Observers noted the significance of the question coming from Kavanaugh, a [[donald-trump]] nominee who has spoken about coaching basketball for his daughters' teams, one of which attended his 2018 confirmation hearing.[3] See [[united-states-v-skrmetti]].[3]

## Arizona voting law order (2024)

Reporting on the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]]'s August 22, 2024 order concerning Arizona's proof-of-citizenship voting law noted that at least one of Kavanaugh or Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]] joined the four justices who denied the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]]'s other request.[4]
