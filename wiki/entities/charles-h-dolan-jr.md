# Charles H. Dolan Jr.

**Charles H. "Chuck" Dolan** is a longtime Democratic Party operative and public-relations executive identified during Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s prosecution of [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] as one of the sources behind material in the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]].[1]

## Background and political career

Dolan is described in Badlands Media's account as a longtime Democratic operative and power broker whose involvement in high-level national politics dated back to the presidency of Jimmy Carter.[1] His decades-long standing inside Democratic political circles is what made his appearance in the dossier's sourcing chain significant: it indicated that material presented to the [[fbi|FBI]] as Russian-origin intelligence was in part sourced from a domestic partisan operative.[1]

## Role in the dossier and the Danchenko trial

The FBI learned during the course of its work that one of the dossier's sources was Dolan.[1] Durham surfaced this fact at the Danchenko trial as part of a broader body of evidence that had been concealed from the public for more than five years, alongside disclosures about the Bureau's failure to verify any dossier allegation and its awareness that Danchenko had himself been the subject of a counterintelligence investigation.[1] In the Badlands Media framing, the Dolan revelation undercut the premise of the [[russiagate-hoax|RussiaGate]] narrative by showing that the dossier's supposed Russian sourcing partly traced back to the American political operation that commissioned it.[1]

## Day one of the Danchenko trial

Dolan's emails with [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] were among the first evidentiary disputes argued on the opening day of the 2022 trial.[2] Defence counsel [[stuart-sears|Stuart Sears]] told [[anthony-trenga|Judge Trenga]] that the information Dolan passed to Danchenko — in particular an email sent on or about 20 August 2016 — had all been public, and that the defence had found a series of contemporaneous articles covering every detail in it, which it wished to admit to prove the material was publicly available.[2] The defence also argued the emails were inadmissible because agents interviewing Danchenko never specifically asked for them, while [[john-durham|Durham]] responded that the requirement to disclose had been "made abundantly clear" and pointed to Danchenko's [[danchenko-immunity-agreement|immunity agreement]] obliging full disclosure.[2] Judge Trenga barred mention of the publicly available articles in opening statements.[2]

In the prosecution's opening statement, Assistant Special Counsel [[michael-keilty|Michael Keilty]] told the jury that Danchenko had described himself to Dolan as "working on a very important project against Donald Trump," and announced that Dolan would testify and that four [[fbi|FBI]] agents would give evidence about him.[2] FBI intelligence analyst [[brittany-hertzog|Brittany Hertzog]] prepared a report on Dolan establishing his connections to the Russian government and to Danchenko, and Agent [[kevin-helson|Helson]] wanted to interview Dolan, but the Bureau never did — an omission the prosecution attributed to Danchenko's lies about him.[2]

## Day three of the Danchenko trial

Dolan was the first witness called on the third day of the trial, examined on direct by Assistant Special Counsel [[michael-keilty|Michael Keilty]].[3] Walking through his work history, Dolan testified that he had worked on every Democratic presidential campaign except Barack Obama's — because he was then working for the Clintons — a list that includes 2016, and that as part of his work for Ketchum and [[kglobal|kglobal]] he met "regularly" with officials of the Russian government.[3] He had previously been employed at [[cassidy-and-associates|Cassidy & Associates]], the firm where lobbyist [[gregg-hartley|Gregg Hartley]] served as Vice Chairman and Chief Operating Officer.[3] In the Danchenko indictment Dolan is the figure designated "PR Executive-1," with kglobal as "PR Firm-1."[3]

Keilty used documents to map Dolan's relationships. Exhibit 702, an April 29, 2016 email from [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]] to Dolan, concerned a letter detailing kglobal's services; Dolan testified he never met [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]] or Christopher Burrows and that [[orbis-business-intelligence|Orbis Business Intelligence]] and kglobal never established a business relationship.[3] Exhibit 703, sent the same day and copying Hartley, attached an Orbis document prepared by Danchenko that Dolan described as an "intelligence briefing note on Kompromat Nadzor in the Russian banking sector"; Dolan said he read it but was unsure what it was about.[3] Dolan met with Danchenko and [[olga-galkina|Olga Galkina]] in Washington, D.C. in March 2016.[3]

Through the spring of 2016 Dolan corresponded with Danchenko about the [[young-presidents-organization|Young Presidents' Organization]] and a planned Moscow conference, telling the organizer that "Mr. Danchenko could be useful to us on this."[3] He had been drawn into the project by Washington lawyer [[steve-kupka|Steve Kupka]] of King & Spalding, who wanted to stage an October 2016 Moscow conference called "Inside The Kremlin" and sought Dolan out for his Russian government connections.[3] Dolan also stayed in touch with Galkina, planning travel to Cyprus to meet her and pitch her employer; kglobal and service.com eventually signed a contract.[3] Dolan, Danchenko and Russian government officials all attended the October 2016 Moscow conference, and the two men met again after returning to the United States.[3]

The most damaging portion of the direct examination concerned Exhibit 712A, Danchenko's August 19, 2016 email and Dolan's replies of August 19 and 20.[3] Keilty obtained Dolan's admission that he had lied to Danchenko in that exchange, having taken material from television and a POLITICO article while presenting it as coming from a Republican insider — information that then appeared in dossier report 95 two days later, which Keilty displayed side by side with the email for the jury.[3] Dolan also acknowledged reading the January 10, 2017 BuzzFeed article publishing the dossier, and phone records showed he called Danchenko immediately afterward even though Danchenko's role as a dossier source was not then public.[3] Danchenko had met Dolan in person on January 1, 2017 at 12:19 p.m. in Tuckahoe Park, a little over a week before publication, and photographed him there.[3]

On cross-examination [[stuart-sears|Stuart Sears]] contrasted Dolan's August 31, 2021 Special Counsel interview, in which he said he had missed the Manafort portion of the article and dossier report, with his September 7, 2021 interview, and it emerged for the first time publicly that **Dolan was himself a subject of the Special Counsel's investigation**.[3] Sears also established that the FBI had taken investigative steps regarding Dolan after January 2017, following an investigative referral generated by information Danchenko gave the Bureau — steps that had never previously been reported.[3] Dolan noted that Danchenko "speaks excellent English" and that the two communicated by email, phone calls and in person over meals, making no mention of messaging apps.[3]

## Day two of the Danchenko trial

On the second day of trial [[john-durham|John Durham]] devoted an extended passage of his direct examination of [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]] to Dolan.[4] Auten agreed that Dolan's name had first come up in the FBI's October 2016 discussions with [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]] and that the Bureau had "developed some information" on him between then and January 2017, yet no one raised Dolan during the three days of Danchenko interviews — an omission that also implicated Danchenko's obligations under his immunity agreement, since Danchenko described the June 2016 Moscow conference while leaving out Dolan's name.[4] Durham then produced a binder of [[kglobal|KGlobal]] emails and business records; the defense had moved in limine against portions of it, above all the record in which Dolan speculated that Danchenko might be KGB or a Russian agent.[4] Admitted exhibits included an August 19, 2016 email from Danchenko to Dolan — two months before the FBI met Steele and five months before it interviewed Danchenko — together with Dolan's reply and subsequent exchanges.[4] Noting Dolan's decades of Democratic Party and Clinton work, the Badlands correspondent read the exchanges as evidence that the two men were cooperating to gather material on Donald Trump and his campaign for use in the dossier, and that the [[fbi|FBI]] never performed the simple investigative work that would have revealed it.[4] Defense counsel [[danny-onorato|Danny Onorato]] countered that the Lewandowski and [[paul-manafort|Manafort]] allegations Dolan sent Danchenko had appeared in the media some six days earlier and were therefore open-source, a point aimed at Count One; on redirect Durham answered that what mattered was that Danchenko had in fact obtained the information from Dolan and then lied about it to the Bureau.[4]

## Day four testimony and referral for investigation

On the final day of the trial, Special Agent [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]] testified that Dolan's name had never come up in his initial discussions with [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]] and that he himself had been unaware of Dolan, while Special Agent [[amy-anderson|Amy Anderson]] and analyst [[brittany-hertzog|Brittany Hertzog]] at FBI Headquarters were "working on some project or some report that involved Mr. Peskov and Dolan."[5] Anderson supplied Helson with specific questions about Dolan, which he put to Danchenko in a recorded interview on June 15, 2017 without result: Danchenko revealed neither his messages and meetings with Dolan nor that Dolan-sourced information had ended up in the dossier.[5] Hertzog folded her Dolan material into a case file on [[olga-galkina|Olga Galkina]] and serialized it in three places — the Washington Field Office, FBI Headquarters and the Inspector General — even after the Special Counsel's Office directed the team "not to take further action on the matter involving Mr. Dolan and Mr. Danchenko's relationship."[5] Anderson separately submitted a memorandum on Dolan to her superiors urging investigative steps, and portions of her testimony recounting what Galkina had told her about Dolan were struck as hearsay.[5]

Information Danchenko eventually provided led to an FBI investigative referral on Dolan in January 2018; that investigation was opened, Dolan's email, phone and social media records were obtained by search warrant, and the matter was later transferred to the [[durham-special-counsel-investigation|Durham Special Counsel]], where he remains a subject.[5] Prosecution exhibits included a chart of Dolan and Danchenko phone records covering March 2016 through January 2017, prepared with Special Agent [[ryan-james|Ryan James]].[5] The two men met in person in a park near Falls Church, Virginia on January 1, 2017 and had breakfast or lunch together on a number of occasions, leading the Badlands writer to speculate that any case against Dolan would be brought in the Eastern District of Virginia.[5] The article's closing assessment was that the trial was less about Danchenko than about the [[fbi|FBI]], the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]], and Dolan — described as a Clinton crony whose fabrications supplied the "Russian disinformation" later used against [[carter-page|Carter Page]].[5]
