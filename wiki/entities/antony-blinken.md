# Antony Blinken

Antony Blinken is the U.S. Secretary of State whose public declaration on Ukraine's NATO accession anchors the Badlands article "A Bright Red Line."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) Speaking at a summit of [[nato|NATO]] foreign ministers, Blinken stated unequivocally that "Ukraine WILL become a member of NATO," ending years of official insistence that Russian fears of Ukrainian membership were paranoid invention.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

The article highlights the shift in his language: where officials had previously spoken of discussions, preparations, or negotiating the possibility of a Membership Action Plan, Blinken's formulation presents membership as decided and inevitable.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) Badlands argues this is precisely the step that removes any possibility of negotiation with Russia, and that Blinken's [[state-department|State Department]], along with neoconservatives in the [[us-security-state|Security State]] and [[us-congress|Congress]], is fully aware of that consequence and welcomes it.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) His statement stands in direct tension with the 2008 assessment of [[william-burns|William Burns]] that Ukrainian entry into NATO was "the brightest of all redlines" for the Russian elite.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## September 2024 European tour

In September 2024 Blinken wrapped up a three-nation, Ukraine-focused European tour in Poland after hearing repeated appeals from Ukrainian officials to use Western-supplied weaponry for long-range strikes inside Russia.[2] He travelled to Warsaw after spending a day in Kyiv with British Foreign Secretary [[david-lammy|David Lammy]], during which the two pledged to bring the Ukrainian requests to their leaders.[2] "As what Russia's doing has changed, as the battlefield has changed, we've adapted," Blinken said at a news conference in Warsaw.[2]

The trip coincided with signs that both Washington and London were growing more receptive to deeper strikes, ahead of a meeting between President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] in the United States.[2] Biden had permitted Ukraine to fire U.S.-provided missiles across the border in self-defense but largely limited their range, and Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] had pushed back on the long-range request in a meeting with [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]].[2] Polish Foreign Minister [[radoslaw-sikorski|Radoslaw Sikorski]] publicly backed Ukraine's case, while President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] warned that approval would mean [[nato|NATO]] countries were "at war" with Russia.[2]

## Remarks on Iranian involvement in the October 7 attacks

In an interview with [[cnn|CNN]] that aired on Sunday, October 8, 2023, Blinken — then Secretary of State — addressed reporting that [[iran|Iran]] had helped plan the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]] on Israel: "We have not yet seen evidence that Iran directed or was behind this particular attack, but there is certainly a long relationship."[3] Another U.S. official said of the alleged Beirut planning meetings, "We don't have any information at this time to corroborate this account."[3] The statements stood in tension with [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]] reporting that [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] officers had worked with [[hamas|Hamas]] on the operation since August 2023.[3]

## Beijing visit (June 2023)

Blinken concluded a visit to Beijing on June 20, 2023, meeting [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] amid mounting tensions between the United States and [[china|China]].[4] The visit was immediately overshadowed by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s remarks the following evening calling Xi a dictator, which drew a formal rebuke from the Chinese Foreign Ministry.[4] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the trip as having been "followed by sharp rebukes by Xi," who warned that the administration must choose between "cooperation and conflict."[4]

## Claim to credit for the Gaza peace deal (October 2025)

In October 2025 Blinken posted a twelve-part thread asserting that "it's good that President Trump adopted and built on the plan the Biden Administration developed after months of discussion with Arab partners, Israel and the Palestinian Authority," suggesting that at least part of the [[gaza-ceasefire|Gaza ceasefire]] brokered between [[israel|Israel]] and [[hamas|Hamas]] originated under his tenure.[5] The claim was widely mocked by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and conservative commentators; asked about it, Trump said "everybody knows that's a joke" and that the Biden team "did such a bad job — that was weak leadership."[5] Representative Derrick Van Orden called Blinken "a lying tool," the phrase that supplied the story's headline.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] used the episode to question who had in fact been negotiating with Arab partners during the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration, framing that period as one in which an incapacitated president and an autopen fronted for others, and recalling that a Chinese foreign minister had publicly called Blinken "two-faced."[5]

## De-escalation diplomacy, August 2024

In August 2024, as Secretary of State, Blinken took a leading role in the effort to prevent the killings of [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]] in Tehran and [[fuad-shukr|Fuad Shukr]] in Beirut from triggering a regional war.[6] Speaking after a meeting with Australian Foreign Minister Penny Wong and Defense Minister Richard Marles, he said Washington had communicated to both [[israel|Israel]] and [[iran|Iran]] that the conflict must not escalate.[6]

"It's urgent that everyone in the region take stock of the situation, understand the risk of miscalculation, and make decisions that will calm tensions, not exacerbate them," Blinken said, adding: "We've been engaged in intense diplomacy with allies and partners, communicating that message directly to Iran. We've communicated that message directly to Israel."[6] [[white-house|White House]] officials believed the diplomatic push was having an effect on Iran's calculations, even as the administration was reported to be enraged by the timing of the Haniyeh assassination.[6]

## Ukraine long-range missile decision (September 2024)

[[scott-ritter|Scott Ritter]] said in September 2024 that Blinken and President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had overruled the military's initial decision to deny Ukraine's long-range missile request because the matter had become political.[7] [[pentagon]] officials subsequently intervened at the September 13 meeting with British Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] and told the two leaders the deal was off, reversing what [[state-department|State Department]] leadership had publicly announced a day earlier.[7]

## Response to the October 2024 Iranian missile attack

After [[iran|Iran]] fired about 200 ballistic missiles at [[israel|Israel]] on October 1, 2024, Blinken called the attack "totally unacceptable, and the entire world should condemn it."[8] His remarks were part of a calibrated shift in the [[joe-biden|Biden administration]]'s messaging, which drew a distinction between Israel's expanded operations against [[hamas|Hamas]] and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and Iran's retaliatory strike, defending Israeli attacks on Hezbollah leaders in Lebanon while still pressing for peace and vowing retribution.[8] President Biden praised the U.S. and Israeli militaries for defeating the barrage and warned, "Make no mistake, the United States is fully, fully supportive of Israel."[8] See [[october-2024-iranian-missile-attack-on-israel|October 2024 Iranian missile attack on Israel]].[8]

## June 2023 Beijing visit

On Monday, June 19, 2023, Blinken met Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] as he concluded a flying two-day visit to Beijing.[9] A Blinken–Xi meeting had been widely expected, but neither side confirmed it would take place until roughly an hour before the talks, which were regarded as the key test of the trip's success.[9] Earlier the same day, senior Chinese diplomat [[wang-yi|Wang Yi]] told Blinken that the visit came "at a critical juncture in China-U.S. relations" and that it was "necessary to make a choice between dialogue and confrontation, cooperation or conflict," urging Washington to reverse the downward spiral in relations.[9] Wang paired that message with a warning on Taiwan and a reassertion of Beijing's claim to vast swathes of the South China Sea.[9]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the trip through [[devolution|Devolution Theory]], arguing that the visible decoupling between the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration and Xi is optically damaging to Biden's globalist backers while escalating war rhetoric for public consumption, and that behind it lies coordination between a devolved Trump administration and Xi against their respective deep states.[9]

## Rebuff of Australia over Assange

Weeks before U.S. Ambassador [[caroline-kennedy|Caroline Kennedy]] floated a possible plea deal in August 2023, Blinken rebuffed Australian calls to drop the prosecution of [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]], telling reporters after a July meeting with Foreign Minister [[penny-wong|Penny Wong]] in Brisbane that Assange was "charged with very serious criminal conduct" for publishing classified American government materials.[10] Assange's brother Gabriel Shipton warned that absent an off-ramp there could be "a repeat of a very public rebuff delivered by Tony Blinken to the Australian Foreign Minister."[10]

## Admission on Iran regime change (December 2024)

Speaking at the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] in New York on December 18, 2024, Blinken publicly admitted that Washington had over the previous two decades conducted experiments seeking regime change in Iran, and that efforts to topple the Islamic Republic's leadership had failed.[11] Asked whether the United States should adopt a policy of regime change in Iran, he replied, "I think if we look at the last 20 years, our experiments in regime change have not exactly been resounding successes," to laughter from the audience.[11]

Blinken attributed the failure to the absence of diplomatic relations between Washington and Tehran since April 1980, when President [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]] severed ties following the 1979 hostage crisis.[11] He stressed the "complicated" dynamics inside Iran, said the United States was in many ways "not the best source of a clear view" because the two countries had been disengaged, and claimed the narrative of opposition to the Islamic Republic was "reflective of at least half the population" but "not as clear-cut as that."[11] He acknowledged a "very conservative element in Iran that's significant in number that probably remains beholden to the regime," and conceded that Washington had tried to stir up Iranian society while noting "it's really hard to do from the outside."[11]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded with a lengthy historical rebuttal in [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], mocking the Council on Foreign Relations and rejecting Blinken's dating of failed diplomacy with Persia to 1980.[11] He traced Anglo-American involvement to the [[anglo-iranian-oil-company|D'Arcy Concession]] and the 1908 Masjed Soleiman discovery, through [[mohammad-mosaddegh|Mosaddegh]]'s nationalization, the [[1953-iranian-coup|1953 CIA coup]], the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] settlement, and the [[iranian-revolution|Iranian Revolution]] of 1979 — concluding, "And that, my friends, is how you radicalize a region for profit, over the course of a century. Regime change, Blinken? Please."[11]

## Role in the Gershkovich prisoner exchange

Reporting on the negotiations that freed Wall Street Journal reporter [[evan-gershkovich|Evan Gershkovich]] from Russian custody suggested that Blinken, rather than President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], was directing the effort.[12] At the White House Correspondents' Dinner, Gershkovich's mother Ella — acting on instructions from hostage-affairs envoy [[roger-carstens|Roger Carstens]] — asked Biden to telephone German Chancellor [[olaf-scholz|Olaf Scholz]]; Biden said he had already done so, and Blinken subtly corrected him, saying they would make the call.[12] No call followed; a letter with Biden's signature was sent instead, and Badlands commentary argued the episode raised the question of who was actually managing US diplomatic efforts and running the country.[12]

## Final Gaza ceasefire push (January 2025)

On January 6, 2025, Blinken called for a final push to secure a [[gaza-ceasefire|Gaza ceasefire]] before President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] left office, after a [[hamas|Hamas]] official told Reuters the group had cleared a list of 34 hostages to be released first under a truce.[13] "We very much want to bring this over the finish line in the next two weeks, the time we have remaining," he told a news conference in South Korea when asked whether a deal was close.[13]

At the time Israel had sent mid-ranking officials to Qatar for talks brokered by Qatari and Egyptian mediators, with [[mossad|Mossad]] chief [[david-barnea|David Barnea]] reportedly expected to join, though it remained unclear how far apart the parties were.[13] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] predicted no deal would be reached before [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took office, arguing there was zero upside for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[13]

## China as the "number one threat" to the liberal world order (2023)

In late September 2023, Blinken told The Atlantic's Jeffrey Goldberg at the Atlantic Festival that China, not Russia, posed the greater challenge to the United States and the US-led [[liberal-world-order|liberal world order]], saying Beijing has a "much greater ability certainly than Russia to try to shape what the international system looks like."[14] The remarks came while Washington was directing billions of dollars into the Ukraine conflict on the stated rationale of weakening Russia.[14] Blinken said of China: "I think they want a world order, but the world order that they seek is profoundly illiberal in nature; ours is liberal with a small 'L.' And that's the fundamental difference," adding that China "seeks to be the dominant power in the world militarily, economically, diplomatically."[14]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the establishment's abrupt turn on Beijing — after decades of accommodation from Clinton through Obama — as evidence for the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] thesis and the emergence of a multipolar order led by China and Russia, suggesting the "powers that would be" had belatedly realized they controlled less of China than they assumed.[14] Citing [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]]'s formulation that every nation has an "evil twin" penetrated by the same subversive network, Burning Bright concluded that the turn on communism came "far too late in the game" and that "the mask came off when they forced everyone else to put one on."[14]

## Remarks on Wagner and the Niger coup (August 2023)

In an August 2023 interview with the BBC's Focus on Africa programme, Blinken said that Russia's [[wagner-group|Wagner]] mercenary group was "taking advantage" of the instability in [[niger|Niger]] following the ousting of President [[mohamed-bazoum|Mohamed Bazoum]] by a military junta.[15] He stated that he did not believe Russia or Wagner had instigated the coup, saying "what happened, and what continues to happen in Niger was not instigated by Russia or by Wagner, but... they tried to take advantage of it."[15] Blinken said the United States was concerned about the group "possibly manifesting itself" elsewhere in the Sahel, noting Wagner's known presence in neighbouring Mali.[15]

He characterized the group's record in stark terms: "Every single place that this Wagner group has gone, death, destruction and exploitation have followed," adding that "insecurity has gone up, not down" and that Niger risked "a repeat of what's happened in other countries, where they brought nothing but bad things in their wake."[15] [[badlands-media|Badlands]] commentary was skeptical of this framing; [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that the deposed government was backed by the [[european-union|European Union]], the [[united-nations|United Nations]] and what he called the "Deep State Department," while the coup was backed by Russia and Wagner, and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the western establishment was "fully on board with the Wagner coup narrative" despite lying "about everything."[15]

## Kremlin drone attack and Hunter Biden allegations (May 2023)

Speaking on May 3, 2023 at a Washington Post event previewing the 2023 World Press Freedom Index, Blinken cast doubt on Russia's claim that the overnight [[2023-kremlin-drone-attack|Kremlin drone attack]] was a Ukrainian assassination attempt on [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], saying "I can't in any way validate them," that "we simply don't know," and that he would "take anything coming out of the Kremlin with a very large shaker of salt."[16] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] used the episode to argue that the [[state-department|State Department]] operates as the deep state under the [[devolution]] framework.[16]

The same week, Senators [[ron-johnson|Ron Johnson]] and [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] formally accused Blinken — described as a close lieutenant of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] — of having given Congress a "patently false" account in 2020 of his earlier dealings with [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] during the Obama years.[16] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] wrote that although observers had long suspected Blinken was "in bed with the Biden's et al in China and beyond," the evidence was now in hand: Blinken did speak with Hunter Biden despite saying he could not recall, and "cracks in the dam are starting to appear."[16]

## October 2024 Riyadh visit and Saudi-Israel normalization push

On Wednesday, 23 October 2024, Blinken sought progress on normalizing relations between [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and [[israel|Israel]], flying directly from Tel Aviv to Riyadh as part of a Middle East tour just days before the U.S. election, in the hope that a historic deal could create incentives to end the war in [[gaza|Gaza]].[17] Speaking in Tel Aviv before departing, he emphasized keeping "eyes on the strategic prize" of Israel–Saudi normalization even as ceasefire efforts struggled, saying: "There remains, despite everything that's happened, an incredible opportunity in this region to move in a totally different direction."[17]

In Riyadh, Blinken met Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan and held an hour-long conversation with Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]]; the [[state-department|State Department]] later confirmed the discussions covered "the need to end the war in Gaza" and building lasting regional stability through greater integration among Middle Eastern nations.[17] Saudi recognition of Israel would be a significant milestone given the kingdom's role as guardian of Islam's two holiest sites, and before the October 7 [[hamas|Hamas]] attack Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had been optimistic about formal ties.[17] U.S. officials nonetheless acknowledged that Riyadh was unlikely to move without meaningful progress toward an independent Palestinian state, a condition the Crown Prince had stated publicly the previous month.[17] Biden and [[kamala-harris|Harris]] faced criticism from their party's left for not pressuring Israel more forcefully, while Senator Lindsey Graham suggested the Senate would likely approve a Saudi-Israel deal during Biden's term.[17]

Badlands commentary set the visit against the simultaneous confirmation by a Saudi defense ministry spokesman that the Royal Saudi Naval Forces had already concluded a joint naval exercise with [[iran|Iran]]'s navy and other countries in the Sea of Oman — a day after Iranian reports of planned drills.[17] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued Blinken was "obviously rushing over to Riyadh" in reaction, and noted the conspicuous absence of a flag in the Saudi Foreign Ministry's 17-second video of the Blinken–MBS meeting, in contrast to the ministry's footage of meetings with King Abdullah of Jordan and the prime ministers of Spain and Greece.[17]

## Mexico City migration trip (December 2023)

Blinken traveled to Mexico City in late December 2023 to discuss a new agreement with President [[andres-manuel-lopez-obrador|Andrés Manuel López Obrador]] aimed at controlling the surge of migrants at the southern border.[18] Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] and White House Homeland Security Advisor Liz Sherwood-Randall joined him for talks on "unprecedented irregular migration in the Western Hemisphere," with a [[state-department|State Department]] press release framing the goals as "identifying ways Mexico and the United States will address border security challenges, including actions to enable the reopening of key ports of entry across our shared border."[18] ZeroHedge characterized the visit as "nothing more than optics" for an administration whose open-border policies had allowed millions of illegal crossings ahead of the 2024 election cycle, noting a Harvard CAPS-Harris poll showing only 38% approval of [[joe-biden|Biden]]'s handling of immigration, down from 46% in November.[18]

## NATO "no fatigue" remarks and Netanyahu meeting (November 2023)

Following a meeting of [[nato|NATO]] foreign ministers in Brussels on Wednesday, November 29, 2023, Blinken concluded that the alliance was holding strong on supporting Ukraine despite an avalanche of negative reports about Ukrainian battlefield performance.[19] He told a press briefing there was "no sense of fatigue" among NATO allies and that "we must and we will continue to support Ukraine" — words that ZeroHedge read as a flat rejection of headlines warning of war fatigue among Western publics and leaderships since at least the summer.[19] The fact that Blinken had to address alliance fatigue head-on was itself described as telling, coming after eight weeks in which the Israel-Gaza conflict dominated global attention and the era of [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]] addressing packed parliaments to rock-star receptions had passed.[19] NATO Secretary General [[jens-stoltenberg|Jens Stoltenberg]] warned in Brussels that Russia had amassed a large missile stockpile ahead of winter.[19]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] called Blinken the head of the "(Deep) State Department" and argued that the [[state-department|State Department]] is a truly independent-acting cadre of [[deep-state|Deep State]] stalwarts from the Clinton and Obama eras, "replete with intel agency washing," rather than a body simply directed by the Biden White House.[19] He held that the [[devolution|Devolution]] operation had them "hemmed in on a Deep State archipelago," and read the international picture as one in which "[they] are running out of cards to play, and the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] is calling their bluff."[19]

The same week, Blinken met Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] in Jerusalem; on leaving that meeting Netanyahu warned the United States that "nothing will stop" Israel's army from "eradicating" [[hamas|Hamas]], amid mounting pressure from Washington to scale back the assault on Gaza.[19]

## Reported post-election counseling sessions (November 2024)

In November 2024 the [[state-department|State Department]] was reported to have held therapy sessions for employees after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] won the 2024 election, a report that was never confirmed.[20] Representative [[darrell-issa|Darrell Issa]] wrote to Blinken as secretary of state criticising the alleged sessions, saying he was "concerned that the Department is catering to federal employees who are personally devastated by the normal functioning of American democracy through the provision of government-funded mental health counseling because [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] was not elected President of the United States."[20] Issa added that it was "disturbing that ostensibly nonpartisan government officials would suffer a personal meltdown over the results of a free and fair election, something the United States champions around the world," and asked Blinken how many sessions had been held, how many were planned, and what they cost.[20] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] placed Blinken alongside [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[john-kerry|John Kerry]] as figures for whom departmental staff had "run ops" under the traditional assumption that they were above the law.[20]

## Badlands assessment of the October 2024 U.S.–Israel trust crisis

Amid Axios reporting that the Biden administration had grown deeply distrustful of the Israeli government's stated military and diplomatic plans, Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the reported rift was largely irrelevant because [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] effectively controls Blinken, who he said pledged fealty to the Israeli prime minister after October 7, 2023 with the words, "I come to you, not as an American diplomat, but as a Jew."[21] In the same analysis, [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] were described as largely ignored and given busywork, while [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] was described as present but disregarded.[21] The contributor concluded that many in the West Wing may genuinely have been in the dark on the evolving situation — not because their hands were clean, but because nobody respected them enough to brief them — while people in the [[state-department|State Department]], parts of the [[white-house|White House]] and probably the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] knew.[21]

## Named defendant in Daily Wire v. State Department

Blinken was named as a defendant, along with five other officials, in the December 2023 lawsuit brought by [[daily-wire|The Daily Wire]], [[the-federalist|The Federalist]] and the state of Texas against the [[state-department|State Department]] over the [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]]'s alleged funding of censorship technology aimed at domestic media.[22] The complaint, filed by the [[new-civil-liberties-alliance|New Civil Liberties Alliance]], described the program as among the gravest First Amendment abuses in American history and asked the court to permanently enjoin it.[22]

## Admission on pressure against Israel (January 2025)

In an interview with the New York Times released as a podcast on Saturday, January 4, 2025, Blinken admitted that public pressure on Israel had caused [[hamas|Hamas]] to pull back from potential ceasefire and hostage deals.[23] Per the State Department transcript, he said the administration's question had been "how can we most effectively both shape the conflict but also bring an end to the conflict," and that a ceasefire/hostage agreement was in their estimation the quickest and most durable route; "when they saw Israel under pressure publicly, they pulled back."[23] He added that the other factor was Hamas's hope for a wider conflict — that [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], Iran, and other actors would attack Israel and leave it with its hands full — and said "we've worked very hard to make sure that that didn't happen."[23] Breitbart noted that Hezbollah and Iran did in fact attack Israel, and that Israeli missile defenses and counterattacks, often against the wishes of the Biden-Harris administration, proved effective.[23]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used the admission to argue that the negotiations were never entered in earnest by the [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] government, contending that the near-ratified Saudi-Israeli normalization deal — which would have required Israel to recognize Palestine, end its West Bank occupation and halt settlement construction — presented terms Netanyahu would never accept, and that this explains the facilitation of the October 7 operation.[23]

## September 2023 Kyiv visit and $1 billion aid announcement

On September 6, 2023, Blinken announced more than $1 billion in new American aid to Ukraine while speaking in Kyiv, in one of the highest-level visits by a U.S. official to the Ukrainian capital since [[joe-biden|President Biden]]'s February trip.[24] Speaking at a joint news conference with Ukrainian Foreign Minister [[dmytro-kuleba|Dmytro Kuleba]], he said the aid went beyond security assistance for Ukraine's counteroffensive to include financial and humanitarian support intended to help "rebuild a free, resilient, thriving" country after the war.[24] The package included $300 million to help Ukrainian law enforcement "restore and maintain law and order in liberated areas," along with the first U.S. delivery of mine-resistant ambush protected vehicles (MRAPs) to Ukraine's border guards and police, following earlier MRAP deliveries to the Ukrainian military.[24]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] connected the announcement to the arrest of [[ihor-kolomoisky|Ihor Kolomoisky]] in [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]]'s anti-corruption probe, which he had predicted would trigger a media about-face on Ukrainian money, followed a day later by a [[new-york-times|New York Times]] headline on "vanishing billions" blamed on Ukrainian military grifting.[24] He credited [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] with flagging a [[steve-bannon|Bannon]] clip laying out the mechanisms of what he called a U.S. Deep State money laundering scheme, and argued that the Times' fresh piece on the [[state-department|State Department]] sending another billion signaled an emerging open information war between the legacy media and Kyiv over "who's got the stolen US taxpayer money under their mattress."[24]

## April 2024 visit to China

Blinken's three-day visit to China in late April 2024 was widely characterised as a diplomatic humiliation.[25] Only a low-level official was on hand to greet him when he stepped off the plane in Shanghai on the Wednesday, and it was unclear whether any Chinese official appeared at the Beijing airport to see him off at the end of the trip; the send-off was in any event low-key, and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] slighted America's top diplomat at the close of the troubled stay.[25] Charles Burton of the Prague-based Sinopsis think tank, a former Canadian diplomat who had served in Beijing, told the Gatestone Institute that "the Chinese government flouted international protocols at the airport on the secretary of state's arrival in Shanghai and departure from Beijing," calling the treatment "petty" and "more than a slight" — "aside from a calculated insult to the dignity of the United States, the move indicates Xi Jinping is making clear that the accepted norms of diplomacy will not be respected by China anymore."[25]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] placed the episode in a pattern of foreign leaders taking turns humiliating the Biden administration, likening [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and Xi to batters in a baseball lineup, and noting that Blinken kept pressing for engagement despite the snubs.[25]

## Netzah Yehuda sanctions (April 2024)

In April 2024, Axios reported that Blinken as Secretary of State was expected to announce sanctions against the [[netzah-yehuda-battalion|Netzah Yehuda Battalion]] of the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] for alleged human rights violations in the occupied West Bank.[26] It would be the first time the United States imposed sanctions on an Israeli military unit, barring the battalion of over 900 soldiers and its members from receiving US military assistance or training.[26] The move came as the [[us-house-of-representatives|House of Representatives]] passed a bill containing more than $14 billion in military aid to Israel.[26]

## G7 Tokyo remarks on Gaza, November 2023

Speaking at a Group of Seven summit in Tokyo in November 2023, Blinken told a press briefing that "it is clear that Israel cannot occupy Gaza" on a permanent basis, affirming that Washington had been intensely involved in talks about the post-war order.[27] "Gaza cannot be continued to be run by Hamas. That simply invites repetition of Oct. 7," he said after meeting G7 foreign ministers, allowing that "there may be a need for some transition period at the end of the conflict" while adding, "we don’t see a reoccupation and what I’ve heard from Israeli leaders, is that they have no intent to reoccupy Gaza."[27] The remarks were widely read as a sign of growing public divergence with Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], who had told ABC News that Israel would hold security responsibility for Gaza "for an indefinite period" and who had rejected President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]’s request for a three-day humanitarian pause, amid mounting international pressure over a Gaza civilian death toll that had surpassed 10,000.[27]

## Ukraine's NATO membership (April 2024)

Speaking to reporters in Brussels on April 4, 2024, Blinken reaffirmed the U.S. commitment for [[ukraine|Ukraine]] to eventually join [[nato|NATO]] while holding back firm commitments ahead of the alliance's annual summit, scheduled for Washington that July.[28] "Ukraine will become a member of NATO. Our purpose at the summit is to help build a bridge to that membership," he said.[28] NATO allies had agreed at the 2023 Vilnius summit that Ukraine could join once certain conditions were met, a formulation criticized by [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] and Baltic allies for failing to set concrete goals or a timeline for Kyiv.[28] The remarks came amid concerns over waning U.S. support, with House Republicans holding up additional assistance and fears that a return of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to the White House could see a U.S. retreat from allied commitments.[28]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the announcement as part of a "Narrative and Actual Panic Pattern," arguing that Blinken and [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] were "[[deep-state|Deep State]] Department stooges" chasing Trump around the map to circumvent his meetings with foreign leaders while prepping the logistical game board to continue the proxy war against Russia without U.S. backing.[28]

## Beijing visit and meeting with Xi Jinping (April 2024)

Blinken met Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] in Beijing on Friday, April 26, 2024, wrapping up a three-day visit to China dominated by contentious issues and by warnings from his hosts of another "downward spiral" in relations.[30] The two met at the Great Hall of the People, an ornate and cavernous building next to Tiananmen Square.[30] Xi noted that the year marked the 45th anniversary of the establishment of US-China diplomatic relations and said the two countries "should be partners rather than adversaries," adding that "the world is big enough to accommodate the simultaneous development and prosperity of both China and the United States" and that relations would stabilize once the US took "a positive and constructive view of China's development."[30] Speaking to reporters afterward, Blinken said the United States did not aim to hold back China's development or to decouple the world's two largest economies: "We want China's economy to grow," he said, but "the way China grows matters."[30]
