# Lev Tahor

Lev Tahor is an extremist ultra-Orthodox Jewish sect that has run into legal problems in multiple countries and became the subject of a December 2024 law-enforcement operation in Guatemala after reports of systematic abuse within its community.[1]

## December 2024 Guatemalan raid

On a Friday in late December 2024, Guatemalan authorities searched the sect's compound and took at least 160 minors and 40 women into protective custody following reports of abuse.[1] Interior Minister Francisco Jiménez said the National Civil Police and members of the military participated in the raid on the group's community, described in one account as roughly 55 miles (90 kilometers) southeast of the capital and in another as a farm compound in Oratoria, southwest of Guatemala City.[1] "The protection of boys and girls is an absolute priority," Jiménez said.[1]

Guatemala's Attorney General's Office stated on the social platform X that suspected bones of one child were found at the site.[1] The office said a complaint had been filed in November alleging possible crimes including forced pregnancies, mistreatment of minors and rape.[1]

## Attempted recapture of the children

Two days after the raid, Guatemalan officials said members of the sect broke into the shelter where the rescued minors were being held in an attempt to recapture them.[1] About 100 relatives of the children belonging to the group gathered outside the care center in Guatemala City on Sunday to demand their return, and members then forced open the gate around 4:30 p.m. local time, "abducting the children and adolescents sheltered there," according to a statement from the Attorney General's Office.[1] Those outside the shelter attempted to prevent authorities from bringing the minors back, leading to scuffles with police witnessed by an AFP photographer at the scene.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the story as "disgusting and despicable" and anticipated accusations of antisemitism for covering it, arguing that Americans across the political spectrum have been conditioned to embrace identity politics and to lump entire demographics together.[1] He criticized Christian Zionists for conflating the citizenry of [[israel|Israel]] — which he noted is only 73% Jewish — with the worldwide Jewish population, citing figures of roughly 15.8 million Jews globally against 7.2 million with Israeli citizenship, or fewer than half (45%).[1]

He further argued that the Republican Party has trained Americans to equate criticism of Israel, a foreign government, with antisemitism, which he called among the most draconian and un-American achievements of a mainstream political party.[1] Discussing doctrine, he noted that Judaism lacks a centralized doctrinal authority in the way he attributes to Christianity and Islam, and that practices sometimes attributed online to Islam — including child marriage and pedophilia — appear in the Talmud, practices he associated with Lev Tahor.[1]

A search of the sect, he wrote, yields accounts of child trafficking, child marriage, forced pregnancy and systemic oppression of women comparable to what is commonly attributed to Sharia law, with reports emerging from Bosnia, Guatemala, Mexico, Israel and New York City.[1] He cited a March 2024 case in which three brothers who led Lev Tahor from Monsey, New York, faced criminal trial for trafficking children for sex, arriving in court with a copy of the Talmud and telling the judge that they did not recognize U.S. or New York law but followed Talmudic law; the judge explained that rejection of secular law is not a criminal defense.[1] He drew a parallel to his earlier coverage of the Pale of Settlement, the zone Russia created to contain rabbinic communities that refused to acknowledge Russian law, and concluded that people should stop lumping all Jewish people together and stop conflating the Jewish religion with the political actions of the Israeli government, which he called an institution of mortal men.[1]
