# Texas National Bank

Texas National Bank (TNB) was a Houston bank created in 1953 through the merger of South Texas National and Union National Bank — the same year [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] and J. Hugh Liedtke, later head of Pennzoil, incorporated Zapata Petroleum Corporation in Houston.[1] The Badlands article treats this coincidence of timing as evidence that TNB was "a CIA bank set up to service a CIA front company."[1]

TNB quickly established mutually profitable ties with [[zapata-offshore|Zapata Off-Shore Company]], an 80-percent-owned Zapata Petroleum subsidiary. Loans from TNB for drilling rigs and tending vessels made Zapata one of the bank's most significant oil-related accounts in the late 1950s, and TNB financed three ships — the *Houston*, the *Barbara J* and the *Zapata* — that Bush's firm loaned to the CIA for the 1961 [[bay-of-pigs-invasion|Bay of Pigs invasion]].[1]

In 1964 Texas National Bank merged with the National Bank of Commerce to form [[texas-commerce-bank|Texas Commerce Bank]], the institution into which [[mario-ruiz-massieu|Mario Ruiz Massieu]] would later deposit nearly $10 million in [[gulf-cartel|Gulf Cartel]] drug money.[1]
