# United Nations

The United Nations is the international body established after the Second World War, alongside the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods System]], as an attempt to create a durable postwar order.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) Badlands Media commentary presents the UN Charter as the authentic foundation of modern international law, in contrast to the [[rules-based-international-order|Rules Based International Order]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## The UN Charter

The Charter is described as premising international law on the sacredness of national sovereignty, the non-military interference of one state into another, and mutual cooperation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) The author argues that the discussions at the 2025 [[shanghai-cooperation-organisation|SCO]] summit in Tianjin were shaped around the common good, common security and improvement of the conditions of life of peoples and sovereign nations as outlined by the Charter.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) For the technocratic "Great Reset" agenda to succeed, he contends, the Charter would have to be erased or rewritten.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Origins and intent

The UN as envisioned by [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] or Henry Wallace was never meant to be a world government, but rather a platform for dialogue and the harmonization of security and economic interests among civilizations.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) The universal freedoms expressed in Roosevelt's [[four-freedoms|Four Freedoms]] were restated in the UN Declaration of Human Rights of 1947.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Historical lineage

The article situates the UN within a sequence of postwar settlements including the [[peace-of-westphalia|1648 Peace of Westphalia]], the [[congress-of-vienna|Congress of Vienna]] of 1815, and the League of Nations formed after the First World War.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) The author notes that such conferences were sometimes shaped by honest agendas and sometimes not, but that in all instances the parties agreed a new system was necessary to avoid a plunge into war.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## AI governance roadmap

In June 2020 the United Nations published a statement on [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] holding that AI "brings enormous benefits to the digital era, but it can also significantly compromise the safety and agency of users worldwide," and calling for enhanced multi-stakeholder efforts on global AI cooperation to build capacity for development and use that is trustworthy, human rights-based, safe and sustainable, and promotes peace.[3] The accompanying Roadmap put forward the Secretary-General's proposal to establish a multistakeholder advisory body on global AI cooperation addressing inclusion, coordination, and capacity-building, with discussion and consultations ongoing.[3]

Badlands Media's July 2023 essay treats this as the collaborative blueprint for all the relevant entities to work together on AI, noting that three years later every one of them — the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], [[mckinsey-and-company|McKinsey & Company]], and [[pwc|PwC]] among them — was proceeding full steam ahead, with no evidence of any pause in what the article calls the globalist machine.[3]

## US withdrawals and the dues dispute (2026)

In January 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] withdrew the United States from 66 international organizations, half of them UN bodies, including the climate change platform and other forums.[4] UN Secretary-General's spokesman Stephane Dujarric subsequently threatened to revoke the United States' right to vote on the grounds that Washington had not been paying its dues.[4] Badlands treated the exchange as evidence of the accelerating [[erosion-of-the-postwar-international-order|erosion of the postwar international order]].[4] Separately, Iran lodged a letter with the United Nations on the anniversary of the assassination of [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]].[4]

## Security Council session on Lebanon (April 2026)

At an April 2026 UN Security Council meeting, Pakistan delivered a sober rebuke of Israel, describing it as "an occupier that does not listen to anyone, that does not heed any advice, even from its friends—if there are any left," and demanded that Israel withdraw completely from Lebanon.[5] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that South Korea, historically a strong U.S.-aligned ally of Israel, gave Pakistan the floor at its request.[5] Separately, Italy's suspension of its defence memorandum with Israel followed Israeli warning shots fired at Italian troops serving with the UN peacekeeping mission UNIFIL in southern Lebanon.[5]

## Afghanistan cash shipment oversight (2023)

The United Nations played a central role in channelling U.S. assistance into Afghanistan after the country's collapse in August 2021. A November 2023 report by [[john-sopko|John Sopko]] of [[special-inspector-general-for-afghanistan-reconstruction|SIGAR]] recorded that the U.N. had assured him that all of the roughly $11 billion in aid was "placed in designated U.N. accounts in a private bank," and was not "deposited in the central bank or provided to the [[taliban|Taliban]]."[6] The U.N. Assistance Mission in Afghanistan (UNAMA) stated that the cash shipments — about $80 million every 10 to 14 days — were "carefully monitored, audited, inspected, and vetted in accordance with U.N. financial rules and processes."[6] SIGAR nonetheless warned that the Taliban had previously siphoned cash from U.N. shipments and charged royalties and fees on them.[6]

Separately, Russian foreign ministry spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]] invoked the U.N. nuclear watchdog in November 2023, asking where the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] and its inspectors were after an Israeli minister suggested nuclear weapons use in Gaza was "a possibility."[6]


## Israeli ban on the Secretary-General (October 2024)

On October 2, 2024, Israeli Foreign Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] declared Secretary-General [[antonio-guterres|António Guterres]] persona non grata and barred him from [[israel|Israel]] for failing to directly condemn [[iran|Iran]] over its 181-missile barrage the previous night.[7] Katz accused the UN chief of kowtowing to [[hamas|Hamas]], [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], the Houthis and Iran — "the mothership of global terror" — and of never designating Hamas a terrorist organization.[7] The move was expected to deepen the already wide rift between Israel and the organization; Germany, which had summoned Iran's envoy over the attack, called the declaration unhelpful.[7]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that while Israel had not technically banned the UN from its territory, that would become the narrative, and argued that although some in the Truth Community view the UN as the vehicle of a globalist one-world-government plot, the ban did not help the perception that the Israeli government was "descending into madness" and had become an international pariah.[7]

## One-sided resolutions on Israel (November 2023)

In November 2023 the watchdog group [[un-watch|UN Watch]] criticized the United Nations for passing multiple resolutions condemning [[israel|Israel]] for various alleged human rights violations while making no condemnation of [[hamas|Hamas]] or other groups or nations, at a time when antisemitism was on the rise.[8] UN Watch executive director Hillel Neuer called the "torrent of one-sided resolutions, just one month after the largest massacre of Jews since the Holocaust, and on the 85th anniversary of Kristallnacht" surreal, and said the only purpose of the eight lopsided condemnations was "to demonize the Jewish state."[8]

The UN's Second Committee, which handles economic and financial issues, announced the passage of three such resolutions, including one demanding that Israel cease the "exploitation, damage, cause of loss or depletion and endangerment" in the Golan Heights region of Syria.[8]

## Assessment of Gaza famine risk (January 2024)

By the start of January 2024, with aid delivery restricted and aid workers unable to move safely within the territory, the United Nations assessed that half of [[gaza|Gaza]]'s roughly 2.2 million people were at risk of starvation.[9] The assessment was reported alongside local health authority figures of more than 20,000 killed in Gaza since the war began, most of them civilians, as [[israel|Israel]] announced a [[israeli-troop-withdrawal-from-gaza-2024|partial troop drawdown]].[9]
