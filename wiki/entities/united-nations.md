# United Nations

The United Nations is the international body established after the Second World War, alongside the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods system]], as an attempt to build a framework capable of preventing another plunge into war.[1]

## The UN Charter

The UN Charter premises international law on the sacredness of national sovereignty, the non-military interference of one state into another, and mutual cooperation.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] contrasts this with the [[rules-based-international-order|rules-based international order]] invoked by unipolarists, and argues that any technocratic world government would require erasing or rewriting the Charter.[1] The discussions of the 2025 [[shanghai-cooperation-organisation|SCO]] summit and the broader [[multipolar-alliance|multipolar alliance]] are described as shaped around the common good and common security as outlined by the Charter.[1]

## FDR's conception

The UN as envisioned by Franklin Roosevelt or Henry Wallace was never meant to be a world government, but rather a platform for dialogue and the harmonization of security and economic interests among civilizations.[1] The universal freedoms of the [[four-freedoms|Four Freedoms]] were restated in the UN Declaration of Human Rights of 1947 and in the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence emerging from the 1955 Bandung Conference, which set the tone for the Non-Aligned Movement.[1]

## Historical lineage

The article places the UN in a sequence of peace settlements that includes the 1648 Peace of Westphalia, which emerged from the Thirty Years' War and established the modern sovereign nation-state system at the heart of international law; the 1815 Congress of Vienna, described as a weak attempt following two decades of Napoleonic wars; and the League of Nations after the First World War.[1] In all cases, the article notes, parties agreed a new system was necessary to avoid war, though the agendas behind such conferences were not always honest.[1]
