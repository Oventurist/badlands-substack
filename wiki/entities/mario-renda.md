# Mario Renda

Mario Renda was a deposit broker with mafia ties who, in the account of Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]], ran one of the largest money laundering operations of the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan crisis]] while remaining almost entirely unknown.[1]

Renda made deals with owners of thrift savings institutions to deposit large sums with them on condition that the money be lent only to people he designated — all of whom were other mafia figures.[1] Journalist Jonathan Kwitny reported that Renda directed about $5 billion per year of deposits into 130 thrifts, every single one of which later failed, with depositors having to be repaid their federally insured deposits soon after.[1]

Lange presents Renda as a demonstration of both the scale of the fraud and the way [[deposit-insurance|deposit insurance]] guaranteed that the public would absorb the losses: a man laundering $5 billion a year and responsible for 130 thrift failures whose name nobody knows.[1]
