# Richard Dearlove

Sir Richard Dearlove is a former head of [[british-intelligence|British Intelligence]], identified in transparency litigation as one of the foreign intelligence figures whose communications with American officials bear on the origins of the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]]/[[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate]] investigation.[1]

## Durham records litigation

[[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]]'s January 2026 Freedom of Information Act lawsuit against the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] seeks unredacted records of former Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s referrals to other federal agencies and communications involving Dearlove, U.S. intelligence officials, [[stefan-halper|Stefan Halper]] and [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]].[1] The broader request also encompasses [[john-brennan|John Brennan]]'s 2016 White House briefing notes and unredacted transcripts of Durham's interviews with figures including [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] and [[john-podesta|John Podesta]].[1]

## Promotion of the Wuhan lab leak hypothesis

In June 2020 Dearlove became a loud public proponent of the [[lab-leak-theory|Wuhan lab leak hypothesis]], a development treated by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as a signal that the theory was being steered rather than discovered.[2] Ehret describes him as a "byzantine shadow creature" who operated at the highest echelons of British intelligence and who understood the art of misdirection, noting that he was well aware of the [[pentagon|Pentagon's]] vast array of internationally extended bioweapons laboratories.[2]

The case against taking Dearlove's advocacy at face value rests on his record: he oversaw the "yellowcake" dodgy dossier that helped launch the Iraq war, knew of fallacious [[mi6|MI6]]-sponsored reports of nerve gases used by the governments of Libya and Syria, and had overseen major components of Russiagate as a color-revolutionary process inside the United States.[2] He also knew of the [[porton-down|Porton Down]] laboratories that manufactured the Novichok used in the Skripal Affair.[2]

Ehret concedes that, despite these alarm bells, no smoking-gun evidence for an alternative laboratory origin surfaced that could be taken to court, leaving Dearlove's operation with the upper hand because receipts from [[anthony-fauci|Fauci's]] [[national-institutes-of-health|NIH]] to the Wuhan lab did make headlines and functioned as a smoking gun in the minds of many.[2]
