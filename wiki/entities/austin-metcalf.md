# Austin Metcalf

Austin Metcalf was a Texas high school football star who was fatally stabbed at a track meet in April 2025, in a killing that became a nationally contested criminal case.[1] The teenager accused of the stabbing, [[karmelo-anthony|Karmelo Anthony]], was 17 years old at the time of the incident.[1]

## The case

Anthony was charged in connection with Metcalf's death and initially held on a $1 million bond, which was later reduced to $250,000 after his defense argued that the family faced financial difficulties.[1] He was released from jail on the reduced bond in mid-April 2025.[1]

Reporting cited in the Badlands News Brief noted that, following his release, Anthony was living with his family in a roughly $900,000 home inside the gated community of Richwoods in Frisco, Texas, where rent was estimated at about $3,500 a month, and that the family had reportedly purchased a new car — details that fueled public argument over the bond reduction and over the fundraising surrounding the defendant.[1]

## Significance in commentary

Badlands treated the Metcalf killing as a "bonus item" illustrating what its contributors describe as an asymmetry in how the press and activist fundraising networks treat violent crime depending on the identities of those involved, a theme it also applied to the [[kilmar-abrego-garcia|Kilmar Abrego Garcia]] deportation controversy in the same brief.[1]

## Indictment of the accused (June 2025)

On Tuesday, June 24, 2025, Collin County District Attorney [[greg-willis|Greg Willis]] announced that a grand jury had indicted [[karmelo-anthony|Karmelo Sincere Anthony]] for first-degree murder in Metcalf's April 2, 2025 stabbing death at a high school track meet in Frisco, Texas.[2] Both boys were 17 at the time of the incident, and the Texas criminal justice system treats 17-year-olds as adults.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that since Metcalf's death the public had been "gaslit" into believing his attacker was the true victim and the case an example of institutionalized racism, and asked how that must have felt to Metcalf's family, especially the twin brother who held him as he died.[2] She noted that CCTV footage of the event had finally been released days earlier and disputed the widespread narrative, showing the stabber as the aggressor, and that legal experts believed the video undercut the defense.[2] She also observed that more than half a million dollars had been raised for Anthony.[2]
