# Terry Doughty

Terry Doughty is a U.S. district judge, nominated to the bench by former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who issued the July 2023 injunction restricting federal government contacts with social media companies in the case commonly known as [[missouri-v-biden|Missouri v. Biden]].[1]

## Social media injunction

In a 155-page opinion, Doughty held that federal officials likely violated the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] in how they had communicated with [[twitter|Twitter]], [[meta-platforms|Meta]], and other technology companies about removing or restricting content related to the pandemic, the 2020 election, and other political subjects.[1] The ruling would block federal agencies and officials from communicating with social media platforms about content on those platforms.[1] The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] filed a notice of appeal with the [[fifth-circuit-court-of-appeals|5th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals]] in New Orleans on Wednesday, July 5, 2023, saying it was appealing the ruling that restricted the Biden administration's efforts to "stem the spread of misinformation."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that the Biden administration's public disdain for the First Amendment was "a massive red pill for the American public," while Doughty's decision was "a massive white pill."[1] He argued that the more the Deep State thrashed publicly against the Constitution, the more it revealed itself, while the constitutional judges Trump installed — from the Supreme Court down — continued to erect bulwarks against further incursions on rights.[1]

Separately, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the judge in the same case had denied the plaintiffs' motion to enjoin and constrain the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] from colluding with social media companies to censor speech, meaning the agency could still work with Twitter to ban users sharing dissenting medical studies.[1]

## Kennedy censorship ruling (2024)

In an order issued Tuesday, August 20, 2024, Doughty found that [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] could proceed with his censorship lawsuit against the Biden administration despite the Supreme Court's June 2024 decision denying standing to state and individual plaintiffs in the related case.[2] Doughty concluded that Kennedy satisfied the standard the Court had set, pointing to "ample evidence" that he had previously been censored at the direction of government actors and a "substantial risk" that such censorship would continue.[2] He summarized the [[murthy-v-missouri|Murthy v. Missouri]] test as requiring "specific findings that a particular defendant pressured a particular platform to censor a particular topic before the platform suppressed a particular plaintiff's speech on that topic."[2]

## Kennedy v. Biden injunction (February 2024)

In February 2024 Doughty granted a preliminary injunction to [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] in his class action alleging federal censorship of vaccine-related speech, while staying the order until the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] ruled in the related case brought by Missouri and Louisiana.[3] He held an injunction was warranted because Kennedy had shown he was likely to succeed on the merits of his claims.[3]

Doughty found that the [[white-house|White House]] defendants, the Surgeon General defendants, the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] defendants, the [[fbi|FBI]] defendants and the [[cisa|Cybersecurity & Infrastructure Security Agency]] defendants had likely violated the Free Speech Clause of the First Amendment.[3] He concluded that these defendants "jointly participated in the actions of the social media" companies by "'insinuating' themselves into the social-media companies' private affairs and blurring the line between public and private action."[3] The suit, brought with health care professional Connie Sampognaro and [[childrens-health-defense|Children's Health Defense]], alleged the government induced Facebook, Google (YouTube) and X to suppress statements that might make people hesitant about COVID vaccine mandates.[3]

## Fallout from the July 4, 2023 ruling

Doughty's July 4, 2023 order granting a temporary restraining order against the federal government produced immediate operational effects: the following day the [[state-department|State Department]] cancelled its regular monthly meeting with [[facebook|Facebook]] on 2024 election preparations and hacking threats, and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] filed notice of appeal within hours.[4] In his ruling Doughty characterised the federal government's documented conduct as a massive attack on the First Amendment, possibly the most extensive in the nation's history, and found that the plaintiff attorneys general "have produced evidence of a massive effort by Defendants, from the [[white-house|White House]] to federal agencies, to suppress speech based on its content."[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that mainstream coverage framing the "Trump appointed judge" as a threat to national security inadvertently conceded the existence of the censorship apparatus it defended.[4]
