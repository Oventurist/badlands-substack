# Terry Doughty

Terry Doughty is a U.S. district judge, nominated to the bench by former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who issued the July 2023 injunction restricting federal government contacts with social media companies in the case commonly known as [[missouri-v-biden|Missouri v. Biden]].[1]

## Social media injunction

In a 155-page opinion, Doughty held that federal officials likely violated the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] in how they had communicated with [[twitter|Twitter]], [[meta-platforms|Meta]], and other technology companies about removing or restricting content related to the pandemic, the 2020 election, and other political subjects.[1] The ruling would block federal agencies and officials from communicating with social media platforms about content on those platforms.[1] The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] filed a notice of appeal with the [[fifth-circuit-court-of-appeals|5th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals]] in New Orleans on Wednesday, July 5, 2023, saying it was appealing the ruling that restricted the Biden administration's efforts to "stem the spread of misinformation."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that the Biden administration's public disdain for the First Amendment was "a massive red pill for the American public," while Doughty's decision was "a massive white pill."[1] He argued that the more the Deep State thrashed publicly against the Constitution, the more it revealed itself, while the constitutional judges Trump installed — from the Supreme Court down — continued to erect bulwarks against further incursions on rights.[1]

Separately, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the judge in the same case had denied the plaintiffs' motion to enjoin and constrain the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] from colluding with social media companies to censor speech, meaning the agency could still work with Twitter to ban users sharing dissenting medical studies.[1]
