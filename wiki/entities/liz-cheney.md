# Liz Cheney

Liz Cheney is a former Republican member of the U.S. House of Representatives from Wyoming who served as Vice Chair of the House select committee investigating the events of January 6, 2021.[1] Daughter of former Vice President [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney]], she became one of the most prominent Republican critics of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the public face of the committee's prime-time hearings.[1]

## Role on the January 6 Committee

According to reporting by The Federalist cited in the Badlands News Brief, Cheney's [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]] suppressed evidence that President Trump had pushed for 10,000 National Guard troops to protect the nation's capital, a fact contained in a previously hidden transcript.[1] The committee publicly claimed it had "no evidence" to support Trump officials' assertions that the White House had communicated a desire for 10,000 National Guard troops, even though an early transcribed interview conducted by the committee contained precisely that evidence from a key source.[1] Cheney attended and personally participated in that interview, which was withheld from public release.[1]

The interview in question was the January 28, 2022 transcribed session with Deputy Chief of Staff [[anthony-ornato|Anthony Ornato]], who told Cheney and committee investigators that he overheard White House Chief of Staff [[mark-meadows|Mark Meadows]] urge Washington, D.C. Mayor [[muriel-bowser|Muriel Bowser]] to request as many National Guard troops as she needed.[1] Ornato also testified that Trump had suggested 10,000 troops would be needed to keep the peace at the rallies and protests scheduled for January 6, 2021.[1]

## Preliminary findings of the House Administration Subcommittee on Oversight

Preliminary findings released by the [[house-administration-subcommittee-on-oversight|House Administration Subcommittee on Oversight]] in March 2024 were described by Badlands writers as damning for Cheney.[1] Among the key findings: the committee hired Hollywood producers to assist with its prime-time hearings; it refused to adopt rules, allowing it to operate without limits; and Cheney was not the minority ranking member but served as Vice Chair, a position reserved for the majority under House rules, having been appointed by Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] as one of her eight majority appointments.[1] As a result, the minority party was not meaningfully represented on the committee.[1]

The findings further alleged that the committee's leadership, including Cheney, failed to turn over evidence used in the prime-time hearings, hid multiple transcribed interviews and other evidence contradicting its narrative, promoted [[cassidy-hutchinson|Cassidy Hutchinson]]'s sensational testimony despite its being directly contradicted by the Secret Service agent driving the presidential SUV, and colluded with Georgia prosecutor [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] on her prosecution of Trump.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that "November 3 was a coup, J6 was a cover-up of the coup, and the J6 Committee was the cover-up of the cover-up," calling for accountability beginning with Pelosi, [[bennie-thompson|Bennie Thompson]], and Yogananda Pittman, as well as Cheney and [[adam-kinzinger|Adam Kinzinger]].[1]

## Presidential Citizens Medal and threatened FBI probe (January 2025)

On January 2, 2025, the Washington Post reported that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would award Cheney and nineteen others the [[presidential-citizens-medal|Presidential Citizens Medal]] for purportedly performing "exemplary deeds of service for their country or fellow Americans."[2] The award came as Cheney braced for a potential [[fbi|FBI]] probe, after House Republicans demanded the Bureau investigate her for allegedly tampering with "at least one" witness before the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]], where she served as vice chair.[2] President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s nominee for FBI director, [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]], had not signaled whether he was open to investigating her.[2] Cheney maintained that she should not go to jail for alleged wrongdoing during her tenure on the panel, while a Rasmussen Reports poll found 57 percent of likely voters supported an FBI investigation of her.[2]

## 2024 campaign appearances with Kamala Harris

In the closing weeks of the 2024 campaign, Cheney appeared alongside Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] in the crucial Milwaukee suburbs, where the pair sat side by side and delivered what Politico described as compelling political sermons.[3] The event was explicitly aimed at moderates and onetime Republicans still up for grabs, but the magazine judged that the rhetoric was better suited to the already-converted audience wearing Lincoln Project apparel.[3]

Together the two criticized [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s lack of decency, his disregard for the Constitution and the rule of law, asked attendees to imagine him without guardrails, said allies abroad were nervous, and recounted in detail what Trump did and did not do on January 6.[3] Politico concluded that the discussion was backward-looking and Trump-focused — "all about yesterday and him rather than tomorrow and her" — and unlikely to move the small number of genuinely undecided voters.[3]

## Invoked as a cautionary example against Massie (2025)

In March 2025 Cheney's political fate became rhetorical shorthand in President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s attacks on Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]].[4] In a Truth Social post declaring that Massie "SHOULD BE PRIMARIED," Trump wrote that the Kentucky congressman "reminds me of Liz Chaney(sic) before her historic, record breaking fall (loss!)."[4] Coverage noted that Cheney was one of the ten House Republicans who voted to impeach Trump following the January 6, 2021 Capitol riot, and that Trump-endorsed primary challenger Harriet Hageman handily defeated her in the 2022 Republican primary for Wyoming's at-large congressional district before winning the general election and being re-elected in 2024.[4] Cheney, described as a vociferous Trump critic, supported then-Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] in the 2024 presidential election.[4]

## Endorsement of Kamala Harris (September 2024)

Speaking at Duke University on September 4, 2024, the former Wyoming congresswoman said she would vote for Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] in November, according to audio obtained by CNN.[5] She stressed the importance of voting for Harris in states like North Carolina, saying, "I don't believe that we have the luxury of writing in candidates' names, particularly in swing states," and made the announcement in that battleground state deliberately, according to a source close to her.[5] "And as a conservative, as someone who believes in and cares about the Constitution, I have thought deeply about this, and because of the danger that Donald Trump poses, not only am I not voting for Donald Trump, but I will be voting for Kamala Harris," she said.[5] The Harris campaign highlighted the endorsement and pointed to other Republicans backing her, including former Illinois Rep. [[adam-kinzinger|Adam Kinzinger]] and former Georgia Lt. Gov. Geoff Duncan.[5]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contrasted Cheney's remarks with President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s joking endorsement of Harris the same week, calling Cheney the "Wyoming Witch" and writing that "the Uniparty grifters are finally ripping off their masks," with the Republican Party "as big of a joke as the Democrat Party."[5]

## Considered for a preemptive pardon (December 2024)

In December 2024, Politico reported that senior aides to President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] were debating whether to issue [[preemptive-pardons|preemptive pardons]] to current and former officials who could be targeted after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s return to office, and Cheney was named among those who could face exposure.[6] The reporting grouped her with members of Congress' [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]] such as Senator-elect [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]], noting that Trump had previously said Cheney "should go to Jail along with the rest of the Unselect Committee!"[6] Badlands contributor Burning Bright argued that offering pardons to figures such as Cheney and [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] served to seed the "justice phase" in the public mind, and that accepting a pardon would amount to a de facto admission of guilt before any charge was brought.[6]

## Third-party bid consideration and "Oath and Honor" (December 2023)

In an interview with the [[washington-post|Washington Post]] published Monday, December 4, 2023, Cheney said she was weighing a third-party presidential bid against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], while issuing dire claims that his return to power in 2024 could mean the end of American democracy.[7] "Several years ago, I would not have contemplated a third-party run … I happen to think democracy is at risk at home, obviously, as a result of Donald Trump's continued grip on the Republican Party, and I think democracy is at risk internationally as well," she said.[7] The following day she released the book [[oath-and-honor|"Oath and Honor: A Memoir and a Warning"]], which focuses on the fallout of the 2020 race and the events around January 6 and describes Trump as "the most dangerous man ever to inhabit the Oval Office."[7]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] responded that Cheney "should be in prison for engaging in a coup (Nov 3), cover-up of the coup (J6), and cover-up of the cover-up of the coup (J6 committee)," recalling that Cheney lost her 2022 primary to [[harriet-hageman|Harriett Hageman]], whom she praised as an impressive America First leader.[7] Ashe argued it was implausible that a candidate unable to win her own House district primary could meaningfully affect Trump's 2024 general-election chances, and suggested instead that a Cheney run would function as "plausible deniability for election fraud."[7] Citing the work of [[draza-smith|Draza Smith]], who discovered Edison Zero, she wrote that third-party candidates are useful to the regime by masking real-time vote manipulation on election night, and that two such candidates would make this easier while supplying narrative cover about voters defecting from the two-party system — defections she characterized as being from the uniparty, and therefore votes for Trump or possibly [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|RFK]].[7] She closed by quipping that the only people who would seriously consider Cheney were [[ron-desantis|Ron!]] supporters who did not think [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] went far enough — "All five of them."[7]

In Badlands commentary on the [[2023-trump-cpac-speech|2023 Trump CPAC speech]], contributor [[brad-getz|Brad Getz]] cited "the Liz Cheneys of the world" as figures who had been exposed as part of the establishment wing of the [[republican-party|Republican Party]], arguing that their political defeat demonstrated that "there is no going back" to the pre-2016 party.[9]

## Loudermilk referral and immunity questions (December 2024)

At the end of December 2024, ZeroHedge reported that Cheney appeared to be preparing to fight any federal or congressional probe into her role as vice chairman of the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Select Committee]].[8] The piece argued that few people had worked harder over the preceding years to put [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] behind bars than the former Wyoming congresswoman, casting her pursuit as revenge for Trump's longtime criticism of her father's "weapons of mass destruction" claims, and noting her frequent insistence that "no one is above the law."[8]

Text messages obtained by Representative [[barry-loudermilk|Barry Loudermilk]], chair of the House subcommittee examining the J6 committee, were said to show that Cheney had colluded behind the scenes with star witness [[cassidy-hutchinson|Cassidy Hutchinson]], who dramatically changed her testimony after connecting with Cheney.[8] The report characterized the communications as potentially amounting to witness tampering, subornation of perjury, and obstruction, noting that every former White House official — including the driver of the presidential vehicle on January 6 — had refuted Hutchinson's account of Trump's behavior that day.[8] Based on his ongoing inquiry, Loudermilk determined that "numerous federal laws were likely broken by Liz Cheney" and called for the FBI to investigate her.[8]
