# Vancouver Rape Relief

Vancouver Rape Relief (VRR), located in the province of British Columbia, is Canada's oldest rape crisis shelter and, according to reporting cited by Badlands, the only known single-sex shelter in the country despite Canada's vast size.[1]

## 2019 defunding

In 2019, Vancouver Rape Relief was stripped of its city funding after asserting that it had a right to provide services exclusively to females.[1] Transgender activists successfully lobbied the Vancouver city government to make the shelter's funding contingent on accepting males who identified as transgender.[1]

## Significance in the 2025 Edmonton shelter case

The VRR case was invoked in Badlands commentary on the January 2025 charging of a trans-identified male in Edmonton, Alberta, over the alleged sexual assault of multiple women at a women's shelter.[1] Badlands host [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the defunding of VRR illustrated a Canadian policy environment in which demanding women-only spaces is treated as transphobic, leaving women without safe single-sex refuge.[1]

## The Kimberly Nixon complaint

A February 2024 Badlands Media essay by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] dates the Canadian conflict over women-only spaces to February 2007, when [[kimberly-nixon|Kimberly Nixon]], a transgender person, asked to work as a peer rape counsellor at the shelter — then styled the Vancouver Rape Relief & Women's Shelter Society — and accused it of discrimination when refused.[2] The society was ultimately found not guilty of discrimination on the grounds of freedom of association, which allowed it to operate as a women-only venue regardless of gender identification.[2]

The essay argues the verdict was rendered moot in practice: in retaliation for the shelter's refusal to permit a biological man to counsel women on rape trauma, the City of Vancouver withdrew municipal funds because the shelter did not satisfy its trans equity and inclusion requirements, and the shelter subsequently closed.[2] The case is presented as an early illustration of [[gender-ideology|gender ideology]] overriding legal outcomes through funding and administrative pressure — a form of [[lawfare|lawfare]].[2]
