# M23

M23 is an armed rebel movement operating in the eastern [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]], and a member of the [[congo-river-alliance|Congo River Alliance]] (AFC) coalition that signed a framework peace agreement with Kinshasa on November 15, 2025 in Doha.[1]

## Territorial control and humanitarian impact

M23 has violently taken over areas of the North and South Kivu provinces around the cities of Bukavu and Goma.[1] The [[united-nations|United Nations]] reported in November 2025 that the DR Congo faces a starvation crisis with 25 million people in acute hunger, concentrated in precisely those M23-held areas.[1]

## Badlands characterization

Badlands commentary describes M23 as a terrorist group operating out of [[rwanda|Rwanda]] — whose president, [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]], is characterized as an ardent Zionist and Israel ally — and asserts the group was likely dispatched by the Israeli diamond cartel to reclaim mines lost after the sanctioning of [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]].[1] The framework agreement it signed lays out eight protocols to be negotiated, including an immediate cessation of hostilities, disengagement of forces, humanitarian access, conditions for the return of displaced civilians, and the "full restoration of state authority over the entire national territory," including rebel-held areas.[1]

## Capture of Uvira (December 2025)

On Wednesday, 10 December 2025, M23 fighters entered the city of [[uvira|Uvira]] shortly before midday and took control without a fight, according to local media, intensifying their offensive in the mineral-rich east despite a high-profile US-brokered peace agreement signed the previous week.[2] Uvira, on Lake Tanganyika near the [[burundi|Burundi]] border, had been an important military and administrative base for Kinshasa in South Kivu following the capture of the provincial capital, Bukavu, earlier that year.[2] The Congolese government said more than 100 people had been killed, over 200,000 newly displaced, and thousands more forced to flee in a "generalized offensive" along the Kamanyola–Uvira axis conducted by Rwanda's Defense Forces and the rebels, adding that "tactical weapons, notably suicide drones," had been used against civilians.[2]

Badlands analysis placed the offensive in a sequence beginning with the seizure of Goma in December 2024 and Bukavu in February 2025, arguing that the purpose of taking these cities is to control the facilities where raw ore from the region's rare earth deposits — valued at an estimated $24 trillion — is refined into metals sold to Fortune 500 companies.[2] According to that account the minerals are mined with child slave labour, smuggled into [[rwanda|Rwanda]] after refining, and issued forged certificates marking them as cleanly sourced.[2] The capture of Uvira is widely suspected to make traceability of these minerals effectively impossible, with the entire North and South Kivu provinces under Rwandan control.[2]

[[apple|Apple]] has been singled out for knowingly participating in the scheme; the company is the subject of a civil action brought by the government of the DR Congo and is under criminal investigation by European authorities, who have already arrested and jailed commodity executives from firms such as [[glencore|Glencore]] and [[trafigura|Trafigura]].[2] Commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] and allied interests lost control of the region's minerals through President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]], signed in December 2017, and suspected M23 and its allies of acting as agents of the Israeli diamond cartel seeking to recover them.[2]

## Withdrawal from the Angola peace talks (March 2025)

In March 2025 the Rwanda-backed M23 armed group announced it would withdraw from peace talks set for Tuesday in Angola, where it had planned direct negotiations with the government of the Democratic Republic of the Congo to end the conflict in the country's east.[3] The withdrawal was announced through the [[alliance-fleuve-congo|Congo River Alliance]] of rebel groups, which said it was pulling out because of sanctions imposed earlier that day by the [[european-union|European Union]] against M23 and Rwandan officials.[3] The coalition stated that the EU actions were aimed at "obstructing the much-anticipated talks."[3]

Official figures cited at the time recorded at least 8,500 people killed in the country since clashes between the rebels and Congolese forces began at the start of the year, including children and international peacekeepers.[3] Badlands commentary described M23 as funded by [[rwanda|Rwanda]] under President [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]] and argued that the towns M23 was seizing along the border host the processing plants for the region's rare earth minerals, deposits valued in excess of $24 trillion.[3]

## Forced recruitment allegations (September 2025)

In September 2025 the Congolese government accused M23 of serious human rights violations, including the abduction and forced recruitment of young people.[4] Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Interior and Security [[jacquemain-shabani|Jacquemain Shabani]] told a press conference in Kinshasa that daily abuses were occurring against civilians in the conflict-hit North and South Kivu provinces, and said that "abductions and kidnappings of young people continue, to forcibly recruit them into armed movements."[4]

Major Nestor Mavudisa, spokesperson for the Congolese army's third defense zone, said the rebels had "arrested and detained several young people, including some minors, whom they sometimes use as trackers, but also as a human shield."[4] The accusations came weeks after government representatives and rebels signed a declaration in Doha, Qatar, setting a timeline under which negotiations would begin on August 8 and a peace deal be finalized by August 18.[4]

Badlands commentary framed the episode as the "Israeli Diamond Cartel's proxy militia" violating a peace deal brokered by [[donald-trump|President Trump]] while conscripting kidnapped youths, and asked pointedly why the [[state-department|State Department]] was not enforcing the agreement.[4]
