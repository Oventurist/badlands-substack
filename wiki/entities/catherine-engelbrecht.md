# Catherine Engelbrecht

Catherine Engelbrecht is the founder and public face of [[true-the-vote|True the Vote]], the conservative election-integrity organization whose mass challenges to voter eligibility in Georgia became the subject of a multi-year federal lawsuit.[1]

## The Fair Fight litigation

Engelbrecht was a named defendant in the suit brought by Fair Fight, the liberal voting-rights group that alleged True the Vote's challenges to the eligibility of hundreds of thousands of Georgia voters ahead of the January 2021 U.S. Senate runoffs amounted to unlawful voter intimidation under Section 11(b) of the [[voting-rights-act-of-1965|Voting Rights Act]].[1] On January 2, 2024, Judge Steve C. Jones of the United States District Court for the Northern District of Georgia issued a 145-page opinion rejecting Fair Fight's claims and directing the clerk to enter judgment in favor of the defendants.[1]

The ruling was not a wholesale endorsement of Engelbrecht personally. Jones wrote that the court "maintains its prior concerns" about how True the Vote pursued its challenges, and singled Engelbrecht out for criticism of her credibility, noting that she "was impeached a number of times at trial" and that even where her prior testimony was not "blatantly inconsistent" with her trial testimony, "many of Engelbrecht's skillful answers were obviously self-serving—and to the detriment of her overall candor."[1] The court nonetheless found no violation of Section 11(b) by any named defendant.[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

Badlands Media commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the decision as a First Amendment victory, arguing that whatever the court's personal assessment of Engelbrecht and her collaborator [[gregg-phillips|Gregg Phillips]], their conduct was lawful and vindicated as two distinct efforts "aimed at the same purpose (i.e., reduce voter fraud and pursue election integrity)."[1] She noted the irony that the ruling came from an Obama-appointed judge in Georgia and announced that she and co-host Brian would break down the case on the program *Why We Vote*.[1]

## Statement on the Georgia GBI investigation

In a statement to Badlands Media published in February 2026, Engelbrecht disputed the account given by former [[georgia-bureau-of-investigation|GBI]] Director [[vic-reynolds|Vic Reynolds]] of how [[true-the-vote|True the Vote]]'s Georgia [[ballot-harvesting|ballot harvesting]] material was handled.[2] She wrote that the group met Reynolds and his team once, in the Governor's office in early 2021, walked them through its initial findings, and then "made repeated attempts to re-engage with the GBI and never received a response."[2]

Engelbrecht said the FBI offered to host a secure database so the GBI and other law enforcement agencies could access the information and preserve chain of custody across the states involved, while stating clearly that it was not conducting an investigation or collecting evidence.[2] She said the next time Reynolds surfaced was when a letter he had addressed to True the Vote appeared in the Atlanta Journal-Constitution, which the organization had not received at the time of publication; it later responded directly to the Governor.[2] She rejected the claim that the matter stalled because the group failed to name an informant, writing, "We were shut out early, and as public pressure mounted, the focus shifted to blaming us rather than examining the evidence."[2]
