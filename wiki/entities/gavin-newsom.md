# Gavin Newsom

Gavin Newsom is the Governor of California and a prominent national Democratic figure frequently discussed in Badlands coverage of Democratic Party politics.[1]

## February 2026 book tour controversy

Newsom faced criticism after a video clip from a book tour stop in Atlanta showed him telling Atlanta Mayor Andre Dickens and a reportedly majority-black audience, "I'm like you … I'm no better than you. I'm a 960 SAT guy."[1] In the remarks he referenced his low SAT score and his past struggles with dyslexia, claiming "you'll never see me read a speech, because I can't read a speech."[1]

As the clip circulated on social media, critics unearthed past video of Newsom bragging about his reading ability.[1] Newsom and his office rejected the criticism as manufactured outrage, noting that he has publicly discussed his dyslexia and test scores for years.[1]

## Response to Sean Hannity

On Monday, February 23, 2026, Newsom posted a profanity-laden reply to Fox News host Sean Hannity on X: "You didn't give a shit about the President of the United States of America posting an ape video of President Obama or calling African nations shitholes — but you're going to call me racist for talking about my lifelong struggle with dyslexia?" He added, "Spare me your fake fucking outrage, Sean."[1] Republican pundits used the episode to spotlight what they characterized as Democratic double standards on matters of race.[1]

## DOJ investigation (June 2026)

In June 2026 Newsom announced that California was facing a [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] investigation and characterized the probe as politically motivated.[2] He said the state intended to challenge the federal action while seeking additional details about the scope of the inquiry.[2] The [[white-house|White House]] said there were multiple investigations into Newsom and his wife, including with regard to their taxes.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated Newsom's complaint as part of a broader pattern of officials invoking [[weaponization-of-government|weaponized government]], noting sardonically that a governor allegedly cheating on his taxes is a curious candidate for the label while affirming the presumption of innocence.[2]

## 2028 Democratic power rankings

Axios' July 2026 Democratic "power rankings" named Newsom one of the three strongest early contenders for the party's 2028 presidential nomination, alongside former Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] and Representative [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]].[3] The list was assembled from interviews with strategists, donors, elected officials, and party insiders and weighed influence, fundraising strength, and national profile, while noting the field remained fluid as other governors and senators considered bids.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the trio as evidence that Democrats were not fielding competent contenders.[3]

## March 2026: response to Iran drone-threat bulletin

After an [[fbi|FBI]] bulletin circulated to California law enforcement warned that [[iran|Iran]] had aspired to launch a drone attack on the US West Coast, Newsom and other California officials said in March 2026 that there was no known imminent threat.[4] State authorities said they remained in close coordination with federal agencies and were monitoring the situation.[4]

## Weaponization fund tax strategy (2026)
[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] identified Newsom as pursuing a strategy similar to Senator [[michael-bennet|Michael Bennet]]'s regarding the Justice Department [[weaponization-fund|weaponization fund]] — proposals to tax weaponization disbursements at 100 percent so that recipients such as [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] could not benefit from them.[5]

## Reedley biolab funding, 2023

Reporting cited in the August 2023 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] stated that Newsom, as California's Democratic governor, had helped fund the Chinese-linked COVID biolab discovered the previous month in Fresno County, meaning its existence likely came as no surprise to him.[6] The unlicensed facility, housed in a warehouse owned by [[prestige-biotech|Prestige Biotech]], contained a stockpile of "infectious agents" including coronavirus, nearly a thousand dead lab mice, and vials of unidentified biological fluids.[6] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that a Russian lab in Florida would have drawn ferocious media treatment of Governor DeSantis, and found it baffling that Newsom could be "caught funding potential bioterrorism in cahoots CCP and have zero consequences," predicting the mainstream press would bury the story while any 2024 opponent would be foolish to pass up the campaign-ad opportunity.[6]

## August 2024 response to Trump

In August 2024, Newsom joined Representative [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] and others in mocking former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] after Trump posted on Truth Social that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] might crash the Democratic National Convention and attempt to reclaim the nomination he had ceded to [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[7] Newsom replied on X with the words, "Oh, he's scared."[7] Schiff answered separately with "It's ok to be scared," and two Republican anti-Trump organizations, Republican Voters Against Trump and The Lincoln Project, also went after Trump over the post.[7]

## June 2025 Los Angeles deployment dispute

During the June 2025 protests over immigration enforcement raids in Los Angeles, Newsom said President Donald Trump was escalating the situation by threatening the deployment of Marines into the city.[8] The federal response placed roughly 2,000 [[california-army-national-guard|California Army National Guard]] soldiers under federal command to protect federal personnel and property, and put about 500 [[united-states-marine-corps|Marines]] from Twentynine Palms in a prepared-to-deploy status.[8] See [[2025-los-angeles-immigration-enforcement-protests|the 2025 Los Angeles immigration enforcement protests]].[8]

### Restraining order bid and "Democracy at a Crossroads" address

Newsom sued Trump, Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] and the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]], seeking a temporary restraining order to block the federalizing of the National Guard and the deployment of U.S. Marines, with nearly 5,000 troops on the ground in Los Angeles by June 10, 2025.[10] [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] lawyers responded that the motion was "legally meritless" and sought "an extraordinary, unprecedented, and dangerous court order" that would jeopardize [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] personnel and interfere with federal operations.[10] U.S. District Judge [[charles-breyer|Charles Breyer]] granted the administration's request for roughly 24 hours to file a response brief, denying Newsom the same-day ruling he had sought and setting argument in open court for Thursday at 1:30 p.m. Pacific.[10]

That evening Newsom delivered a televised address, billed as "Democracy at a Crossroads," to nearly 40 million Californians and a national audience, condemning what he called Trump's unlawful militarization of Los Angeles.[10] "What we're witnessing is not law enforcement — it's authoritarianism," he said. "What Donald Trump wants most is your fealty. Your silence. To be complicit in this moment. Do not give in to him."[10] He recounted federal raids in Latino neighborhoods, the commandeering of 4,000 California National Guard members and the deployment of more than 700 active-duty Marines without consultation with state or local officials, describing it as "a military dragnet across Los Angeles" and "weakness masquerading as strength."[10] Urging peaceful action, he said, "The most important office in a democracy is not President or Governor — it's citizen."[10]

The [[white-house|White House]] responded that Newsom and Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] should instead be thanking Trump for removing criminal illegal immigrants from Los Angeles streets.[10]

## 2024 Biden replacement speculation

Following the February 2024 [[hur-report|Hur report]], Newsom was repeatedly named among the figures most likely to replace President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] atop the Democratic ticket.[9] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] forecast a contested [[2024-democratic-national-convention|Democratic National Convention]] producing a Newsom, [[michelle-obama|Michelle Obama]], or [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] candidacy rather than the clean pre-convention ouster predicted by [[nigel-farage|Nigel Farage]].[9] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] welcomed the prospect, describing Newsom and [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]] as "two of the most-reviled political figures in the United States, whose tyranny was highlighted more than any other during the Covid era."[9]

## Beijing trip, October 2023

Newsom's meeting with Chinese leader [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] in Beijing in late October 2023 — billed as a summit addressing climate change and the fentanyl crisis — coincided with news that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would not appear on the New Hampshire Democratic primary ballot.[12] Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the two events together, predicting roughly a month until Biden was out and "Greasy Gavin" became the Democratic nominee.[12]

## $25 million anti-Trump legal infusion (January 2025)

On the same day in January 2025 that outgoing Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] affirmed that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had defeated her fairly and presided over the certification of his victory, Newsom spent 45 minutes laying out his topline budget priorities, touting a $332 billion budget without a deficit after two years of tens of billions in shortfalls — though little was said afterward about how those deficits had been erased.[13] Just days after the election, Newsom had anointed himself the leader of the anti-Trump resistance, convened a special legislative session, and announced that he wanted to boost the state Justice Department's budget by $25 million to wage legal fights with the incoming administration.[13]

Badlands writer Burning Bright described this as "the seed," noting that in the midst of a humanitarian and economic crisis that may or may not have been engineered — the [[january-2025-los-angeles-wildfires|January 2025 Los Angeles wildfires]] — California residents were being told their governor wanted $25 million more to sue the incoming Trump administration, and asking whether the sequence was organic exposure or planned demolition.[13]

## Position on Trump ballot access (December 2023)

During the December 2023 ballot-access fight, Newsom broke with fellow California Democrats who sought to remove [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the state's 2024 primary ballot, stating: "There is no doubt that Donald Trump is a threat to our liberties & even to our democracy, but in CA we defeat candidates we don't like at the polls …Everything else is a political distraction."[14] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] credited Newsom and his executive team with "the right view of the ballot access drama," arguing that elections belong to the people and that bureaucrats should never be able to unilaterally neutralise their political opposition — a criticism she directed at Maine.[14] She also questioned his motives, noting that he was a popular choice to replace [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] as the Democratic candidate and asking whether he wanted to run against Trump or merely to appear fair and uncorrupted to the more than 68 percent of likely US voters most concerned about corruption's impact on democracy.[14] Secretary of State [[shirley-weber|Shirley Weber]] ultimately certified Trump on the ballot on 28 December 2023, rejecting the request from Lieutenant Governor [[eleni-kounalakis|Eleni Kounalakis]].[14]

## 2023 debate with DeSantis

A televised debate between Newsom and Florida governor Ron DeSantis was scheduled for November 30, 2023, and previewed as a contest in which neither man was really debating the other so much as auditioning for the nomination within his own party.[15] In this reading, slights and barbs aimed at the opponent were designed to impress voters in the taunter's own party rather than win over the general electorate, with DeSantis targeting the early states of Iowa and New Hampshire.[15] Newsom, by contrast, had to assume that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would sail through the first primaries and accumulate enough delegates after Super Tuesday to secure the nomination, meaning he was effectively auditioning before the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]] as a potential Biden replacement — and in particular had to show he was preferable to Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], who was seen unfavourably over verbal gaffes and an apparent lack of leadership skills.[15] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] dismissed the matchup as a battle "to determine who wears the skin of false reality projection more unconvincingly within their respective dying political parties," predicting it would damage both.[15]

## 2025 redistricting counterpunch and National Guard litigation

In August 2025, Newsom and former House Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] announced that California would hold a special election in early November to place a new congressional map before voters as a ballot measure, a plan projected to gain Democrats five seats and to temporarily bypass the state's independent redistricting commission ahead of the 2026 elections.[16] "We are talking about emergency measures to respond to what's happening in Texas and we will nullify what happens in Texas," Newsom said at a Friday press conference flanked by California lawmakers and Texas Democrats who had fled their state.[16] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] wrote that Newsom was "staging a fake election to give himself cover for redistricting."[16] See [[2025-california-redistricting-counterpunch|2025 California Redistricting Counterpunch]].

Separately, lawyers for Newsom and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were set to face off in a California courtroom on Monday, August 11, 2025, over whether the president violated a 147-year-old law when he deployed the [[national-guard|National Guard]] to quell protests over immigration raids in Los Angeles against the governor's wishes.[16] In June, as hundreds gathered in Los Angeles to protest immigration raids that left dozens detained or deported, Trump federalized and deployed 4,000 National Guard members over the objections of Newsom and local officials, who said the deployment would only cause further chaos; Trump invoked a rarely used law permitting federalization during actual or threatened rebellion or invasion, or when regular forces cannot enforce US laws.[16]

## Ninth Circuit stay in the National Guard case (June 2025)

Newsom's legal victory over the federalization of the [[california-army-national-guard|California National Guard]] proved short-lived. After U.S. District Judge [[charles-breyer|Charles Breyer]] ruled on June 12, 2025 that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had broken the law and ordered the troops returned to Newsom's control by noon the following day, the [[united-states-court-of-appeals-for-the-ninth-circuit|9th Circuit Court of Appeals]] issued a late-night emergency stay pausing the order.[17] A three-judge panel scheduled a hearing for the following Tuesday, leaving the Guard federalized through the weekend.[17] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that California and Newsom "got less than a day of celebrating their win against Trump" before the appellate court intervened.[17]

## Trump's endorsement of Newsom's arrest (June 2025)

Speaking to reporters at the White House on Monday, June 9, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly voiced support for the arrest of a sitting state governor.[18] Fox News correspondent [[peter-doocy|Peter Doocy]] asked about the ongoing back-and-forth between Newsom and Trump's "border czar" [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]]: "Gavin Newsom is daring Tom Homan to come and arrest him. Should he do it?"[18] Trump endorsed the idea, responding, "I would do it if I were Tom," and adding: "I think it's great. Gavin likes the publicity, but I think it would be a great thing."[18] He went on to call Newsom a "nice guy," but "grossly incompetent."[18]

The same day, California sued the administration over the [[california-v-trump-administration-national-guard-deployment|National Guard deployment]], arguing that Trump had "unlawfully bypassed" Newsom by federalizing the Guard without his permission under Section 12406, a statute the state said requires call-up orders to pass through governors.[18] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a Kayfabian reading in which Newsom's escalation — including calls for a federal tax revolt amid a California "death spiral" — was inadvertently serving Trump's purposes, joking, "You tryna become one of my guys, Gav?!"[18] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] suggested the confrontation could be used to charge California officials with insurrection and permanently bar them from government service.[18]

## Red vs. Blue State Debate (November 2023)

On November 30, 2023, Newsom debated Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] for 90 minutes in Alpharetta, Georgia, in [[great-red-vs-blue-state-debate|"The Great Red vs. Blue State Debate"]], a [[fox-news|Fox News]] program moderated by [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]]; Newsom was not himself a candidate for president.[19] He said he took part in order to "tell the truth about the Biden-Harris record and also compare and contrast Ron DeSantis' record," and, as a [[joe-biden|Biden]] campaign surrogate, graded the president an "A."[19]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Newsom "is a toolbox, and didn't perform well," citing a moment when he corrected DeSantis's pronunciation of [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s name and declared "It's Madame Vice President, to you!"[19] She nonetheless judged that the format favored him, since it forced a presidential candidate to attack California — the state with the most electoral votes — an advantage Newsom effectively capitalized on.[19] She considered him "largely irrelevant until he announces" a candidacy of his own.[19] At the close Newsom proposed extending the debate another half hour, and both DeSantis and Hannity agreed before the program was abruptly ended after the commercial break.[19]

## California defiance on transgender athletes (July 2025)

On Monday, July 7, 2025, California declined to ban transgender athletes from K-12 school sports or change its anti-discrimination policies, becoming the second state after Maine to defy the Trump administration on the issue.[20] US Education Secretary [[linda-mcmahon|Linda McMahon]] had given the state 10 days from June 27 to rescind sports prizes awarded to transgender athletes and impose a ban after the [[department-of-education|Education Department]]'s Office of Civil Rights found a violation of federal anti-discrimination law.[20] Newsom's office called McMahon's proposed agreement a "political document" that carried no weight and would force the state to illegally violate its own anti-discrimination laws.[20] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] noted that California was asserting states' rights and cities' rights on the same day, pairing the dispute with Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]]'s confrontation with federal agents at MacArthur Park.[20]

## Proposition 50 campaign (2025)

Newsom was the leading supporter of [[california-proposition-50|Proposition 50]], the legislatively approved constitutional amendment on California's November 4, 2025 special election ballot that would allow temporary congressional maps beginning in 2026 in place of those drawn by the [[citizens-redistricting-commission|Citizens Redistricting Commission]].[21] He described the measure as a way to counter [[mid-decade-redistricting|mid-decade redistricting]] efforts in Republican-led states such as Texas, Florida and Ohio that could expand Republican control of Congress.[21]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that by openly stating the partisan purpose of the redraw — "Gavin said the manipulation bit publicly" — the governor may have handed opponents the judicially cognizable interest the Supreme Court found lacking in [[texas-v-pennsylvania|Texas v. Pennsylvania]], and asked what it would look like if Newsom lost the ability to manipulate elections in the state holding the most electoral votes in the country.[21]

## Arrest of former chief of staff (November 2025)

On Wednesday, November 12, 2025, Newsom's former chief of staff [[dana-williamson|Dana Williamson]], 53, was arrested on charges of stealing $225,000 from inactive political campaign funds and transferring the money to a friend's account.[22] Williamson served as Newsom's chief of staff from late 2022 through late 2024, the period during which the alleged crimes took place, according to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[22] Prosecutors allege that from February 2022 to September 2024 she conspired with a colleague to funnel funds through various business entities before moving them into personal accounts, and separately conspired to create false, backdated contracts after receiving a January 2024 civil subpoena concerning Paycheck Protection Program loans to her business.[22]

## AB 1840 home loans for illegal immigrants (August 2024)

In late August 2024 Newsom was left to decide the fate of a bill making illegal immigrants eligible for up to $150,000 in home loans for first-time buyers, after the California State Assembly passed a final version on August 28.[23] Assemblyman Bill Essayli (R-Corona) led Republican opposition, but Democrats approved the measure by a three-to-one veto-proof majority and sent it to the governor's desk.[23] The measure would expand an existing program that requires no down payment or interest payments but obliges borrowers to repay the principal plus a share of the home's appreciation.[23] Badlands Media framed the bill as part of a pattern of creating incentives for illegal migration by extending taxpayer-funded benefits, contrasting it with reports of Venezuelan gang activity in [[aurora-colorado|Aurora, Colorado]].[23]

## Halle Berry's DealBook criticism

At The New York Times' DealBook Summit in early December 2025, actress Halle Berry publicly attacked Newsom for vetoing the Menopause Care Equity Act (AB 432) for the second consecutive year, saying that having overlooked and devalued 'half the population' he 'probably should not be our next president either.'[24] Her remarks reportedly drew gasps from an audience awaiting Newsom's own appearance later that afternoon.[24] Badlands host Ashe in America sided with the veto, arguing the bill was about who pays for menopause care rather than access to it.[24]

## Response to the Portland deployment

After a federal court blocked the federalization of the Oregon National Guard, Newsom announced on October 5, 2025 that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was instead deploying 300 [[california-army-national-guard|California National Guard]] personnel into Oregon, calling the order "un-American."[25] He vowed to sue, writing that the troops were "on their way there now," that "we are taking this fight back to court," and that "the public cannot stay silent in the face of such reckless and authoritarian conduct by the President of the United States."[25] The episode followed rulings by Judge [[charles-breyer|Charles Breyer]] that had returned control of federalized California troops to Newsom and later barred their use for domestic law enforcement in California under the [[posse-comitatus-act|Posse Comitatus Act]], with the underlying case, *Newsom v. Trump*, remaining before the [[united-states-court-of-appeals-for-the-ninth-circuit|Ninth Circuit]].[25]

## January 2025 California wildfires

As California burned in January 2025, Newsom sat for a half-hour podcast interview with former Obama administration official Jon Favreau, drawing sharp criticism from The Federalist, which wrote that "if excuses were good for putting out fires" the governor could have fixed his state in a jiffy.[26] In the interview Newsom characterized every criticism leveled at him as "misinformation," described himself as just as much a victim as anyone who watched their home burn, and maintained that he was helping fix the problem even though it was not his responsibility.[26] When Favreau suggested Newsom was "ultimately responsible as the governor of the state" and that a newly announced investigation into a water shortage looked to some like an effort to pass the blame, Newsom rejected the framing, saying, "I've got that question… And I want to get the answers."[26]
