# John Hinckley Jr.

John Hinckley Jr. attempted to assassinate President [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] in 1981.[1] The Badlands Media essay "Patsies of the CIA and FBI" treats the episode as another instance of its central pattern, focusing less on Hinckley's stated motives than on the social proximity between his family and that of the sitting Vice President.[1]

## The Bush family connection

The article states that the Hinckley family and the Bushes had been family friends for decades.[1] It reports that [[george-hw-bush|George Bush]]'s son [[neil-bush|Neil]] was supposedly scheduled to have lunch with John Hinckley's brother the day before the assassination attempt.[1] The Hinckleys are described as donors to Bush political campaigns over the years; the two families lived close to each other in Midland, Texas, and even shared the same lawyer.[1]

From these facts the article draws a motive question. Bush was in line to become President had Reagan died, and his connection to the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] was direct — he had served for a year as Director of the agency, and the article asserts he was believed to have worked behind the scenes to coordinate the assassination of [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]].[1] What author [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] says is hard to understand is how Bush was never accused of involvement in the attempt on Reagan's life, concluding wryly that the CIA "really is that powerful."[1]

## Alleged MK ULTRA involvement

Hinckley appears in the article's list of figures suspected of having taken part in the CIA's [[mk-ultra|MK ULTRA]] mind-control program, together with [[lee-harvey-oswald|Lee Harvey Oswald]], [[jack-ruby|Jack Ruby]], [[sirhan-sirhan|Sirhan Sirhan]], [[charles-manson|Charlie Manson]], Jim Jones, Mark Chapman, David Koresh, [[timothy-mcveigh|Timothy McVeigh]], and John Salvi.[1] Within the article's taxonomy of uses, Hinckley belongs with Oswald, Ruby and Sirhan in the category of those deployed to eliminate — or attempt to eliminate — people in power who opposed the CIA.[1]

## The Bush dinner connection

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 2" recounts that only months after his inauguration [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] was struck by Hinckley's bullet, a shot that but for a quarter of an inch would have placed Trilateralist [[george-hw-bush|George H. W. Bush]] in the Oval Office seven years before his time.[2] The article notes that Hinckley's brother had scheduled dinner with Bush's son [[neil-bush|Neil Bush]] the very night Reagan was shot.[2]
