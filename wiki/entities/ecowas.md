# Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS)

The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) is a regional bloc of West African states described in Badlands commentary as affiliated with the [[united-nations|United Nations]].[1]

## Conflict with the Alliance of Sahel States

The [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]] (AES) has been in rebellion against ECOWAS since 2022, when Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] led a military coup in [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] against his own commander, who had himself overthrown the government eight months earlier.[1] The former ECOWAS member states now constitute the AES.[1]

## Badlands critique

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argues that organizations such as ECOWAS exist to control and suppress developing nations, preventing modernization so that natural resources can be plundered and populations exploited for labor, trafficking and terrorism.[1] In that reading, terrorist organizations serve the [[state-department|State Department]] and its partners by destabilizing the Sahel states so that ECOWAS and the UN can justify intervening and replacing their governments, a course they have threatened for years.[1] The commentary predicted that mainstream media would frame the U.S.–Nigerian strike on [[abu-bilal-al-manuki|Abu-Bilal Al-Manuki]] as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] siding with ECOWAS against the AES, while contending the opposite was true.[1]

## Response to the 2023 Niger coup

Following the July 26, 2023 overthrow of Nigerien President [[mohamed-bazoum|Mohamed Bazoum]], ECOWAS — described in reporting as a West-friendly bloc and led at the time by Nigerian President Bola Tinubu — imposed sanctions on the coup leaders and threatened full military intervention in [[niger|Niger]].[2] In line with those sanctions, [[nigeria|Nigeria]] announced that it would cut the electricity supply to its neighbour.[2] The junta did not budge amid the external pressure, and the ruling juntas of Mali and Burkina Faso sent delegations to Niamey to demonstrate solidarity with Niger's coup leaders.[2] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that punishing the people of Niger with sanctions, which she called a common Western anti-populist tactic, could backfire by generating popular support for the restoration of ECOWAS and Bazoum.[2]

## Guinea-Bissau transition dispute (2025)

In December 2025 ECOWAS rejected a proposed transition roadmap put forward by the military leaders of [[guinea-bissau|Guinea-Bissau]] following that country's November 26 coup, demanding a swift return to constitutional rule and warning that it could impose sanctions.[3] The military government had published a 29-article transitional charter setting a 12-month framework ending in presidential and legislative polls, with a 65-member National Transition Council including ten senior military officers acting as a legislative body.[3] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the episode as a continuation of the regional "revolt against ECOWAS," noting that [[nigeria|Nigeria]] heads the bloc and is therefore where the power controlling the region resides.[3]

## Origins and structure

ECOWAS was founded in 1975 following the post-colonial independence of the region, beginning as an economic collaboration and later growing to include joint military operations and a standing peacekeeping force.[4] By the time of the [[2023-niger-coup|2023 Niger coup]], [[mali|Mali]], [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] and Guinea had all been suspended from the bloc.[4]

## Threatened intervention in Niger, August 2023

After the July 2023 coup, ECOWAS threatened to use force to reinstate [[mohamed-bazoum|Mohamed Bazoum]], prompting Mali and Burkina Faso — themselves under military rule — to declare that they would come to [[niger|Niger's]] defence and that "any military intervention against Niger would be tantamount to a declaration of war against Burkina Faso and Mali."[4] The ECOWAS Commission stated that it "condemns in the strongest terms the attempt to seize power by force and calls on the coup plotters to free the democratically elected president of the republic immediately and without any condition."[4] The United States and United Kingdom issued statements of support for ECOWAS and called for an end to the conflict.[4]

## Nigeria, the Sahel, and the 2025 Trump military threat

In November 2025 commentary accompanying President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s threat of military action against [[nigeria|Nigeria]], Badlands host GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described Nigeria as the headquarters of ECOWAS, characterizing the bloc as a political and economic union comparable to the European Union that controls West Africa.[5] He noted that several member states had voluntarily left the bloc, and that Guinea had been suspended by ECOWAS in 2021 following a military coup, with those sanctions lifted in 2024.[5]

He further asserted that ECOWAS has been engaged in a proxy war against Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger since those countries departed the bloc and formed the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]], and that Nigeria and Ivory Coast, another ECOWAS power center, have engaged in subversive action against Sahelian leadership.[5] Russia and others have openly accused France and Ukraine of funding [[isis|ISIS]] terror cells attacking the Sahel states.[5]

## Position after the French withdrawal from Niger

When French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] announced in September 2023 that France would end its military presence in [[niger|Niger]] by year's end, Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that ECOWAS had "yet to accept defeat," even though to the rest of the world the conflict appeared finished and the West — "despite the denials of African NATO" — had lost.[6]

## Approval of the Sahel states' withdrawal (December 2024)

In December 2024 ECOWAS leaders approved the withdrawal of three military-run member states — [[mali|Mali]], [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] and [[niger|Niger]] — while offering a six-month grace period for them to reconsider.[7] The three had announced plans to leave in January after refusing the bloc's demand to restore democratic rule; because all three were founding members when ECOWAS was established in 1975 to advance economic and political integration in West Africa, their exit was described as a major blow to what had been Africa's most developed trade grouping.[7]

Citizens of ECOWAS states enjoy the right to live and work throughout the bloc and goods circulate freely, but ECOWAS had not said whether it would restrict people or goods from the departing states, which formed the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]] (AES).[7] The ECOWAS Commission in Abuja was mandated to work out those issues and future relations between the two blocs; the AES meanwhile announced visa-free travel and residency rights for ECOWAS citizens, framing the decision as an act of friendship rooted in centuries-old ties among African peoples.[7]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that whatever its original intent, ECOWAS had become a system for subverting the national sovereignty of its member states, "not unlike the European Union for the nations of Europe."[7] He argued that logic and historical precedent suggested certain powers would attempt to overthrow the three governments and install puppet leaders capable of returning them to ECOWAS within the six-month grace period — a danger he said explained why Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] and his Sahel partners dissolved their governments.[7]
