# Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab

Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab was an 18th-century Arabian preacher whose orthodox interpretation of Islam is known to the world as [[wahhabism|Wahhabism]], and who co-founded the first Saudi state through the [[pact-of-diriyah|Pact of Diriyah]] with [[mohammed-bin-saud|Mohammed bin Saud]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Background and doctrine

He was born in the early 1700s to a family of jurists (Islamic judges) in the [[najd|Najd]] region of central Arabia and raised under a strict orthodox view of Islam.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) By that era — nearly 1,100 years after the death of the Prophet [[muhammad|Muhammad]] — the religious landscape of Arabia had, in the article's account, come full circle back toward pre-Islamic idolatry: most identified as Muslims but had deviated from the hadiths, worshipping hundreds of secular idols and engaging in ancient occult magic and mysticism.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

Of firebrand temperament, he developed a passion for reformation — a restoration, as he saw it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) He rejected taqlid (conformity or group-think) in favor of ijtihad (independent legal reasoning through research of scripture) as the basis for re-establishing societal norms.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) His abrasiveness toward the status quo, especially regarding occult magic and idolatry, led his father and brother to excommunicate him from his own family.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Travels and expulsion from Uyayna

He traveled to Mecca and Medina on Arabia's west coast and to Basra in the far north — modern southern Iraq — in a continuing quest for knowledge of the Prophet's teachings, concluding firsthand that the people of Arabia had embraced a secular, self-indulgent existence that repulsed him.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Returning to his home base in the oasis town of Uyayna, a short distance from Diriyah, he won the loyalty of its emir and persuaded him to destroy the venerated tomb of a companion of the Prophet being worshipped as an idol, to cut down trees locals held sacred, and to implement the stoning of women who confessed to adultery.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) When word reached a more powerful emir who held influence over Uyayna through tax collection, the local emir was ordered to excommunicate al-Wahhab, and he was expelled along with all his followers.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## The pact

Invited to Diriyah, al-Wahhab accepted an alliance with Mohammed bin Saud but refused to endorse taxation, arguing conquest would enrich the ruler more than bleeding his own people — a position credited with the fact that no tax is imposed or collected by the Saudi government to this day.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) He told his new partner: "You are the settlement's chief and wise man. I want you to grant me an oath that you will perform jihad against the unbelievers. In return, you will be imam, leader of the Muslim community and I will be leader in religious matters."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The alliance was sealed with a bay'ah in 1744 and by the marriage of bin Saud's son to al-Wahhab's daughter.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Neither he nor his followers gave their ideology a specific name.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)
