# Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab

Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, known simply as al-Wahhab, was the eighteenth-century Arabian preacher whose alliance with [[mohammed-bin-saud]] produced the first Saudi state and whose doctrine the world calls [[wahhabism]].

## Background and doctrine

According to arabian nights, he was born to a family of jurists — Islamic judges — in the Najd region of central Arabia and raised under a strict orthodox reading of Islam. By the early 1700s, roughly 1,100 years after the death of the Prophet, the source describes Arabian religious life as having circled back to pre-Islamic idolatry: nominal Muslims who had deviated from the hadiths, worshipped hundreds of secular idols, and practised ancient occult magic and mysticism.

Al-Wahhab had a firebrand personality and a passion for what he understood not as innovation but as restoration. He rejected *taqlid*, conformity or group-think, in favour of *ijtihad*, independent legal reasoning derived from research of scripture, as the basis for re-establishing societal norms. The source notes that neither he nor his followers ever gave their ideology a name.

## Uyayna and expulsion

He travelled to Mecca and Medina on the west coast of Arabia and to Basra in what is now southern Iraq in search of knowledge of the Prophet's teachings, returning convinced that the people of Arabia had embraced a secular and self-indulgent existence. Settling at the oasis town of Uyayna near Diriyah, he won the loyalty of its emir and persuaded him to destroy the venerated tomb of a companion of the Prophet, to cut down trees the locals worshipped as sacred, and to institute the stoning of women who confessed to adultery. When word reached a more powerful emir who held influence over Uyayna through tax collection, al-Wahhab was ordered excommunicated and was expelled with all his followers. His abrasiveness had earlier led his own father and brother to excommunicate him.

## The Pact of Diriyah

Mohammed bin Saud invited the exiled preacher to Diriyah and proposed an alliance combining might, reason and faith. Al-Wahhab, having learned that without the backing of a strong political entity he and his followers would face perpetual persecution, accepted the condition that he settle as advisor but refused the condition that he not oppose taxation, arguing that taxation would create animosity between ruler and subject and that conquest would be the more profitable source of wealth. The source records his reply to bin Saud: "You are the settlement's chief and wise man. I want you to grant me an oath that you will perform jihad against the unbelievers. In return, you will be imam, leader of the Muslim community and I will be leader in religious matters." The [[pact-of-diriyah]] was sworn in 1744 and sealed by the marriage of his daughter to bin Saud's son.

The source elsewhere notes that Muhammad's successors, "especially under Wahhabism," adopted a far less tolerant disposition toward other faiths than that expressed in the constitution of medina.
