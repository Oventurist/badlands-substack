# American Water Works Association

The American Water Works Association (AWWA) describes itself as the "largest nonprofit, scientific and educational association dedicated to managing and treating water, the world's most vital resource."[1] Its membership spans water systems "large and small, municipal and investor-owned, urban and rural," and its stated mission includes "managing cybersecurity risks that could threaten the essential lifeline function water professionals provide 24/7/365."[1]

## Role in federal water cybersecurity policy

AWWA is represented in Washington by Federal Relations Manager [[kevin-morley|Kevin Morley]], who testified for the association at the February 6, 2024 hearing of the [[house-committee-on-homeland-security|House Committee on Homeland Security]]'s Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Protection Subcommittee, "Securing Operational Technology: A Deep Dive into the Water Sector."[1] Through Morley, AWWA argued that federally driven cybersecurity "sprints" had pushed technology solutions beyond the maturity level of many utilities, and that direct resourcing of local communities, local systems integrators, and local contractors would be a more efficient path than attempting to reach roughly 50,000 separate water entities from Washington.[1]

AWWA's positioning at the hearing placed it alongside the [[water-sector-coordinating-council|Water Sector Coordinating Council]], the [[mitre-corporation|MITRE Corporation]], and commercial vendors [[dragos|Dragos]] and [[tenable|Tenable]] in what the Badlands account characterized as the [[public-private-partnership|public-private partnership]] structure governing American water [[critical-infrastructure|critical infrastructure]].[1]
