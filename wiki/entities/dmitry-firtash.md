# Dmitry Firtash

Dmitry Firtash is a Ukrainian oligarch who was indicted on U.S. criminal charges in 2014 and who figures in reporting about the early conduct of the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller special counsel investigation]].[1]

## The alleged Weissmann overture

According to reporting by [[john-solomon|John Solomon]] in *The Hill*, cited at length in the Badlands Media feature "Enjoy the Show," Robert Mueller's chief deputy [[andrew-weissmann|Andrew Weissmann]] quietly approached Firtash's American lawyers in early June 2017 — days after Mueller's appointment papers were signed — with an offer: provide damaging information on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the Russia case, and Mueller's team might make Firtash's 2014 U.S. criminal charges go away.[1] Solomon reported that the specifics of the overture were confirmed by multiple sources with direct knowledge and by contemporaneous defense memoranda.[1]

Firtash's defense team said Weissmann told them he believed a company called Bayrock, tied to former FBI informant Felix Sater, had "made substantial investments with Donald Trump's companies," and that prosecutors were hunting for dirt on Trump's son-in-law [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]].[1] The oligarch's lawyers said Firtash rejected the offer because he did not possess credible information or evidence on the topics Weissmann raised.[1]

## Interpretation in the article

Badlands author [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats the episode as evidence that the Mueller team knew the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] was an uncorroborated mess and that Weissmann was effectively shopping for witnesses — potentially suborning perjury — in order to manufacture a case against Trump.[1] The article links the timing to lead FBI agent [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]]'s text message, sent days before the overture, that "there's no big there there."[1]
