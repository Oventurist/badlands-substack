# Aldous Huxley

Aldous Huxley was the British author of *Brave New World*, named by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] in the February 5, 2024 Badlands Media essay "Control Through Language" as one of a group of British writers who moved in the same circles during the first two decades of the 1900s, alongside [[george-orwell|George Orwell]], [[h-g-wells|H.G. Wells]], Huxley's brother [[julian-huxley|Julian Huxley]] and [[margaret-sanger|Margaret Sanger]].[1]

Carlson writes that at least some of this group supported [[eugenics|eugenics]], believing in control over who reproduced and how often, and that Adolf Hitler took many of his ideas from eugenics supporters in early twentieth-century America and England.[1] He adds that Julian Huxley was instrumental in the large psychedelic drug push in America during the 1960s, and argues more generally that the [[cabal|Cabal]]'s plan was shared with these authors, accounting for the predictive accuracy of their fiction.[1]
