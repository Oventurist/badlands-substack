# Antarctic Impulsive Transient Antenna

The Antarctic Impulsive Transient Antenna (ANITA) is a balloon-borne radio detection experiment operating over Antarctica, designed to search for neutrinos — extremely difficult-to-detect particles emitted by high-energy sources — by analyzing radio signals traveling to Earth with a variety of instruments.[1]

## Anomalous radio signals

In results published in the journal Physical Review Letters and reported in June 2025, researchers using ANITA described the detection of mysterious radio waves appearing to come from beneath Antarctica's ice.[1] [[stephanie-wissel|Stephanie Wissel]], an associate professor of physics, astronomy and astrophysics at Penn State and one of the researchers, said the signals were found while the team was searching for neutrinos, and that the team had no explanation for how such particles could have been detected in that configuration.[1]

The researchers cross-referenced their findings against two other experiments and found that the results did not match, indicating that what they had detected were not neutrinos but something else entirely.[1] Wissel noted that some theories proposed dark matter as an explanation, but said this could not be confirmed and the signals remained a mystery.[1] She added: "My guess is that some interesting radio propagation effects occur near ice and also near the horizon that I don't fully understand, but we certainly explored several of those, and we haven't been able to find any of those yet either."[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] treated the anomaly as an opening for longstanding alternative theories about Antarctica — including speculation about non-human intelligences, hidden bases, energy experiments, and portals — while noting that none of these are established and that mainstream science had offered no answer for the signals.[1]
