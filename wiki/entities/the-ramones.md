# The Ramones

The Ramones were an American [[punk-rock]] band whose members, none of whom were related, all adopted the stage surname "Ramone."[1] Badlands Media uses the band as a case study in punk's original ideological pluralism, noting that its internal political disagreements were open, sustained, and mutually respected.[1]

## Internal political debate

In an interview with [[newsmax]], drummer [[marky-ramone|Marky Ramone]] — described as the last surviving member — recalled the political arguments that filled the band's touring van: "Johnny was a conservative and Joey was a liberal… So the arguments would go back and forth about why, what for, what's the reason, you know the usual generalized stuff, but then they would get very specific."[1] [[johnny-ramone|Johnny Ramone]] was the band's conservative voice and Joey Ramone its liberal one.[1]

Marky said the band valued the freedom to disagree: "That's the country we live in, thank God, and we're able to do that. So I respect people's politics."[1]

## Anti-communism

Badlands Media contrasts the Ramones' hostility to communism with contemporary bands that, in the article's account, openly advocate anarcho-socialism.[1] The publication argues that the modern hostility toward conservatism within self-described punk circles would have been alien to the band.[1]
