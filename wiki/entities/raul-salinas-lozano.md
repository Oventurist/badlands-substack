# Raúl Salinas Lozano

Raúl Salinas Lozano was the patriarch of the Salinas family, father of Mexican president [[carlos-salinas-de-gortari|Carlos Salinas de Gortari]] and of [[raul-salinas-de-gortari|Raúl Salinas de Gortari]], and Mexico's Minister of Commerce during the period in which the North American Free Trade Agreement was negotiated.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats this as the hinge of the entire NAFTA story: the man negotiating the trade deal with [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] was, according to his own former private secretary in a statement to U.S. authorities, "a leading figure in narcotics dealings" that also involved his son Raúl and his son-in-law [[jose-francisco-ruiz-massieu|José Francisco Ruiz Massieu]], the number-two official in the [[institutional-revolutionary-party|PRI]], who was assassinated in 1994.[1]

Global Research, quoted in the article, describes Bush and Salinas Lozano as "íntimo amigos" and reports that Bush had developed close personal ties with both Carlos Salinas and his father.[1] On this account, when Carlos Salinas was inaugurated the entire Mexican state apparatus became criminalized, with key government positions occupied by members of the cartel, and the sweeping privatization program launched under [[imf|IMF]] advice evolved into a multibillion-dollar money-laundering operation in which narco-dollars were channelled into the acquisition of state property and public utilities.[1] Lange concludes that the arrangement was "a family affair": the father negotiated [[nafta|NAFTA]], one son ran the presidency, and the other green-lighted drug shipments to the U.S. border.[1]
