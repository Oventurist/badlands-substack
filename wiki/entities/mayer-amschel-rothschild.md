# Mayer Amschel Rothschild

Mayer Amschel Rothschild is presented in Badlands Media commentary as the founding figure of the [[rothschild-family|Rothschild]] banking dynasty and as the author of a widely circulated quotation on the primacy of controlling a nation's money supply over controlling its laws.[1] In Joe Lange's November 2022 essay "Money and Oil," the quotation is reproduced as evidence that the family's influence over governments derives from its position as creditor rather than from any formal political office.[1]

## Role in the Badlands narrative

Lange argues that the Rothschilds first controlled banks, then kings, and now control most governments around the world.[1] On this account the family did not accumulate wealth and power by lending to individuals but by lending to governments and monarchs, so that sovereign debt became the mechanism of political leverage.[1] The dynasty is described as having become a set of global "puppet masters" by exploiting the war between Napoleon and England: knowing before anyone else in London that Napoleon had lost, the family is said to have deliberately provoked a panic sell-off of British bonds in order to buy them back at fire-sale prices — though Lange treats that trade as minor compared with the debt into which England had been placed by Rothschild funding of the war itself.[1]

## Wars as a financial model

The essay generalises the pattern: wars are described as the Rothschilds' largest source of profit ever since, with the family funding both sides and remaining indifferent to the outcome, because the victor is indebted to them permanently and the loser is compelled to sell assets and resources at distressed prices.[1] [[world-war-i|The First World War]] is offered as the paradigm case, with Rothschild-controlled central banks said to have financed both belligerent blocs while millions died and states accumulated massive debts.[1] Germany's subsequent bankruptcy, the purchase of its companies and resources "for pennies on the dollar," the collapse of its currency and the resulting poverty are then framed as having set the stage for [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]] and the Second World War.[1]

## Origins in Frankfurt

Ryan DeLarme's "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 6" gives the founder's birth name as Mayer Amschel Bauer, a German Jew born on February 23, 1744, in the Frankfurt quarter known as the [[judengasse|Judengasse]], or Jew lane, at a time when the city was described as a hotbed of anti-Semitism stemming from the published philosophies of Immanuel Kant and Johann Fichte.[2] The young Mayer originally studied to become a rabbi and was said to be well schooled in Haskalah, an amalgamation of religion, Hebrew law and reason popularized during the Age of Enlightenment; the death of his parents caused him to leave rabbinical school and apprentice at a banking house.[2]

## Patronage of William IX and the occult

Mayer rose to become court financial agent to [[william-ix-of-hesse-kassel|William IX]], royal administrator of Hesse-Kassel and a prominent Freemason, a connection through which DeLarme speculates he was initiated into the "High Cabal" that had operated out of Venice and later Geneva.[2] He is said to have ingratiated himself with William, only a year his senior, by sharing his interests in [[freemasonry|Freemasonry]] and antiquities, and — given his rabbinical training — to have developed a deep understanding of the ancient mysteries, particularly the Jewish Cabala, at the very period when Cabalistic metaphysics began fusing with Masonic tradition.[2] His client list also came to include the royal German family of Thurn und Taxis, which administered the courier service of the Holy Roman Empire and gave the Rothschilds early access to news of market trends, commodity prices and political events, as biographer [[derek-wilson|Derek Wilson]] noted.[2] To protect their correspondence the family wrote in Judendeutsch, German rendered in Hebrew characters.[2]

## The Frankfurt Five and the family name

DeLarme repeats the account that the family's initial fortune came from the diversion of funds William IX received from the British government for supplying Hessian soldiers against the American colonists, money handed to Mayer for investment but used instead to establish his son [[nathan-rothschild|Nathan]] in London; Mayer eventually repaid it, but the episode is treated as the origin of the Rothschild fortune.[2] Wanting his sons to succeed independently and to extend the business across Europe, Mayer kept his eldest in Frankfurt and sent the other four to London, Paris, Vienna and Naples, the group becoming known as the "Frankfurt Five."[2] It was around this time that he changed the family name from Bauer to Rothschild, derived from the German *zum rothen Schild*, "at the red shield," after the sign on the family's Judengasse house.[2] *The New Encyclopaedia Britannica* is quoted as saying Mayer "set the pattern that his family was to follow so successfully—to do business with reigning houses by preference and to father as many sons as possible."[2]
