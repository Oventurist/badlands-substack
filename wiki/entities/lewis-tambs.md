# Lewis A. Tambs

Lewis A. Tambs was the United States Ambassador to Costa Rica during the mid-1980s and a participant in the covert supply operation for the Nicaraguan rebels.[1]

According to a senior government official in Central America, Tambs helped secure initial Costa Rican permission to build a secret airstrip for the rebels in early 1986 and was deeply involved in overseeing its use.[1] U.S. government and Nicaraguan rebel officials reported that Tambs and other officials at the American Embassy in Costa Rica maintained close contact with participants in the so-called private supply network for the Contras set up by Lieutenant Colonel [[oliver-north|Oliver L. North]] of the White House National Security Council.[1] The airstrip itself was built by [[udall-research|Udall Research]], a Panama-based company tied to the financial machinery of the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra scandal]].[1]

The article uses Tambs's involvement to argue that even Costa Rica — a country that had abolished its army in 1948 and was therefore never fully controlled by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] through [[school-of-the-americas|School of the Americas]] military training — was still used as a transit platform for the agency's operations and for drugs moving toward the United States.[1]
