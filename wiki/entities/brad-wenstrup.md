# Brad Wenstrup

Brad Wenstrup is a Republican member of the United States [[house-of-representatives|House of Representatives]] from Ohio who chaired the congressional select subcommittee investigating the origins of COVID-19.[1]

## COVID-19 origins investigation

In May 2024 Wenstrup said that after reviewing classified [[state-department|State Department]] documents he came away with the impression that the [[wuhan-lab|Wuhan]] lab leak theory is accurate, stating that the documents contained enough credible evidence to support the assertion that the coronavirus accidentally leaked from a laboratory in Wuhan, China, where research on the disease was being conducted.[1] Based on what he saw, Wenstrup concluded that the Chinese government had attempted to cover up the incident.[1]

The documents had been requested by his committee in February of the previous year. The State Department declined to hand the records over physically, but permitted certain lawmakers and committee staff to view them.[1] A heavily redacted version obtained by the press carried section headers including "Who Ordered the Cover Up? Signs point to Beijing, not Local Officials" and "Beijing Knew Earlier Than They Admit."[1] Wenstrup pressed publicly for the documents to be declassified.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media commentary placed the Wenstrup disclosures within a cluster of near-simultaneous COVID-era revelations in early May 2024 — including public backpedalling by former pandemic media figures, fresh disclosures concerning Anthony Fauci and Wuhan, and vaccine-injury developments surrounding AstraZeneca — arguing that the pattern indicated establishment retreat rather than advance in what the writers describe as an information war.[1]

### CIA COVID Discovery Team inquiry (September 2023)

As chairman of the House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic, Wenstrup joined House Intelligence Committee chairman [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]] in a letter dated 12 September 2023 to [[cia|CIA]] Director [[william-burns|William Burns]], requesting all documents, communications and pay information relating to the agency's COVID Discovery Team by 26 September.[2] The request followed whistleblower testimony from a senior CIA officer alleging that the agency had offered to pay six of the seven team analysts to abandon their low-confidence conclusion that COVID-19 had originated from a laboratory in Wuhan, China, and to endorse animal-to-human transmission instead.[2]

### HHS records release (February 2023)

In February 2023, as chairman of the newly Republican-led [[house-select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]], Wenstrup requested phone records, calendars and dozens of sets of internal records from top federal health officials, including [[national-institutes-of-health|National Institutes of Health]] Director [[francis-collins|Francis Collins]], former White House COVID adviser [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] and Health and Human Services Secretary [[xavier-becerra|Xavier Becerra]].[3] The [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] released roughly 900 pages of records to the panel, which said it was probing whether COVID-19 originated from a lab in Wuhan, whether the Chinese Communist Party covered it up, and whether U.S. taxpayer dollars had been sent to the lab.[3]

Wenstrup told [[cbs-news|CBS News]] he would consider subpoenas against Fauci, Becerra and other key officials if full responses were not provided, saying, "I think there will be subpoenas for some. Just being honest with you — I would hope that we don't have to do it very often."[3]

## Letter on Fauci's CIA visit (September 2023)

In a letter sent on the night of Tuesday, September 26, 2023 to the Inspector General of the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]], Wenstrup alleged that [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] had been "smuggled" into [[cia|CIA]] headquarters "without a record of entry," where he participated in the analysis in order to "influence" the agency's COVID-19 origins investigation.[4] The letter demanded documents, communications and other evidence of contacts between Fauci and the CIA.[4]

Wenstrup's letter also recounted allegations from a self-described senior-level CIA whistleblower that six of the seven members of the agency's COVID review team believed the intelligence and science supported a low-confidence assessment that the virus originated in a laboratory in Wuhan, China, while the seventh and most senior member favored zoonosis.[4] According to the whistleblower, the six — described as experienced officers with significant scientific expertise — were given "a significant monetary incentive to change their position," producing the agency's eventual public determination of uncertainty.[4]

## Cuomo nursing home hearing (September 2024)

In early September 2024, Wenstrup, as chairman of the [[house-select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]], pressed for answers over nursing home deaths in New York during the pandemic ahead of a scheduled public appearance by former governor [[andrew-cuomo|Andrew Cuomo]] on September 10.[5] Emphasising the need for accountability, Wenstrup said that "Andrew Cuomo owes answers to the 15,000 families who lost loved ones in New York's nursing homes during the Covid-19 pandemic."[5] The hearing followed Cuomo's seven-hour closed-door testimony before House Republicans in June 2024, after he had been subpoenaed by the subcommittee.[5]

## EcoHealth Alliance debarment

In May 2024, as Chairman of the [[select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]], Wenstrup released a statement confirming that the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] had accepted the subcommittee's recommendation to formally debar [[ecohealth-alliance|EcoHealth Alliance, Inc.]][6] HHS agreed to initiate official debarment proceedings and to impose a government-wide suspension of U.S. taxpayer funds to the organization, including a halt on all active grants.[6]

## Request for Fauci's personal records, May 2024

On Wednesday, May 29, 2024, Wenstrup requested access to the cellphone and personal email records of former National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases director [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]], ahead of Fauci's scheduled testimony the following week before the [[house-select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|House Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]].[7] Wenstrup said new evidence suggested Fauci had used personal channels for government communication.[7]

The request followed the disclosure that Fauci's senior advisor [[david-morens|David Morens]] had emailed former [[ecohealth-alliance|EcoHealth Alliance]] president [[peter-daszak|Peter Daszak]] in 2021 stating he could route material to Fauci privately to dodge Freedom of Information Act requests: "I can either send stuff to Tony on his private gmail, or hand it to him at work. He is too smart to let colleagues send him stuff that could cause trouble."[7] Wenstrup called the information concerning and opened an investigation into a "secret back channel" Morens had alluded to in a separate email.[7]
