# Ryan James

Ryan James is an FBI Supervisory Special Agent who was the final prosecution witness at the trial of [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] in October 2022.[1]

## Background

James worked at an investment firm in Chicago before becoming a police officer and then joining the [[fbi|FBI]] in 2009.[1] He was assigned to the New Haven, Connecticut office and remained there until September 2018, when he was promoted to Supervisory Special Agent and moved to the Jackson, Mississippi field office, where he supervises "a squad of agents that investigate public corruption and civil rights violations."[1] In February 2021 he was assigned to the FBI's Inspection Division at the Hoover Building headquarters, and in May 2021 he was pulled into the [[durham-special-counsel-investigation|Durham Special Counsel]] to work the Danchenko case.[1]

## Testimony

James and the investigators he worked with obtained the Amtrak, phone, toll, and other records that featured throughout the case.[1] [[john-durham|John Durham]] moved Exhibit 607, a portion of Danchenko's Facebook records, into evidence and displayed Exhibit 1400, a travel-records exhibit; further exhibits included Exhibit 503 ([[sergei-millian|Sergei Millian]]'s Verizon records), Exhibit 1604 (a chart James helped prepare of [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Dolan]] and Danchenko phone records covering March 2016 through January 2017), Exhibit 618 (additional Facebook records) and Exhibit 1603 (phone records).[1] Admission of Exhibit 1603 was briefly delayed because both counsel had failed to redact the names and phone numbers of uninvolved individuals; Durham filled the time by asking James how he had acquired the information.[1] James then explained the chart to the jury and testified that the calls in the records fell into categories — known contacts such as [[olga-galkina|Galkina]], Dolan and [[christopher-steele|Steele]], telemarketing calls, or "unavailable" numbers — none of which lasted more than a minute, undercutting the claim of a lengthy anonymous call.[1] After the lunch break, the defense declined to cross examine, and Durham rested his case.[1]
