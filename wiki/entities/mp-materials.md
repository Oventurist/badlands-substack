# MP Materials

MP Materials is the American rare-earth mining company that operates the [[mountain-pass-mine|Mountain Pass Mine]] in San Bernardino County, California — one of the largest rare-earth mines in the world.[1]

## Mountain Pass operations

The company's open-air mine, nearly 500 feet deep, sits just north of Interstate 15 near Bailey Road at roughly 5,000 feet elevation, on the route between Southern California and Las Vegas; more than 200,000 drivers a day pass it, most without realizing what it is.[1] MP Materials Chief Financial Officer [[ryan-corbett|Ryan Corbett]] described the deposit as "almost a freak of nature," formed billions of years ago when fault lines allowed rock formations bearing rare-earth minerals to reach the surface in unusually high concentration and in a form easily refined with the company's technology.[1] Of the seventeen rare earth elements on the periodic table, many are present at Mountain Pass.[1]

## Strategic and political significance

Industry has leaned increasingly on the Mountain Pass operation amid a growing trade war, making MP Materials central to United States critical-minerals supply.[1] Badlands host [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited commentary from Lt. Gen. [[steve-kwast|Steve Kwast]] arguing that recent Trump administration deals with Intel, MP Materials, and other corporations represent "a new model for the governance of a Republic," rather than the crony capitalism critics alleged.[1]

## Pentagon equity investment (July 2025)

In July 2025 the Defense Department agreed to buy $400 million of MP Materials preferred stock, becoming the company's largest shareholder.[2] The company described Mountain Pass, about 60 miles outside Las Vegas, as the only operational rare earth mine in the United States, and said proceeds from the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] investment would be used to expand its rare earths processing capacity and magnet production.[2] Shares soared roughly 50 percent to close at $45.23, lifting market capitalization to $7.4 billion — an increase of about $2.5 billion from the previous trading session.[2]

CEO [[james-litinsky|James Litinsky]] characterized the arrangement as a public-private partnership that would accelerate the buildout of an end-to-end [[rare-earth-elements|rare earth]] magnet supply chain in the United States.[2] Under its terms the Pentagon agreed to purchase 100 percent of the magnets produced at a new facility called 10X for ten years after construction, [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan]] and [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]] supplied $1 billion in financing for the plant, and the government guaranteed a minimum price of $110 per kilogram for ten years on neodymium-praseodymium oxide stockpiled or sold by the company, taking 30 percent of the upside above that level once a second magnet facility becomes operational.[2] Interior Secretary [[doug-burgum|Doug Burgum]] had signaled in April 2025 that the administration was weighing direct equity investments in critical mineral companies to break dependence on [[china|China]], which accounted for about 70 percent of U.S. rare earth imports in 2023.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] concluded that whatever the eventual taxpayer return, MP Materials had "won the lottery."[2]
