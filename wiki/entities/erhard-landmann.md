# Erhard Landmann

**Erhard Landmann** is described in [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]]'s [[the-werewolf-game|Werewolf Game]] series as an obscure researcher whose works have never been translated into English.[1]

The series credits Landmann with what it calls one of the most astounding historical discoveries of recent decades: the claim that the ancient German language was once spoken by all the peoples of the world.[1] According to the article, ancient German words can be found everywhere — from the Aztecs to the tribes of Africa, the tribes of central Asia, the Polynesian islands, and beyond.[1] The author tells readers that they are among the first people in the entire English-speaking world to be made aware of the discovery.[1]

The author argues that the claim, if true, alters the generic conception of history almost in its entirety, and calls it a crime against humanity that the information has been kept hidden, on the grounds that people have a right to be told the truth about their past.[1] Readers seeking the full account before the next installment are directed to a chapter in [[frederick-dodson|Frederick Dodson]]'s book *Levels of Heaven and Hell*.[1]

Within the series' argument, Landmann's thesis is used to support the significance of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s ancestral family name *Drumpf* — a German name — as evidence that Trump descends from a culture of massive worldwide influence, and as a key the author says will help decode one of [[qanon|Q]]'s posts in a following article.[1]

## Transmission and stellar claims

The installment "On Languages and Cover-ups" identifies Landmann as the German author from whom [[frederick-dodson|Frederick Dodson]] learned much of the [[ancient-german-language-thesis|ancient German language]] material, and states that there is no better way to study the subject than to learn German and go to the original source — something the author says he plans to do some day.[2]

According to Dodson as relayed in that essay, Landmann's life's work revealed not only the ancient German connection to all world languages but a great deal about mankind's connection to the stars.[2] Among these findings, the [[pleiades|Pleiades]] are said to play a major role in human history and to be referred to as "Min" or "Maia," while the vessels that carried mankind's starry ancestors to Earth and back were usually egg-shaped, denoted by the syllables *ai* and *eck*, or shaped as a pillar of light, denoted by *sal* and *sul*.[2] The author argues that with these elements much history can be reanalysed, offering the name Solomon — containing *Sal* and *Min* — and a series of cross-linguistic words for "path" as examples of what Landmann's method makes visible.[2]

## Neglect of the discovery

Part 5 of the series repeats that Landmann's works have never been translated and are very hard to find, and that apart from Landmann and [[frederick-dodson|Frederick Dodson]] the author is unaware of any other source on the matter.[3] The essay notes that Landmann first published his book on the subject in 1986, meaning the information had been available for nearly four decades at the time of writing without sparking worldwide fascination.[3] The author treats this neglect as only partly attributable to the [[deep-state|Deep State]], arguing that ordinary people also have a role to play in keeping knowledge alive, and that it is absurd that the near-identity of Aztec and ancient German has gone unexamined for so long.[3]
