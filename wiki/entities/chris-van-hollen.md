# Chris Van Hollen

Chris Van Hollen is a Democratic member of the [[united-states-senate|United States Senate]].[1]

## Lutnick questioning

At a Senate Appropriations Subcommittee hearing on February 10, 2026, Van Hollen was among the Democratic senators who pressed Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] over his ties to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] revealed in newly released [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] documents, focusing on discrepancies with Lutnick's prior claims about when he cut ties and accusing him of lacking transparency.[1]

## El Salvador trip to meet Abrego Garcia (April 2025)

On Thursday evening, April 17, 2025, Van Hollen met in [[el-salvador|El Salvador]] with [[kilmar-abrego-garcia|Kilmar Armando Abrego Garcia]], the deported illegal alien whose case had become a focal point of the immigration debate, along with Abrego Garcia's lawyer.[2] Salvadoran President [[nayib-bukele|Nayib Bukele]] publicized the encounter on X with several photos, writing that Abrego Garcia had "miraculously risen from the 'death camps' & 'torture'" and was "now sipping margaritas with Sen. Van Hollen in the tropical paradise of El Salvador."[2] Bukele said that now that Abrego Garcia had been confirmed healthy, "he gets the honor of staying in El Salvador's custody."[2]

Badlands commentator Ashe in America argued that provoking outrage on the left was at least part of the point of Bukele's messaging.[2]

## Salvadoran refusal of his release request (April 16, 2025)

Van Hollen's meeting followed a rebuff the previous day. On Wednesday, April 16, 2025, El Salvador's government refused his request that it free Abrego García, whose case had become a flash point in the fight over the Trump administration's mass deportation campaign.[3] Van Hollen had flown to El Salvador that day to lobby for the release of the Salvadoran-born Maryland resident, who a month earlier had been deported along with hundreds of others and imprisoned without due process in the megaprison known as [[cecot|CECOT]].[3]

Speaking to reporters in El Salvador, Van Hollen said that "President Trump and our attorney general, [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]], and the vice president of the United States are lying when they say that Abrego García has been charged with a crime or is part of [[ms-13|MS-13]]," adding, "That is a lie."[3] The administration maintained it could not secure his release and accused him of gang membership, an allegation his lawyers denied and which a federal judge found unsupported by evidence.[3]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] mocked the trip as a "Telenovela field trip," noting that Van Hollen went live from the airport, from the car after landing, and at a local press conference after being refused, and that he asserted Abrego García had been "kidnapped" from America even as damaging reporting on the case broke in the United States.[3]

## True Shutdown Fairness Act (October 2025)

During the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|October 2025 government shutdown]], Van Hollen sponsored the [[true-shutdown-fairness-act|True Shutdown Fairness Act]], which would have paid all federal workers, members of the military and federal contractors during the funding lapse and would additionally have prevented the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration from attempting mass layoffs of federal workers, the restructurings known as reductions in force.[4] On Thursday, October 23, 2025, Sen. [[ron-johnson|Ron Johnson]] of Wisconsin objected to Van Hollen's request that the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] immediately consider and pass the bill, one of two Democratic-sponsored shutdown pay bills Johnson blocked on the floor that day.[4]
