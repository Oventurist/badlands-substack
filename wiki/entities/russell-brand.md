# Russell Brand

**Russell Brand** is a British comedian, actor and independent commentator whose 2023 deplatforming campaign Badlands Media presents as a flagship operation in the censorship offensive leading into 2024.[1]

## Allegations and the smear-campaign framing

In September 2023 Brand became the subject of widely publicized allegations of sexual abuse.[1] Badlands states that allegations of sexual abuse should always be taken seriously, but argues that unproven accusations have historically been weaponized because they reliably trigger anger in men and bonding sympathy in women regardless of their truth.[1] The article places Brand in a lineage with [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]], who denounced sexual-abuse allegations against him as a CIA smear campaign; Matt Gaetz, investigated by the Biden administration with insufficient evidence found to convince a jury; and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the E. Jean Carroll and Stormy Daniels matters.[1] The author explicitly declines to adjudicate the truth of the claims, arguing instead that the timing reflects a coordinated effort to remove effective communicators from the 5GW chessboard before 2024.[1]

## Selective enforcement argument

Badlands notes that the same pundits and media conglomerates have protected accused men who remain loyal to establishment dogma — naming Prince Andrew, Bill Clinton and Joe Biden — arguing the censorship regime targets only defectors.[1]

## Personal transformation

The article emphasizes that Brand has been open about his former hedonistic lifestyle and has since become a man who answers to a higher power, advocates a natural and sober lifestyle, and has started a family.[1] A widely shared post by Kate Shemirani summarizing this arc — sobriety, remarriage, children, a truth-telling podcast, then attack — is reproduced in the article.[1]

## Government and platform response

The [[british-government|British Government]] took an active interest in the allegations: legislator [[caroline-nokes|Caroline Nokes]] urged police in Britain and the United States to investigate, and Prime Minister [[rishi-sunak|Rishi Sunak]] addressed them through spokesman [[max-blain|Max Blain]].[1] Badlands stresses that Brand is one of Sunak's biggest critics and questions whether the Prime Minister acted out of compassion or self-interest.[1] The government asked big-tech platforms to censor Brand's content, connecting the episode to the [[online-safety-bill|Online Safety Bill]] and the [[trusted-news-initiative|Trusted News Initiative]].[1] Journalists subsequently demanded that [[rumble|Rumble]] deplatform Brand — one of its biggest channels — over an alleged crime for which he had not been convicted.[1]
