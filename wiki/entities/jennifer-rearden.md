# Jennifer Rearden

Jennifer H. Rearden is a United States district judge in the Southern District of New York who, in December 2023, sentenced former [[fbi|FBI]] counterintelligence chief [[charles-mcgonigal|Charles McGonigal]] to 50 months in prison for conspiring to violate U.S. sanctions and for money laundering in connection with work for sanctioned Russian oligarch [[oleg-deripaska|Oleg Deripaska]].[1]

## The McGonigal sentencing

Rearden imposed a sentence of just over four years — 50 months, plus a $40,000 fine — below the statutory maximum of five years sought by prosecutors, and ordered McGonigal to surrender to prison on February 26.[1] She said McGonigal "repeatedly flouted and manipulated the sanctions regimes vital" to U.S. national security interests, and that "the undeniable seriousness of this and the need to respect the law … compels a meaningful custodial sentence."[1]

At the same time, Rearden said his conduct did "not all together stamp out" his distinguished career or the "profoundly important contributions" he made to the United States, describing the sentence as designed to balance more than two decades of law enforcement service against crimes she said threatened national security.[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] criticized the sentence as an example of asymmetrical justice, arguing that the four corruption charges carried a potential 80 combined years and that the court's reasoning effectively presumed all of McGonigal's other investigative work — including matters touching [[carter-page|Carter Page]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — was above reproach.[1]
