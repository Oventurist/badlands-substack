# Michael Atkinson

Michael Atkinson served as Intelligence Community Inspector General during the first [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1]

## Role in the Ukraine impeachment

According to reporting cited by Badlands, the 2019 claims by "Witness 2" — later identified as [[gavin-wilde|Gavin Wilde]] — were critical in helping Atkinson push Ukraine whistleblower [[eric-ciaramella|Eric Ciaramella]]'s complaint forward.[1] Wilde's "Russiagate"-linked biases were concealed from House investigators during the impeachment process, an obstruction that [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] discussed in *Government Gangsters*.[1]

## Gabbard declassification (April 2026)

On 14 April 2026 the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]], under Director [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], released never-before-seen documents that it said exposed a coordinated effort by elements within the Intelligence Community — including Atkinson as former IC Inspector General — to manufacture a conspiracy used as the basis to impeach President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2019.[2] According to the ODNI press release, during his preliminary investigation into Trump's July 2019 phone call with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, Atkinson did not follow standard inspector-general procedures and relied on politicized, manufactured narratives.[2]

ODNI stated that Atkinson interviewed only four individuals: the whistleblower; the whistleblower's friend, who was a co-author of the January 2017 Russia Hoax [[intelligence-community-assessment|Intelligence Community Assessment]] and a close colleague of former [[fbi|FBI]] agent [[peter-strzok|Peter Strzok]]; and two character references with no firsthand knowledge of the call.[2] Despite the absence of firsthand evidence, Atkinson was said to have weaponized the whistleblower process and exceeded his statutory jurisdiction by ignoring Department of Justice guidance and relying on second-hand testimony to ensure the complaint was released to [[congress|Congress]], referred to the FBI, and leaked to the media.[2]

Gabbard stated that "Inspector General Atkinson failed to uphold his responsibility to the American people, putting political motivations over the truth," describing the episode as an example of "the deep state playbook on how to weaponize the Intelligence Community."[2] The narrative was then used by [[house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence|HPSCI]] Chairman [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] and Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] to spark the [[first-impeachment-of-donald-trump|first impeachment of Donald Trump]] in December 2019.[2]

## Criminal referral (April 2026)

In April 2026 the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] referred both the whistleblower behind the complaint tied to President Trump's 2019 impeachment and Atkinson, as former Inspector General, to the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] for potential criminal violations.[3] The referrals cite possible federal law violations tied to 2019 briefings before the [[house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence|House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence]] and follow document releases by DNI [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] describing a coordinated effort within the intelligence community to manufacture a conspiracy used to support the impeachment.[3] Newly disclosed transcripts of Atkinson's testimony show that he found indications of political bias from the whistleblower but still advanced the complaint as an "urgent concern"; ODNI says he failed to conduct basic due diligence and exceeded his authority.[3]
