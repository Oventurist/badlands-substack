# Nigeria

Nigeria is a West African state led in 2026 by President [[bola-tinubu|Bola Tinubu]], and the site of a long-running insurgency waged first by [[boko-haram|Boko Haram]] and later by [[islamic-state|Islamic State]]-affiliated militants.[1]

## Joint operation against Islamic State leadership (May 2026)

Nigeria's State House announced on 16 May 2026 that Nigerian and U.S. forces had conducted a joint counterterrorism operation killing [[abu-bilal-al-manuki|Abu-Bilal Al-Manuki]], also known as Abu-Mainok, along with several of his lieutenants at his compound in the Lake Chad Basin.[1] The Nigerian Army placed the strike in Metele, Borno State, carried out in close coordination with U.S. [[africom|Africa Command]] between roughly 12:01 a.m. and 4 a.m. on a Saturday, with no Nigerian casualties or asset losses reported.[1]

## Insurgency and trafficking

Badlands commentary noted that before Islamic State-branded groups operated in the region, Boko Haram — affiliated with [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] — was the dominant militant organization in Nigeria, and that Al-Manuki's most notorious operation was the 2014 kidnapping of hundreds of Nigerian schoolgirls, victims the commentary asserted were most likely trafficked.[1] It further noted that 110 of the girls were rescued in Nigeria in March 2018, in an area where U.S. Special Forces were operating according to analysis of the [[tongo-tongo-ambush|Tongo Tongo Ambush]].[1]

## Role in the ECOWAS crisis

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted in December 2025 that Nigeria heads [[ecowas|ECOWAS]], meaning it is where the power that controls the West African region resides — a point raised in connection with the bloc's rejection of the military transition roadmap in [[guinea-bissau|Guinea-Bissau]] and with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s recent threat to enter Nigeria "guns-a-blazing."[2]

## Trump threat of military action (November 2025)

In a Truth Social post on Saturday, November 1, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] ordered the [[department-of-war|Department of War]] to prepare for possible military action in Nigeria, accusing the West African nation of allowing the killing of Christians by Islamist militants.[3] Trump accused Abuja of failing to protect Christians and warned that the United States "may very well go into that now disgraced country, guns-a-blazing, to completely wipe out the Islamic Terrorists," adding, "If we attack, it will be fast, vicious, and sweet, just like the terrorist thugs attack our cherished Christians!"[3] He offered no evidence of the alleged targeting of Christians but warned that "the Nigerian government better move fast" in confronting the insurgency; Pentagon chief [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] replied on X that his department "is preparing for action."[3]

Nigeria is split between a Muslim-majority north and a largely Christian south, and has long been plagued by violence from groups such as [[boko-haram|Boko Haram]], responsible for massacres, kidnappings, and bombings nationwide.[3] The attacks have killed tens of thousands and displaced more than two million people since around 2009, targeting both Christians and Muslims; though often characterized as sectarian, analysts cite land disputes, resource competition, and ethnic tensions as key drivers.[3]

Badlands host GhostofBasedPatrickHenry situated the threat in a regional frame, noting that Nigeria is the headquarters of [[ecowas|ECOWAS]], the political and economic union governing West Africa, which has been engaged in a proxy conflict against Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger since those states left the bloc to form the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]].[3] He said Nigeria and Ivory Coast, another ECOWAS power center, had engaged in subversive action against Sahelian leadership, and argued it was unsurprising for a globalist power center to target Christians within its own citizenry.[3] He also noted that Trump had invited Burkina Faso's President Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] to his January inauguration, an invitation Traoré declined.[3]
