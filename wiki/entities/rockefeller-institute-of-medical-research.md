# Rockefeller Institute of Medical Research

The Rockefeller Institute of Medical Research was a medical research organization founded in 1901 with funding from the Rockefeller family. In Badlands Media commentary by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]], its founding is identified as the defining turning point in the capture of the American healthcare system — the first of what Carlson calls the four pillars of government control over Americans.[1]

## Role in the "four pillars" framework

Carlson argues that the American population is governed by four interlocking systems — healthcare, mainstream media, the financial system and the election system — and that if a chronological order of construction were assigned to them, the healthcare pillar came first, dated to the Institute's 1901 founding.[1] The remaining benchmarks he cites are 1913 for finance (the creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] and the income tax), 1960 for elections, and the 1960s launch of [[operation-mockingbird|Operation Mockingbird]] for media.[1]

## Alleged method of capture

According to Carlson, the Rockefellers' takeover of medicine was gradual rather than abrupt. The family first funded medical schools and medical research; once those schools became financially dependent on Rockefeller money, the funders were positioned to influence curricula. This produced a shift away from natural remedies toward chemical-based treatments and gave rise to the modern pharmaceutical industry, in which vaccines and pharmaceutical drugs became the preferred response to every ailment and potential ailment.[1]

With schools and research funding aligned, Carlson writes, one obstacle remained — licensure — and the [[american-medical-association|American Medical Association]] was created to close it. Control of licensing meant control over who could legally practice medicine; physicians who continued to practice the natural medicine that had been the norm for thousands of years were, in his account, dismissed as cranks.[1]

## Broader Rockefeller influence

Carlson contends the family's reach was not confined to medicine but extended to the other three pillars, and to organized religion and education as well. He alleges the Rockefellers funded and promoted Christian leaders who would dilute the religion while simultaneously removing Christianity from schools, and promoted concepts such as evolution as a widely accepted alternative, such that "science became a religion to many." He treats the contemporary "woke" school system as the delayed product of that slowly implemented plan rather than an organic development.[1]

## Claimed consequences and decline

The result, in this account, is a healthcare system that profits from keeping Americans sick, with rising use of pharmaceutical drugs alongside growing rates of cancer, obesity and diabetes.[1] Carlson cites polling showing that early in the COVID-19 pandemic 28.5% of Americans did not trust doctors and hospitals, and that fewer than four years later the figure had reached 59.9% — roughly 60% distrust before [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] had even entered the incoming Trump administration.[1] He predicts the healthcare pillar has no chance of surviving 2025, noting that most structures can stand on three pillars but no fewer.[1]
