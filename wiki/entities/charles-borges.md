# Charles Borges

Charles Borges is a United States federal data executive who served as Chief Data Officer of the [[social-security-administration|Social Security Administration]] (SSA) and who resigned from that post in late August 2025 amid a dispute over data-handling practices at the agency.[1]

## Whistleblower report

Days before resigning, Borges filed a whistleblower report alleging policy violations connected to the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]]'s decision to upload sensitive Social Security data to what he characterized as a "vulnerable cloud environment."[1] In his resignation letter, submitted to Social Security Administrator [[frank-bisignano|Frank Bisignano]], he wrote that he had been made aware of "several projects and incidents which may constitute violations of federal statutes or regulations," involving "the potential safety and security of high value data assets in the cloud," possibly "unauthorized or inappropriate access to agency enterprise data storage solutions," and possibly "unauthorized data exchange with other agencies."[1]

## "Culture of panic and dread"

Borges also described the internal climate at the agency, writing that "newly installed leadership in IT and executive offices created a culture of panic and dread, with minimal information sharing, frequent discussions on employee termination, and general organizational dysfunction."[1] He said staff were afraid to raise concerns for fear of termination or other retribution.[1] The SSA did not immediately comment on the letter.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the resignation was less about technology than culture, noting that government cloud environments are broadly vulnerable because security is a shared responsibility and agencies cannot match private-sector talent.[1] She contended that, given what DOGE claimed to have found in Social Security data, it was unsurprising that the Chief Data Officer would "dread sunlight on his work product," adding that accountability for the period in which the alleged data fraud occurred still rested with him despite his departure, and citing a base salary reported at upwards of $200,000 per year.[1]
