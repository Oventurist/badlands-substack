# Team B

Team B was a think tank formed in 1976 to produce a counter-argument to the official National Intelligence Estimate — the assessment referred to in the exercise as "Team A" — on Soviet strategic intentions.[1] It is documented at length by [[paul-fitzgerald|Paul Fitzgerald]] and [[elizabeth-gould|Elizabeth Gould]] in [[valediction-three-nights-of-desmond|Valediction: Three Nights of Desmond]].[1]

## Origins

Team B revived the earlier [[committee-on-the-present-danger|Committee on the Present Danger]] led by financier [[paul-nitze|Paul Nitze]], who in 1950 had used that organisation to spearhead passage of [[nsc-68|NSC-68]], the document that first justified the notion that the United States should maximise its build-up of nuclear warheads on the supposition that it was in a moral equivalent of war with Russia.[1]

Throughout the 1960s, saner forces pushed back against Nitze's Committee, producing the nuclear test ban treaty, the Open Skies Treaty, the Space Treaty and other trust-building measures.[1] The 1972 [[salt-i|SALT]] agreement extended those mechanisms, limiting the growth of U.S. nuclear warheads on the presumption that Russia would do the same while both sides respected each other's spheres of influence.[1] In the minds of Nitze, [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]] and a growing hive of neoconservative right-wingers including [[paul-wolfowitz|Paul Wolfowitz]], [[richard-pipes|Richard Pipes]], [[richard-perle|Richard Perle]] and [[george-hw-bush|George H. W. Bush]] — rising in power and prestige across the presidencies of [[gerald-ford|Ford]], [[jimmy-carter|Carter]] and [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]] — this push toward trust and cooperation had to stop.[1]

## The exercise

Where the NIE at the time still maintained that the threat posed by Russia would decrease if its sense of security and stability were increased, Team B asserted the opposite, promoting what the article calls the fictitious idea of an evil empire committed to becoming a global Soviet hegemon.[1] The debates were heavily tilted in Team B's favour: the champions selected to represent the Team A assessment were staffed by incompetent second-rate minds, wholly out of their depth and incapable of refuting the vast data-crunching sophistry of Nitze and his neoconservative team.[1]

## Legacy

Though history has demonstrated Team B's thesis to be an artificial construction, the propaganda succeeded, and by 1978 the Trilateral-run coup of U.S. intelligence was nearly complete.[1] A newly reorganised system of international clandestine operations was then launched to conduct asymmetric warfare against Russia and against any other force, east or west, that did not fit Brzezinski's emerging "technetronic age."[1]

Fitzgerald and Gould characterise the phenomenon in these terms: "developed by an inbred class of former Trotskyist intellectuals, the Team B approach represented a radical transformation of America's national security bureaucracy into a new kind of elitist cult."[1] [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]] was premised on the lies of the Team B analysis of Soviet ambitions, and the Soviet entry into Afghanistan was later presented as the living proof Team B's fictitious thesis required.[1] [[george-hw-bush|George H. W. Bush]], as CIA director, defended the Team B takeover of U.S. intelligence estimates.[1]
