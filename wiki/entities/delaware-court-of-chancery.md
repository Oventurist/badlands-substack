# Delaware Court of Chancery

The Delaware Court of Chancery is the equity court of the State of Delaware, cited by Badlands Media as the clearest surviving example of eighteenth-century British equity law operating inside the United States.[1]

## Jurisdiction as evidence of British continuity

A February 16, 2023 Badlands Media essay quotes the court's own description of its authority, that the "general equity jurisdiction of the [Delaware] Court [of Chancery] is measured in terms of the general equity jurisdiction of the High Court of Chancery of Great Britain as it existed prior to the separation of the American colonies."[1] From this the article concludes that British equity law, as practiced in the eighteenth century, was imported into Delaware and operates there to this day.[1]

The essay notes as a side point that Delaware was also the first state to ratify the Constitution, the origin of its nickname "the first state."[1] It presents the court alongside the training of early American jurists at the [[middle-temple|Middle Temple]] and the other [[inns-of-court|inns of court]] — institutions the article credits with creating [[equity-jurisprudence|equity jurisprudence]] and the chancery courts — as evidence that separation from the [[british-crown|British Crown]] was never complete, and that this legal continuity helped pave the way for [[federal-reserve|the Federal Reserve]].[1]
