# Eyal Zamir

Lt. Gen. Eyal Zamir is the Chief of the General Staff of the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] (IDF).[1] He was appointed by Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] after his predecessor was made the scapegoat for the October 7 attacks, and is a career soldier who served on tank crews — a background that Badlands commentary treats as giving him credibility regarding the perspective of the average IDF soldier.[1]

## Warning of IDF collapse

In remarks during a security cabinet meeting reported in March 2026, Zamir warned that the IDF "could soon collapse if there is no solution to the shortage of manpower," telling ministers, "I am raising 10 red flags before the IDF collapses into itself," according to reporting confirmed by *The Jerusalem Post*.[1]

Zamir's warning centered on a persistent shortfall of enlisted soldiers — reportedly between 10,000 and 15,000 men since October 7 — and the resulting over-reliance on reserve mobilization, with some reservists serving as many as six or seven combat tours in the period since.[1]

## Context

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the structural analysis Zamir voiced had circulated among outside commentators for years: the IDF is a comparatively small force that has never fought a protracted war, and because Israeli reservists constitute the working population of the Israeli economy, sustained mobilization over a roughly two-and-a-half-year period generates severe economic strain, closing small businesses and degrading supply chains and services.[1] Roughly 375,000 reservists had reportedly been mobilized since October 7, and the [[knesset|Knesset]] approved the mobilization of an additional 400,000.[1]

Zamir's warning coincided with Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] announcing a large-scale invasion of southern Lebanon aimed at destroying [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and annexing territory south of the Litani River, alongside possible escalation with [[iran|Iran]].[1] Badlands commentary concluded that a force stretched across a multi-front war and dependent on repeatedly redeployed reservists faces a rising likelihood of collapse or mutiny against military or government leadership.[1]

## Condemnation of looting and indiscipline (April 2026)

In remarks reported on April 29, 2026, Zamir publicly condemned a series of unethical incidents by Israeli soldiers, warning that discipline had eroded during the multi-front wars since the October 7, 2023 [[hamas|Hamas]] attack.[2] Speaking to senior officers at Ramat David Airbase, he described looting in southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]], the destruction of a statue of Jesus in a Lebanese Christian village that prompted international outrage, and soldiers wearing unauthorized Velcro patches bearing inciteful, political, religious or messianic messages — including one reading "Stop the hatred. It is time for violence" — as a "rebellion against IDF values."[2] He said such conduct "could stain the entire IDF" and declared, "We must not compromise on our values. The erosion of norms could be no less dangerous than operational threats."[2]

Zamir cited accountability measures already taken, including the dismissal from combat service and jailing of two soldiers involved in smashing the Jesus statue on April 19, with the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] later replacing the statue.[2] He also addressed alleged looting of homes in depopulated southern Lebanese areas and troops' misuse of social media for self-promotion or controversial messaging, vowing full investigations and enforcement against both regular and reserve soldiers, while noting the military must remain prepared for intense fighting through 2026.[2] Days earlier he had ordered investigations into the looting claims, stating, "I am not willing for us to become an army of looters."[2]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] linked these statements to Zamir's earlier warning about imminent IDF collapse from manpower shortages, and argued that the circulation of soldiers' looting and desecration videos without consequence served to inflame Israel's neighbors, swelling militia ranks and raising the odds of a state intervention that could in turn justify further IDF territorial seizures.[2] He characterized Zamir as an earnest actor attempting to rein in an increasingly immoral military fighting its first prolonged campaign, noting reported spikes in suicide and depression among reservists attributed to combat trauma.[2] Zamir also issued public condemnations after Ultra-Orthodox extremists broke into the home of the chief of the Military Police, an escalation tied to arrests of yeshiva students refusing conscription after the Israeli Supreme Court ended their exemption.[2]

## Pentagon visit on Iran (2026)

In February 2026 Zamir led a senior Israeli military delegation on a previously undisclosed visit to the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] to present intelligence and discuss military options concerning [[iran|Iran]], amid the growing U.S. force presence in the region.[3] The visit coincided with reporting that U.S. officials were preparing for potential conflict with Iran while able to carry out only limited strikes.[3]
