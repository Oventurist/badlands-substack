# Charles Masterman

**Charles Masterman** was a British writer and Liberal Member of Parliament appointed by future Prime Minister David Lloyd George to head [[wellington-house|Wellington House]], Britain's War Propaganda Bureau, formed in 1914.[1]

Masterman invited 25 leading British authors to Wellington House to discuss the best ways of promoting Britain's interests during the First World War, with their biggest focus being to draw the United States into the war on Britain's side.[1] Attendees included Arthur Conan Doyle, G. K. Chesterton, Thomas Hardy and H. G. Wells, while Rudyard Kipling was invited but unable to attend.[1] The bureau he ran operated under the supervision of the British Foreign Office and distributed literary propaganda worldwide.[1]
