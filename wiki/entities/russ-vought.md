# Russ Vought

Russ Vought is the Director of the Office of Management and Budget in the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]], the official responsible for directing federal agencies' budget planning and shutdown contingency preparations.[1]

## Shutdown reduction-in-force strategy (September 2025)

In late September 2025, ahead of a possible lapse in appropriations on October 1, Vought's [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]] instructed federal agencies to prepare reduction-in-force (RIF) plans for mass firings during a potential government shutdown, specifically targeting employees working on programs not legally required to continue.[1] A memo shared with Politico ahead of its release to agencies directed them to identify programs, projects and activities whose discretionary funding would lapse on October 1 with no alternative funding source available, and to begin drafting RIF plans that would go beyond standard furloughs by permanently eliminating jobs in programs not consistent with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s priorities.[1]

The approach marked a significant break from how shutdowns had been handled in recent decades, when most furloughs were temporary and employees were restored once [[congress|Congress]] voted to reopen the government.[1] Vought was described as using the threat of permanent job cuts as leverage, raising the stakes in the spending standoff with congressional Democrats.[1] OMB wrote in the memo that "programs that did not benefit from an infusion of mandatory appropriations will bear the brunt of a shutdown."[1] Badlands contributor Jordan Sather responded to the reporting with a two-word endorsement: "SEND IT."[1]

## October 2025 shutdown actions and Republican unease

After the shutdown took effect on October 1, 2025, Senate Majority Leader [[john-thune|John Thune]] publicly distanced himself from Vought's approach, saying, "This is the risk of shutting down the government and handing the keys to Russ Vought," and that Democrats should have expected [[office-of-management-and-budget|OMB]] to broadly target government workers and programs.[2] Several Republicans aired discomfort the same day: Rep. Mike Lawler of New York objected to Vought's decision to hold up major transportation projects in his state, while Reps. Blake Moore of Utah and Brian Babin of Texas raised concerns about potential mass layoffs on a private House GOP call with Vought.[2]

Reporting noted that Vought's actions risked distracting from the Republican message placing the onus on Democrats to reopen the government; asked whether Vought was muddying the waters, Thune replied, "The only thing I would say about that is yes, and we don't control what he's going to do."[2] Separately, an OMB memo confirmed that construction of the [[white-house-ballroom|White House ballroom]] would continue during the shutdown because it was privately funded and not tied to a FY26 enacted appropriation.[2]

## Pocket rescissions (July 2025)

Speaking on CNN's "State of the Union" on Sunday, July 27, 2025, Vought said the administration was considering the use of [[pocket-rescissions|"pocket rescissions"]] — a maneuver that withholds appropriated funds during the 45-day congressional review window at the close of the fiscal year so that they expire without congressional action.[3] "It's very possible that we might use pocket rescissions. It's one of our executive tools. It's been used before," he said.[3]

Critics called the approach an overreach of executive power and [[congress|Congress]] expressed concerns, but Vought countered that the Government Accountability Office "has said it was legal in the 1970s."[3] In a clip circulated by Badlands host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]], Vought described it as "a fully legal approach to using the impoundment control act... to send up a recissions bill later in the year and be able to have that evaporate at the end of the fiscal year," adding that "nothing is taken off the table for us to be able to deal with the deficits we have."[3] Herold noted that he and [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] had discussed the tactic weeks before it surfaced publicly.[3]

## Shutdown enforcer

During the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|October 2025 government shutdown]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly cast Vought as the instrument of permanent cuts to "Democrat agencies," posting on Truth Social that "I have a meeting today with Russ Vought, he of PROJECT 2025 Fame."[4] The BBC described the director of the [[office-of-management-and-budget|Office of Management and Budget]] as a figure who "may not be a household name" but whose [[project-2025|Project 2025]] blueprint — a conservative governing plan assembled largely by former Trump officials while Republicans were out of power — had featured prominently in the previous year's presidential campaign, frequently invoked by Democratic nominee [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] as Trump's "dangerous plan."[4] Trump was reported to be holding up Vought, author of the blueprint's chapter on executive power, as a budgetary angel of death, and on the Thursday night of the shutdown's first week shared an AI-generated parody music video portraying Vought as the grim reaper set to altered lyrics of Blue Öyster Cult's "Don't Fear the Reaper."[4] Badlands host [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] said he and [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] had forecast the Trump–Vought cut strategy in advance and that it was proceeding as expected.[4]
