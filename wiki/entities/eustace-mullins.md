# Eustace Mullins

Eustace Mullins was an American conspiracy writer and former assistant to Senator Joseph McCarthy, identified by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as one of the principal students of [[george-sylvester-viereck|George Sylvester Viereck]].[1]

Mullins, along with Ben Freedman and the pro-Nazi revisionist David Irving, used Viereck's 1932 book The Strangest Friendship in History: Woodrow Wilson and Colonel House as the foundation of his own explanatory model of history — a model Ehret characterizes as neglecting the British imperial agencies and occultists running the American deep state while popularizing the Protocols of the Learned Elders of Zion forgery.[1]

In his essay "Victims," Mullins described his friendship with Viereck and his indignation at [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]]'s persecution of him, writing that "another friend, George Sylvester Viereck, was marked for personal persecution by the insane cripple, Franklin Delano Roosevelt, because Viereck refused to turn his back on his own heritage and do the bidding of this twisted and misshapen madman."[1]
