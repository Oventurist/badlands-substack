# Kevin McCarthy

Kevin McCarthy is the American Republican politician who was elected Speaker of the House of Representatives in January 2023, and who is treated in Badlands Media commentary as one of the central ambiguities of the period.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## The speakership fight

Writing in June 2023, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] recalled that his initial assessment of McCarthy during the speakership contest was that he was "just another weak Republican, maybe even a RINO," and that he sided with the [[house-freedom-caucus-holdouts|Congressmen who opposed him]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) Carlson noted the apparent contradiction that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] endorsed McCarthy while also endorsing the members blocking him, and concluded from this that Trump must have had leverage over McCarthy.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

After McCarthy was confirmed and the members who had opposed him began receiving significant leadership roles on congressional committees, Carlson judged the sequence coherent: it needed to appear that McCarthy had been forced into those appointments in order to win the gavel, so that the [[neoconservatives|Neocons]] and Democrats would still regard him as working with them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) On that reading Carlson did not fully trust McCarthy but accepted that he was "playing ball" with Trump.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

Supporting that interpretation, Carlson argued that McCarthy's elevation was treated as a threat by the [[deep-state|Deep State]], because immediately afterward events went "into over-drive" — the discovery of boxes of classified documents, train derailments, Chinese spy balloons, and the uncovering of the Nord Stream pipeline sabotage — all read as signs of full-on panic.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## The debt limit reversal

The [[debt-ceiling-agreement-2023|debt limit agreement]] forced Carlson to reconsider. He asked whether an increased debt limit with virtually no spending cuts could be part of Trump's plan, whether more evidence of a [[uniparty|Uniparty]] was needed to wake people up, whether the economy had to worsen before Trump returned, and whether the episode served the destruction of the Cabal banking system.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

He also raised the possibility that Trump endorsed McCarthy specifically so that he could be quickly ousted — that McCarthy "caving" to the Democrats was itself the mechanism for exposing the RINOs and Neocons — noting that America First members had pushed for and obtained rules changes making it easier to impeach the Speaker.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) Carlson's conclusion is that McCarthy must be viewed from multiple angles at once: working willingly with Trump, or working with him unwillingly as an instrument of exposure.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## Included in Jack Smith's message review (disclosed 2026)

[[chuck-grassley|Sen. Chuck Grassley]] disclosed in July 2026 that McCarthy, as then-GOP House Leader, was among the 44 members of [[us-congress|Congress]] whose text messages were obtained and reviewed by former special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s team.[2] The messages had been sent to senior [[white-house|White House]] officials in the final weeks of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term and were produced to prosecutors by the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]] in August 2023.[2]

## Promotion of the debt ceiling deal (2023)

Having "negotiated" the [[debt-ceiling-agreement-2023|2023 debt ceiling deal]] on behalf of America's conservatives, McCarthy repeatedly pointed to the resulting spending reduction on his Twitter feed, treating it as an achievement.[3] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] characterized the underlying figure — roughly a 0.2% of GDP cut in nominal spending — as "paltry" and "laughable," noting that it would not stop total debt from reaching $35 trillion by the end of [[joe-biden|Biden]]'s term.[3]

## Removal as Speaker and succession (October 2023)

McCarthy was ousted from the speakership following a motion to vacate led by Rep. [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] (R-Fla.), leaving the House without a Speaker for three weeks.[4] The Republican conference cycled through several nominees before electing Rep. [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] (R-La.), who won 220 votes on the first floor ballot; Reps. [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]] (R-La.) and [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] (R-Ohio) had failed to gather sufficient support, and Rep. [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]] (R-Minn.) withdrew hours after winning the nomination.[4]

Gaetz cast the transition as evidence of a shift in the party's center of gravity, saying that "moving from Kevin McCarthy to MAGA Mike Johnson shows the ascendance of this movement and where the power in the Republican Party truly lies."[4] See [[2023-speaker-of-the-house-election]].

## Rules changes and the 2023 ouster

When McCarthy was elected Speaker at the start of the 118th Congress, the [[house-freedom-caucus-holdouts|Freedom Caucus]] negotiated changes to the House rules to get him over the finish line.[5] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that House leadership subsequently suspended those rules when they conflicted with its in-the-moment desires, which is what pushed Rep. [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] to lead the motion that successfully removed McCarthy from power in October 2023.[5] The ensuing vacancy left McCarthy ally [[patrick-mchenry|Patrick McHenry]] as Speaker Pro Tempore and froze the chamber for three weeks.[5]

## Removal from the speakership

McCarthy was voted out of the speakership on Tuesday, October 3, 2023, in what the Associated Press called an extraordinary showdown and a first in U.S. history, forced by a contingent of hard-right conservatives and throwing the House and its Republican leadership into chaos.[6] Neither the right-flank Republicans who engineered his ouster nor the Democrats who piled on appeared open to negotiating, leaving McCarthy — who had said repeatedly that he never gives up — with almost no options remaining.[6] He told lawmakers that evening that he would not run again for Speaker, putting the gavel up for grabs, and action in the House was halted until the following week.[6]

At a Capitol press conference McCarthy alternated between an upbeat assessment of his speakership and angry score-settling against those who removed him, saying, "I may have lost this vote today, but as I walk out of this chamber I feel fortunate to have served."[6] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that McCarthy functions in the [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]] as the emblem of the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] Speaker, the Old Guard and the Establishment — everything [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], [[america-first-movement|America First]] and Matt Gaetz stand opposed to — and that if McCarthy had been Trump's pick for Speaker, the purpose was to help expose the regime rather than reform it.[6] See [[ouster-of-kevin-mccarthy]].

## January 6 footage and Ukraine aid (February 2023)

In February 2023 McCarthy reached an apparent deal granting [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] access to thousands of hours of Capitol security footage from January 6, 2021, an arrangement that surprised Capitol Police Chief [[thomas-manger|Thomas Manger]] and House Sergeant at Arms [[william-mcfarland|William McFarland]], who told associates they learned of it only when [[axios|Axios]] broke the story on Monday, February 20.[7] [[politico|Politico]] reported that [[united-states-capitol-police|Capitol Police]] had been extremely reluctant to share large swaths of the footage over safety concerns for lawmakers, aides and officers.[7]

In the same period Rep. [[ralph-norman|Ralph Norman]] (R-SC) told [[breitbart-news|Breitbart News]] that McCarthy had set the "gears in place" for conservatives on the House Rules Committee to strip Ukraine aid from must-pass spending bills such as omnibus packages and continuing resolutions.[7] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] wrote that McCarthy was "beginning to silence the doubters," calling the footage release big and arguing that stripping Ukraine funding would be "huge," since the Regime had likely assumed the Speaker lacked the political will to do it while the war remained deeply unpopular.[7]

## Market fallout from the ouster

The 216-to-210 vote that removed McCarthy on the evening of October 3, 2023, was driven by hardline Republicans angered at his willingness to fund Ukraine's war rather than direct the money to the southern border and to restoring order in major U.S. cities.[8] His removal weighed immediately on defense stocks, as traders concluded that any successor would struggle to secure further Ukraine funding, and European defense names including Rheinmetall, Saab, BAE Systems, and Leonardo slid the following morning.[8] [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]] analyst Alec Phillips wrote that the leadership change raised the odds of a November government shutdown and that the next Speaker would face even greater pressure than McCarthy to avoid another temporary funding extension or additional Ukraine aid.[8]

### Biden impeachment inquiry (September 2023)

On 12 September 2023, McCarthy announced that the House would open an impeachment inquiry into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] over an array of alleged crimes, bypassing the formal House floor vote he had previously suggested would precede such a step.[9] McCarthy said the inquiry would be spearheaded by Oversight Committee chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] and Judiciary Committee chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]].[9] Justifying the move, McCarthy alleged that "Biden used his official office to coordinate with Hunter Biden's business partners, about Hunter's role in [[burisma|Burisma]] — the Ukrainian energy company," and added that "it appears that the president's family has been offered special treatment by Biden's own administration."[9] Badlands Media commentators framed the announcement less as a likely path to removal than as a disclosure vehicle, comparing its potential reach to the ratings and cultural saturation of the Trump impeachment hearings.[9]

## Blocking Schiff and Swalwell from the Intelligence Committee (2023)

On January 24, 2023, in his first month as Speaker, McCarthy made good on his pledge to block California Democratic Representatives [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] and [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] from sitting on the [[house-intelligence-committee|House Intelligence Committee]].[10] Special rules for the panel allow the Speaker to unilaterally halt the seating of lawmakers without a full floor vote, and McCarthy informed House Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] of the decision in a letter, writing that "in order to maintain a standard worthy of this committee's responsibilities, I am hereby rejecting the appointments of Representative Adam Schiff and Representative Eric Swalwell to serve on the Intelligence Committee."[10] Jeffries had recommended both men despite McCarthy's earlier vow to block them along with [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]].[10] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] observed that McCarthy had so far lived up to his word, recalling [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]]'s comment after the drawn-out speakership vote that "trust is earned," but cautioned against complacency given that the 118th Congress was only one month old and would present many further tests of which side McCarthy was on.[10]

## October 2023 motion to vacate threat

On October 1, 2023, McCarthy responded on CBS News' "Face the Nation" to a threat by Representative [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] (R-FL) to remove him from the Speakership, saying the dispute was "personal" for the Florida lawmaker.[11] "That's nothing new, he's tried to do that from the moment I ran for office," McCarthy said, adding, "I'll survive, you know this is personal with Matt."[11] He also attacked Gaetz's record, noting that "Matt voted against the most conservative bill to protect our border."[11] Gaetz, appearing the same morning on ABC's "This Week," announced he would file a [[motion-to-vacate|motion to vacate]] the chair that week.[11]

The immediate trigger was reporting that McCarthy had cut a Ukraine funding deal with Democrats and did not disclose it to his own conference until after the 45-day [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]] had already passed the House.[11] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that if the reporting was accurate, "Kevin has to go," recalling that the January 2023 Speaker fight — in which Gaetz and roughly twenty other members forced fifteen ballots — had produced rules concessions specifically aimed at eliminating omnibus bills and continuing resolutions, concessions that secured McCarthy the gavel.[11] In her reading, McCarthy broke both that agreement and House rules, demonstrating that "the machine will do whatever it takes to get whatever it wants: More billions for Ukraine," and she noted it was not the first time McCarthy had crossed his own caucus during a shutdown panic.[11]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a competing frame, describing the Gaetz–McCarthy confrontation as "perhaps the most obvious bit of [[kayfabe|Kayfabe]] at work in the Info War," on the theory that both men were [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s choices — one for Speaker, the other for opposition to that Speaker — and that the resulting spectacle served an exposure function.[11]

## Protest votes after his ouster (October 2023)

McCarthy remained a factor in the speakership fight that followed his historic removal: in the first ballot on October 17, 2023 he received six protest votes from Republicans declining to back nominee [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], behind [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]]'s seven.[12] Jordan's 200-vote tally that day was reported as lower than any McCarthy had recorded during his own fifteen rounds of voting over four days in January 2023.[12]

## Defense of Trump over the July 2023 target letter

Following Trump's July 2023 announcement that he expected to be indicted and arrested over January 6, House Speaker Kevin McCarthy told reporters that the move followed Trump surpassing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in re-election polling: "So what do they do now? Weaponize government to go after their No. 1 opponent."[13] He added, "This is not equal justice. They treat people differently and they go after their adversaries."[13] Badlands commentary cited McCarthy, alongside [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]], as an example of GOP figures publicly rallying to Trump in what it described as a war for the public mind.[13]

## September 2023 revolt over Ukraine supplemental

In early September 2023 McCarthy faced an internal revolt from House Freedom Caucus members over the Biden administration's $40 billion supplemental spending request, which included $24 billion in additional funding for Ukraine and which many members wanted separated from the rest of the bill.[14] ZeroHedge described the episode as another round of "shutdown theatre," and labeled McCarthy "Uniparty" for his handling of it.[14] Representative [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] and others warned him that he also needed to launch an impeachment inquiry into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] over his family's foreign business dealings, with Gaetz telling radio host Todd Starnes that "if Speaker McCarthy stands in our way, he may not have the job long."[14] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] maintained that McCarthy held the speakership only because [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had directly encouraged [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]], Gaetz and other Freedom Caucus members to acquiesce in January 2023, and read the ongoing feud as accelerationism dissolving the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] establishment in the public mind.[14] See [[september-2023-ukraine-supplemental-spending-request]].[14]

## Account of Biden's decline in the 2023 debt-ceiling talks

McCarthy, who negotiated with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] over lifting the debt ceiling in 2023, later told The Wall Street Journal that the president's demeanor and command of the details seemed to shift from one day to the next.[15] On some days Biden had loose and spontaneous exchanges with Republicans; on others he mumbled and appeared to rely on notes.[15] "I used to meet with him when he was vice president. I'd go to his house," McCarthy said in an interview. "He's not the same person."[15]

## Response to the Bragg indictment

In late March 2023, as Speaker of the House, McCarthy said Congress would take action to punish Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] after Bragg brought charges against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] following months of downgrading violent felonies against other offenders.[16] His statement came as House Republicans, including Representative [[austin-scott|Austin Scott]], coalesced around proposals to strip politicized local prosecutors of the legal immunity that protects them from civil liability.[16]

## June 2023 rule-vote revolt

On June 6, 2023, eleven Republicans led by [[house-freedom-caucus|House Freedom Caucus]] members broke with their party on a routine procedural rule vote in retaliation for McCarthy's leadership on the previous week's [[debt-ceiling-agreement-2023|debt ceiling]] vote, throwing the week's legislative schedule into disarray in the first failed rule vote in nearly two decades.[17] Having stopped short of moving to oust him, the group demanded a meeting with McCarthy, with Rep. [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] declaring, "We're not going to live in the era of the imperial speaker anymore."[17] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that McCarthy had repeatedly reneged on the concessions he made during the January speakership negotiation — the [[january-6-capitol-security-footage-release|J6 footage release]], fiscal responsibility, and the rules commitments — and that his ability to assemble a passing coalition on the debt ceiling bill showed the holdouts' leverage was largely illusory.[17]

## Successor in the 20th District

In May 2024, California State Assembly member [[vince-fong|Vince Fong]] — a McCarthy protégé who carried the former speaker's endorsement as well as that of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — won a special election to complete the remainder of McCarthy's term through January, defeating Tulare County Sheriff [[mike-boudreaux|Mike Boudreaux]] in the Central Valley district.[18]

## Reported consideration as Trump chief of staff (2024)

In March 2024 a Bloomberg report, relayed by the National Pulse, held that McCarthy was under consideration for White House Chief of Staff should [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] retake the presidency in that November's election.[19] McCarthy had allegedly turned down the same role during Trump's first administration, opting instead to compete for the Speakership — a leadership battle he lost to [[paul-ryan|Paul Ryan]].[19]

Citing anonymous sources, Bloomberg contended that Trump saw McCarthy as possessing valuable assets for the post, including his knowledge of Capitol Hill, his experience in government budgeting, and his alignment with Trump on regulatory priorities.[19] The reporting also noted that McCarthy retained detractors among Trump's most outspoken supporters, including Rep. [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]], who spearheaded [[ouster-of-kevin-mccarthy|McCarthy's ouster]] as Speaker.[19]

Badlands Media commentary was skeptical, calling the report a likely product of fake news bearing "all the classic earmarks, with vague assertions and anonymous sources," while conceding that Trump and McCarthy retain a relationship — Trump having endorsed him for Speaker — and that Trump himself had remained largely silent on the matter.[19]

## September 2023 shutdown standoff

In September 2023 McCarthy faced what was described as perhaps the biggest challenge of his eight months as Speaker: avoiding another government shutdown with a fractured caucus before the September 30 funding deadline.[20] He had cleared a major hurdle the week before by announcing an impeachment inquiry into the Biden family, which preceded a Sunday-night proposal for a 30-day stopgap bill allowing House GOP leaders to move separate measures funding the Pentagon and DHS.[20] Doubts persisted over whether he had the votes, as more than a dozen conservatives objected that the [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]] continued to fund the Ukraine war and "woke" policies, leaving him a reported four-vote margin.[20] The episode is covered at [[september-2023-government-shutdown-standoff|September 2023 government shutdown standoff]].[20]

## "File the f***ing motion" outburst (September 2023)

On Thursday, September 14, 2023, McCarthy lost his temper in a closed-door GOP conference meeting, telling colleagues, "If you want to file the motion, file the fucking motion," in response to threats to remove him over unmet demands from [[house-freedom-caucus|Freedom Caucus]] members.[21] The outburst followed criticism earlier that week from Rep. [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]], who had presented a list of demands while threatening a [[motion-to-vacate|motion to vacate]] the chair, and who argued that McCarthy's belated announcement of a Biden impeachment inquiry was insufficient.[21] "I showed frustration in here because I am frustrated," McCarthy told reporters afterward. "Frustrated with some people in the conference."[21]

Gaetz responded that "instead of emotionally cursing, maybe the Speaker should just keep his word from January on balanced budgets, term limits and single-subject spending bills."[21] Other Republicans, including Rep. [[brian-mast|Brian Mast]], sided with McCarthy's exasperation, saying that if somebody wanted to file a motion to vacate they should do so and "stop holding it over people's head" like a noose.[21] The episode unfolded alongside another round of what ZeroHedge called shutdown theater over stopgap funding and Ukraine aid.[21]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that it was rare to see McCarthy abandon decorum and that his visible pressure from the Freedom Caucus was a good sign, noting he had yet to deliver on balanced budgets, term limits or single-subject spending bills; she judged that the Freedom Caucus lacked the votes to vacate the chair without Democratic help, which was unlikely because McCarthy had been useful to Democrats.[21] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the fight as kabuki theater in Congress, observing that McCarthy was seen as representative of the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] establishment yet was also [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s pick for Speaker while Gaetz and the Freedom Caucus served as Trump's de facto representatives — a net effect he described as America First rising while the controlled demolition of the Uniparty continued.[21]

## Attack on Alvin Bragg at the 2023 House GOP Retreat

At a press conference on March 19, 2023, as House Republicans gathered for the first day of a three-day retreat to formulate their 2023 agenda, Speaker McCarthy sharply criticized Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]] over the charges expected that week against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[22] Asked by [[breitbart-news|Breitbart News]] what he would say to the 75 million Americans who voted for Trump in 2020, McCarthy replied, "I wouldn't just talk to 75 million Americans — I would talk to 330 million Americans."[22]

"It doesn't matter what side of the issue you're on. It doesn't matter if this was President Trump or if this was a Democrat. It should be equal justice in America. And stop going after people because you have political differences," McCarthy said.[22] He added that he regularly encounters people he disagrees with politically but respects their opinions, asking, "why would you ever try to use the law for it?"[22] McCarthy said Bragg's conduct "goes directly against everything this Manhattan DA has professed," citing Bragg's record of downgrading felonies to misdemeanors while New Yorkers were "afraid to walk the streets," crediting Republican election wins in New York partly to that record, and asserting that the statute of limitations in the Trump matter had expired.[22] He concluded that "this is the type of thing America hates and divides us and is wrong."[22]

## Resignation from Congress (December 2023)

On December 6, 2023, McCarthy announced in a Wall Street Journal opinion piece that he would resign his California congressional seat at the end of the year, two months after being [[ouster-of-kevin-mccarthy|ousted as House speaker]].[23] "No matter the odds, or personal cost, we did the right thing," he wrote, saying he would "depart the House at the end of this year to serve America in new ways" and that his work was "only getting started."[23] He pledged to keep recruiting candidates for elected office and to lend his experience to "the next generation of leaders," adding that "it often seems that the more Washington does, the worse America gets" and that the country's challenges "are more likely to be solved by innovation than legislation."[23]
