# Bank of Canada

The **Bank of Canada** is Canada's central bank, and is notable in recent politics as the institution once headed by [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] before his entry into elected office.[1]

## Carney's tenure and political significance

Carney led the Bank of Canada during the 2008 economic downturn, and later served as governor of the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]] through the Brexit turmoil, after an earlier career in investment banking.[1] That central-banking résumé became the core of his political appeal in the [[2025-canadian-federal-election|2025 Canadian federal election]]: his background in finance and his seemingly unflappable demeanour helped convince voters that he was the candidate best able to handle U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his sometimes erratic policies.[1] Carney had never held political office before being named leader of the [[liberal-party-of-canada|Liberal Party]] in March 2025.[1]

## Financial Stability Report warning on tariffs (May 2025)

On Thursday, 8 May 2025 the Bank of Canada warned that market volatility resulting from United States tariffs could produce a sharp repricing in assets and, in extreme circumstances, result in "market dysfunction."[2] Governor [[tiff-macklem|Tiff Macklem]] said in prepared remarks in Ottawa that "the Canadian economy and financial system face a new threat," adding that "[t]ariffs and uncertainty have sharply reduced prospects for global economic growth" and that "financial markets have been rocked by chaotic policy announcements and reversals."[2]

The warning came in the central bank's Financial Stability Report, which set out the key risks facing Canada's financial system and identified President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s protectionist trade policies — which had roiled global markets the previous month — as an ongoing source of uncertainty for the Canadian economy.[2]
