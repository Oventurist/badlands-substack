# Ken Buck

Ken Buck (R-Colo.) was a Republican member of the United States House of Representatives from Colorado during the 118th Congress.[1] He represented Colorado's 4th congressional district and was a former prosecutor who frequently invoked his "firsthand experience with the deterrence power of prison."[3]

## Elections and 2020 election posture

On a GOP-wide virtual call held on December 2, 2020, Buck sought to reassure Colorado voters, saying that "it is so important that people have confidence in their elections," and that while he could not speak for other states, in Colorado "we're doing it the right way and we have confidence in our election results."[3] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], a constituent, wrote that she had previously regarded Buck as an honorable man but concluded after the 2020 election that he had "sided with the regime."[3]

Buck continued to defend Colorado's election administration despite the three Mesa County forensic reports, the [[useip|USEIP]] Colorado canvassing report, and a system-vulnerabilities report published on April 24, 2021.[3] He also remained silent on Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]]'s permanent rules blocking Arizona-style audits, her pursuit of [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]], and legislative changes centralizing election oversight from Colorado's counties to the state.[3]

In the run-up to the 2022 election Buck was defeated at the district assembly by floor nominee Bob Lewis, who took 62% of the vote; Buck nonetheless made the primary ballot and won both the primary and re-election, an outcome Ashe in America attributed to rigged elections.[3] Buck later publicly denied the dissident account of January 6 and exchanged barbs with Georgia Representative [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] over the treatment of January 6 defendants.[3]

## September 2023 letter on January 6 defendants

On August 21, 2023, [[todd-watkins|Todd Watkins]], vice chairman of the El Paso County GOP, sent a letter to the [[colorado-republican-party|Colorado Republican Party]] seeking assistance in contacting legislators about the treatment of Americans arrested or charged in connection with the January 6, 2021 incident at the US Capitol; more than 200 Coloradans, many of them Republican officials, signed in support.[3] Buck responded on September 1, 2023 with a letter accusing Watkins of making "a number of factually incorrect claims" and of overstating the threat to the Republic on that basis.[3]

Buck's letter asserted that no defendants had been jailed "pending charges," that all held without bond had been charged with felonies, that conditions in the [[dc-jail|DC jail]] were bad for all inmates rather than uniquely for January 6 defendants, that only the Christopher Worrell contempt episode constituted a valid medical-abuse allegation, that restrictive housing was imposed for defendants' own safety and courts had accommodated counsel access, and that January 6 defendants underwent "the process every federally indicted criminal defendant in this country goes through."[3] The letter cited AP News, The Guardian, The New York Times, CBS News, Just Security, and WUSA9 as authority.[3]

Journalist [[mel-hawley|Mel Hawley]], interviewed for the rebuttal, disputed each claim, pointing to defendants such as [[jonathan-mellis|Jonathan Mellis]], Ryan Samsel, Eric Christie, Darrell Neely and [[brandon-fellows|Brandon Fellows]], to the two-year denial of visitation and religious services, to [[diesel-therapy|diesel therapy]], and to the denial of cancer treatment to [[christopher-worrell|Christopher Worrell]].[3] Buck closed by urging Republican activists to focus on the Biden administration's failures — the Afghanistan withdrawal, open borders, fentanyl deaths, urban crime and inflation — which Ashe in America characterized as plausible deniability by which the right wing of the [[uniparty]] retains power without delivering conservative outcomes.[3]

Buck also declined to back a Biden impeachment inquiry, saying "right now, I'm not convinced that that evidence exists."[3] Ashe in America concluded that Buck had sworn an oath to defend God-given rights from tyranny yet cited mainstream media as an excuse for not doing so, and expressed hope that he would take his own advice to "cease disseminating false information."[3]

## 2023 speakership fight

During the multiple floor ballots on the speakership nomination of [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] in October 2023, Buck cast his vote for Majority Whip [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]] rather than Jordan.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], a constituent of Buck's, cited that vote in describing him as "my horrible congressman" and treating his support for Emmer as disqualifying — an illustration of the grassroots America First backlash against Republicans seen as aligned with the party establishment during the leadership crisis.[1]

## 2024 resignation

Buck, who had already announced that he would not seek reelection in 2024, resigned his Colorado seat in March 2024, announcing his retirement on X in a two-sentence statement that said in part, "I look forward to staying involved in our political process as well as spending more time in Colorado and with my family."[2] House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] said he had not known the resignation was coming and was surprised by the announcement; Buck's office said the congressman had telephoned Johnson to inform him but, receiving no answer, left a voicemail.[2]

The departure left the GOP-controlled House with 218 members against 213 Democrats, narrowing the majority so that Republicans could afford to lose only two members on any party-line vote with all members present.[2] Badlands analyst [[just-human|Just Human]] read the resignation as part of a possible [[uniparty]] effort to hand the House back to Democrats before the 2024 election in order to end ongoing investigations, hearings and impeachment efforts, and potentially to obstruct certification of [[donald-trump|Trump]] electors — while noting that reforms to the Electoral Count Act pushed after January 6, 2021 had made such obstruction harder.[2]

## 2022 assembly challenge

At the [[2022-colorado-republican-state-assembly|2022 Colorado Republican State Assembly]], Buck was challenged in the fourth congressional district by Bob Lewis, a candidate essentially unknown to CD4 delegates who was nominated from the floor and took 62% of the vote, appearing ahead of Buck on the primary ballot.[4] Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], a constituent of Buck's, characterized him as an incumbent who dissents from the establishment only when the establishment has enough votes to win without him.[4] Buck nonetheless won both his primary and the general election, an outcome she grouped with those of [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]] and [[doug-lamborn|Doug Lamborn]] as preserving the status quo in Colorado's congressional delegation.[4]
