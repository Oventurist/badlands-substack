# L. Gordon Crovitz

L. Gordon Crovitz is an American media executive, former publisher of the Wall Street Journal, and co-founder and co-CEO of [[newsguard|NewsGuard]] alongside [[steven-brill|Steven Brill]].[1] Badlands Media's "The Information Imperium" identifies Crovitz as one half of the pairing that created NewsGuard's news-rating system, describing both founders as heavily credentialed figures with deep ties to corporate media, the [[state-department|State Department]] and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]].[1]

## Background and NewsGuard

The article notes that Crovitz ran the Wall Street Journal's publisher arm before turning to the ratings business, and frames the transition as characteristic of the legacy-media establishment appointing itself the judge of online credibility: "these are the guys deciding what's 'credible' for you plebs."[1]

NewsGuard under Crovitz and Brill rates websites on nine weighted criteria and has reviewed more than 6,000 sites, with its extension distributed through over 700 libraries in the United States and Europe.[1] The article argues that the resulting scores systematically favour legacy outlets — the largest U.S. newspapers mostly scoring a perfect 100 — while smaller dissenting publications such as [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] (49.5/100) and the [[new-york-post|New York Post]] (75) are marked down.[1]

## Criticism

"The Information Imperium" places Crovitz's company inside a wider network funded by U.S. government agencies, citing NewsGuard contracts with the Department of Defense and financing linked to pharmaceutical interests.[1] During 2023 congressional hearings on the weaponization of the federal government, [[michael-shellenberger|Michael Shellenberger]] told Rep. [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] that NewsGuard and the [[global-disinformation-index|Global Disinformation Index]] were "U.S. government-funded entities who are working to drive advertisers' revenue away from disfavored publications and towards the ones they favor."[1] The article treats Crovitz's venture, together with [[internews|Internews]], as one of the two principal media-facing arms of the [[usaid|USAID]]–[[national-endowment-for-democracy|NED]]–[[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] nexus it describes.[1]
