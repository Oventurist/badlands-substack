# Daniel Jernigan

Dr. Daniel Jernigan is a U.S. public health official who serves as director of the National Center for Emerging and Zoonotic Infectious Diseases at the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]].[1]

## Testimony on vaccine safety surveillance

Jernigan appeared at a February 15, 2024 hearing of the House [[select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]] titled "Assessing America's Vaccine Safety Systems."[1] When Rep. [[nicole-malliotakis|Nicole Malliotakis]] pressed federal officials on whether extended safety surveillance was being conducted on recipients of the earliest [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]], [[peter-marks|Peter Marks]] of the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] deferred part of the answer to Jernigan.[1] Jernigan responded on the narrow question of myocarditis, saying the CDC had monitored the issue "with various different data systems" and that the most recent data indicated a person was "about eight times less likely to get myocarditis if you're vaccinated compared to those that are unvaccinated."[1]

Critics characterized the exchange as a side-step of the underlying question about long-term surveillance; [[liz-willner|Liz Willner]], founder of [[openvaers|OpenVAERS]], told [[epoch-times|The Epoch Times]] that "there is no system in place for long-term vaccine safety surveillance in this country."[1] Badlands Media writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the testimony undercut public-health officials who had labeled vaccine-hesitant parents "anti-vaxxers" and "science deniers" while declining to track long-term outcomes.[1]
