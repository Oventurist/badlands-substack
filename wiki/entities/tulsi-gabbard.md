# Tulsi Gabbard

Tulsi Gabbard is a former member of the U.S. Congress whose Syria positions Badlands Media presents as a case study in how the national-security establishment slanders dissenters as "Russian agents."[1]

## 2017 meeting with Assad

Gabbard met with [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] in January 2017 during what she described as a "fact-finding" mission to Syria.[1] The meeting was notable because she was the first member of the U.S. Congress to meet with Assad since the Syrian civil war began in 2011.[1]

## Backlash

Gabbard then, in the article's framing, "committed the most heinous and unforgivable sin": she called for the United States to stop arming and funding proxy groups in Syria.[1] The Democratic establishment immediately distanced itself from her.[1] Following her selection as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s pick for Director of National Intelligence, the article says the story was revived and she was again cast as a "Putin puppet" — described as the official slander for anyone who deviates from the U.S. security state's multi-decade effort to make Russia a permanent enemy.[1]

## Director of National Intelligence and the Fulton County warrants

By 2026 Gabbard was serving as Director of National Intelligence, appointed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[2] In January 2026 she was present, at the president's request, during the [[fbi|FBI]]'s execution of court-authorized search warrants at the [[fulton-county|Fulton County]] Election Hub and Operation Center, where agents seized materials tied to the 2020 presidential election.[2] She subsequently informed [[congress|Congress]] in a letter that interference in U.S. elections poses a threat to national security.[2]

## Kent resignation and the Iran war

In March 2026 Gabbard's top aide [[joe-kent|Joe Kent]], director of the [[national-counterterrorism-center|National Counterterrorism Center]], resigned as the first senior official in the Trump administration to break over the ongoing U.S. war in [[iran|Iran]].[3] In the hours following Kent's resignation, Gabbard broke her long silence on the Iran war.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that [[uniparty|Uniparty]] figures were participating in a "communist struggle session" against Kent and that Gabbard appeared to be their next target, asking why dissidents on [[israel|Israel]] policy inside the administration were being hunted down and removed from office.[3]

## Calls for declassification (May 2026)

Writing amid the [[2026-iran-deal-negotiations|Iran deal negotiations]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] urged the declassification of both the COVID and 2020 election investigations — specifically any material implicating the GOP in the coup against [[donald-trump|President Trump]] — as the most effective way to blunt an expected Republican revolt against the deal.[4] He wrote that he "would love to see Tulsi break all of their toys on her way out," describing such disclosures as the best available means of hurting the Uniparty by exposing traitors within the GOP.[4]

## ODNI review of the 2020 election (2026)

As Director of National Intelligence, Gabbard is overseeing an intelligence community review examining potential voting machine vulnerabilities and foreign interference in the 2020 presidential election, running parallel to a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] criminal investigation.[5] She personally participated in an [[fbi|FBI]] raid on a Fulton County, Georgia election facility, where agents seized nearly 700 boxes containing 2020 physical ballots, tabulator tapes, ballot images, and voter rolls, citing alleged violations related to record preservation and false ballots; President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] congratulated the agents by phone during the operation.[5]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the confirmation of a separate ODNI inquiry as reassuring after several days of claims that the DOJ was sabotaging election fraud investigations, and noted that "the Tulsi turmoil" ran in parallel with the concurrent narrative storm over [[ed-martin|Ed Martin]].[5] Having said the day before that "the hunt is heating up," she read the ensuing "election threat" messaging campaign as confirmation, arguing that those who stole elections and weaponized government against political opponents now claim victim status as their conduct becomes public.[5]

## Declassification of the 2020 NIC memo (April 2026)

In April 2026 Gabbard declassified a January 15, 2020 [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] memo, prepared by then–National Intelligence Officer for Cyber [[christopher-porter|Christopher Porter]], warning that foreign adversaries could compromise U.S. voting infrastructure, especially internet-connected voter registration databases, pollbooks, and election websites.[6] The release contradicted the public assurance by [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|CISA]] director [[chris-krebs|Chris Krebs]] and other officials that the November 2020 election was "the most secure in U.S. history," and the Intelligence Community's inspector general opened a full investigation into the alleged suppression of the warnings.[6]

## ODNI statements on politicized intelligence
Statements issued by the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] under Gabbard alleged that prior Intelligence Community processes had been influenced by politicized or flawed intelligence products in matters including the 2019 impeachment inquiry and earlier intelligence assessments.[7] Those statements were cited by the administration alongside the July 2026 launch of the [[white-house-election-integrity-portal|White House Election Integrity Portal]] as evidence of a broader pattern of intelligence suppression on election security.[7]

## Departure from ODNI

Gabbard's departure as Director of National Intelligence was announced with an effective date of June 30, 2026, and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] selected [[william-pulte|Bill Pulte]] to succeed her in an acting capacity.[8] Trump indicated he did not intend to nominate Pulte permanently, and told *The Wall Street Journal* that he had privately instructed the incoming acting director to begin cutting personnel at the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]], an agency he described as "unnecessary and/or too big."[8]

## Biolab disclosure (June 2026)

In June 2026 Gabbard's office released a statement announcing newly declassified evidence concerning a [[us-funded-global-biolab-program|U.S.-funded global network]] of more than 120 biological laboratories operating in more than 30 countries.[9] The [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] said the network included laboratories in Ukraine, asserted that some facilities conducted research involving hazardous pathogens — including instances it characterized as gain-of-function research — and said at least one U.S.-funded laboratory in Ukraine had been assessed as vulnerable to attack, seizure, or damage during the Russia–Ukraine war.[9] The statement said Gabbard directed increased intelligence collection on overseas laboratories associated with the program in order to identify laboratory locations, pathogens, and ongoing activities.[9]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded that "reruns are first runs for many," recalling that she had learned of the U.S.-funded biolabs from Victoria Nuland's 2022 Senate testimony, and criticized legacy media for continuing to treat the subject as a conspiracy theory after disclosure.[9] She wrote that the "breaking news" framing did little to calm criticism about the "drip drip of disclosure rug pulls" among those who had known for years, while allowing that many people waking up to the material was itself a good thing.[9]

## Overseas biolab probe (May 2026)

As Director of National Intelligence, Gabbard was reported in May 2026 to be leading a probe into more than 120 [[us-funded-biological-laboratories-abroad|U.S.-funded biological laboratories]] located in over 30 countries, including more than 40 in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] that may be at risk amid the ongoing war.[15] The laboratories had received U.S. taxpayer funding for years, often through the [[cooperative-threat-reduction-program|Pentagon's Cooperative Threat Reduction Program]], to study pathogens and improve biosecurity.[15] The review follows President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s executive order banning federal support for gain-of-function research in high-risk nations and aims to identify exact locations, pathogens studied, and any dangerous experiments.[15]

## Investigation into concealed 2020 election threat evidence

In May 2026, Gabbard launched an investigation into allegations that intelligence agencies concealed evidence of foreign election threats during the 2020 election cycle, including claims involving [[china|China]] and [[venezuela|Venezuela]], according to unclassified memos reviewed by [[just-the-news|Just the News]].[19] The documents show that her now-disbanded [[directors-initiative-group|Director's Initiative Group]] learned of whistleblower allegations during a broader review conducted the prior year, including claims that officials downplayed or omitted information about Chinese election interference from final assessments, congressional briefings, and the President's Daily Brief; the material was referred to Intelligence Community Inspector General [[christopher-fox|Christopher Fox]] for further review and possible declassification.[19]

The memos allege the [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] concluded in 2020 that China had accessed multiple state voter registration databases, with estimates ranging from 12 to 18 states, and that former national intelligence cyber officer [[christopher-porter|Christopher Porter]] raised internal concerns about the handling of that intelligence.[19] Emails cited in the memos reportedly show efforts to alter briefing materials intended for President [[donald-trump|Trump]] and to pressure [[cia|CIA]] analysts to revise records connected to discussions of Chinese election interference, while a 2020 raw intelligence report alleging China produced fraudulent U.S. driver's licenses to facilitate fraudulent mail-in voting was recalled and ordered deleted before a full investigation.[19] Commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the disclosures as a "Level Two" revelation — that China and Venezuela were known threats and that officials were directed to mislead the President and Congress — and argued the case must be built by the book to support a treason-scale prosecution.[19]

## Recall of the classified Venezuela report

In a move that raised major questions inside the intelligence community, Gabbard directed the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]] to recall a classified report concerning Venezuela, despite agency staff affirming that the report was accurate and complied with all NSA policies.[22] The report related to Venezuela and to one of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s special envoys, [[richard-grenell|Richard Grenell]], and included conversations Grenell had held with [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] — whom the United States has accused of drug trafficking — as well as information about Grenell collected by the NSA.[22] After the report's release, an unclassified addendum was issued in April at Gabbard's direction stating: "The report is accurate and in accordance with all NSA policy, directives, and guidance; however, the DNI directed NSA to recall the report," and instructing staff to "remove the original report from all manual and computer files."[22] Multiple sources described Grenell as close to Gabbard; he declined to comment.[22] Badlands host [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] dismissed the affair as a "clown show," speculating that Grenell had simply been in Caracas posturing at Maduro.[22]

## NOFORN directive on Ukraine negotiations (2025)

According to CBS News reporting relayed by the Kyiv Post in August 2025, Gabbard signed a classified memo on July 20 directing U.S. intelligence agencies to designate all analysis and reporting on the Russia–Ukraine peace negotiations as "NOFORN," meaning no foreign dissemination.[24] The order effectively barred the [[five-eyes|Five Eyes]] alliance — the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and New Zealand — from intelligence they had long relied on to coordinate with Washington, and restricted distribution even within the U.S. intelligence community to the agencies that produced the material, with the sole exception of reports already made public.[24] The directive did not cover diplomatic reporting or military intelligence unrelated to the negotiations, but experts told CBS News it risked undermining decades of allied trust.[24]

## Intelligence Community whistleblower disclosure (July 2025)
