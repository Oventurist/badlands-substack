# Anthony Merry

Anthony Merry was the British Ambassador to the United States in the early nineteenth century whose correspondence, according to [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], provides the smoking-gun documentation of [[aaron-burr|Aaron Burr]]'s recruitment by the British Empire.[1]

Three weeks after Burr killed [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]] in July 1804, and after meeting with Burr's confidante Col. [[james-wilkinson|James Wilkinson]], Merry wrote to the British Foreign Secretary that he had "just received an offer from Mr. Burr, the actual vice president of the United States (which situation he is about to resign) to lend his assistance to His Majesty's Government in any manner in which they may think fit to employ him, particularly in endeavouring to effect a separation of the western part of the United States from that which lies between the Atlantic and the mountains, in its whole extant."[1] Merry added that despite "the profligacy of Mr. Burr's character," Burr's political isolation — cast off by both the democratic and the federal party — combined with his remaining connections, "great ambition and spirit of revenge against the present administration," might induce him to serve his employers with fidelity.[1]

The letter was only discovered decades later and therefore played no part in Burr's 1807 treason trial, contributing to his acquittal.[1]
