# U.S. Court of International Trade

The United States Court of International Trade is the federal court of specialized jurisdiction that hears civil actions arising out of the customs and international trade laws of the United States. It became a central venue in the 2025 litigation over the tariffs imposed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] by executive order.[1]

## The 2025 tariff ruling

On the night of Wednesday, May 28, 2025, the Court of International Trade issued an order blocking enforcement of most of the contested Trump tariffs.[1] The underlying litigation consisted of two lawsuits: one brought by a coalition of companies including V.O.S. Selections, Inc., and another brought by states including Oregon, New York, and Illinois, both arguing that the tariffs had been improperly enacted through executive order rather than by Congress.[1]

The ruling was short-lived. The following day the [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-federal-circuit|U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit]] granted the administration's request and temporarily reinstated the tariffs, stating that the trade court's ruling was "temporarily stayed until further notice while this court considers the motions papers."[1] The administration had warned it would seek emergency relief from the Supreme Court as soon as Friday if the tariffs were not promptly restored.[1] Under the Federal Circuit's schedule, plaintiffs were to respond to the government's motion by June 5, with a reply due June 9, and both sides were directed to keep the appellate court apprised of any activity in the lower court on related motions.[1]

## Significance

The episode placed a specialized trade tribunal at the center of a first-order constitutional dispute over the scope of presidential authority to set tariff policy unilaterally — a fight that Badlands coverage treated as one of the defining legal battles of Trump's second term.[1]
