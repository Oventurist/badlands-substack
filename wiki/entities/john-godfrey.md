# John Godfrey

John Godfrey is an American career official of the [[senior-executive-service|Senior Executive Service]] (SES) who served as United States ambassador to [[sudan|Sudan]], appointed by President Joe Biden as part of the restoration of a full US diplomatic mission in Khartoum.[1]

## Appointment and mission

The United States closed its embassy in Sudan in 1996 and ended diplomatic relations with the Sudanese government; Biden restarted the diplomatic mission in early 2022, according to Badlands commentary, on the heels of news that the Sudanese government was nearing the end of a multi-year review of Vladimir Putin's proposal to construct a [[russian-naval-base-in-sudan|Russian naval base]] at Sudan's main port.[1] Biden dispatched Godfrey to serve as the new ambassador, and after months of bureaucratic process and Senate confirmation he arrived in Sudan in August 2022.[1]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that Godfrey "immediately threatened their government with sanctions if they accepted Russia's proposal."[1]

## Civil war and evacuation

The Sudanese government announced in February 2023 that it had accepted Russia's proposal, and roughly two months later civil war broke out between the [[sudanese-armed-forces|Sudanese army]] and the paramilitary [[rapid-support-forces|Rapid Support Forces]].[1] Godfrey and his staff were evacuated and the embassy shut down.[1] Badlands commentary framed the outcome as "a tentative success" for a mission of destabilizing Sudan so that the Russians would not get their base built, and explicitly accused Godfrey of spearheading a color revolution (regime change) operation in Sudan on behalf of the [[state-department|State Department]].[1] The same commentary noted that Russia and Sudan announced in 2025 that all details of the naval base agreement had been finalized and construction would begin as soon as possible.[1]
