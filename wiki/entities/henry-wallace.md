# Henry Wallace

**Henry Wallace** is named in Badlands Media commentary alongside [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] as a figure whose conception of the [[united-nations|United Nations]] differed sharply from what the organisation later became.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## The UN as Wallace envisioned it

Contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that the UN as envisioned by Roosevelt or Wallace was never meant to be a world government, but rather a platform for dialogue and for the harmonisation of security and economic interests among civilizations.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation) This framing supports the essay's wider claim that the drive toward supranational technocratic government associated with the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] represents a departure from, not a fulfilment of, the post-war design.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Context

Wallace is placed within the same anti-colonial post-war vision that Ehret associates with the original [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods]] arrangements — fixed exchange rates to block speculation and long-term credit for large-scale development intended to internationalise the New Deal.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-gateway-to-a-new-world-of-cooperation)

## Postwar targeting

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that after 1945 the [[truman-doctrine|Truman Doctrine]] and the Anglo-American "special relationship" replaced [[franklin-d-roosevelt|FDR]]'s anti-colonial vision, and an anti-communist witch hunt turned America into a fascist police state under FBI surveillance in which everyone friendly to Russia was targeted for destruction.[2] Wallace and [[harry-dexter-white|Harry Dexter White]] were the first of FDR's close allies to feel that targeting; White's death in 1948 came while he was campaigning for Wallace's presidential bid, and it ended the era of anti-colonialists running the [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]].[2]

## Vice presidency and the Pacific vision

Wallace served as [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin D. Roosevelt]]'s vice president from 1941 to 1945 and was among the most important figures pressing for the internationalization of New Deal projects so that former colonies could achieve genuine economic independence.[4] Having helped initiate thousands of American water, energy, agriculture and transportation projects under the New Deal, he held that any durable international order must rest on large-scale development that eradicated poverty and elevated minds.[4] In his 1944 book [[our-job-in-the-pacific|Our Job in the Pacific]] he dated the march of the peoples of the East to the 1911 Chinese revolution inspired by [[sun-yat-sen|Sun Yat-sen]]'s teachings, and argued that post-war Asia required neither communism nor capitalism alone but a higher synthesis, possibly geared together by an international government bank.[4] He considered a core alliance of Russia, China and America vital to all three, writing that "we cannot have prosperity for ourselves alone."[4]

Wallace worked closely with FDR and other New Deal Democrats to shape the 1942 [[atlantic-charter|Atlantic Charter]] and the [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods System]] in order to internationalize the New Deal in the post-war world, and against Wall Street monetarists argued that long-term credit for agro-industrial projects in poor countries carried no inflationary risk.[4]

## Fall from power

With FDR's death in April 1945 the plan did not unfold, and Wallace and other New Deal Democrats loyal to FDR's vision were labelled communist sympathizers for resisting the emerging Cold War consensus.[4] Wallace was demoted upon [[harry-truman|Truman]]'s rise to the presidency and summarily fired in 1946 after attempting to promote peace between Russia and America.[4] [[harry-dexter-white|Harry Dexter White]], brought before the House of Un-American Activities and replaced by Wall Street lawyer John J. McCloy, died of a heart attack in 1948 while campaigning for Wallace as a third-party candidate.[4] Wallace had warned in 1944 that if his international New Deal were rejected, "fascism in the postwar inevitably will push steadily for Anglo-Saxon imperialism and eventually for war with Russia."[4]

## Bering Strait tunnel revival

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that after two early color revolutions and the murder of Czar [[nicholas-ii|Nicholas II]] halted Russia's pre-war tunnel plans, the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait Tunnel]] project was revived by American Vice-President Henry Wallace and his Russian counterparts in 1942, before the Cold War derailed the program once more.[3]
