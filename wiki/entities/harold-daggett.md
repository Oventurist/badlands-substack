# Harold Daggett

Harold Daggett is the president of the [[international-longshoremens-association|International Longshoremen's Association]] (ILA), the dockworkers' union that struck East and Gulf Coast ports beginning October 1, 2024.[1]

## 2024 port strike

Daggett led the ILA into its first labor action since 1977, a walkout of more than 45,000 members across three dozen facilities and 36 ports.[1] Announcing the strike, he said: "We are prepared to fight as long as necessary, to stay out on strike for whatever period of time it takes, to get the wages and protections against automation our ILA members deserve," adding that the [[united-states-maritime-alliance|US Maritime Alliance]] "must now meet our demands for this strike to end."[1] Talks between the two sides had been under way since June 2024, and the ILA rejected a USMX offer that would have raised wages 50 percent over six years with limits on automation, holding out instead for a 77 percent pay increase.[1]

## Relationship with Donald Trump

Badlands commentary highlighted a dig by [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] into Daggett's connections to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], noting an ILA statement in which Daggett asked union members to pray for Trump and the victims of the July 2024 Pennsylvania rally shooting and recalled a "productive meeting" with Trump the previous November.[1] Badlands writers treated the timing of the strike — commencing the same day Trump published an economic op-ed in [[newsweek|Newsweek]] — as evidence of a coordinated sequence rather than coincidence.[1]

## Numerological reading in Q for Dummies

Chapter 20 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series, published October 5, 2024, treats Daggett as a figure surrounded by Q "comms" following the suspension of the port strike until January 15.[2] The author notes that [[donald-trump|President Trump]] has a relationship with Daggett and had met him at Mar-a-Lago some months earlier.[2] A list of details is then offered as significant: the $1.7 million worth of Daggett's home; its size of 7,163 square feet, whose digits sum to 17; his 76-foot yacht, read as an allusion to 1776; his statement that 17 deaths had occurred at ports within three months; and the figure of 45,000 workers going on strike.[2] The author concludes that the situation is "laden with far too many comms" to ignore and that more lay behind the strike than was publicly known, invoking the Q instruction to [[watch-the-water|watch the water]].[2]
