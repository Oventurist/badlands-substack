# Roald Dahl

Roald Dahl was a British author of children's fiction whose works—among them *James and the Giant Peach*, *Fantastic Mr Fox*, and *Matilda*—became the subject of a 2023 controversy over posthumous editorial revisions to his texts.[1]

## The 2023 rewriting of Dahl's books

In February 2023, it emerged that Puffin, Dahl's publisher, had issued new editions carrying a notice on the copyright page stating that "words matter" and that the publisher "regularly review[s] the language to ensure that it can continue to be enjoyed by all today."[1]

By comparing the new editions with earlier printings, The Telegraph identified hundreds of changes to Dahl's stories. Language relating to weight, mental health, violence, gender, and race was cut or rewritten: the Cloud-Men in *James and the Giant Peach* became the Cloud-People, the Small Foxes in *Fantastic Mr Fox* were made female, and in *Matilda* a reference to Rudyard Kipling was removed and replaced with Jane Austen.[1]

Commentary published by American Thinker argued that targeting Dahl for racism and misogyny was incoherent given that nearly all the adult characters in his books are deliberately frightening or grotesque, and that children take pleasure in the macabre when it is paired with humour and a morally clear outcome in which the good are rewarded and the bad punished.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that the campaign against Dahl was not an organic outgrowth of left-wing irrationalism but a designed feature of [[cancel-culture|cancel culture]], in which a cabal acts through proxies—corporations, celebrities, and influencers—to censor individuals while intervening more directly in the censorship of groups and cultural artefacts. He contended that fiction serves as part of the "spiritual immune system" of a culture by providing a safe place to explore humanity's capacity for evil, and that suppressing it produces more malevolence rather than less.[1]
