# Working Families Party

The **Working Families Party (WFP)** is a left-of-center American political party and organizing vehicle identified as one of the lead sponsors of the post-2024 progressive mobilization against the incoming second administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Role in the 2024 post-election coalition

In the lead-up to the 2024 presidential election, the WFP had already joined with other sponsoring organizations — including [[moveon-org|MoveOn]], which began as an emailed petition in 1998; the [[indivisible|Indivisible Project]], which emerged from a Google doc in 2017; Public Citizen; and the [[american-civil-liberties-union|American Civil Liberties Union]] — in preparing a coordinated response to a possible Trump victory.[1] Organizers said the coalition ultimately aligned more than 100 groups, along with Democratic U.S. Representative [[pramila-jayapal|Pramila Jayapal]] of Washington, and set a schedule of eight roughly weekly "Mass Calls" held 8–9 p.m. EST beginning November 7, 2024.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterized this gathering as "Resistance 2.0," a sequel to the movement assembled after Trump's 2016 win, and described it as NGO world, Hollywood and possibly even the [[state-department|State Department]] running a domestic [[color-revolution|color revolution]] against MAGA.[1]

## Composition and alignments

According to research cited in the article, the WFP is filled with union personnel and has backed many prominent establishment politicians on the left, including [[bill-de-blasio|Bill de Blasio]], [[andrew-cuomo|Andrew Cuomo]] and [[letitia-james|Letitia James]].[1]

## Maurice Mitchell's stated posture

During the 2024 election cycle, WFP national director [[maurice-mitchell|Maurice Mitchell]] said activists were focused primarily on preventing Trump from winning reelection but would also take actions to respond to a second Trump term.[1] He framed the planning question as: "A lot of folks are in the mind frame of, What can we learn from the past…How can we apply those lessons going forward? And how can we think through the various scenarios that might present themselves, and how might we leverage everything that we have?"[1]

## Critical framing

DeLarme argued that when one attempts to identify what precisely these groups feel compelled to fight, they can point only to [[project-2025|Project 2025]], an initiative penned by the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]] that he contends has nothing to do with Trump himself.[1] In his reading, the groups function as establishment attack dogs against a populist candidate, and — rioting having proven less effective than [[lawfare|lawfare]] — the web of NGOs and their lawyers would be a primary vector of attack against a second Trump term.[1]
