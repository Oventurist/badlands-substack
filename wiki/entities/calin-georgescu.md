# Călin Georgescu

Călin Georgescu is a prominent Romanian political figure who announced on February 17, 2025, that he intends to ban the entire [[george-soros|Soros]] network within [[romania|Romania]] if elected president.[1]

## Pledge to ban the Soros network

Georgescu made the declaration at a press event, emphasizing a commitment to safeguarding Romania from perceived threats posed by foreign influence, particularly influence associated with billionaire philanthropist George Soros.[1] "We have ways to do this. We know who they are, it's very clear," he said, indicating a strategic plan to identify and eliminate the influence of organizations linked to Soros.[1]

Coverage framed the statement as reflecting a growing sentiment among certain Romanian political factions that view the Soros network as a detrimental force undermining national sovereignty, and as part of a broader political narrative in a country navigating questions of identity, European Union membership and global influences.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] remarked that such bans have been successful in small countries around the world, but argued that the same could not be done in the United States because Americans are "cucked to the television and in thrall to Tucker Carlson and Joe Rogan."[1]

## Candidacy barred (March 2025)

In March 2025 Georgescu, the far-right frontrunner in Romania's presidential vote, was barred from running in May's election.[2] After a 10-4 vote, the country's electoral bureau (BEC) said it was invalidating his candidacy due to his "failure to comply with the electoral regulations," a decision taken despite pressure from Washington to "listen" to voters' demands.[2] It followed by just over a week the opening of a criminal investigation by Romanian prosecutors accusing him of crimes such as attempting to subvert the constitutional order and establishing a fascist organization; when he left the courthouse where he was questioned the previous month, he made what many interpreted as a Nazi salute to supporters.[2] Georgescu said the decision had dealt "a direct blow to the heart of democracy worldwide," and his supporters took to the streets of Bucharest, some tearing up asphalt to pelt police guarding the BEC building.[2] See [[2025-romanian-presidential-election-ban|2025 Romanian presidential election ban]].[2]


## Invoked in Breton’s German Election Threat

In January 2025, former European Commissioner [[thierry-breton|Thierry Breton]] cited the annulled Romanian vote as a precedent that could be repeated in Germany, with French broadcaster BFMTV recalling Georgescu's TikTok campaign in its coverage.[3] Georgescu, widely labelled a 'far-right' contender, had come out on top in the first round in a 'shock' outcome that left political opponents claiming Russian election interference, with the purported smoking gun being social media posts on platforms such as TikTok.[3] A top Romanian court then annulled the first round, creating what amounted to a do-over election.[3]

## First-round win and the interference claims (December 2024)

Georgescu's surprise victory in the first round of Romania's presidential election in late 2024 prompted Romanian officials to demand a "redo" of the vote on the grounds of alleged Russian interference.[4] Described by establishment media as a 62-year-old "obscure far-right populist," he took the most votes and advanced to a second round set for December 8 against the reformist [[elena-lasconi|Elena Lasconi]] of the Save Romania Union party, having also beaten incumbent Prime Minister [[marcel-ciolacu|Marcel Ciolacu]] of the Social Democratic Party and leaving the ruling party without a runoff candidate for the first time in Romania's 35-year post-communist history.[4]

A report by the Bucharest-based think tank [[expert-forum|Expert Forum]] said engagement on Georgescu's TikTok account before the vote had risen in a manner that appeared "sudden and artificial, similar to his polling results"; the aggregated coverage noted that Expert Forum works in collaboration with the European Commission, Council of Europe, World Bank and United Nations Development Programme.[4] Romanian officials seized on the report to argue that Georgescu had "benefited from massive exposure due to preferential treatment" granted by [[tiktok|TikTok]] and that Russian interference explained his win.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the episode as another installment of fake elections, comparing the accusations to Russiagate and the Steele Dossier, and noted that Romania is a key US offshoring destination for back-office functions such as finance, human resources and document management — making it, in her account, "a globalist chess piece" whose electorate had now rejected the status quo.[4]

## First-round victory and interference claims (December 2024)

Georgescu, 62 and described by establishment media as an "obscure far-right populist," upended Romania's political landscape by taking the most votes in the first round of the November 2024 presidential election and advancing to a runoff set for December 8 against the reformist [[elena-lasconi|Elena Lasconi]] of the progressive Save Romania Union.[4] He also defeated incumbent Prime Minister [[marcel-ciolacu|Marcel Ciolacu]] of the Social Democratic Party, leaving the ruling party without a runoff candidate for the first time in Romania's 35-year post-communist history.[4]

The Bucharest think tank [[expert-forum|Expert Forum]] reported that Georgescu's [[tiktok|TikTok]] account had seen an explosion of engagement before the vote that appeared "sudden and artificial, similar to his polling results."[4] Romanian officials seized on the report to argue he had "benefited from massive exposure due to preferential treatment" from TikTok and to allege Russian interference, with some demanding a rerun of the vote.[4]

The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] compared the claims to Russiagate and the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]], describing them as a familiar establishment response to unwelcome election results.[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] added that Romania has long served as a key U.S. offshoring destination for back-office work such as finance, human resources and document management, making it "a globalist chess piece," and framed Georgescu's win as part of a global rejection of the status quo during 2024.[4]


## Arrest and the March 2025 protests

A week before March 4, 2025, Georgescu was arrested on six charges, including "anti-constitutional acts," and was barred by the court from leaving the country and prohibited from appearing on television or posting on social media.[5] Badlands commentary mocked the measures as fighting fascism with fascism.[5] The arrest was immediately denounced by Elon Musk on X, and Georgescu publicly requested help from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in fighting the Romanian Deep State.[5]

On Saturday, March 1, 2025, tens of thousands of demonstrators gathered in Bucharest's Victory Square to demand that the second round of the annulled election take place rather than a new vote be held.[5] The rally was organized by the Alliance for the Union of Romanians (AUR), whose leader [[george-simion|George Simion]] attended alongside Georgescu; organizers claimed nearly a million participants, though several outlets reported lower figures.[5] In January 2025 Romania's ruling coalition had approved a rerun of the presidential election, with a first round scheduled for May 4 and a runoff on May 18 if no candidate secured more than 50%.[5] Romanian authorities cited supposed "irregularities" in Georgescu's campaign and claimed without proof that Russia had covertly supported him; he is known as a critic of NATO and the EU and a staunch opponent of aiding Ukraine.[5] The cancellation triggered impeachment proceedings against incumbent president [[klaus-iohannis|Klaus Iohannis]], who resigned in disgrace in February 2025.[5]

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that Trump is universally recognized across the world as the symbol of defiance and populist revolt, with people under tyranny calling out his name as Gotham City looks to the night sky for the Batsignal — a promise of retribution against criminals and gangsters.[5]

## January 2025 protests over the annulled election

On Sunday, January 12, 2025, tens of thousands protested in [[romania|Romania]]'s capital against the Constitutional Court's decision the previous month to annul the presidential election after Georgescu, a far-right populist outsider, unexpectedly emerged as the frontrunner by winning the first round on November 24.[6] Demonstrators in Bucharest honked horns and waved Romanian flags while brandishing placards reading "Democracy is not optional" and "We want free elections," with many demanding the presidential race resume from the second round.[6] The court had made the unprecedented move to annul the election just two days before the December 8 runoff, after allegations of electoral violations and Russian interference emerged.[6]

[[george-simion|George Simion]], leader of the [[alliance-for-the-union-of-romanians|Alliance for the Unity of Romanians]], told the protest: "We are protesting against the coup d'état that took place on Dec. 6. We are sorry to discover so late that we were living in a lie and that we were led by people who claimed to be democrats, but are not at all."[6]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] summarized the affair as "everything that threatens my grip on power is Russian interference," observing that the same officials who claim to be saving democracy were making a habit of cancelling elections wherever they could not win them, and that experts now urging traditional right and left parties to unite against the populists were pulling off the mask on a long-standing Uniparty.[6]
