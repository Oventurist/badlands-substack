# Black Lives Matter

Black Lives Matter (BLM) is an American activist movement whose persuasive power and influence peaked in 2020 during the reckoning that followed the death of George Floyd, a period of rare consensus when roughly two-thirds of Americans expressed support for the cause according to the Pew Research Center.[1] By 2023, only half of Americans continued to support BLM and fewer than a quarter did so strongly, with Pew finding that a majority of Americans believed the increased focus on race and racial inequality had not produced changes improving the lives of Black people.[1]

## 2024 statement on Kamala Harris

In late July 2024, BLM issued a statement sharply critical of the Democratic Party's decision to elevate Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] to the nomination without a primary.[1] "Democratic Party elites and billionaire donors are attempting to manipulate Black voters by anointing Kamala Harris and an unknown vice president as the new Democratic ticket without a primary vote by the public," the statement read, adding that "while the potential outcome of a Harris presidency may be historic, the process to achieve it must align with true democratic values. We have no idea where Kamala Harris stands on the issues."[1] The group called for a "virtual snap primary" to let voters register their preferences.[1]

Reporting on the statement noted the contrast with expectations rooted in the group's foundational emphasis on identity politics, invoking Bernie Sanders' argument that "it is not good enough for somebody to say, I'm a woman; vote for me."[1] Badlands commentary read the coverage as a warning signal from establishment narrative-setters to their own side against mounting an identity-politics defense of Harris.[1]

## 2023 corporate retreat and the Israel–Hamas war

In October 2023 the group's Chicago chapter posted — and later deleted — an image of a paraglider with the text "I stand with Palestine," a reference to the [[hamas|Hamas]] attackers who paraglided into an Israeli music festival and killed hundreds of attendees on October 7.[2] Sen. [[ted-cruz|Ted Cruz]] highlighted the post on his podcast and criticized [[coca-cola|Coca-Cola]] for its earlier support of BLM; the morning after the October 18 episode, Coca-Cola deleted website language describing a $500,000 Sprite contribution to the Black Lives Matter Global Network.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the corporate retreat was driven not by [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s revelations about the [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] narrative but by BLM's refusal to side with Israel after October 7.[2] She recounted her own 2020 experience of a corporate employer donating to BLM and encouraging staff to attend a racial justice march organized by the Colorado Communist Party, and characterized "the summer of burn, loot and murder" as a critical component of an American [[color-revolution|color revolution]] intended to destabilize society and distract from changes to local election processes ahead of November 3, 2020.[2]

### The Chicago paraglider post

Contemporaneous reporting dated the controversy to Tuesday, October 10, 2023, when the BLM Chicago account posted a graphic of a paraglider carrying the Palestinian flag with the words "I Stand With Palestine" and the caption "That is all that is it!"[3] X, formerly Twitter, later appended a community note recording that on October 7, 2023, [[hamas|Hamas]] terrorists in paragliders attacked a music festival in Israel, killing over 260 people.[3] Thousands of attendees at the Tribe of Nova music festival in southern Israel were ambushed that morning, with hundreds killed and others taken captive, and video of the rapes, murders and kidnappings circulated widely on news and social media platforms in the following days.[3]

The chapter deleted the post and addressed it on X the following Wednesday morning: "Yesterday we sent out msgs that we aren't proud of. We stand with Palestine & the people who will do what they must to live free. Our hearts are with, the grieving mothers, those rescuing babies from rubble, who are in danger of being wiped out completely."[3] The episode was covered by [[fox-news|Fox News]] as an instance of a domestic activist organization sparking outrage over alignment with the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]].[3]

## Aston Mack arrest (2025)

In May 2025, [[aston-mack|Aston Mack]], a 35-year-old Black Lives Matter activist known for his role in Florida protests, was arrested in a child sex sting operation after allegedly soliciting an undercover officer posing as a 14-year-old girl.[4] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Mack had been globally celebrated as a social-justice role model — the English football club Arsenal wore a kit honoring Mack and Black Lives Matter, and later deleted a promotional post about it — and described the movement as "the gift that keeps on giving for comical corruption stories."[4]

## Objection to the 2024 Democratic nominee substitution

Following [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s July 21, 2024 withdrawal and the rapid consolidation of party support behind [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], Black Lives Matter publicly objected and called for a democratic nominating process.[5] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the organization's reaction as evidence that even parts of the left were angered by what she described as the oligarchy overriding primary voters for the third consecutive Democratic cycle.[5]
