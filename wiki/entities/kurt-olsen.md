# Kurt Olsen

Kurt Olsen is a [[donald-trump|Trump]]-appointed director of election security and integrity whose referral initiated the [[fbi|FBI]]'s 2026 investigation into [[fulton-county|Fulton County]]'s handling of 2020 presidential election ballots.[1]

## Background and role

Olsen had previously assisted with legal challenges to the 2020 election results before his appointment.[1] The federal probe detailed in search warrant affidavits by FBI Special Agent [[hugh-evans|Hugh Evans]] was initiated following Olsen's referral, and led in January 2026 to court-authorized searches of the Fulton County Election Hub and Operation Center.[1]

## Move to the Justice Department (June 2026)

Olsen, described as a White House official who assisted President Trump's efforts to examine the 2020 election results, joined the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] as a senior attorney in the US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of Florida, beginning the role on Monday, June 1, 2026, per a Department spokesperson.[2] He reports to US Attorney [[jason-reding-quinones|Jason Reding Quinones]], a prosecutor involved in developing a broad [[grand-conspiracy-probe|grand conspiracy inquiry]] examining whether past investigations and prosecutions involving Trump amounted to criminal conspiracy.[2] That effort — covering the [[russiagate-hoax|Russian Collusion investigation]], cases brought by former Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], and the [[fbi|FBI]]'s 2022 Mar-a-Lago search — is supervised by [[joe-digenova|Joe diGenova]], counselor to acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]].[2] Olsen has also been involved in examining possible foreign interference in US elections and in collecting voting machines and election materials in Puerto Rico, Georgia and Arizona.[2]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] said on Badlands Daily that if any attorney in the nation understands election fraud and malfeasance on every level — fake voters, fake ballots and fake counts, and their evidentiary application across legal challenges, cyber expert analysis and legislative endeavors — it is Olsen.[2] She noted that Olsen spent the years between Trump administrations as [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]]'s attorney and "went everywhere the evidence was."[2] [[patrick-byrne|Patrick Byrne]], who welcomed the appointment, shared that he and Olsen had agreed in December 2020 that Olsen would take election fraud "to the firing squad" — a date that complicates Ashe's prior assumption that Lindell's January 15, 2021 photo op, in which Olsen's name appeared on papers under Lindell's arm, was Olsen's origin story in the election fight.[2]

## Intelligence directive on 2020 election material (February 2026)

In February 2026 an anonymous US intelligence official and "a person with knowledge of the matter" told *NBC News* that President Trump had instructed US intelligence agencies to provide 2020 election-related intelligence to Olsen.[3] *Politico* reported the same story citing "four people with knowledge of the effort."[3] According to the anonymous sources, the directive concerned claims of foreign interference in the 2020 election and was issued to US spy agencies including the [[cia|CIA]].[3] The six anonymous sources across the two stories reported that intelligence officials raised concerns about "sharing classified information with a private attorney."[3]

The Brief noted that Olsen had represented [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] in her 2022 election challenges as well as numerous other election cases, that the President had appointed him as a special advisor on elections the previous year, and that he became a federal "special government employee" in October.[3]

### Ashe in America on Olsen's significance

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that if one person in the United States has a comprehensive, macro-level view of election issues, it is Kurt Olsen.[3] She recounted meeting him in August 2021 at the Cyber Symposium, working with him on several projects over the following year, and interviewing him with [[canncon|CannCon]] both on and off Badlands.[3] She objected to media reports describing him as a "private attorney," noting that he had been appointed a special government employee in October and that before his legal career he was a Navy SEAL.[3]

She traced Olsen's entry into the election narrative to January 2021, when *The Washington Post* reported on [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]]'s White House visit and photographers captured a glimpse of papers Lindell was carrying — the story of how Lindell and Olsen met, before years of collaboration on election-related projects.[3] In her assessment, essentially any lawsuit, legislative effort or cyber analysis pursued by grassroots election researchers and activists during the intervening administration had at least Olsen's awareness.[3] She argued that the significance of Olsen becoming the clearinghouse for the intelligence agencies' election-related portfolios cannot be understated, and that as those agencies attempt to rewrite their records and roles in stolen US elections, Trump had ordered them to hand everything to the one person capable of informedly fact-checking them.[3]

## Continued media attacks (February 2026)

Attacks on Olsen continued into February 2026, with the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] publishing a piece headlined "Trump's Director of Election Security is an Election Denier."[4] The item ran alongside coverage of the [[save-act|SAVE Act]]'s passage through the House, which Badlands treated as part of the same fight over [[election-denial|election denial]] framing and election integrity.[4]

## Activist Campaign Against His DOJ Role (2026)

In June 2026 NBC News reported that election-focused nongovernmental organizations were raising concerns about Olsen's appointment to a senior [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] position, citing his involvement in post-2020 election litigation and his efforts to challenge election procedures and results.[5] Critics argued that his earlier election work called into question his suitability for overseeing election matters at the department; NBC News described him as "an election denier who tried to overturn Donald Trump's 2020 election loss."[5] The most prominent critic was [[free-speech-for-people|Free Speech For People]], which called for scrutiny of his role and warned about public confidence in DOJ election enforcement.[5] Supporters countered that Olsen is an experienced attorney whose prior election work amounted to lawful advocacy for clients and causes, and characterized the campaign against him as part of a broader effort to marginalize election-integrity concerns.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing after Olsen's October 2025 appointment as special advisor to the President, argued that no attorney in the country has a more complete understanding of alleged [[2020-election-fraud-claims|2020 election fraud]] than Olsen, and framed the attacks on him as predictable [[lawfare|lawfare]] by an organization whose litigation tactics he knows first-hand.[5] She noted that Olsen, who by 2026 was working for the DOJ in Florida, is familiar with Free Speech For People's conduct in the Colorado canvassing litigation and in similar cases nationwide, concluding that he is targeted precisely because "he's a threat."[5]

## Counsel in Lake v. Hobbs (2023)

Olsen served as an attorney for [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] in [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]], her challenge to the 2022 Arizona gubernatorial election.[6] He told the court that "11 of the signature verification workers approved 170k signatures at a rate of less than 0 and 2.99 seconds with a 99.97% approval rating," and in closing argument stated that approximately 274,000 ballot signatures had been compared and verified in less than three seconds each.[6] Judge Thompson nonetheless dismissed the case on May 22, 2023, a ruling [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] summarized as holding that "anything goes" and that "laws are suggestions."[6]

## Closing argument in the Count III trial (2023)

As attorney for [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] in the remanded Count III trial, Kurt Olsen told the court: "This election should be set aside … Your Honor, the election was unlawful…16-550 was not complied with. Defendants don't dispute our expert. They don't dispute the evidence."[7] The argument rested on evidence that [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] had not conducted [[signature-verification|signature verification]] in the manner required by [[arizona-revised-statutes-16-550|A.R.S. § 16-550]], with Lake's team hammering that the statute's "shall compare" language requires an actual comparison against at least one exemplar signature.[7] Judge [[peter-thompson|Peter Thompson]] nonetheless dismissed the claim on May 22, 2023.[7]
