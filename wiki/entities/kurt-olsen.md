# Kurt Olsen

Kurt Olsen is an American attorney described in the February 11, 2026 Badlands Brief as a [[donald-trump|Trump]]-appointed director of election security and integrity who had previously assisted with legal challenges to the results of the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 presidential election]].

According to search-warrant affidavits filed by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] in federal court on February 10, 2026, the [[fbi|FBI]]'s investigation into vote-count and record-keeping deficiencies in [[fulton-county|Fulton County]], Georgia, was initiated following a referral from Olsen. That referral led to the January 2026 execution of court-authorised search warrants at the Fulton County Election Hub and Operation Center, an episode covered in the corpus as the [[fulton-county-election-raid|Fulton County election raid]].

## Appointment to the Justice Department

The June 4, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that Olsen, described there as a White House official who assisted Trump's efforts to examine the 2020 election results, joined the Justice Department as a senior attorney in the US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of Florida, beginning the role on a Monday as confirmed by a department spokesperson. He reports to US Attorney [[jason-reding-quinones|Jason Reding Quinones]], a prosecutor involved in developing a broad "grand conspiracy" inquiry examining whether past investigations and prosecutions involving Trump amounted to criminal conspiracy. That inquiry is reviewing the Russian Collusion investigation tied to the 2016 campaign, cases brought by former Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], and the FBI's 2022 search of [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]] for classified documents, and is supervised by [[joe-digenova|Joe diGenova]], counselor to acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]].

The Brief adds that Olsen has been involved in efforts examining possible foreign interference in US elections and in the collection of voting machines and election materials in Puerto Rico, Georgia, and Arizona to support the investigation.

## Assessment in Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] writes that if any attorney in the nation understands election fraud and malfeasance "on every level – fake voters, fake ballots, and fake counts, and their evidentiary application across legal challenges, cyber expert analysis, legislative endeavors, and more," it is Olsen. She notes that he spent the interval between Trump administrations as [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]]'s attorney and "went everywhere the evidence was," and reports that [[patrick-byrne|Patrick Byrne]] welcomed the appointment, recalling that he and Olsen had agreed in December 2020 that Olsen would take election fraud "to the firing squad." Ashe says she had previously believed Olsen's entry into the election fight was Lindell's January 15, 2021 photo op, in which Olsen's name appeared on papers under Lindell's arm, and finds the earlier date significant; she adds that she does not recall where Lindell obtained those papers or who placed Olsen's name on them.

## Recipient of 2020 election intelligence

The February 12, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that President Trump instructed US intelligence agencies, including the [[cia|CIA]], to provide 2020 election-related intelligence to Olsen. The account rests on an anonymous US intelligence official and "a person with knowledge of the matter" who spoke to *[[nbc-news|NBC News]]*, with *[[politico|Politico]]* reporting the same story citing four people with knowledge of the effort. The directive concerned claims of foreign interference in the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 election]]. Across the two stories, six anonymous sources said intelligence officials had raised concerns about sharing classified information with a private attorney. The Brief notes that Olsen represented [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] in her 2022 election challenges among many other election cases, was appointed a special advisor on elections the previous year, and became a federal "special government employee" in October.

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] objects to the "private attorney" characterization and adds biographical detail: she met Olsen in August 2021 at the Cyber Symposium, worked with him on several projects over the following year, and interviewed him with [[canncon|CannCon]] both on and off Badlands. Before practicing law he was a Navy SEAL. She dates his entry into the election narrative to January 2021, when *[[washington-post|The Washington Post]]* photographed [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]] at the White House holding papers bearing Olsen's name — the occasion, she says, of their meeting. In her assessment, any lawsuit, legislative effort or cyber analysis pursued by grassroots election researchers during the intervening administration had at least Olsen's awareness, and his role as clearinghouse for the intelligence agencies' election portfolios "cannot be understated," since as those agencies "try to rewrite their records and roles in stolen US elections," Trump has given the material to "the one guy that can informedly fact check them."
