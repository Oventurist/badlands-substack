# First Solar

**First Solar** is an American manufacturer of thin-film photovoltaic solar panels and one of the four largest US solar companies identified in Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s May 2023 essay on [[energy-consolidation|energy consolidation]].[1] In that piece it functions as the central anomaly in Carlson's stock-price argument and as his primary example of political favoritism in green-energy policy.[1]

## Stock performance anomaly

Carlson compiled the share-price performance of nine energy-transition companies from [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s inauguration on January 20, 2021 through May 2023, expecting that firms positioned to benefit from climate policy would have appreciated.[1] Eight of the nine had fallen — some catastrophically — while First Solar was up 108 percent.[1] Carlson highlighted the coincidence that the single outperformer was also the company he associated with prior political connections, writing that he could not prove wrongdoing but could say "for sure it received massive favoritism during Obama's presidency."[1]

## Alleged Obama-era favoritism

According to Carlson, before [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s 2008 election, [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]] was heavily invested in First Solar and was simultaneously a major contributor to Obama's campaign, as were other large donors with First Solar holdings.[1] After taking office, Obama appointed two Goldman Sachs executives — [[bruce-heyman|Bruce Heyman]] and [[david-heller|David Heller]] — to his finance committee.[1]

Carlson writes that First Solar subsequently began to suffer from both the recession and what he characterizes as its own incompetency, at which point government money "started to pour into" the company: 2009 stimulus funds, [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] guaranteed loans, and green energy subsidies.[1] He frames this as taxpayers keeping the company afloat so that Obama's allies would not absorb losses.[1] During the same period, he states, First Solar's chief executive sold company stock netting $68.5 million.[1]

## St. Clair Solar transaction

The most specific allegation in the essay concerns $455.7 million First Solar received to subsidize the sale of solar panels to two solar farms in Canada owned by a small corporation called [[st-clair-solar|St. Clair Solar]].[1] Carlson notes that St. Clair Solar was a wholly owned subsidiary of First Solar, meaning, in his framing, that "Obama used our tax money to pay First Solar to sell their solar panels to themselves."[1] He adds that Bruce Heyman, the Goldman Sachs executive placed on Obama's finance committee, later became United States Ambassador to Canada — the jurisdiction of the solar farms — and asks whether this was coincidence.[1]
