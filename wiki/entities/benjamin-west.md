# Benjamin West

Benjamin West (born 1737) was an American-born painter who became a co-founder and, in 1791, president of the [[royal-academy-of-fine-arts|English Royal Academy of Fine Arts]], and who is presented in Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as the incubator and coordinator of an American renaissance movement operating from inside the British Empire.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) He was a friend of [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] and the mentor of [[samuel-morse|Samuel F. B. Morse]], and was heralded during his lifetime as the greatest living painter.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Origins in Pennsylvania

West was born in 1737 — the same year as [[king-george-iii|King George III]] — into a Quaker family in Swarthmore, Pennsylvania, and was entirely self-taught, having received no formal artistic training.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) He learned to paint through his family's friendly relations with Delaware natives, who taught him to make pigments from clay and bear grease drawn from a nearby lake.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) His father, a close friend of Benjamin Franklin who ran a boarding house, in 1751 or 1752 made his home the first building in the world to be fitted with a lightning rod.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Through these connections the young West was drawn into the networks created by Franklin's Pennsylvania Philosophical Society.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

Strict Quakers regarded all forms of art as corrupting because art awakened passions thought dangerous to fallen mankind, and West's family and community initially resisted his vocation.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) An emergency meeting was convened that ran all night, concluding that if God had given him the skill, perhaps he was the one who could raise painting to a level at which it would make people better.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) With that approval, and after friends of Franklin saw his early Philadelphia canvas *The Last Days of Socrates*, depicting the philosopher taking hemlock, patrons sponsored his voyage to Europe.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Europe and England

Franklin's network financed West's 1769 passage to France and then Italy, where he studied the classical masters for two years.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) A planned brief sojourn in London before returning home became permanent: he attracted the attention of influential figures including an advisor to King George III, and rose rapidly within the English cultural scene.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) He became a founding member of the Royal Academy of Fine Arts established in 1769 and its president in 1791.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) He never returned to America, and was denounced as a traitor by Americans who lived through the Revolution while simultaneously being accused by the British of being an American spy — the latter charge, the article suggests, closer to the truth.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Method: Platonic irony and anomaly

West is described as a man of the classical Platonic tradition who deliberately embedded anomalies and ironies in his canvases as a language of communication, intended to provoke perplexity, then wonder, and finally discovery in the viewer.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Many of his students, Samuel Morse foremost among them, adopted the technique.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The article credits the Canadian-born historian [[pierre-beaudry|Pierre Beaudry]] with recovering this dimension of West's work, arguing that without Beaudry's pioneering studies the story would remain hidden.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

A worked example is the pair of self-portraits of 1770 and 1776. The later portrait, composed several weeks after George III received the Declaration of Independence, shows darker, more worldly eyes in contrast to the wide-eyed naivety of the earlier picture, and in both West tugs at the suffocating British formal collar — read as a symbol of the tightness of English formalistic culture.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The decisive anomaly is the rag hanging over the canvas frame in his left hand: blank in 1770, in 1776 it carries a human figure, identified as the priest and red-coated soldier from the right side of his own 1770 painting *The Death of General Wolfe*.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## History painting

West was principally a history painter and held a philosophy that a society will value the memories it keeps, and that the painter's purpose is to convey moments of the collective past and enshrine them so that universal lessons about who we are may be learned.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The article contrasts this with the modern presumption that historical painting must be either sterile objectivity or emotionally manipulative propaganda.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

*The Death of General Wolfe* (1770) depicts the 1759 death of [[james-wolfe|General Wolfe]] at the [[battle-of-quebec|Battle of Quebec]] on the Plains of Abraham.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) West broke with the European convention that history paintings be staged in Greco-Roman costume and used contemporary dress, despite establishment threats that he would never receive another commission.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) He defended the choice in a letter to a friend, arguing that the event took place in a region unknown to the Greeks and Romans and that "the same truth that guides the pen of the historian should govern the pencil of the artist," asking how posterity could understand him if he substituted classical fictions for facts.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) George III, asked to arbitrate the debate, was so impressed that he purchased the painting, settling the matter.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

The composition is itself an intervention. Wolfe in reality died behind a bush attended by two people, yet West assembled a crowd including [[william-johnson|William Johnson]], shown in native stockings and moccasins, and placed a Mohawk warrior at the centre carrying almost more weight than the dying general — a contrast between the weakness of the British aristocratic Wolfe and, by implication, the dying British Empire.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The Platonic ambiguity is deliberate: an American republican and a British aristocrat looking at the same scene receive two different messages.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

His 1771 canvas of the [[penn-treaty-of-1682|Penn Treaty]] celebrates the 1682 agreement between [[william-penn|William Penn]] and the Delaware natives, depicting not the signing but the respectful negotiations preceding it, with textiles and goods rather than alcohol or guns being offered, and the famous cedar tree in the background.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) An anomalous shadow falls across the central figures with no identifiable source, possibly signifying the dangers ahead in European–native relations after 1682.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Virtue, sovereignty and the paintings of 1769–1792

*The Departure of Regulus for Carthage* (1769), purchased by George III for a thousand pounds, depicts the Roman consul Regulus refusing to break his word and returning to certain execution in Carthage rather than live dishonourably, with a single pensive woman in the composition serving as a mirror for the audience wrestling with the philosophical problem.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The article links this to Socrates' acceptance of the judgement of the Athenian demos as recounted in Plato's *Apologia*, *Crito* and *Laws*.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The recurring theme across West's subjects — Socrates, Regulus, Cicero, William Penn — is identified as the sovereignty of the individual, achievable only by breaking free of the fear of losing sensual gratification, ego, comfort and life itself; a society trapped by such fears is a society of subjects incapable of self-government.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

*Cicero Discovering the Tomb of Archimedes* shows workers clearing weeds from the lost tomb, a story recounted by Cicero in his *Tusculan Disputations* of 45 BC.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) West is read as drawing a parallel between Cicero, a foreigner to Syracuse who recovered what the Syracusans had forgotten, and himself, a foreigner in Britain attempting to revive a healthier tradition among a wayward people.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

Three later works are read as direct messages to the king. *Saul and the Witch of Endor* retells the Old Testament episode in which a fearful King Saul consults a witch, receives a forecast of defeat and death, and by his superstitious belief ensures the outcome — a loss of free will the article compares to Shakespeare's Hamlet and Macbeth, and connects to the occultism embedded in the British imperial elite and the [[hellfire-club|Hellfire Club]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) His 1791 scene from *King Lear*, Act 3, shows Lear refusing shelter from Tom Bedlam because the storm in his head exceeds the physical storm, and acknowledging his suffering as justice for neglecting the poor of his kingdom; West painted Lear repeatedly, and the play is read as a message to any king struggling with insanity in an unjust world.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) The 1792 mural *Pharaoh and Host Lost at Sea* shows the sea collapsing on the Egyptian army pursuing the Jews across the Red Sea, a pointed image amid British efforts to reabsorb the lost colonies.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Franklin, Copley and the American school

West's 1783 painting *The Peace of Paris* depicts the American delegation — John Jay, John Adams, Benjamin Franklin, Henry Laurens and Franklin's grandson [[william-temple-franklin|William Temple Franklin]] — and remains unfinished because the British delegation never appeared for the sitting; the article treats the half-finished canvas as emblematic of an unfinished revolution.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) His 1816 *Benjamin Franklin Drawing Electricity from the Sky* is described as an unsubtle embodiment of the Promethean principle, painted after George III's final descent into insanity had left West without royal patronage and, in Ehret's reading, with nothing left to lose.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Beaudry noted the irony of the five cherubs, one bearing adult attributes and another a native headband, suggesting that Promethean discovery requires adults to recover child-like wonder and that harmonising European and First Nations cultures is the solution being pointed to.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

West's 1778 depiction of the death of [[william-pitt-the-elder|Lord Chatham]] in Parliament functions as a republican critique of empire that the British elite mistook for homage.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) [[john-singleton-copley|John Singleton Copley]], born the same year as West and one of his students, produced a rival 1781 version of the same moment.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Among the American painters West trained in England were Charles Willson Peale, Rembrandt Peale, Gilbert Stuart, John Trumbull and Copley.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Teacher of the American school

A companion Badlands article by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents West, alongside [[samuel-morse|Samuel Finley Breese Morse]], as one of two figures who played a causal role in shaping both the success of the revolution of 1776 and the foundation of a new renaissance tradition underlying the greatest leaps of progress in nineteenth-century America — a tradition it says has been nearly wiped from collective memory.[2] West trained [[william-dunlap|William Dunlap]] in England after [[george-washington|George Washington]] sponsored the seventeen-year-old's passage in 1783, and also taught [[thomas-cole|Thomas Cole]], later a collaborator of Morse and a founder of the [[hudson-river-school|Hudson River School]], which Ehret says arose out of Morse's academy and out of the earlier school led by West.[2] Raphael Sanzio is identified as the greatest inspiration for West's method, including the contrast between motion and stasis that Morse later deployed in his portrait of the [[marquis-de-lafayette|Marquis de Lafayette]].[2] Morse's own English training, which took place during the War of 1812 under the president of the Royal Academy of Fine Arts, is the thread the article leaves for a promised second part.[2]

## The Royal Academy as a republican outpost

A third Badlands essay, "In Defense of Benjamin Franklin," describes West as the close friend and fellow Philadelphian of [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] who was sent to Italy and then Britain, where the great republican painter organised the creation of the [[royal-academy-of-arts|Royal Academy of the Arts]] in the heart of the Empire in 1772 and remained its president until his death in 1830.[3] It credits West with painting the famous image of Franklin plucking electricity from the heavens.[3] The essay argues that West's access to the courts of Europe gave this Philadelphian republican artist entry to conversations within the inner court of George III, which undoubtedly provided invaluable intelligence for his colleagues in France and the young republic before, during and after the revolutionary war.[3]
