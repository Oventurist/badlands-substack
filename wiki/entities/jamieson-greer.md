# Jamieson Greer

Ambassador Jamieson Greer is the United States Trade Representative in the second Trump administration, heading the [[office-of-the-united-states-trade-representative|Office of the United States Trade Representative]].[1]

In July 2026 Greer announced that, at President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s direction, the administration was imposing a 25% tariff on certain imports from Brazil under [[section-301-of-the-trade-act-of-1974|Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974]], following a yearlong investigation into unfair trade practices.[1] Officials determined that several Brazilian policies were harming American businesses, workers and farmers.[1] Greer framed the tariffs as part of the administration's "America First" trade policies, arguing that Brazil's practices had limited American access to a market of more than 210 million consumers.[1]

## Confirmation hearing and universal tariffs (February 2025)

As President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s nominee for United States Trade Representative, Greer told his confirmation hearing that universal tariffs were worth studying and said he would work to restructure global trade ties.[2] A trade lawyer and official in Trump's first administration, Greer said a universal tariff "is something that should be studied and considered" to see whether it could reverse the direction of the US trade deficit and offshoring, responding to lawmakers' concerns that sweeping duties could drive up prices.[2] Trump had raised the idea of across-the-board tariffs on all US imports on the campaign trail, and Greer would be a crucial figure in implementing that agenda if confirmed.[2] His vote in the Senate Finance Committee was scheduled for Tuesday 11 February 2025 but was delayed because a member was absent.[2]
