# Hu Jintao

Hu Jintao is the former President of China and General Secretary of the [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]] whose administration immediately preceded that of [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]].[1] In the Badlands Media China series feature "Empire's Enigma," the Hu regime is presented as the institutional counterpoint against which Xi's consolidation of power should be measured.[1]

## Collective leadership

Citing the Journal of Democracy, the article describes governance under Hu as "collective leadership," defined as "a system with a division of responsibilities among individual leaders in an effort to prevent arbitrary decision-making by a single top leader."[1] The same source is quoted as observing that "Hu let senior CCP figures govern their own policy domains and build their own patronage networks," whereas Xi has been "systematically demolishing the organizational bases for patronage networks controlled by others."[1]

## The 2022 humiliation

The article frames Xi's unprecedented third term as having been accompanied by the public humiliation of Hu and of other figures the author characterises as globalist-aligned leaders within the party.[1] It reports that in the direct aftermath of that third term and the public humiliation of Hu, some of the wealthiest Chinese business magnates — described by the author as corruption magnates — liquidated their assets and fled the country.[1]

## Legacy deals

The piece argues that the great majority of the China-emboldening and United States–weakening trade arrangements attributed to Beijing were put in place under administrations preceding Xi, during the long march of the American oligarchy to replace onshore production with cheap foreign labour, and that the resulting Chinese labour crisis left workers serving foreign iPhone manufacturers.[1]

## Removal at the 20th Party Congress (Lange, 2023)

Badlands Media's "The Master Builder" (February 2, 2023) describes Hu's removal during the [[20th-national-congress-of-the-chinese-communist-party|20th Party Congress]] as an event witnessed live by the world, in which [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]]'s predecessor was taken from the hall publicly and in humiliating fashion.[2] Asking why the removal was ordered, the article quotes Foreign Policy's assessment that during Hu's tenure corruption rose — and, more dangerously for the party, so did public coverage of corruption, freedom of speech online and, to a limited extent, civil society groups and NGOs.[2] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] uses the NGO element to draw a link to [[george-soros|George Soros]], presenting the removal as one moment in a wider [[anti-corruption-campaign-in-china|anti-corruption campaign]] against officials he associates with foreign-funded networks.[2]
