# Steve Witkoff

Steve Witkoff is a US Special Envoy involved in the Trump administration's negotiations with [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## Doha talks

In June 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said Iran had requested a meeting with the United States and announced talks would take place in Doha, [[qatar|Qatar]].[1] The [[white-house|White House]] said Witkoff and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] would travel to Doha for discussions aimed at advancing implementation of the US-Iran memorandum of understanding.[1]

Iran publicly contradicted the announcement, with Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei stating that no negotiations with the United States were scheduled "at any level" in the coming days while confirming that an Iranian delegation would travel to Doha for technical discussions on the existing agreement.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the sequence as a game of chicken in which one side denies attending and then appears at the last minute, a theory he said would be tested given that Witkoff and Kushner were ostensibly en route to Qatar.[1]

## March 2026 ceasefire outreach

During the March 2026 escalation between [[israel|Israel]] and [[iran|Iran]], Badlands Media reported that Witkoff had reached out to Iran — presumably on Israel's behalf — to request a ceasefire, and that Iran refused.[2] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that if accurate, the refusal indicated Israel was taking the heavier losses and was the side less eager to continue fighting, consistent with [[haaretz|Haaretz]] reporting that Iranian missiles had penetrated the [[iron-dome|Iron Dome]] to strike central Israel.[2]

## Named in the DIA espionage assessment

In June 2026, NBC News and [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] reported that the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]'s [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]] had raised its assessment of Israeli espionage to a "critical" threat level, with Witkoff named — alongside [[elbridge-colby|Elbridge Colby]] and [[michael-dimino|Michael DiMino]] — among the U.S. officials allegedly targeted by Israeli intelligence collection.[3] The reported concern centered on efforts to gather information about administration deliberations on [[iran|Iran]] and [[lebanon|Lebanon]], areas central to Witkoff's negotiating portfolio.[3] The Pentagon declined comment, a [[white-house|White House]] official called the story false, and the Israeli Embassy in Washington said its intelligence activities target adversaries rather than allies.[3]

## Oman talks and Abu Dhabi trilateral (February 2026)

In early February 2026, Witkoff and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] were expected to meet Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] and his team in [[oman|Oman]] for nuclear talks requested by [[iran|Iran]].[4] Ahead of that meeting Witkoff met Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Israeli defense officials in Israel, while US officials said privately they remained skeptical the negotiations would produce a breakthrough.[4]

Witkoff also posted the readout of the second trilateral meeting among United States, [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and Russian Federation delegations held in Abu Dhabi over February 4 and 5, 2026.[4] The readout reported agreement that Russia and Ukraine would each release 157 prisoners of war — the first exchange in five months — and that the United States and [[russia|Russia]] would establish a military-to-military dialogue led by General [[alexus-grynkewich|Alexus Grynkewich]] of [[united-states-european-command|US European Command]].[4] The delegations discussed methods to implement a ceasefire and monitor the cessation of military activities, agreed to report back to their capitals and continue talks, and thanked the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] for hosting and President [[donald-trump|Donald J. Trump]] for his leadership.[4]

## February 2026 Abu Dhabi and Istanbul round

In early February 2026, Witkoff was scheduled to hold a series of meetings across two regional hubs: talks with Russian and Ukrainian representatives in Abu Dhabi and separate discussions with Iranian officials in [[istanbul|Istanbul]] on [[iran|Iran]]'s nuclear program.[5] The meetings came amid heightened tensions involving Iran, [[russia|Russia]], and Ukraine, with Russian and Iranian officials acknowledging ongoing contacts with Washington over the nuclear deal framework while US officials warned of possible military consequences should diplomacy fail.[5] After US warnings, Iranian authorities said they had no plans to conduct live-fire naval exercises in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], signaling temporary de-escalation.[5] Badlands commentary characterized the Abu Dhabi track as "charting the course of the Golden Age," contrasting it with the public frustration of [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]]; the round is covered at [[2026-witkoff-abu-dhabi-and-istanbul-talks|the 2026 Witkoff Abu Dhabi and Istanbul talks]].[5]

## January 2026 Iran crisis

During the January 2026 Iran protests and the accompanying [[backchannel-diplomacy|backchannel]] exchanges, Witkoff was identified as advocating diplomacy over military intervention while President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] communicated to Tehran that the United States did not intend to strike.[6] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited his posture, alongside Iranian disclosures of alleged Israeli and Western intelligence involvement, as evidence that the attempted regime-change operation was collapsing.[6]

## Geneva round with Iran (February 2026)

Witkoff took part in the second round of high-stakes indirect United States–Iran negotiations, which concluded in Geneva on Tuesday, February 17, 2026.[7] The Oman-mediated talks featured a U.S. delegation that included Witkoff as special envoy alongside [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]], with Iranian officials reporting that the two sides had reached an understanding on core principles aimed at resolving the long-running nuclear dispute.[7] Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] said progress had been made and that Tehran would submit detailed proposals within two weeks, while making clear Iran would accept nuclear concessions in exchange for sanctions relief but would not abandon uranium enrichment or negotiate over its ballistic missile program.[7]

## Launch of Phase Two in Gaza (January 2026)

On January 15, 2026, Witkoff, as Special Envoy for Peace Missions, announced the start of Phase Two of the Gaza ceasefire framework, stating: "Today, on behalf of President Trump, we are announcing the launch of Phase Two of the President's 20-Point Plan to End the Gaza Conflict, moving from ceasefire to demilitarization, technocratic governance, and reconstruction."[8] Witkoff credited [[egypt|Egypt]], [[qatar|Qatar]] and [[turkey|Turkey]] with finalizing the plan's particulars.[8]

The announcement followed days of heightened regional tension involving [[israel|Israel]], [[iran|Iran]] and the United States, during which Iran briefly closed its airspace and unsourced reports of an imminent US strike on Iran circulated in regional and Russian state media.[8] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the Iran war narrative functioned as a distraction operation covering the finalization of Phase Two, and described Phase Two as the critical step because it is when Arab states take control of Gaza from the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]].[8]

## USS Abraham Lincoln visit (February 2026)

In a sign of heightened U.S.–[[iran|Iran]] tensions in February 2026, Tehran was informed in advance of a visit by Witkoff, as U.S. special envoy, and adviser [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] to the aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln in the region — a move read as part of Washington's pressure campaign even as indirect negotiations continued in [[oman|Oman]] and were planned for [[istanbul|Istanbul]].[9]

## Iranian rejection (March 2026)

In March 2026, amid the fourth week of the Iran–Israel exchange of fire, Iran rejected negotiations with Witkoff and fellow US envoy [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]], branding the pair "backstabbers" over prior strikes that followed February talks.[10] Tehran signaled a preference for dealing with Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] instead.[10] The rejection came as Washington circulated a one-month ceasefire proposal through Pakistani intermediaries demanding that Iran dismantle its nuclear capabilities, abandon its proxy militias, forswear nuclear weapons, and keep the Strait of Hormuz open in exchange for full sanctions relief and civilian nuclear assistance.[10]

## Moscow trip and Putin meeting (March 2025)

In March 2025, Witkoff — described as White House Middle East envoy — was planning to travel to Moscow later that week for a meeting with Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], according to a source with direct knowledge.[11] Axios reported that the meeting was set to take place several days after a key meeting between US and Ukrainian officials in Saudi Arabia focused on a possible ceasefire in the war with Russia.[11] Witkoff had been expected to participate in the meeting with the Ukrainians, but instead appeared set to serve as the main channel of communication with Russia as part of the division of labor inside President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s foreign policy and national security team; it would be his second meeting with Putin.[11]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] commented that "it doesn't take a faith-based approach to understand that the deals are already done, and that what we're watching is a translation of the story of the peacemakers to a world long calcified against its possibility," adding that "Trump and Putin are rapidly cultivating mandate" and advising readers to "let them cook."[11]

## Denial of departure reports (October 2025)

In October 2025, Witkoff denied a story published by [[middle-east-eye|Middle East Eye]] reporting that he planned to step back from the Trump administration following his successful mediation of a Gaza peace deal.[12] "This story is 100 percent Fake News and should be immediately retracted. I often wonder where these so-called 'reporters' come up with such laughable nonsense," Witkoff wrote on X, adding, "I am more engaged on the peace process than ever, and I am continuing to proudly serve the President of the United States!"[12] MEE had reported that two sources familiar with the matter said Witkoff planned to step back after "gruelling" diplomacy that achieved a Gaza ceasefire, in order to refocus on his business.[12] Witkoff was designated a special envoy by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], although he formally served as a State Department employee for the first half of that year.[12]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said he did not understand why anybody would be suspicious of the reports, arguing Witkoff had done his job by forcing [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] into signing a deal he did not want to sign, and that enforcing the deal was never in Witkoff's job description — that task falling to Trump and the Arabs if Netanyahu betrays them.[12]

## July 2025 Gaza negotiations

Ahead of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s second White House meeting with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on July 8, 2025, Witkoff publicly expressed hope that a Gaza ceasefire and hostage release deal could be reached that week.[13] He had been slated to fly to Qatar that Tuesday to join ongoing proximity talks between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]] in Doha, but two sources familiar with the matter said he pushed back his flight, with no new departure date scheduled.[13] Witkoff also met with a delegation of senior Qatari officials at the White House for more than three hours before Netanyahu's follow-up session, and reports indicated that "a significant amount of progress still needs to be made."[13]

## Role in the January 2025 Gaza Ceasefire

As President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Middle East envoy, Witkoff joined Brett McGurk, the longtime Middle East negotiator for President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], in Doha for the final push that produced the ceasefire-for-hostages deal announced by Qatar's prime minister on January 15, 2025.[14] A senior Biden administration official described the cooperation between the outgoing and incoming administrations as "almost unprecedented," made possible by a rare intersection of interests between bitter rivals who both saw an opening after Trump's November victory.[14] Once the agreement was announced, both presidents claimed full credit for it.[14]

## Leading the Oman nuclear delegation (April 2025)

Witkoff was designated to lead the US delegation for nuclear talks with Iran in Oman on Saturday, April 12, 2025, according to sources cited by *Axios*.[15] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had surprised the world on Monday, April 7 by announcing the high-level meeting, with reporting warning that if diplomacy failed the next stage was likely war.[15] At the time only "negotiations about the negotiations" had occurred: Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]], reported to be Tehran's chief negotiator, insisted the talks would be "indirect" with Omani mediators passing messages, while Trump insisted they would be direct — a plan two US officials confirmed.[15]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that the announcement, made during Trump's Oval Office press huddle with Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], visibly unsettled the Israeli leader, and noted that [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]]'s reporting confirmed Israel had been briefed in advance yet still found the moment embarrassing.[15] He tallied Witkoff's expanding portfolio — negotiating with Hamas in Qatar, with [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] in Russia, and now with the Iranians in Oman — and asked whether fellow special envoy [[adam-boehler|Adam Boehler]] would also attend, arguing that rhetoric from both men about Hamas and the Qataris suggested a brokered peace with the Arab world, and by extension the wider Muslim world, was closer than it appeared.[15]

## Anticipated departure (late 2025)

Reporting cited by *The Times of Israel* in late September 2025 indicated that Trump envoy Witkoff was expected to step down at the end of the year, as a Gaza ceasefire deal neared finalization.[16]

## Post-war Gaza plan (August 2025)

On Tuesday, August 26, 2025, Witkoff announced that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would chair a "large meeting" at the [[white-house|White House]] the following day to discuss the [[post-war-gaza-comprehensive-plan|comprehensive plan]] the administration was assembling for the post-war management of Gaza.[17] The remarks appeared to be the first time Witkoff had revealed the existence of a US plan for the so-called day after, as Washington had largely deferred on the issue to its Arab allies since Trump's February remarks about taking over Gaza and permanently relocating its residents.[17] Speaking in a Fox News interview, Witkoff said that "many people are going to see how robust it is and how well meaning it is, and it reflects President Trump's humanitarian motives," without elaborating further.[17]

## Rejection of Starmer's Ukraine peace plan (2025)

Ahead of ceasefire talks in Saudi Arabia in March 2025, Witkoff publicly rejected British Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]]'s peace plans for Ukraine as "a posture and a pose," in an interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]].[18] Starmer had proposed a "coalition of the willing" of European and NATO nations prepared to provide military assistance and put boots on the ground in Ukraine after a ceasefire.[18] Witkoff called the plan "a combination of a posture and a pose and a combination of also being simplistic," dismissing what he described as the notion "that we have all got to be like Winston Churchill" and that "Russians are going to march across Europe," which he called preposterous given the existence of NATO.[18] He also praised Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], saying "I don't regard Putin as a bad guy. He's super smart," and adding that he did not believe Putin wanted to take all of Europe.[18]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] agreed with Witkoff's framing that the Donbas has always been Russian — effectively casting Ukrainians as the invaders — but criticized his claim that "no one wants to talk about" the issue, noting that many in independent media had discussed it for years, and asking whether things only become true once Tucker Carlson discusses them.[18]

## United Hatzalah gala remarks (June 2025)

Speaking at a fundraising gala for the emergency response group United Hatzalah in New York on Wednesday, June 11, 2025, Witkoff quipped that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] could serve simultaneously as U.S. president and Israeli prime minister.[20] Describing Trump as "an incredible friend to the Jewish people," he said: "No disrespect to Prime Minister Netanyahu, who I actually spoke to today, but I think President Trump could be the first sitting president who could be prime minister of Israel at the same time," drawing laughter and applause from the audience.[20] Witkoff also stressed that the two countries must act together to prevent [[iran|Iran]] from acquiring nuclear weapons, "no matter the cost," while Tehran continued to insist its nuclear program was peaceful.[20] The remarks came amid reports of a strong rift between Trump and Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[20]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the quip as deliberately disrespectful to Netanyahu, arguing that Witkoff had been sent in to do what Netanyahu had refused to do and that the two had likely been at odds for months.[20] He noted the timing — the same day Netanyahu was "flexing like he never has before" with strikes on Iran — and argued that Netanyahu could not object because he now had to appeal to Trump for U.S. military support.[20]

## Second round of US-Iran talks in Rome (April 2025)

On Saturday, April 19, 2025, Witkoff led the American delegation at a second round of U.S.-Iran nuclear talks in Rome, opposite Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]]; the session lasted four hours.[19] Whereas the first round the previous weekend in Oman focused on setting the tone and format, U.S. officials said their goal for the Rome round was to produce a framework for how the negotiations would proceed, with expert-level talks expected to continue on April 26 in Oman.[19] The talks unfolded amid continuing debate inside the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration, and between Washington and Israel, over whether diplomacy or military strikes were more likely to prevent Tehran from obtaining a bomb.[19]

Reporting by [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]] placed Witkoff in a faction of private-sector businessmen led by Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] and including [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]], [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], and [[adam-boehler|Adam Boehler]], which sought a negotiated peace, against a group of neoconservatives — [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]], [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]], and [[tom-cotton|Tom Cotton]] — aligned with Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[19] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that once Trump and Witkoff had "sued for peace" with Iran, a new narrative emerged that Netanyahu was considering a strike independent of Trump and the West.[19]

## Structure of the Gaza three-phase deal

Badlands commentary in February 2025 credited Middle East envoy Witkoff with constructing the original Gaza agreement as a three-phase deal, with the second phase — intended to lead to a permanent ceasefire and a full withdrawal of Israeli forces from Gaza — set to unfold in the days after [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s scheduled White House meeting with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[21]


## Putin's defense of Witkoff (September 2025)

At a press conference closing his four-day visit to China in September 2025, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] publicly defended Witkoff, saying the US envoy "accurately" conveys both Moscow's and Washington's positions on ending the Ukraine conflict.[22] Putin stressed that Witkoff is presenting "the position of the American president himself," adding: "Our talks in Anchorage showed that he quite accurately conveys the position of the Russian side."[22] The endorsement followed sustained criticism of Witkoff in Western media after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] met Putin in Alaska the previous month in an effort to mediate a ceasefire; Trump had since urged direct talks between Putin and [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], and Putin said he was ready to host the Ukrainian leader in Moscow.[22]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the remarks as an acknowledgment that Trump's team fully understands the Russian perspective, identifying understanding and trust as the foundation of a Putin–Trump relationship he argued would have generational impact.[22] Russian special economic envoy [[kirill-dmitriev|Kirill Dmitriev]] also publicly defended Witkoff in the same period while accusing the [[european-union|European Union]] of sabotaging the US-led peace process, a posture Badlands framed as isolating the European elite and drawing the Putin and Trump teams closer together.[22]

## Gaza aid inspection trip (July 2025)

Axios reported that Witkoff, as President [[donald-trump|Trump's]] envoy, was expected to fly to [[israel|Israel]] on July 30, 2025 to discuss the humanitarian crisis in Gaza, and that he might also visit Gaza and tour [[gaza-humanitarian-foundation|GHF]] aid centers.[23] The trip came amid conflicting reports on starvation among the population in Gaza, violence at aid distribution centers, and the political narrative that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] was continuing the war to remain in power; observers argued Israel was losing the propaganda war over its conflict with [[hamas|Hamas]].[23] Trump had shown interest in obtaining a better understanding of conditions on the ground, and Witkoff's visit was seen as part of that effort.[23]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that many of Trump's appointments were made not for altruism but because their histories would help expose the breadth of the Deep State, while Trump's lifelong friends serving in "Special Envoy/Employee" roles were doing much of the heavy lifting on geopolitical crises.[23] He noted that Witkoff and [[adam-boehler|Adam Boehler]], two Special Government Employees who had become thorns in the side of Netanyahu's government, are both Jewish, and predicted that Witkoff would validate many of the accusations being levied at Israel — with his background blunting the usual attempts to discredit him.[23]

## Leaked Ushakov call and Putin's defense (November 2025)

In late November 2025, Bloomberg News published a transcript of a leaked telephone call between Witkoff and Kremlin foreign policy aide [[yuri-ushakov|Yuri Ushakov]].[24] Critics seized on the call to accuse Witkoff of one-sidedness toward Russia at a time when the Trump administration was pressing a Kremlin-friendly plan to end the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[24]

Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] publicly defended the envoy against accusations of pro-Kremlin bias, calling him "an intelligent man" who was properly representing the position of his country and its leader.[24] Speaking at a news conference in Kyrgyzstan during a state visit, Putin said, "Mr. Witkoff is clearly traveling to Moscow on President Trump's orders to negotiate with us," and added that given that assignment, "it would probably be surprising if he cursed us with obscenities in his conversations with Ushakov."[24] Witkoff had met Putin several times in Moscow and was scheduled to return the following week to continue negotiations over Trump's peace proposal.[24] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] remarked that the spectacle of pro-Israel commentators turning on Witkoff was itself revealing about who was most angered by the negotiations.[24]

## December 2025 Moscow trip after Miami talks

U.S. and Ukrainian delegations met for five hours in Miami on Sunday, November 30, 2025 to outline a potential peace framework with Russia, after which Witkoff was slated to travel to Moscow to discuss the updated framework with President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[25] The revised proposal documents were to reflect changes discussed with Kyiv and European officials, with territorial concessions and postwar security guarantees remaining the central unresolved issues.[25] Ukrainian officials described the negotiations, which focused largely on where a future territorial boundary would be drawn, as difficult but productive, while Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] said progress had been made but cautioned that "more work" remained.[25]

## October 2025 call with Yuri Ushakov

Bloomberg published on November 25, 2025 a transcript of an October 14 phone call lasting just over five minutes between Witkoff, in his capacity as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s special envoy for peace missions, and [[yuri-ushakov|Yuri Ushakov]], [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s most senior foreign-policy adviser.[26] In the exchange Witkoff said that "if we can get the Russia-Ukraine thing solved, everybody'll be jumping for joy," and Ushakov joked that only one problem needed solving — the Russian-Ukrainian war.[26] Ushakov asked whether it would be useful for "our bosses" to speak by phone, and Witkoff agreed, suggesting that a follow-up conversation "could lead to big stuff"; Witkoff also mentioned that [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]] was coming to the White House that Friday, to which Ushakov replied that he already knew.[26] Badlands commentator Burning Bright read the transcript alongside Trump's remark that the war would end at the "Final Stage" — echoing a Putin statement from June — arguing that "the deals are already done."[26]

## Gaza ceasefire and Accords expansion (January 2025)

In announcing that he would use the momentum of the Gaza ceasefire to expand the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]], President-elect Donald Trump wrote on Truth Social that his national security team, "through the efforts of Special Envoy to the Middle East, Steve Witkoff," would continue to work closely with Israel and US allies to ensure that Gaza never again becomes a terrorist safe haven.[27]

## June 2025 Abraham Accords expansion remarks

Speaking to CNBC on June 25, 2025, Witkoff said the White House expects "big announcements on countries that are coming into the Abraham Accords," describing expansion of the pact as one of President Donald Trump's key objectives.[28] He noted that he and his team were working in coordination with Secretary of State Marco Rubio and the State Department to "get more countries to come into" the accords.[28] Witkoff added that the administration hoped for "normalization across an array of countries that people never would have contemplated would come in," framing further accession as a stabilizer for the Middle East.[28] Badlands commentary responded to the remarks by asking whether Iran itself might be among the prospective entrants.[28]

## April 2025: envoy feud and Kremlin signaling

Commenting on remarks by Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] in April 2025 about [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s respect for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the Kremlin was signaling its belief that Trump would side with Witkoff in an unfolding feud against [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]], [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and the [[neoconservatives|NeoCons]].[29] The NeoCons' criticism of the businessmen-turned-envoys Witkoff and [[adam-boehler|Adam Boehler]] was that face-to-face meetings with Putin, Hamas and the Iranians "legitimized" adversaries; the envoys countered that they could not properly negotiate with anyone without the opportunity to look them in the eye.[29] The commentary noted Trump's announced travel to Riyadh and the Middle East on May 13 and anticipated a matching announcement from Putin.[29]

## December 2025 Moscow trip

Witkoff, President Donald Trump's special envoy, traveled to Moscow with the president's son-in-law Jared Kushner in early December 2025 for talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin.[30] The visit came a day after European leaders held a phone call in which they expressed deep distrust of US peace diplomacy and warned that Washington might betray Ukraine on the question of territory.[30]

## January 2025 shuttle diplomacy

Witkoff, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Middle East envoy, arrived in Israel on Wednesday, January 29, 2025 after holding meetings in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], as part of administration efforts to include the possibility of Israeli-Saudi normalization in a future regional agreement.[31] His trip was aimed at preparing the ground for major negotiations on the second stage of the hostage deal, ensuring Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] would proceed despite domestic political risk and would act to free all hostages, reach a cease-fire, and withdraw from the Gaza Strip.[31]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that after negotiating the ceasefire deal on January 19 — the day before Trump formally took office — Witkoff announced there would be no normalization deal with the Saudis if Israel did not immediately end the war, and that Witkoff had just become the first U.S. diplomat to enter Gaza in twenty years.[31]

## Claims of Russian concessions after the Alaska summit (August 2025)

Witkoff, serving as U.S. special envoy, said that Russia gave in quickly to concessions during [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]]'s meeting with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in Anchorage, Alaska — describing the concessions as coming "almost immediately."[32] His remarks came as the U.S. and European allies sorted out security guarantees to accompany a peace deal, and after he said that Putin was on board with permitting the U.S. and other European allies to provide additional protection for Ukraine akin to NATO's Article 5 mutual defense clause.[32] Speaking to Fox News, Witkoff declined to detail the discussions: "I don't want to delve into the specifics of what was discussed, because we're in a negotiation, mediating, and I think we're going to be very successful because of the leadership of President Trump."[32] Badlands contributor Burning Bright read the Trump-Putin dynamic not as a complex web of negotiations but as a grand story meant to destroy the globalist empire.[32]
