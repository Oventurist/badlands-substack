# Robert Dorgan

Robert Dorgan was a 56-year-old Rhode Island man identified as the perpetrator of the February 2026 mass shooting at an ice arena in Pawtucket, Rhode Island, in which he killed his former wife and one of their adult sons before taking his own life.[1] Dorgan also went by the name Roberta Esposito and had undergone gender reassignment surgery several years before the attack; he carried out the shooting while dressed in women's clothing.[1]

## The Pawtucket shooting

On a Monday afternoon in February 2026, Dorgan opened fire inside the Dennis M. Lynch Arena during a crowded "Senior Night" high school hockey game, while members of his family were in the stands watching another of his sons compete on the ice.[1] He fatally shot his 52-year-old ex-wife, Rhonda Dorgan, and his 23-year-old son, Aidan Dorgan; Rhonda died at the scene and Aidan later died at Rhode Island Hospital in Providence.[1] Three additional relatives and associates — Rhonda's parents, Linda and Gerald Dorgan, and a family friend, Thomas Geruso — were critically wounded and hospitalized.[1]

A bystander intervened during the attack and initially succeeded in disarming Dorgan, but he produced a second firearm and used it to kill himself.[1] Police characterized the incident as domestic-related. Family members described Dorgan's long history of mental health struggles and long-standing disputes tied to his gender transition, which contributed to the couple's divorce around 2020.[1] For a fuller account of the incident, see [[pawtucket-ice-arena-shooting-2026|the Pawtucket ice arena shooting]].

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the case particularly gruesome even by the standards of mass shootings, emphasizing that Dorgan chose to murder his family at his own child's hockey game and thereby traumatized the surviving son, who witnessed the killings of his mother and brother.[1] She declined to name the shooter on the grounds that he "wanted to be remembered" and that publicity rewards terrorists, and framed the killings as the second act of what she described as trans-involved terrorism within a single week, asking rhetorically how many deaths would be required before institutional psychiatric care was restored.[1] She also predicted that the episode would generate calls for disarmament and for greater social acceptance of transgender individuals rather than scrutiny of the perpetrator's conduct.[1]
