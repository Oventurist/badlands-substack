# Robin Westman

Robin Westman is the person identified by officials as the alleged gunman in the [[2025-minneapolis-church-shooting|2025 Minneapolis church shooting]].[1]

## Identification

At a press briefing on Wednesday, August 27, 2025, officials identified Westman — described in reporting as a trans-identifying male in his twenties — as the suspect who opened fire during Mass at the [[annunciation-catholic-church|Annunciation Catholic Church]].[1] Two children were killed and at least seventeen other people were injured.[1]

## Coverage and commentary

Badlands commentary invoked the "72-hour rule" regarding early reporting on the attack and situated the case within a broader argument about violence committed by trans-identifying individuals, while criticizing calls for gun control in its aftermath.[1] Detailed coverage of the case was attributed to [[brad-zerbo|Brad Zerbo]] on the program WWG1WGA After Dark.[1]

## Identification of the former girlfriend

In early September 2025 the New York Post reported that Westman had recently ended a relationship of several years with a longtime girlfriend before the attack, raging about her "blue hair and pronouns" and complaining about being unable to murder her because doing so "would really f–k up" the plan to massacre children.[2] Westman, 23, killed two children and injured 18 other people at a Minneapolis Catholic school; the breakup had previously been disclosed by Westman's father.[2]

The ex-partner was identified by the Daily Mail as 22-year-old Abigail Bodick, whom the outlet described as a "furry," noting she had been pictured on social media wearing cat ears and whiskers at an anime convention earlier that year.[2] In documents linked to Westman after the shooting, Westman repeatedly referred to a person named "Aby" and "Abbey," calling them a "catalyst" for the behavior and the "root of my suffering."[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the coverage seemed designed to elicit sympathy for the ex-girlfriend while conceding the shooter blamed her for his psychosis, and urged that attention return to the two dead children and 18 injured rather than to the ideology and lifestyle invoked around the case.[2]
