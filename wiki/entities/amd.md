# AMD

AMD (Advanced Micro Devices) is a U.S. semiconductor company named, alongside [[nvidia|Nvidia]], as one of the foreign AI-chip providers that Beijing's [[china-ai-data-center-buildout|AI data center buildout]] plan explicitly aims to displace.[1]

Under the plan, at least 80 percent of core technology — notably AI chips — is to be sourced from Chinese suppliers such as [[huawei|Huawei]], accelerating the maturation of domestic alternatives to AMD and Nvidia hardware.[1]

## Exclusion from Chinese government procurement (2024)

In March 2024, [[china|China]] issued new government procurement guidelines phasing AMD processors, along with those of [[intel|Intel]], out of state PCs and servers as part of a drive to replace foreign technology with domestic alternatives.[2] The guidance also targeted Microsoft's Windows operating system and foreign database software, and accompanied a localization push inside state-owned enterprises.[2] The measure was described as retaliation for US restrictions on Chinese access to advanced Nvidia AI chips and ASML lithography equipment.[2]

## US export roadblock on China AI chip (March 2024)

In early March 2024, AMD hit a U.S. government roadblock in attempting to sell an artificial intelligence chip tailored for the Chinese market, part of Washington's crackdown on the export of advanced technologies to China.[3] The company had hoped to gain a green light from the [[department-of-commerce|Commerce Department]] to sell the AI processor to Chinese customers; the chip had lower performance than what AMD sells outside China and was designed specifically to meet U.S. export restrictions.[3] U.S. officials nonetheless told AMD the chip was still too powerful and that the company must obtain a license from Commerce's [[bureau-of-industry-and-security|Bureau of Industry and Security]] to sell it. AMD did not immediately comment and the bureau declined to comment; it was not clear whether AMD would apply for a license.[3]

The episode reflected a broader U.S. effort to limit Chinese access to cutting-edge semiconductors capable of developing AI models, and to the tools used to manufacture them, out of concern that Beijing would gain a military edge. The [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration unveiled an initial set of export controls in 2022 and strengthened them in October 2023 to cover more technology and curb sales to intermediary nations that might undermine the ban.[3] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the move as one of the "Actuals" described in [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]]'s [[devolution|devolution]] research — surface-level contradictions to the "China Joe" narrative, with Biden's policy actions resembling anything but globalist subservience while President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] similarly focused on on-shoring production.[3]
