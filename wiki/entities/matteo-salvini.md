# Matteo Salvini

Matteo Salvini is the Deputy Prime Minister of Italy and leader of Italy's League party, and a longtime political ally of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Position on Russia and European security

Salvini said he does not regard Russia as Europe's principal threat, arguing instead that the continent should concentrate on challenges including illegal immigration, economic competitiveness, and internal security.[1] His comments came amid continued debate within Europe over defense spending and long-term policy toward Moscow.[1] He reiterated his support for pursuing diplomacy to end the war in Ukraine while cautioning against policies that could further escalate tensions with Russia, and criticized aspects of the European Union's approach to sanctions and defense policy, maintaining that Italy should prioritize its national interests.[1] His remarks highlighted ongoing divisions within Europe over how to balance military support for Ukraine, relations with Russia, and broader security priorities as NATO members continue to increase defense spending and reassess the continent's strategic posture.[1]

## Legal and political pressure

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed Salvini's intervention as a reminder that Italy is part of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and that Prime Minister [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] is aligned with that project.[1] In this account, the European Union placed Salvini's political party under investigation in 2019, accusing it of being funded and controlled by Vladimir Putin; Salvini himself was not specifically targeted in that case.[1] He was investigated in 2018 for closing Italian ports to prevent migrants from entering the country, and again in 2019 on similar grounds, being charged with "migrant kidnapping," and was ultimately acquitted of all charges.[1]

During 2018–2019 Salvini served as Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of the Interior.[1] Despite being described as the most popular politician in Italy and one of the faces of Europe's right-wing populist movement, he was consistently prevented from becoming Prime Minister; the Italian political elite understood that his ascent to the premiership would likely bring Italy into alignment with Russia and upset the balance of power in Europe.[1] On that reading, Meloni emerged as a critical figure at the moment Italy needed a new face to carry the torch for Salvini, and Trump's public spats with Meloni are interpreted as intended to help her consolidate power inside and outside Italy — a deception strategy adopted after the direct path to power in Italy was stonewalled by entrenched elites.[1]

## Lega, Trump branding, and Rosneft

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry recalled that in 2017 Salvini, later Deputy Prime Minister of Italy, launched Lega with branding adopted from the 2016 Trump campaign down to the templating of campaign signage.[2] Salvini and Lega were subsequently placed under investigation by Italian and European authorities who accused the group of allegiance to the Kremlin after party financing was allegedly connected to the Russian energy company Rosneft — the same company the commentator says is now building nuclear power plants worldwide for the Sovereign Alliance.[2] Lega aligned with Giorgia Meloni's Brothers of Italy in 2022 to elect her Prime Minister.[2]

## Attacks on Macron (August 2025)

In August 2025 Salvini, then Italian Deputy Prime Minister, claimed that some 80% of France's population "can't wait" for President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] to leave office, and asserted that Macron's warlike rhetoric — including talk of sending troops to Ukraine — was aimed only at boosting his dwindling popularity at home.[3] The remarks came shortly after Paris summoned Italian Ambassador Emanuela D'Alessandro over Salvini's previous statements about the French president; the week before, Salvini had mockingly urged Macron to take up arms and go fight for Ukraine alone, since "not even [one] Frenchman would follow him."[3]

The diplomatic incident apparently left Salvini unfazed, and he reiterated his position at a public event for his right-wing Lega party in Trentino, linking Macron's comments to his tanking ratings: in an attempt to raise his approval, Macron "one day attacks [US President Donald] Trump, one day [Russian President Vladimir] Putin, one day [Israeli PM] Netanyahu, one day the Italian government."[3] Salvini added: "For months now, he's been harping on about war, the nuclear umbrella, bazookas, missiles, and a European army. Do you know why? In my opinion, they cooked all this up because Macron's popularity at home is at its lowest."[3]

## Badlands commentary on Lega financing

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterised Salvini as "#OurBoyMatteo" and as an earnest populist whose emergence in the Italian Parliament and on the geopolitical stage preceded the rise of [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] in 2022.[3] The commentator recalled that Salvini adopted President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2016 campaign branding for his own 2018 campaign and that in 2019 he called [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] "the best politician and statesman in the world," after which he was targeted by the media and accused of all sorts of scandal.[3] Reading Salvini's Wikipedia entry on the founding of his 2018 party, GhostofBasedPatrickHenry highlighted the appearance of both [[rosneft|Rosneft]] and Banca Intesa — names he had previously recorded as entities of the Sovereign Alliance from on-stream conversations with Alex Krainer of Zero Hedge — and suggested that Putin was working with [[intesa-sanpaolo|Banca Intesa]] during Trump's first term to covertly fund Salvini's populist party.[3]
