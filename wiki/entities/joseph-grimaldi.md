# Joseph Grimaldi

**Joseph Grimaldi** was an English performer of the late 1700s and early 1800s, conventionally credited with originating the familiar powder-white, red-haired clown; the Badlands Media essay "[[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] — What is Clown World?" reports this attribution and then disputes it.[1]

The author objects that Grimaldi was dressed as a clown and put on stage for the first time at age two or three, and doubts that a toddler could have invented an entire style of entertainment built on an original character; he adds that the harlequin type of clown predates Grimaldi in any case.[1] In his view, a closer look reveals secret societies operating behind Grimaldi: citing Paul Stobbs, author of *The Nephilim Looked Like Clowns*, he states that Grimaldi was made to dress like a clown by a Freemason named Charles Dibden, and asks rhetorically where Dibden got the idea.[1] This claim supports the essay's larger argument that the clown archetype descends from occult sources rather than from ordinary theatrical invention.[1]
