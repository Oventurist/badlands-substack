# World Professional Association for Transgender Health (WPATH)

The World Professional Association for Transgender Health, commonly abbreviated WPATH, is described in Badlands Media coverage as the world's leading transgender health lobby group and the body responsible for issuing the [[wpath-standards-of-care|Standards of Care]] that guide clinical practice in gender medicine.[1]

## Jacobs controversy

In March 2024, [[reduxx|Reduxx]] reported that [[laura-a-jacobs|Laura A. Jacobs]], a WPATH-certified New York psychotherapist and trans-identified male, had influenced the development of WPATH guidance on "gender dysphoria" in adolescents and had served as an influential member of the committee that established the most recent Standards of Care.[1] Jacobs had previously contributed a chapter to an anthology promoting BDSM practices including "age play" and "genital torture," and had described himself as a "kinky, BDSM sexworker" who counsels clients from "adolescents to the elderly."[1]

The report, amplified by circulating footage of a TEDx talk in which Jacobs speculated about "artistic and creative" genital surgeries, intensified existing criticism of WPATH's internal standards-setting process and of the professional backgrounds of those shaping adolescent gender medicine guidance.[1]

## The 2010 de-psychopathologization statement

In 2010 the WPATH Board of Directors issued a statement "strongly urg[ing] the de-psychopathologisation of gender variance worldwide," arguing that gender characteristics and identities not stereotypically associated with one's assigned sex at birth are a common and culturally diverse human phenomenon that should not be judged inherently pathological or negative.[2] The statement held that psychopathologization reinforces stigma, makes prejudice and discrimination more likely, and increases risks to the mental and physical well-being of transgender and transsexual people, and it urged governmental and medical professional organizations to review their policies to eliminate stigma toward gender-variant people.[2]

A February 2024 Badlands Media essay by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that this statement deliberately conflates people with the clinical condition of [[gender-dysphoria|gender dysphoria]] with the purposefully ambiguous category of "gender-variant people," producing disorder in medical systems that adopt WPATH's framing and deterring many doctors from providing [[gender-affirming-care|gender affirmative care]].[2] He described the resulting practice — affirming children without first evaluating their mental health, for fear of being labelled a bigot — as the tip of the spear of radical affirmative care, and characterized the claim that mental-health safeguards endanger mental health as Orwellian doublespeak.[2]

## International resistance

The essay reported that Finland, Norway, the United Kingdom, and Sweden have all resisted the WPATH paradigm and erected medical gatekeeping prioritizing mental health, while North American mainstream media gave that dissent little coverage.[2] It cited the [[nhs|NHS]] interim service specification of October 2022, which required specialist tertiary paediatric providers with strong mental-health partnerships and robust safeguarding, as a radical departure from WPATH's model — an action WPATH and allied activists called transphobic and claimed would "kill trans kids" because it limited access to [[puberty-blockers|puberty blockers]].[2] By contrast, the article stated the model had been fully adopted in [[canada|Canada]] by legislators, politicians, and medical professionals, and pointed to [[planned-parenthood|Planned Parenthood]] as illustrating WPATH's influence in the United States.[2]

The essay also cited a Christina Button article as refuting WPATH-associated claims that transgender individuals are born with the brain of the other sex and that gender identity is brain-determined.[2]
