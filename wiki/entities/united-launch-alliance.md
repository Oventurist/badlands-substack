# United Launch Alliance

United Launch Alliance (ULA) is an American launch services provider that operates the Atlas 5 rocket family and flies missions from Space Launch Complex 41 at Cape Canaveral on Florida's Space Coast.[1]

## ViaSat-3 F2 scrub, November 2025

On the night of Wednesday, November 5, 2025, ULA scrubbed a planned Atlas 5 launch carrying the ViaSat-3 F2 communications satellite for the California-based operator Viasat, after a valve problem could not be resolved inside the launch window.[1] The company said that "an issue encountered while cycling the booster liquid oxygen tank valve during final checkouts" forced it to stand down and target the following day.[1] The rescheduled liftoff from Space Launch Complex 41 was set for 10:16 p.m. EST (0316 UTC), the opening of a 44-minute window, with the rocket heading due east after departing the Space Coast.[1]

The scrub was reported by Space Flight Now, and subsequent local reporting confirmed that ULA was "now targeting 10:16 p.m. Nov. 6 for the liftoff of the ViaSat-3 F2 mission."[1] The attempt came amid a busy Cape Canaveral cadence in which Florida was set to tie its yearly launch record, following a successful [[spacex|SpaceX]] launch earlier the same day.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], reporting from Cocoa Beach during the tenth stop of the [[great-american-restoration-tour|Great American Restoration Tour]], described watching the scrubbed launch from the beach and speaking with satellite personnel staying at the same hotel.[1] Individuals identifying themselves as Viasat employees told her the satellite would provide high-speed internet for U.S. domestic flights, far faster than existing service, and that Viasat satellites already supplied the Delta fleet.[1]
