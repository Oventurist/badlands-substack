# Viktor Yushchenko

Viktor Yushchenko was one of the two leading candidates in Ukraine's 2004 presidential election and the beneficiary of the [[orange-revolution|Orange Revolution]].[1]

Yushchenko represented pro-Western democratic reforms and closer ties to Europe and [[nato|NATO]], in contrast to [[viktor-yanukovych|Viktor Yanukovych]], who had a good working relationship with Vladimir Putin.[1] Official run-off results declared Yanukovych the winner with 49.5% to Yushchenko's 46.6%, but fraud allegations triggered weeks of mass protest in Kyiv's Independence Square by demonstrators wearing orange, Yushchenko's campaign color.[1] The Supreme Court annulled the result and a December 26 rerun gave Yushchenko 52% of the vote; he was inaugurated in January 2005, marking a temporary shift toward EU and NATO integration.[1]

Badlands Media calls the outcome pyrrhic: Yushchenko's government fractured through infighting, paving the way for Yanukovych's return in 2010 and the 2014 [[euromaidan|Maidan]] events.[1]

## Backing during the Orange Revolution

"Two Wings, One Ugly Bird" states that during Ukraine's 2004 [[orange-revolution|Orange Revolution]] the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]] coached pro-Western parties including Yushchenko's bloc, while the [[national-democratic-institute|National Democratic Institute]] poured money into "voter education" and opposition training.[2] The article argues the resulting uprising, though presented as spontaneous, was underwritten by millions in [[national-endowment-for-democracy|NED]] grants and aligned with State Department objectives.[2]
