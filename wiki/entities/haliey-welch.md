# Haliey Welch

Haliey Welch, known online as the "Hawk Tuah Girl," is an American internet celebrity whose viral fame was converted into a cryptocurrency venture, the memecoin [[hawk-memecoin|$HAWK]], which collapsed shortly after its December 2024 launch and generated litigation from investors.[1]

## Viral fame and the HAWK token

Welch became a viral internet personality in 2024, and the memecoin HAWK was launched in association with her persona.[1] The token gained popularity chiefly through that association rather than any technical utility.[1] Its price crashed within hours of launch, wiping out many first-time cryptocurrency investors and prompting insider-trading allegations.[1]

## Investor lawsuit

In December 2024, twelve plaintiffs who collectively lost more than $151,000 filed suit in a District Court of New York alleging that HAWK operated as an unregistered security in violation of securities laws and seeking a jury trial.[1] The complaint argued that a common enterprise existed because investor funds were pooled and the token's success was tied to the collective efforts of Welch and her project team, and that marketing around the token gave purchasers a reasonable expectation of profits.[1] "Despite these clear indications of its status as a security, the $HAWK Token was not registered by Defendants," the complaint asserted.[1] Plaintiffs further alleged that a wallet controlled by the Tuah Foundation, an entity associated with the project, collected $3 million in token transaction fees.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Ashe in America wrote that Welch and her developers "may have just prematurely ruined the fun for everyone" in the largely unregulated memecoin space.[1] She described the recurring shitcoin lifecycle — presale shilling by large accounts, hype-filled AMA sessions that dodge questions about utility, and a developer rug pull roughly seven to ten days after launch — and predicted that authorities would use this high-profile collapse to justify new legislation and regulation of crypto markets, noting that the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|SEC]] had already been "flirting with shitcoins for a while."[1]
