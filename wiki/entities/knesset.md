# Knesset

The Knesset is the national legislature of [[israel|Israel]], described in Badlands Media's coverage as the "Israeli Congress."[1] In the Badlands Brief of March 13, 2026, the Knesset appears as a central actor in the political controversy surrounding allegations of sexual abuse of a Palestinian detainee held at the Sde Teiman military detention facility during the Gaza war.[1]

## Role in the Sde Teiman detainee abuse controversy

According to the Brief, when the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] arrested four soldiers accused of assaulting a Palestinian prisoner at [[sde-teiman|Sde Teiman]], Israeli settlers led by members of the Knesset — together with National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] — stormed the military base where the accused were being held and attempted to free them, with IDF personnel physically fighting back against demonstrators who tore down barriers.[1]

The Knesset subsequently held a public debate, broadcast online, on whether it should be lawful for men to sodomize male prisoners with objects.[1] During that debate, MK [[hanoch-milwidsky|Hanoch Milwidsky]] of the [[likud|Likud]] party argued, "Shut up! If he is a Nukbha, then everything is legitimate to do!"[1] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry characterized the episode as evidence that the underlying question in Israeli politics was not whether the abuse occurred — which he described as widely accepted — but whether it should be permitted.[1]

## Assessment in Badlands coverage

Badlands writers treated the Knesset's willingness to hold such a debate, and the participation of sitting members in demonstrations supporting the accused soldiers, as an indicator of what they termed cultural rot within the Israeli political establishment, and contrasted it with simultaneous public discourse in the West about the "rise of antisemitism."[1] The legislature's political calculus also frames Badlands' analysis of Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s position ahead of Israel's October elections, in which his corruption trial and the course of the war with [[iran|Iran]] are treated as determinative.[1]

## Kroizer remarks controversy (2026)

A video circulated online in 2026 of Knesset member [[yitzhak-kroizer|Yitzhak Kroizer]] speaking in the chamber, in which he reportedly stated that there are no innocent people in Palestine and that killing all the women and children there would be a fair price to pay to ensure [[israel|Israel]]'s security.[2] Badlands commentary cited the remarks, alongside [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]]'s publicizing of handcuffed [[global-sumud-flotilla|Global Sumud Flotilla]] detainees, as illustrating an escalating pattern of rhetoric among Israeli officials.[2]

## Bennett's constitutional platform (June 2026)

In June 2026 Knesset member and prime-ministerial candidate [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] called for the establishment of an Israeli constitution "in the spirit of the [US] Declaration of Independence," setting out his vision for governance should he be elected in October.[3] Bennett said his platform would address the cost of living, education, crime, housing costs, Israel's international standing, and security, and that his first order of business would be a state commission of inquiry into the October 7, 2023 attacks and the failures that permitted them.[3]

Bennett further proposed cutting benefits to ultra-Orthodox draft evaders, prioritizing artificial-intelligence development, and attacking high prices by breaking up monopolies, cutting regulation, and opening the market to imports — a program Badlands commentary read as a repudiation of the socialist elements embedded in Israel's founding political economy.[3] Badlands also noted that Bennett and his team had expressed an intent to restart the war with [[iran|Iran]], positioning the Knesset's October election as a determinant of whether the [[us-iran-memorandum-of-understanding|US–Iran memorandum of understanding]] would hold.[3]

## Haredi conscription exemption (2026)

In mid-2026 Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] sided with the Ultra-Orthodox faction in the Knesset to codify into law the long-practiced exemption of Haredi yeshiva students from military conscription.[4] As described in the Badlands Brief, the [[haredi-conscription-exemption-law|resulting statute]] bars the government from arresting draft-dodgers, meaning that more than 80,000 able-bodied Haredi men would not report for service — a measure critics linked to the deepening manpower crisis in the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]].[4]

## Trump's address (October 13, 2025)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] addressed the Knesset on Monday, October 13, 2025, receiving what coverage described as a rapturous reception as the final twenty living hostages were released from [[gaza|Gaza]] under the U.S.-brokered [[gaza-ceasefire|ceasefire]].[5] Trump declared "the historic dawn of the new Middle East" and told lawmakers that [[israel|Israel]] had achieved all it could "by force of arms" and must now work toward peace: "You've won. I mean, you've won. Now it is time to translate these victories against terrorists on the battlefield into the ultimate prize of peace and prosperity for the entire Middle East."[5]

During the same speech Trump called for Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] to be pardoned in his ongoing corruption trial, later telling reporters the appeal was unplanned and prompted by the strength of the applause in the chamber.[5] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the address as an ultimatum delivered to the Knesset — an instruction to stop warmongering and pursue peace, with the implicit threat that Trump could walk away if Israel's political class remained defiant.[5]

## Ritual abuse hearings (June 2025)

In June 2025 a special joint Knesset session heard testimony from survivors, mainly from the ultra-Orthodox and religious Zionist communities, describing years of childhood abuse suffered during "sadistic sexual rituals" performed in religious ceremonies in Israel.[6] Several MKs appeared visibly shaken; Likud MK Eli Dalal called the accounts horrifying and demanded action from police, government ministries and the justice system, while MK Naama Lazimi of the Democrats party, chair of the Youth Affairs Committee, said she "couldn't breathe" on learning that such a network existed and that nothing was being done to stop it.[6] See [[knesset-ritual-abuse-testimony-2025|the 2025 Knesset ritual abuse testimony]].[6] Separately, opposition MK and former defense minister [[avigdor-lieberman|Avigdor Lieberman]] used a radio interview that same week to reveal that Israel was arming an ISIS-aligned gang in Gaza at the prime minister's direction.[6]

## Coalition mathematics in May 2025

Badlands analysis of the Israeli coalition in May 2025 laid out the seat arithmetic sustaining [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s government: [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]]'s [[religious-zionism-party|Religious Zionist Party]] holds 14 seats, and its withdrawal would drop the coalition's 68-seat majority below the 60-seat threshold required to govern.[7] [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]]'s Jewish Power Party holds six seats, so its temporary January resignation still left a 62-seat majority.[7]

## Non-binding West Bank annexation motion (July 2025)

On July 24, 2025, Knesset lawmakers voted 71–13 in favor of a non-binding motion for the agenda supporting the annexation of the West Bank.[8] Speaker [[amir-ohana|Amir Ohana]] declared following the vote that "the land of Israel belongs to the people of Israel" and that "Jews cannot be the occupiers" of their own ancestral homeland, asserting that in 1967 "the occupation did not begin; it ended, and our homeland was returned to its rightful owners."[8] See [[west-bank-annexation]].

The same period saw the Knesset consumed by the ultra-Orthodox conscription dispute, in which [[united-torah-judaism|United Torah Judaism]] chairman [[yitzhak-goldknopf|Yitzhak Goldknopf]] warned that the Haredi community would leave Israel en masse if yeshiva students who refuse military service continued to be arrested.[8]

## Allegations of criminal influence

A July 2024 Badlands News Brief revisited a 2017 *[[times-of-israel|Times of Israel]]* article headlined "Is Israel becoming a mafia state?", which it said reported that 75 percent of Knesset members were involved in questionable dealings.[9] The Badlands account attributed the underlying phenomenon to the migration of a Jewish-Ukrainian mafia to Israel in 1989, and cited [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]]' publication of U.S. Embassy cables expressing grave concern over the election of Inbal Gavrieli — niece of a leading crime boss — to the Knesset for [[likud|Likud]].[9]

## 1996 elections

The 1996 Knesset elections featured [[natan-sharansky|Natan Sharansky]]'s [[yisrael-baaliyah|Yisrael BaAliyah]] party, representing Russian-speaking "Refusenik" immigrants, whose polling and strategy were designed by the young [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]]; the party later merged into Likud.[9]

## Debate over abuse of Palestinian detainees (2024)

In 2024 the Knesset held a publicly televised session to debate the case of [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] soldiers arrested by military police over the alleged gang rape of a male Palestinian prisoner, opening the session with CCTV footage from the base in question.[10] Badlands Media reported that during the debate one member asked, "To insert a stick in a person's rectum, is that legitimate?" and another lawmaker replied, "Shut up! YES, if he is Nukhba (Hamas), everything is legitimate to do."[10] The outlet cited the exchange as evidence of what it described as a broader moral crisis in Israeli political culture.[10]

## Vote to ban UNRWA (October 2024)

On Monday, October 28, 2024, the Knesset voted to ban the operations of [[unrwa|UNRWA]], the United Nations agency for Palestinian refugees and the main humanitarian aid agency operating in [[gaza|Gaza]].[11] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] issued a statement on the legislation reiterating accusations that UNRWA employees were involved in terrorist activities in the region, and his office said that UNRWA personnel "must be held accountable."[11]

A U.N. Office of Internal Oversight Services investigation closed in August 2024 had debunked some of the allegations and found others insufficiently evidenced, though the agency said nine employees may have been involved in the October 7 attack if the evidence were authenticated, and Commissioner-General [[philippe-lazzarini|Philippe Lazzarini]] said their contracts would be terminated.[11] The legislation carried a ninety-day delay before taking effect, during which Netanyahu's office said Israel stood ready to work with international partners to keep facilitating humanitarian assistance to civilians in Gaza in a manner that did not threaten Israeli security.[11]

## Preliminary West Bank sovereignty vote (October 2025)

On Wednesday, October 22, 2025, the Knesset passed in a preliminary reading, and against the prime minister's wishes, a bill that would apply Israeli sovereignty to all [[west-bank|West Bank]] settlements.[12] The vote drew immediate criticism from Washington: Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] said as he departed [[israel|Israel]] the next day that it had offended him and was "very stupid," while President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] warned in remarks published the same day that Israel would lose "all support" from the United States over [[west-bank-annexation|annexation]].[12] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the timing of the vote, as Vance was flying to Israel, a flagrant act of defiance and disrespect toward Trump.[12]

## Judicial appointments law (March 2025)

On Thursday, March 27, 2025, the Knesset approved a key component of Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s plan to overhaul the judiciary, passing by 67 votes in the 120-seat chamber a law giving politicians more power over the appointment of judges.[13] The vote came days after the government initiated a process to dismiss Attorney General [[gali-baharav-miara|Gali Baharav-Miara]] and discharged [[ronen-bar|Ronen Bar]], head of the internal security agency [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]] — both critics of Netanyahu.[13] Critics called the law a "catastrophe" and a "nail in the coffin of Israeli democracy," and the opposition swiftly petitioned the Supreme Court against it; the broader judicial reform package had sparked one of the largest protest movements in Israel's history in 2023 before public attention was overtaken by the war in Gaza.[13]

Badlands analysis noted that the enacted version was ostensibly less extreme than originally proposed — it did not eliminate the opposition party from the selection committee entirely — but still achieved Netanyahu's goal of structuring the nine-member committee to expand the ruling coalition's voting power.[13] The commentary explained that Israel uses a parliamentary system in which citizens vote for party lists and a prime minister must assemble a coalition majority in the Knesset.[13]

Netanyahu held a 63-seat coalition until [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]]'s [[otzma-yehudit|Jewish Power]] party resigned from the government in protest on January 19, following [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]]'s successfully negotiated ceasefire with [[hamas|Hamas]]; when the ceasefire was broken, Ben-Gvir returned, restoring a 68-seat majority.[13] The analysis stressed that since 2022 a large share of that majority consisted of fringe radicals previously unwilling to join or barred from serving in the Knesset over championed views of Jewish racial supremacy — a ban created by a 1994 law that Netanyahu reversed in 2022 to gain their support after losing political moderates amid scandals.[13]

Those legislators were now positioned to join the nine-member selection committee appointing judges to the Israeli Supreme Court, potentially seating jurists sympathetic to figures such as [[meir-kahane|Meir Kahane]] and [[benzi-gopstein|Benzi Gopstein]].[13] Gopstein was prosecuted for incitements made in 2015, defended by Ben-Gvir; Kahane was murdered in New York City in 1990.[13] The commentary presented this context to argue that warnings from [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] and retired Supreme Court chiefs that Netanyahu's polarization was sending Israel careening toward civil war were not necessarily hyperbole.[13] The opposition coalition walked out and refused to cast votes on the bill.[13]
