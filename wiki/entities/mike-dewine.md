# Mike DeWine

Mike DeWine is the Republican Governor of [[ohio|Ohio]]. At the end of December 2023 he vetoed [[ohio-house-bill-68|House Bill 68]], legislation that would have prohibited doctors from prescribing puberty blockers and cross-sex hormones to minors and barred boys from competing in girls' sports.[1]

## Veto of House Bill 68

The veto came just hours before the statutory deadline and provoked a heated intra-Republican debate.[1] DeWine defended the decision by arguing that "were Ohio to pass HB68, it would be saying that the state knows better, what is medically best for their child, than the two people who love that child the most: their parents."[1]

## Hospital donations

Criticism of the veto centered on donations DeWine had received from organizations that support transgender medical care. A review of contributions from 2018 to 2023 found the governor received a total of $40,300 from the Ohio Children's Hospital Association (OCHA), Cincinnati Children's, Nationwide Children's Hospital, and ProMedica Children's Hospital.[1] The OCHA gave $10,000 to the Mike DeWine and Jon Husted Transition Fund on December 28, 2018, and a further $10,000 on December 7, 2022; under Ohio's campaign finance handbook, such a transition fund allows candidates to spend donations on "transition activities and inaugural celebrations."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] summarized the episode as one in which "the people of Ohio and their legislature agree that they don't want to sterilize and mutilate children, but the people who want to sterilize and mutilate children paid the Governor tens of thousands of dollars," after which he vetoed the legislature's agreement and "gaslit all the people of Ohio about why he did it."[1]

She contrasted the parental-rights rationale with Ohio's conduct during COVID-19, noting that the state had mask mandates and vaccine mandates and that parents "were stripped of their rights" during that period, when medical freedoms did not exist.[1] The resulting inconsistency, she argued, is that parents are not permitted to make mask decisions for their children "because science," yet "can totally consent to sterilize and mutilate their kids, according to the chief executive of the state of Ohio."[1] She characterized the governing posture as "exploitation wrapped in manufactured empathy," treating the rights and protection of children "as an inventory management or supply chain optimization issue — not, rightfully, as a matter of criminal negligence and systemic abuse."[1]

## Ohio Senate vacancy (January 2025)

Following [[jd-vance|JD Vance]]'s resignation from the Senate to become vice president, Ohio law placed the appointment of his replacement with Governor DeWine, who would name a successor to serve for the next two years before a special election on November 3, 2026 determines who holds the seat until the term expires in 2029.[2] In January 2025 [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] emerged as a leading contender and entered advanced discussions with GOP leaders, with President-elect Donald Trump personally appealing to him to accept the appointment if DeWine offered it.[2]
