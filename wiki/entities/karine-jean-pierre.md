# Karine Jean-Pierre

Karine Jean-Pierre served as White House Press Secretary in the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration and was the administration's principal public voice in response to the [[fitch-ratings|Fitch]] downgrade of the United States credit rating on August 1, 2023.[1]

In a statement issued after the downgrade, Jean-Pierre said that "it defies reality to downgrade the United States at a moment when President Biden has delivered the strongest recovery of any major economy in the world," and went on to blame Republicans for the state of the economy, saying that "extremism by Republican officials—from cheerleading default, to undermining governance and democracy, to seeking to extend deficit-busting tax giveaways for the wealthy and corporations—is a continued threat to our economy."[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the [[white-house|White House]] response as gasping in horror and predictable in its partisan attribution.[1]

## Georgia foreign agents law

After the Republic of Georgia's parliament adopted a [[georgia-foreign-agents-law|"foreign agents" law]] on May 14, 2024, Jean-Pierre told reporters that the administration was "deeply troubled by Georgia's Kremlin-style 'foreign agents' legislation," adding that it "runs counter to democratic values and would move Georgia further away from the values of the European Union and, let's not forget, also NATO."[2] The remarks came as [[white-house|White House]] criticism failed to deter a parliament that passed the bill 84–30, above the 76 votes needed to override the promised veto by President [[salome-zourabichvili|Salome Zourabichvili]].[2]

## White House cocaine incident (2023)

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] accused Jean-Pierre of lying to the press room about President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s whereabouts during the [[white-house-cocaine-incident|White House cocaine investigation]], calling it an easily disputed claim.[3] Jean-Pierre also described as "irresponsible" the question of whether the cocaine found in a secure area of the White House belonged to a Biden, conduct Ashe argued went beyond irresponsibility to the impeachable.[3]

## Remarks on Venezuela's 2024 election

At a press briefing aboard Air Force One on March 26, 2024, Jean-Pierre criticized the government of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] for blocking the registration of the Venezuelan opposition's presidential candidate.[4] She said the administration was "deeply concerned by the National Electoral Council's decision to prevent the registration of … the democratic opposition party's candidate, Dr. [[corina-yoris|Corina Yoris]]," and that the United States was working with other members of the international community to ensure Venezuelans could participate in inclusive and competitive elections, urging Maduro's representatives to allow all candidates to run.[4] She added that the administration supported the right of Venezuelans and their political parties to select presidential candidates and was monitoring the situation closely, and suggested Venezuela could lose sanctions relief absent free and fair elections.[4]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the hypocrisy palpable given simultaneous legal actions against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and observed that Jean-Pierre notably did not invoke the word "democracy" — speculating that the term was being phased out because the public had come to associate it with "despotism derived from stealing elections."[4]

## Border funding ultimatum, February 2024

In February 2024 Jean-Pierre delivered the administration's warning that immigration enforcement would be curtailed unless House Republicans passed the [[bipartisan-senate-border-security-bill-2024|bipartisan Senate border security bill]], which paired border reforms with funding for Israel and Ukraine.[5] Speaking to reporters aboard Air Force One, she said that "because congressional Republicans are choosing partisan politics over our national security and refusing to pass the bipartisan national security agreement that includes significant border reforms and funding, over the coming weeks, ICE will be forced to reduce operations because of budget shortfalls."[5] She added that "we have asked Congress for additional funding and resources, and every time Congress has provided less than we asked for, or most recently, completely ignored our supplemental request."[5] Critics characterized the remarks as the [[white-house|White House]] threatening open border chaos if Republicans did not capitulate.[5]

## February 2024 briefings

At the White House press briefing on Wednesday, February 14, 2024, Jean-Pierre appeared alongside National Security Adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]], who addressed the cryptic national security threat warning issued that day by [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]].[6] In the same briefing, reporters pressed Jean-Pierre on when they would next have an opportunity to question President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]; Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that she was non-committal and argued the White House was hiding the president from the public amid the controversy over his refusal to take the [[montreal-cognitive-assessment|Montreal Cognitive Assessment]].[6]

## Defending Biden after the June 2024 debate

On Tuesday, July 2, 2024, the White House announced that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would meet with congressional leaders and Democratic governors, sit for a network television interview and hold a press conference — a blitz designed to counter growing pressure for the 81-year-old to step aside after his debate performance against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[7] As press secretary, Jean-Pierre said of the calls for Biden to bow out, "We really want to turn the page on this," and stated the president had no intention of stepping aside, characterizing the debate failings as simply "a bad night" when he had a cold.[7]

At a campaign event later that day Biden offered a different explanation, blaming jet lag from two back-to-back European trips: "I wasn't very smart. I decided to travel around the world a couple of times," adding that he "didn't listen to my staff" about travel and joking that he "fell asleep on stage."[7] Democratic leaders were increasingly signalling they did not accept the White House framing of the halting performance as a momentary lapse.[7]

## Briefing-room arrangement with Kirby (February 2024)

Jean-Pierre had frequently been joined at the podium by [[john-kirby|John Kirby]], President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s top national security spokesperson, to brief on national security issues — particularly after the Hamas attacks on Israel of October 7 and Israel's subsequent operations in Gaza.[8] When Kirby was promoted in February 2024 to White House national security communications adviser and elevated to assistant to the president, officials said his appearances alongside Jean-Pierre in the briefing room would continue whenever national security issues were the dominant themes of the day.[8]


## Defense of the Hunter Biden pardon

Jean-Pierre held her first White House briefing after President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] pardoned his son [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], and was met with questions about the controversial clemency after having said on multiple occasions that Biden would not pardon his son.[9] She defended the decision on the grounds that "circumstances have changed, they have," blaming Republicans who she said were "not going to let up" as Hunter Biden's sentencing in his gun case approached, and saying the president felt Hunter and his family had been through enough.[9] "He wrestled with these circumstances, with these changing circumstances, ultimately, and the combination of that the president changing his mind and issuing this pardon," she added.[9]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] focused on the communications tradecraft of the episode, arguing that describing a presidential statement as being "in his own voice" is internal comms jargon for a tightly controlled channel that a professional should never invoke publicly, since doing so undermines the credibility of the communications program.[9] The commentary speculated that Jean-Pierre had argued internally that Biden should address the public "in his own voice" to project strength and authenticity, while gaining the ability to claim the statement stood on its own and dodge questions, and asked pointedly who was actually writing in the president's voice.[9]

## Switch to independent and the 2025 book

In June 2025 Jean-Pierre announced that she had left the Democratic Party to become an independent, urging Americans to "embrace life as independents" — a striking turn for a figure who had worked for two Democratic presidents and held one of the most public-facing roles in the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration.[10] She made the case in a forthcoming book, *Independent: A Look Inside a Broken White House, Outside the Party Lines*, scheduled for release in October by Hachette Book Group's Legacy Lit imprint and promoted as a "groundbreaking, revelatory assessment of America's broken two-party system."[10]

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the premise, writing that there is "no universe" in which Jean-Pierre was swindled by the [[white-house|White House]] into believing what she told the American people from her privileged position, and no way she missed that an unelected council was running the country.[10] As Press Secretary, Ashe argued, Jean-Pierre was Biden's voice to the press and the public, spun every scandal and strategised with the cabinet on every narrative — citing occasions when [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] and [[john-kirby|John Kirby]] joined her in the briefing room to field questions about the president's condition and gaffes.[10] She concluded that the cabinet must have known the president was not actually running the country, that the book's "revelatory" discovery of a [[uniparty|uniparty]] was "a poorly executed Hail Mary" to persuade the public and the administration to move on, and asked whether the country can move on without accountability.[10]
