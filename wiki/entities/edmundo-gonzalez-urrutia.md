# Edmundo González Urrutia

Edmundo González Urrutia was the principal opposition candidate in Venezuela's July 2024 presidential election, campaigning in partnership with the conservative activist [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]].[1]

## 2024 election dispute

After the government-controlled electoral authority proclaimed [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] the winner on the Monday morning following Sunday's vote, González maintained that he had won and that the election had been stolen.[1] On that Monday he and Machado said they could prove their victory, having obtained 73.2% of the voting tallies from polling stations — the official records showing the number of votes cast at each station at closing.[1] Maduro described his own claimed victory as "irreversible" despite widespread international doubt.[1]

The dispute triggered mass protests, the detention of Voluntad Popular national coordinator [[freddy-superlano|Freddy Superlano]] by Maduro's security services, and a refusal by at least nine Latin American countries to recognise Maduro's third term.[1] The [[white-house|White House]] said it was reviewing available data and that there were "signs" the official results did not reflect the will of the people, while Venezuela's top military leadership in Caracas declared in a televised address that it remained loyal to Maduro.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writers cautioned that the enthusiastic international backing for the Venezuelan opposition bore "all the hallmarks of a color revolution," arguing that it could simultaneously be true that publicising large-scale election fraud is useful narrative seeding and that globalist interests had been moving against Venezuela for years.[1] The commentary singled out The Guardian for describing Maduro as an "authoritarian regime leader" and "Venezuelan strongman" while lionising his opposition — the same media framing once applied to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over the 2020 U.S. election.[1]
