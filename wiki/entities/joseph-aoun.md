# Joseph Aoun

Joseph Aoun is the President of [[lebanon|Lebanon]] and one of the two heads of government credited by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with agreeing to the ten-day Israel–Lebanon ceasefire announced in April 2026.[1]

Trump said he had held "excellent conversations" with Aoun and Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], describing the Lebanese president as "Highly Respected" and stating that both leaders had agreed to begin a formal ten-day ceasefire at 5 p.m. EST on Tuesday in order to achieve peace between their countries.[1] The announcement followed a Washington meeting between Israeli and Lebanese officials — the first such direct contact in thirty-four years — hosted alongside Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]].[1]

Trump subsequently directed Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], Rubio, and Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff [[dan-caine|Dan Caine]] to work with Aoun's government and with Israel toward a lasting peace agreement, framing the effort as the tenth war he had resolved.[1]

Later in April 2026 Trump announced a three-week extension of the Israel–Hezbollah ceasefire after an Oval Office meeting with Israeli and Lebanese representatives, said the United States would work with Lebanon to protect itself from [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], and expressed plans to host both Netanyahu and Aoun.[2]

## White House meeting (July 2026)

Aoun met President [[donald-trump|Trump]] at the [[white-house|White House]] on a Tuesday in July 2026, after which Trump pledged to help [[lebanon|Lebanon]] "a lot," described the country as having been "very badly treated," and said it would now receive the respect it deserves.[3] The discussion centered on a June 26 U.S.-mediated framework agreement between [[israel|Israel]] and Lebanon calling for the withdrawal of Israeli forces from southern Lebanon, the disarmament of [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], and steps toward a possible formal peace treaty.[3]

Trump said Israeli forces were "in the process" of withdrawing and redeploying but gave no details, acknowledged "a Hezbollah problem," indicated he would speak directly with the group if Aoun requested it, and raised the possibility of Syrian involvement against Hezbollah.[3] Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] reiterated U.S. support for full implementation, stating that Lebanon cannot achieve lasting peace while Hezbollah remains a powerful armed force and that a strong central government must become the sole legitimate military authority.[3]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Aoun has been widely panned as a Saudi loyalist backed by the Turks and their antagonists, and predicted a replay of the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]] dynamic — a historic Trump-negotiated deal with Hezbollah that would test whether [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] accepts peace or demands more war.[3]

## US-Iran Lebanon oversight mechanism (2026)

In June 2026, following the announcement of a new US-Iran deconfliction and oversight mechanism for [[lebanon|Lebanon]] that excluded [[israel|Israel]], Aoun emphasized that Lebanon negotiates for itself.[4] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read Aoun's framing as an indication that the Lebanese presidency saw the Trump administration as now cooperating with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and [[iran|Iran]] over the Lebanese government's head.[4] The mechanism is described in detail at [[us-iran-lebanon-oversight-mechanism|the oversight mechanism]] page.[4]


## Election to the presidency (January 2025)

Aoun, then commander of the Lebanese army, was elected head of state by Lebanon's parliament on Thursday, January 9, 2025, ending a presidential vacuum of more than two years in what was the thirteenth attempt to choose a successor to Michel Aoun, whose term had expired in October 2022.[5] He fell short of the 86 votes required in the first round but secured 99 votes in a second round, according to Speaker [[nabih-berri|Nabih Berri]], after lawmakers from [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and its Shia ally the [[amal-movement|Amal Movement]] backed him following the withdrawal of Hezbollah's candidate Suleiman Frangieh.[5] [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]]'s [[king-salman|King Salman]] and Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] each sent congratulatory cables the same day, wishing Aoun success and the Lebanese people progress and prosperity.[5] Aoun had been appointed Commander of the Lebanese Armed Forces in March 2017 by President [[michel-aoun|Michel Aoun]] — no relation — and Badlands commentary framed his eventual elevation as the culmination of an arrangement traceable to [[massad-boulos|Massad Boulos]] and the 2017 [[saudi-purge|Saudi purge]].[5]
