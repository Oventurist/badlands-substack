# Marco Rubio

Marco Rubio is the United States Secretary of State in the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration, and in that capacity has served as the senior American diplomat in the negotiations that produced a ten-day ceasefire between [[israel|Israel]] and [[lebanon|Lebanon]] in April 2026.[1]

## Role in the Israel–Lebanon ceasefire

Rubio hosted the Washington, D.C. meeting at which Israeli and Lebanese officials met face to face for the first time in thirty-four years, a session Trump described as the precursor to the ceasefire announcement.[1] In his Truth Social statement announcing the agreement, Trump singled out "our Great Secretary of State, Marco Rubio" as the American official present for that encounter.[1]

Following the announcement, Trump said he had directed Rubio, Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], and Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff [[dan-caine|Dan "Razin" Caine]] to work with both governments toward a lasting peace agreement rather than a temporary truce.[1] Reporting cited in the same coverage indicated that Washington was simultaneously pursuing a further round of talks with [[iran|Iran]], to be held in Pakistan, with officials described as optimistic about the prospects for a deal.[1]

Rubio's assignment placed the State Department at the center of an initiative Trump framed as the tenth war he had resolved, a framing that Badlands commentators read as evidence that the president was deliberately decoupling the United States from the Israeli campaign against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[1]

## Munich Security Conference address (2026)

At the February 2026 [[munich-security-conference|Munich Security Conference]], Rubio outlined the administration's foreign-policy approach, emphasizing national sovereignty, border security and greater [[nato|NATO]] burden-sharing.[2] Tracing the conference's origins to a 1963 Europe divided by the Berlin Wall and shadowed by the Cuban Missile Crisis, he argued that the euphoria following the Cold War victory produced "a dangerous delusion: that we had entered… 'the end of history'; that every nation would now be a liberal democracy; that the ties formed by trade and by commerce alone would now replace nationhood; that the rules-based global order… would now replace the national interest."[2]

Rubio urged European allies to increase defense spending, criticized mass migration policies, and questioned the effectiveness of some post-World War II international institutions while affirming continued US commitment to the transatlantic alliance.[2] European reaction was mixed, and commentators described the address as a formal articulation of a "MAGA" foreign-policy doctrine — realist to supporters, a source of transatlantic strain to critics.[2] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called it "a pretty good speech by Little Marco," reading it as terms of surrender delivered to Europe's old guard.[2]

## Netanyahu's February 2026 visit

Ahead of Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s February 2026 meeting with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Netanyahu arranged to meet Rubio on the morning before seeing the president.[3] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the scheduling as Netanyahu wanting to gameplan how to handle Trump, who had just declared "I am against annexation" in response to the Israeli Security Cabinet's [[west-bank-annexation|West Bank measures]].[3]

## Iran strikes rationale (2026)

As Secretary of State during the March 2026 [[iran-war|U.S.–Iran war]], Rubio stated that the United States had preempted attacks on American forces, citing known Israeli plans and Iran's bad-faith conduct in nuclear negotiations.[4] His justification accompanied warnings about Iran's advancing nuclear and missile capabilities that preceded the opening strikes.[4]

## Rationale for the Iran strikes (2026)

When the United States joined [[israel|Israel]] in military strikes against [[iran|Iran]] in late February 2026, Rubio said the United States acted because it expected Iran would retaliate against Israel's planned action and potentially target US forces.[5] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s own explanation — that he ordered US forces to join because he believed Iran was preparing to strike first — followed Rubio's remarks.[5]

## Campaign against the ICC (July 2026)

On July 13, 2026, Rubio announced a campaign — described by Badlands as "whole-of-government" — aimed at dismantling the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]], arguing that the tribunal threatens U.S. sovereignty and improperly targets American military personnel and officials.[6] The effort includes encouraging other countries to distance themselves from the court.[6] The announcement coincided with renewed debate over the ICC's 2024 arrest warrant for Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and New York Mayor [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]]'s declaration that he would seek Netanyahu's arrest.[6] Rubio's [[state-department|State Department]] subsequently welcomed [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s withdrawal from the court and called on other countries to follow suit.[6]

## Senate testimony on Venezuela (January 2026)

Rubio testified before the Senate Foreign Relations Committee on January 28, 2026 regarding US policy toward [[venezuela|Venezuela]], telling the committee that Venezuela's new leaders were moving toward closer ties with Washington and that there was no immediate need for further US military action.[7] Praising the hydrocarbons overhaul signed by Acting President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodriguez]], he said: "The authorities there deserve some credit. They have passed a new hydrocarbon law that basically eradicates many of the Chavez era restrictions on private investment in the oil industry. It probably doesn't go far enough to attract sufficient investment, but it's a big step from where they were three weeks ago. So that's a major change."[7] His testimony preceded the announcement that commercial airspace over Venezuela would reopen and the easing of certain oil-sector sanctions following the capture of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[7]

## Cuba negotiations (March 2026)

In March 2026, Cuban President [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Díaz-Canel]] publicly confirmed that Cuba was in open negotiations with the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]], appearing alongside [[raulito-castro|Raulito Castro]], a grandson of [[raul-castro|Raúl Castro]].[8] Badlands commentary said the announcement all but confirmed Axios reporting by Marc Caputo that Rubio and Castro had been conducting secret negotiations over Castro potentially assuming leadership of [[cuba|Cuba]].[8]

## Lebanon security framework (June 2026)

In June 2026, as the United States backed a new [[israel-lebanon-ceasefire|Israel-Lebanon ceasefire framework]] requiring [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] to withdraw from areas south of the [[litani-river|Litani River]], Rubio said the goal is a security arrangement in which armed non-state actors no longer operate in the designated zones, with security responsibility passing to the [[lebanese-armed-forces|Lebanese Armed Forces]].[9]

## Lebanon framework agreement (2026)

Following President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s July 2026 [[white-house|White House]] meeting with Lebanese President [[joseph-aoun|Joseph Aoun]], Rubio reiterated U.S. support for full implementation of the June 26 framework agreement between [[israel|Israel]] and [[lebanon|Lebanon]].[10] He stated that Lebanon cannot achieve lasting peace while [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] remains a powerful armed force, and that a strong central government must become the sole legitimate military authority in the country.[10]

## AI policy alignment (February 2026)

Politico reported in February 2026 that Rubio and Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] had publicly aligned with [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s AI policy, while the outlet leaned on anonymous sources to describe internal concerns over national security and political backlash.[11] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that the writers quoted Trump's critics at length as authentic populist voices but conceded they did not know where Rubio and Vance would ultimately land — evidence, in his reading, that the [[media-protectorate|Media Protectorate]] is uncertain about the administration's real power centers.[11]

## Afghanistan wrongful-detention designation (2026)

On March 9, 2026, Rubio announced that the United States had designated [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] a "State Sponsor of Wrongful Detention," citing the [[taliban|Taliban]]'s detention of US nationals and other foreign citizens on grounds Washington deemed unjustified or politically motivated.[12] The [[state-department|State Department]] classification identifies governments that detain foreign nationals for diplomatic leverage and permits Washington to escalate pressure and coordinate efforts to free detainees.[12]

## Campaign against the ICC (2026)

As Secretary of State, Rubio said in July 2026 that the United States was launching a campaign to dismantle the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]]'s threat to national sovereignty.[13] The statement accompanied an accelerating [[icc-withdrawal-wave|wave of withdrawals]] from the court, with [[chad|Chad]] and [[venezuela|Venezuela]] both announcing exits in late July 2026, and followed the February 2025 executive order signed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] imposing sanctions on the court over its investigations of [[israel|Israel]].[13] The [[state-department|State Department]]'s Bureau of African Affairs publicly welcomed Chad's move and urged more of the court's 125 member states to withdraw.[13]

## Justification of the March 2026 Iran strikes

Speaking in March 2026, Marco Rubio said the United States decided to launch military strikes against [[iran|Iran]] after determining that [[israel|Israel]] was planning its own attack on Iranian targets that would trigger Iranian retaliation against American forces.[14] "We knew that there was going to be an Israeli action, we knew that that would precipitate an attack against American forces, and we knew that if we didn't preemptively go after them before they launched those attacks, we would suffer higher casualties," Rubio said.[14]

He made the comments before a closed-door briefing to [[congress|Congress]] on the joint US–Israeli operation.[14] House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] and other Republican lawmakers likewise said Israel was prepared to attack Iran "with or without American support," and that US involvement reflected planning to protect American forces from anticipated retaliation.[14] Rubio also described US military objectives as including the degradation of Iran's ballistic missile and naval capabilities, while stating that the United States was not currently postured for ground forces in Iran.[14]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the remarks as an admission — "Marco just said the quiet part out loud" — arguing that the statement effectively settled the debate over Israeli influence on US policy, while allowing that the maneuver might be a deliberate one by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and allied factions.[14]

## Shield of the Americas statement on Colombia

As Secretary of State, Rubio publicly shared the July 10, 2026 joint statement issued by the United States and twelve regional partners through the [[shield-of-the-americas|Shield of the Americas]] coalition, which voiced alarm at [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]]'s attempts to challenge [[colombia|Colombia]]'s certified June 21 election result.[15] The statement was released through the [[state-department|State Department]] Office of the Spokesperson.[15] A separate Rubio comment from June 23 was cited in Badlands commentary alongside President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s announcement that the United States would become the [[guardian-of-the-hormuz-strait|Guardian of the Hormuz Strait]].[15]

## Sovereign nations framework and Cuba talks (2026)

The White House summary of Rubio's February 2026 Munich speech described President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s foreign policy as one that "brings sovereign nations together in common purpose" while rejecting "outdated globalist structures" — language the administration deployed in place of traditional [[nato|NATO]] alliance framing.[16]

Rubio also led the American side of negotiations with [[cuba|Cuba]] following a US oil blockade imposed in early 2026, talks confirmed in mid-March by Cuban President [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Díaz-Canel]] in which Havana sought sanctions relief including a waiver of Title III of the [[helms-burton-act|Helms-Burton Act]] and removal from the state sponsors of terrorism list.[16] Cuba's senior diplomat in Washington, [[lianys-torres-rivera|Lianys Torres Rivera]], publicly invited expanded economic cooperation as part of that process.[16]

## Global counter-terrorism initiative (July 2026)
As Secretary of State, Rubio convened officials from more than 65 countries in Washington in July 2026 to launch an international initiative against transnational far-left political terrorism, part of the administration's [[global-crackdown-on-far-left-political-terrorism|global crackdown on far-left political terrorism]].[17] The initiative envisaged expanded international cooperation, terrorist designations, sanctions and visa restrictions against networks the administration alleged had become a growing global security threat.[17] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] urged readers to read the signal from Rubio, [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] and [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]] as targeting the foundational bedrock of what he called an insurgency, rather than merely far-left violence.[17]

## "New era in geopolitics" remarks (February 2026)

Speaking to reporters in February 2026, Rubio said that "the world is changing very fast right in front of us. The old world is gone ... We live in a new era in geopolitics, and it's going to require all of us to sort of re-examine what that looks like and what our role is going to be."[18] The [[state-department|Department of State]] amplified the remarks by posting video of them on X.[18]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said the statement was so aligned with Badlands analysis that "it almost feels as if we wrote that statement for Rubio," calling it overdue as a formal admission and reflecting that he may have underestimated how profound the impact on the status quo would be.[18] He described Americans as standing "at the threshold of cataclysmic transformation," conditioned to fear that any change raising the station of a foreign ally or competitor would end the American Dream — a dream he argued had already died for large swaths of the country.[18]

## Venezuela oil normalization (February 2026)

In a February 2026 Truth Social post on [[venezuela|Venezuela]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said relations had become "extraordinary," that Washington was "dealing very well with President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodriguez]], and her Representatives," that oil was starting to flow, and that "Marco Rubio, and all of our Representatives, are doing a fantastic job."[18] Trump used the same post to stress that only persons approved by Rubio's [[state-department|State Department]] were authorized to represent the United States, disavowing businessman [[harry-sargeant-iii|Harry Sargeant III]].[18]

## Cuba information-withholding allegation (February 2026)

In February 2026, amid Trump's characterization of [[cuba|Cuba]] as "a failed nation" suffering blackouts and fuel shortages, media reports alleged that Rubio was withholding information from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] about the island's situation.[19] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the story "probably nonsense," but added that if true it would fit a pattern of the [[state-department|State Department]] frustrating the president's major policy decisions, noting that it is Trump's business associates such as [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] who appear to be negotiating peace deals instead.[19]

## Cayo Falcones boat shooting (February 2026)

After Cuban border guards killed four people aboard a Florida-registered speedboat in Cuban waters near Cayo Falcones, Rubio said the United States had no information beyond what Cuban authorities had released.[20] He stated that agencies including the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] and the [[us-coast-guard|Coast Guard]] were investigating whether any of the occupants were US citizens or lawful permanent residents.[20] His measured posture contrasted with that of Florida Attorney General [[james-uthmeier|James Uthmeier]], who declared the Cuban government untrustworthy and ordered state prosecutors to open an investigation.[20] See [[cayo-falcones-boat-incident|Cayo Falcones boat incident]].[20]

## Israel-Lebanon talks (2026)

As Secretary of State, Rubio led the first high-level talks between Israel and Lebanon since 1993, a trilateral working-level meeting hosted at the State Department in April 2026 with Israeli Ambassador Yechiel Leiter and Lebanese Ambassador Nada Hamadeh Moawad.[21] The agenda covered implementation of the November 2024 ceasefire understanding, steps toward formal direct negotiations, dismantling armed groups such as Hezbollah, strengthening the Lebanese state's authority, and humanitarian issues.[21] Both sides agreed to proceed with direct negotiations at a time and place to be determined, and Rubio stressed that any ceasefire must be reached directly between Israel and Lebanon with U.S. involvement, positioning Washington as facilitator.[21]

## Cuba report (July 2026)

The [[state-department|State Department]] said in July 2026 that it was preparing to release a report alleging that [[cuba|Cuba]]'s communist government has spent decades supporting radical left-wing movements in the United States and the Western Hemisphere, examining organizations including [[codepink|Code Pink]] and the [[peoples-forum|People's Forum]] and funding from [[neville-roy-singham|Neville Roy Singham]].[22] The report came as Rubio made countering transnational left-wing political influence a priority of US foreign policy, with the administration presenting the document as a way to highlight Cuba's historical role in supporting revolutionary movements throughout the Americas and to inform future policy toward the regime.[22]

## Venezuela policy and the Landau episode (July 2026)

In July 2026 Rubio was reported to have personally reiterated United States policy to exiled Venezuelan opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]], leading to the reversal of one of her planned trips, after Deputy Secretary of State [[chris-landau|Chris Landau]] allegedly signaled support for her return to Venezuela in conversations with Dutch and Panamanian officials.[23] A [[state-department|State Department]] spokesperson dismissed reports of internal conflict, saying the department remained aligned with President [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Rubio's foreign policy agenda.[23] Badlands commentary noted the irony that Rubio, one of six Republican senators who signed an August 2024 letter nominating Machado for the Nobel Peace Prize, was now ostensibly leading the effort to block her from power.[23]

## January 2026 Venezuela testimony

In late January 2026, Rubio said the Trump administration was prepared to use force to secure cooperation from [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s acting president, [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]], while signaling that the United States expected compliance through political and economic leverage before taking further action.[24] The remarks were prepared for his first congressional appearance since the January 3 US operation in Caracas that captured [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[24]

Rubio said Rodríguez had agreed to open Venezuela's energy sector to US companies, grant preferential access to production, and direct oil revenue toward purchasing American goods.[24] "We are prepared to use force to ensure maximum cooperation if other methods fail," he said in the statement submitted to the [[senate-foreign-relations-committee|Senate Foreign Relations Committee]]. "It is our hope that this will not prove necessary, but we will never shy away from our duty to the American people and our mission in this Hemisphere."[24] He was scheduled to meet [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] at the [[state-department|State Department]] following the hearing.[24]

## 2026 letter on Israel's nuclear arsenal

As Secretary of State, Rubio was the recipient of a May 2026 letter from 30 Democratic House members led by Rep. Joaquin Castro demanding that the administration disclose information about Israel's nuclear arsenal and related policies.[25] The letter criticized the United States for fighting "side by side with a country whose potential nuclear weapons program the United States government officially refuses to acknowledge" and urged an end to nuclear ambiguity in support of a coherent regional nonproliferation policy.[25]



## Suggested as a Cuban leader (January 2026)

[[donald-trump|President Trump]] reposted a message from a private user suggesting that Rubio — a Cuban-American and key architect of Trump's foreign policy in Latin America — could become the president of [[cuba|Cuba]], adding the comment "Sounds good to me!"[26] The repost sparked commentary but did not reflect an official policy proposal, and Cuban officials responded by asserting their nation's sovereignty.[26]

## Dismantling of the international courts (July 2026)

The Badlands Brief reported in July 2026 that Rubio had announced the United States was dismantling the international courts.[27] Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] tied the announcement directly to the position of former special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], who had trained at The Hague, writing that with those institutions being dismantled "Smith has nowhere to run."[27]

## June 2026 Gulf trip

In the week before the late-June 2026 escalation in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], Rubio flew to the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]] to meet officials from the UAE, [[bahrain|Bahrain]], and [[kuwait|Kuwait]].[28] Establishment outlets characterized the trip as an attempt by Rubio to "clean up" the mess created by [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] with their peace deal.[28]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] found the trip suspicious, noting that President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s three most important Arab allies — [[qatar|Qatar]], [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], and Oman — were visibly absent from the meeting, which he took as a signal that it may have been convened by the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[28] He pointed to neoconservative consternation over the peace deal and their approving coverage of Rubio's trip as further grounds for suspicion, and observed that Iran subsequently struck targets specifically in Kuwait and Bahrain.[28]

## Manila meeting with Lavrov (July 2026)

In July 2026 Rubio met Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] in Manila, where he said the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] remained prepared to play a "constructive role" in helping end the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] while cautioning that there was no quick path to a negotiated settlement.[29] He described the discussion as "good" and "frank" but declined to disclose details, saying diplomacy is best conducted privately.[29]

The outreach followed the administration's decision to withhold portions of previously planned military assistance to Ukraine pending a review of US weapons stockpiles, a pause officials framed as protecting American military readiness.[29] It coincided with Ukraine's new commander-in-chief Lt. Gen. [[mykhailo-drapatyi|Mykhailo Drapatyi]] declaring that "Russia has no right to exist," a juxtaposition Badlands commentary read as evidence that pro-war factions were working to undercut Rubio's diplomacy.[29]

## Greenland acquisition diplomacy (January 2026)

[[greenland|Greenland]] and [[denmark|Denmark]] jointly requested a high-level ministerial meeting with Rubio in January 2026 to address escalating US interest in acquiring the Arctic territory, after repeated unsuccessful attempts throughout 2025 to engage him.[30] In a closed congressional briefing Rubio clarified that recent administration rhetoric was not signaling an imminent invasion but was intended to facilitate negotiations for purchasing Greenland from Denmark.[30]

## Recognition of Moroccan sovereignty over Western Sahara (2025)

Meeting Moroccan Foreign Minister [[nasser-bourita|Nasser Bourita]] on April 8, 2025, Rubio declared that Washington recognizes Moroccan sovereignty over [[western-sahara|Western Sahara]] and supports [[morocco|Morocco]]'s "serious, credible, and realistic" autonomy plan as the only basis for a solution to the longstanding territorial dispute, according to a [[state-department|State Department]] press release.[31] The statement reaffirmed a position the United States had previously taken on the non-self-governing territory, which Morocco annexed after Spain's 1975 withdrawal and where it faces the Algerian-backed [[polisario-front|Polisario Front]].[31]

## Riyadh talks with Russia (February 2025)

Rubio led the American delegation to the February 18, 2025, US-Russia talks in Riyadh, [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], alongside national security adviser [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]] and Middle East envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]]; Russia was represented by Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] and presidential aide [[yury-ushakov|Yury Ushakov]], and neither Ukrainian nor EU diplomats attended.[32] Rubio styled the meeting as a follow-up to [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s phone call with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], telling CBS's "Face the Nation" that "the next few weeks and days will determine whether it's serious or not" and that "one phone call does not make peace. One phone call does not solve a war as complex as this one."[32]

His remarks contradicted comments by Ukraine envoy [[keith-kellogg|Keith Kellogg]], who had said Kyiv would be involved in peace talks but European nations would not; [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] asked whether the two were playing good cop, bad cop, quipping, "Did Little Marco not get the message?"[32] Rubio and Witkoff were also filmed meeting Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and Saudi Foreign Minister Faisal bin Farhan in Riyadh.[32]

## Antifascist terrorist designation (November 2025)

On Thursday, November 13, 2025, Rubio inserted the United States into a long-running European controversy by declaring the antifascist group that attacked a 2023 neo-Nazi rally in Hungary a terrorist organization.[33] Although the designation authority is aimed at foreign groups, Rubio said he was acting in accordance with President Donald Trump's [[national-security-presidential-memorandum-7|National Security Presidential Memorandum-7]], which concerns purported "domestic" terrorists.[33] The Intercept characterized the move as exposing supporters of anti-Nazi militants to potential U.S. material-support terrorism charges, and contrasted it with Hungarian Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]]'s comparative leniency toward the annual fascist rally itself.[33]

## Russia sanctions rumor (April 2025)

In late April 2025, amid rumors of an imminent Russia-Ukraine deal, Rubio dismissed reports that the United States was considering lifting sanctions on Russia as part of a potential agreement.[34] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the Secretary of State's denial in arguing that circulating rumors of a completed deal between Moscow and Kyiv were false, even as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said publicly that he believed a deal had been reached with both sides.[34]

## USAID contract cancellations (March 2025)

In March 2025 Rubio announced that the Trump administration had officially canceled 83% of US foreign aid contracts, writing on X that "the 5200 contracts that are now cancelled spent tens of billions of dollars in ways that did not serve, (and in some cases even harmed), the core national interests of the United States."[35] Of roughly 6,200 total contracts — programs appropriated by [[congress|Congress]] in the last budget approval — the remaining 1,000 or so would be administered by the [[state-department|State Department]], which had absorbed [[usaid|USAID]] the previous month.[35] Rubio thanked [[doge|DOGE]], the Department of Government Efficiency headed by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], for implementing "overdue and historic reform."[35] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] criticized the move as insufficient, arguing that no USAID contracts should have been retained.[35] See [[2025-usaid-contract-cancellations|2025 USAID contract cancellations]].[35]

## Geneva talks and the Venezuela designation (November 2025)

In late November 2025 Rubio reshaped the Trump administration's Ukraine diplomacy after a week of chaos.[36] Army Secretary [[dan-driscoll|Dan Driscoll]] had spent two days delivering a hard message to Ukraine and its European allies — accept the 28-point peace proposal by Thanksgiving, or else — before Rubio arrived in Geneva on Sunday, November 23 to join him and resume discussions with senior Ukrainian officials.[36] Rubio's arrival changed the tenor of the talks: the U.S. team backed away from a firm Thursday deadline and, in the view of European and Ukrainian participants, began taking their positions more into account, according to four people familiar with the discussions.[36] An official from a NATO country said Rubio's engagement was important and that the pace had slowed after his arrival, "and that's good."[36]

Separately, Rubio had announced earlier in November that the United States would designate Venezuela's [[cartel-de-los-soles|Cartel de los Soles]] as a foreign terrorist organization over its alleged role in importing illegal drugs into the United States; the designation was formalized on November 24, 2025.[36]

## Sanctions by Russia and China (2024)

At the time of his nomination as Secretary of State, Russia had sanctioned Rubio and had no plans to lift those sanctions until U.S. sanctions against Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergei Lavrov]] were lifted.[37] China had separately imposed sanctions on Rubio in 2019 for criticizing its handling of the Hong Kong protesters, making him the first Secretary of State to face Chinese sanctions — a fact Badlands commentary said undermines the argument that it is the CCP that controls the U.S. government.[37] Rubio was also described in contemporaneous CBS reporting as an Iran hawk who, alongside national security adviser pick [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]], was apparently unaware of [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s meeting with Iran's UN ambassador [[amir-saeid-iravani|Amir Saeid Iravani]].[37] His forthcoming departure from the Senate prompted speculation about his Florida seat, with CNN reported to be advocating for the appointment of [[hulk-hogan|Hulk Hogan]].[37]

## Closure of USAID (March 2025)

As Secretary of State, Rubio formally announced on March 28, 2025 the closure of [[usaid|USAID]], following the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s victory in a federal court case challenging the cost-cutting actions of the [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]-led [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]].[38] Rubio explained that "foreign assistance done right can advance our national interests, protect our borders, and strengthen our partnerships with key allies," but that "USAID strayed from its original mission long ago" and as a result "the gains were too few and the costs were too high."[38]

## Jerusalem visit and Gaza offensive (September 2025)

Rubio visited Jerusalem on Monday, 15 September 2025, where he showed no daylight between himself and Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], saying [[israel|Israel]] could "count on our unwavering support" for its new military push in Gaza and its stated goal of eradicating [[hamas|Hamas]].[39] Hours later, witnesses told AFP there was "heavy, relentless bombing on Gaza City" that levelled homes and left people trapped under the rubble, with one 25-year-old resident, Ahmed Ghazal, saying "We can hear their screams."[39]

## UN two-state solution conference boycott (July 2025)

Rubio and [[state-department|State Department]] spokeswoman [[tammy-bruce|Tammy Bruce]] publicly rejected the [[2025-un-two-state-solution-conference|United Nations conference on the two-state solution]] held in New York City in late July 2025, dismissing it as a "publicity stunt" designed to help [[hamas|Hamas]].[40] The United States did not attend and therefore did not sign the resulting declaration, in which Qatar, [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], Egypt and other states demanded that Hamas disarm, hand its weapons to the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], and end its rule of Gaza.[40]

## State Department reorganization notice to Congress (May 2025)

In late May 2025, Rubio spearheaded a reorganization of the [[state-department|State Department]] set to eliminate or consolidate more than 300 of the agency's offices and bureaus.[41] He had first announced plans in April to restructure the department on the grounds that it was "bloated, bureaucratic, and unable to perform its essential diplomatic mission."[41] The department submitted a notice to [[congress|Congress]] on Thursday, May 29, disclosing the overhaul, which senior officials described to Fox News Digital as the largest restructuring of the agency since the Cold War.[41] The congressional notice said the plan aimed to cut red tape so the department could respond to threats more quickly, place greater emphasis on its primary mission of representing the United States abroad, and eliminate "bureaucratic overgrowth" in Washington.[41] One senior official put it bluntly: "We have too many godd--- offices. We're trying to shrink offices rather than create them."[41]

## Chinese student visas and the Venezuela waiver dispute

## Guyana visit and warning to Venezuela, March 2025

## Rejection of the WHO's 2024 health regulation amendments

On Friday, July 18, 2025, one day before the deadline, the [[state-department|State Department]] under Secretary Rubio, together with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, formally refused the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]]'s 2024 amendments to the International Health Regulations, which would otherwise have become binding.[58] The rejection argued the amendments would give the WHO "undue influence on our domestic health responses."[58] Rubio said: "Terminology throughout the amendments to the 2024 International Health Regulations is vague and broad, risking WHO-coordinated international responses that focus on political issues like solidarity, rather than rapid and effective actions."[58]

Bush appoint no special envoys.[59] He predicted the dispute would sharpen into a division between Witkoff and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] on one side and neoconservative Republicans on the other, with Trump ultimately taking Witkoff's side.[59]

## Closure of the State Department disinformation office (2025)
After President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s April 2025 executive order instructed agencies to eliminate duplicative and unnecessary functions — including the [[foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-office|Foreign Information Manipulation and Interference Office]] (FIMI), successor to the [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]] — Secretary of State Rubio announced the office's closure.[60] A federal judge in California temporarily blocked the shutdown on June 13, 2025, halting layoffs and requiring the [[state-department|State Department]] to seek court approval before making further changes.[60]

## Dismantling of the R/FIMI hub (2025)

In March 2025, Secretary of State Rubio and Acting Under Secretary for Public Diplomacy [[darren-beattie|Darren Beattie]] terminated over 100 contractors associated with the [[counter-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-hub|Counter Foreign Information Manipulation and Interference]] (R/FIMI) hub, which Badlands Media described as a web of private and governmental operatives tasked with policing speech in a way that served the deep political establishment.[63] R/FIMI had been created by the outgoing Biden administration as a rebranded continuation of the [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]], retaining the same staff after Congress declined to reauthorize the GEC's funding.[63]

Rubio subsequently sat down with former State Department cybersecurity official and free-speech advocate [[mike-benz|Mike Benz]] to discuss the shake-up at the [[state-department|State Department]].[63] The action followed an op-ed Rubio published in The Federalist on April 16, 2025, in which he argued that the government's censorship efforts had been specifically designed to bankrupt and destroy outlets that countered official narratives.[63] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], who described himself as striving to remain nonpartisan, wrote that he agreed with Rubio's assessment and actions, adding that cheering Marco Rubio was not something he had written on his 2025 bingo card.[63]

## Restoration of NED funding (2025)

As Secretary of State, Marco Rubio oversaw the [[state-department|State Department]]'s restoration of the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]]'s access to frozen congressionally appropriated funds beginning March 10, 2025, following legal pressure from the endowment's suit against the administration.[64] Badlands Media groups Rubio with GOP figures who defend the NED, arguing its work aligns with U.S. interests by countering authoritarian regimes — a position at odds with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s request that the organization be zeroed out.[64]

## Reaction to the 2024 Secretary of State nomination

Rubio's selection as Secretary of State during the November 2024 transition drew a skeptical response from Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]], who wrote that he was "less than thrilled" by the pick.[65] With the hope in that community being that Trump would gut and reform the security state and the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]], DeLarme argued a Rubio appointment to the [[state-department|State Department]] suggested continuity with establishment foreign-policy practice by politicians with neoconservative tendencies.[65] He singled out Rubio's apparent belief that Russia blew up its own pipeline as disqualifying-adjacent.[65]

DeLarme nonetheless offered mitigating readings: Trump himself would be president and was unlikely to let "little Marco" keep things business as usual; the pick could be a "Boltonesque play" to scare adversaries, with Rubio publicly fired like [[john-bolton|John Bolton]] if he grew too hawkish; and Trump may simply have wanted Rubio's Senate seat vacated so [[lara-trump|Lara Trump]] could be swapped in.[65]
