# Mohammed bin Saud

Mohammed bin Saud Al Muqrin — referred to in the article as the original "MBS" — was the 18th-century founder of the [[house-of-saud|House of Saud]] and co-signatory of the [[pact-of-diriyah|Pact of Diriyah]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Background

Contrary to popular misconception, he was neither a Bedouin nomad nor a tribal warlord.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) He was the emir (chief) of a small farming community, Diriyah, on the outskirts of the town of [[riyadh|Riyadh]] ("The Gardens"), a name derived from the string of oases around which communities formed and on which [[bedouin|Bedouin]] nomads depended to traverse the desert.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) An ancient symbiosis existed between the sedentary farmers who protected the oases and the more warlike nomads who obtained resources through trade or pillage; from these alliances the politics of government were formed.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

He became emir of Diriyah after the collapse of the previous ruling family, the al-Diriyah, produced a power struggle in which he had to fight and defeat all three of his own brothers in battle.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) He was also a competent desert traveler and warrior — a skillset that, since leaving a landlocked oasis without desert knowledge was suicide, won him the respect and loyalty of the Bedouin who crossed the area.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) He organized and funded long-distance merchant caravans with the Bedouin and built an expanding network of influence across the [[najd|Najd]] region.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## The alliance with al-Wahhab

When [[muhammad-ibn-abd-al-wahhab|Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab]] was expelled from the nearby oasis town of Uyayna along with his followers, Mohammed bin Saud invited him to settle in Diriyah.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) He agreed with al-Wahhab that Arabia had fallen into moral decay and sought change, alongside his own ambitions, offering an alliance combining "might, reason, and faith."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) He set two conditions: that al-Wahhab settle in Diriyah as his advisor, and that al-Wahhab not oppose his collection of taxation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Al-Wahhab accepted the first and rejected the second, arguing that taxation would breed animus between ruler and people and that conquest would yield more wealth than bleeding his own subjects.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

Per Arabian historians, he declared: "This oasis is yours, do not fear your enemies. By the name of God, if all Nejd was summoned to throw you out, we will never agree to expel you."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The pact was sealed with a bay'ah (mutual oath of loyalty) in 1744 and cemented by wedding his son to al-Wahhab's daughter.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

He named his empire, the [[emirate-of-diriyah|First State of Saud]], in honor of his father, Saud bin Muhammad Al Muqrin.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The author counts him, alongside [[hashim-ibn-abd-manaf|Hashim]] and [[muhammad|Muhammad]], among the region's key historical figures who were men of honor and reason rather than the ruthless ideologues Western media portrays.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)
