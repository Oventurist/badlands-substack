# Mohammed bin Saud

Mohammed bin Saud Al Muqrin was the eighteenth-century emir of Diriyah and the founder of the [[house-of-saud]] as a political dynasty. The Badlands Media essay arabian nights refers to him by the same initials as the modern crown prince, MBS, deliberately drawing the parallel between the two.

## Background

The source insists that, contrary to popular misconception, Mohammed bin Saud was neither a Bedouin nomad nor a tribal warlord. He was the emir of a small farming community outside Riyadh, in the Najd region of central Arabia. His lineage is traced to the Banu Bakr and the Hanifa tribe, both pre-dating Islam; the Banu Bakr were a Christian clan before Muhammad's conquests.

He became emir of Diriyah after the collapse of the previous ruling family, the al-Diriyah, produced a power struggle in which he fought and defeated all three of his own brothers in battle. He was also a capable desert traveller and warrior — a skill the source treats as decisive, since leaving a landlocked oasis without knowledge of the desert meant death — and this won him the respect and loyalty of the bedouin. He organised and financed long-distance merchant caravans with them, building an expanding network of influence across the Najd.

## The alliance with al-Wahhab

When the preacher [[muhammad-ibn-abd-al-wahhab]] was expelled from the nearby oasis town of Uyayna, Mohammed bin Saud invited him to settle in Diriyah. He shared al-Wahhab's assessment that Arabia had fallen into moral decay and saw in a socio-religious reform movement — the combination of might, reason and faith — the vehicle for his own ambitions, judging that an orthodox doctrine would appeal to the Bedouin.

He set two conditions: that al-Wahhab settle in Diriyah as his advisor, and that he not oppose taxation. Al-Wahhab accepted the first and rejected the second, arguing that taxation would create natural animus between ruler and people and that conquest of enemies would yield more wealth than bleeding one's own subjects. Bin Saud agreed. The source records his declaration: "This oasis is yours, do not fear your enemies. By the name of God, if all Nejd was summoned to throw you out, we will never agree to expel you." The resulting [[pact-of-diriyah]] was confirmed by a *bay'ah* in 1744 and cemented by the marriage of bin Saud's son to al-Wahhab's daughter.

## Legacy

Bin Saud named his state in honour of his father, Saud bin Muhammad Al Muqrin; it is known as the First State of Saud or the [[emirate-of-diriyah]]. The essay's closing argument names him, alongside Hashim and Muhammad, as one of the key historical figures of Arabia who were "men of honour and reason, and not the ruthless ideologues that western media has gaslit us into believing them to be."
