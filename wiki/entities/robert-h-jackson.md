# Robert H. Jackson

Robert H. Jackson was an American jurist who served as Attorney General of the United States and later as an Associate Justice of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]]. He is cited in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary chiefly for his 1940 address on the ethics and dangers of federal prosecutorial power.[1]

## "The Federal Prosecutor" (1940)

In a speech delivered in Washington on April 1, 1940, Jackson warned about the inversion of ordinary law enforcement, in which the offense is identified after the target has been chosen: "In such a case, it is not a question of discovering the commission of a crime and then looking for the man who has committed it, it is a question of picking the man and then searching the law books, or putting investigators to work, to pin some offense on him."[1]

He went on to describe where the greatest danger of abuse lies: "It is in this realm—in which the prosecutor picks some person whom he dislikes or desires to embarrass, or selects some group of unpopular persons and then looks for an offense, that the greatest danger of abuse of prosecuting power lies. It is here that law enforcement becomes personal, and the real crime becomes that of being unpopular with the predominant or governing group, being attached to the wrong political views, or being personally obnoxious to or in the way of the prosecutor himself."[1]

## Invocation in the Trump prosecution debate

Writing in June 2023, [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] invoked Jackson's warning — delivered "some 80 years ago" — as a description of what he argued was occurring in the [[2023-trump-classified-documents-indictment|classified documents prosecution]] of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] In DeLarme's framing of [[deep-state|deep state]] [[lawfare|lawfare]], the prosecution has "one job that it must accomplish by any means necessary: to get Donald Trump," making Jackson's account of the prosecutor who selects the man before the offense directly applicable to Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s conduct of the case.[1]
