# Yugoslavia

Yugoslavia was the multi-ethnic socialist state whose breakup Badlands Media presents as the proving ground for post–Cold War [[nonviolent-action|nonviolent action]].[1]

## Debt and restructuring

The country had accepted development loans from the [[world-bank|World Bank]] and other Western financial institutions, and by the late 1980s Western creditors led by the [[imf|IMF]] were using their leverage to push Yugoslavia toward neoliberal economic restructuring.[1] In January 1991 [[slobodan-milosevic|Slobodan Milosevic]], leader of the Yugoslav Republic of Serbia, defied those policies by signing a law requiring Serbian-controlled national banks to issue $1.8 billion in new money to pay pensioners and farmers and avoid industrial bankruptcies.[1] Western retaliation followed: hostile international press, termination of U.S. aid, and threats to veto credit at the World Bank and IMF.[1]

## War and NATO intervention

The decade that followed was, in the article's words, characterized by atrocity, with Yugoslavia essentially compelled into the violence of the Yugoslav Wars.[1] The [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] helped train the separatist Albanian [[kosovo-liberation-army|Kosovo Liberation Army]] and encouraged it to launch a rebellion in southern Serbia to undermine Milosevic.[1] [[nato|NATO]] imposed rounds of sanctions conditioned on permanent occupation and full privatization — terms deliberately designed to be rejected — and then conducted a 78-day aerial bombing campaign.[1]

## Aftermath

Western Europe viewed Milosevic's regime as an obstacle to integrating the Balkans into the European Union, a longtime goal shared by the EU, UN and NATO.[1] After the [[otpor|Otpor!]] movement and the [[bulldozer-revolution|Bulldozer Revolution]] removed him in 2000, the successor states fared poorly: the countries of the former Yugoslavia, which the article says benefited from political freedom and a relatively high standard of living under socialism, are today consistently among the most impoverished in Europe.[1]
