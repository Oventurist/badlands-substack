# Ernest Lundeen

Ernest Lundeen was a United States Senator from Minnesota whom [[george-sylvester-viereck|George Sylvester Viereck]] recruited to the Nazi cause and effectively controlled, according to [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]].[1]

Albert H. Kahn's 1942 book Sabotage: The Secret War Against America gave particular attention to Viereck's hold over Lundeen, for whom the propagandist ghostwrote speeches delivered on the Senate floor.[1] One such Viereck-Lundeen composition, reprinted and mailed under the Senator's franking privilege, became known to tens of thousands of Americans under the title "Six Men and the War," a tirade against United States preparedness efforts on which Viereck worked particularly hard in order to catch the exact flavor of the Senator's oratorical style.[1]

When House Un-American Activities investigations were launched, Lundeen was the one senator who began cooperating with federal authorities on Viereck's Nazi penetration of the government.[1] He died in a plane crash near Lovettsville, Virginia on 31 August 1940 that killed all 24 passengers, including two federal investigators aboard, snuffing out his chance to testify about Viereck's occult networks in America.[1]
