# State Department

The United States Department of State appears in the article as one of several federal bodies whose formal terrorism designations undercut the political case made for the Libyan rebels in 2011.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

The article states flatly that "even the State Department designated the LIFG as Al-Qaeda," placing it alongside the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]], the U.S. military's Combating Terrorism Center at West Point, and the United Nations 1267 Committee in having formally identified the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] as an [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] affiliate.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) Against that background it asks whether anyone can seriously believe that [[john-mccain|McCain]] and [[barack-obama|Obama]] did not know the "freedom fighters" they promoted and supported were part of Al-Qaeda.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article closes by promising a subsequent piece on the attack on the U.S. consulate in [[benghazi|Benghazi]] and the targeting of Ambassador Stevens.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Role in labor operations abroad

A separate Badlands piece places the State Department at the centre of the covert use of American labor unions.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) Under Secretary of State [[edward-stettinius|Edward Stettinius]] the department explicitly coordinated with unions to ensure post-war European labor movements aligned with American free-market ideals, preventing Soviet-style worker councils from taking root.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) For the department and the nascent [[cia|CIA]], workers' rights were secondary to corralling the workforce into an ideological box, which meant crushing any outside attempt to organize labor.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

During the Cold War the department amplified union operations through diplomatic cables directing ambassadors to collaborate with union envoys, and cables from the [[lane-kirkland|Lane Kirkland]] era show ambassadors tasking union representatives with gathering intelligence on leftist organizers.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) Declassified cables are also cited as revealing consulate coordination with pro-democracy unions during the 2019–2020 Hong Kong protests, and as showing organizers in Venezuela and [[ukraine|Ukraine]] reporting on strikes, protests and opposition leaders to U.S. embassies via secure apps.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The department is named alongside the CIA, the Defense Department and the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] as part of the "deep government" network behind the [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]].[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Weapons authority and the Benghazi response

"A Dereliction of Duty" notes that the Secretary of State authorizes private U.S. weapons sales around the world, and alleges [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] used that power to funnel arms to Al Qaeda in Libya and onward to [[isis|ISIS]] in Syria, with Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|Christopher Stevens]] acting as liaison.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Stinger missiles turned over to [[qatar|Qatar]] in early 2011 were, according to Special Operations sources cited by the New York Post, intended by Clinton's department for anti-Gaddafi forces in Libya.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Departmental spokeswoman Victoria Nuland publicly maintained in June 2012 that "we are not in the business of arming in Syria."[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

On the night of the [[benghazi-attack|Benghazi attack]], the department's Diplomatic Command Center in Washington received word immediately, yet the notice reportedly took about an hour to reach the [[pentagon|Pentagon]].[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Department of Defense Chief of Staff Jeremy Bash offered assistance that night, saying forces "are spinning up as we speak," a fact revealed through a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) At the 7:30 p.m. deputies meeting the department emphasized that any deployment of U.S. forces into Libya needed approval from the Government of Libya.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Its Accountability Review Board later concluded the interagency response was "timely and appropriate," a finding contradicted by an Aviano Air Base squadron member who said a team was armed, briefed and ready but never received the order.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Diplomatic Security Agents in Benghazi radioed, "If you guys don't get out here, we're all going to f—-ing die."[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Special government employees and the Teneo network

The America for Sale series presents the department under [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] as the hub of a [[pay-for-play|pay-for-play]] arrangement.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) It granted "[[special-government-employee|special government employee]]" status to [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]], allowing her to advise the Secretary while simultaneously collecting $15,000 a month from [[teneo-holdings|Teneo Holdings]] and consulting for the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] — an arrangement the article calls an obvious conflict of interest given her access to secret information at the highest levels.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The department also approved the paid consulting relationship between [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and [[doug-band|Doug Band]].[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

A parallel arrangement covered [[declan-kelly|Declan Kelly]], whom Clinton personally selected as economic envoy to Northern Ireland with special status outside normal diplomatic channels.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Kelly took no departmental salary and hired his own staff, retaining $2.4 million paid by Dow Chemical through FTI Consulting between August 2009 and January 2011.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Politico reported that despite job titles that sounded official and regular interaction with department staff and Irish diplomats, none of the envoy office personnel were government employees and therefore faced no constraints on outside activities; a department statement said, "The State Department does not have a record of these individuals being employed by the Department."[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Several of them listed Declan Kelly Consulting on their résumés and became among the first Teneo employees, escaping the oversight normally given to State employees.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

In July 2009 Band emailed Abedin asking the department to arrange diplomatic passports for himself and two Clinton aides who did not work there, ahead of the secret mission to [[north-korea|North Korea]]; Abedin replied that she would "figure it out."[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Approval of the Uranium One sale

Because uranium is treated as a strategic asset with national-security implications, the sale of [[uranium-one|Uranium One]] to Russia's [[rosatom|Rosatom]] required approval from a committee composed of representatives of several federal agencies.[5] Among the agencies that signed off was the State Department, then headed by [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], whose office's approval the article says was necessary for the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]] to proceed despite her repeated denials of involvement.[5] The article notes that most of the Obama cabinet also had to approve, and stresses that the transaction covered uranium in [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]] as well as in the United States.[5]

## The January 2017 Gaza disbursement

In "Bad Actors," the department is the instrument of the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration's final act in office. Acting spokesman Mark Toner told The Independent: "In a final act, then-Secretary of State [[john-kerry|John Kerry]] instructed USAID to release $220.3 million for Gaza recovery programs," with the House informed hours before [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s inauguration and [[congress|Congress]] overruled by the President.[8] The recipient was the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], and the Trump administration froze the package shortly after taking office.[8]

Joe Lange argues that the department and [[usaid|USAID]] "go hand-in-hand because both are controlled by the CIA," which made the [[cia|CIA]] by far the largest recipient of American foreign aid money; Obama's 2016 budget proposal designated almost $33 billion to the two bodies combined.[8] On this reading the department functioned as a conduit for funding America's enemies rather than as a diplomatic agency, and the Gaza payment was the repayment of a political debt owed to [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]].[8]

## Israel–Lebanon diplomacy (2026)

Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] hosted the April 2026 Washington meeting at which Israeli and Lebanese officials met for the first time in thirty-four years, the encounter that preceded President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s announcement of a ten-day [[israel|Israel]]–[[lebanon|Lebanon]] ceasefire.[9] Trump directed Rubio, together with Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] and Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff [[dan-caine|Dan Caine]], to work with both governments toward a lasting peace agreement.[9]

## Meetings with Alberta separatists (2025–2026)

State Department officials met three times in Washington with members of the [[alberta-prosperity-project|Alberta Prosperity Project]] since April 2025, according to Financial Times reporting; APP co-founder and legal counsel Jeffrey Rath reportedly attended.[10] Rath said "the US is extremely enthusiastic about a free and independent Alberta," and the group planned a fourth delegation the following month while seeking a $500 billion US line of credit contingent on a successful independence referendum.[10] A department spokesperson downplayed the contacts, saying "the department regularly meets with civil society types" and that "no commitments were made."[10] Canadian Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] said he expects the administration to respect Canadian sovereignty.[10]

## ICC withdrawal campaign (July 2026)

Under Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], the department announced in July 2026 a campaign aimed at dismantling the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]], arguing the tribunal threatens U.S. sovereignty and improperly targets American military personnel and officials, and encouraging other countries to distance themselves from it.[11] The department welcomed [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s decision to withdraw from the court and called on other countries to follow suit, according to a statement reported by Anadolu Agency.[11] Badlands read the move as using Venezuela's withdrawal as a rallying cry and as evidence of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s ability to turn the Deep State against itself.[11]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] separately asked to what extent the department and [[congress|Congress]] were involved in the alleged trafficking of Ukrainian children described by Russian Duma Deputy Speaker [[anna-kuznetsova|Anna Kuznetsova]], suggesting it might explain bipartisan support for Ukraine's war effort.[11]

## Lebanon drawdown (February 2026)

In late February 2026 the State Department ordered the departure of nonessential diplomatic staff from Lebanon, citing security concerns linked to broader tensions with [[iran|Iran]].[12] The order came as indirect US–Iran nuclear negotiations concluded in Geneva without agreement and as the United States deployed two aircraft carrier strike groups and more than 100 fighter jets to the Middle East.[12]

## State Sponsor of Wrongful Detention designations (2026)

On March 9, 2026, Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] announced that the Department had designated [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] a "State Sponsor of Wrongful Detention" in response to the [[taliban|Taliban]]'s detention of US nationals and other foreign citizens on grounds Washington deemed unjustified or politically motivated.[13] The designation is a State Department classification identifying governments that detain foreign nationals to gain diplomatic leverage or concessions, and it allows the US government to increase diplomatic pressure and coordinate efforts to secure detainees' release.[13] Afghanistan joined other countries previously labeled under the same framework.[13]

## Bureau of African Affairs and the ICC withdrawals (2026)

In late July 2026 the State Department's Bureau of African Affairs welcomed [[chad|Chad]]'s withdrawal from the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] in a post on X, calling on more of the court's 125 member states to withdraw and "reclaim their sovereignty from this flawed institution."[14] The statement followed Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]]'s announcement earlier that month that the United States was launching a campaign to dismantle the ICC's threat to national sovereignty, and formed part of the broader [[icc-withdrawal-wave|ICC withdrawal wave]] that also included [[venezuela|Venezuela]], Niger, Mali, and [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]].[14]

## Global counter-terrorism summit (July 2026)
In July 2026 the State Department, under Secretary [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], convened officials from more than 65 countries in Washington to launch an international initiative against transnational far-left political terrorism.[15] The department's plans included expanded international cooperation, terrorist designations, sanctions and visa restrictions, coordinated with the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] and the White House as part of the [[global-crackdown-on-far-left-political-terrorism|global crackdown on far-left political terrorism]].[15]

## Venezuela re-entry (2026)

Following the capture of former Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in January 2026, the State Department took responsibility for long-term diplomacy with [[venezuela|Venezuela]] and for assessments related to reopening the US embassy, with a small team already in Caracas evaluating the facility that had been shuttered since 2019.[18] The [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] led the initial re-entry effort because of ongoing instability, establishing an intelligence annex for informal liaison channels before full diplomatic operations resumed.[18] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] stated that the United States had been "asked" to reopen the embassy and claimed the administration would effectively "run" the country during the transition.[18] See [[cia-re-entry-into-venezuela-2026|CIA re-entry into Venezuela (2026)]].[18]

## Jalisco shelter-in-place advisories (February 2026)

After Mexican forces killed [[el-mencho|Nemesio Oseguera Cervantes]] in Tapalpa and the [[jalisco-new-generation-cartel|CJNG]] launched reprisals across multiple Mexican states, the Department of State issued shelter-in-place warnings for American citizens in Jalisco and other affected areas.[20] The advisories accompanied local emergency measures including "code red" alerts, canceled public events and suspended classes as at least 14 people were reported killed in the unrest.[20]

## Report on Cuban support for radical left-wing activism (2026)

In July 2026 the department said it was preparing to release a report alleging that [[cuba|Cuba]]'s communist government has spent decades supporting and cultivating radical left-wing movements in the United States and across the Western Hemisphere.[21] According to excerpts previewed ahead of release, the report argues the Cuban regime provided ideological, organizational, and political support to networks that influenced activist movements beyond Cuba's borders.[21]

The report is expected to examine organizations including [[codepink|Code Pink]] and to discuss financial backing from businessman [[neville-roy-singham|Neville Roy Singham]] for several left-wing groups, including Code Pink and the [[peoples-forum|People's Forum]], alleging these organizations promoted positions aligned with the Cuban government and other authoritarian regimes opposed to US interests.[21] It came as Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] made countering transnational left-wing political influence a priority of US foreign policy, and the administration said the document was intended to highlight Cuba's historical role in supporting revolutionary movements throughout the Americas and to inform future US policy toward the regime.[21] See [[state-department-report-on-cuban-support-for-radical-left-wing-activism|the report]] for fuller coverage.[21]

## Israel-Lebanon talks (2026)

The State Department hosted the first high-level talks between Israel and Lebanon since 1993 in April 2026, a trilateral working-level meeting led by Secretary of State Marco Rubio with the Israeli and Lebanese ambassadors participating.[22] The department framed U.S. support around bolstering Lebanese state authority, dismantling Hezbollah, and continued dialogue, with both parties agreeing to move toward direct negotiations.[22]

## Venezuela policy dispute and the Landau episode (July 2026)

In July 2026 Deputy Secretary of State [[chris-landau|Chris Landau]] was accused by senior Trump administration officials of twice miscommunicating US policy regarding exiled Venezuelan opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]]'s efforts to return to Venezuela after the June 24 earthquakes.[23] Landau denied contradicting official policy, saying he had also communicated that the United States was neutral on her travel, and a department spokesperson dismissed reports of internal conflict, stating the department remained aligned with President [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Secretary [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]]'s agenda.[23] Badlands commentary used the episode to argue that the State Department harbors "rogue actors" inclined to overthrow foreign governments and install preferred leaders.[23]

The department also maintains the [[state-sponsors-of-terrorism-list|State Sponsors of Terrorism list]], from which President Trump announced in July 2026 that [[syria|Syria]] would be removed for the first time since the list's creation in 1979.[23]

## Cuba subversion report (July 2026)

On a Monday in July 2026 the Department released a report titled around Cuba as "the capital of 21st century communism," alleging that Havana had run a nearly seven-decade campaign of subversion, espionage, and ideological warfare against the United States.[24] The [[state-department-cuba-subversion-report-2026|report]] claimed that Cuban espionage had penetrated the State Department itself, along with the National Security Council and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], and tied Cuban networks to the George Floyd riots, the rise of [[antifa|Antifa]], and campus pro-terror activism.[24]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], drawing on analysis by [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]], characterized the document as an attempt to project the State Department's own crimes onto external factions, treating the Department as effectively synonymous with the [[cia|CIA]] and describing the CIA in turn as a proxy of British Intelligence within the global regime.[24] He argued that the specific accusations levied at [[cuba|Cuba]] — regime-change operations, terrorist training, cultural subversion via institutions and education — describe conduct the CIA itself undertook through Operation Gladio, Operation Condor, and the sponsorship of groups including [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] and [[isis|ISIS]].[24] That the Department was now advancing such a report, he wrote, "reeks of desperation."[24]

## February 2026 arms sale approvals

In late January and early February 2026, the State Department approved Foreign Military Sales notifications totaling approximately $15.7 billion to [[israel|Israel]] and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]].[25] The package included about $6.67 billion for Israel — 30 AH-64E Apache attack helicopters with associated weapons and equipment, plus 3,250 Joint Light Tactical Vehicles — and about $9 billion for Saudi Arabia, primarily Patriot Advanced Capability-3 (PAC-3) missile systems and roughly 730 interceptors with related support equipment.[25] The approvals were transmitted to [[congress|Congress]] amid ongoing regional tensions involving [[iran|Iran]]; see [[2026-foreign-military-sales-to-israel-and-saudi-arabia|the 2026 Foreign Military Sales to Israel and Saudi Arabia]].[25]

## Venezuela travel advisory (January 2026)

In January 2026 the State Department maintained its Level 4 "Do Not Travel" advisory for [[venezuela|Venezuela]], reiterating that US citizens and lawful permanent residents in the country should depart immediately.[26] The advisory cited security risks and the limited ability of the US government to provide emergency assistance inside the country.[26] It was issued during the same period in which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] pressed [[cuba|Cuba]] to reach an agreement with Washington and [[us-central-command|US Central Command]] announced large-scale strikes on [[isis|ISIS]] targets in Syria.[26]


## Venezuela travel advisory (2026)

As Venezuela dominated headlines in January 2026, news that the State Department had ordered Americans to leave the country circulated widely, framed around warnings of roving gangs seeking to kidnap and ransom U.S. citizens.[27] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the advisory had actually been issued on December 3, 2025 and only became broadly visible later, and that it represented no substantive change: Venezuela had been at Level 3 since the mid-2010s and the U.S. closed its embassy in Caracas in 2019, moving it to a Level 4 equivalent that had been regularly reissued ever since.[27] Drawing on her corporate experience receiving such advisories, she observed that a genuine escalation would trigger business continuity protocols, cancelled travel and projects, and evacuation of personnel — making the recirculated advisory "just a narrative deployment," the story of change rather than change itself.[27]

## Escalation toward Russia (April 2026)

While President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signaled tactical de-escalation with [[china|China]] in April 2026, US officials escalated rhetoric and scrutiny toward actors linked to [[russia|Russia]]'s war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]], including State Department claims involving foreign support networks and thousands of Cuba-linked fighters supporting Russia.[28] Badlands commentary read the pattern as Washington easing pressure on Beijing in the Middle East theater while maintaining or increasing pressure on Moscow and its partners.[28] Contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry predicted that Trump would soon have a public mandate to significantly reduce, or abolish, State Department activities abroad.[28]

## Foreign funding disclosure at U.S. universities (February 2026)

In February 2026 the State Department announced a new interagency agreement with the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] to strengthen enforcement of foreign funding disclosure requirements at American colleges and universities, citing President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s [[executive-order-14282|Executive Order 14282]] on transparency regarding foreign influence in higher education.[29] Under the agreement, the department's Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs supports administration of [[section-117-higher-education-act|Section 117 of the Higher Education Act of 1965]], which requires U.S. institutions to report foreign gifts and contracts totaling $250,000 or more in a calendar year.[29]

Officials said the partnership was designed to improve the accuracy and transparency of reporting while allowing national security experts to more effectively assess potential foreign influence or threats.[29] The agreement expands the department's role in managing and reviewing data from the Section 117 reporting portal; leveraging its expertise in national security and foreign-national academic admissions, State helps review compliance, shares information with federal stakeholders, and works to ensure the data can be used proactively to safeguard U.S. research and students.[29]

## Continuous vetting of 55 million visa holders (August 2025)

On Thursday, August 21, 2025, the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] said it was reviewing more than 55 million people holding valid U.S. visas for any violations that could lead to deportation, part of a growing crackdown on foreigners permitted to be in the United States.[39] In a written answer to a question from The Associated Press, the State Department said all U.S. visa holders — including tourists from many countries — are subject to "continuous vetting," with an eye toward any indication they could be ineligible for permission to enter or stay.[39] Should such information be found, the visa would be revoked, and a holder inside the United States would be subject to deportation.[39] The department's language suggested the vetting process, which officials acknowledge is time-consuming, is far more widespread than previously understood and could mean even those approved to be in the U.S. abruptly lose permission.[39] Badlands host [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] noted that the Migration Policy Institute estimates temporary visa holders represent only 5% of total foreign-born U.S. residents, quipping that either the estimate is far off "or we have 1.1 billion illegal aliens here."[39]

## Closure of the Global Engagement Center (December 2024)

In a Monday court filing in December 2024, the State Department revealed that it was "substantially likely" to shut down its $100 million [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]], which had been revealed in early 2023 to have funded a "disinformation" tracking group that pressured advertisers to demonetize outlets accused of spreading disinformation.[54] Critics observed that the department was in reality "realigning" the center's staff and funding to other departmental offices and bureaus dealing with foreign information manipulation, rather than eliminating the function.[54]

The move came amid a lawsuit from Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] and several conservative media outlets named on a GEC-funded "dynamic exclusion list" of disfavored websites, which was distributed to ad tech firms such as Microsoft's Xandr to "defund and downrank these worst offenders."[54] It was unclear how the closure would affect the litigation, with the filing noting that lawyers for all parties were still discussing the implications.[54] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] had called the GEC the "worst offender in U.S. government" on censorship in March 2023, and [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] disclosures showed the center funded groups such as the [[atlantic-council|Atlantic Council]]'s [[digital-forensic-research-lab|Digital Forensic Research Lab]], which supplied Twitter with account blacklists.[54]


## Reported 2025 Reorganization Draft

In April 2025 a draft order circulated within the State Department outlining a complete overhaul of the agency to "streamline mission delivery" while eliminating "waste, fraud and abuse," according to The New York Times.[55] The plan reportedly recommended eliminating the bureaus handling African affairs along with all "non-essential embassies and consulates in sub-Saharan Africa," shuttering bureaus on democracy and human rights, refugees and migration, and international organizations, abolishing the special envoy on climate, and shrinking the US diplomatic presence in Canada to a "significantly reduced team" overseen directly by Secretary [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]].[55] It also proposed ending the foreign service's "outdated and disorganized generalist global rotation model" in favor of specialization, and terminating the recruitment contract for fellowship candidates from Howard University.[55] Rubio called the report "fake news" in a post on X, but Politico reported that diplomats were rattled and believed the memo could be genuine.[55] Badlands commentary endorsed abolishing the department outright, arguing most international problems trace back to it, praising special envoys such as [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] and [[adam-boehler|Adam Boehler]] as evidence that diplomacy is best conducted by businessmen and pragmatists, and describing the [[cia|CIA]] as "the heart and soul of the State Department."[55]
