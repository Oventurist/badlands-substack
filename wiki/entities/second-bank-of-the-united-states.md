# Second Bank of the United States

The Second Bank of the United States was the early-nineteenth-century central bank whose establishment provoked the opposition of President [[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

When the Second Bank was established, Jackson concluded that the bankers were overreaching and acting as a fourth branch of government run by the elite.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) He judged that if the bankers succeeded in their ultimate plan they would enslave every citizen in the country, and he sought to root them out, believing they would direct loans to prominent people over the common citizen and use their power to enable further corruption.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The article treats the Second Bank episode as the nineteenth-century precursor to the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], the institution the [[cabal]] ultimately obtained in 1913.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Chartering after the War of 1812

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] situates the bank's creation in the debt cycle that followed the [[war-of-1812|War of 1812]]: with the United States in massive debt after the war, the government chartered the Second Bank, once again centralizing the banking system in the hands of a small group of private bankers.[2] Those bankers enriched themselves so greatly that they turned the American people against them.[2]

[[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]] was elected president vowing to be rid of the central bank and dissolved it, after which he paid off the entire public debt from past wars using import duties and public land sales, leaving the government debt free and no longer controlled by foreign private bankers.[2] Jackson's refusal to reauthorize the bank's charter was the stated motive of the 1835 assassination attempt against him, the first ever against a U.S. president.[2] The federal surplus that followed was dispersed to state banks under the [[deposit-act-of-1836|Deposit Act of 1836]].[2]

## The COVFEFE Dig

"The COVFEFE Dig" (March 2023) recounts the [[bank-war|Bank War]] as the political struggle over rechartering the Second Bank of the United States during the presidency of [[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]], a conflict that ended in the Bank's shutdown and its replacement by state banks.[3] [[gmoney|GMONEY]] treats this episode as the precedent for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s supposed campaign against the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], noting that Jackson's portrait was the first Trump hung in the White House.[3]

## Founding and Demise

Five years after the charter of the [[first-bank-of-the-united-states|First Bank of the United States]] expired, the federal government chartered its successor, the Second Bank of the United States.[4] [[james-madison|James Madison]] signed the charter with the intention of stopping the runaway inflation that had plagued the country during the five-year interim, and the institution was essentially a copy of the First Bank, with branches across the country.[4]

[[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]], who became president in 1828, denounced the new bank as an "engine of corruption."[4] On September 10, 1833, Jackson announced that the government would no longer use the Second Bank and used his executive power to remove all federal funds from it, the final salvo of what is called the [[bank-war|Bank War]].[4] Jackson was unable to get the bank dissolved but refused to renew its charter, which ended in 1836.[4] The banking world then passed through the "free banking" era and the "national banks" era, which lasted until the creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve System]].[4]

## As a Rothschild-dominated bank

Ryan DeLarme's "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 6" describes the bank as the second instance of Rothschild control over the American money supply, writing that in 1816 the American Congress passed a bill allowing "yet another Rothschild-dominated central bank" with a twenty-year charter.[5] [[andrew-jackson|Andrew Jackson]]'s 1832 campaign slogan "Jackson And No Bank!" is presented as his pledge to take control of the money system away from the [[rothschild-family|Rothschilds]], and his removal of federal deposits the following year is said to have prompted the bankers to contract the money supply and cause a depression.[5] Jackson finally expelled the bank when its charter was not renewed, and DeLarme dates the family's third American central bank — the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] — to 1913.[5]
