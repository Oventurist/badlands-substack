# Lee Harvey Oswald

Lee Harvey Oswald is the man identified by U.S. authorities as the gunman who shot President John F. Kennedy during a visit to Dallas, Texas, on 22 November 1963.[1] Many Americans continue to believe that Oswald did not act alone, a view that has sustained conspiracy theories about the assassination for more than sixty years.[1]

## The 2025 records release

Questions about Oswald's role were renewed in March 2025 when the U.S. government began releasing a final trove of documents relating to the assassination, following an executive order signed by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] in January of that year requiring unredacted files in the case to be made public.[1] Trump estimated that 80,000 pages would be unsealed, telling reporters, "You got a lot of reading... I don't believe we're going to redact anything."[1]

Historians did not expect many ground-breaking revelations from the records, and it was unclear how much of the material released by the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives and Records Administration]] was genuinely new, since many documents had previously been released in partially redacted form.[1]

Badlands commentary on the release highlighted material in which intelligence assets were reminded that the notion of the government killing Kennedy was merely a "political murder conspiracy," which Jon Herold characterized as "not the best look."[1]

## Alleged CIA relationship and the patsy thesis

The Badlands essay "Patsies of the CIA and FBI" argues that Oswald thought he was working for the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]], and states that documentation shows senior CIA officials admitted to using Oswald for intelligence purposes several weeks prior to the assassination he was accused of carrying out.[2]

The essay places particular weight on Oswald's 1957 posting to the Atsugi Naval Air Facility in Japan, described as a launching pad for U-2 spy flights over the Soviet Union and also a hub of the CIA's research into psychedelic drugs.[2] Some Oswald researchers, it notes, believe he was among the marines on whom the CIA performed its acid tests, though those claims are impossible to verify.[2]

On motive, the article reports that according to "conspiracy theorists" the CIA wanted [[john-f-kennedy|Kennedy]] dead because he was a threat to its power and even its existence: Kennedy had recently fired agency head [[allen-dulles|Dulles]] over the failed [[bay-of-pigs|Bay of Pigs]] operation against [[fidel-castro|Fidel Castro]]'s communist government in Cuba, and was said to want not only the CIA dismantled but the United States out of the [[vietnam-war|Vietnam War]], leading to a reduction of militarism generally.[2] To use Oswald as a patsy — and possibly to blame the assassination on the Soviet Union — the agency is said to have made him believe he was working as a spy for them, a double agent, while building a backstory explaining why he would want to kill Kennedy.[2]

The article recounts his movements as evidence of that backstory. After studying Russian in the military Oswald was discharged and, with literally no money, took a boat to England, then a military transport flight to Finland, where he stayed at two of the most expensive hotels in Helsinki, before taking a train to Moscow and announcing his defection at the US Embassy.[2] He remained in the Soviet Union for two and a half years before mysteriously returning to the United States.[2] Oswald heads the essay's list of figures suspected of having taken part in the [[mk-ultra|MK ULTRA]] program, and is grouped with [[jack-ruby|Jack Ruby]], [[sirhan-sirhan|Sirhan Sirhan]] and [[john-hinckley-jr|John Hinckley Jr.]] among those used to eliminate people in power who opposed the CIA.[2] His own killer, Ruby, was treated by Dr. [[louis-jolyon-west|Louis Jolyon West]], alleged to have been an MK ULTRA doctor — a coincidence the author declines to accept as such.[2] See also [[intelligence-patsy|intelligence patsy]].[2]
