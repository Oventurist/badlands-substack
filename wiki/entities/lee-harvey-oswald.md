# Lee Harvey Oswald

Lee Harvey Oswald is the man identified by U.S. authorities as the gunman who shot President John F. Kennedy during a visit to Dallas, Texas, on 22 November 1963.[1] Many Americans continue to believe that Oswald did not act alone, a view that has sustained conspiracy theories about the assassination for more than sixty years.[1]

## The 2025 records release

Questions about Oswald's role were renewed in March 2025 when the U.S. government began releasing a final trove of documents relating to the assassination, following an executive order signed by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] in January of that year requiring unredacted files in the case to be made public.[1] Trump estimated that 80,000 pages would be unsealed, telling reporters, "You got a lot of reading... I don't believe we're going to redact anything."[1]

Historians did not expect many ground-breaking revelations from the records, and it was unclear how much of the material released by the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives and Records Administration]] was genuinely new, since many documents had previously been released in partially redacted form.[1]

Badlands commentary on the release highlighted material in which intelligence assets were reminded that the notion of the government killing Kennedy was merely a "political murder conspiracy," which Jon Herold characterized as "not the best look."[1]
