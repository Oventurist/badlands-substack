# Hurras al-Din

**Hurras al-Din** (Arabic: "Guardians of Religion") was the Syrian affiliate of [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]], designated on Washington's list of terrorist organizations.[1] In a statement posted online on Tuesday, 28 January 2025, the group announced "the dissolution of the Hurras al-Din organisation," saying the move followed a decision by "the general command of Al-Qaeda."[1]

The announcement was notable because it was the first occasion on which the group had officially identified itself as "the branch of Al-Qaeda" in Syria.[1] Hurras al-Din said the decision came "in light of developments" in Syria, where a rebel alliance led by the Islamist group [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] (HTS) ousted [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] on 8 December 2024 after a lightning offensive.[1]

## CENTCOM strike and Badlands commentary

Badlands commentary highlighted the timing of subsequent events: on 29 January 2025 Hurras al-Din announced its dissolution, and on 30 January [[centcom|CENTCOM]] killed the group's leader in an airstrike.[1] The commentary noted that the United States military had been killing Al-Qaeda and ISIS operatives in Syria continuously since the fall of Assad, but observed that a "career head-chopper from Al-Qaeda" — a reference to [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]], formerly known as Abu Mohammad al-Julani — consolidated power and named himself president on the same day that his presumed rival within Al-Qaeda was killed in an airstrike, one day after that rival had announced the dissolution of his militia.[1] The writer concluded that "something about all of this seems strange," treating the sequence as evidence that the dissolution and the strike were connected to a factional consolidation rather than to a straightforward counterterrorism campaign.[1]
