# Dan Gertler

Dan Gertler is a businessman whose mining interests in the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] made him a central figure in litigation and reporting on Congolese copper and cobalt assets.[1]

## Glencore relationship

When the financial crisis struck in 2007–2008, Gertler faced insolvency and accepted a $45 million investment from [[glencore|Glencore]] in exchange for an ownership stake in his companies.[1] He subsequently brokered deals bringing both [[mutanda-mining|Mutanda Mining]] and [[kamoto-copper-company|Kamoto Copper Company]] under Glencore's control while retaining some ownership stake.[1]

## Sanctions

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]] on December 20, 2017, which lists Gertler as number three on the associated sanctions list, placing Mutanda and Kamoto in the middle of a geopolitical conflict.[1] The affected assets were frozen, numerous court cases — mostly in foreign courts — followed, and mineral rights were stripped away from the companies.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] highlighted the irony of a US-backed group now seeking to acquire those same formerly Gertler-linked assets.[1]

## Congolese monopoly and family background

Prior to the 2017 sanctions, Gertler — described in Badlands commentary as an Israeli oligarch — effectively held a monopoly over the Congo's minerals thanks to a special relationship with former Congolese president [[joseph-kabila|Joseph Kabila]], to the tune of more than $360 million in reported bribes.[2] Gertler is the grandson of Moshe Schnitzer, founder of the world's largest diamond exchange and a scion of the Israeli diamond cartel.[2]

## Yossi Cohen episode

Former [[mossad|Mossad]] chief Yossi Cohen was banned for life from the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|DR Congo]] after being caught traveling covertly around the country with Gertler in 2019, allegedly attempting to launch a coup against the new government that had ousted Kabila in an election.[2] Journalistic revelations of the episode led to Cohen's forced resignation from the leadership of Mossad in 2022.[2]

## Role in the 2025 DRC minerals debate

In March 2025, as witnesses at a [[house-foreign-affairs-committee|House Foreign Affairs Committee]] subcommittee hearing urged Washington to counter China's exploitation of corruption in the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]], Badlands commentary placed Gertler at the center of the longer history of the country's exploitation, describing him as the present-day scion of the cartel family behind the Israeli Diamond Exchange.[3]

The commentary recounted that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] froze Gertler's assets in 2017, after which those holdings were picked apart in lawsuits around the world, including suits brought by the DRC government, though Gertler won at least one ruling awarding him millions of dollars on the sale of some assets.[3] It further noted that shortly after the sanctions were imposed, the long-reigning Congolese president who had granted Gertler his monopoly over the country's resources was publicly shamed into withdrawing from his presumed reelection campaign, leading to the election of [[felix-tshisekedi|Félix Tshisekedi]].[3]

The commentary argued it was reasonable to assume that the Rwandan-backed [[m23|M23]] group was acting on behalf of Gertler and his cartel, citing Rwandan President [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]]'s loyalty to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], Gertler's family history with the Israeli oligarchy, and reported instances in which Gertler and then-Mossad director [[yossi-cohen|Yossi Cohen]] were caught operating covertly in the country — accused by Tshisekedi's aides of attempting to facilitate a coup — as well as the failed 2024 coup attempt led by Congolese-American [[christian-malanga|Christian Malanga]].[3]

## Katanga Mining and Glencore

Badlands commentary in November 2024 recalled Gertler as the Israeli oligarch and scion to the Israel Diamond Cartel who was famously targeted by [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]] for using child slaves to work his mines in Africa, most notably in the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of Congo]].[4] His company, [[katanga-mining|Katanga Mining]], possessed the exclusive mining rights for the region for which it is named.[4] After experiencing financial trouble in 2007, Gertler sought a $45 million investment from [[glencore|Glencore]] to stay solvent.[4] Following the 2017 freezing of his assets, Glencore was forced to buy out his shares of Katanga Mining and became its sole owner.[4]

## Cited in Devolution commentary

Badlands commentary describes Gertler as an Israeli oligarch and ally of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] whose US sanctions were secretly reversed on January 15, 2021, in the closing days of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term.[5] According to [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], Israeli ambassador [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] achieved the reversal by working his contacts inside the [[state-department|State Department]], an episode cited as proof that the bureaucracy remained under hostile control and that [[devolution|Devolution]] was therefore necessary.[5]

## Relevance to the 2025 DR Congo conflict

Badlands commentary in March 2025 identified the freezing of Gertler's assets under Executive Order 13818 in December 2017 as the moment the "globalist regime" lost its monopoly over rare earth minerals in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo.[6] The commentary argued that Gertler's mineral rights had since been stripped piece by piece by various courts and authorities, and that regaining control through legal means appeared unlikely.[6]

On this reading, the violence carried out by [[m23|M23]] with the backing of Rwandan President [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]] represented an extralegal route to the same end, since the border towns being seized contain the processing plants for minerals ultimately purchased by corporations such as Apple, Sony and Lockheed Martin — deposits valued in excess of $24 trillion.[6]

## The 2025 Congo escalation

Badlands analysis of the February 2025 fighting in eastern Congo restated the Gertler thesis in compressed form: President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] froze the assets of the Israeli mining oligarch in December 2017 using Executive Order 13818, and because Gertler was "the golden goose for the entire Israeli elite" according to the Paradise Papers leak, every effort has since been made to regain control over his lost mineral rights.[7] The estimated value of Congolese minerals is put in excess of $24 trillion.[7]

In this reading, the mercenaries who descended on the eastern Congo from [[rwanda|Rwanda]] were plausibly directed by Rwandan President [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]] on behalf of Netanyahu and Gertler.[7] The associated rabbit hole was said to implicate not only the Israeli elite but the largest technology companies in the world, including [[apple|Apple]], Intel, Sony and Lockheed Martin, which a report by [[amsterdam-and-partners|Amsterdam & Partners]] accused of smuggling rare earth minerals out of the Congo to be laundered as "clean" through bodies such as the [[international-tin-supply-chain-initiative|ITSCI]] and the [[responsible-minerals-initiative|Responsible Minerals Initiative]].[7] Congolese President [[felix-tshisekedi|Félix Tshisekedi]], who in 2018 replaced [[joseph-kabila|Joseph Kabila]] — the man who granted Gertler his mining monopoly in the first place — subsequently offered those same mineral rights to the United States in exchange for help against the Rwandan mercenaries.[7]

## Sanctions reversal and reimposition (2021–2022)

Badlands commentary recounted that on January 15, 2021, during the final days of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term, then-Israeli ambassador [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] used his network in Washington to enter the [[state-department|State Department]] and reverse the sanctions imposed on Gertler under [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]], as reported by Bloomberg.[8] The reversal went largely unnoticed while the press was consumed by January 6.[8]

Treasury Secretary [[janet-yellen|Janet Yellen]] reissued the sanctions against Gertler in March 2021 once the press finally reported the development, and the Biden [[justice-department|Justice Department]] then successfully prosecuted the case, stripping Gertler of his African mining rights in 2022.[8] Those rights were subsequently acquired legally by members of what Badlands calls the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] — the UAE, Russia and China — an outcome [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] presented as both a [[devolution|Devolution]] proof and a Sovereign Alliance proof.[8]

## Place in the DR Congo minerals narrative

Badlands commentary on the June 2025 [[rwanda-drc-peace-agreement-2025|Rwanda–DR Congo peace agreement]] positions Gertler, described as a scion of the Israeli Diamond Cartel, at the origin of the story: he was targeted by [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]], signed December 20, 2017.[9] His longtime business partner, DRC President [[joseph-kabila|Joseph Kabila]], was subsequently displaced by [[felix-tshisekedi|Félix Tshisekedi]], after which Gertler and former Mossad director [[yossi-cohen|Yossi Cohen]] were alleged to have made the first of several attempts to overthrow the new government.[9] The wider affair also encompassed arrests of high-profile figures at the world's top three commodity trading houses, including [[glencore|Glencore]], over access to an estimated $24 trillion in Congolese rare earth minerals.[9]
