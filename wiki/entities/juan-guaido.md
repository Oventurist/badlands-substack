# Juan Guaidó

Juan Guaidó is a Venezuelan opposition politician and former head of the country's National Assembly.[1]

## Essequibo arrest warrant

In December 2023, as [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] moved to annex the oil-rich Essequibo region of [[guyana|Guyana]] following a Sunday referendum, Venezuela's Prosecutor's Office issued arrest warrants for a dozen opposition members, including Guaidó.[1] Also targeted were three staff members of opposition presidential candidate [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]], charged with conspiring against the referendum.[1]

The warrants accompanied Maduro's placement of the disputed territory under military jurisdiction, a step that jeopardized [[exxonmobil|Exxon]] and [[chevron|Chevron]] operations there, while Guyana conducted joint military drills with the United States.[1]

## Self-declaration as interim president

On January 23, 2019, Guaidó — then a 35-year-old industrial engineer and president of the National Assembly — declared himself interim president of [[venezuela|Venezuela]] in a Caracas plaza.[2] Within hours the United States recognized him, followed by the European Union, Canada, Brazil, and most of Latin America, while [[russia|Russia]] and [[china|China]] stood by Maduro.[2] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] calls him "the Alexei Navalny of Venezuela" and the product of a decade-long project overseen by Washington's regime change trainers.[2]

## The parallel government

What followed, in DeLarme's account, was a masterclass in 21st-century regime-change theater.[2] The [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] froze $7 billion in [[citgo|Citgo]] assets — the U.S. refining subsidiary of [[pdvsa|PDVSA]] — and handed control to Guaidó's shadow government.[2] On February 23, 2019, Guaidó orchestrated a televised "humanitarian aid" convoy at the Colombia–Venezuela border; U.S.-branded trucks loaded with [[usaid|USAID]] rice and medicine were blocked by Venezuelan troops and the aid burned, with both sides accusing the other of arson, and declassified cables later revealed USAID had budgeted $60 million for the operation, including payments to Colombian protesters.[2] In May 2020, [[operation-gideon|Operation Gideon]] — a seaborne invasion led by former Green Beret [[jordan-goudreau|Jordan Goudreau]] and funded by Venezuelan exiles in Florida — was crushed within hours; Goudreau claimed Guaidó had signed a $213 million contract, which Guaidó denied.[2]

## Collapse

The Guaidó experiment collapsed by 2021.[2] Hyperinflation had been tamed through partial dollarization, but the parallel president governed exactly zero square meters of Venezuelan soil.[2] The Biden administration quietly phased out recognition in 2023, shifting to "maximum pressure lite" while negotiating oil-for-debt swaps with [[chevron|Chevron]].[2] DeLarme describes [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] as Guaidó's spiritual successor.[2]

His party, [[popular-will|Popular Will]], was characterized by Dr. Jill Stein in February 2019 as U.S.-funded and "the most violent faction of Venezuela's rightwing opposition."[2]
