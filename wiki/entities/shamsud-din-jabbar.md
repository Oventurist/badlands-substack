# Shamsud-Din Jabbar

Shamsud-Din Jabbar was identified by police and federal investigators as the man who carried out the New Year's Day 2025 vehicle-ramming attack on Bourbon Street in New Orleans, driving a truck flying an ISIS flag into a crowd of revelers in the early hours of January 1, 2025.[1] Initial reporting placed the death toll at fifteen killed and more than thirty-five injured, with contemporaneous accounts describing "more than a dozen" dead and dozens wounded.[1]

## FBI assessment

On January 2, 2025, Christopher Raia, deputy assistant director of the [[fbi|FBI]]'s counterterrorism division, told reporters that investigators believed Jabbar acted alone and that the incident was "an act of terrorism," describing it as "premeditated and an evil act."[1] Raia said the Bureau did not assess that anyone else was involved and expressed confidence that there were "no accomplices," a notable departure from the FBI's messaging a day earlier, when the agency had warned that Jabbar may not have acted alone and cited "significant new developments."[1] Raia also said Jabbar had posted several videos before the attack professing support for ISIS.[1]

## Houston mosque and CAIR referral

A mosque near Jabbar's Houston home, Masjid Bilal, posted an Instagram story after his identity was released condemning the "terrible acts" while instructing congregants not to respond to media inquiries and, "if approached by the FBI and a response is necessary," to refer to the [[council-on-american-islamic-relations|Council on American-Islamic Relations]] (CAIR) and the [[islamic-society-of-greater-houston|Islamic Society of Greater Houston]].[1] Breitbart characterized CAIR as a radical organization that had previously assisted terrorists' families, citing the 2015 case in which the group's local branch aided the families of the ISIS-affiliated attackers who carried out the San Bernardino mass shooting.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writers treated the official narrative around Jabbar with open skepticism. GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that the public was being conditioned toward renewed Middle East intervention, and pointed to details he read as evidence of a sloppily staged scene: users on X observed that the ISIS flag on Jabbar's truck was mounted upside down, which he suggested meant either the attacker could not read Arabic or that those staging the scene were poorly trained.[1] He likewise questioned press reports that bomb-making materials were found at Jabbar's house next to a Quran opened to a passage on martyrdom, and criticized the apparent absence of crime-scene control that allowed reporters to walk through the property.[1]

Burning Bright framed the Bourbon Street attack as one narrative layer among several, noting that it was followed by an "absurdly memetic" Tesla Cybertruck incident outside a Trump property and came directly after a media push to split [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] optically.[1] He argued that observers need not know what happened on an actual level to see that the public was tracking the threads binding the narratives together.[1]

## FBI point-of-view video

In early January 2025 the [[fbi|FBI]] released a nearly four-minute silent compilation of video showing Jabbar apparently planning the [[new-orleans-bourbon-street-attack|New Year's Day attack]], drawn from area surveillance cameras and from footage he recorded himself using Meta smart glasses.[2] The agency said he had visited New Orleans twice, in October and November, before staging the attack.[2]

The first clip, dated October 31, shows Jabbar filming from his glasses while riding a bicycle through the French Quarter; a second October clip shows him testing the glasses by recording himself looking into a mirror at the home he rented.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that the resulting footage could double as an advertisement for [[meta-platforms|Meta]] glasses, part of a pattern of emerging technology featuring in mass trauma events.[2]

## Citizenship and the border narrative

In "Hysteria and Error," Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] emphasized that Jabbar was identified as a 42-year-old United States citizen and Army veteran, a fact he said was obscured by conservative commentary linking the attack to illegal immigration.[3] DeLarme noted that the perpetrators of both New Year's incidents — Jabbar in New Orleans and [[matthew-alan-livelsberger|Matthew Livelsberger]] in Las Vegas — were American citizens who had served in the armed forces.[3]

DeLarme described Jabbar as a previously banal figure who worked as a manager of residential apartment buildings, served in the Army and held paper-pushing jobs, with family members saying his focus on extremist versions of Islam had emerged only within the previous year.[3] He criticized a [[fox-news|Fox News]] report, echoed by Louisiana attorney general [[liz-murrill|Liz Murrill]], claiming the suspect had driven across the border; the vehicle in fact never crossed, having merely been driven around Eagle Pass more than a month earlier, and Fox subsequently corrected the report.[3] DeLarme also observed that at the time of writing [[isis|ISIS]] had not claimed responsibility for the attack, and mocked the [[fbi|FBI]] under [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] for a press conference he said replayed the righteous outrage act of the post-9/11 era.[3]
