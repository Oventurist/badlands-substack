# Media Matters for America

Media Matters for America is a left-leaning American nonprofit media watchdog organization, characterized in Badlands Media coverage as a Democratic Party activist group that has operated for roughly two decades and has been one of the most influential organizations in political media.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) It was founded by [[david-brock|David Brock]], a former conservative writer whose political reversal in the late 1990s preceded the organization's creation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

## Origins and purpose

A theory advanced by independent journalists holds that Brock founded Media Matters both as a vehicle to funnel money into the Democratic establishment and to build an effective media pressure group.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) The organization proved a powerful instrument for what the article calls the "Clinton cartel": during Hillary Clinton's 2008 primary contest against Barack Obama and again in 2016, Media Matters was described as fighting to secure her victory at any cost and attacking anyone even slightly critical of her.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

## Methods

The article characterizes Media Matters' modus operandi as the elimination of political debate rather than participation in it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) Two techniques are singled out: labeling anything that detracts from establishment liberalism as racist, homophobic, antisemitic, white-nationalist or transphobic; and conducting what the author calls dubious "studies" whose conclusions are then cited as authority, exploiting a public disposition to believe almost any claim prefaced by the words "study finds."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

A related tactic is advertiser pressure. Media Matters, the [[anti-defamation-league|ADL]] and similar organizations contact corporations that advertise on platforms permitting open speech and argue that such placement risks associating the brand with bigotry or hate speech.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) Where a letter to legacy platforms such as Twitter, Facebook or Google once sufficed to secure content removal, free-speech platforms including [[x-platform|X]] and [[rumble|Rumble]] have required more elaborate approaches.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

## Shift in mainstream treatment

Outlets once accurately described Media Matters as a liberal or Democratic activist group; establishment publications such as the Washington Post and the New York Times, the article argues, now treat it as an authoritative and near-infallible arbiter of truth.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) This shift is attributed directly to the emergence of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the America First contingent.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) The [[international-fact-checking-network|International Fact-Checking Network]] and its merger with legacy media are framed as a parallel response to anti-establishment populism, a constituency that has expanded to include supporters of Robert F. Kennedy Jr., Cornel West and Vivek Ramaswamy.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

## The X and Rumble reports

In November 2023 Media Matters published a report headlined "As Musk endorses antisemitic conspiracy theory, X has been placing ads for Apple, Bravo, IBM, Oracle, and Xfinity next to pro-Nazi content."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) The article criticizes the report's opening assertion that [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] was in a "descent into white nationalist and antisemitic conspiracy theories" as unsupported, and argues that the accompanying screenshots were manufactured by repeatedly refreshing the feeds of extremely obscure accounts until a major brand's advertisement appeared.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) The report precipitated Musk's "thermonuclear lawsuit."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

An earlier March 2023 Media Matters piece claimed that "Ads for Netflix are appearing next to Holocaust denial videos on Rumble."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) Rumble's traffic data reportedly showed that the Media Matters employee was the only human to view the instance of the video carrying the Netflix advertisement, that average watch time was one minute and ten seconds, and that the video had zero engagement until 14 March — the publication date of the Media Matters article — after which views rose to a peak of roughly thirty.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) The author notes the irony that by Media Matters' own reasoning the organization was itself responsible for a large proportional increase in consumption of Holocaust-denial content.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) [[netflix|Netflix]] nonetheless withdrew its advertising from Rumble.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

## Legal exposure

Media Matters faces the lawsuit filed by Musk, a public statement by Rumble founder [[chris-pavlovski|Chris Pavlovski]] alleging an identical hit job against his platform, and an investigation into potential fraudulent activity opened by Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism) The article treats these developments as a boomerang returning to Brock and the organization, and expresses hope that the Poynter Institute, the ADL, the Southern Poverty Law Center and the Center for Countering Digital Hate will face comparable legal scrutiny.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-cancer-on-modern-journalism)

## X Corp. lawsuit and Texas investigation (November 2023)

On Monday, 20 November 2023, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[x-corp|X]] followed through on his weekend pledge to file a "thermonuclear lawsuit" against Media Matters and "all those who colluded" for "completely misrepresenting" the real user experience on the platform.[2] The complaint, filed in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Fort Worth Division, alleged that the organization's tactics were manipulative and deceptive.[2] In the interval between Musk's announcement and the filing, pro-censorship commentators publicly speculated that he would not follow through, with one CNBC reporter suggesting "Musk may have been lying, like he has done before."[2]

In parallel, Texas Attorney General [[ken-paxton|Ken Paxton]] announced that the Office of the Attorney General was opening an investigation into Media Matters for potential fraudulent activity under the Texas Business Organizations Code and the Deceptive Trade Practices Act.[2] Paxton described Media Matters as "a radical anti-free speech organization" and said he was "extremely troubled by the allegations" that it had fraudulently manipulated data on X.com.[2] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the twin actions as a developing "lawsuit cascade" in the [[information-war|Info War]] and as the beginning of a template-setting campaign against those who had assaulted constitutional free-speech rights ahead of the 2024 election.[2]

## X lawsuit and Carusone blog posts (2023)

On Monday, 20 November 2023, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[x-corp|X]] sued Media Matters, claiming the [[david-brock|David Brock]]-founded group had manipulated the platform to display major advertisements next to Nazi imagery.[3] The complaint alleged "the end result was a feed precision-designed by Media Matters for a single purpose: to produce side-by-side ad/content placements that it could screenshot in an effort to alienate advertisers," and that all but one of the companies featured in the Media Matters piece withdrew all advertising from X, including Apple, Comcast, NBCUniversal and IBM.[3]

Coverage characterized the Media Matters campaign as a successful attempt to brand Musk and his platform as antisemitic, after Musk drew fire for agreeing with a post about liberal Jews.[3] Days later, blog posts written in the early 2000s by Media Matters president [[angelo-carusone|Angelo Carusone]] — originally surfaced by the Daily Caller's Peter Hasson in 2019 and described as "super antisemitic" — recirculated widely.[3] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary framed the sequence as an "Ouroboros" spiral within the Media Industrial Complex, educating the public on both the coordination and the hypocrisy of the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s narrative arm.[3] See [[x-corp-v-media-matters|X Corp. v. Media Matters]].[3]

## Missouri investigation (December 2023)

On Monday, December 11, 2023, Republican Missouri Attorney General [[andrew-bailey|Andrew Bailey]] formally opened an investigation into Media Matters for America over its allegedly fraudulent solicitation of donations from Missouri residents amid its campaign against [[x-corp|X]].[4] In a letter to Media Matters president and CEO [[angelo-carusone|Angelo Carusone]], Bailey wrote that "a federal lawsuit has been filed against Media Matters, raising serious allegations that your firm falsely and deceptively manipulated the algorithm on X (formerly known as Twitter) through coordinated, inauthentic behavior and that you did so in an attempt to defame the organization and cause advertisers to pull their support from the platform, thus harming free speech."[4] The investigation came three weeks after [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] sued the group for swaying corporations into pulling advertising.[4]

The organization's November 2023 reports claiming corporate ads on X were running beside "white nationalist and pro-Nazi content" prompted IBM, [[apple|Apple]], Disney, Lionsgate Entertainment, Sony and other firms to boycott the platform, after which Musk responded with what he called "thermonuclear" litigation.[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] wrote that the group had moved from legal efforts undermining individual liberty to "outright fraud," arguing that leftist NGOs must fabricate instances of hate speech to fulfill their mission and calling Media Matters "the Jesse Smollett of media organizations."[4] She added that had comparable conduct come from the political right, someone would be going to prison, while expressing doubt that equal justice would suddenly prevail.[4]

## Attempt to halt the Missouri investigation (May 2024)

In May 2024, Missouri Attorney General [[andrew-bailey|Andrew Bailey]] said he had filed a motion to block Media Matters from shutting down a Missouri court, after the organization filed a motion in federal court in Washington, D.C. to halt his investigation and lawsuit into its allegedly fraudulent business practices.[5] Bailey had sued Media Matters in March to force it to turn over documents related to the investigation, with litigation continuing in state court.[5] "D.C. courts have no control over Missouri courts. I have filed a motion to keep this suit in Missouri, where it belongs. I will not allow Media Matters to run to a D.C. court to evade Missouri law," Bailey said.[5]

According to the attorney general, Media Matters came under scrutiny over allegations that it deceitfully manipulated the algorithm of [[x-corp|X]] (formerly Twitter) so that advertisers' content appeared beside controversial posts, causing the platform to suffer major financial losses as advertisers withdrew.[5] Media Matters subsequently remained outspoken in efforts to defame the platform over its refusal to censor disfavored viewpoints.[5] The Missouri investigation began in November 2023 after evidence emerged regarding solicited donations, conduct the attorney general said violated the Missouri Merchandising Practices Act.[5]
