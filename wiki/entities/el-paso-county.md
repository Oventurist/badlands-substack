# El Paso County, Colorado

El Paso County is the most populous county in Colorado and the only county in the United States that is home to five military commands, a status that Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described as making it the capital of the Military Industrial Complex within the state.[1] It comprises Colorado's fifth congressional district and has been a focal point of both grassroots Republican organizing and election-integrity disputes.[1]

## Precinct organizing

El Paso County was the site of the most significant [[precinct-strategy|precinct strategy]] effort in Colorado, as longtime [[america-first-movement|America First]] Republicans held coffee-shop meetups in early 2021 to educate newcomers and fill vacant precinct committeeman positions, succeeding in taking over the party apparatus in several counties.[1] Representative [[doug-lamborn|Doug Lamborn]], who represents the fifth district, declined to participate in the 2022 assembly process and petitioned onto the primary ballot instead, a route Ashe in America attributed to his unpopularity among Republicans in his own district.[1]

## Role at the 2022 assembly

At the [[2022-colorado-republican-state-assembly|2022 state assembly and convention]], El Paso County delegates received their [[edivote100-clickers|EdiVote100 clickers]] last of all counties, in a distribution process that integrity-focused delegates described as "chaotic and insecure" and that delayed the start of voting statewide.[1] County chair Vickie Tonkins confirmed that in El Paso County the clickers were neither assigned by delegate nor tracked, in contrast to counties such as Routt where delegates had to sign the devices out.[1]

## 2022 recount

The county was also the venue for the [[2022-el-paso-county-recount|2022 El Paso County recount]], covering four races, where Logic and Accuracy Testing produced a roughly 57% ballot adjudication rate and observers reported an election worker altering original primary batch totals to match recount figures, as well as ballot boxes with broken seals.[1] Recounts for County Clerk and Recorder candidate Peter Lupia and Coroner candidate Dr. Rae Ann Weber were overseen by El Paso County Clerk and Recorder Chuck Broerman, while the State Senate recount for Lynda Zamora Wilson fell under Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]].[1]
