# Shanghai Cooperation Organization

The Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) is a Eurasian political, economic and security bloc that Badlands Media commentary treats as one of the principal institutional vehicles of the emerging [[multipolar-world|multipolar world]].[1]

## Role in the International North-South Transport Corridor

In June 2024 commentary on NATO's contingency planning for a European war with [[russia|Russia]], contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the [[north-south-transport-corridor|International North-South Transport Corridor]] had been organized by the SCO.[1] He described the corridor as a project that would ultimately divert freight and oil traffic away from the Suez Canal — which he characterized as controlled by London oligarchs — and route it instead through Iran's port at Bandar Abbas.[1] Egypt, Saudi Arabia and the wider Arab League were said to be investing in the new port, a development he framed as "a huge middle finger to London and the Globalist Empire."[1]

On this reading, the corridor is the "death nail" for [[nato|NATO]] and Vladimir Putin's checkmate against the Anglo-American empire, with Germany — the anchor of the European economy — depicted as dependent on Russian energy notwithstanding official policy.[1]

## 2024 Astana summit

The SCO's annual session for 2024 ran Wednesday through Thursday in Astana, the capital of [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]], and hosted the second face-to-face meeting in as many months between Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]].[2] Putin told Xi that "Russian-Chinese relations of comprehensive partnership and strategic cooperation are in the best period of their history," a characterisation Russian state media likened to a golden age in relations.[2]

"Russia-China relations are on the highest level in history, and they are not aimed against anyone, we do not create blocs or unions, we just act in the interests of our nations," Putin stressed, while Xi said in brief opening remarks that "in the face of the turbulent international situation and external environment, the two sides should continue to uphold the original aspiration of friendship for generations to come."[2] ZeroHedge assessed that the two were nonetheless drawing closer principally to counter the United States while competing for influence among the bloc's Central Asian members.[2]

The Central Asian membership includes the ex-Soviet republics of Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan; Russia and China are founding members, India and Pakistan joined in 2017, and Iran was admitted as the newest member the previous year.[2] Badlands commentary by [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the summit as the culmination of a Putin tour of allied leaders — [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]], [[mohammed-bin-salman|Saudi Crown Prince MBS]], [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]], [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orban]] and [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] — and read Xi as Putin's peer in what it calls the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], missing only a returning [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[2]

## Saudi partial membership (2023)

On 29 March 2023 the Saudi state news agency reported that the government of [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] had approved partial membership in the organization, described by the Wall Street Journal as a political and security bloc including [[china|China]], [[russia|Russia]] and India.[3] The decision came ahead of a May meeting of foreign ministers from the grouping, at which [[iran|Iran]] had recently gained full member status.[3]

Analysts framed the move as part of a broader effort by Saudi Arabia and neighbouring Persian Gulf states to expand diplomatic partnerships beyond traditional Western allies amid uncertainty about Washington's long-term security commitment to the energy-rich region.[3] It followed the China-brokered restoration of Saudi–Iranian relations and Riyadh's Russia-mediated talks on resuming ties with [[syria|Syria]].[3] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] argued that the logical next step was Saudi accession to [[brics|BRICS]], with attendant consequences for the dollar.[3]

## 2025 Tianjin summit

Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] hosted the 2025 SCO leaders' summit in Tianjin, welcoming Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Indian Prime Minister [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]] among more than 20 heads of state and representatives of ten international organizations.[4] The gathering unfolded against the backdrop of the Ukraine conflict, [[israel|Israel]]'s war in Gaza, and trade disputes sparked by U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s tariff policies, and its agenda covered regional security, economic cooperation, cultural ties, transport, energy, digitalization, AI, and green development.[4] Putin was scheduled to travel on to Beijing for talks with Xi on trade, security, Ukraine, and relations with the United States.[4]

The summit was timed alongside a parade marking the 80th anniversary of the WWII victory, with China having fought on the Allied side.[4] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Xi had parlayed the commemoration into a summit for an organization that had become "the instrument of destruction" for a unipolar hegemony he traced back to the East India Trading Company and, earlier, Venetian and London banking dynasties.[4] He also noted the anticipated arrival of North Korean leader [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]], reading Kim's western suit and slimmed appearance as signals ahead of an expected meeting with Trump.[4]

In related commentary on Türkiye's severing of trade with Israel, the same author described the SCO's North–South Transportation Corridor as the trade route "breaking the back of the Unipolar Hegemony," with Baku, Azerbaijan as the key Caspian node connecting China, India and Iran to Russia, and predicted that [[turkey|Türkiye]] would eventually join the SCO, linking Asia and the Middle East to Europe.[4]
