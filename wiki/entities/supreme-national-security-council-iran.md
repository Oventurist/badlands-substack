# Supreme National Security Council (Iran)

The Supreme National Security Council is the [[iran|Iranian]] body responsible for assessing whether the country should go to war or agree to a ceasefire, according to Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]].[1] Araghchi said in a July 2026 interview that while the council carries out that assessment, the final decision rests with the Supreme Leader.[1]

Araghchi described facing criticism from rival political factions following a temporary understanding with the United States, and said Iran agreed to resume negotiations after Washington again demanded zero uranium enrichment and threatened military action — a return to talks intended, he said, to demonstrate that Iran had exhausted diplomatic options.[1]

## Continuity of government role

The council played a central role in Iran's 2026 wartime preparations, designating successors for key military and civilian officials to prevent leadership gaps in a crisis and drawing on protocols developed in past wartime periods.[2] In early August of the preceding year, Iranian media quoting the council's Secretariat reported that the leadership had approved the establishment of a National Defense Council within the framework of Article 176 of the Iranian Constitution.[2] Deputy Interior Minister for Security and Law Enforcement and National Security Council Secretary Ali Akbar Pour Jamshidian stated on September 27 that the council had directed all Iranian military and civilian officials to designate several successors to ensure continuity of governance in the event of leadership disruption.[2]

Iranian reporting from the same period indicated that the council itself might undergo structural changes focused on the "separation of defense and security affairs from each other," with emphasis on rebuilding the country's intelligence and security institutions.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the resulting arrangement as a [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] protocol closely resembling [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]]'s Devolution Theory, and speculated the institutional rebuild reflected an Iranian conclusion that its security apparatus had been penetrated.[2] See [[iran-continuity-of-government-measures-2026|Iran continuity of government measures (2026)]].[2]
