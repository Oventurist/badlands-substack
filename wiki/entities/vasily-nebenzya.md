# Vasily Nebenzya

Vasily Nebenzya is [[russia|Russia]]'s ambassador to the [[united-nations|United Nations]].[1]

## February 2026 nuclear allegation

At the [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]], Nebenzya claimed that the [[united-kingdom|United Kingdom]] and [[france|France]] were considering transferring nuclear weapons capabilities to [[ukraine|Ukraine]], alleging that Western governments were exploring ways to "circumvent" nonproliferation restrictions in order to arm Kyiv.[1]

He did not present publicly verifiable evidence during the session. Neither the UK nor France has announced any plan to provide nuclear weapons to Ukraine. Both are recognized nuclear-armed states under the [[nuclear-non-proliferation-treaty|Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty]], while Ukraine relinquished its inherited Soviet nuclear arsenal in the 1990s under the [[budapest-memorandum|Budapest Memorandum]].[1]

The claim was reported alongside President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s assertion that Russian security services had uncovered a Ukrainian-linked plot to sabotage Black Sea gas pipeline infrastructure.[1] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the sequence as evidence of desperation among [[european-union|EU]] elites losing a kinetic war, and connected it to the [[nuclear-scare-event|nuclear scare event]] anticipated in [[qanon|Q]] posts.[1]

## Statements on Ukrainian organ transplant legislation

In September 2023, Nebenzya, in his capacity as Russia's ambassador to the United Nations, reported that Ukraine had passed legislation expanding the black market for [[organ-trafficking|organ transplants]] by eliminating long-standing requirements of written consent for organ extraction from living and deceased donors.[2] He specifically alleged that organs were being extracted from children.[2] The claim, carried by [[tass|TASS]], was cited by Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] as part of an argument that Western reluctance-then-capitulation on Ukraine policy serves to conceal criminal enterprises implicating officials in Washington.[2]
