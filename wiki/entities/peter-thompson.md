# Peter Thompson

Peter Thompson is the Maricopa County Superior Court judge who presided over [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]], the election challenge brought by Arizona Republican gubernatorial candidate [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] against Governor [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]].[1]

## Role in Lake v. Hobbs

Following closing arguments on May 19, 2023, in which Lake attorney [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]] argued that at least 70,000 mail-in ballots had not been verified in accordance with Arizona law, the case awaited Thompson's ruling.[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the outcome would turn on how Thompson interpreted the legal standard and Lake's burden of proof, with the two sides advancing sharply different definitions of what Lake was required to prove about [[signature-verification|signature verification]].[1]

Because Thompson had taken fewer than three days to rule the first time the matter came before him in December 2022, Badlands expected a decision within days, and anticipated that the losing party would appeal.[1] Legal analysts quoted over the weekend speculated that there had been enough missteps in the lower courts for the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|US Supreme Court]] to take up an eventual appeal.[1]

## May 2023 remand rulings

Ahead of the remanded trial, Thompson reaffirmed a previous minute entry setting trial dates of May 17, 18 and 19, 2023 on Lake's fraudulent signature verification claim.[2] He ruled that motions by Hobbs and [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] to dismiss Count III were "not persuasive," while dismissing Count II concerning intentional election day tabulator failures and declining to reconsider it in light of newly proffered evidence, on the reasoning that the count had already been fully adjudicated at the December 2022 trial.[2] His order required Lake to demonstrate that the county's higher level signature reviewers "conducted no signature verification or curing and in so doing had systematically failed to materially comply with the law," and additionally to "prove a competent mathematical basis to win" — standards Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described as unusually high.[2]
