# Eric Adams

Eric Adams is an American politician who served as Mayor of New York City immediately prior to [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]].[1] Adams was indicted on federal charges on September 26, 2024, a date that later became a legal and administrative dividing line for his mayoral actions.[1]

On January 1, 2026, Mamdani issued an executive order revoking all remaining executive orders signed by Adams on or after his September 26, 2024 indictment date, a move expected to affect a variety of city policies.[1] The revocation accompanied Mamdani's separate order revitalizing the [[mayors-office-to-protect-tenants|Mayor's Office to Protect Tenants]].[1]

## 2024 federal investigation of the Adams administration

In September 2024, weeks before his own indictment, Adams' administration was engulfed by a federal investigation in which agents raided the homes of several senior officials, including Police Commissioner [[edward-caban|Edward Caban]], Deputy Mayor for Public Safety [[philip-banks-iii|Philip Banks III]], and Deputy Mayor [[sheena-wright|Sheena Wright]].[2] Caban resigned on September 12, 2024, effective the following day, telling the [[new-york-city-police-department|NYPD]] that coverage of the raids had "created a distraction."[2] Adams said he respected Caban's decision and praised departmental achievements under his leadership, including the removal of more than 18,000 illegal guns from the streets and major reductions in shootings and robberies.[2] He appointed [[tom-donilon|Tom Donilon]] to replace Caban.[2]

Federal authorities remained tight-lipped and, according to CBS News, had not indicated that Adams' inner circle itself was being raided.[2] Badlands nevertheless argued that the affair reeked of nepotism and organized crime, pointing to two sets of brothers among those raided and to [[terrence-banks|Terrence Banks]]' firm representing companies with city contracts, and concluded that the mayor's office was "neck deep" in a scandal of public-private partnership corruption.[2]

## Migrant busing crisis (2023)

Amid the busing of migrants from border states to northeastern [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary cities]], Adams warned that the influx "is going to destroy our city."[3] Comedian [[bill-maher|Bill Maher]] recounted in an October 2023 monologue that Adams had traveled to Mexico, Ecuador and Colombia to tell prospective migrants personally that New York had no room for them — a message Maher called "pretty amazing" given the city's own "if you can make it here, you can make it anywhere" mythology.[3]

## Backlash over migrant housing (2023)

By August 2023 more than 100,000 migrants had entered New York City since April 2022, overwhelming the government's capacity to house and feed them and generating resentment toward measures Adams had approved.[4] These included removing wedding parties from hotels, shipping migrants to suburban counties, and housing adult men in the gymnasiums of actively used elementary schools.[4] The city also placed 3,000 migrants in a tent city on Randall's Island at a reported cost of $20 million per month, after converting youth soccer fields and other recreational assets to migrant housing.[4]

On Sunday, August 27, 2023, police were called to Gracie Mansion when black-clad [[antifa|Antifa]] counterprotesters crashed a demonstration against Adams' handling of the crisis, producing street fighting; the rally was headlined by Guardian Angels founder [[curtis-sliwa|Curtis Sliwa]].[4] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the clash was staged groundwork for immigration-driven [[new-york-city-migrant-crisis|social unrest]] in the following election year.[4]

## Federal pressure on NYPD leadership (September 2024)

In September 2024 federal investigators raided the homes of two successive NYPD commissioners connected to Adams' administration: [[edward-caban|Edward Caban]], who resigned over corruption allegations involving his brother and others in the mayor's office, and then interim commissioner [[thomas-donlon|Thomas Donlon]] on September 20.[5] The New York Post speculated that the Donlon raid was federal authorities sending a message to Adams, while Badlands commentary suggested a wider [[southern-district-of-new-york|SDNY]] case was entering a public-disclosure phase and questioned how a RICO prosecution of the network would be brought.[5]

## Expansion of the Turkey probe (2024)

In September 2024 the New York Post reported that federal prosecutors had demanded City Hall produce all communications between the Adams administration and five additional foreign countries, a drastic expansion of the investigation into Turkish campaign donations.[6] The requests came in a July slate of subpoenas and offered the first detailed look into the [[justice-department|US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York]]'s investigations targeting Adams and his allies since the raids on his top campaign fundraiser the previous November.[6] The subpoenas sought details on all interactions between administration officials and Israel, China, Qatar, South Korea and Uzbekistan.[6] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the Adams matter, alongside cases involving [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] and Sean "P. Diddy" Combs, as evidence that [[racketeer-influenced-and-corrupt-organizations-act|RICO]] had come into sharp public focus.[6]

## Superseding indictment signals (October 2024)

At a status hearing on October 2, 2024, Assistant U.S. Attorney Hagan Scotten said federal prosecutors were pursuing "several related investigations" that could expand the case against Adams to include additional charges and co-defendants, calling a superseding indictment "quite likely" and further charges against Adams himself "possible."[7] Adams, the first sitting New York City mayor to face criminal charges, had pleaded not guilty and faced up to 45 years in prison if convicted on all counts, which included bribery and fraud.[7] His legal team had filed a motion on September 30 to dismiss the bribery charge, arguing the alleged conduct did not meet the threshold for bribery, and accused the federal government of leaking information about the investigation.[7] U.S. District Judge Dale E. Ho did not immediately set a trial date; prosecutors estimated the trial would take about four weeks.[7] See [[eric-adams-federal-corruption-case|Eric Adams federal corruption case]].[7]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the related investigations and prospective superseding indictment suggested the mayor's RICO scandal was "just getting started," and argued the case had the potential to publicly deconstruct the public-private partnerships running the country — a network of elected and appointed officials, law enforcement, NGOs and corporations implicated in bribery, corruption and fixing.[7] She concluded that corruption had been the status quo far beyond the mayor's office and New York, and that "the only way to restore public trust is accountability."[7]

## Cancelled border trip (March 2024)

On Saturday, March 23, 2024, Adams cancelled a planned trip to the southern US border after the [[state-department|State Department]] raised safety concerns about a Mexican city on his itinerary.[8] The visit — his second as mayor — had been arranged at the invitation of Sister Norma Pimentel, executive director of Catholic Charities of the Rio Grande, and Adams had said he would meet "national immigration leaders who all applaud what we have done."[8] It was announced as Adams repeatedly pressed state and federal governments for additional aid with the migrant influx: more than 184,000 migrants had passed through New York City's intake system since April 2022 and more than 64,000 remained in the city's care, straining the city's right-to-shelter rules, with officials agreeing the previous week to cap shelter stays for migrant adults at 30 or 60 days.[8] A City Hall spokesperson said the trip was cancelled "due to safety concerns at one of the cities we were going to visit in Mexico."[8]

## FBI seizure of phones (November 2023)

In early November 2023, [[fbi|F.B.I.]] agents seized Adams's electronic devices in what appeared to be a dramatic escalation of a criminal inquiry into whether his 2021 mayoral campaign conspired with the Turkish government and others to funnel money into its coffers.[9] Agents approached the mayor after an event in Manhattan on a Monday evening, asked his security detail to step away, climbed into his S.U.V. with him and, pursuant to a court-authorized warrant, took his devices — at least two cellphones and an iPad — which were returned within a matter of days after investigators copied their data.[9] A lawyer for Adams and his campaign said the mayor was cooperating with federal authorities and had already "proactively reported" at least one instance of improper behavior.[9]

Adams attributed the scrutiny to systemic racism, stating that "Black candidates for office are often held to a higher, unfair standard — especially those from lower-income backgrounds such as myself," and that no campaign of his had ever been charged with a serious fund-raising violation nor had any contribution affected his decision-making as a public official.[9]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] criticized the New York Times coverage for devoting its opening paragraphs to framing the allegations as implausible before detailing at least five influence-peddling schemes Adams had already been caught up in since entering the political arena.[9] She questioned the characterization of those investigations — many leading to criminal indictments — as unserious fund-raising violations, while allowing that Adams "might have a point about the race thing" given that predecessor [[bill-de-blasio|Bill de Blasio]] was investigated in 2017 without charges and that the Biden family had escaped consequences for what she considered far more serious corruption.[9] She added that it was "curious to see investigators looking at Democrats" given the resources she said were devoted to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and other political opponents.[9]

## Indictment (September 2024)

A Manhattan grand jury indicted Adams on federal criminal charges, multiple sources familiar with the matter confirmed to Fox News in late September 2024, with the details of the indictment remaining under seal and sources suggesting it could be unsealed as early as the following day.[10] The [[fbi|FBI]] and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] declined to comment, and it was unclear who Adams had retained as his personal attorney.[10] In a statement obtained by the Associated Press, Adams said, "I always knew that if I stood my ground for New Yorkers that I would be a target — and a target I became," adding, "If I am charged, I am innocent, and I will fight this with every ounce of my strength and spirit."[10] Several people in the orbit of the 63-year-old former NYPD captain had already been raided.[10]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that New York City had become Gotham and noted that Adams had recently defected from [[uniparty|Uniparty]] ranks and begun speaking out about illegal immigrants being shipped to the city from the border, while doubting that this explained the charges and floating the possibility that Adams was cooperating.[10] He said he would nonetheless accept the narrative deployment that the case was Democrat retribution for questioning the central narrative, and framed the surrounding chaos as [[fifth-generation-warfare|Fifth Generation Warfare]] waged by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] against Uniparty elites through lawfare and elusive narrative deployments.[10]

## Lawsuit against migrant bus companies

In January 2024 the Adams administration sued 17 charter bus companies over their collective transport of roughly 33,600 migrants from Texas to New York City, arguing that the carriers should be held liable for the $708 million the city has spent caring for those passengers.[11] The complaint alleged that the operators had earned "millions of dollars" while acting in "bad faith" to carry out Texas Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]]'s agenda.[11] In March 2024, [[roadrunner-charters|Roadrunner Charters]] agreed to stop transporting illegal immigrants to the city while the [[new-york-city-migrant-busing-lawsuit|litigation]] proceeds.[11]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the suit improper, noting the companies were executing a lawful contract with another government entity, and argued that Adams would have to sue Texas directly for relief — a step that would force litigation over New York's [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary city]] declaration and could end the sanctuary city model more broadly.[11]

## "Lone wolf" warning after the October 7 attacks

At a press conference on Tuesday, October 10, 2023, alongside city officials, Adams warned New Yorkers to beware of "lone wolves" in the wake of the deadly [[october-7-attacks|attacks on Israel]] by [[hamas|Hamas]] over the preceding weekend.[12] He said law enforcement would be monitoring social media sites for threats against religious communities as the fighting in Israel and Gaza continued, and cautioned that statements posted on social platforms could exacerbate the problem by radicalizing individuals online.[12] "I think it aggravates the problem and don't underestimate lone wolves," Adams said. "You know, people who are who are being radicalized online. And when you put out statements that I have read on some of these sites, you can motivate terrible behaviors."[12] He urged residents to be on "high alert" and to inform a law enforcement officer if they saw anything suspicious.[12] [[new-york-city-police-department|NYPD]] Chief of Patrol [[john-chell|John Chell]] said at the same event that the department was moving an incredible amount of resources to protect the Jewish community, churches and religious institutions, and had held community calls with Jewish and Muslim faith leaders.[12]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that a growing share of the public reads such headlines as a threat rather than a warning, framing "lone wolf" messaging as predictive programming and Hegelian narrative management rather than organic concern.[12] He wrote that he did not believe the domestic Deep State retained much kinetic power but cautioned against assuming so, advising readers to watch headlines as much for what they threaten as for what they warn, and to "keep your head when those about you are losing theirs" during what he called a chaotic blitzkrieg in the [[information-war|Info War]].[12]

## December 2023 appeal over migrant busing

On Wednesday, December 27, 2023, Adams joined the mayors of Chicago and Denver in renewing pleas for more federal help and for coordination with Texas over the growing number of asylum-seekers arriving in their cities by bus and plane.[13] Texas Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]]'s busing operation had transported more than 80,000 migrants to Democratic-led cities since the previous year and had recently been expanded with chartered planes.[13] The mayors sharply criticized Abbott, saying buses arrived at all hours and outside designated drop-off zones with no details on who was aboard.[13]

"We cannot allow buses with people needing our help to arrive without warning at any hour of day and night," Adams said at a virtual news conference with the other mayors. "This not only prevents us from providing assistance in an orderly way, it puts those who have already suffered in so much in danger."[13] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] criticized the mayors' framing — particularly the complaint about "the visualization of this crisis" hitting their streets — as an admission that their concern was optics rather than the crisis itself, noting that all of the cities involved were [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary cities]].[13]

## Migrant prepaid card program

In February 2024 Adams faced scrutiny after it emerged that he had approved a reported $53 million pilot to distribute prepaid cards to migrant families housed in city hotels, a program he defended at a state legislative budget hearing in Albany as containing cost-saving measures.[15] He clarified that the cards were prepaid food cards rather than American Express credit cards, argued the approach would improve food distribution efficiency and cut migrant support costs by 20 percent, and said the 500-person pilot was expected to save roughly $6.7 million annually.[15] Critics noted that the roughly $350 per month per migrant exceeded SNAP assistance available to low-income New Yorkers by about 40 percent and also exceeded monthly services for single veterans in the state.[15] The initiative is covered in detail at [[new-york-city-migrant-prepaid-card-program|New York City migrant prepaid card program]].[15]


## Unsealing of the federal indictment (September 2024)

The five-count federal indictment against Adams was unsealed by Manhattan prosecutors on Thursday, September 26, 2024, the first ever brought against a sitting New York City mayor.[16] It alleged that Adams took bribes — including $123,000 in free flights and other luxury perks — from foreign nationals in exchange for favors, and detailed an alleged decade-long pattern of corruption that included fraudulently obtaining $10 million in public campaign funds.[16] The charges, returned by a grand jury amid a long-running federal probe, were conspiracy, wire fraud, and bribery.[16]

The 57-page indictment stated: "In 2014, Eric Adams, the defendant, became Brooklyn Borough President. Thereafter, for nearly a decade, Adams sought and accepted improper valuable benefits, such as luxury international travel, including from wealthy foreign businesspeople and at least one Turkish government official seeking to gain influence over him."[16]

## Badlands reading of the Turkish angle

Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read the Turkish element as drawing [[turkey|Turkey]] and the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] into the story, noting Turkey's past Brotherhood ties and Erdogan's public backing of the Brotherhood's Arab Spring in 2011, which he blamed for years of friction between Turkey and other Middle Eastern states.[16] Those hatchets, he argued, had recently been buried, and Turkey — a critical NATO member because of its geography — was being courted by Russia, China, and Iran to join [[brics|BRICS]] and possibly the Shanghai Cooperation Organization.[16] Drawing an analogy to the 2022 Danchenko trial, where Badlands contributor Just Human argued it was really the FBI on trial, he suggested the Adams prosecution might likewise reveal more than it convicts, especially alongside the indictment of Bob Menendez involving payments from Egypt with connections to Qatar.[16] Turkey, Egypt, and Qatar, he wrote, represent the three main Muslim Brotherhood enclaves — the organization having been founded in Cairo in 1928 and now based in Qatar alongside Hamas — a pattern he read as evidence of how proxy groups are used to infiltrate and subvert governments.[16]

[[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]] publicly urged against convicting Adams before trial, a stance Badlands writer Burning Bright read as a deliberate refocusing of attention onto the Southern District of New York itself, the venue of cases from Ghislaine Maxwell to Sean Combs and, older still, the Anthony Weiner laptop narrative.[16]

## "Idealism collides with realism" (September 2023)

In early September 2023 Adams again warned that the migrant crisis would "destroy New York City," a statement ZeroHedge presented under the heading "Idealism Collides With Realism" through a before-and-after visual contrast between the city's earlier sanctuary rhetoric and its later condition.[17] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the imagery alone captured the public safety and policy "lunacy" on display not only in New York but in Chicago, San Francisco and other large progressive cities, and suggested that some residents would have to "experience a bit more darkness before yearning for the light."[17]

## The 2023 Trump arraignment

Ahead of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s April 2023 arraignment in Manhattan — the first of a former American president — Adams warned Trump's supporters to keep the peace, saying the dignity of the proceedings would not be disrupted.[18] Barricades were deployed around the Manhattan Criminal Courts Building and the Police Department issued a stand-ready order to its roughly 35,000 officers, a force described as larger and better trained than some national armies.[18]

## Dismissal of the corruption case (February 2025)

In February 2025 the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] under the new Trump administration moved to drop the corruption charges against Adams.[19] [[emil-bove|Emil Bove]], the acting U.S. deputy attorney general, issued a memo on Monday, February 10 ordering federal prosecutors in New York to abandon the case, arguing in part that it hampered Adams's ability to tackle "illegal immigration and violent crime."[19]

The order prompted the resignation on Thursday, February 13 of [[danielle-sassoon|Danielle R. Sassoon]], the acting U.S. attorney for the Southern District of New York, along with two senior federal prosecutors in Washington, in what NBC News described as a stunning public rebuke of the department's new leadership.[19] Sassoon had appealed the previous day to Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]], expressing alarm at being ordered to drop the case.[19] Asked about Adams at his reciprocal-trade press conference that Thursday, President Trump stated definitively that he had not spoken with the DOJ about the case at all.[19]

## Changle Association donation

Lu Jianshun, former chairman of the [[american-changle-association|American Changle Association]] — the New York non-profit whose Manhattan headquarters the [[fbi|FBI]] raided in the fall of 2022 over an alleged secret Chinese police station — donated $4,000 to Adams' mayoral campaign in 2021, according to The New York Times.[20]

## September 2024 federal indictment

On September 26, 2024, a federal indictment against Adams was unsealed charging him with bribery and fraud following a months-long federal investigation into whether his mayoral campaign illegally conspired with Turkish entities to collect foreign donations.[21] Coverage noted that Adams was the first sitting New York City mayor to face criminal charges while in office.[21] The indictment contained five counts, including two counts of solicitation of a contribution by a foreign national, and the Department of Justice stated that Adams faced up to 45 years in prison if convicted on all counts and given the maximum sentence.[21] The charging document alleged that Adams sought and accepted benefits from wealthy foreign individuals and at least one Turkish government official dating back to his tenure as Brooklyn Borough president.[21]

Speaking at a press conference on the day the indictment was unsealed, Adams asked New Yorkers "to wait and hear our defense before making any judgments," and said his "day-to-day will not change."[21] Badlands Media commentary framed the case as one about public trust rather than mere campaign finance technicalities, drawing a comparison to the standard of avoiding even the appearance of impropriety expected of financial-sector fiduciaries.[21] Writers at the outlet also noted the timing of the case alongside the [[sean-combs|Sean Combs]] prosecution, observing that both were brought in the [[southern-district-of-new-york|Southern District of New York]] and shared a common thread of alleged corruption, and speculated that the investigation of Adams and his associates had always "read as a RICO."[21]

## UnitedHealthcare CEO shooting arrest (December 2024)

On Monday, December 9, 2024, Adams announced at a news conference that police had arrested a suspect in the [[killing-of-brian-thompson|shooting death of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson]] in Manhattan.[22] "We believe we have a strong person of interest," the mayor said of [[luigi-mangione|Luigi Mangione]], who had been detained in Altoona, Pennsylvania, on gun charges.[22]

## New York Post endorsement appeal (August 2025)

In an August 7, 2025 editorial, the [[new-york-post|New York Post]] editorial board urged President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], if he chose to involve himself in that year's mayoral race, to back Adams for a second term in Gracie Mansion, citing his commitment to public safety and civic pride.[23] The board credited Adams with standing up to left-wing legislators in Albany and the City Council — "the radicals who want to legalize theft, hold no one on bail and empty all the jails" — and noted that for the first half of 2025 New York recorded the lowest number of shootings since the city began keeping track.[23] The editorial warned that socialist rival [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]], a "defund the police" activist, would reverse that progress, hobble the [[new-york-city-police-department|NYPD]], drive away businesses and make the streets unsafe.[23]

## End of term (December 2025)

As Adams prepared to leave City Hall at the end of 2025, Politico published a retrospective framing his mayoralty as "bureaucracy as tragedy," noting that he would depart as the first one-term New York City mayor since David Dinkins.[24] The piece described him as perhaps the most eccentric executive ever to occupy Gracie Mansion, more welcome in Republican circles than in national Democratic ones, with job approval ratings that "require sonar to locate."[24] It recalled his five-count federal bribery indictment and rescue by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and the departure of much of his inner circle amid scandal and law enforcement investigations.[24]

Politico argued that voter revulsion toward Adams destroyed the ordinary advantages of incumbency and thereby created the conditions for [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]], a previously unknown state legislator, to win in November on a platform of affordability.[24] The article also lamented that Adams' exit removed Black political power from City Hall for the second time after Dinkins.[24] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded that "bureaucracy is always tragedy," quipped about "the Dawn of Mam," and rejected the premise of the piece on the grounds that [[elections-are-fake|elections are fake]] and no such power was legitimately conferred in the first place.[24]

## November 2023 federal corruption investigation

In early November 2023, federal prosecutors and the [[fbi|FBI]] were reported to be conducting a broad public corruption investigation into whether Adams's 2021 election campaign conspired with the Turkish government to receive illegal foreign donations, according to a search warrant obtained by The New York Times.[25] The inquiry became public when agents raided the Brooklyn home of the mayor's chief fund-raiser, [[brianna-suggs|Brianna Suggs]], in an early-morning operation.[25]

Investigators were also examining the potential involvement of a Brooklyn construction company with ties to Turkey and a small university in Washington, D.C. with ties both to Turkey and to Adams, and whether the campaign kicked back benefits to the construction company's officials and employees and to Turkish officials.[25] Agents seized three iPhones, two laptops, papers, a "manila folder labeled Eric Adams," seven "contribution card binders" and other materials.[25]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed the case alongside contested results in Bridgeport, Connecticut and Springfield, Massachusetts as evidence that election fraud had returned to the news, and floated the theory that a 2020 election "sting" had compromised Deep State politicians — noting that figures who had performed the most conspicuous reversals, such as Adams on illegal immigration and border policy, were now facing election-related scrutiny.[25]

## Guilty plea by former aide Mohamed Bahi (2025)

On August 12, 2025, Mohamed Bahi, a former community affairs liaison to New York City's Muslim community and an aide to Adams, pleaded guilty in Manhattan federal court to one count of conspiracy relating to the organization of illegal campaign contributions, resolving one of the outstanding indictments arising from investigations into the first-term mayor.[26] In a four-minute plea allocution Bahi said a volunteer with the Eric Adams 2021 campaign instructed him to organize a December 2020 fundraiser at which he collected straw-donor contributions from employees that both he and the campaign knew would be reimbursed by their companies' owners, adding, "I understood that the Adams campaign would then seek matching funds for their contributions."[26]

## DOJ direction to dismiss charges (February 2025)

In February 2025 a senior official at the U.S. Department of Justice directed federal prosecutors to dismiss the charges against Adams, a Democrat known for his close ties to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[27] In a two-page memo obtained by The Associated Press, acting Deputy Attorney General [[emil-bove|Emil Bove]] — a former prosecutor in the Manhattan office that brought the case — stated that the decision to drop the charges was made without evaluating the strength of the prosecution and was not intended to undermine the attorneys who filed it.[27] Bove wrote that the timing of the charges and "more recent actions" by former U.S. Attorney [[damian-williams|Damian Williams]] had compromised the integrity of the proceedings, citing increased prejudicial pretrial publicity that he warned could influence potential witnesses and the jury pool.[27] The move followed months of speculation that Trump's Justice Department might intervene to end the case, in which Adams was accused of accepting bribes in the form of free or discounted travel and of receiving illegal campaign contributions.[27]
