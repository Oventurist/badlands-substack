# Ann Selzer

J. Ann Selzer is an American pollster whose Iowa surveys, published through the [[des-moines-register|Des Moines Register]], became the subject of litigation by President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] following the 2024 presidential election.[1]

## The 2024 Iowa poll

Days before Election Day, Selzer released a poll showing Vice President Kamala Harris leading Trump in Iowa by three percentage points.[1] Trump went on to win the Hawkeye State by 14 points, a swing of 17 points from the survey's finding.[1] Selzer retired from polling almost immediately after the election.[1]

## Trump lawsuit

On December 16, 2024, Trump sued Selzer and the Des Moines Register in Iowa state court in Polk County, accusing them of violating Iowa's consumer fraud laws through deception.[1] The complaint asserted that "Selzer's polling 'miss' was not an astonishing coincidence — it was intentional," and sought unspecified damages, an order barring the release of "any further deceptive polls," and disclosure of the information relied upon in the November survey.[1] The filing argued that "for too long, left-wing pollsters have attempted to influence electoral outcomes through manipulated polls that have unacceptable error rates and are not grounded in widely accepted polling methodologies," singling out Selzer for the size of her platform and following.[1] The suit was first reported by Fox News Digital.[1]

The action formed part of a broader pattern of Trump legal fights with media organizations, including suits against ABC, CBS, journalist Bob Woodward and the Pulitzer Prize board; Badlands Media noted the ABC settlement of $15 million plus $1 million in legal fees as an immediate precedent.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer Ashe in America framed the suit as part of a "Justice phase," arguing that a successful outcome could set precedent and deter poll manipulation and false reporting.[1] The commentary noted that the Register was publicly standing by Selzer, the poll and its reporting.[1]

## End of election polling after the 2024 Iowa miss

Selzer announced in November 2024 that she would end her election polling operation after her [[2024-des-moines-register-iowa-poll-miss|final survey]] for [[des-moines-register|The Des Moines Register]] failed to capture [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s strength in Iowa, producing a 16-point miss.[2] The poll had shown Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] leading 47–44 percent among likely voters, a result widely amplified by other outlets even though rival surveys placed Trump comfortably ahead; Trump carried the state 56–43 percent.[2] Selzer later acknowledged the "big miss" and suggested her poll may have energized Republican voters who had expected to coast.[2]

Kristin Roberts, chief content officer of Gannett Media, said the Iowa Poll would "evolve as we find new ways to accurately capture public sentiment" and conceded the paper "did not deliver on that promise."[2] Badlands Media described the survey as an overt suppression poll and greeted the retirement with "good riddance," noting that a product once called "the gold standard" had missed by 16 points.[2]
