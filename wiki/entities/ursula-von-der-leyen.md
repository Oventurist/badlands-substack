# Ursula von der Leyen

Ursula von der Leyen is the President of the European Commission, the executive body of the European Union.[1]

## Davos 2026 and Ukraine

Following Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]]'s critical speech at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in Davos in January 2026 — in which he described the EU as indecisive on security and did not specifically acknowledge nearly €193 billion in European military and financial assistance since 2022 — von der Leyen said the EU stands by Ukraine and that its actions "speak louder than words."[1] She acknowledged that Europe cannot match the sacrifices made by the Ukrainian people but pledged that the bloc would continue its support.[1]

Her position stood against calls from Italian lawmaker [[rossano-sasso|Rossano Sasso]] to cut off aid entirely and the sharper tone of Italian Foreign Minister [[antonio-tajani|Antonio Tajani]].[1]


## Response to the 2026 Hungarian election

Following the April 2026 Hungarian parliamentary election, von der Leyen responded to [[peter-magyar|Péter Magyar]]'s projected supermajority on X, writing: "Hungary has chosen Europe. Europe has always chosen Hungary. A country reclaims its European path. The Union grows stronger."[2] The statement followed [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]]'s concession after sixteen years in office and was read by Badlands commentators as the [[european-union|EU]] leadership celebrating the removal of its most persistent internal dissenter on sanctions, [[ukraine|Ukraine]] aid, and energy policy.[2]

## Trump tariff ultimatum (May 2026)

In May 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly pressed von der Leyen over the Turnberry trade agreement in a Truth Social post, writing that he had "a great call" with her and that the two were "completely united that Iran can never have a Nuclear Weapon."[3] He said the [[european-union|EU]] had promised to cut its tariffs to zero as its side of what he called the largest trade deal ever, and that he had given her until the United States' 250th birthday before EU tariffs "would immediately jump to much higher levels."[3] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the post as a signal that timing on the Iranian question favored Trump and as evidence of his [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]] strategy against the [[globalist-hegemon|globalist hegemon]].[3]

## Davos 2026

At the January 2026 [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] meeting in Davos, von der Leyen publicly questioned [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s trustworthiness on trade deals and characterized the tariffs imposed over [[greenland|Greenland]] as a "mistake between allies."[4] Her speech carried a theme similar to that of French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]], who warned of the deterioration of international law and the emergence of a "might makes right" system.[4] The remarks came as the [[european-union|EU]] weighed retaliation, including its [[anti-coercion-instrument|anti-coercion instrument]] and counter-tariffs, in the [[greenland-tariff-dispute-2026|2026 Greenland tariff dispute]].[4]

## Xi Jinping's Taiwan accusation (2023–2024)

In June 2024 the Financial Times reported that Chinese leader [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] had accused the United States of attempting to trick China into invading [[taiwan|Taiwan]], while insisting his country would not take the bait.[5] According to the report, Xi made the accusation in April 2023 during a meeting with von der Leyen in her capacity as President of the European Commission — the first known instance of him making the claim to a foreign leader, though he had reportedly issued the same warning to officials within China.[5] The accusation did not appear in the press statement released after the meeting, in which Xi said Taiwan was at the "core" of China's interests and warned that anyone expecting China to compromise on the Taiwan question was "having a pipe dream."[5]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] questioned why von der Leyen had withheld the information for a full year and why it surfaced on the day before [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s scheduled arrival in North Korea, treating the timing as evidence of narrative warfare between the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and the [[old-guard|Old Guard]].[5] He also identified her as the official standing beside [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], visibly scowling, in the widely circulated G7 parachute-exhibition video in which the president appeared to wander off.[5]

## 2024 defense union program

In July 2024, seeking re-election as [[european-commission|European Commission]] president, von der Leyen published a political program vowing to transform the [[european-union|EU]] into a defense bloc, declaring that "our work in the next five years will be focused on building a true [[european-defense-union|European Defense Union]]."[6] She specified that member states "will always retain responsibility for their own troops, from doctrine to deployment," while arguing that Europe could do much to support and coordinate efforts to strengthen the defense industrial base, innovation, and the Single Market.[6] She also pledged to appoint a Commissioner for Defense who would work closely with the next High Representative/Vice-President in accordance with the Treaty.[6] Badlands commentary characterized the initiative as "one small step for Europe, one giant step for globalism."[6]


## Call for sanctions on Israel (September 2025)

Speaking in Strasbourg in September 2025, von der Leyen called the situation in Gaza "unacceptable" and urged EU member states to "step up" and impose sanctions on [[israel|Israel]]'s "extremist ministers and on violent settlers."[7] She said, "First, the Commission will do all that it can on its own. We will put our bilateral support to Israel on hold," and proposed that the [[european-union|EU]] partially freeze its free trade agreement with Israel.[7]

Her remarks preceded a push by Irish Deputy Prime Minister [[simon-harris|Simon Harris]] to have member states co-sign a letter to foreign policy chief [[kaja-kallas|Kaja Kallas]] demanding a swift sanctions decision; [[ireland|Ireland]], Spain, Sweden, Denmark and the Netherlands supported suspending the trade deal while Germany, Hungary and the Czech Republic opposed sanctions.[7]


## Breton resignation and governance criticism (September 2024)

On September 16, 2024, [[thierry-breton|Thierry Breton]], the [[european-union|European Union]]'s internal market chief, resigned from the [[european-commission|European Commission]], saying [[france|France]] had been asked to supply a different nominee for the incoming Commission.[8] In his resignation letter to von der Leyen, Breton said her request was "further testimony to questionable governance," stating that she had asked France to withdraw his name "for personal reasons that in no instance have you discussed directly with me," with France to receive a more influential role in the EU executive in return.[8] The episode, reported by [[bloomberg|Bloomberg]], was carried by Badlands as a bonus item illustrating internal Commission friction under her presidency.[8]

## European Democracy Shield (November 2025)

As European Commission President, von der Leyen championed the [[european-democracy-shield|European Democracy Shield]], an [[european-union|EU]] initiative launched in November 2025 to regulate speech and combat disinformation, claiming the programme "protects democracy and civil society."[9] Critics countered that the Shield — with its Monitoring Centre for "false content" and its EU-funded network of fact-checkers — limits freedom under the guise of battling misinformation and marks a trend toward authoritarianism within the bloc.[9] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] attacked her in unusually personal terms while calling for the EU's dissolution.[9]

## July 2025 trade deal with Trump

On 27 July 2025 von der Leyen struck a trade deal with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] that led the [[european-union|European Union]] to suspend its planned counter-tariffs against the United States for six months, as EU officials announced on 4 August.[10] The broader agreement imposes 15% tariffs on most European imports to the U.S., including automobiles, while the EU agreed to eliminate all tariffs on U.S. industrial exports to Europe, commit to $750 billion in U.S. energy purchases, and increase investment in the United States by $600 billion beyond existing levels.[10]

### Centralization of power in the second Commission term
A February 2025 Politico report described von der Leyen tightening her grip over the European Commission as she began her second five-year term.[11] On the day of Donald Trump's inauguration, the communications advisers of EU commissioners received strict instructions from their counterpart in von der Leyen's cabinet: according to an EU official, they were permitted only to repost the Commission president's January 20 message to Trump on social media, and under no circumstances to add words of their own.[11] Such oversight was described as a hallmark of her tenure, with a leadership style growing more controlling than ever and a centralization of power that many saw as evolving toward a fully presidential model under the firm hand of the 66-year-old former German defense minister.[11] The report described a pattern in which she and a close-knit circle of largely German advisers run the Commission, shunning scrutiny from the European Parliament and keeping a tight grip on all messaging and communication.[11] Badlands commentary read the episode as a dark mirror of the Western hegemon: while Trump brokered peace between Russia and Ukraine, the "Old Guard" represented by the NATO bloc was holding meetings to stop exactly that.[11]

## Condemnation of Russia's "instrumentalization of migrants" (November 2023)

In November 2023 von der Leyen joined Finnish Prime Minister [[petteri-orpo|Petteri Orpo]] in condemning what the two described as Russia's "instrumentalization of migrants" at the [[finland|Finnish]]–Russian border, with von der Leyen calling the practice "shameful."[12] The statements came as Finland's Border Guard reported a sharp increase in undocumented migrants — most originating in Africa and the Middle East — arriving at crossings along the roughly 830-mile frontier, which also forms an external border of the European Union, and as the Finnish military bolstered its border barrier.[12]

## Ukraine peace talks phone call

Von der Leyen joined the December 2025 phone call held by Emmanuel Macron and Friedrich Merz with Vladimir Zelensky and other European leaders, held one day before US envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner traveled to Moscow for talks with Vladimir Putin.[13] Der Spiegel reported that the leaders on the call harbored profound distrust of Washington's attempts to end the Ukraine conflict.[13]

## Congratulating Trump on the 2024 election

Von der Leyen congratulated [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on his November 2024 election victory in a social media post, writing: "I warmly congratulate Donald J. Trump. The EU and the US are more than just allies. We are bound by a true partnership between our people, uniting 800 million citizens. So let's work together on a strong transatlantic agenda that keeps delivering for them."[14] Badlands commentary read the rush of European congratulations as the "submission grins" of a fading globalist bloc, noting that Trump had simultaneously threatened tariffs marking up EU goods by 100–200 percent.[14]

## Scotland trade deal and EU recriminations (July 2025)

Reporting by Bloomberg at the end of July 2025 described European Union leaders working through the consequences of the new trading arrangement with the United States and confronting how far the bloc's clout had fallen.[15] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] held court on a Sunday at one of his golf courses on the Scottish coast, touting a newly built clubhouse ballroom and delivering a lengthy tirade about wind turbines, while von der Leyen had to wait for him to finish his round before discussing the transatlantic trade relationship.[15]

The terms entailed a significant hit to European companies: the [[european-union|EU]] accepted a tripling of tariffs to 15% on most exports to the United States while keeping its own levies on American imports at 1% or less.[15] Former Spanish foreign minister Arancha Gonzalez Laya, dean of the Paris School of International Affairs at Sciences Po, told Bloomberg that "economically, this isn't a good agreement and geopolitically speaking, it is a defeat," adding that "this agreement makes the EU smaller."[15] European ambassadors meeting in Brussels the following Monday recognized that von der Leyen had no choice but to swallow the unbalanced deal because the alternatives would have been worse.[15] Badlands contributor Burning Bright linked the capitulation to a longer sequence of what he calls pincer moves between Trump and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], connecting Western sanctions on Russia, Trumpian tariffs and the resurgence of Russiagate to the EU's submission.[15]
