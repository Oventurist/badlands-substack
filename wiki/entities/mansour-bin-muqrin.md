# Mansour bin Muqrin

Prince Mansour bin Muqrin was a senior Saudi royal and deputy governor of Asir province who was killed, along with seven other officials, in a helicopter crash near [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]]'s border with [[yemen|Yemen]] on the night of Sunday, November 5, 2017.[1] He was returning from an inspection tour when his aircraft came down near Abha; the interior ministry did not give a cause for the crash.[1] He was the son of [[muqrin-bin-abdulaziz|Muqrin bin Abdulaziz Al Saud]], the powerful former Saudi intelligence chief.[1]

## Timing and disputed circumstances

The crash occurred hours after a major purge of the kingdom's political and business leadership, in which an anti-corruption body led by 32-year-old Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] ordered the detention of dozens of people, including 11 princes, four ministers and dozens of ex-ministers.[1]

Reports subsequently suggested the crash was not an accident. India Today reported that new accounts from the region described it as a successful assassination, citing the Israeli daily Yedioth Ahronoth's claim — for which the newspaper did not disclose a source — that a Saudi warplane shot down the helicopter carrying the prince and his seven senior aides.[1] Middle East Monitor, quoting the New Khaleej, reported that bin Muqrin had sent a letter to over a thousand princes asking them not to support Mohammed bin Salman's succession to the throne.[1]

Badlands Media argues it is implausible that the death was coincidental, given that it occurred on the same day [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Al Waleed bin Talal]] and hundreds of his supporters were arrested, and suggests the prince may have been fleeing to Yemen — the country Saudi Arabia was fighting a proxy war against and from which a ballistic missile had just been launched at Riyadh.[1]
