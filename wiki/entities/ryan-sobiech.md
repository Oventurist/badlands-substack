# Ryan Sobiech

Ryan Sobiech was a 17-year-old youth hockey goaltender whose death in March 2001 forms the personal centerpiece of the Badlands Media essay "Sports in Society."[1]

Sobiech shared goaltending duties with the article's author during their final year of Midget-eligible competitive hockey.[1] He stood 6'4", held multiple scholarship offers, and in the author's assessment was the better of the two — a player with "athleticism for days and a mind for the game" who was destined for the big time.[1] The two split starts and maintained a competitive but healthy bond, pushing and supporting one another.[1]

On March 2, 2001, Sobiech was killed in a car accident on his way to school.[1] The author, who gave the eulogy at a funeral where teammates wore their jerseys, notes repeatedly that Sobiech would likely have survived had he been wearing his seatbelt, and uses the story as an occasion to urge readers to buckle up.[1]

With his mother's permission, the author changed his jersey number from 29 to Sobiech's 64 for the remainder of the season.[1] The team reached the state semifinals, losing 1–0 in triple overtime, after which Sobiech's mother consoled the grieving goaltender in the locker room and told him that the team's play and his wearing of her son's number had been a deep source of healing for the family: "You and this team have brought us peace. That is worth more than any medal."[1] The team won the bronze medal the following day, and the author later presented that medal and his jersey to the Sobiech family.[1] The episode is offered as evidence of how athletic competition can produce both devastating lows and forms of healing rooted in unbreakable bonds.[1]
