# Jason Crow

Jason Crow is a Democratic member of the U.S. House of Representatives from Colorado and a former Army Ranger from Aurora.[1] He was one of six lawmakers — all veterans of the armed services or intelligence community — who appeared in a November 2025 video urging U.S. service members to uphold the Constitution and defy "illegal orders," the episode that became known as the [[seditious-six|Seditious Six]] controversy.[1]

## Trump sedition accusation

On Thursday, November 20, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] accused Crow and the other participants of sedition "punishable by DEATH," amplifying reposts of the video with his own commentary.[1] In a statement late that morning, Crow and his colleagues responded: "Every American must unite and condemn the President's calls for our murder and political violence. This is a time for moral clarity."[1] Some Democrats characterized Trump's rhetoric as that of a king and suggested it was intended to distract from the soon-to-be-released [[epstein-files|Epstein files]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Colorado "normies" celebrated Crow being "owned" by [[fox-news|Fox News]] host Martha McCallum in a televised exchange, conceding that Crow came across as arrogant, condescending and without substance.[1] Her substantive objection was that Crow could not point to a specific illegal order and instead doubled down on vague warnings that confuse and demoralize service members and impact readiness — which, in her view, established a seditious conspiracy warranting court martial.[1] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] took a deflationary view of the whole affair, arguing the Democrats only implied a military coup and Trump only implied a hanging, and that everyone should stop overreacting.[1]

## Afghan resettlement advocacy

Crow was one of the most prominent advocates for the 2021 resettlement of Afghans in the United States, and commentators noted that he continued to represent that constituency's interests — in the view of critics, more energetically than he represented Colorado's Sixth Congressional District.[2] That record drew renewed scrutiny after the [[2025-washington-dc-national-guard-shooting|November 2025 shooting of two National Guard members in Washington, D.C.]], whose suspect, [[rahmanullah-lakanwal|Rahmanullah Lakanwal]], had entered the country in September 2021 under the Afghan evacuation program.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited Crow's role alongside [[elissa-slotkin|Elissa Slotkin]]'s earlier forecast of National Guard violence and the suspect's [[cia|CIA]] ties as a pattern worth examining.[2]

## 2024 challenge from John Fabbricatore

In September 2024 [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described Crow — then representing Colorado's sixth congressional district — as "the ignorant and arrogant Ukraine activist" and endorsed the candidacy of [[john-fabbricatore|John Fabbricatore]], the former ICE director for Colorado, to replace him.[3] The endorsement was framed around Fabbricatore's warnings about [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] activity in [[aurora-colorado|Aurora]] and the argument that Congress needs members who "know how to do things," though Badlands conceded that effective official gaslighting in Colorado left the challenger with an uphill battle.[3]
