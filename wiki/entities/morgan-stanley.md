# Morgan Stanley

Morgan Stanley is a major American investment bank. In 2023 it was among the large Wall Street institutions — alongside [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan]] and [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]] — that publicly turned negative on U.S. commercial real estate, joining what [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] described as the "gloom parade" over the office sector.[1]

Its research focused attention on the roughly $400 billion wall of commercial real estate debt maturing that year, a central element of the developing [[commercial-real-estate-crisis|commercial real estate crisis]] that also produced [[brookfield|Brookfield]]'s $161.4 million Washington, D.C. office default.[1]

## Cybersecurity leadership and the Guidehouse breach

Before being sworn in to head the [[cisa|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]] in July 2021, [[jen-easterly|Jen Easterly]] served as Morgan Stanley's global head of cybersecurity.[2] The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] noted that this placed her in charge of the bank's cyber function in January 2021, when a data breach at the technology vendor [[guidehouse|Guidehouse]] — which stored Morgan Stanley corporate data — exposed sensitive client information including Social Security numbers.[2] In that role Easterly would have had oversight of the security of that vendor ecosystem, and Morgan Stanley went on to experience additional cybersecurity scandals after her departure five months later.[2]

## April 2023 commercial real estate call

In April 2023, amid a sudden firesale of real estate loans by small US banks, Morgan Stanley projected roughly 40% downside in commercial real estate.[3] The warning accompanied data showing a $37.8 billion plunge in small-bank real estate loans over the second half of March — the largest such decline since the March 2007 collapse of [[new-century-financial|New Century Financial]], the subprime lender whose failure preceded the global financial crisis.[3]
