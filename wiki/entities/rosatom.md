# Rosatom

Rosatom is Russia's state-owned nuclear energy agency and the ultimate acquirer of the Canadian mining company [[uranium-one|Uranium One]] in the transaction the article calls the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Acquisition of Uranium One

The article states that Rosatom was permitted by the Obama administration to purchase Uranium One in 2010 through its subsidiary ARMZ, giving Russia control of roughly 20 percent of United States uranium production capacity.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Because uranium is treated as a strategic asset with national-security implications, the sale required approval from a committee composed of representatives of several United States government agencies, among them the [[state-department|State Department]], then headed by [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The Russians assumed control of Uranium One gradually, in three separate transactions between 2009 and 2013.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

Beyond the American mines, the acquisition delivered to Rosatom the company's holdings in [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]] — mines the article describes as among the most lucrative in the world and the true prize of the deal.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Export controls and the license workaround

Following the approval, the [[nuclear-regulatory-commission|Nuclear Regulatory Commission]] declared in a November 2010 press release that no uranium produced at the relevant facilities could be exported, an assurance repeated in a later letter from then-NRC Chairman Gregory Jaczko to Senator John Barrasso.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The article, citing The Hill, argues this assurance was false: NRC memos show yellowcake uranium was shipped from the Russian-owned American mines to Canada in 2012 through a third party, with some subsequently approved to travel on to Europe.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

Rather than grant Rosatom a direct export license — which the article says would have raised red flags in a Congress already suspicious of the deal — the NRC in 2012 amended an existing export license held by a Paducah, Kentucky trucking firm, RSB Logistics Services Inc., adding Uranium One to its list of clients and raising the permitted shipment volume to the Cameco Corp. plant in Ontario from 7,500,000 to 12,000,000 kilograms.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The move escaped notice in Congress, and uranium flowed from Wyoming to Canada and on to Europe between 2012 and 2014.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Context in the article's thesis

The article frames the Rosatom purchase as the payoff of a plan begun in 2005, when [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and [[frank-giustra|Frank Giustra]] secured Kazakh uranium stakes for [[urasia-energy|UrAsia Energy]], and enabled politically by the [[russian-reset|Russian Reset]], which recast Russia as a friendly partner and cleared what a cited Q drop calls the "pathway" to the deal.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Cash from parties connected to Uranium One, including chairman [[ian-telfer|Ian Telfer]] and Russian investor [[sergei-kurzin|Sergei Kurzin]], flowed to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] while the transaction was under review.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Operation of the Zaporozhye plant

Rosatom has operated the [[zaporozhye-nuclear-power-plant|Zaporozhye Nuclear Power Plant]] — the largest nuclear facility in Europe — under Russian control since 2022.[2] In late May 2026, after a fiber-optic-guided drone punctured the machine hall of the plant's sixth power unit, Rosatom described the strike as Kiev's first deliberate attack on the facility's main equipment and warned that any explosion or fire could cut power and water supplies, creating conditions for a nuclear incident.[2] Rosatom chief executive [[aleksey-likhachev|Aleksey Likhachev]] said radiation from such an event would primarily endanger [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and neighbouring EU countries; Ukrainian authorities denied involvement.[2]

## Sovereign Alliance construction programs

As of September 2025, Rosatom was in the middle of constructing a nuclear reactor for [[turkey|Türkiye]], a fact Badlands contributors raised as context for the [[us-turkiye-memorandum-on-strategic-civil-nuclear-cooperation|US–Türkiye civil nuclear memorandum]] signed during President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]]'s White House visit.[3] The same company was described as planning reactors in Kazakhstan, [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]], Mali and a number of other nations grouped under the Sovereign Alliance thesis.[3] Commentary also noted that EU authorities have accused Rosatom of funding the populist Italian political party that placed the country's current prime minister and deputy prime minister in office.[3]

## Mali agreement (June 2025)

On Tuesday, June 24, 2025, Rosatom announced that Russia and [[mali|Mali]] had agreed to expand cooperation in the peaceful use of nuclear energy, in a deal covering infrastructure development, joint projects in healthcare and agriculture, and the training of Malian specialists.[4] Rosatom head [[aleksey-likhachev|Alexey Likhachev]] called the signing "a significant milestone in the strategic partnership between Russia and Mali," saying it would support the country's energy and scientific development while "opening up wide-ranging opportunities for both countries."[4] The agreement followed a meeting between President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Mali's interim president General [[assimi-goita|Assimi Goita]] and built on a memorandum on nuclear cooperation signed in October 2023, and came a week after a similar Russian agreement with [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]].[4] See [[russia-mali-nuclear-energy-agreement|the Russia–Mali nuclear energy agreement]].[4]

## Burkina Faso agreement (2025)
In June 2025 Rosatom chief executive [[aleksey-likhachev|Alexey Likhachev]] signed an agreement with [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]]'s Energy Minister Yacouba Zabré Gouba to expand peaceful nuclear energy cooperation, including radiation technologies and specialist training, on the sidelines of the St. Petersburg International Economic Forum.[5] Likhachev called the signing "an important milestone in strengthening the partnership between Russia and Burkina Faso" and said Rosatom was "ready to provide advanced technologies and expert support to implement joint projects aimed at sustainable development and enhancing the region's energy security."[5] Rosatom's statement said the pact builds on a roadmap signed the previous March with Burkina Faso's Ministry of Energy during the ATOMEXPO forum in Sochi.[5] See [[2025-russia-burkina-faso-nuclear-energy-agreement]].[5]
