# Bureau of Industry and Security

The **Bureau of Industry and Security** (BIS) is the unit of the U.S. [[department-of-commerce|Commerce Department]] responsible for administering export controls on advanced technology.[1]

In March 2024, BIS licensing authority became the obstacle in [[amd|Advanced Micro Devices]]'s attempt to sell an artificial intelligence chip tailored for the Chinese market. AMD had hoped for a green light from Commerce to sell the processor — which was deliberately designed with lower performance than its non-China products in order to fall within U.S. export restrictions — to Chinese customers.[1] U.S. officials instead told AMD the chip was still too powerful and that the company would need a license from BIS to proceed. The bureau declined to comment, and it was not clear whether AMD would apply.[1]

The episode fit into a broader Washington effort to limit Chinese access to cutting-edge semiconductors capable of developing AI models, and to the tools used to manufacture them, out of concern that Beijing would gain a military edge. The [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration unveiled an initial set of export controls in 2022 and strengthened them in October 2023 to cover more technology and to curb sales through intermediary nations that might undermine the ban.[1]
