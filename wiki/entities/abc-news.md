# ABC News

ABC News is a US television news organization that, in March 2026, became the subject of a public dispute with the [[white-house|White House]] over its reporting on an alleged Iranian drone threat to California.[1]

## Iranian drone report and retraction demand

ABC News reported that the [[fbi|FBI]] had assessed that [[iran|Iran]] had considered or aspired to conduct drone attacks in California in retaliation for US actions, citing law-enforcement sources.[1] The report stated that investigators were examining intelligence indicating Iran had explored launching drones from ships or other platforms near the US West Coast.[1]

White House press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] called on ABC News to retract the reporting, saying it was inaccurate and misrepresented intelligence about potential Iranian retaliation, and demanding a correction or retraction.[1] No Iranian attack on California occurred, and officials said authorities continued to monitor potential threats.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands writer Burning Bright treated the episode as a data point in a broader argument that a false-flag attack attributable to Iran had been contemplated by pro-war factions and "rug-pulled" by the Trump administration, and that the White House's aggressive public response to the ABC story was consistent with that reading.[1] The dispute paralleled President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s simultaneous attacks on the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] over its coverage of the Iran conflict.[1]

## Reporting on the Abrego Garcia dismissal

ABC News reported that U.S. District Judge [[waverly-crenshaw|Waverly Crenshaw]] dismissed the human-smuggling case against [[kilmar-abrego-garcia|Kilmar Abrego Garcia]] on May 22, 2026 after granting a motion alleging vindictive prosecution.[2] ABC further reported that Crenshaw found the government had not rebutted a presumption of vindictiveness — the factual basis on which the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] built its account of the subsequent ethics complaint against Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]].[2]


## Whistleblower allegations over the September 2024 debate

In September 2024, an ABC News whistleblower released a sworn, notarized statement dated September 9, 2024 — the day before the network's September 10 presidential debate — alleging that the [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] campaign had "restricted the scope" of the debate questions and demanded that only [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] be fact-checked during the event.[3] Reporting by RedState said the whistleblower had been expected to expose both the network and the Harris campaign for allegedly rigging the [[2024-presidential-debate|debate]] in the Democratic nominee's favor, including by giving Harris advance notice of the questions.[3] Badlands carried the account among its bonus items.[3]

## June 2024 poll on the Trump prosecution

An ABC News/Ipsos poll released in June 2024 reported that 47% of Americans believed [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]]'s prosecution of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was politically motivated while 38% did not, and that 50% thought the guilty verdict was correct.[4] Among respondents who said they followed the case closely, 57% called the verdict correct.[4] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed the result as a narrative deployment, suggesting the network had effectively polled its own audience about its own coverage, and argued that ABC and [[ipsos|Ipsos]] were working to sustain the impression of a 50/50 electorate.[4]

## December 2024 headquarters decay and ratings decline

In December 2024, The National Pulse reported that ABC's "Good Morning America" faced declining ratings and deteriorating conditions at its longtime Upper West Side headquarters as the network transitioned to a new facility in downtown Manhattan.[5] The flagship morning show had been losing ground to NBC's "Today," sparking concern among Disney and ABC executives.[5] Sources described the building, which had housed ABC since the 1980s, as "falling apart"; its antenna was ripped off over a weekend with no plans for replacement, and staffers working on-site reported freezing temperatures, no Wi-Fi, and poor maintenance, with one insider quipping "The heat is off!" and another reporting, "It smells like crap."[5]

The decay came as the building awaited demolition: ABC had sold the property in 2018 for nearly $1 billion while remaining a tenant during its move to a new Hudson Square facility, and insiders said the current owners had little incentive to maintain a complex slated for replacement by high-rise development.[5] Staff also dealt with infestations of mice and fleas and broken elevators and escalators, with one employee predicting, "The building is going to get condemned."[5] Badlands Media carried the report as a bonus item, its headline framing the network as "Failing ABC."[5]

## December 2024 Trump defamation settlement

On Saturday, December 14, 2024, ABC News and its top anchor [[george-stephanopoulos|George Stephanopoulos]] reached a settlement with President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in his defamation suit, agreeing to pay $15 million as a charitable contribution to a presidential foundation and museum to be established by or for Trump, along with $1 million in his attorney fees.[6] The publicly filed settlement allowed both parties to avoid a costly trial.[6] The network and Stephanopoulos additionally issued statements of "regret" as an editor's note at the bottom of a March 10, 2024 online article, and an ABC News spokesperson said the network was "pleased" that the case had concluded.[6]

The suit arose from Stephanopoulos's repeated on-air assertion — made ten times during a March 2024 *This Week* interview with Representative [[nancy-mace|Nancy Mace]] — that Trump had been found "liable for rape," when the jury in the [[e-jean-carroll|E. Jean Carroll]] case had in fact found him liable for "sexual abuse," a distinct category under New York law.[6] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the settlement as evidence that "legal outcomes are starting to feel like justice."[6]

## Coverage of the Navalny sanctions announcement (February 2024)

ABC News reported on February 20, 2024 that the [[white-house|White House]] would announce a "major sanctions" package that Friday "to hold Russia accountable" for the death of [[alexei-navalny|Alexei Navalny]], quoting national security spokesman [[john-kirby|John Kirby]]'s statement that President [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] and his government were responsible.[7]

## Reporting on the Cheatle resignation

ABC News reported the July 23, 2024 resignation of [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]] Director [[kimberly-cheatle|Kimberly Cheatle]] and obtained both her resignation email to agency employees and the message sent to staff by acting director [[ron-rowe|Ron Rowe]].[8] The outlet had previously reported on Cheatle's appearance before Congress and on the agency's failure to prevent the assassination attempt on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[8]
