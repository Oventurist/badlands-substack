# Conservative Political Action Conference

The Conservative Political Action Conference (CPAC) is an annual gathering of American conservative activists, officeholders and media figures that functions as a major platform for speeches to the movement's base.[1] By 2024 its stage had increasingly been used by populist and nationalist figures from outside the United States as well as domestic Republicans.[1]

## 2024 conference and the Bukele address

At the 2024 conference, El Salvadoran president [[nayib-bukele|Nayib Bukele]] delivered a widely covered address in which he blasted [[george-soros|George Soros]], globalism and the American financial system, telling the audience that globalism "is already dead" and that in America "dark forces are taking over your country."[1] Bukele, who had just won re-election with roughly 84% of the vote, used El Salvador's own descent into becoming the "murder capital of the world" as a cautionary template, arguing that his countrymen had not been equipped to understand how radically their society was changing because the change happened over decades, one increment at a time.[1] He likened the process to slowly boiling a frog, while urging Americans not to despair because "you can still jump before the water boils."[1]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the speech as an "ultra based" address to an American audience concerned with the direction of their nation, delivered by a leader using his own country as a white-pill template.[1] The remarks were reported by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] and LifeSiteNews and carried in the Badlands News Brief.[1]

At the March 2023 conference, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] delivered the address later dubbed his "[[2023-trump-cpac-speech|call to arms]]," in which he denounced the old [[republican-party|Republican Party]] as one "ruled by Freaks, Neocons, Globalists, Open Border Zealots, and Fools" and named [[paul-ryan|Paul Ryan]], [[karl-rove|Karl Rove]] and [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] as the leadership the party would "never go back to."[2] Badlands contributor [[brad-getz|Brad Getz]] wrote that the line's reception by the CPAC hall — cheers rather than the boos such a statement would have drawn in 2015 — demonstrated how far the audience had shifted, and treated the speech as evidence that the conference had become a venue for openly anti-establishment and anti-[[forever-wars|forever war]] rhetoric.[2]
