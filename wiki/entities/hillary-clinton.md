# Hillary Clinton

Hillary Clinton served as United States Secretary of State during the 2011 intervention in Libya and the September 11, 2012 Benghazi attack.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Libya and the weapons pipeline

The article asserts that after the overthrow of Muammar Gaddafi, Clinton — together with [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and [[john-mccain|John McCain]] — installed a Libyan government controlled by Al Qaeda elements such as [[abdelhakim-belhadj|Abdelhakim Belhadj]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) As Secretary of State she was responsible for authorizing private U.S. weapons sales worldwide, and is accused of using that authority to funnel weapons to enemies while money flowed into the Clinton Foundation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article links the years of her tenure to the years of [[isis|ISIS]] expansion, citing Q drop 2640.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

Stinger missiles that the [[cia|CIA]] turned over to Qatar in early 2011 — weapons her State Department intended for anti-Gaddafi forces — were reportedly passed by Qatar to the Taliban, one of which struck a U.S. Army CH-47 in Kunar province on July 25, 2012.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Benghazi night and the "smoking gun"

The article states that the Diplomatic Command Center at the State Department in Washington learned of the attack immediately, but that notice took roughly an hour to reach the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] — a delay the author attributes to a deliberate choice by Clinton not to have the military respond.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) At a 7:30 p.m. "Deputies Meeting" convened by the [[white-house|White House]], Clinton was the senior official present while both Obama and Secretary of Defense [[leon-panetta|Leon Panetta]] were absent; her stated first concern was that any deployment of U.S. forces into Libya required approval from the Government of Libya.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

Citing the [[house-select-committee-on-benghazi|House Select Committee]] report as relayed by The Hill, the article says the committee found a "smoking gun": a direct order of the Commander in Chief was obstructed by Secretary of State Hillary Clinton.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The author asks why she faced no consequences, concluding that Obama defended her and that the two coordinated their public narrative, including a focus on a protest over an online video.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

As the 2016 Democratic presidential nominee, Clinton was unexpectedly defeated on election night by [[donald-trump|Donald J. ## Teneo, the Clinton Foundation and pay-for-play

The America for Sale series casts Clinton's tenure as Secretary of State as the engine of a [[pay-for-play|pay-for-play]] operation.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Her closest aide, [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]], was granted "[[special-government-employee|special government employee]]" status that allowed her to draw a $15,000-a-month consulting fee from [[teneo-holdings|Teneo Holdings]] and to work for the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] while advising Clinton at the [[state-department|State Department]].[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Abedin served as liaison between foundation donors seeking favours and Clinton's State Department, and the article argues Clinton used her power as Secretary to grant those favours while Teneo acted as the go-between.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

While Secretary of State, Clinton approved a paid consulting arrangement between [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and [[doug-band|Doug Band]], under which Teneo paid the former president $2.5 million per year; Bill Clinton also served for a time as Teneo's "honorary chairman," giving clients rare access to the couple and their network of world leaders.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Clinton personally sought out [[declan-kelly|Declan Kelly]], her 41-year-old former fundraiser, as her economic envoy to Northern Ireland, giving him special status outside normal diplomatic channels while he continued earning about $2.4 million from Dow Chemical.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The article says [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed]] was among the foundation's biggest donors during her tenure and that she was to be installed for eight years after [[barack-obama|Obama]] as part of a sixteen-year plan — but "Hillary wasn't supposed to lose."[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The Guardian is cited noting that her involvement as Secretary of State contributed to a negotiated settlement in the IRS case against [[ubs|UBS]], after which UBS donations to the foundation grew sharply.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## The Russian Reset and Uranium One

"America for Sale — Part 3" places Clinton at the center of the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]].[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) In March 2009, as Secretary of State, she presented Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov with a "Reset" button whose Russian text mistakenly read "overcharged," a gesture meant to symbolize the Obama administration's outreach to Moscow.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The article argues the [[russian-reset|Russian Reset]] was planned well in advance and served to recast Russia publicly as a partner rather than an enemy, clearing what a cited Q drop calls the "pathway" for the uranium transaction.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

Because uranium is a strategic asset, the sale of Uranium One to Russia's [[rosatom|Rosatom]] required sign-off from a committee of federal agencies including the [[state-department|State Department]], which she headed; the article notes she consistently denied any role in the approval while her office's assent was necessary for it to proceed.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The transaction gave Russia one-fifth of American uranium production capacity along with the company's lucrative [[kazakhstan|Kazakhstan]] mines.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Undisclosed donations

As Secretary of State, Clinton had agreed with the Obama White House to publicly identify all [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] donors in order to avoid conflicts of interest.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The New York Times is cited reporting that Uranium One's chairman [[ian-telfer|Ian Telfer]] used his family charity, the [[fernwood-foundation|Fernwood Foundation]], to make four donations totaling $2.35 million that were never publicly disclosed, alongside between $1.3 million and $5.6 million in reported contributions from people tied to Uranium One or [[urasia-energy|UrAsia Energy]].[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The author calls the non-disclosure tax evasion — the same offense for which [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]] was indicted and pardoned — and asks why Clinton would allow Russia to acquire so much uranium and then, six years later, accuse Vladimir Putin of helping [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] steal the 2016 election.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

The agreement followed months of resistance to August subpoenas, and House Republicans had advanced criminal contempt of Congress resolutions — three Democrats voted for contempt against Hillary Clinton — before Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] announced on February 3 that the effort was "on pause."[7] Neither Clinton has been accused of wrongdoing related to Epstein's abuse of minors, and both say they were unaware of his crimes before public charges; they have criticized the probe's focus on them rather than the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s handling of the Epstein files.[7] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called the depositions "a shame" and praised Hillary Clinton as a "smart woman," while Rep. In a September 25, 2023 interview with [[msnbc|MSNBC]], Clinton maintained her assertion that Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had interfered in American elections and warned he would do so again in the 2024 presidential race "if he has a chance."[25] "I don't think, despite all of the deniers, there's any doubt that he interfered in our election or that he has interfered in many ways in the internal affairs of other countries—funding political parties, funding, you know, political candidates, buying off, you know, government officials in different places," she said.[25] She described Putin as hating democracy, the West and the United States, and said the Russians had "proved themselves to be quite adept at interfering."[25]

And he's quite good at it."[25] The 2016 Democratic nominee said Putin had "a lot of apologists and enablers in our own country," and believed part of the reason he "worked so hard against me is because he didn't think that he wanted me in the White House."[25] She also insisted the United States was heading toward fascism, urging rejection of "a kind of creeping fascism, almost, of people who are really ready to turn over their thinking, their votes, to wannabe dictators."[25] During the 2016 race she had accused [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s campaign of colluding with Russia.[25]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the Russia collusion framing had a "bicameral" intent for the [[deep-state|Deep State]]: to link Trump with Putin in the American mind, leveraging a century of anti-Soviet programming, and to hint at the possibility that American patriots did coordinate with Russian counterparts to "unrig" the 2016 election as the Q drops suggested.[25] He proposed inverting the Hegelian framing — that there may have been coordination rather than collusion, and that this would be a good thing.[25] [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] called the episode déjà vu, noting that with the 2024 election more than a year away the Clinton camp had already begun warning of Russian interference and of "misinformation," "disinformation" and "outright lies," and predicted the rhetoric would intensify as Trump's polling strengthened toward November 2024.[25]

## October surprise warning (September 2024)

Badlands commentary read the interview as a signal rather than a caution, noting the timing at the start of October, connecting it to the [[sean-combs-federal-trial|Sean Combs]] scandal then piercing mainstream awareness and to Harris's support from the hip hop world, and recalling that Clinton had served as a U.S. Senator from New York — the jurisdiction through which the highest-profile RICO cases had surfaced.[27]

## 2016 election post-mortem and the Russiagate strike force

Newly declassified evidence shows that the [[fbi|FBI]] was alerted in 2016 to intelligence indicating that Clinton planned to smear then-candidate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] by linking him to Russian leader [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], after which bureau leaders played into the campaign's strategy by conducting a sweeping investigation into false claims of Trump-Russia collusion.[28] Clinton's own words in a 2016 election post-mortem are described as likely to be key evidence for investigators, with public records showing that Clinton, in coordination with campaign general counsel [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]], campaign manager [[robby-mook|Robby Mook]], campaign chairman [[john-podesta|John Podesta]], communications director [[jennifer-palmieri|Jennifer Palmieri]], and policy adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]], launched an effort to tie Trump to Putin as the 2016 race unfolded.[28]

A central question hangs over the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] strike force set up by Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] to investigate the affair: whether it was merely a political dirty trick or rose to the level of a criminal conspiracy.[28] Badlands commentary placed the events on a tight timeline in which Clinton approved the plan to vilify Trump on July 26, 2016, [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] told the DNC on July 27 that Trump "cozies up to Putin," and [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] briefed Obama on the Clinton plan on July 28 as the Fusion Cell was created.[28] On August 4, 2025, Bondi signed an order directing a federal prosecutor to present evidence to a grand jury over the [[russiagate-hoax|Russia collusion hoax]].[28]

## COP28 extreme heat claims (December 2023)

Addressing the [[cop28|COP28]] climate conference in December 2023, Clinton claimed that "extreme heat" had killed half a billion people, most of them women and girls, without citing a source.[34] She said that climate-related deaths were beginning to be counted and recorded and that "by far the biggest killer is extreme heat," asserting that extreme heat had killed 61,000 people in Europe during the previous summer.[34] "We don't have that kind of number yet from Africa, Asia, Latin America but we know and estimate that we probably could measure about 500,000 deaths and the majority of those are women and girls and particularly pregnant women," she added.[34]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the remarks as a signal that "the new regime talking points have dropped" ahead of the 2024 election season, pairing them with [[bill-nye|Bill Nye]]'s statement that voting was the most important climate action, and noted rumors that Clinton might mount another presidential run should [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] be replaced by his own party.[34]

## "We lose total control" remarks on social media censorship (2024)

In early October 2024, Clinton told CNN host Michael Smerconish that social media companies must moderate content on their platforms or else "we lose total control."[35] Critics asked who exactly Clinton meant by "we," suggesting the referent was not the American people but what they termed the Censorship Blob — a mixture of federal agencies, legacy media, big tech, and fact-checkers that had held a monopoly on narrative control for decades.[35]

Clinton pointed to state-level action, saying "We can look at the state of California, the state of New York, I think some other states have also taken action," but added that "we need national action, and sadly, our [[congress|Congress]] has been dysfunctional when it comes to addressing these threats to our children."[35] She called for repealing [[section-230|Section 230]] of the Communications Act, which shields social media platforms from liability for third-party content, arguing that the original view of platforms as mere "pass-throughs" was "an overly simple view."[35] She warned that if platforms "whether it's Facebook or Twitter/X or Instagram or TikTok, whatever they are," fail to moderate and monitor content, "we lose total control," and stressed that the consequences were not merely social and psychological but "real life."[35]

Badlands commentary treated the remarks as evidence of establishment panic at the collapse of narrative dominance, drawing an analogy to the [[hartford-convention|Hartford Convention]] of 1814 and the decline of the [[federalist-party|Federalist Party]], and arguing that some actors care about power so completely that they would rather see the world burn than see somebody else wield it.[35]

## Memoir comments on Melania Trump (2024)

In her book "Something Lost, Something Gained," released on September 17, 2024, Clinton recounted meeting [[melania-trump|Melania Trump]] face-to-face for the first time since the 2016 election at a memorial service for Rosalynn Carter, writing that Melania "had a look on her face – very smiley but uncertain – that reminded me of the little kid at the birthday party who doesn't know anyone and is waiting at the edge of the circle, hoping people are going to be nice."[38] Clinton, then 76, wrote that neither White House staff nor the Secret Service detail had briefed her on Mrs. Trump's plan to attend, described Laura Bush stepping away from Melania as the other former first ladies entered, and recorded that Jill Biden air-kissed her cheek and [[michelle-obama|Michelle Obama]] gave her a hug while [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] received only a smile and few words.[38] Clinton said she shook Melania's hand and greeted her, adding that she could appreciate how awkward the encounter must have been after Donald Trump's insults directed at the Bushes, Bidens, Obamas and Clintons.[38]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] reacted with hostility to the passage and noted that Clinton had also called for the prosecution of Americans who traffic in "misinformation," a demand he read as aimed at outlets like [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] and warned could rebound on Clinton and her allies.[38]
