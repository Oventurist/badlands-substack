# Inter-Services Intelligence

The **Inter-Services Intelligence (ISI)** is Pakistan's principal intelligence agency, described by Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] as "Pakistan's CIA" and characterised as an open secret that the agency is kept on a tight leash by the United States and effectively runs [[pakistan|Pakistan]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Relationship with Imran Khan

[[imran-khan|Imran Khan]] was at first perceived as having ties to the military establishment including the ISI, particularly during his rise to political prominence, but his relationship with the agency became strained during his tenure and deteriorated sharply after his ouster by a parliamentary no-confidence vote in April 2022.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Khan attributed his removal to military and ISI interference, openly accusing then-ISI chief Nadeem Anjum and former army chief Gen. Qamar Javed Bajwa of orchestrating it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) With Khan gone, DeLarme argues, CIA- and deep state-friendly assets are once again running Pakistan and the ISI.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Clearinghouse function

One function of the [[cia|CIA]]-controlled ISI during the 1980s, when the Reagan government was arming and funding the [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] precursors against the Soviets, was to act as a major clearinghouse for U.S. weapons, equipment and funds destined for CIA and Department of Defense conflict zones — a function the article says continues today.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Operation Cyclone

Pakistan's Inter-Services Intelligence was the conduit through which American and Saudi money and weapons reached the Afghan [[mujahideen]] during [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]].2 The economist Michel Chossudovsky describes the ISI as having been essentially controlled by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] since the [[soviet-afghan-war|Soviet-Afghan War]], and Badlands Media argues the arrangement existed so that Western intelligence officials could deny involvement and pin responsibility on [[pakistan]].2

Routing aid through the ISI produced massive misappropriation, with many weapons sold in Karachi markets rather than delivered to the rebels.2 The agency also distributed aid unevenly: of the seven mujahideen groups it supported, the four espousing Islamic fundamentalist beliefs received most of the funding, weapons and training, and those factions later formed the [[taliban]].2 NBC's Michael Moran described [[osama-bin-laden|bin Laden]]'s [[maktab-al-khidamat|Maktab al-Khidamat]] as "nurtured by Pakistan's state security services... the CIA's primary conduit for conducting the covert war against Moscow's occupation."2

## Conduit for CIA money in the Soviet-Afghan War

"Who Is The Real Enemy?" describes the ISI as directly connected to the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], a relationship it identifies as one reason the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] scheme survived as long as it did — the bank being run jointly, in the article's account, by the intelligence services of the United States, Saudi Arabia and [[pakistan|Pakistan]].[3]

The CIA funded [[gulbuddin-hekmatyar|Gulbuddin Hekmatyar]]'s [[hezb-e-islami|Hezb-e Islami]] [[mujahideen|mujahideen]] organization through the ISI from the start of the [[soviet-afghan-war|Soviet–Afghan War]] in 1979.[3] Bags of dollar bills were flown into Pakistan and handed over to ISI director Lieutenant General [[akhtar-abdur-rahman|Akhtar Abdur Rahman]], who banked the cash in ISI accounts held at the National Bank of Pakistan, BCCI and the Bank of Oman.[3]

Former Afghan president [[hamid-karzai|Hamid Karzai]] later told Der Spiegel that the extremist attacks in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] from about 2005 onward were organized by intelligence in Pakistan and carried out by the [[taliban|Taliban]], with the Taliban's sanctuaries located there — a finding he said the United States confirmed independently before choosing to fund Pakistan's military instead of acting on it.[3]
