# Ronald Hayes

Ronald Hayes was a professor of environmental science at Dalhousie University and a Canadian civil servant, described by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as one leading Canadian scientist who took an early stand against the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]]-driven transformation of national science policy.[1]

## The Chaining of Prometheus

In his 1973 book *The Chaining of Prometheus: The Evolution of a Power Structure for Canadian Science*, Hayes identified [[maurice-lamontagne|Maurice Lamontagne]] as a minion of the god Zeus as portrayed in Aeschylus' drama *Prometheus Bound* — the ancient Greek play in which the demi-god Prometheus is punished for ten thousand years for teaching humanity how to use the fire Zeus had monopolized for himself.[1]

## The "Egyptian Syndrome"

Attacking the call to deconstruct the entire 1938–1971 science funding structure and rebuild it under a new technocratic regime, Hayes said the main problem with the Lamontagne approach was what he called the Egyptian Syndrome: the notion that if only everything built up could be destroyed and the land reduced to desert, everyone would be equal and a better world could be started.[1] In this vein, he charged, Lamontagne wanted to destroy the National Research Council, the body that had nurtured and launched much of Canada's government research and got graduate programs going in its universities — a fault of the Trudeau administration which Lamontagne echoed.[1]

## Critique of the Treasury Board

Hayes also attacked the newly formed powers of the Treasury Board, which had been given exceptional control of science policy under what he saw as a new scientific dictatorship, writing that "the most subtle exercise of power, which obviates the necessity of close control, is infiltration by reliable people — the creation of a ruling elite," and noting that as such Englishmen had become known the world over as rulers of the British Empire, so the Public Service Commission was grooming future Canadian government managers to follow the general policies and precepts of the Treasury Board.[1]

Ehret presents Hayes as an anticipation of the more forceful response later given by [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]] in *There Are No Limits to Growth* (1982).[1]
