# Seth Keshel

Seth Keshel is an election-integrity analyst and author associated with the American election-fraud research community.[1]

## The American War on Election Corruption

Keshel appeared on episode 144 of [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]]'s program Culture of Change to discuss his book *The American War on Election Corruption*, with the conversation ranging across fake voters, fake ballots, and fake counts, but concentrating most heavily on voter registration.[1] Ashe summarized the thesis that fake voters are the foundation of the corrupt election machine and that none of its other components function without them.[1] During the discussion Keshel also spoke about his recent meetings in Washington, DC.[1] The segment appeared alongside coverage of the [[north-carolina-voter-id-law|North Carolina voter ID law]] ruling in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]].[1]

## Early-vote turnout as a leading indicator

Keshel's analytical framework treats early-voting turnout as a leading indicator of election outcomes rather than a mere convenience statistic.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] invoked his work when assessing reported Republican early-vote turnout in [[nevada|Nevada]] during the [[2024-early-voting|2024 early-voting period]], arguing that if the pattern reflected a national trend also visible in Georgia, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] could win the popular vote — while cautioning that turnout by party registration is not the same as vote totals.[2]

## The elections casino thesis (April 2025)

In an April 10, 2025 essay for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] titled "Inside the Modern Elections Casino," Keshel argued that American elections operate like casino games: winning is possible, but the rules are structured so that "the house" profits most of the time, and even more reliably when players "show up drunk, bet foolishly, or get too aggressive."[3] Drawing on childhood memories of accompanying his father to casinos in Gulfport, Mississippi, and on his own knowledge of card counting and textbook Blackjack strategy, he framed [[automatic-voter-registration|Automatic Voter Registration]], universal [[mail-in-voting|mail-in voting]], [[ballot-harvesting|ballot harvesting]], drop boxes and [[ranked-choice-voting|ranked choice voting]] as house advantages that his forecasting model now weights explicitly.[3]

Keshel distinguished his position from that of Badlands colleague [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] and from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s description of elections as outright "fake" when Trump signed his [[2025-executive-order-on-elections|2025 executive order on elections]], saying he was "not so far down the rabbit hole" as to believe Trump had won an entirely fake election after surviving years of lawfare and even bullets.[3]

He had predicted that [[susan-crawford|Susan Crawford]] would win the [[2025-wisconsin-supreme-court-election|Wisconsin Supreme Court race]] by at least five points; she won by ten, exceeding [[janet-protasiewicz|Janet Protasiewicz]]'s spring-election record by nearly 300,000 votes while [[brad-schimel|Brad Schimel]] simultaneously set a new record on the losing side.[3] His related post on X drew more than 1.4 million views and prompted a rebuttal from [[the-clay-travis-and-buck-sexton-show|The Clay Travis & Buck Sexton Show]].[3]

Analyzing the [[2024-us-general-election|2024 general election]], Keshel noted that [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] trailed [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s vote count in 44 states but exceeded it in Utah, Maine, North Carolina, Wisconsin, Nevada and Georgia — four of them battlegrounds — and that removing Georgia, Nevada, Wisconsin and North Carolina from Trump's column would have left him at 264 electoral votes, short of 270.[3] He concluded that without major reforms Americans are "doomed to quasi-elections," and warned that a future GOP nominee lacking Trump's fame "will get to see exactly what it is the house has in terms of insurance."[3]
