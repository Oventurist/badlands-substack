# Bashar al-Assad

Bashar al-Assad is the President of Syria and the target of the regime-change campaign the article describes as the direct sequel to Libya.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

[[john-mccain|John McCain]] and the lobbyist [[mouaz-moustafa|Mouaz Moustafa]] were, in the article's account, working to overthrow Assad, which is why McCain travelled to Syria with Moustafa in 2013 to meet with [[isis|ISIS]] — two years after the pair had been jointly involved in trying to overthrow [[muammar-gaddafi|Gaddafi]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The [[syrian-emergency-task-force|Syrian Emergency Task Force]] ostensibly raised money for a Syrian rebel army fighting Assad.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

The article situates Assad within a broader geopolitical frame, describing [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] as a CIA asset who was a major part of the plan to expand ISIS and "take down another Putin ally, Bashar al-Assad in Syria."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) It also notes that the United States had no diplomatic relations with Syria at the time of McCain's secret visit.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## The Libya-to-Syria weapons pipeline

In "A Dereliction of Duty," Badlands Media argues that the entire Benghazi operation existed to arm ISIS for the purpose of overthrowing Assad.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|Christopher Stevens]] is described as the liaison between weapons dealers and Al Qaeda in Libya and as a coordinator of weapons transfers from Libya to Syria to arm ISIS in its campaign against Assad.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The Times reported that ships carrying surface-to-air missiles from Libya anchored off the Lebanese coast while the weapons were ferried ashore under cover of darkness and passed to Syrian rebel fighters, and that among the cargo were shoulder-launched, heat-seeking missiles capable of downing the warplanes and attack helicopters Assad's regime was increasingly deploying to retain power.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) An arms dealer quoted in that reporting said Libyan leader [[mustafa-abdul-jalil|Abdul Jalil]] wanted to weaken the Libyan militias, so there was an agreement to send the weapons to Syria.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Separately, the World Socialist Web Site reported that [[cia|CIA]] agents deployed to [[turkey|Turkey]] directed a massive smuggling operation to arm the Syrian opposition, financed by [[qatar|Qatar]], [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and Turkey.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## Name

In its section on [[arabic-naming-conventions|Arabic naming conventions]], "Arabian Nights" notes that the Syrian president's surname, al-Assad, means "the lion."[3] During the 19th century an ancestor was given the laqab (honorary title) al-Wahsh, meaning "the wild beast," because he was such a powerful fighter; in 1927 the family changed it to the more noble version.[3]


## Fall from power (December 2024)

An unexpected offensive by rebel forces led by [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] and its allies produced the dramatic collapse of Assad's regime in December 2024.[4] The rebels launched their campaign on November 27, 2024, captured Aleppo, and by December 8 had taken Hama, Homs, and Damascus, leading to Assad's reported resignation and departure from [[syria|Syria]] for Moscow.[4] The rapid advance was facilitated by the withdrawal of Syrian government forces, possibly reflecting a lack of support from allies such as [[russia|Russia]] and [[iran|Iran]], who were preoccupied with other conflicts.[4]

Badlands Media argued that the Syrian population was not unanimously joyful at his removal: while many welcomed relatives released from government prisons, religious strongholds held Assad in high regard, notably Alawites, from whom Assad hails, and minorities such as Christians historically wary of Sunni Islamist opposition groups.[4] The article placed Assad in a sequence of leaders — Saddam Hussein, Muammar Gaddafi, the Taliban, and Vladimir Putin — subjected to the same "overthrown tyrant" media template, while noting that Washington remains cozy with dictators in Egypt and Saudi Arabia.[4] [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] had met Assad in January 2017 on a self-described fact-finding mission, the first member of the U.S. Congress to do so since the civil war began in 2011.[4]

## December 2024 statement denying a planned exit

On Monday, December 16, 2024, Assad issued his first public remarks since the fall of Damascus, saying he had fled Syria only after the capital had fallen and denouncing the country's new leaders as "terrorists."[5] The statement, released on the ousted presidency's Telegram channel, said: "My departure from Syria was neither planned nor did it occur during the final hours of the battles, as some have claimed," adding that he had remained in Damascus carrying out his duties until the early hours of Sunday, December 8.[5] It stated that as "terrorist forces infiltrated Damascus," he moved to Latakia in coordination with Russian allies to oversee combat operations, arriving at the Hmeimim base that morning.[5] As the field situation deteriorated and the Russian base came under intensified drone attack, Moscow requested that the base command arrange an immediate evacuation to Russia on the evening of December 8.[5] Five former officials had previously told AFP that Assad was already out of the country hours before militants seized Damascus.[5]

Assad captioned the post by saying that "after several unsuccessful attempts to release the statement through Arabic and international media outlets, the only viable option was to publish it on the social media accounts of the former presidency," and promised further details of the evacuation.[5] [[kevork-almassian|Kevork Almassian]] said he had verified the statement's authenticity through contacts in Syria, while Badlands host [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] observed that Assad's continued control of official Syrian government social media accounts felt "Devolution-y."[5]

Badlands read the statement as supporting its own timeline suggesting that Israel and Russia had come into direct conflict, since the drone strikes on the Russian airbase at Latakia coincided with widely reported Israeli strikes on "airbases in Latakia" around December 10.[5] The Brief also addressed a competing narrative — based on photographs of printed correspondence — that the Israeli government and the Assad regime had been in secret contact and working together against Iran, with an Israeli operative code-named "Mousa" messaging former Syrian Defense Minister Lt. Gen. Ali Mahmoud Abbas as far back as May 2023 to coordinate strikes on Iranian proxies using Russia as an intermediary, allegedly in exchange for Israel sparing Syrian military sites.[5] Badlands dismissed this as a [[mossad|Mossad]]- and CIA-driven effort to sow division between Assad and the Syrian people and between Russia and Iran, noting Russia's security agreements with Iran and its long-standing military presence in Syria, bolstered in 2015 to help Assad fight what the Brief calls the CIA's military coup, commonly referred to as ISIS.[5]

## Exile in Moscow and the 2025 poisoning claim

In October 2025 the UK-based [[syrian-observatory-for-human-rights|Syrian Observatory for Human Rights]] cited an anonymous source claiming that Assad had been discharged from a hospital in the Moscow Region after a supposed poisoning attempt in September, a rumor widely recirculated by Western and Russian outlets.[6] Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] rejected the claim, stating that Assad "has no problem living in our capital," that "no poisonings have occurred," and that Russia had granted asylum to Assad and his family "for purely humanitarian reasons" after they faced threats of physical harm following the change of power in Damascus.[6]

During a Kremlin visit that same week, Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]] was reported to be preparing to formally ask Moscow to hand Assad over to face trial over alleged crimes against Syrians, a request Russia was seen as unlikely to grant.[6]

### Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry characterized the poisoning story, alongside a separate report that Assad had become addicted to first-person shooter video games, as psychological operations designed to extinguish whatever residual relevance Assad retains to Syria's future.[6] He argued the portrayal is implausible given that Assad is a trained surgeon who had no political ambitions before the death of his older brother in the 1990s, when his father recalled him from London to prepare as successor.[6] He noted that the Israeli outlet Ynet's article also carried a February video of Assad's son Hafez, filmed in an upscale Moscow neighborhood, in which Hafez said there had been "no plan, not even a backup plan, to leave Damascus, let alone Syria," recounted being in Moscow for doctoral studies while his mother was there for medical treatment, and described the family's December 2024 departure from an empty Damascus International Airport around three in the morning aboard a Russian military plane to Hmeimim Airbase near Latakia.[6] Concluding that the continued media targeting of Assad — despite a reported "vow of silence" he took for [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] — suggests the Uniparty remains threatened by him, the author asked what leverage or knowledge Assad might still hold and said he remains "skeptical of everything."[6]

## Aftermath of the regime's fall (December 2024)

Following the toppling of Assad's government in December 2024, Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] claimed extensive credit for the outcome, prompting Badlands commentary to ask whether the timing of the Syrian collapse was connected to Netanyahu's own long-delayed corruption trial.[7] Israeli forces moved to secure additional territory in the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]], and Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] announced plans for a "sterile zone" in southern Syria - a rationale Badlands questioned by pointing to the Al Qaeda-linked fighters said to have been employed to overthrow Assad.[7]

## Saudi realignment in Badlands commentary

Reviewing [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s September 2024 thanks to Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] for brokering the [[2024-us-russia-prisoner-swap|US–Russia prisoner swap]], Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] revisited MBS's October 2015 visit to Russia as then–defence minister for talks on Syria, arguing that the episode contradicted the mainstream narrative that the Saudis opposed Assad and were assisting NATO's attempt to overthrow him.[8] He connected this to MBS's role in bringing Assad and Syria back into the [[arab-league|Arab League]] on May 19, 2023, eleven years after their 2012 expulsion, which he attributed to Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[8]

## Departure and asylum in Moscow (December 2024)

As opposition forces entered [[damascus|Damascus]] in December 2024, unverified reports placed Assad and top officials in Dubai, in Latakia, or in transit, while jihadist fighters reached the heart of the capital and Syrian forces abandoned the city without a battle.[9] Senior Russian diplomat [[mikhail-ulyanov|Mikhail Ulyanov]] said early the following Monday that Assad and his family were in Moscow, apparently confirming reports that the former president had been granted asylum, with Russian agencies citing diplomatic sources describing asylum "on humanitarian grounds."[9] The [[russian-foreign-ministry|Russian Foreign Ministry]] said Assad agreed to step down after back-channel talks with unspecified armed groups and instructed officials to conduct "a peaceful transfer of power."[9] The Syrian Army stood down as [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] and US-armed [[free-syrian-army|Free Syrian Army]] fighters advanced and declared him deposed.[9]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that this account was largely semantic, noting that Assad had reportedly been in Moscow meeting [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] the previous week and doubting he would return to a collapsing warzone capital only to flee again — suggesting he never left Moscow and the "flight" reporting was dramatic effect.[9]

## Motives ascribed to the regime's overthrow

Badlands commentary offered three explanations for the [[fall-of-the-assad-regime|fall of the Assad regime]]. Around 2009 two competing Persian Gulf pipeline projects — one from Iran terminating in Lebanon, one from Qatar through Turkey into Europe — would have challenged Russia's Nord Stream, and both required Syrian transit; Assad reportedly rejected both.[9] Second, after the death of his older brother, the ophthalmologist Bashar became the unlikely heir to his father Hafez al-Assad and in 2005 proposed the [[five-seas-vision|Five Seas Vision]], a rail network connecting the Mediterranean, Red, Black and Caspian seas and the Persian Gulf with Aleppo at its heart; Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and China had reportedly approached him about reviving it under the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] and [[saudi-vision-2030|Saudi Vision 2030]].[9] Third, the [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] thesis, with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] boasting of ordering annexation in the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]] and [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] having claimed Damascus for Israel on scriptural grounds.[9] Netanyahu separately claimed credit for starting the "historic" chain of events that felled the regime, attributing it to blows against Iran and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[9]
