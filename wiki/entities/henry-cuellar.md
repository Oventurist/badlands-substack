# Henry Cuellar

Enrique "Henry" Cuellar is a Democratic member of the United States House of Representatives from Texas, known for his heterodox positions on border security and immigration within his party.[1] In 2024 he was indicted on charges of bribery and money laundering, with related charges filed against his wife, Imelda.[1]

## Presidential pardon

On Wednesday, December 3, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced on Truth Social that he would pardon Cuellar, extending the pardon to Imelda Cuellar as well.[1] Trump accused the former Biden administration of having targeted the congressman over his opposition to its mass immigration policies.[1]

"For years, the Biden Administration weaponized the Justice System against their Political Opponents, and anyone who disagreed with them," Trump wrote, describing the prosecution of Cuellar as one of the clearest examples of that pattern and asserting that "Crooked Joe used the FBI and DOJ to 'take out' a member of his own Party" after Cuellar spoke out against open borders and what Trump called the Biden border "Catastrophe."[1]

Trump added that "Sleepy Joe went after the Congressman, and even the Congressman's wonderful wife, Imelda, simply for speaking the TRUTH," and concluded by announcing "my full and unconditional PARDON of beloved Texas Congressman Henry Cuellar, and Imelda," telling him: "Henry, I don't know you, but you can sleep well tonight — Your nightmare is finally over!"[1]

The pardon drew attention because Cuellar had previously accused Trump of inciting insurrection and had voted for his impeachment.[1]

## 2024 bribery and foreign influence indictment

On Friday, May 3, 2024, the Justice Department released an indictment against Cuellar and his wife, [[imelda-cuellar|Imelda Cuellar]], charging the pair with bribery and money laundering related to their ties with a bank in Mexico and an oil and gas company controlled by Azerbaijan.[2] NBC News was first to report that the charges were coming; the congressman and his wife were each released on a $100,000 bond after a first appearance in federal court in Houston, a Justice Department spokesperson said.[2]

According to the indictment, from 2014 to 2021 the Cuellars allegedly accepted roughly $600,000 in bribes from the two foreign entities in exchange for the congressman performing official acts. The department stated that the payments were "allegedly laundered, pursuant to sham consulting contracts, through a series of front companies and middlemen into shell companies owned by Imelda Cuellar, who performed little to no legitimate work under the contracts."[2]
