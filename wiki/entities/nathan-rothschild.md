# Nathan Rothschild

Nathan Rothschild was an English central banker of the [[rothschild-family|Rothschild]] banking dynasty who, in Badlands Media's account, attempted to coerce the United States into renewing the charter of the [[first-bank-of-the-united-states|First Bank of the United States]].[1]

## The 1811 charter threat

When the First Bank's twenty-year charter came up for renewal in 1811, Congress declined to renew it despite a reported threat from Nathan Rothschild, who is quoted as saying: "Either the application for renewal of the charter is granted, or the United States will find itself involved in a most disastrous war."[1] A year later, the article notes, thousands of Americans died in a war against Britain; the piece observes that the conflict could have been worse but that the British were still fighting Napoleon and could not mount much of an assault, with the war ending in 1814 with America undefeated.[1]

## Interpretation

Badlands Media presents the episode as an illustration of its broader thesis that there has long existed a class of international money changers who see themselves as above nations and their elected leaders, ruling by way of creating private central banks and loaning money at interest (usury) so as to control nations through perpetual debt.[1] The quotation and its causal link to the War of 1812 are contested by mainstream historians and rest on sources the article itself flags as belonging to the "crazy conspiracy theorist" tradition.[1]

## Founding of the London house

Ryan DeLarme's "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 6" identifies Nathan Mayer Rothschild — "N. M." as he liked to be called — as the son of [[mayer-amschel-rothschild|Mayer Amschel Rothschild]] sent to London as part of the "Frankfurt Five," and repeats the dissenting account that his banking operation was launched with money originating in Hesse-Kassel that had been paid to [[william-ix-of-hesse-kassel|William IX]] for supplying Hessian troops against the American colonists.[2] Sympathetic biographer [[derek-wilson|Derek Wilson]] conceded that "it was the temporary diversion of the immense sums of money originating in Hesse-Kassel which enabled N. M. to launch his banking operation, providing him with both liquidity and prestige."[2] Wilson also noted that Nathan was merely one of several London financiers in 1810 but by 1815 had become the principal financier to the British government and the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]], a coup achieved through dealings "encased in a secrecy which cannot now be penetrated."[2]

## Waterloo and the consols coup

In 1815 the brothers supplied gold to both Wellington's army, through Nathan in England, and Napoleon's, through Jacob in France.[2] A Rothschild courier named "Rothworth" is said to have brought Nathan news of the British victory at Waterloo a full twenty-four hours before Wellington's own messenger; Nathan then had his floor traders dump British consols, triggering a panic sell-off, before quietly buying them back, reportedly earning about twenty to one when the true result became public.[2] DeLarme argues the maneuver gave the family effective control of the British economy — then the financial centre of the world — and forced England to establish a new Bank of England.[2]

## "I control the British money supply"

Nathan is credited with the frequently quoted 1815 statement: "I care not what puppet is placed upon the throne of England to rule the Empire on which the sun never sets. The man who controls Britain's money supply controls the British Empire, and I control the British money supply."[2] He is also said to have boasted that in his seventeen years in England he had increased the £20,000 stake given him by his father 2,500 times, to £50 million, making him the most successful of the five brothers.[2] [[niall-ferguson|Niall Ferguson]] traced Nathan's entry into royal circles to his father's purchase of debts owed by George, Prince Regent — later King George IV — and his brothers.[2]
