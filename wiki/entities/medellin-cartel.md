# Medellín Cartel

The Medellín cartel, led by [[pablo-escobar|Pablo Escobar]], was in the late 1980s the most powerful drug-trafficking organization in the world.[1]

In the Badlands account, [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] established banking and money-laundering links between the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and the Medellín and [[cali-cartel|Cali]] cartels while working in Caracas, helping lay the groundwork for the Reagan-Bush administration's 1980s covert war against Nicaragua and leftist guerrillas in El Salvador; cartel money helped finance the Nicaraguan contras in return for CIA-supplied weapons.[1] The article states the cartel was CIA-connected and that the agency trained its accountants and private bankers to launder billions each year.[1]

As Colombian violence escalated, Escobar and the government entered indirect negotiations by 1988. In 1989 the cartel dispatched Vallejo to Miami to present a proposal to a lawyer who had previously resolved legal difficulties in the United States for cartel member Jorge Ochoa and who was working with a team of corporate attorneys headed by Jeb Bush — the idea being that the lawyer would persuade the president's son to speak privately to his father about the deal.[1]
