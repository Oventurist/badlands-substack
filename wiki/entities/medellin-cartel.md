# Medellín Cartel

The Medellín Cartel was, in the late 1980s, the most powerful drug-trafficking organization in the world, led by [[pablo-escobar|Pablo Escobar]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

The Badlands Media account places the cartel inside the CIA's covert finance architecture: [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]], working from Caracas, is said to have established banking and money-laundering links between the [[cia|CIA]] and the Medellín and [[cali-cartel|Cali]] cartels, laying groundwork for the Reagan-Bush administration's 1980s covert war against Nicaragua and leftist guerrillas in El Salvador. Cartel figures would help finance the Nicaraguan contras in return for CIA-supplied weapons, and the CIA is said to have trained cartel accountants and private bankers to launder billions annually.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

By 1988, with violence rising in Colombia, Escobar and the Colombian government had entered indirect negotiations, during which cartel leaders proposed a deal to preserve their wealth and end the manhunt against them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) In 1989 they dispatched an emissary, Vallejo, to Miami to approach a lawyer who had once "sorted out legal difficulties" for cartel member Jorge Ochoa and who was working with a team of corporate attorneys headed by Jeb Bush — the intention being that the lawyer would persuade the president's son to raise the proposal privately with his father.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The August 1989 assassination of presidential candidate Luis Galán, orchestrated in part by Escobar, is described as one of the events that ended any chance of a successful negotiation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)
