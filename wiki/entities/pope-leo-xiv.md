# Pope Leo XIV

Pope Leo XIV is the reigning pontiff of the Catholic Church, who in April 2026 became the subject of a public clash with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] Born Cardinal Robert Francis Prevost, he was elected by the Vatican conclave in May 2025 as the first American-born pope, taking leadership of the world's 1.4 billion Catholics.[5]

## Election (May 2025)

Prevost, 69 at the time of his election and originally from Chicago, chose the papal name Leo XIV.[5] He had most recently served as head of the dicastery responsible for the appointment of bishops and as archbishop emeritus of Chiclayo, Peru.[5] His first public words, delivered from the balcony of St. Peter's Basilica, were "Peace be with all you," and were met with joyful cheering from a crowd of roughly 150,000 people according to Italian officials.[5]

Badlands commentator Jordan Sather urged readers not to let enthusiasm over an American pope cloud their judgment, arguing that Prevost's social-media timeline suggested he was "quite woke."[5] Sather noted that despite Prevost being from the United States, roughly 90% of his timeline was in Spanish, and said the new pope had spent only two of the previous twenty years in the States.[5] He further asserted that Prevost had promoted COVID-19 vaccines and George Floyd/Black Lives Matter themes, and had recently criticized Trump and [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] over immigration policy.[5]

## Clash with Donald Trump

Trump intensified a public confrontation with Leo XIV after criticizing the Pope's leadership and amplifying social-media content that portrayed Trump in religious imagery many online observers considered representative of Jesus Christ.[1] The posts circulated widely and prompted backlash from religious commentators, Catholic voices, and political observers, many of whom described the imagery as provocative and inappropriate.[1] The episode added to a broader pattern of tension between Trump and Catholic leadership, particularly over immigration, nationalism, and the role of moral authority in global politics.[1]

## Defense by Masoud Pezeshkian

Iranian President [[masoud-pezeshkian|Masoud Pezeshkian]] publicly defended the Pope against Trump's criticisms while signalling that [[iran|Iran]] remained willing to make a nuclear deal under the correct conditions.[1] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the intervention as evidence against the claim that Muslims are uniformly hostile to Christianity, noting alongside it a photograph of a [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] soldier cleaning a statue of the Virgin Mary while on patrol in Syria.[1]

## Visit to Lebanon (November–December 2025)

The plane carrying Leo XIV landed in Beirut on Sunday, 30 November 2025, beginning a three-day visit to Lebanon that formed the second leg of his first foreign journey since being elected.[2] According to the official schedule, the pope was to visit the Monastery of Saint Maron in Annaya and pray at the tomb of a major saint, meet Catholic patriarchs, hold an interreligious encounter in Beirut, meet with youth groups, and visit a psychiatric hospital.[2] He was also to celebrate an outdoor Mass on the Beirut Waterfront and pray at the site of the 2020 Beirut port explosion.[2]

## Inauguration Mass (May 2025)

Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] was in Rome in May 2025, attending Pope Leo's inauguration Mass at the Vatican on the Sunday before [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s planned ceasefire calls with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Kyiv.[3]

## Warning on Artificial Intelligence (May 2025)

In one of his first public speeches after becoming the first US-born pope, Leo XIV addressed the cardinals who had elected him on Saturday, May 10, 2025, raising longstanding themes of faith and care for the indigent alongside a concern the Catholic Church has taken up only recently: artificial intelligence.[4] Explaining his choice of the name Leo — last used by Pope Leo XIII at the end of the 19th century — he drew a parallel between the Industrial Revolution of that era and the potential of a second revolution in work driven by AI.[4]

He pointed to Leo XIII's 1891 encyclical "On Capital and Labor," which sought greater respect for workers' rights and unions, limited working hours and fair wages, and quoted its warning that "the discussion is not easy, nor is it void of danger" and that "it is no easy matter to define the relative rights and mutual duties of the rich and of the poor, of capital and of labor."[4] Leo XIV said he saw clear parallels between the Gilded Age conditions his predecessor addressed and the present AI boom, with its massive data centers, AI bots and tech billionaires.[4]
