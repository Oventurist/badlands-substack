# Bank of North America

The Bank of North America was the first quasi-national bank of the United States, opened in 1782 through the efforts of [[robert-morris|Robert Morris]] in his capacity as Superintendent of Finance.[1]

## Founding

The Articles of Confederation had extended to Congress the sovereign power to generate bills of credit; in 1782 Congress passed an ordinance to incorporate a privately subscribed national bank, following in the footsteps of the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]].[1] A significant portion of the founding fathers were strongly opposed to any national banking system, in part because England's attempt to place the colonies under the monetary control of the Bank of England had been seen by many as the "last straw" of oppression that led directly to the American Revolutionary War.[1]

## Failure to become a true national bank

The bank was ultimately thwarted in fulfilling its intended role as a national bank because of objections over "alarming foreign influence" and "fictitious credit."[1] Its unfinished business was taken up in 1791 by [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]], a former Morris aide, whose compromise with Southern legislators produced the [[first-bank-of-the-united-states|First Bank of the United States]].[1]
