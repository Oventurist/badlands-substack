# Turkey

Turkey is a NATO member state bordering Syria, and in the Badlands Media corpus it is treated as the principal transit country for weapons allegedly moved from Libya to armed groups fighting the Syrian government.

## Role in the Badlands corpus

A Dereliction of Duty describes Turkey as a knowing participant in the alleged smuggling pipeline. Citing the World Socialist Web Site's summary of a June 2012 New York Times report, the article states that [[cia|CIA]] agents were deployed to Turkey to organise the arming of the "so-called rebels" in Syria, directing a smuggling operation in which "automatic rifles, rocket-propelled grenades, ammunition and some antitank weapons" were funnelled mostly across the Turkish border through a network of intermediaries including the Syrian [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]], and "paid for by [[qatar|Qatar]], [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and Turkey."

Turkey also features in the article's account of the shipping routes: quoting The Times, it says vessels originated in [[benghazi|Benghazi]], docked in Alexandria, Egypt, where the manifest was changed, and then continued to [[lebanon|Lebanon]] or Turkey. Fox News reporting cited by the article confirmed that the Libyan-flagged vessel Al Entisar ("The Victory"), reportedly carrying weapons bound for Syrian rebels, was received at the Turkish port of Iskenderun — thirty-five miles from the Syrian border — on September 6, 2012, five days before the Benghazi attack.

Most pointedly, the article notes that on the night of September 11, 2012, in what would become his last known public meeting, Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|Christopher Stevens]] met with Turkish Consul General [[ali-sait-akin|Ali Sait Akin]] and escorted him out of the consulate's front gate roughly one hour before the assault began at approximately 9:35 p.m. local time. The article presents the timing, and the choice of the poorly defended Benghazi consulate rather than the embassy in Tripoli as the meeting place, as evidence that Stevens was set up.


## April 2026: host of the Antalya Diplomacy Forum

The Brief of April 20, 2026 reports that Turkey hosted [[egypt|Egypt]], [[pakistan|Pakistan]], and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] at the [[antalya-diplomacy-forum|Antalya Diplomacy Forum]] to discuss the [[iran|Iran]] situation. In the security architecture outlined by the commentary, Turkey is a NATO member with the largest army in the Middle East — one of the five countries (with Iran expected to join) that could stop the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]]'s campaign to establish Greater Israel, "backed by Russia, China, and Europe."

## Host of the Ankara NATO summit (July 2026)

The July 8, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that Turkey hosted the [[nato|NATO]] summit in Ankara, where President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] exchanged warm compliments "much to the chagrin of the rest of the so-called alliance," with Trump saying the only reason he attended was that the summit was hosted by his "dear friend." Contributor [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] reads the episode as evidence that Trump is prioritizing the relationship with Turkey over the relationship with Europe — predicting similar treatment for other Muslim nations of the Middle East, including [[iran|Iran]] — and as evidence of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] endeavoring to supplant the unipolar hegemony with a new multipolar world order, with Turkey "the stronger partner at the moment."

## ISF volunteers and the Board of Peace (February 2026)

The February 20, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that [[turkey|Turkey]] has volunteered soldiers and police for the [[international-stabilization-force|International Stabilization Force]] in [[gaza|Gaza]] — a contribution President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he wants represented in the force — while Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and other Israeli officials "emphatically stated" that Turkish participation "will never be allowed." Turkey's earlier mediation role, alongside [[qatar|Qatar]], in filling the vacuum created by Israel's snub of the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] is also noted in the Brief.
