# Turkey

Turkey is described in the article as a transit state and partner in the smuggling of Libyan weapons to Syria.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

Citing the World Socialist Web Site's account of a June 2012 New York Times report, the article says [[cia|CIA]] agents were deployed to Turkey to organize the arming of Syrian rebels, directing a smuggling operation in which automatic rifles, rocket-propelled grenades, ammunition and antitank weapons were funneled mostly across the Turkish border through intermediaries including the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]], paid for by [[qatar|Qatar]], Saudi Arabia and Turkey.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Arms ships originating in [[benghazi|Benghazi]] docked in Alexandria, Egypt, where manifests were changed, before continuing to Lebanon or Turkey.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The Libyan-flagged vessel Al Entisar ("The Victory"), reportedly carrying weapons bound for Syrian rebels, was received at the Turkish port of Iskenderun — 35 miles from the Syrian border — on September 6, 2012, five days before the [[benghazi-attack|Benghazi attack]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) On the night of September 11, Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|Christopher Stevens]] held his last known public meeting with Turkish Consul General Ali Sait Akin, escorting him out the consulate front gate about an hour before the assault began.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The article treats the meeting's location and timing as more than coincidence.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)


## Role in the 2024 Syrian offensive

Following the collapse of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]]'s government in December 2024, Turkey denied supporting [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] despite aiding some rebel factions, according to Badlands Media.[2] The article noted that the management of the power transition raised concerns for various factions inside [[syria|Syria]], including Kurds and Turkish-backed groups.[2]

## Target of the 2026 escalation narrative

In July 2026 Ukrainian drones struck the Krasnodarskaya compressor station serving the [[blue-stream-pipeline|Blue Stream]] pipeline, which transports Russian gas to Türkiye across the Black Sea; [[gazprom|Gazprom]] reported minor damage and uninterrupted deliveries.[3] Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] said [[russia|Russia]] expected countries affected by the pipeline, including Türkiye, to respond.[3] The separate [[turkstream-pipeline|TurkStream]] pipeline also carries Russian gas through Türkiye to several European countries.[3]

The strike came days after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] visited Turkey for a [[nato|NATO]] summit and repeated high praise for President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Erdoğan]] and Turkey — a move Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said triggered widespread rebuke among the Israeli diaspora and "Mossad Media assets."[3] He argued Ukraine's indirect attack on Turkey was carried out on behalf of [[israel|Israel]], which had been "pounding the war drums" toward Turkey for months, and noted that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had identified [[greece|Greece]] as one of Turkey's intended targets.[3] Watchpoints named for a possible NATO/Israeli operation included [[cyprus|Cyprus]], the TurkStream route, and Turkey's Aegean west coast.[3]

## April 2026: Antalya Diplomacy Forum host

In April 2026 Turkey hosted [[egypt|Egypt]], [[pakistan|Pakistan]], and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] at the [[antalya-diplomacy-forum|Antalya Diplomacy Forum]] to discuss the [[iran|Iran]] situation.[4] Badlands analysis described Turkey as a NATO member with the largest army in the Middle East and counted it among the four states forming an emerging regional security architecture expected eventually to include Iran.[4] The Brief asked what would follow when Turkey, Egypt, and Pakistan concluded that the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]]'s campaign to conquer the Middle East had to be stopped kinetically.[4]

## "Tremendous ally" framing (July 2026)

In July 2026 President [[donald-trump|Trump]] publicly hailed Turkey as a "tremendous ally" while simultaneously criticizing Israeli military operations during a meeting with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[5] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the juxtaposition as a repetition of the September 2025 pattern in which Trump hosted President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] the day before Netanyahu's UN General Assembly address, using Turkey to signal his real intent, and asked what moves would be made against Turkey after Netanyahu's visit.[5]

## Cultural refuge role (2026)
[[kanye-west|Kanye West]]'s surprise June 2026 Istanbul performance, which drew a record-scale crowd near the Bosphorus after European venues and festivals cancelled his appearances, was cited as evidence of Turkey's growing role as an alternative destination for artists, influencers and public figures facing restrictions or reputational pressure in parts of Europe and North America.[6] See [[kanye-west-istanbul-concert-2026|Kanye West Istanbul concert (2026)]].[6]

## February 2026 regional expansion

In early February 2026 Turkey advanced a broad push across the Middle East, North Africa, and Eurasia combining economic diplomacy, military expansion, and mediation.[7] In [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], Ankara and Riyadh agreed to cooperate on $2 billion in solar energy projects, with Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar saying the investments would be "financed entirely through external financing" alongside credit from international financial institutions.[7] With [[egypt|Egypt]], Turkey issued a joint declaration after the second Strategic Cooperation Council meeting, committing to expanded trade, energy, defense, and regional security cooperation, backing de-escalation in the Middle East and renewed US–[[iran|Iran]] negotiations, and scheduling the next High-Level Strategic Cooperation Council for 2028 in Ankara.[7]

Turkey also positioned itself as a diplomatic intermediary, reportedly preparing to host US–Iran talks in Istanbul while separate de-escalation talks were arranged in Muscat, [[oman|Oman]], confirmed publicly by Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]].[7] Militarily, Turkey deployed additional F-16 fighter jets abroad, extending its footprint from [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]] to [[somalia|Somalia]] according to unnamed Somali officials and satellite imagery analyzed by Middle East Eye.[7] Badlands commentary read these deployments, combined with the Turkey–Egypt defense pact and the Turkey–Saudi–[[pakistan|Pakistan]] arrangement, as covering the region's three continental gateways and marking Turkey's drift away from [[nato|NATO]] and the EU toward [[brics|BRICS]] and the [[shanghai-cooperation-organisation|Shanghai Cooperation Organisation]].[7]

## Role in Gaza Phase Two (January 2026)

Special Envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] credited Turkey, alongside [[egypt|Egypt]] and [[qatar|Qatar]], with finalizing the particulars of Phase Two of the [[comprehensive-plan-to-end-the-gaza-conflict|20-Point Plan to End the Gaza Conflict]], announced on January 15, 2026.[8] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] specifically wanted Turkey and Qatar involved in the International Stabilization Force, despite opposition from most of the Israeli political establishment.[8]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Turkey and [[israel|Israel]] had spent two and a half years exchanging public threats, including inferences about sending special forces to capture or kill the other country's leader, and observed that Turkey's [[nato|NATO]] membership means an Israeli attack on Turkish forces could trigger [[article-5|Article 5]] and bring the alliance and the United States into the conflict.[8]

## Black Sea strikes and Israeli naval warnings (July 2026)

In July 2026 a Russian-appointed governor in Kherson said Ukrainian drone strikes had targeted 13 Turkish civilian vessels operating in the Black Sea, killing one Turkish sailor and wounding three others.[9] Turkish authorities did not publicly confirm the casualty figures, the circumstances surrounding the reported attacks remained disputed, and Ankara announced no military response, while [[russia|Russia]] condemned the strikes as attacks on civilian shipping.[9]

Separately, Israeli Diaspora Affairs Minister [[amichai-chikli|Amichai Chikli]] said [[israel|Israel]] should prepare for the possibility of a future naval confrontation with Turkey, citing Ankara's expanding regional posture and tensions over Gaza and the Eastern Mediterranean, and urged stronger Israeli naval capabilities without suggesting a clash was imminent.[9] Turkey has maintained relations with both [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and Russia while continuing to criticize Israeli operations in Gaza; despite sharpening rhetoric between Ankara and Jerusalem the two are not in direct military conflict.[9] Badlands commentary asked whether [[article-5|Article 5]] might be activated against Ukraine for attacking a NATO member, describing the prospect as a quagmire for the Uniparty regime.[9]

## In Israel's Crosshairs (June 2026)

In June 2026 Turkey and [[israel|Israel]] traded escalating threats after President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] condemned Israeli operations in [[syria|Syria]] and [[lebanon|Lebanon]] and warned that Israel's regional conduct could eventually threaten Turkey itself.[10] Erdoğan accused Israel of expansionism, renewed calls for the eventual "liberation" of Jerusalem, and told the Turkish Parliament that Israel's [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] project must never be allowed to succeed; Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] replied by calling him an "antisemitic dictator" who supports [[hamas|Hamas]].[10] Turkish officials increasingly portrayed Israel's strikes on [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]]-linked targets in Lebanon and military assets in Syria as destabilizing, while Israeli leaders defended them as necessary against Iranian-backed groups.[10]

Badlands analysis noted that Turkey has already paid for F-35 aircraft and trained pilots and maintenance crews, but that the jets are withheld over Erdoğan's relationship with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and fears Russia would gain access to the technology; President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], asked about the sale, praised Erdoğan and said he was unconcerned that Turkey would start a war with Israel.[10] The same commentary argued that as the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] advances toward [[beirut|Beirut]] it approaches Turkey's practical sphere in Syria, that Ankara most fears an Israeli move on Damascus jeopardizing Turkish–Saudi plans to rebuild Syria, and that Israel's new security pact with [[greece|Greece]] could be used to provoke Turkey in the Aegean or around [[cyprus|Cyprus]] — a Greek–Turkish clash between [[nato|NATO]] members would not trigger [[article-5|Article 5]], whereas an Israeli attack on Turkey would.[10] The Greek–Turkish feud was traced back to the fall of Byzantine Constantinople to the Ottomans.[10]

## Sweden's NATO accession and Die Welt distrust piece (2023)

In March 2023 the German newspaper Die Welt published an opinion piece by Clemens Wergin arguing that [[nato|NATO]] should treat Turkey and [[hungary|Hungary]] as partners "with reservation," citing President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]]'s continued blocking of Sweden's NATO accession and Hungary's linkage of its approval to EU funds.[11] Wergin claimed both nations had cultivated "unseemly" ties to [[russia|Russia]] and suggested NATO should stop sharing certain sensitive data with Ankara and Budapest, describing both as having turned toward authoritarianism and away from the alliance's community of values.[11] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the article as establishment and globalist media bullying states that were staying neutral as the [[multipolar-world|multipolar world order]] took shape.[11]

## Istanbul talks between US and Russian officials (February 2025)

Russian and U.S. officials met for six hours in Turkey on Thursday, February 27, 2025, in an effort to restore the normal functioning of their embassies.[12] The discussions were held at the U.S. consul general's gated residence in Istanbul and focused on diplomatic matters such as staffing levels, visas, and banking.[12] No public statements followed the meeting.[12] Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]] said the Istanbul discussions were expected to be the first in a series of meetings aimed at easing tensions and resolving long-standing diplomatic disputes.[12]

Badlands commentary argued that, following the Riyadh summit that cast the Saudis and Arabs as peacemakers, the Turkish summit had the potential to bring down [[nato|NATO]] by moving Ankara out of Europe's sphere of influence and into Asia's — a shift consistent with President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]]'s public comments on BRICS, the Shanghai Cooperation Organization, and Russia.[12] The analysis also held that with the IDF moving on Damascus, which the Turks also want, and with Syria described as under Turkish control, Israel could not attack Turkish forces without triggering [[article-5|Article 5]].[12]

## 2025 severing of trade with Israel

In August 2025, Türkiye severed all commercial and economic ties with [[israel|Israel]] and closed its airspace to some Israeli flights, as announced by Foreign Minister [[hakan-fidan|Hakan Fidan]] in an address to the national parliament.[13] The two countries had been at odds for months over Israel's military campaign in Gaza, with Türkiye accusing Israel of committing genocide.[13] Fidan said Türkiye had "completely cut off our trade with Israel" and "closed our ports to Israeli ships," adding that container ships carrying weapons and ammunition to Israel would not be allowed into Turkish ports nor aircraft into Turkish airspace.[13] Turkish-flagged ships were barred from calling at Israeli ports, and Israeli vessels barred from Turkish ports.[13]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Turkey as rising as "the vertebrae of the python" squeezing [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s Israel, along an axis extending from Azerbaijan through Armenia into Turkey and ultimately Syria, carrying oil and natural gas intended to revive the Syrian economy.[13] He linked the move to the concurrent [[shanghai-cooperation-organization|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]] summit in Tianjin and its North–South Transportation Corridor, predicting eventual Turkish accession to the SCO.[13] He further argued that [[donald-trump|Trump]] had elevated President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Erdogan]] over Israel in the December conquest of Syria, and that the IDF, worn down by years of war, could not take on the largest army in the Middle East, especially if backed by Iran.[13]


## Intervention in the ICJ genocide case (August 2024)

On Monday, August 5, 2024, Foreign Minister [[hakan-fidan|Hakan Fidan]], speaking at a news conference in Cairo, said Turkey would formally submit its declaration of intervention in [[south-africa|South Africa]]'s genocide case against [[israel|Israel]] at the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]] in The Hague that Wednesday.[15] Turkey had announced in May 2024 that it would join the case as part of a wider set of measures against Israel over the war in [[gaza|Gaza]], subject to the completion of the necessary legal preparations.[15] Badlands Media noted that Turkey continued to supply Israel with oil even as President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] spoke of invading it, and that Israeli Foreign Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] had called for [[nato|NATO]] to expel Turkey — a step the outlet argued would free the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] to act against Turkish bases on Cyprus.[15] The ICJ filing was read as an indication that Erdogan was pivoting away from NATO and the European Union toward the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[15]

## Readiness to deploy to Gaza (December 2025)

In December 2025, Turkish security sources cited across Turkish media said the Turkish military was ready to send troops to the Gaza Strip as part of the [[international-stabilization-force|International Stabilization Force]].[14] "We have no problem with the troops being sent to Gaza to join the ISF. The Americans also very much want us there, while Israel opposes it. The Americans are pressuring Israel to have Turkish troops," the sources were quoted as saying.[14] They affirmed that as a guarantor of the Israel-Hamas ceasefire and a signatory to the peace process, Turkey "must be there," adding, "Our forces are ready. We have prepared all the necessary units. The moment the order is given, we will immediately form a modular unit."[14] The sources argued that one cannot say "Turkish troops cannot come" when Turkey was among the countries that worked hardest to establish the ceasefire and coordinated the exchange of hostages for Palestinians detained in Israeli prisons.[14] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Erdogan]] "and the boys are ready to roll," that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had said in his [[2025-national-security-strategy|National Security Strategy]] that he wants Turkey in the ISF and in Gaza, and that Trump was summoning [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] to Washington to discuss the next phase even as Netanyahu insisted he would not allow Turkish troops into Gaza or Israel.[14]

## Discord access ban (October 2024)

Turkey's Information Technologies and Communication Authority published an access-ban decision against [[discord|Discord]] on its website in October 2024.[16] Transport and infrastructure minister Abdulkadir Uraloglu said the nature of the platform made it difficult for authorities to monitor and intervene when illegal or criminal content is shared: "Security personnel cannot go through the content. We can only intervene when users complain to us about content shared there."[16] He added that "Since Discord refuses to share its own information, including IP addresses and content, with our security units, we were forced to block access."[16] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] cited the Turkish ban, reported by Reuters on October 9, 2024, as evidence that governments had moved from pressuring platforms to censor toward outright force.[16]
