# Kenneth Darlington

Kenneth Darlington is a retired American lawyer, aged 77 at the time of the events described, who on November 8, 2023 shot and killed two anti-mining protesters who were blockading a highway in [[panama|Panama]].[1] Video of the shooting circulated widely online the following day, showing Darlington confronting a group of demonstrators, drawing a handgun and opening fire in broad daylight.[1]

## The shooting

The popular framing of the incident presented Darlington as an ordinary, frustrated motorist — a man simply wanting to get home who snapped at "climate change activists" blocking the road, an image compared in commentary to the film *Falling Down*.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argued that this framing omitted the substance of the protest: the demonstrators were not generic climate activists but opponents of a mining concession the Panamanian government had recently granted to the Canadian firm [[first-quantum-minerals|First Quantum Minerals]].[1]

## Background and prior record

Reporting summarised from a November 9, 2023 article in the *US Sun* described Darlington as a dual citizen of the United States and Panama who was taken into custody after the killings.[1] Citing Newsroom Panama, that reporting said Darlington had a criminal history dating back eighteen years: in 2005 he was arrested following a raid on an apartment in the Paitilla neighbourhood of Panama City, where two M-1 rifles and an AK-47 were recovered.[1] He was released on bail after claiming the weapons formed part of a "war collection."[1] Newsroom Panama also reported that Darlington had previously been linked to the financier Marc Harris, who was convicted of money laundering in the United States.[1]

## Interpretation

Carlson posed the question of why Darlington would kill two protesters in daylight, on camera, in the knowledge that he would be arrested, and speculated that the killings functioned as a message to Panamanian voters ahead of a referendum on the mining contract — that opposing the concession could carry personal danger.[1] He further asked whether the widely distributed video was released to steer audiences toward discovering the underlying mining corruption, or instead to portray opponents of climate activism as murderous, a framing he suggested may have backfired as researchers looked deeper.[1] These readings are the author's speculation rather than established fact.[1]
