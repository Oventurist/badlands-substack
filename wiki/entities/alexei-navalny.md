# Alexei Navalny

Alexei Navalny is invoked in "A Bright Red Line" as the figure around whom, in Western narratives, a supposedly pro-Western segment of the Russian population is said to rally.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The article dismisses this as "a complete myth," describing Navalny as a puppet and arguing that the idea of a Russian constituency enamored of Western hegemony is refuted by the internal assessment of [[william-burns|William Burns]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

Burns reported that across more than two and a half years of conversations with key Russian players, "from knuckle-draggers in the dark recesses of the Kremlin to Putin's sharpest liberal critics," he had found no one who viewed Ukraine in [[nato|NATO]] as anything other than a direct challenge to Russian interests — evidence, Badlands argues, that the Russian people, though not always in complete agreement, are united against NATO encroachment.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Death and Ukrainian intelligence assessment

Navalny, President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s most prominent critic, died on February 16, 2024 in one of Russia's toughest prisons in northern Siberia, while serving a 19-year sentence on charges denounced by Putin's critics as political retribution for his opposition activity.[2] His body was handed to his mother, Lyudmila Navalnaya, more than a week after his death.[2]

On February 25, 2024, [[kyrylo-budanov|Kyrylo Budanov]], chief of the Main Directorate of Intelligence of Ukraine's Ministry of Defense, told journalists at the "Ukraine. Year of 2024" forum that Navalny "indeed died as a result of a blood clot," adding that the conclusion "wasn't sourced from the internet, but, unfortunately, natural [causes]."[2] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] presented Budanov's statement as evidence that even the Ukrainian proxy was stepping out of line with Western anti-Putin propaganda, rebuking the mainstream media's conspiratorial theorizing about the death.[2]

## US sanctions response

On February 20, 2024, [[white-house|White House]] national security spokesman [[john-kirby|John Kirby]] announced that the administration would unveil a "major sanctions" package that Friday "to hold Russia accountable" for Navalny's death, saying that "whatever story the Russian government decides to tell the world, it's clear that President Putin and his government are responsible for Mr. Navalny's death."[3] Kirby did not detail the package's contents but said the measures would also hold Russia accountable for its ongoing war with Ukraine.[3]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterised what he called "the Navalny Deployment" as an example of the [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian Dialectic]] — a false flag intended both to trigger mass psychological mania toward Russia in the aftermath of Putin's interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] and to manufacture the appearance of a mandate for the sanctions being handed down.[3] He argued the sanctions would not work, citing Russia's rising GDP and a national currency strengthening against the petrodollar, and contended that their real function was to serve as a scarlet letter marking a line of demarcation between the "rules-based order" and those who reject it — a posture he said Russia and the emergent [[brics|BRICS]] nations had stopped humouring.[3]

## US intelligence assessment of Putin's role

In April 2024 the Wall Street Journal reported that United States intelligence agencies had determined [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] likely did not order Navalny to be killed at the Arctic penal colony in February, a finding said to deepen the mystery surrounding the circumstances of his death.[4] The assessment did not dispute Putin's overall culpability, but concluded he probably did not order the killing at that moment; the finding was described as broadly accepted within the intelligence community and shared by several agencies, including the [[central-intelligence-agency|Central Intelligence Agency]], the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]], and the [[state-department|State Department]]'s intelligence unit.[4] Navalny's death had prompted a new wave of sanctions targeting Russia's economy, upended delicate prisoner-exchange negotiations between Russia and the West, and left Russia's limited opposition in disarray and divided in exile.[4]

## Death and Western coverage (February 2024)

Navalny died on Friday, February 16, 2024 at the age of forty-seven.[5] The New Yorker, in a widely circulated postscript, described him as the Kremlin's most formidable opponent — jailed and released, poisoned and survived, warned to stay away from Russia and returning anyway, arrested before dozens of cameras, held for three years in solitary confinement with his lawyers cut off and arrested, moved across the country to serve his sentence in the Arctic, and still laughing at his jailers on courtroom video.[5] His stated promise was that he would outlive his persecutors and lead what he called the Beautiful Russia of the Future.[5]

Hours after news of the death broke, his widow [[yulia-navalnaya|Yulia Navalnaya]] addressed the [[munich-security-conference|Munich Security Conference]], saying she did not know whether to believe reporting that came only from Russian state-controlled sources, but that if it was true she wanted [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and everyone around him to know "they will be held responsible for what they have done to our country, to my family, and to my husband," adding that "the day of reckoning will come very soon."[5]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] deliberately selected the most dramatic Western account of the death in order to illustrate a media-analysis point: when the media moves in lock step to advance a coordinated, emotionally manipulative narrative immediately after an ostensibly random event, he argued, the opposite reading is often the better starting point.[5] He pointed to the black-and-white, "Hollywoodesque" headshot used as the article's title card as a marker of pre-planned messaging.[5] Against the Western portrait of Navalny as savior and freedom fighter, Burning Bright reported that many Russians regard him as a fomenter of [[color-revolution|color revolution]], an avowed Nazi sympathizer, and a self-described neo-Marxist globalist who sought to remake Russia as an EU- and UN-aligned state.[5] He argued the timing of the death — days after the Tucker Carlson interview with Putin — was provoking the very awakening such coverage was meant to suppress.[5]
