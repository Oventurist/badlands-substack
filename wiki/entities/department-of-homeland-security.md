# Department of Homeland Security

The **Department of Homeland Security (DHS)** is the US cabinet department responsible for border security, immigration enforcement, and a range of domestic security functions. In June 2026 it stood at the centre of two intersecting stories: a multi-year funding package and a large-scale accounting of missing migrant children.[1]

## Full funding under the Secure America Act

The [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] announced in June 2026 that DHS was fully funded following passage and signing of a major immigration and border-security package providing tens of billions of dollars in additional funding for the department, [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]], and [[customs-and-border-protection|Customs and Border Protection]] through the remainder of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s term.[1] The legislation allocates roughly $70 billion in additional immigration-enforcement funding over three years.[1] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] identified the measure as the [[secure-america-act|Secure America Act]] and framed its signing as a president choosing "to fund the hunters rather than the networks."[1]

## Missing migrant children

At a Thursday press conference, Homeland Security Secretary [[markwayne-mullin|Markwayne Mullin]] said the administration had located approximately 146,000 migrant children who had previously lost contact with federal authorities after entering the United States as unaccompanied minors.[1] Mullin said nearly 300,000 additional children remained unaccounted for and that investigators were pursuing reports of severe abuse involving some of those minors, including allegations from recovered children who said they had been raped "600 to 700 times."[1]

The effort follows earlier findings by the DHS Inspector General that hundreds of thousands of unaccompanied minors transferred out of federal custody were not consistently tracked through immigration proceedings, fuelling concerns about exploitation, trafficking, and abuse.[1] DHS and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] have made locating missing children and investigating trafficking networks a major enforcement priority, with indictments landing, new resources funded for child-exploitation investigators, and ICE and Homeland Security Investigations surging into sanctuary jurisdictions.[1]

Burning Bright placed the numbers in a wider frame, writing that roughly 450,000 minors were smuggled across the border between 2021 and 2024 and describing [[child-trafficking|child trafficking]] as an industrial-scale operation that thrived under policies of deliberate neglect.[1]

## 2026 oversight and spending controversies

Under Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]], the department came under bipartisan congressional scrutiny in March 2026 over its mass deportation operations and its contracting practices.[2] At a [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]] hearing on March 3, lawmakers criticized a $220 million advertising campaign featuring Noem herself and a $143 million no-bid contract awarded to a company created just eleven days earlier, which subcontracted work to a firm tied to the husband of a former top DHS spokeswoman.[2] Noem denied personal involvement in the contracts and defended the advertising as effective.[2]

The department's enforcement arms, [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] and [[customs-and-border-protection|CBP]], drew separate criticism for [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]] in Minnesota, where two U.S. citizen protesters were fatally shot amid clashes in Minneapolis and St. Paul.[2] Badlands commentary highlighted an internal contrast in which field operations under [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]] drew bipartisan praise while Noem absorbed bipartisan criticism, with enforcement continuing unimpeded.[2]

## Election citizenship verification (2026)

DHS holds a central role in the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's election integrity architecture: the March 2026 executive order [[ensuring-citizenship-verification-and-integrity-in-federal-elections|"Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections"]] directs the department to develop a "state citizenship list."[3] In May 2026 DHS election integrity official [[heather-honey|Heather Honey]] — described as a protege of attorney [[cleta-mitchell|Cleta Mitchell]] — participated in [[white-house|White House]] interagency talks on the plan alongside the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] and the [[united-states-postal-service|Postal Service]].[3] Deputy Homeland Security Advisor Anthony Salisbury was also named among the participants, and DHS and [[uscis|USCIS]] officials said internal deliberations were ongoing.[3]

## Detention authority upheld (February 2026)

In February 2026 the [[fifth-circuit-court-of-appeals|Fifth Circuit]] reversed two district-court orders that had required bond hearings or release for detained noncitizens, holding that the government may detain certain "applicants for admission" without bond during removal proceedings under 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A).[4] Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] said courts had previously ordered releases on the basis of claims that DHS was breaking the law, and characterized the ruling as supporting the department's position.[4]

## Conflict with state prosecutors over immigration enforcement (2026)

In March 2026 the Department responded to a Minnesota criminal investigation of federal immigration agents by asserting that enforcement is a federal responsibility and that state prosecutors lack the authority to charge federal officers.[5] The investigation, opened by Hennepin County Attorney [[mary-moriarty|Mary Moriarty]], targeted the conduct of [[united-states-border-patrol|US Border Patrol]] agents including the senior official [[gregory-bovino|Gregory Bovino]].[5]

The dispute unfolded alongside other controversies over DHS immigration operations, including the death of a nearly blind Myanmar refugee, Nurul Amin Shah Alam, found dead in Buffalo, New York, five days after Border Patrol released him at a coffee shop near his last known address.[5] A Reuters/Ipsos poll during the same period found that a majority of Americans support deporting unauthorized immigrants but disapprove of aggressive enforcement tactics.[5]

## SAVE voter-registration checks (2025–2026)

The Trump administration ran at least 67 million voter registrations through the Department of Homeland Security's [[save-program|SAVE program]], according to Associated Press reporting in May 2026, in eligibility checks critics said could lead to valid voters being removed from the rolls before the November elections.[6] [[uscis|USCIS]] said 60 million checks identified about 24,000 potential noncitizens, and at least 25 states had used SAVE for voter-roll checks since April 2025.[6] The administrative effort paralleled the [[save-act|SAVE Act]], H.R. 22, which the House passed 220–208 on 10 April 2025.[6]

## Role in the election executive order

President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s executive order on election administration directs the Department of Homeland Security to compile lists of confirmed US citizens eligible to vote and provide them to the states.[7] In July 2026 a federal appeals court blocked key portions of that order in 23 Democrat-led states pending litigation, leaving the citizenship-verification framework unenforceable in those jurisdictions and setting up a likely [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] case.[7]

## Cayo Falcones investigation (February 2026)

Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] said that DHS, together with the [[us-coast-guard|Coast Guard]], was investigating whether any occupants of the Florida-registered speedboat fired upon by Cuban border guards near Cayo Falcones were US citizens or lawful permanent residents.[8] The department's inquiry proceeded while the United States had no information beyond what [[cuba|Cuba]] had released and had not independently confirmed who fired first or the identities of the four killed.[8] See [[cayo-falcones-boat-incident|Cayo Falcones boat incident]].[8]

## Democratic funding demands (February 2026)

In February 2026, bicameral minority leaders [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] and [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] sent Republican leaders a formal list of ten demands attached to congressional DHS funding negotiations.[9] The list would require judicial warrants before DHS officers enter private property, end indiscriminate arrests, and require verification that a person is not a U.S. citizen before immigration detention; ban [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] and other immigration agents from wearing face coverings; and require officers to visibly display agency, unique ID number, and last name and to identify themselves verbally on request.[9]

Further demands would bar enforcement funding near sensitive locations including medical facilities, schools, childcare facilities, churches, polling places, and courthouses; prohibit stops or searches based on race, ethnicity, language, accent, occupation, or presence at certain locations; codify a reasonable use-of-force policy with expanded training and removal from field duty pending investigation; preserve state and local authority to investigate DHS officers and require consent for large-scale operations; impose detention facility minimum standards with immediate access to counsel, state suit rights, and unrestricted congressional visits to ICE facilities; mandate body-worn cameras with footage rules and a ban on databases tracking First Amendment activity; and standardize uniforms and equipment to civilian law-enforcement rather than paramilitary norms.[9] The leaders also called for Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] to be removed from her position.[9]

## Operation Metro Surge (January 2026)

In early January 2026 DHS launched a large-scale deployment of roughly 2,000 federal agents drawn from [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]], Customs and Border Protection and Homeland Security Investigations to Minneapolis and St. Paul under an operation dubbed [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]].[10] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] subsequently ordered an additional 1,000 CBP agents to the Twin Cities, potentially bringing the total to approximately 3,000 federal personnel conducting immigration enforcement operations there.[10] White House deputy chief of staff for policy [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]] told Fox News that the officers carried federal immunity in the conduct of their duties and that any official, agitator or other person obstructing them would be committing a felony.[10]

## Deportation of Noncitizen Voters (June 2026)

On June 9, 2026, DHS announced new guidance directing [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] to pursue deportation proceedings against noncitizens who illegally vote in American elections or who falsely claim United States citizenship.[11] DHS General Counsel James Percival said the policy simply reflects existing provisions of the [[immigration-and-nationality-act|Immigration and Nationality Act]] and follows the administration's broader election-integrity initiatives.[11] Under the directive, ICE attorneys may seek removal on the basis of evidence of illegal voting or a false claim of citizenship even where no criminal conviction has yet occurred.[11] Departmental officials argued that unlawful voting by noncitizens undermines election integrity and independently triggers grounds for removal under federal immigration law, and framed the guidance as part of a wider push on voter-eligibility verification and election security.[11]

## Minneapolis–St. Paul enforcement surge (January 2026)

In early January 2026 DHS described the deployment of approximately 2,000 federal personnel to the Minneapolis–St. Paul area as the largest immigration enforcement operation ever conducted there, drawing on officers from [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]], [[homeland-security-investigations|Homeland Security Investigations]] and other DHS components.[12] According to the department, the operation focused on enforcing federal immigration law and investigating related offenses including alleged fraud, unlawful employment practices and human smuggling, and followed ongoing investigations in [[minnesota|Minnesota]] as part of broader national enforcement efforts.[12]

During the operation a federal immigration agent fatally shot 37-year-old [[renee-nicole-good|Renee Nicole Good]] on January 7, 2026; DHS said the agent acted in self-defense after she allegedly attempted to strike officers with a vehicle, and the incident was placed under investigation.[12] The department stated the wider operation remained ongoing and that additional arrests or enforcement actions could follow.[12]

## Hilton franchise dispute (January 2026)

DHS publicly condemned a Hampton Inn by Hilton franchise in Lakeville, Minnesota, after the hotel canceled reservations made by DHS and [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] agents on official government emails and government rates, with staff citing the guests' "immigration work."[13] A DHS post on X characterized the cancellations as a "coordinated campaign" by [[hilton-worldwide|Hilton]] to impede federal law enforcement, prompting boycott calls before Hilton terminated the franchise agreement.[13]

## Response to Denver Executive Order 152 (2026)

After Denver Mayor [[mike-johnston|Mike Johnston]] signed [[denver-executive-order-152|Executive Order 152]] barring federal immigration agents from city property and prohibiting city agencies from sharing databases or technology with DHS or its immigration partners absent legal compulsion, the department issued a statement on Friday, February 27, 2026 calling the order "legally illiterate."[14]

## January 6, 2025 certification designation

In September 2024 Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] designated the January 6, 2025 certification of the presidential vote as a [[national-special-security-event|national special security event]], the first time the department had applied that status to the certification process.[15] The designation placed the [[congress|congressional]] session on the same footing as the Super Bowl or the U.N. General Assembly and authorized measures to prevent a repeat of the 2021 Capitol riot, with the [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]] leading the integrated security plan.[15]

## Disinformation Governance Board

DHS created the short-lived [[disinformation-governance-board|Disinformation Governance Board]], headed by [[nina-jankowicz|Nina Jankowicz]], whose stated purpose was to coordinate disinformation efforts across the department on topics ranging from migration to plots attributed to Russia and Iran.[16] The board never held a meeting and was heavily criticized by the GOP, with the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] dubbing it the "Ministry of Truth."[16] In May 2023 Jankowicz sued [[fox-news|Fox News]] for defamation over its coverage of her role, a case Badlands commentators framed as a test of [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] protections for criticism of government officials.[16]

## Southern California marijuana farm raids (July 2025)

On Thursday, July 10, 2025, federal immigration authorities conducted coordinated enforcement operations at two licensed cannabis cultivation sites in Southern California, at Carpinteria and Camarillo.[18] DHS stated that officers executed criminal warrant operations at the marijuana grow sites, rescuing at least 10 migrant children from potential exploitation, forced labor, and human trafficking, and arresting approximately 200 illegal aliens across both locations.[18] The department also said that "ICE and CBP Law Enforcement" had to "dodge literal bullets from rioters" who appeared to have been forewarned about the operation.[18]

The raids coincided with Los Angeles Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]]'s announcement of cash assistance for families affected by immigration enforcement and an executive order barring city departments from cooperating with federal immigration authorities.[18]



## Campaign against left-wing networks (September 2025)

On 15 September 2025, White House Deputy Chief of Staff [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]], appearing on *The Charlie Kirk Show* hosted by Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], named the Department of Homeland Security among the agencies that would be mobilised against left-leaning groups after the assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[19] Miller pledged the government would use "every resource we have at the Department of Justice, Homeland Security and throughout this government to identify, disrupt, dismantle and destroy these networks and make America safe again for the American people."[19]

## Theo Von deportation video controversy (September–October 2025)

On September 23, 2025, DHS posted a video to its various social media platforms promoting deportation efforts, showing [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] officials detaining immigrants alongside a clip of comedian [[theo-von|Theo Von]] saying, "I heard you got deported, dude. Bye."[20] Von objected in a since-deleted post on X the same day, writing that he had not approved the use and asking the department to take it down and "please keep me out of your 'banger' deportation videos."[20]

Von later said on his podcast that the resulting online harassment had left him "paranoid," and that a "high government official" texted him offering extra security or police patrols in his neighborhood.[20]

## Reversal on Israel boycott certification for disaster funds, 2025

On Monday, 4 August 2025 the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] reversed course on requiring U.S. cities and states to rebuke boycotts of Israeli companies in order to receive disaster funds, and deleted the earlier policy from its website.[21] DHS removed its statement that states must certify they would not sever "commercial relations specifically with Israeli companies" to qualify for funding.[21]

Reuters reported that the language applied to at least $1.9 billion that states rely on to cover search-and-rescue equipment, emergency manager salaries and backup power systems, among other expenses, across 11 agency grant notices reviewed by the outlet.[21] The reversal marked a shift for an administration that had previously sought to penalize institutions not aligned with its views on [[israel|Israel]] or antisemitism.[21]

Badlands commentary noted that DHS posted on X that no such requirement existed and was met with a Community Note clarifying that the department had only just removed the requirements and that they had in fact existed, with the net effect that the condition was rescinded while the public learned it had once been [[fema|FEMA]] policy.[21]

## Shelter and Services Program grants (August 2024)

On August 28, 2024, DHS Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] announced that more than $380 million in Shelter and Services Program grants would go to state and local governments and to non-governmental organizations providing food, shelter, clothing, acute medical care and transportation to migrants recently released from DHS custody while awaiting immigration court proceedings.[22] The SSP was created by the Biden-Harris administration and funded by Congress to channel taxpayer money to cities and NGOs assisting in resettling migrants released into the US interior from the southern border.[22] The award followed an earlier round of hundreds of millions of dollars in 2024.[22] Badlands Media characterized the program as a slush fund and argued that the department was incentivizing sanctuary cities and NGOs while obscuring the flow of money through non-profits.[22]

## Voting machine seizure question (2020–2023)

Reporting published in April 2023 revealed that former top national security officials had testified to a federal grand jury that they repeatedly told President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his allies that the government lacked the authority to seize voting machines after the 2020 election.[23] Former acting Homeland Security secretary [[chad-wolf|Chad Wolf]] and his former deputy [[ken-cuccinelli|Ken Cuccinelli]] were questioned about internal administration discussions of DHS seizing voting machines, with Cuccinelli testifying that he "made clear at all times" that DHS had no such authority.[23] Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] agreed with the legal conclusion, noting that voting machines are the property of the states and localities where they sit and that federal seizure by executive fiat would be a dangerous precedent absent a judicial order.[23]


## Minnesota visa fraud review and driver's license data settlement (December 2025)

Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] told a White House roundtable on December 3, 2025, that a departmental review had found 50 percent of the Minnesota visas examined to be fraudulent, and blamed Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] for allowing the scheme to proceed.[24]

Separately, four Republican states — Florida, Indiana, Iowa and Ohio — agreed in a settlement filed on Friday, November 28, 2025, in a federal lawsuit to help the department gain access to state driver's license data through a nationwide law enforcement computer network, as part of the administration's hunt for alleged noncitizen voters.[24] The suit had originally been brought by the states against the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] for allegedly not doing enough to help states verify voter eligibility.[24] Under the settlement the department must continue developing the citizenship verification program known as SAVE, which federal officials had earlier in the year repurposed into a system capable of scanning millions of state voter records for noncitizen registrations.[24] In return the states agreed to support the department's efforts to access the National Law Enforcement Telecommunications System, or Nlets, an obscure network that lets police look up out-of-state driving records.[24] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] welcomed the settlement as bad news for [[david-becker|David Becker]] and called for the prosecution of ERIC states and their accomplices.[24]

## California cash-benefits subpoenas (May 2025)

On Monday, May 12, 2025, the department issued subpoenas to the government of California seeking records related to alleged disbursements of federal funds to illegal immigrants.[25] The subpoenas were filed by the Los Angeles office of Homeland Security Investigations, the investigative arm of [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]], and targeted the state's [[cash-assistance-program-for-immigrants|Cash Assistance Program for Immigrants]].[25] Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] said "radical left politicians in California prioritize illegal aliens over our own citizens" and that the administration was working to ensure people in the country illegally do not receive federal benefits, adding that although the subpoena focused only on Los Angeles County, "it is just the beginning."[25]

## Arrests of Iranian nationals (June 2025)

DHS officials announced on Tuesday, June 24, 2025 that federal agents had arrested 11 Iranian nationals living in the United States over the preceding days, with almost all arrests made on the Sunday after U.S. military planes bombed key Iranian nuclear sites.[26] The department labelled one of the detainees, Yousef Mehridehno, a "suspected terrorist" who had lied on a visa application, and identified others as a former member of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and a former Iranian army sniper found carrying an Islamic Republic of Iran Army identification card.[26] See [[2025-ice-arrests-of-iranian-nationals|the 2025 ICE arrests of Iranian nationals]].[26]

## Screening of Chinese nationals, 2024

In 2024 DHS was reported to have dramatically simplified the vetting process for Chinese nationals encountered at the border, reducing the number of interview questions from approximately forty to just five.[29] Critics argued the change prioritised "processing efficiency" over thorough national security checks, leaving vulnerabilities that the [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]] could exploit to insert espionage agents or exert influence inside the United States.[29] The change was described as a consequence of border agents being so overwhelmed by illegal crossings and fraudulent asylum claims under the Biden administration that fundamental security measures were abandoned.[29] In the first two days of May 2024, more Chinese nationals entered the U.S. illegally than in all of 2021.[29]

## Homeland Intelligence Experts Group

In September 2023 Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas announced the formation of the [[homeland-intelligence-experts-group|Homeland Intelligence Experts Group]] to "provide advice and perspectives on intelligence and national security efforts" to the Department.[27] Newly released internal files reported in June 2024 showed the now-disbanded group had classified supporters of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], members of the military and people with religious views as persons likely to commit "domestic violent extremist" attacks.[27] [[america-first-legal|America First Legal]] called it "a completely partisan group designed to provide top cover for the Department's radical agenda," and sued alongside former Ambassador [[richard-grenell|Richard Grenell]], alleging violations of the [[federal-advisory-committee-act|Federal Advisory Committee Act]].[27] To avoid further litigation and scrutiny the Department agreed in May 2024 to disband the group and turn over its records, though it may have operated for a year, having met for as long as four months before its public announcement.[27] Badlands commentary situated the episode alongside the [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]] and the [[disinformation-governance-board|Disinformation Governance Board]] as successive forms of the same unconstitutional activity.[27]

## February 2025 senior leadership purge

In the week of February 17, 2025, the Trump administration prepared to fire hundreds of high-level DHS employees as part of an effort to rid the country's third-largest agency of people deemed misaligned with the administration's goals, according to three sources familiar with the matter.[28] The sources described a "centralized plan" and a list of individuals in high-level positions across every component of the department who were to be targeted that week.[28]

The firings came on top of hundreds of more general cuts that had begun across DHS the previous Friday night, which targeted the [[fema|Federal Emergency Management Agency]], [[uscis|U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services]], and the [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]].[28] Those cuts were part of a governmentwide effort to reduce the federal workforce.[28]
