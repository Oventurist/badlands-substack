# Rafah

Rafah is the southernmost city of the Gaza Strip, situated on the border with Egypt. During the 2023–2024 Gaza war it became the focal point of international controversy after roughly one million refugees settled there having fled fighting in northern Gaza, turning the city and its surrounding tent encampments into the largest concentration of displaced Palestinians in the territory.[1]

## Strategic and humanitarian significance

By May 2024 Rafah had become the last major population centre in Gaza that [[israel-defense-forces|Israeli forces]] had not entered in strength, and the [[white-house|White House]] repeatedly urged [[israel|Israel]] not to send forces into the city without a clear plan to safely evacuate civilians, noting that the war had already left tens of thousands of Palestinians dead.[1] Commentary published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] emphasised the scale of the civilian presence, citing more than 600,000 children in the refugee encampment and questioning what military objective could justify the use of armour columns, special operations personnel and 2,000-pound bombs against a city sheltering more than a million displaced people.[1]

## The May 2024 operation

Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] insisted that destroying [[hamas|Hamas]] required going after its leadership hiding in Rafah, and the IDF initially confined itself to airstrikes and limited ground incursions in the city's east after dropping thousands of leaflets telling civilians to leave the area.[1] Israel released its own footage of armoured columns and bulldozers entering the Rafah area; Badlands commentary argued that this imagery was being used by Hamas as a recruiting tool and that the operation was swelling rather than diminishing the group's ranks.[1]

Rafah also gave its name to the [[rafah-border-crossing|Rafah border crossing]], the principal land link between Gaza and Egypt, whose seizure by an IDF tank unit in May 2024 gave Israel control of every Gaza border crossing for the first time in roughly two decades.[1]

## US policy line

President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] made Rafah the explicit test of American support, warning on 8 May 2024 that he would stop supplying Israel with offensive weapons such as bombs and artillery shells if Israeli forces launched a full invasion of the city.[1] Reporting cited in the same coverage indicated that the administration nonetheless greenlighted the more limited takeover of the crossing itself, and that Washington regarded the "red line" as uncrossed so long as the operation avoided mass civilian displacement and humanitarian catastrophe.[1]

### Inducements offered to Israel

Working urgently to stave off a full-scale Israeli invasion of Rafah, the Biden administration offered Israel substantial assistance in an effort to persuade it to hold back, including sensitive intelligence to help the Israeli military pinpoint the location of Hamas leaders and locate the group's hidden tunnels, according to four people familiar with the offers.[2] American officials also offered to help provide thousands of shelters so Israel could build tent cities, and to help construct delivery systems for food, water, and medicine, so that Palestinians evacuated from Rafah would have a habitable place to live.[2]

Biden and his senior aides made such offers over several weeks in hopes of persuading Israel to conduct a more limited and targeted operation in the southern Gaza city, where some 1.3 million Palestinians were sheltering after fleeing there from other parts of Gaza under Israeli orders.[2] Israel vowed to enter Rafah with "extreme force," and Netanyahu took steps that raised fears at the White House that the long-promised invasion was materializing.[2] Senior Biden aides sought to convince Israeli officials that they could destroy the remaining Hamas battalions in Rafah with more targeted strikes assisted by the United States — which had supplied intelligence throughout the seven-month war — rather than a full-scale invasion.[2] Administration officials, including experts from the U.S. Agency for International Development, told Israel it would take several months to safely relocate the hundreds of thousands of Palestinians living in decrepit and unsanitary conditions in Rafah, an assessment Israeli officials disputed.[2]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the most telling element of the story was that the [[cia|CIA]] evidently knew where Hamas leaders were and yet none were being targeted in the military operation, contrasting this with the precision strikes and leadership raids used against [[isis|ISIS]] by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[2]

## May 2024 tent camp strike and border shooting

In late May 2024 Rafah became the site of two incidents that sharply escalated international pressure on Israel.[3] An Israeli airstrike set fire to a camp housing displaced Palestinians in the city, killing at least 45 people according to local officials, an event Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] described on May 27 as a "tragic mistake"; it is covered in detail at [[2024-rafah-tent-camp-strike|the 2024 Rafah tent camp strike]].[3] The same day, an Egyptian soldier was killed in an exchange of fire with Israeli troops near the [[rafah-border-crossing|Rafah border crossing]], described at [[2024-rafah-border-shooting-incident|the 2024 Rafah border shooting incident]].[3] Both followed the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]] order the previous Friday demanding that Israel halt its offensive in Rafah, and Israel's seizure of the Rafah crossing from the Gaza side earlier that month.[3]
