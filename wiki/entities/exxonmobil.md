# ExxonMobil

ExxonMobil is a major American integrated oil and gas corporation whose posture toward post-Maduro [[venezuela|Venezuela]] became a point of friction with the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration in January 2026.[1]

After the company's chief executive described Venezuela as "uninvestable," Trump indicated he might block ExxonMobil from investing in the country, according to a Reuters report carried by The Guardian.[1] The remark came as Trump publicly stated the United States would be "using oil" and "taking oil" from Venezuela and that U.S. oversight of the country's oil sector would last "much longer" than a year.[1]

ExxonMobil's caution stands in contrast to the position taken by the [[us-oil-and-gas-association|U.S. Oil & Gas Association]], which argued that the overwhelming show of American military force resolved producers' security concerns and cleared the way for a return to Venezuelan operations.[1]

## Guyana offshore production

ExxonMobil leads oil production in the oil-rich offshore waters attached to the [[essequibo-dispute|Essequibo region]], territory controlled by [[guyana|Guyana]] but claimed with increasing intensity by [[venezuela|Venezuela]].[2] Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] warned in March 2025 that there would be "consequences" if Venezuela used force to press those claims.[2] Badlands commentary noted that the Guyanese seabed fields form part of a larger field belonging to Venezuela, and cited an analyst's assessment that Caracas could bar companies operating in Guyana from working in Venezuela's own oil fields.[2]

## Venezuela and Guyana oil rights, 2025

Badlands analysis of the December 2025 US-Venezuela confrontation noted that although Exxon is the company most often associated with Venezuelan oil, it has held no oil rights in [[venezuela|Venezuela]] since 2007, following the nationalizations of that era.[3] Its regional position instead rests on neighboring [[guyana|Guyana]], which shares territorial waters with Venezuela where the disputed reserve is located.[3]

Exxon had retained a right of first refusal over [[hess-corporation|Hess]]'s Venezuelan-linked rights, but [[citgo|Citgo]] bypassed it by acquiring the entire Hess company in July 2025; Exxon's lawsuit challenging the transaction was denied, finalizing the deal.[3] Days after that denial, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] granted Citgo an exemption from the Venezuela sanctions he had imposed in 2019, allowing Venezuelan crude to be brought to the US for refining and sale.[3]

## Honolulu climate litigation

In 2020 the city of [[honolulu]] sued several major fossil fuel companies, including Exxon and [[chevron|Chevron]], claiming their products cause greenhouse gas emissions and global warming and that they failed to warn consumers about the risks.[4] The city invoked state-law theories including public nuisance and trespass and sought billions of dollars to abate the effects of climate change, and the [[hawaii-supreme-court|Hawaii Supreme Court]] ruled the case could proceed to trial.[4] Observers warned that success would set a precedent allowing Democrat-run states to overwhelm energy companies with lawsuits until they accepted [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]]-like parameters nationwide.[4] By April 2024 the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] was weighing many states' requests to intervene.[4] See [[honolulu-climate-lawsuit]].
