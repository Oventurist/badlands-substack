# Democratic Republic of the Congo

The Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) is a central African state whose mineral wealth and eastern insurgency made it a focus of US policy and Badlands commentary in January 2026.[1]

## US–DRC minerals partnership

[[corneille-nangaa|Corneille Nangaa]], leader of the Rwanda-linked Alliance Fleuve Congo, publicly denounced the US–DRC minerals partnership as unconstitutional — a claim the DRC presidency rejected.[1] Badlands framed this as the leader of the [[m23-rebels|M23 Group]] accusing President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and President Félix Tshisekedi of violating the law through the implementation of their arrangement.[1]

## Kabila and the eastern insurgency

Agenzia Fides reported that former Congolese President [[joseph-kabila|Joseph Kabila]] was killed on January 25, 2026 in an explosion while meeting with representatives of a guerrilla group; the blast reportedly killed around 40 people in addition to Kabila, though no official source had confirmed his death.[1] Badlands commentary described the incident as a rumored drone strike on a meeting with [[m23-rebels|M23]] rebels in Uvira, the latest town M23 had seized.[1]

Kabila had been accused in 2025 of aiding and abetting the M23 rebels, charged with treason, and convicted in absentia of a failed coup, with the death penalty sought and granted.[1] Congress was reported to be discussing sanctions against [[rwanda|Rwanda]] over its role.[1] Badlands also noted that China planned to move 100,000 tons of copper to the United States.[1]

## 2026 Ebola outbreak

In May 2026 the DRC was the center of an [[2026-ebola-outbreak|Ebola outbreak]] of the Bundibugyo virus strain, originating in Ituri province in the northeast and spreading into North Kivu and South Kivu, with confirmed cases in Goma and across the border in Kampala, Uganda.[2] No vaccines or treatments exist for the Bundibugyo strain.[2]

The [[international-rescue-committee|International Rescue Committee]] said "the warning signs are flashing red" and that the epidemic was outpacing responders, while [[world-health-organization|WHO]] Director-General [[tedros-adhanom-ghebreyesus|Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus]] described authorities as "playing catch-up."[2] Congolese officials stated that response teams were being strengthened daily and called the situation in the affected provinces "well contained," and roughly $500 million was pledged for response and prevention.[2]

## Uvira offensive and the mineral trade (December 2025)

In December 2025 [[m23|M23]] fighters seized the city of [[uvira|Uvira]] in South Kivu without a fight, despite a high-profile US-brokered peace agreement signed the previous week.[3] The Congolese government reported more than 100 people killed and over 200,000 newly displaced in a "generalized offensive" on the Kamanyola–Uvira axis conducted by [[rwanda|Rwanda]]'s Defense Forces alongside the rebels, and said tactical weapons including suicide drones had been used, causing civilian casualties and trapping populations.[3]

Badlands analysis situated the capture in a campaign that began with Goma in December 2024 and Bukavu in February 2025, arguing the objective is control of the facilities where raw ore from the region's rare earth deposits — an estimated $24 trillion from this area alone — is refined for sale to Fortune 500 companies.[3] [[apple|Apple]] is being sued by the DR Congo government in a civil action over its role in the trade.[3] [[burundi|Burundi]], a rival of Rwanda and an ally of Kinshasa, was described as forming with the DRC the principal opposition to [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]]'s government.[3]

## February 2025: massacre, minerals, and sanctions

On 13 February 2025, seventy Christians were beheaded with machetes by militants of the [[allied-democratic-forces|Allied Democratic Forces]], an [[isis|Islamic State]] affiliate, in the Lubero district of the eastern DRC, according to the monitoring organization [[open-doors|Open Doors]].[4] The victims were rounded up from their homes and executed inside a Protestant church in Kasanga, and churches, schools and health centres in the area had already closed because of the deteriorating security situation.[4] Despite the scale of the atrocity, it received little international media attention.[4]

In his first interview after a Rwanda-backed armed group seized swaths of Congolese territory, President [[felix-tshisekedi|Félix Tshisekedi]] offered the United States and Europe a stake in the country's mineral wealth, a sector then dominated by China.[4] Congo is considered among the world's wealthiest countries in natural resources, supplying much of the world's coltan — used in smartphones and computers — and holding more than half of global cobalt reserves used in electric vehicles, with unmined deposits valued in excess of $24 trillion.[4] Tshisekedi likened the conflict to Russia's invasion of Ukraine and accused [[rwanda|Rwanda]] of a "mania to be the apex predator," while the European Union had agreed to pay Rwanda about $935 million for access to tin, tungsten and gold.[4]

The United States sanctioned Rwandan deputy foreign minister Gen. [[james-kabarebe|James Kabarebe]] over the violence, a designation Kigali called "unjustified and unfounded."[4] Acting for the Congolese government, the law firm [[amsterdam-and-partners|Amsterdam & Partners]] published a report accusing [[apple|Apple]], Intel, Sony and Lockheed Martin of smuggling Congolese rare earth minerals through neighbouring states to be laundered as "clean" via certification bodies such as the [[international-tin-supply-chain-initiative|ITSCI]] and the [[responsible-minerals-initiative|Responsible Minerals Initiative]], and subsequently filed suit against Apple.[4]

## US critical minerals agreement (2025)

In April 2025 the United States was reported to be closing in on a [[us-dr-congo-critical-minerals-deal|strategic minerals agreement]] with the DR Congo that could reshape the global critical minerals landscape and bolster American influence in central Africa.[5] The deal would facilitate American private-sector investment in the country's vast lithium, copper and cobalt reserves in exchange for greater U.S. support for President [[felix-tshisekedi|Félix Tshisekedi]]'s embattled government, with the [[development-finance-corporation|U.S. International Development Finance Corporation]] expected to underwrite portions of the investment.[5] Badlands commentary estimated the DRC's subsoil mineral wealth at some $24 trillion and framed the deal as a reversal of Chinese dominance in the sector.[5]

## December 2025 Washington summit with Rwanda

President Donald Trump hosted DRC President Felix Tshisekedi and Rwandan President Paul Kagame in Washington on December 4, 2025 to sign deals intended to stabilize the region and attract Western mining investment, building on an economic integration compact agreed the previous month and an unimplemented US-brokered peace deal from June.[6] Analysts said American diplomacy had paused escalation in eastern Congo without resolving core issues, and Badlands commentary argued the conflict persists because a cartel that lost control of eastern Congolese minerals under Executive Order 13818 wants them back.[6]

## May 2024 coup attempt and Israeli connections

On Sunday, May 19, 2024, the Congolese military thwarted an attempted coup led by naturalized American [[christian-malanga|Christian Malanga]] and a roughly 40-strong militia of foreigners and Congolese nationals in the Gombe area of northern Kinshasa, near the Palais de la Nation housing the president's offices; Malanga was shot dead and dozens of attackers were arrested, including his son [[marcel-malanga|Marcel Malanga]] and American [[benjamin-zalman-polun|Benjamin Zalman-Polun]].[7] Faculty at [[morehouse-college|Morehouse College]] displayed a large DRC flag behind President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] throughout his commencement address that same weekend, as antiwar activists called for international aid amid worsening violence in the country.[7] Badlands commentary tied the coup attempt to earlier reporting on former [[mossad]] chief [[yossi-cohen|Yossi Cohen]]'s 2019 visits to the DRC with mining billionaire [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]], which ended in Cohen's deportation and ban from the country.[7]

## Ceasefire disputes and the minerals pact (2025)

In September 2025 a coalition of armed groups, the [[congo-river-alliance|Congo River Alliance]] led politically by [[corneille-nangaa|Corneille Nangaa]] and including [[m23]], accused the Congolese government of breaching agreements intended to end the decades-old conflict in the country's eastern provinces, citing attacks in South Kivu.[8] M23 had intensified its offensive earlier in the year across the mineral-rich east, capturing key mining hubs including Goma and Bukavu and reportedly killing thousands.[8] In June the DR Congo signed a U.S.-brokered agreement with [[rwanda|Rwanda]], which Kinshasa accuses of arming the rebels — a claim Kigali denies — and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the pact, which includes calls for a joint security mechanism, gives Washington rights to local mineral wealth.[8] Badlands commentary tied the storyline to the earlier death of American-Congolese figure [[christian-malanga|Christian Malanga]] during a failed coup and argued there are "24 trillion reasons" behind outside interest in Congolese mineral rights.[8]
