# Corporation for Public Broadcasting

The Corporation for Public Broadcasting (CPB) is the federally chartered entity that channels congressional appropriations to American public broadcasters, including [[pbs|PBS]] and [[npr|NPR]].[1]

## 2025 defunding

In 2025 the Republican-controlled [[congress|Congress]] enacted a $1.1 billion cut in federal appropriations for the CPB, acting in response to [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s repeated calls to defund PBS and NPR over allegations of liberal bias.[1] Following the cut, the CPB began steps toward closure.[1]

## Downstream effects

The loss of CPB funding produced immediate programming consequences at the network level. PBS News Weekend, on air since 2013, aired its final live broadcast on January 11, 2026, with anchor [[john-yang|John Yang]] attributing the cancellation directly to the loss of federal funding for public media.[1] PBS substituted two pre-taped 30-minute single-topic programs, produced during the week, for the live weekend newscast as a cost-reduction measure.[1]

## Dissolution (January 2026)

In January 2026 the CPB board voted to dissolve the 58-year-old organization after [[congress|Congress]] fully rescinded its federal funding, citing sustained political attacks and an inability to fulfill its mandate under the 1967 Public Broadcasting Act.[2] The board, led by Chair [[ruby-calvert|Ruby Calvert]], described the outcome as "devastating" but assured that remaining funds would support public media transitions, preserve historical archives in partnership with the University of Maryland, and maintain the American Archive of Public Broadcasting, emphasizing that [[npr|NPR]] and [[pbs|PBS]] would continue independently.[2] The board framed the wind-up as an "act of responsible stewardship."[2]

Badlands commentators read the decision as capitulation rather than strategic retreat: [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the board conspicuously avoided the alternative of privatization, which he took as a tacit admission that the apparatus could not survive without coerced taxpayer support.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that CPB, having functioned for decades as a "leftist mood ring," could not last even a year on audience funding, and asked when the collapse would extend to its local sister stations.[2] See [[dissolution-of-the-corporation-for-public-broadcasting]].[2]

## July 2025 clawback of federal funding

In July 2025 [[congress|Congress]] approved a package clawing back $1.1 billion in federal funding to public media, targeting Corporation for Public Broadcasting appropriations.[3] Staffers at local [[npr|NPR]] and [[pbs|PBS]] stations were reported to be devastated, with small and rural stations that rely heavily on federal funding bracing for possible staff cuts and dozens of stations' futures placed in jeopardy.[3] Some stations argued they were being punished over a fight between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the national public broadcasting organizations that bore little relationship to the service smaller outlets provide.[3] Badlands writer Ashe in America rejected that argument as "emotional blackmail," citing [[colorado-public-radio|Colorado Public Radio]]'s reporting on litigation involving Badlands as evidence that local public media is itself propaganda, and concluded: "Defund it all."[3] The episode is treated on this wiki as the [[public-media-rescission-of-2025|public media rescission of 2025]].[3]

## August 2025 wind-down announcement

The Corporation for Public Broadcasting announced in August 2025 that it would begin winding down operations after its funding was eliminated by the Trump administration and Congress.[4] Founded in 1967 as a private nonprofit steward of public media funding, the CPB distributed money to 1,500 local public radio and television stations as well as PBS and NPR, and employed about 100 people.[4] President Trump signed an executive order in May instructing the organization to cease federal funding for PBS and NPR; in June the House approved a White House request to claw back $1.1 billion in already appropriated funds, and the Senate Appropriations Committee's 2026 bill eliminated CPB funding for the first time in more than 50 years.[4] President and CEO Patricia Harrison said the organization faced "the difficult reality of closing our operations" despite the efforts of millions of Americans who petitioned Congress, and the CPB told employees a majority of staff positions would conclude at the end of the fiscal year on September 30, 2025, with a small transition team remaining through January 2026.[4]
