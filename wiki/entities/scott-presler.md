# Scott Presler

Scott Presler is an American conservative activist best known for organizing large-scale voter registration drives on behalf of Republican candidates, particularly in the swing states of Pennsylvania and Wisconsin. In March 2026 he became a point of controversy inside the election integrity movement after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] highlighted him in a Truth Social post tied to the campaign to pass the [[save-america-act|SAVE America Act]].[1]

## Role in the SAVE America Act debate

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described Trump's promotion of Presler as "curious," arguing that if the SAVE America Act were properly executed Presler "will be out of business," because voter registration drives of the kind he runs do not themselves verify voter eligibility.[1] Ashe recounted asking Presler during an X Spaces discussion several years earlier how he verified eligibility in his drives, and said that he blocked her rather than answering.[1]

## Criticism from election integrity activists

The Pennsylvania-based group Audit the Vote PA published a statement, quoted in the Badlands Brief, calling Presler "a grifter and a conman" who "pushed policies that directly hurt election integrity and caused massive losses in municipal/special elections in major swing states like Pennsylvania and Wisconsin."[1] The group argued that Presler had "jump[ed] on the SAVE Act because his early voting and mail-in ballot push was a colossal failure and he needs to re-invent himself," while allowing that his platform was at least drawing attention to the legislation.[1]

Ashe in America extended the critique to Presler's underlying premise, noting that reported 2020 turnout of 94.1 percent was a global outlier according to Pew data — in her reading a "red flag indicator for malfeasance" rather than evidence of a turnout deficit requiring more registration.[1] From that premise she concluded that "Scott Presler's job is to inflate the voter rolls," and that under a properly enforced SAVE America Act he "would be back to picking up trash," a reference to the litter-cleanup events for which he first gained public attention.[1]

## Significance

The dispute over Presler illustrates a broader fault line within the pro-Trump coalition between turnout-maximizing electoral operations and the election integrity current represented by Badlands and allied groups, a tension Ashe argued Trump had deliberately spotlighted and which had "galvanized the election integrity community in a big way behind the scenes."[1]

## Speculation as RNC chair (2024)

Following [[ronna-mcdaniel|Ronna McDaniel]]'s February 2024 announcement that she would resign as chair of the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]], Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] named Presler as a possible successor — but framed the prospect sarcastically, writing that he was "the choice if you want to finish it off" and calling him "mini-Ronna with better hair."[2] She cited his having openly blamed voter behavior, rather than the system, for the problems seen in South Carolina that Saturday, and said she hoped everyone would ignore her and vote him in "so we can finally move on from the RNC."[2]

## Criticism over early-voting advocacy (2023)

Presler's promotion of early voting to Republican voters drew criticism from within the election-integrity movement after the November 2023 Georgia municipal elections.[3] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the hand-count variances in [[spalding-county|Spalding County]] and the irreconcilable tabulations in [[fulton-county|Fulton County]] arose exclusively from advance (early) voting rather than Election Day or absentee ballots — an outcome she argued "should generate heartburn" for Presler and others on the right pushing early voting.[3] See [[2023-georgia-municipal-election-discrepancies]].[3]
