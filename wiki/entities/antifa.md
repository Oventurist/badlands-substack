# Antifa

Antifa is a decentralized militant left-wing movement in the United States whose adherents were, in March 2026, the subject of what federal prosecutors described as the nation's first federal indictment targeting a coordinated group of Antifa cell members engaged in violent criminal activity.[1]

## North Texas prosecution

A federal jury in Fort Worth, Texas convicted nine defendants for their roles in a July 4, 2025 attack outside the [[prairieland-detention-center|Prairieland]] Immigration and Customs Enforcement detention center in Alvarado, according to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[1] Prosecutors described the group as members of a "North Texas Antifa cell" involved in rioting, explosives offenses and providing material support to terrorists.[1]

Authorities said the incident involved fireworks, vandalism and gunfire directed at responding law enforcement.[1] Alvarado Police Lt. Thomas Gross was shot and injured during the confrontation, and Benjamin Song was convicted of attempted murder and firearms offenses related to the shooting.[1] Eight defendants were convicted of charges including rioting, providing material support to terrorists, conspiracy to use explosives and using explosives, with fireworks cited as the explosive devices.[1] Daniel Sanchez-Estrada was convicted of corruptly concealing documents, and he and Maricela Rueda were also convicted of conspiracy to conceal documents tied to the investigation.[1] Sentencing had not been announced.[1]

HSI Dallas Special Agent in Charge Travis Pickard called the case "a historic moment as it represents the nation's initial federal indictment targeting a coordinated group of Antifa cell members engaged in violent criminal activity," describing it as a demonstration of commitment to confronting [[domestic-terrorism|domestic terrorism]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that while the term "domestic terrorism" is usually overused as a vehicle for overreach, attempting to bomb something for political purposes genuinely qualifies.[1] She characterized Antifa as ostensibly an intelligence-community operation that recruits vulnerable people to destabilize the nation, and said her assertion about methamphetamine use among Denver Antifa members rested on first-hand observation.[1] She nonetheless insisted on due process for the accused and warned against expanding the domestic terror label to capture speech, assembly or association absent underlying criminal conduct — noting that the truth community rejects such expansion when the defendant is someone like [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]].[1] "Principles are greater than politics," she concluded; "equal justice under the law is nonnegotiable."[1]

## Minneapolis arrests (February 2026)

Immigration protests in [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]] continued into early February 2026 as opposition to [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] enforcement drew agitators.[2] Authorities reportedly arrested several alleged Antifa agitators for posting death threats against federal agents online.[2] One agitator calling himself Kyle Wagner was reportedly arrested for encouraging his followers to stalk and kill ICE agents, and another agitator known to federal authorities was arrested after allegedly posting threats of violence against special agents in Minneapolis.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that marching, chanting and even occupying are protected by the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] regardless of how radical the government deems the cause, but that calling for the murder of ICE agents while doxxing them with photographs is terrorism rather than peaceful protest or civil disobedience.[2] She argued that threats of violence against specific individuals or groups and actual violence — striking an agent with a car, assaulting officers, damaging property, or violently resisting with a concealed firearm — are not protected speech: "Words are protected. Violence is not."[2]

## Federal funding investigations (2026)

By March 2026 antifa-linked financing had become the explicit target of multiple federal initiatives.[3] The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] was assembling a task force to probe antifa-linked funding, US attorney offices were directed to assign domestic terrorism coordinators, and [[fbi|FBI]] and [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] agents were creating a joint task force — described as a "mission control command center" based at the FBI — to investigate nonprofits suspected of funding [[domestic-terrorism|domestic terrorism]] or political violence.[3] These steps followed a December memo from Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] instructing federal agencies to prioritize investigations of antifa and other extremist groups, flagging potential violence, anti-American and anti-religious ideologies, and tax fraud, and ordering law enforcement to compile intelligence on such groups for the FBI.[3] The memo built on a September executive order from [[donald-trump|President Trump]] on domestic terrorism issued after high-profile incidents including the 2025 assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[3]

## Gracie Mansion counterprotest (2023)

On Sunday, August 27, 2023, black-clad Antifa counterprotesters appeared at a demonstration outside the New York City mayor's mansion against Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]]' handling of the [[new-york-city-migrant-crisis|migrant crisis]], and police struggled to contain the resulting fistfights and body slams.[4] Counterprotesters carried signs reading "NO ONE CHOOSES TO BE A REFUGEE" and "HUMANITY HAS NO BORDERS," with some shouting obscenities at the NYPD.[4] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the opposition to the anti-migrant rally came not from migrants themselves but from Antifa agitators, whom she described as cheap and easy to mobilize, and read the confrontation as an engineered rehearsal for a repeat of the 2020 "Summer of Love" the following summer.[4]

## Terrorist designation push (September 2025)

On Wednesday, 17 September 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that Antifa would be designated a "major terrorist organization" in the United States, following the assassination of conservative activist [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[5] The move drew rapid international emulation: the following day the parliament of the [[netherlands|Netherlands]] adopted a motion from opposition leader [[geert-wilders|Geert Wilders]] calling on the Dutch government to designate Antifa a terrorist organization, alleging that Antifa cells in the country threatened politicians, disrupted public events, and intimidated students and journalists.[5] Analysts noted that implementation in the Netherlands would broaden law enforcement powers over surveillance, monitoring, and prosecution of suspected Antifa activity.[5]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright observed that Trump had first signaled his intention to designate Antifa a terrorist organization in 2020, arguing that the 2025 push was not a reaction to the Kirk assassination but the cultivation of political mandate on the back of it, and that "mapping began long ago."[5] The same commentary linked the designation effort to a [[capital-research-center|Capital Research Center]] report published the same day alleging that the [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]] had directed more than $80 million to groups tied to terrorism, including the Antifa-linked [[stop-cop-city|Stop Cop City]] campaign — material that could serve as a predicate for the designation and for RICO charges against [[george-soros|George Soros]].[5]

## Trump's designation comments (15 September 2025)

On Monday, 15 September 2025 — two days before formally announcing the designation — President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would consider naming Antifa as domestic terrorists, previewing a plan to crack down on what he cast as a widespread network of left-wing radicals inciting violence.[6] Asked whether he would assign a domestic terrorism label to the movement, he replied, "It's something I would do, yeah," saying he would proceed if he found support from Cabinet officials and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]].[6] Speaking in the Oval Office he added, "Antifa is terrible. There are other groups."[6]

It was not clear who or what exactly Trump would designate, as Antifa is a loosely organized movement without a distinct leader, membership lists or structure.[6] The comments followed signals from a host of administration officials, in the wake of the assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]], that they would target what they claimed was a coordinated left-wing effort to incite violence; no evidence had publicly emerged that the alleged assassin was working as part of a larger coordinated effort.[6]

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] pointed back to the memo Trump issued on 5 January 2021 ordering an assessment of whether to classify Antifa as a terrorist organization — noting it was issued the day before what Badlands characterises as the January 6 false flag.[6]

## October 2025 White House roundtable on FTO designation

In October 2025, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and administration officials discussed designating Antifa a [[foreign-terrorist-organization|foreign terrorist organization]] during a [[white-house|White House]] roundtable with right-wing media figures.[7] Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] compared Antifa to designated terrorist groups such as [[hamas|Hamas]] and ISIS, stating that they are equally dangerous and aim to destroy the United States.[7]

Trump suggested that an FTO designation could lead to severe sanctions for alleged supporters, despite Antifa being a decentralized movement rather than a specific organization.[7] He also reiterated his stance on flag burning, claiming that a one-year prison sentence for the act effectively took away freedom of speech by equating it to inciting riots.[7] Administration officials accused Democratic leaders in cities such as Portland and Chicago of covering up Antifa's actions, with Trump suggesting criminal prosecution for some.[7]

### Badlands commentary

[[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] questioned which foreign country the designation would reference and what it means for American citizens to be treated as foreign combatants, concluding that the framing is defensible: one cannot be America First while serving a global Regime, and if the global Regime directed the usurpation of the country, its non-America-First foot soldiers would qualify as foreign combatants.[7]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] offered a more ambivalent take, saying she had "mixed feelings" about the roundtable.[7] She affirmed that Antifa is real based on personal experience in 2020, when activists screamed in her face and followed her to her car, and said she believes they are paid — possibly in methamphetamine.[7] She observed that during weekly marches in Colorado from election day 2020 to inauguration day 2021, Antifa appeared only once: the day the Proud Boys showed up in colors, suggesting they mobilize only when properly incentivized.[7]

Ashe argued that the money trail behind such violence probably leads to a foreign entity, but noted that after consulting AI she concluded the U.S. legal framework makes a "foreign combatant" designation difficult absent a domestic declaration of war, since that status arises under military law while domestic terrorism on U.S. soil is governed by civilian law, with military applications largely barred by the [[posse-comitatus-act|Posse Comitatus Act]].[7] The AI response she quoted held that if Antifa violence escalated to an insurrection, the President could invoke the [[insurrection-act|Insurrection Act]] to deploy troops, after which U.S. citizens would be transferred to civilian courts for charges such as [[domestic-terrorism|domestic terrorism]] (up to life if deadly), material support to an FTO (up to 20 years), or treason if proven to be paid agents of a foreign power levying war against the United States.[7]

Her deeper concern was the "whole of government approach" to pursuing loosely affiliated groups of Americans.[7] Citing her own experience defending freedom of association in federal court, she wrote that she believes "in my core that rights must supersede political and social change efforts," and asked how much of a "party-inverted rerun" the approach would prove to be.[7]

## Atlanta "cop city" shootout (January 2023)

On Wednesday, January 18, 2023, the [[georgia-bureau-of-investigation|Georgia Bureau of Investigation]] and other agencies raided an "autonomous zone" in a wooded area south of Atlanta at the site of the future Atlanta Public Safety Training Center, which Antifa and other far-left extremists from across the United States had occupied since June 2021 to block construction of what they called "cop city."[8] A Georgia State Patrol trooper was shot and severely injured by a man camped in the area; officers returned fire and killed the gunman, who allegedly used a pistol.[8] Seven militants, all from out of state, were arrested and charged with domestic terrorism.[8] See [[stop-cop-city|Stop Cop City]].[8]

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that Antifa is far more than the "idea" President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] described after the 2020 riots, describing it as a loose-knit willingly militant civilian proxy army of the far left, comparable to the pre-Nazi Sturmabteilung, whose rank-and-file are manipulated "useful idiots" directed by more organised elements as part of fourth- and [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]].[8] He noted that after the Georgia shooting militants called for violence against police and anyone they deemed not a "comrade."[8]

## Atlanta and Boston unrest (January 2023)

Following the killing of the environmental activist known as [[tortuguita|Tortuguita]] by Georgia authorities, a Saturday night march in downtown Atlanta turned violent, with masked activists dressed in all black throwing rocks, lighting fireworks outside the skyscraper housing the [[atlanta-police-foundation|Atlanta Police Foundation]], burning a police cruiser and covering walls in anti-police graffiti.[9] [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] described the events as Antifa-led protests reminiscent of the 2020 "summer of love," and criticised FOX 5 reporter Tyler Fingert for terming them "mostly peaceful" on air as a cruiser burned behind him.[9]

Days later, [[jared-dowell|Jared Dowell]] — the child of House Minority Whip [[katherine-clark|Katherine Clark]] — was arrested in Boston on charges of assault by means of a dangerous weapon, destruction or injury of personal property, and damage of property by graffiti, allegedly while participating in rioting meant to show solidarity with the Antifa rioting in Atlanta.[9]

## September 2025 terrorist designation

On September 17, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced he would designate antifa a "major terrorist organization," describing the movement in a Truth Social post as "A SICK, DANGEROUS, RADICAL LEFT DISASTER."[10] He said he would be "strongly recommending" that those funding antifa be thoroughly investigated "in accordance with the highest legal standards and practices," days after saying he would "100%" consider the designation.[10] Badlands commentary characterized antifa's 2020 activity as selective and coordinated, citing Craigslist advertisements and recorded Zoom meetings as evidence of a well-funded effort behind street violence that some described as election interference.[10] See [[antifa-terrorist-designation|Antifa terrorist designation]].
