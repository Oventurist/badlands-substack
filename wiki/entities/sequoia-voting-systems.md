# Sequoia Voting Systems

Sequoia Voting Systems was a major American provider of voting equipment whose ownership history links [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]] to [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] in the account of election-system consolidation presented in the June 9, 2026 *Badlands Brief*.[1]

In 2005 Smartmatic purchased Sequoia from the British firm [[de-la-rue|De La Rue PLC]] for $16 million, a transaction that represented a $19 million loss for De La Rue relative to the $35 million it had invested four years earlier.[1] Two years later Smartmatic announced it would divest Sequoia. An April 2008 Delaware Court filing, however, showed that Smartmatic retained a $2 million unsecured promissory note from the divestiture and — more significantly, in the Brief's telling — that Sequoia was still using Smartmatic's intellectual property.[1]

A few months after acquiring [[premier-election-solutions|Premier Election Solutions]] under a Justice Department divestiture order, Dominion Voting Systems purchased Sequoia as well.[1] Dominion's press release, quoted in the Brief, announced that it had "acquired the assets of Sequoia Voting Systems, a major U.S. provider of voting solutions serving nearly 300 jurisdictions in 16 states," and that the transaction included "Sequoia's inventory and all intellectual property, including software, firmware and hardware, for Sequoia's precinct and central count optical scan and DRE voting solutions."[1]

Badlands treats this sequence — Smartmatic buys Sequoia, sells it while retaining an interest and continuing IP presence, and Dominion then buys it — as the mechanism by which foreign-originated code became embedded across American election infrastructure.[1]
