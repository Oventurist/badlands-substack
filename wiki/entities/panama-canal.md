# Panama Canal

The Panama Canal is a strategic interoceanic waterway through Panama that became a flashpoint in United States–China relations during 2025, following President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s vow to take control of the canal.[1]

## Port ownership dispute

Two ports at either end of the canal are owned by the Hong Kong–listed firm [[ck-hutchison|CK Hutchison]].[1] After Trump's statements about reasserting American control, CK Hutchison sought to sell the ports to a US-led consortium, but Beijing blocked the transaction.[1] Sources cited by The Wall Street Journal reported that Beijing had already hoped to use the Panama port issue as a bargaining chip in negotiations with Washington, and The Economist assessed that [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]]'s refusal to clarify his position on the deal indicated an attempt to build leverage over tariffs, Taiwan, and related disputes.[1]

## US security engagement

On April 8, 2025, US Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] met Panamanian President [[jose-raul-mulino|José Raúl Mulino]] for talks amid rising tension over global trade and alleged Chinese influence in the waterway, with Hegseth afterward hailing "increased security cooperation" between the two countries.[1] The visit followed Panama's announcement that it would seek an investigation into local executives of the ports' owner.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the episode as the removal of a prospective bargaining chip from the board amid the broader trade conflict, while endorsing protection of US southern trade routes and characterizing the Chinese Communist Party as notoriously exploitative.[1]

## 2025 US military planning

In March 2025 the [[white-house|White House]] directed the US military to draw up options for increasing the American troop presence in Panama in service of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s stated goal of "reclaiming" the canal, according to two US officials familiar with the planning.[2] In a joint address to Congress earlier that month Trump had said, "to further enhance our national security, my administration will be reclaiming the Panama Canal," and administration officials subsequently declined to define what "reclaiming" meant.[2] [[united-states-southern-command|US Southern Command]] developed potential plans ranging from closer partnership with Panamanian security forces to the less likely option of US troops seizing the canal by force, with the use of force said to depend on the degree of Panamanian cooperation.[2] The stated objective was to diminish China's influence over the 50-mile waterway; both Panama and China denied any foreign interference, and the canal's neutrality is enshrined in Panama's constitution.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] highlighted the casual disclosure that the US Army Corps of Engineers still operates the canal's locks, and juxtaposed the coverage with [[blackrock|BlackRock]]'s $23 billion acquisition of the canal's ports — a transaction Trump had backed.[2] Citing Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]]'s characterization of alleged Chinese involvement as custodial rather than controlling, he suggested the Panama narrative served a purpose beyond its surface, and speculated that it might unravel into public disclosure of CIA regime-change operations in Central and South America, connecting the Green Berets' long history in Panama to Operation Condor.[2]

## 2025 BlackRock ports acquisition

On March 4, 2025, the Hong Kong-based conglomerate [[ck-hutchison|CK Hutchison]] agreed to sell the units operating ports near the canal to a consortium including [[blackrock|BlackRock Inc.]] in a $23 billion transaction, after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] alleged Chinese interference with the operations of the critical shipping lane.[3] The consortium — BlackRock, Global Infrastructure Partners and Terminal Investment Limited — acquired 90% interests in Panama Ports Company, which owns and operates the ports of Balboa and Cristobal, as part of a broader portfolio of 43 ports in 23 countries.[3] See [[blackrock-panama-canal-ports-deal|the BlackRock Panama Canal ports deal]].[3]
