# Benghazi

Benghazi is the eastern Libyan city that served as the stronghold of the 2011 uprising against [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] and, in the Badlands Media account, as the operational centre of an alleged CIA/shadow-government program to train and move Islamist fighters.[1]

## McCain's 2011 visit

In April 2011 Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] became the most senior U.S. official to visit the rebel-held city, meeting opposition leaders — among them [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] — and declaring, "What I have witnessed today in Benghazi is a powerful and hopeful example of what a free Libya could be."[1] He also insisted the fighters "are not Al-Qaeda."[1] The article asks why the most senior American official to travel to Benghazi was McCain, and answers that his role was to lend legitimacy to the groups being backed.[1]

## Training hub

A 2013 McClatchy DC report described roughly a dozen Syrians arriving weekly at Benghazi's airport for what was described as insurgent training, flying out with fake Libyan passports; officials said the city had become "a regional hub for Islamist extremists seeking to hone their combat skills," with Tunisian and Algerian fighters also training there, and that "the training process is better organized and financed than had been realized."[1] The article argues that these Al-Qaeda training camps in Benghazi replaced those lost in Afghanistan after 9/11, supplying a steady flow of fighters, and that trainees were flown on Belhaj's airline to Syria via Turkey to join [[isis|ISIS]] while Obama dismissed ISIS as the "J.V. team."[1]

## Consulate security

Security at the U.S. consulate in Benghazi was assigned in part to the [[february-17-brigade|February 17 Brigade]], the Belhaj-commanded unit that had helped topple Gaddafi; the article states the brigade did nothing to stop the attack.[1] It concludes that Obama, Hillary Clinton and McCain had a clear "conflict of interest" where Benghazi was concerned, and promises a subsequent article on the attack and why Ambassador Stevens was targeted.[1]
