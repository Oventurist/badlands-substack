# Benghazi

Benghazi is the Libyan city that, in the article's account, became both the hub of a covert weapons pipeline and the site of the September 11, 2012 attack on American facilities.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

After Muammar Gaddafi was overthrown, Benghazi became a safe haven for Al Qaeda training camps and the focal point for the funding and supplying of jihadists fighting U.S. forces in Iraq and Afghanistan.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The Times reported that arms ships bound for Syrian rebels originated in Benghazi, docking in Alexandria, Egypt, where the manifest was changed before continuing to Lebanon or [[turkey|Turkey]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|Christopher Stevens]] operated from the city as liaison between weapons dealers and the Al Qaeda-linked Libyan authorities.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The article stresses that the U.S. embassy was in Tripoli, which had more security, and repeatedly asks why Stevens was in Benghazi on the night he died.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) It concludes that "Benghazi was at the center of it all" — the [[benghazi-attack|attack]], the cover-up, and the arms transfers to [[isis|ISIS]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

## The 2011 uprising

Benghazi was the stronghold and birthplace of the 2011 insurgency that, with NATO's help, toppled [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]].2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) [[john-mccain|Senator John McCain]] visited the city in April 2011, becoming the most senior U.S. official to reach the rebel-held east, and praised the anti-Gaddafi fighters as "heroes" while urging every nation to recognise the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]] as the legitimate voice of the Libyan people.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) He declared that "what I have witnessed today in Benghazi is a powerful and hopeful example of what a free Libya could be" and insisted the fighters "are not Al-Qaeda" but "Libyan patriots."2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The men he met were leaders of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]], headed by [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]], and the purpose of the visit is characterised as conferring legitimacy on them in the eyes of the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Regional training hub

McClatchy DC reporting from December 2013, cited in the article, described about a dozen Syrians arriving each week at Benghazi's airport for insurgent training and departing with fake Libyan passports, with fighters from Tunisia and Algeria also driving across Libya's borders to train there.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The organised air movement of Syrians indicated that the training process was "better organized and financed than had been realized."2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article argues that these camps were a deliberate relocation of the Al-Qaeda training infrastructure that had been based in Afghanistan before 9/11, undertaken by the [[cia|CIA]] to maintain a supply of fighters, and that recruits were flown onward to Syria through Turkey on Belhaj's airline to join ISIS with taxpayer funding.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Consulate security

The [[february-17-brigade|February 17 Brigade]], the militia Belhaj commanded from [[qatar|Qatar]] during the uprising, was later assigned to the security detail at the U.S. consulate in Benghazi and, the article states, did nothing to stop the attack.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) It frames the conduct of Obama, Hillary Clinton and McCain over Benghazi as a plain conflict of interest.2(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)
