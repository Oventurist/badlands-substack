# American Bar Association

The American Bar Association (ABA) is the professional association that accredits U.S. law schools, an authority that came under direct legislative threat in July 2026.[1]

## Move to strip accreditation authority

Rep. [[brandon-gill|Brandon Gill]], R-Texas, said he intends to pursue legislation that would remove the ABA's authority to accredit U.S. law schools.[1] Gill, who chairs the House Oversight Committee's Task Force on Defending Constitutional Rights and Exposing Institutional Abuse, said lawmakers are examining federal legislation that could reduce the ABA's accreditation role while leaving states free to decide whether to work with the organization.[1] He alleged that the association has exercised significant influence over law school admissions, curricula and continuing legal education.[1]

## DEI controversy

Gill's comments followed a House hearing at which ABA President [[michelle-behnke|Michelle Behnke]] defended the organization's approach to diversity, equity and inclusion, testifying that diversity initiatives strengthen the legal profession and that diversity and excellence are "not mutually exclusive."[1] The ABA has begun rescinding its DEI requirements for law schools after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] signed an executive order addressing such mandates; Gill criticized the previous requirements as discriminatory against certain students.[1]

## Loss of DOJ access

In 2025, Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] sent a letter to the ABA stripping its preferred access to the judicial nomination process, writing that "while the ABA is free to comment on judicial nominations along with other activist organizations, there is no justification for treating the ABA differently from such other activist organizations and the Department of Justice will not do so."[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the ABA as "the center of the beast when it comes to controlling lawyers and judges and the institution of law itself," describing NGOs and professional associations as the connective tissue of a corrupt shadow control structure now being exposed and stripped of its special powers.[1]

## "The Guilds of Florence" critique

In June 2026 [[ed-martin|Ed Martin]] declared that "The Bar Associations are ending themselves. Soon, they will be like the Guilds of Florence" — a reference to the Florentine guilds abolished by decree in 1770 after a popular uprising.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] amplified the argument in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], describing professional associations as entirely unaccountable bodies wielding enormous power, the institutional form of what she calls the expert class.[2]

She argued the bar associations, considered authorities over the legal profession, act more like unions when attorney or law firm conduct confirms their political biases, citing the treatment of Jeff Clark, John Eastman, Rachel Alexander and Brian Blehm.[2] She recalled the Justice Department letter from [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] stating that while the ABA "is free to comment on judicial nominations along with other activist organizations, there is no justification for treating the ABA differently from such other activist organizations and the Department of Justice will not do so."[2] The occasion for the exchange was an ethics complaint filed against Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] after the dismissal of the case against [[kilmar-abrego-garcia|Kilmar Abrego Garcia]].[2]

## DOJ ends ABA vetting of judicial nominees (May 2025)

On Thursday, May 29, 2025, Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] sent a letter to the American Bar Association announcing that the Justice Department would no longer vet judicial nominees through the group because of its alleged bias.[3] The letter followed the ABA's practice of rating President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s nominees "unqualified" more often than those of other administrations, though the vast majority of his nominees were nonetheless approved by the association.[3]

Bondi accused the ABA of failing to correct the bias in its ratings and said the administration would no longer treat it differently from other organizations, while acknowledging it remained free to comment on nominations.[3] "Unfortunately, the ABA no longer functions as a fair arbiter of nominees' qualifications, and its ratings invariably and demonstrably favor nominees put forth by Democratic administrations," she wrote to ABA President William Bay, adding that "there is no justification for treating the ABA differently from such other activist organizations and the [DOJ] will not do so."[3] The changes included revoking the ABA's access to certain non-public information about nominees and barring nominees from sitting for interviews with the association or answering its written questions.[3]

## Badlands reaction

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the move "definitely a win," writing that through her own court experience she had learned there is broad consensus that the bar incentivizes behavior contrary to the ends of justice and that its rewards and punishments are asymmetrical and often political.[3] She characterized the outcome as reducing the ABA to "just another leftist NGO," asked whether the ACLU could be next, and predicted that partisan non-governmental organizations would eventually lose their tax statuses and their special privileges in court.[3]
