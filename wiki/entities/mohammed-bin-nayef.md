# Mohammed bin Nayef

Mohammed bin Nayef is a Saudi royal who served as Crown Prince of Saudi Arabia until June 2017 and was, in Badlands Media's characterization, a longtime ally of the [[cia|CIA]].[1]

## Displacement by MBS

In June 2017 — one month after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] visited Riyadh and received the ceremonial sword dance — then Deputy Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] was named Crown Prince by his father, King [[salman-bin-abdulaziz|Salman]], usurping bin Nayef in what Badlands Media described as "a dramatic display of power projection by the emerging Sovereign Alliance."[1] The succession is presented in the Badlands framework as a rupture between the Saudi state and the Anglo-American intelligence networks that had cultivated bin Nayef.[1]



## Displacement in June 2017

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s article "Trump Takes Out the King" gives a detailed account of bin Nayef's removal, drawing on the [[new-yorker|New Yorker]].[2] The New Yorker described him as having forged close relationships with American officials — "the go-to person on counterterrorism," according to a senior counterterrorism official in the Obama administration — and noted that for King [[salman-bin-abdulaziz|Salman]] the choice of bin Nayef was politically astute because his only children were two daughters, meaning no one in his bloodline could succeed him.[2] Lange treats those American connections as evidence that bin Nayef was tied to the network of puppets controlled by [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Alwaleed bin Talal]], and argues that [[donald-trump|Trump]] knew who controlled him and was not going to let him become king.[2]

On the evening of June 21, 2017, viewers of the Saudi state channel Al Arabiya saw [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] stride up to his rival, theatrically kiss his hand and drop to his knees, with bin Nayef declaring, "I pledge allegiance to you, through the best and the worst."[2] The New Yorker reported that the transfer of power was anything but amicable: the night before, bin Nayef had been summoned to the palace, where guards surrounded him, confiscated his phone and demanded he abdicate.[2] He refused, but agreed to surrender his position as dawn neared.[2] M.B.S. installed a loyal relative as interior minister and bin Nayef was confined to his house, where even powerful American friends including former [[cia|CIA]] directors [[george-tenet|George Tenet]] and [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] were unable to reach him.[2] Lange highlights that detail as evidence of who bin Nayef's patrons had been.[2] Crown Prince Mohammed had also been backed for the succession by [[mohammed-bin-zayed|Mohammed bin Zayed]] of the United Arab Emirates.[2]
