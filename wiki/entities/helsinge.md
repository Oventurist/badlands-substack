# Helsinge

Helsinge is an oil-trading and consulting firm with offices in Geneva, Switzerland and Miami, Florida that sat at the center of the corruption case surrounding Venezuela's state oil company, [[pdvsa|PDVSA]].[1]

## The Geneva and Miami raids

In 2018, attorney [[david-boies|David Boies]] filed civil lawsuits against 42 oil-industry executives in the District of Southern Florida and, in parallel, in Geneva, where the companies involved all maintained offices.[1] Swiss authorities immediately raided Helsinge's Geneva office and seized computer servers, material that launched a series of additional investigations and led to numerous arrests of industry officials in subsequent years.[1]

Three months later the firm's Miami office was raided, and German national [[matthias-krull|Matthias Krull]] was arrested and charged with laundering $1.2 billion stolen from PDVSA.[1] Krull's subsequent cooperation opened [[operation-money-flight|Operation Money Flight]].[1]

## Continuing relevance

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argues that when the evidence seized in the Helsinge raids is combined with discovery from the paused 2018 PDVSA lawsuits and any new suspicious activity reports generated once [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] lifts Venezuela sanctions, the result will be a wide web of conspiracy implicating numerous individuals and corporations.[1]

## Raids following the Boies filings

In March 2018, [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]]'s lawyer [[david-boies|David Boies]] filed a RICO-styled corruption case against 42 entities in both the Southern District of Florida and Geneva, Switzerland.[2] Within days of the filing, Swiss authorities raided the office of the lawsuit's primary defendant, Helsinge, and four months later [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] raided the Helsinge office in Miami, arresting banker [[mathias-krull|Mathias Krull]] and charging him with laundering over $1.2 billion for oil companies, banks, and the cartel.[2] Badlands treats Krull as the keystone of the case, alongside [[executive-order-13850|Executive Order 13850]].[2]
