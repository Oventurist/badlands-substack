# William McFarland

William McFarland served as Sergeant at Arms of the United States House of Representatives, one of the officials with responsibility for security of the Capitol complex.[1]

## January 6 footage arrangement

Like Capitol Police Chief [[thomas-manger|Thomas Manger]], McFarland told associates that he learned of Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s apparent deal to grant [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] access to thousands of hours of January 6, 2021 Capitol security footage only around the time [[axios|Axios]] broke the news on Monday, February 20, 2023.[1] [[politico|Politico]] reported that the arrangement surprised officials with oversight responsibility over the footage, which Capitol Police had been extremely reluctant to release out of concern for the safety of lawmakers, aides and officers.[1]

## Vote against State of the Union fencing (February 2023)

Earlier, in the week before President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s February 2023 [[state-of-the-union|State of the Union]] address, McFarland cast the lone dissenting vote as the three-member [[capitol-police-board|Capitol Police Board]] split 2–1 in favor of erecting a temporary security fence around the Capitol complex.[2] A person familiar with the vote said his rationale was that there was "no actionable intelligence to necessitate putting up a fence," noting that State of the Union addresses before the January 6, 2021 attack had not required one, although Capitol Police had reinstalled fencing for the 2022 speech.[2] The fencing went up regardless, and both Capitol Police and House Sergeant at Arms officials declined to comment on the split.[2] Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] called it notable that Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s own sergeant at arms had voted against the fence.[2]
