# Anna Cross

Anna Cross is an attorney who represented Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] during the February 2024 evidentiary hearing on whether Willis should be disqualified from the Georgia election interference prosecution of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his co-defendants.[1]

## Role in the February 16, 2024 hearing

Cross conducted the cross-examination of [[terrence-bradley|Terrence Bradley]], the business partner and divorce attorney of special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]], whose direct examination by defense attorney [[ashleigh-merchant|Ashleigh Merchant]] had been repeatedly interrupted by objections on grounds of attorney–client privilege.[1] Rather than rehabilitating the witness, Cross sought to impeach Bradley and discredit him before the court — a move Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described as a "puzzling turn of events," because Bradley had spent the day protecting Wade's account.[1]

Ashe in America suggested that the decision to abandon Bradley coincided with the judge's agreement to review, *in camera*, text messages exchanged between Merchant and Bradley — evidence the Willis team had objected to on privilege grounds.[1] Whatever value Bradley held for the defense, the assessment ran, he had to be impeached once that review was granted.[1]

## Possible waiver of privilege

Commentators noted a significant risk in the tactic: by attacking her own side's cooperative witness, Cross may have opened the door for Merchant and the defense to compel Bradley to answer questions he had earlier refused on privilege grounds, effectively forfeiting the privilege he had claimed.[1] The impeachment effort included public accusations that Bradley had committed sexual assault during the period he worked with Wade, and the calling of an additional witness to testify about "going to clubs" and other conduct; that witness was excluded as irrelevant and in violation of the rules.[1] Ashe in America characterized the episode as a "public humiliation ritual."[1]
