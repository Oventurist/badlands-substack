# Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court

The Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court (FISC), often referred to in press coverage simply as "the nation's spy court," is the specialized federal tribunal that reviews government applications for surveillance authorities under the [[foreign-intelligence-surveillance-act|Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act]] (FISA). Its proceedings are conducted almost entirely in secret, and its docket rarely surfaces publicly except when the government itself moves to declassify or share material generated in the course of a FISA matter.[1]

## Role in the Russiagate disclosures

In July 2025 the court quietly approved a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] request to review information tied to the FISA warrants that had targeted former Trump campaign associate [[carter-page|Carter Page]], as FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] sought to hand additional Russiagate material over to [[us-congress|Congress]].[1] The Justice Department's filings with the court showed that the FBI intended to release further information about the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] investigation in response to document requests from the Republican-led [[house-judiciary-committee|House]] and [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate]] Judiciary Committees.[1]

The step followed an earlier tranche of declassifications that Patel had ordered in April 2025 at the direction of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], covering documents tied to what reporting described as the bureau's "deeply flawed and politically motivated" Trump-Russia inquiry.[1] Because the underlying records were generated in classified FISA proceedings, the court's sign-off functioned as a procedural gate: without it, the executive branch could not lawfully transmit the material to congressional oversight committees.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writers treated the court's approval as a significant signal rather than a technicality, arguing that an active review of the origins of the Russia collusion narrative amounted to an investigation "of the investigators."[1] Contributor Burning Bright framed the timing — coming alongside the announcement of investigations touching [[james-comey|James Comey]] and [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] — as evidence that accountability for the 2016-era intelligence activity was advancing, even if the mechanism and schedule remained opaque.[1] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] raised a related question: why a sitting CIA director could generate criminal referrals on Brennan after reviewing intelligence activity surrounding the 2016 election when Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] had reviewed comparable material and had not.[1]
