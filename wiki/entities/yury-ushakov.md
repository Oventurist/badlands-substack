# Yury Ushakov

**Yury Ushakov** is a Kremlin foreign policy aide who serves as a senior adviser to the Russian presidency on international affairs and frequently articulates Moscow's position on high-profile negotiations.[1]

## Role in the 2026 Iran nuclear track

On June 23, 2026, Ushakov commented on the state of the [[2026-iran-deal-negotiations|United States–Iran negotiations]] following President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s announcement that [[iran|Iran]] had agreed to expanded nuclear inspections.[1] Ushakov stated that while the developments were positive, achieving a lasting peace would take time.[1] He further indicated that [[russia|Russia]] was open to hosting Iran's enriched uranium, framing the offer as a concession to all the powers involved in the dispute.[1]

Badlands Media commentary treated Ushakov's remarks as evidence that Moscow was deliberately supplying diplomatic off-ramps at the moment the escalation path preferred by the [[globalist-hegemon|Globalist Hegemon]] had been foreclosed, and as part of a broader pattern in which Russia positions itself alongside rather than against Trump on the Iranian file.[1] In this reading, hosting the enriched uranium would prevent weaponization while preserving Iran's on-ramp to a civil nuclear energy program, consistent with the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and multipolar framework the outlet has advanced.[1]

## Statements on Ukraine settlement talks (November 2025)

In November 2025 Ushakov, as an aide to President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], said Moscow and Washington were continuing their dialogue on resolving the Ukraine conflict in line with the understanding reached at the Alaska meeting between Putin and U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in August.[2] Although that summit failed to yield a breakthrough, Moscow praised what it called Washington's willingness to mediate and to consider the conflict's underlying causes.[2] Russian officials also maintained that continued dialogue creates opportunities for trade and economic cooperation despite the U.S. decision the previous month to sanction the oil companies [[rosneft|Rosneft]] and Lukoil.[2]

Speaking to journalist Pavel Zarubin on Sunday, November 16, Ushakov said Russia was receiving "many signals" from the United States, with the Anchorage meeting still acting as the basis for talks, adding, "We do believe it is a good way forward."[2] He said the understandings remain relevant because Washington has never explicitly stated that they are no longer valid.[2] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the remarks as confirmation that "the understanding is still in force" and the alliance still at work, and predicted a follow-up summit in Budapest, possibly before Christmas.[2]
