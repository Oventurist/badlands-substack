# Bill Pulte

Bill Pulte is a Trump administration official who, as of mid-2026, serves as acting Director of National Intelligence, heading the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]].[1]

## Acting DNI tenure and declassification authority

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told reporters in early July 2026 that Pulte would serve in the acting role for only "a month or two," but that he is authorized to "declassify almost everything."[1] That grant of authority situates Pulte at the center of the administration's declassification push, which runs in parallel with a [[white-house|White House]] task force gathering intelligence documents on the 2020 presidential election from the [[cia|CIA]], the [[national-security-agency|NSA]], ODNI, the [[fbi|FBI]], and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]].[1]

## Commentary

Independent commentators have treated the ODNI-linked disclosure effort as a bellwether. Writing in the Badlands Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the anticipated release as "one of those anticipated disclosures that will serve as a check on whether we're actually winning," arguing that there can be no "Golden Age without a Justice Phase" and that the core of any such phase must be elections.[1] The same commentary allowed that "this ODNI thing could be a rug," or alternatively "the exact catalyst for change we desperately need," and expressed hope that it would drop before the United States' 250th birthday.[1]

## Retention as acting DNI (June 2026)

In June 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that he was canceling [[jay-clayton|Jay Clayton]]'s Senate confirmation hearing to become director of national intelligence, a decision that left Pulte on track to assume the role of acting DNI that Friday following the departure of [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]].[2] In a Truth Social post, Trump said Republicans had agreed to remove Pulte as acting DNI in exchange for Democratic support on reauthorization of [[section-702|Section 702]] of the [[foreign-intelligence-surveillance-act|Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act]], but claimed Democrats later indicated they would oppose the measure, writing that "The Republicans wound up having fulfilled their commitment, but Dumocrats broke the Deal."[2] Trump added that he would not approve FISA reauthorization without the [[save-america-act|SAVE AMERICA Act]] attached, arguing that Republicans had "fallen into a trap."[2]

## Campaign against Jerome Powell (July 2025)

On July 2, 2025, Pulte called on [[congress|Congress]] to investigate [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]], alleging that Powell's recent Senate testimony about the central bank's planned headquarters renovations was "deceptive."[3] Writing on X, Pulte said the testimony, together with what he described as Powell's political bias, was "enough to be removed 'for cause,'" though he did not provide evidence for his claim that Powell made inaccurate statements.[3] He had already urged Powell to resign in a separate post and, as head of a major housing regulator, pushed the Fed to lower rates, arguing that high borrowing costs were hurting Americans.[3]

_The Hill_ described the effort as "an unprecedented campaign from the helm of one independent financial regulatory agency to oust the head of another," noting that as [[federal-housing-finance-agency|FHFA]] director Pulte oversees the financial stability of [[fannie-mae|Fannie Mae]] and [[freddie-mac|Freddie Mac]] and works with fellow regulators such as Powell to protect Americans from major financial shocks.[3] Pulte also circulated a post noting that, for the first time since 2006, purchasing an entry-level home cost more than twice as much per month as renting.[3] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] amplified Pulte's call for Powell's resignation that evening.[3]

## Portable mortgages (November 2025)

On Wednesday, November 12, 2025, Pulte said in a social media post that the administration was "actively evaluating [[portable-mortgages|portable mortgages]]," days after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s call for 50-year mortgages fell flat with industry and consumers.[4] He offered no details on how such a program would work; in theory a portable mortgage would allow a homeowner to transfer an existing mortgage rate from one home to another.[4] The proposals came as the Trump administration sought to address rising voter concerns about affordability, a key issue in a slate of elections Republicans lost by wider-than-expected margins the previous week, and Pulte had been quick to praise Trump's weekend post on 50-year mortgages.[4]

## Cook mortgage-fraud referral (August 2025)

In August 2025 Pulte's [[federal-housing-finance-agency|FHFA]] referred [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Governor [[lisa-cook|Lisa D. Cook]] to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] over allegations that she falsified bank documents and property records to obtain favorable mortgage terms on homes in Ann Arbor, Michigan, and Atlanta, Georgia.[5] Anticipating retaliation, Pulte posted on X: "Watch. The hit pieces will start to POUR IN because I am exposing the MORTGAGE FRAUD of the Elites," naming a reporter he said was preparing a story alleging that he was not in charge of the government-sponsored enterprises under the president.[5] He discussed the Cook investigation in a widely circulated clip in which he also attacked Representative [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]].[5]

## Portable mortgage evaluation (November 2025)

On November 12, 2025 Pulte wrote on X that "we are actively evaluating [[portable-mortgages|portable mortgages]]," providing no further details, days after suggesting that [[fannie-mae|Fannie Mae]] and [[freddie-mac|Freddie Mac]] were "evaluating how to do assumable or portable mortgages, in a safe and sound manner."[6] Portable mortgages would let homeowners transfer existing rates, terms, and amortization schedules to a new residential property — an option unavailable in the United States but present in Canada, the United Kingdom, and parts of the European Union — while assumable mortgages allow a buyer to take over a seller's existing loan.[6] The remarks were reported as the country's top housing regulator signaling another tool to address America's affordability challenges, alongside Fannie Mae's [[fannie-mae-minimum-credit-score-elimination|elimination of its 620 minimum credit score]].[6]
