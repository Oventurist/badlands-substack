# Shin Bet

The Shin Bet is Israel's domestic security force.[1]

Its former head, [[ami-ayalon|Ami Ayalon]], said in a January 2024 interview that Israel would only achieve peace and security if Palestinians were given hope for the future, and advocated a two-state solution — an assessment cited in Badlands Media commentary criticizing the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]]'s renewed bombardment of northern [[gaza|Gaza]] in April 2024.[1]

## Attempted dismissal of Ronen Bar (March 2025)

In March 2025 the agency was at the center of a constitutional confrontation when Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] moved to dismiss its chief, [[ronen-bar|Ronen Bar]].[2] Attorney General [[gali-baharav-miara|Gali Baharav-Miara]] told Netanyahu he could not proceed unless a "factual and legal basis" for the removal was established, and on Monday, March 17 he accused her of abusing her authority.[2] Israeli press reported that both offices were examining whether approval from a civil service vetting commission was required first, which could delay the move by weeks.[2]

Badlands commentary reported that the opposition intended to argue Netanyahu had a conflict of interest, since Bar had been investigating the prime minister's inner circle and the events surrounding October 7, including potential connections to [[qatar|Qatar]].[2] Netanyahu separately filed a police complaint against former Shin Bet director [[nadav-argaman|Nadav Argaman]], accusing him of blackmail.[2]

## Role in the March 2025 Gaza strikes

The Shin Bet and the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] issued a joint statement announcing that they had begun attacking numerous [[hamas|Hamas]] targets across the [[gaza|Gaza]] Strip in strikes that started around 2 a.m. local time, resuming the war after a two-month ceasefire.[2]

## October 7 failures probe and the Qatar dispute (March 2025)

In early March 2025 the Shin Bet published a probe into the failures leading up to Hamas's October 7, 2023 onslaught, concluding among other findings that the attack would have been prevented had the agency acted differently.[3] The report pointed, among many factors, to Israel's yearslong policy of facilitating payments by [[qatar|Qatar]] to Hamas-ruled Gaza, and cited that money as having contributed to Hamas's force buildup.[3] Doha responded on Wednesday, March 5, 2025, by lambasting the probe, insisting that "no aid has ever been delivered to Hamas's political or military wing" and accusing the agency of "scapegoating Qatar."[3] The Gulf state, with which Israel has no diplomatic relations, has long hosted some of Hamas's political leadership.[3]

Separately, Haaretz reported that Shin Bet chief [[ronen-bar|Ronen Bar]] had warned Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] in late May 2023 that a war in Gaza was inevitable, a disclosure that surfaced amid an escalating war of words and strategic leaks between the premier and the agency's head.[3] Badlands commentary described the Shin Bet as Israel's counter-terrorism agency and noted that leaked assessments from Aman, Israeli military intelligence, had reached similar conclusions.[3]

## Warnings over settler violence (August 2024)

The Shin Bet alerted the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] on the evening of August 15, 2024 that a group of Israelis travelling in vehicles was heading to the Yitzhar area to commit a "nationalistic crime," a warning that preceded the settler rampage in the West Bank village of Jit.[4] Badlands Media reported that the head of the Shin Bet, alongside Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]], subsequently criticized National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] for encouraging such attacks and for failing to deploy police to stop them or arrest those responsible.[4]
