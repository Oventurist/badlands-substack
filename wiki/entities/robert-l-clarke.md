# Robert L. Clarke

Robert L. Clarke was the man [[ronald-reagan|President Ronald Reagan]] appointed as Comptroller of the Currency — in effect the government official in charge of all the banks — and, in the account of Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]], a [[george-hw-bush|Bush]] colleague installed to look the other way.[1]

Journalist Jonathan Kwitny reported that Clarke had once helped organise a bank that laundered thrift money into an overseas account.[1] Lange argues that the [[office-of-the-comptroller-of-the-currency|Office of the Comptroller of the Currency]], a supposedly independent bureau chartering and regulating all national banks and thrift institutions, completely failed at its job during the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan crisis]] — and that the failure was by design.[1] With [[deregulation|deregulation]] having stripped away the safeguards in place since the Great Depression, the Comptroller was the one official who could have blown the whistle early and prevented the scandal from becoming a major crisis; instead, Lange writes, Clarke was placed there to turn a blind eye and then manage the taxpayer bailout.[1]
