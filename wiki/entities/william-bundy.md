# William Bundy

**William Bundy** was an American intelligence and foreign policy official named in the Badlands Media essay "The Ivy Dominion" as a [[skull-and-bones|Skull & Bones]] member who "helped run psychological operations during the Vietnam War."[1]

He is cited alongside [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] to support the article's contention that Yale's Skull & Bones is "not a club" but "a covert network," a direct CIA recruitment ground whose members seeded leadership in the CIA, the [[state-department|State Department]] and intelligence-adjacent financial institutions.[1] His brother [[mcgeorge-bundy|McGeorge Bundy]] is likewise cited for the transition from National Security Advisor to the presidency of the [[ford-foundation|Ford Foundation]], illustrating the alleged circulation of Bonesmen through the [[ivy-dominion|Ivy Dominion]]'s intelligence and philanthropic arms.[1]

## Bones lineage and later career

"The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 8" identifies William Bundy as a member of [[skull-and-bones|Skull and Bones]] in the Yale class of 1939, and as a son of [[hollister-bundy|Hollister Bundy]], Secretary of War [[henry-stimson|Henry Stimson]]'s special assistant and Pentagon point man for the [[manhattan-project|Manhattan Project]].[2] With his brother [[mcgeorge-bundy|McGeorge Bundy]] he exercised significant impact on the flow of information and intelligence during the Vietnam War from positions in the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] and the [[state-department|State Department]], and as Special Assistant to Presidents Kennedy and Johnson.[2] He later became editor of *Foreign Affairs*, the influential quarterly of the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]].[2] [[antony-sutton|Antony Sutton]] treated the Whitney–Stimson–Bundy succession as the model of the Order's "vertical chain" of influence.[2]
