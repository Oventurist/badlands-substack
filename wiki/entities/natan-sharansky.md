# Natan Sharansky

Natan Sharansky is a former Soviet dissident and Israeli politician who founded the [[yisrael-baaliyah|Yisrael BaAliyah]] party to represent Russian-speaking immigrants in Israel.[1]

## Yisrael BaAliyah and the 1996 elections

In 1995, while still a student at Oxford, the future Israeli strategic affairs minister [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] conducted all of the polling and formulated the campaign strategy for Sharansky's Yisrael BaAliyah party ahead of the 1996 [[knesset|Knesset]] elections.[1] The party had been created to represent the interests of Russian immigrants in Israel — in particular the community known as "Refuseniks," an unofficial term for Soviet Jews — and was later integrated into [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s [[likud|Likud]] party.[1] Badlands commentary linked the origins of that constituency to the [[pale-of-settlement|Pale of Settlement]], and through it drew a line from Dermer to Ukraine, Russia and Israel.[1]

## The Case for Democracy

Sharansky and Dermer co-authored *The Case for Democracy* (2004), a book widely reported to be a favorite of President George W. Bush.[1] Badlands commentary cited the book, along with Politico reporting on Dermer's role as a "key Mideast go-between," as evidence of the pair's standing among Bush-era neoconservatives.[1]
