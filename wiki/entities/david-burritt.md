# David Burritt

**David Burritt** is the chief executive officer of [[us-steel|U.S. Steel]], the American steelmaker founded as a holding company in 1903.[1] He became a focus of controversy in early 2025 over his pursuit of a $14.9 billion sale of the company to Tokyo-based [[nippon-steel|Nippon Steel]], a transaction that collapsed after it failed to secure U.S. regulatory approval.[1]

## Criticism over the Nippon Steel deal

Activist investor [[ancora-holdings-group|Ancora Holdings Group]], a significant U.S. Steel stakeholder, accused Burritt in a February 10, 2025 letter to the board of leading the company "off a cliff" in pursuit of a $72 million transaction-related payout attached to the sale, and called for a major leadership shake-up.[1]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the disclosed payout revealed the deal's true motive, writing that the company's modern leadership were "every bit the vultures as its founders, seeking to further exploit the American steel worker by selling off the final pieces of our steel industry to a foreign corporation."[1] He summarized the episode as "selling out America for a pay day," adding sardonically that "David Burritt would probably make a great politician."[1]

## Context

U.S. Steel was assembled in 1903 by [[jp-morgan|J.P. Morgan]] as a vehicle to buy up steel businesses across the United States — most of them then located in Trenton, New Jersey — before consolidating operations in Pittsburgh, which is how that city earned the nickname "The Steel City."[1] Badlands commentary noted that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] blocked the Nippon sale in January 2025, in this reading giving President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] time to take office and negotiate an alternative structure, and that "a foreign investment in America's manufacturing core is much more promising than a buyout."[1]
