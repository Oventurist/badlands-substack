# Christopher Luxon

Christopher Luxon is a New Zealand conservative politician who was elected the country's prime minister following the general election held on Saturday, 14 October 2023.[1] His victory was reported as the installation of New Zealand's most conservative government in decades, ending the run of the Labour Party that had previously been led by [[jacinda-ardern|Jacinda Ardern]].[1]

## 2023 election

Luxon led the centre-right National Party, which was projected to take roughly 50 seats in the 121-seat New Zealand parliament.[1] Because that total fell short of a majority, National was expected to form a coalition government with one or two minor parties, most likely the ACT party with its 11 seats, producing a combined 61 seats and a slim governing majority.[1] Analysts cited by Goldman noted the results were largely in line with pre-election polling, and that the incumbent Labour Party was on track to lose its outright parliamentary majority for the first time since 2017.[1]

Addressing supporters at an event in Auckland alongside his wife Amanda and their children, Luxon told the crowd, "You have reached for hope and you have voted for change," to what was described as rapturous applause.[1] Outgoing Prime Minister [[chris-hipkins|Chris Hipkins]], who had held the office for nine months after Ardern's abrupt resignation, said late that Saturday that he had telephoned Luxon to concede.[1]

## Commentary

Coverage aggregated by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] framed the result as a repudiation of pandemic-era governance in New Zealand, pointing to vaccine mandates, government designation of state news as the sole authoritative "truth," and the arrest of peaceful protesters opposed to pandemic authoritarianism as factors in the incumbent party's defeat.[1]
