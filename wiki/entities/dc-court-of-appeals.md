# D.C. Court of Appeals

The District of Columbia Court of Appeals is the highest court of the District of Columbia and the body responsible for admitting attorneys to, and disciplining members of, the D.C. Bar. Its disciplinary machinery includes the D.C. Board on Professional Responsibility and the D.C. Office of Disciplinary Counsel, which together investigate and recommend sanctions against attorneys convicted of crimes.[1]

## Suspension of Hunter Biden's law license

On Tuesday, June 25, 2024, the court suspended the law license of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] following his conviction earlier that month on three federal gun charges.[1] The court order stated that Biden was "suspended immediately from the practice of law" in the District, entered after consideration of the indictment and the jury verdict in his federal gun trial.[1] The appeals court further directed the D.C. Board on Professional Responsibility to "institute a formal proceeding to determine the nature of the offense and whether it involves moral turpitude."[1]

The action followed an order by the D.C. Office of Disciplinary Counsel earlier in June finding that Biden's offenses in the federal gun case amounted to a "serious crime," a designation that triggers automatic suspension under the counsel's rules.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the Badlands News Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that "turpitude" is a synonym for depravity and questioned whether a formal hearing was needed to establish depravity given that the Marco Polo report on the Biden laptop had been admitted in the trial.[1] She noted that sentencing had not been scheduled and that Biden had moved for a new trial on the theory that the federal court in Delaware lacked jurisdiction — a motion she argued should have been filed before trial commenced.[1] She also flagged that Biden was due back in court in California in September in the tax case brought by Special Counsel [[david-weiss|David Weiss]].[1]
