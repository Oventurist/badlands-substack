# Sound Money Defense League

The Sound Money Defense League is an advocacy organization campaigning to end state taxation of precious metals and to restore gold and silver to a monetary role in the United States.[1]

## Legislative activity

The league, together with Money Metals Exchange and in-state dealers and investors, backed Mississippi Senate Bill 2862, which made Mississippi the 43rd state to end sales taxes on gold and silver.[1] The bill, sponsored by Sen. Juan Barnett, passed the state senate 52-0 and the House of Representatives 115-0, with an effective date of July 1, 2023, building on a multi-year grassroots campaign waged by sound money activists.[1]

## Rationale

Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that taxing gold and silver discourages people from buying it and from protecting their savings against dollar devaluation, and notes that states do not tax other investments such as stocks and bonds.[1] More importantly, he writes, removing sales tax means the metals are treated like money rather than a commodity — a first step toward making them legal tender able to compete with the fiat Federal Reserve Note issued under the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] system.[1] Seven states had not removed the tax: New Mexico, Hawaii, Wisconsin, Kentucky, Maine, New Jersey and Vermont.[1] The league's work is presented as part of the broader movement toward a [[gold-standard|gold standard]].[1]
