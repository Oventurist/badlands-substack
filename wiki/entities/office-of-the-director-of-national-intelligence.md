# Office of the Director of National Intelligence

The Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) is the U.S. agency that oversees and coordinates the Intelligence Community. Under Director [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] in the second Trump administration, ODNI has issued statements alleging that prior Intelligence Community processes were influenced by politicized or flawed intelligence products in matters including the 2019 impeachment inquiry and earlier intelligence assessments.[1]

Those statements were cited alongside the July 2026 launch of the [[white-house-election-integrity-portal|White House Election Integrity Portal]], which the administration presented as evidence of a broader pattern of intelligence suppression surrounding election security.[1]

## Pulte appointment and part-time leadership debate

In June 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] appointed [[william-pulte|William J. Pulte]] Acting Director of National Intelligence while allowing him to retain his posts at the [[federal-housing-finance-agency|Federal Housing Finance Agency]] and as chairman of [[fannie-mae|Fannie Mae]] and [[freddie-mac|Freddie Mac]].[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that ODNI functions as a clearing house within a community of 18 intelligence agencies — including the Space Force element — and questioned how such a role could be performed part time.[2] She suggested that if [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]]'s tenure had genuinely modernized the community's people, process, and technology, oversight might become a lighter-weight function.[2]

## Puerto Rico voting equipment examination

Reuters reported on February 4, 2026 that ODNI, then led by [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], had coordinated with the [[fbi|FBI]] in the spring of 2025 to obtain and examine electronic voting machines and related data from [[puerto-rico|Puerto Rico]].[3] ODNI confirmed the action in a statement that was quoted only in part and never published in full, saying it "found extremely concerning cyber security and operational deployment practices that pose a significant risk to U.S. elections" and citing its "broad statutory authority to coordinate, integrate, and analyze intelligence related to election security."[3] The agency described the seizure of machines and data as "standard practice in forensic analysis" and attributed some vulnerabilities to the machines' use of cellular technology.[3]

Anonymous officials told Reuters the goal had been to investigate claims that [[venezuela|Venezuela]] hacked voting machines in the territory, though the probe found no clear evidence of Venezuelan interference.[3] Badlands sources disputed that framing, asserting the analysis did occur but was not about Venezuela — "it's likely about the Americans" — and characterized the Reuters piece as an attempt to discredit ongoing election investigations.[3]

## Biolab declassification (June 2026)

In June 2026 the office of Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] released a statement saying newly declassified evidence concerned a [[us-funded-global-biolab-program|U.S.-funded global network]] of more than 120 biological laboratories operating in more than 30 countries.[4] ODNI said the network included laboratories in [[ukraine|Ukraine]], that some facilities conducted research involving hazardous pathogens including instances it characterized as gain-of-function research, and that at least one U.S.-funded laboratory in Ukraine had been assessed as vulnerable to attack, seizure, or damage during the Russia-Ukraine war.[4] The statement said Gabbard had directed increased intelligence collection on overseas laboratories associated with the program in order to identify their locations, pathogens, and ongoing activities.[4]

## Rebuttal of the Gabbard whistleblower reporting

In February 2026, ODNI publicly rejected [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]] reporting alleging that a stalled classified whistleblower complaint concerning DNI Gabbard existed within a US intelligence agency.[5] In an official statement the office said it was not aware of any legitimate or substantiated complaint against Gabbard being improperly handled or suppressed, and described claims to the contrary as misleading.[5] ODNI stressed that classified whistleblower complaints are governed by strict statutory processes and that a complaint's failure to advance may reflect threshold determinations — jurisdictional defects, credibility issues, or the absence of a qualifying protected disclosure — rather than suppression.[5] The office noted that no complaint had been referred to an Inspector General, transmitted to [[congress|Congress]], or produced findings, charges, or disciplinary action, leaving the matter an uncorroborated media claim disputed by the intelligence community's top oversight office.[5] The episode is treated in full at [[tulsi-gabbard-whistleblower-complaint-allegation|the Tulsi Gabbard whistleblower complaint allegation]].[5]

## 2020 election declassification effort (July 2026)

ODNI is among the agencies from which a [[white-house|White House]] task force has been gathering documents related to the 2020 presidential election for declassification, alongside the [[cia|CIA]], the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]], the [[fbi|FBI]], and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]].[6] The task force, which operates in addition to ongoing Justice Department probes into the 2020 election, consists of *Just The News* journalist [[john-solomon|John Solomon]] and former [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration officials including former national security aide [[derek-harvey|Derek Harvey]], with lawyers reviewing gathered documents for months.[6] The initiative involves thousands of pages from US intelligence agencies, focused on ballot irregularities, potential voting machine issues, and possibly other topics, with members encouraged to limit redactions even where that means retaining officials' names.[6]

President Trump said acting DNI [[bill-pulte|Bill Pulte]] will serve for only "a month or two" but is authorized to "declassify almost everything."[6] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that "this ODNI thing could be a rug," or alternatively the catalyst for change, and tied the disclosure to hopes for accountability on both [[2020-election-fraud-claims|fake elections]] and the [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of government]].[6]

## COVID-19 origins declassification (2026)

ODNI released a press statement in June 2026 announcing the publication of communications and documents, not previously made public, concerning government assessments of COVID-19 origins and the role of former National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases Director [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]].[7] Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] said the release was the product of a yearlong declassification review that included testimony from multiple Intelligence Community whistleblowers.[7]

The agency alleged that Fauci influenced Intelligence Community assessments of COVID-19 origins and that the released correspondence contradicts his 2024 congressional testimony.[7] It also said it had collected whistleblower accounts alleging retaliation against analysts who challenged prevailing conclusions, and that examples were referred by Gabbard to the [[intelligence-community-inspector-general|Intelligence Community Inspector General]], with no outcome indicated.[7] See [[odni-covid-origins-declassification-2026|the 2026 ODNI COVID-19 origins declassification]] for a fuller account.[7]

## Criminal referrals over the 2019 impeachment (April 2026)

In April 2026 ODNI referred both the whistleblower behind the complaint tied to President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s 2019 impeachment and former Inspector General [[michael-atkinson|Michael Atkinson]] to the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] for potential criminal violations, according to materials reviewed that Wednesday.[8] The referrals cite possible federal law violations tied to 2019 briefings before the [[house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence|House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence]], and follow document releases by DNI [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] describing a coordinated effort within the intelligence community to manufacture a conspiracy used to support the impeachment.[8] Newly disclosed transcripts of Atkinson's testimony show that he found indications of political bias from the whistleblower but still advanced the complaint as an "urgent concern"; ODNI stated that he failed to conduct basic due diligence and exceeded his authority.[8]

## Gabbard resignation and acting leadership (2026)

Fox News reported in May 2026 that Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] was resigning, having notified President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during an Oval Office meeting, with her last day at ODNI expected to be June 30, 2026.[9] Her resignation letter was reported to cite her husband Abraham's diagnosis with an extremely rare form of bone cancer.[9] Trump announced on Truth Social that principal deputy director of national intelligence [[aaron-lukas|Aaron Lukas]] would serve as acting DNI.[9] Badlands commentary framed the transition as a critical juncture for the office's declassification agenda, expressing hope that Gabbard would release outstanding files, including material relating to the 2020 election, before departing.[9]


## Release of the 2025 Tren de Aragua memo

In May 2025 the ODNI publicly released an April memo from the [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] assessing links between Venezuelan officials and the [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] gang.[10] The released document concluded that the [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] regime probably is not systematically directing Venezuelan migration outflows, softening an earlier [[fbi|FBI]] assessment.[10]


## 2023 report on commercially available information

In late 2021, Director of National Intelligence [[avril-haines|Avril Haines]] tasked a panel of senior advisers with untangling a web of secretive business arrangements between commercial data brokers and members of the US intelligence community.[11] The panel informed her more than a year before publication that the government had been secretly amassing a "large amount" of "sensitive and intimate information" on American citizens, and the resulting declassified report described the size and scope of that effort soberly and at length.[11]

Reported by [[wired|Wired]] in June 2023, the disclosure drew condemnation from privacy advocates who called it a "nightmare scenario," with [[sean-vitka|Sean Vitka]] of [[demand-progress|Demand Progress]] accusing intelligence agencies of "flouting the law and buying information about Americans that Congress and the Supreme Court have made clear the government should not have."[11] The report described the use of "craven interpretations of aging laws" to bypass privacy rights as domestic surveillance limits eroded — a practice covered in more detail at [[government-purchase-of-commercially-available-data|government purchase of commercially available data]].[11]

## Former DNI on Fauci and gain-of-function research (2023)

In April 2023, a former Director of National Intelligence publicly stated that [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] had misled Congress and the American public regarding [[gain-of-function-research|gain-of-function research]] and the origins of COVID-19.[12] The acknowledgment, reported by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], was treated in independent media as a significant institutional concession, given that the office's prior posture had aligned with the official rejection of laboratory-origin hypotheses.[12] Commentary published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] listed the former DNI's admission alongside other disclosures then reshaping the information landscape.[12]

## COVID-19 origins declassification mandate (2023)

The [[covid-19-origin-act-of-2023|COVID-19 Origin Act of 2023]], signed on March 20, 2023, required the Office of the Director of National Intelligence to declassify information on any possible links between a laboratory in China and the origins of the Covid-19 pandemic.[13] Director [[avril-haines|Avril Haines]] was given 90 days to declassify all information concerning possible links between the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute of Virology]] and the pandemic's origins.[13] [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] said in signing that his administration would share as much as possible consistent with protecting national security.[13] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] anticipated that any release would amount to a limited hangout.[13]

## SMART ePANTS announcement (2023)

On August 22, 2023, ODNI announced that the intelligence community's advanced research and development arm, [[iarpa|IARPA]], would develop its computerized clothing program — Smart Electrically Powered and Networked Textile Systems, or [[smart-epants|SMART ePANTS]] — over the following three and a half years.[16] ODNI publicized the effort as the first of its kind by the intelligence community to fashion smart clothing.[16] Badlands Media characterized the announcement as confirmation that the intelligence community had begun turning civilian clothing into another avenue for surveillance.[16]

## 2024 election security finding

On Friday, September 6, 2024, ODNI announced that it had not "observed" any foreign interference in the 2024 election.[14] The finding was published by its [[foreign-malign-influence-center|Foreign Malign Influence Center]] in a two-page report stating that the intelligence community "has not observed any foreign actor seeking to interfere in the conduct of the 2024 elections," while noting that Russia "poses the most active foreign influence threat" to the election.[14] The report was first reported by Natalie Winters of the Steve Bannon WarRoom podcast and was widely read as undercutting warnings about foreign influence issued days earlier by Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]], warnings critics said had been used to smear conservative pundits.[14]

## Report on commercially available information (2023)

A report produced under Director of National Intelligence [[avril-haines|Avril Haines]] documented federal agencies' purchase of vast quantities of Americans' personal data from private brokers, categorised as [[commercially-available-information|commercially available information]] (CAI).[15] The report warned that "CAI Increases the Power of the Government," noting that "the government would never have been permitted to compel billions of people to carry location tracking devices on their persons at all times, to log and track most of their social interactions, or to keep flawless records of all their reading habits."[15] Badlands Media cited the document as an official admission of the scale of contemporary [[mass-surveillance|mass surveillance]].[15]
