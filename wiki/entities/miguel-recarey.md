# Miguel Recarey

Miguel Recarey was a Cuban émigré and Medicare fraud suspect who built the Miami-based HMO [[international-medical-centers|International Medical Centers]] by flexing political muscle, contributing to both Democrats and Republicans and hiring well-connected lobbyists. He used to be invited to the White House under Reagan. From 1980 to 1987 the U.S. government gave him kid-glove treatment and almost $1 billion from Medicare.[1] At the time his was the largest Medicare fraud case in U.S. history.[1]

Among his supporters was [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]], the defeated Florida GOP gubernatorial candidate, who both did business with Recarey and intervened on his behalf when a federal agency was about to crack down on him. Federal law barred healthcare groups from drawing more than 50 percent of enrollees from Medicare — a cap intended to limit the scale of potential fraud — and Jeb Bush telephoned the agency to lobby for an exemption, which was granted.[1] Bush agreed to call urging "fair treatment" but denied seeking special favors; Recarey paid Bush's real-estate company $75,000, a fee some regarded as payment for the intervention, though Bush said it was for real-estate work and IMC never picked a building he showed. Bush later said he had been unaware Recarey already had an arrest record and had spent 30 days in jail in 1973 for income tax evasion.[1]

Recarey's HMO collapsed amid allegations of shoddy medical care, forged HMO enrollments, phony billings and bribery of union officials, leaving Florida to sort through $230 million in unpaid claims owed to the elderly, medical providers, trade firms, taxpayers and other creditors.[1]

He slipped out of the United States in 1987 and out of Venezuela in 1988, living luxuriously in Venezuela before marrying a Spanish woman in 1989 and becoming a Spanish citizen. He then hid in Spain, and although eventually tracked down and jailed in Madrid, a Spanish court freed him and refused extradition — protection the article attributes to a shadow government.[1]
