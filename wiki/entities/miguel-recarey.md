# Miguel Recarey

Miguel Recarey was a Cuban émigré who built the Miami-based HMO International Medical Centers (IMC) and became the subject of what was at the time the biggest Medicare fraud case in U.S. history.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) He had been invited to the Reagan White House.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

He built IMC by flexing political muscle, contributing to both Democrats and Republicans and hiring well-connected lobbyists. Among his supporters was [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]], who not only did business with Recarey but intervened on his behalf when a federal agency was about to crack down on him. From 1980 to 1987 the U.S. government gave Recarey kid-glove treatment and almost $1 billion from Medicare.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

The intervention concerned a rule barring healthcare groups from having more than 50 percent of enrollees as Medicare patients — a cap designed to limit the scale of potential fraud. Jeb Bush telephoned the federal agency to lobby for an exemption, which was granted.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) Recarey's company paid Bush's real estate firm $75,000, a fee some describe as payment for the intervention; Bush says it was for real estate work, and IMC never selected a building he showed. Bush has since said he was unaware Recarey already had an arrest record and had spent 30 days in jail in 1973 for income tax evasion.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

The HMO then collapsed amid allegations of shoddy medical care, forged HMO enrollments, phony billings and bribery of union officials, leaving the state of Florida to sort through $230 million in unpaid claims owed to the elderly, medical providers, trade firms, taxpayers and other creditors.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

Recarey slipped out of the United States in 1987 and out of Venezuela in 1988, living luxuriously in the latter; in 1989 he married a Spanish woman and became a Spanish citizen. A Spanish court eventually freed him from a Madrid jail cell and refused extradition — protection the article attributes to the shadow government, and it notes he is yet another Cuban exile with Bush ties receiving special treatment.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)
