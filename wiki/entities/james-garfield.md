# James Garfield

James Garfield was elected the twentieth President of the United States in 1880 after nine terms in the U.S. House of Representatives, and his presidency was cut short after 200 days when he was assassinated.[1]

As president, Garfield strengthened federal authority over the [[new-york-custom-house|New York Custom House]], the stronghold of Senator [[roscoe-conkling|Roscoe Conkling]], leader of the Stalwart Republicans and dispenser of patronage in New York.[1] Understanding how the New York patronage system worked, Garfield attempted to place Conkling's bitter rival in charge of the customs house in order to strip him of that power.[1] Conkling contested the nomination, tried to persuade the Senate to block it, and appealed to the Republican caucus to compel its withdrawal, but Garfield refused to submit, framing the fight as settling "whether the President is registering clerk of the Senate or the Executive of the United States" and whether the principal port of entry would be under the control of the administration or of a factional senator.[1]

Joe Lange argues that Garfield's assassination followed his attempt to cleanse the corruption at the customs house that supplied nearly two-thirds of federal revenue, and that this was not a coincidence given the Wall Street and [[citibank|Citibank]] interests involved.[1] Garfield was succeeded by Vice President [[chester-arthur|Chester Arthur]], a protégé of Conkling.[1]
