# National Security Council

The **National Security Council (NSC)** is the principal White House body coordinating national-security and foreign-policy matters for the U.S. president.[1]

## February 2023 aerial object briefings

During the February 2023 [[2023-north-american-aerial-object-shootdowns|shootdowns of high-altitude objects]] over Alaska, Canada, and Lake Huron, NSC spokesman [[john-kirby|Adm. John Kirby]] served as the administration's public voice, taking reporters' questions on February 10 after a fighter jet downed an object over Alaska.[1] Critics including [[nbc-news|NBC]]'s [[chuck-todd|Chuck Todd]] argued that the Council and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] had "missed opportunities for transparency" by withholding details until pressed by the press corps.[1] The episode fed criticism of the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration's information management from both mainstream and independent outlets.[1]

## Kirby email controversy (September 2024)

In September 2024 the NSC was at the center of a communications controversy when its communications adviser [[john-kirby|John Kirby]] replied to all on an email chain that unexpectedly included [[fox-news|Fox News]] Digital, writing that there was "obviously no use in responding" to "a 'handful' of vets indeed and all of one stripe" who had criticized the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration's 2021 [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] withdrawal.[2] The press inquiry had been forwarded to [[white-house|White House]] staffers on the National Security Council before Kirby's reply, which he later explained to a Fox reporter with, "Clearly, I didn't realize you were on the chain."[2]


## Waltz Purge of Detailees (January 2025)

Incoming National Security Adviser [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]] said in January 2025 that all intelligence officials detailed to the NSC under President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would be required to vacate by 12:01 p.m. on Inauguration Day, January 20, 2025, framing the move as removing "Deep Staters" from the council.[3] Under the NSC's structure, the National Security Adviser oversees a team of presidential political appointees who in turn supervise "detailees" — career officials seconded from agencies including the [[fbi|FBI]], the [[cia|CIA]], the NSA and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] — and Waltz said all detailees would be returned to their home agencies.[3]
