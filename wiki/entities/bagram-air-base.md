# Bagram Air Base

Bagram Air Base is a sprawling military airfield in Afghanistan that served as the principal hub of the United States military presence in the country until the American withdrawal of 2021, after which it passed into the hands of the [[taliban|Taliban]] government.[1] Four years after that withdrawal, the base returned to the center of international attention when U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly demanded that it be handed back to American control.[1]

## Trump's 2025 bid to retake the base

Speaking on a Saturday in September 2025, Trump renewed his call to reestablish a U.S. presence at Bagram, stating that "we're talking now to Afghanistan" about the matter without offering details of the purported conversations.[1] Asked by a reporter whether he would consider deploying U.S. troops to take the base, Trump demurred, saying only, "We won't talk about that. We want it back, and we want it back right away. If they don't do it, you're going to find out what I'm going to do."[1] He escalated the following day with a warning that if Afghanistan did not return the facility, unspecified "BAD THINGS ARE GOING TO HAPPEN!!!"[1] Trump had earlier lamented Washington's loss of the base, drawing particular attention to its proximity to China — a strategic argument for restoring an American foothold there.[1]

## Taliban rejection

The Taliban government rejected the bid outright. Chief Taliban spokesman Zabihullah Mujahid urged the United States to adopt a policy of "realism and rationality," invoking the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]] under which, he said, the United States pledged that "it will not use or threaten force against the territorial integrity or political independence of Afghanistan, nor interfere in its internal affairs."[1] Hamdullah Fitrat, deputy spokesman of the Taliban-run Afghan government, added that Kabul had made clear in all negotiations that the country's "independence and territorial integrity are of the utmost importance."[1] Both officials framed Trump's ultimatum as a violation of the 2020 withdrawal agreement.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for Badlands, [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] argued that Trump's original plan for the Afghanistan withdrawal, as described by [[christopher-miller|Christopher Miller]], had always included retaining forces at Bagram for counterterrorism purposes, and that a full withdrawal "was a ruse."[1] On that reading, an eventual American return to Bagram would simply implement the plan drawn up during Trump's first term.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the public confrontation as staged theater — "WrestleMania kayfabe" — and speculated that Taliban officials might one day appear at the [[white-house|White House]] for a photo opportunity.[1]
