# Gerald Brown

Dr. Gerald Brown is the author of a paper titled *Cooperative Federalism*, quoted at length in Badlands Media legal commentary on the constitutional status of the American states.[1]

## Cited argument

Brown's paper is described as "fascinating" by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], who quotes its contention that the [[declaration-of-independence|Declaration of Independence]] "does not portend to create a single nation," since a single entity would have required a singular proper name — "The State of New Britain," "The State of America," or similar — whereas the actual name of the political arrangement is plural.[1] Brown adds that the Declaration created no name at all for the alliance, which arrived only with the [[articles-of-confederation|Articles of Confederation]] two years later, and that the American states at the time of the Declaration were united in purpose against British rule while remaining "free and independent of each other as well."[1]

The passage the article emphasizes most is Brown's inference from the Declaration's reference to Great Britain as a state: "By referring to Great Britain as a state, this paragraph tells us that the Founding Fathers understood a state to have the same status as any nation on the world scene."[1] The Badlands author calls the logic "impeccable," arguing that the colonies and Great Britain cannot hold different status when the same word describes both — a distinction that would be unremarkable in ordinary speech but which he treats as binding within the quasi-formalized register described in [[natural-and-formal-languages|natural and formal languages]].[1]

Beyond this paper, the article supplies no biographical detail about Brown.[1]
