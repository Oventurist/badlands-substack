# Bart De Wever

Bart De Wever is the Prime Minister of Belgium and one of the European heads of government who confronted the United States over the Trump administration's pursuit of Greenland at the January 2026 annual meeting of the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in Davos.[1]

## Davos 2026

Speaking at the Davos meeting held under the theme "A Spirit of Dialogue," De Wever declared that Europe could not be a "miserable slave" to U.S. demands and insisted that red lines had been crossed by Washington's tariff pressure on [[denmark|Denmark]] and its supporters over the Arctic territory of [[greenland|Greenland]].[1] He was widely quoted as stating that, thanks to [[donald-trump|President Trump]], the United States was no longer an ally of Europe.[1]

De Wever framed the imbalance in classic terms, telling the forum: "...the Americans said, 'if you want to speak, speak softly, but carry a big stick.' We don't have a big stick. We can only speak softly."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated De Wever's remarks as an inadvertent admission of European strategic impotence, agreeing that the United States is not an ally of what he called the "inbred European elite" while arguing that Americans remain allies of the European people and should encourage them to reclaim national sovereignty.[1]

## Warnings over the Russian asset loan (October 2025)

At the informal [[european-union|EU]] summit in Copenhagen on October 1, 2025, De Wever — Belgium's prime minister, whose country hosts [[euroclear|Euroclear]] where most EU-held Russian assets sit — cautioned colleagues considering a €140 billion ($164 billion) loan to Ukraine backed by those assets that "there's no free money. There are always consequences."[2] He added, "I want their signature saying, if we take [Russian President Vladimir] Putin's money… we're all going to be responsible if it goes wrong."[2] Luxembourg's prime minister, Luc Frieden, voiced similar apprehensions.[2] The [[kremlin|Kremlin]] characterized the debate itself as the work of a criminal "gang."[2]
