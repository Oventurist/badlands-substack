# Sherri Ann Charleston

Sherri Ann Charleston is the chief diversity and inclusion officer of [[harvard-university|Harvard University]], who became the subject of a formal plagiarism complaint filed with the university in late January 2024.[1] The complaint, reported and independently analyzed by the *Washington Free Beacon*, alleges that Charleston plagiarized extensively across her academic work, lifting large portions of text without quotation marks and taking credit for research conducted by another scholar — her own husband.[1]

## Plagiarism complaint

The complaint makes 40 separate allegations of plagiarism spanning what the *Free Beacon* characterized as Charleston's thin publication record.[1] In her 2009 doctoral dissertation, submitted to the University of Michigan, Charleston is alleged to have quoted or paraphrased nearly a dozen scholars without proper attribution.[1]

The most serious allegation concerns her sole peer-reviewed journal article, a 2014 paper coauthored with her husband, [[lavar-charleston|LaVar Charleston]], in which the couple are said to have recycled much of a 2012 study previously published by LaVar Charleston alone, framing the older material as new research.[1] Through that arrangement, Sherri Ann Charleston effectively took credit for her husband's work.[1] The 2014 paper, which also listed Jerlando Jackson — later dean of Michigan State University's College of Education — as a coauthor and appeared in the *Journal of Negro Education*, shares the same methods, findings, and description of survey subjects as the 2012 study, which involved interviews with black computer science students and was first published by the *Journal of Diversity in Higher Education*.[1]

## Context and commentary

The complaint arrived in the wake of the plagiarism controversy surrounding former Harvard president [[claudine-gay|Claudine Gay]], and coverage framed it explicitly as evidence that the problem was "not just Claudine Gay."[1] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that the episode reflected the incentives of what he termed Neo-Marxist academic ideology, contending that an ideology which treats hard work as meaningless against pervasive structural oppression produces self-serving conduct among its adherents.[1] Esler drew on his own experience in Toronto, where he said academics from the University of Toronto helped lead counterprotests against the 1 Million March 4 Children demonstrations held across Canada in September 2023.[1]
