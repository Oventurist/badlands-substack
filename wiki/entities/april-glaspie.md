# April Glaspie

April Glaspie was the United States ambassador to [[iraq|Iraq]] during the administration of [[george-hw-bush|George H. W. Bush]], and is best known for a face-to-face meeting with [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]] held only weeks before the Iraqi invasion of [[kuwait|Kuwait]] on August 2, 1990.[1]

## The July 1990 meeting with Saddam Hussein

In what became a famous interview with the Iraqi leader, Glaspie told Saddam that "[W]e have no opinion on the Arab-Arab conflicts, like your border disagreement with Kuwait."[1] The [[state-department|U.S. State Department]] had earlier informed Saddam that Washington had "no special defense or security commitments to Kuwait."[1] As *Foreign Policy* summarized the episode, the United States may not have intended to give Iraq a green light, "but that is effectively what it did."[1]

Analysts examining the transcript have focused on whether Glaspie conveyed a clear deterrent threat, or whether she instead gave Saddam reason to believe he could absorb Kuwait without facing a strong American military response.[1] Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that there were no words of deterrence anywhere in this last-minute meeting, and that it functioned as a green light rather than a warning.[1]

## Acting on presidential instructions

According to the account of the meeting, Glaspie's first substantive point was to thank Saddam for the opportunity to discuss matters directly, after which she stated that "President Bush, too, wants friendship."[1] She told Saddam that "the President had instructed her to broaden and deepen our relations with Iraq," reminded him that although "some circles" might oppose that policy, "the U.S. administration is instructed by the President," and added that the president had "very recently reaffirmed his desire for a better relationship," a desire demonstrated by his opposition to certain sanctions bills.[1] When Saddam raised the Kuwait border dispute, Glaspie responded that she had served in Kuwait twenty years earlier and that "then as now, we took no position on these Arab affairs."[1]

Commentators concluded that Glaspie "didn't bungle her assignment" — she was doing what the Bush administration wanted at that crucial meeting, and was simply following the instructions she had been given.[1] Lange treats this as evidence that Glaspie was executing the policy of her superior, President George H. W. Bush, and that the administration was encouraging rather than deterring the invasion of Kuwait.[1]

## Significance

Within the Badlands framework, the Glaspie meeting is one element of a broader argument that the [[gulf-war|Gulf War]] was engineered as a distraction from the unfolding [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] banking scandal, in which the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and figures close to Bush were deeply implicated.[1]
