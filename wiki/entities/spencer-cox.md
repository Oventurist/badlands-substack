# Spencer Cox

Spencer Cox is the Republican Governor of Utah, cited in February 2026 reporting as part of an emerging Republican split over artificial intelligence policy.[1]

## AI policy

Cox has pushed for AI regulations focused on children and social harms while simultaneously supporting AI education and workforce training.[1] His position was contrasted with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s opposition to most AI regulation, which rests on the argument that US companies must be free to innovate in order to compete globally, particularly with China.[1] Cox was grouped with [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] and [[josh-hawley|Josh Hawley]] as Republicans diverging from the administration's deregulatory line ahead of 2028.[1]


## Scrutiny after the Kirk assassination (September 2025)

In the days after the assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] at Utah Valley University, Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] singled out the Utah governor for criticism, writing that people should "take another look at the Utah Governor" because "He's acting weird, and it's not just me saying that."[2] The comment accompanied her broader argument that the killing was a sophisticated operation rather than a lone-wolf attack and that the reported Discord confession by [[tyler-robinson|Tyler Robinson]] had emerged suspiciously soon after officials said the suspect was uncooperative.[2]

## Kirk assassination manhunt (2025)

As governor of Utah, Cox urged the public to help identify a person of interest in the [[assassination-of-charlie-kirk|assassination of Charlie Kirk]] at [[utah-valley-university|Utah Valley University]], as officials released new video of the suspect's escape.[3] The manhunt was conducted by the [[fbi|FBI]] together with the [[utah-department-of-public-safety|Utah Department of Public Safety]], which released additional pictures of the person of interest while the FBI offered a reward of up to $100,000.[3]


## Motive statements after Robinson's arrest (September 2025)

On Sunday, September 14, 2025, Cox said authorities still did not know the motive in the [[assassination-of-charlie-kirk|killing of Charlie Kirk]] but that interviews with people close to suspect [[tyler-robinson|Tyler Robinson]] had produced new information, including that Robinson held a "leftist ideology."[4] Speaking on multiple television networks, including NBC's "Meet the Press," Cox said Robinson had not confessed or cooperated with investigators since his arrest following a 33-hour manhunt.[4] He said relatives described Robinson's political views as "very different" from those of his conservative family, and that the 22-year-old — once a straight-A student — appeared to have been "radicalized" after dropping out of Utah State University, with friends confirming involvement in "that deep, dark internet, the Reddit culture, and these other dark places of the internet."[4]

Cox also said Robinson's partner, whom investigators believed had no prior knowledge of the shooting, "is transitioning from male to female."[4] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] accused the governor of leading what she called "investigative gaslighting," rejecting the official account of the suspect entirely.[4]


## Response to the Kirk assassination

Following the assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]], Utah Governor Cox framed the national reaction as a pivot point, asking: "Is this the end of a dark chapter in our history? Or the beginning of a darker chapter in our history?"[5] Commentators cited the question as capturing the stakes of the recriminations and fears of further violence that followed the killing.[5]
