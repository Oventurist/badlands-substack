# Center for Election Innovation & Research

The Center for Election Innovation & Research (CEIR) is an American election-administration nonprofit led by executive director [[david-becker|David Becker]]. In August 2025 its messaging on [[mail-in-voting|mail-in voting]] became the focus of criticism in the Badlands News Brief.[1]

## 2025 mail-in voting dispute

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported receiving a CEIR email quoting Becker's remarks to CBS News that getting rid of mail-in voting "would make our elections much less secure."[1] The quoted argument held that spreading voting over several days with options like mail-in voting allows additional time to recover from hiccups beyond election officials' control, such as power outages or disinformation, and that flexible voting options limit inconvenience and help prevent disenfranchisement on Election Day.[1]

Ashe treated the invocation of "disinformation" recovery time as an unexpected admission from what she called the election cabal.[1] Her central objection was that the single security control for vote-by-mail is signature verification — the control used to sell the practice to the public — while the same advocates were arguing in court, as in League of Women Voters of Kansas v. Schwab, that signature verification is a poll tax and suppressive and must be eliminated.[1] If that goal succeeded, she argued, mail ballots would carry zero security controls, because for these organizations access is prioritized over security.[1]

The commentary appeared alongside a countervailing column by [[john-r-lott-jr|John R. Lott Jr.]] arguing that mail-in ballots should be abolished outright.[1]
