# Texas Commerce Bank

Texas Commerce Bank was a Houston-based bank formed in 1964 by the merger of the National Bank of Commerce and [[texas-national-bank|Texas National Bank]].[1] In the Badlands account it functioned from its origins as a CIA institution — "the bank of choice for CIA-controlled drug cartels" — and sits at the junction between the [[bush-family|Bush family]], the Mexican narcotics trade and Latin American money laundering.[1]

## Bush and Zapata origins

A centenary vanity book commissioned in 1981 by bank president Ben Love describes the bank's cozy relationship with [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] and his [[zapata-offshore|Zapata Off-Shore Company]], which was linked to the CIA's 1961 [[bay-of-pigs-invasion|Bay of Pigs invasion]]. The book records that the bank's "major predecessor," Texas National Bank, played a significant role in financing the early development of Zapata Off-Shore, an 80-percent-owned subsidiary of Zapata Petroleum, with loans for drilling rigs and tending vessels making it one of the bank's most significant oil-related accounts of the late 1950s.[1]

The bank was founded by the family of [[james-baker|James Baker]], and its international division served as the customary placement for CIA non-official-cover officers — including [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]], hired at his father's request and posted to Caracas in 1977.[1]

## Board and ownership

By 1977 the board included Lady Bird Johnson and the recently defeated President Gerald Ford, who had appointed Bush to lead the CIA in 1976. In the 1980s [[kenneth-lay|Kenneth Lay]], founder of the CIA-connected [[enron|Enron]], joined the board. Howard Hughes's CIA-linked Summa Corporation used the bank to purchase properties on the Las Vegas strip. The bank was bought by Chemical Bank in 1987 and is now part of JPMorgan Chase.[1]

## Drug proceeds

Between December 1993 and February 1995 [[mario-ruiz-massieu|Mario Ruiz Massieu]] made an initial $40,000 deposit and a further 25 cash deposits ranging from $98,000 to $800,000 at the bank's Houston offices, money carried from Mexico in cardboard boxes and suitcases on commercial flights by his bagman Jorge Stergios. The U.S. Justice Department contended the roughly $9.9 million represented bribes paid by drug traffickers for immunity from prosecution; Ruiz Massieu called it family money or presidential "bonuses."[1] A federal judge and jury in Houston confiscated $9 million from the account in 1997 as drug proceeds.[1] The bank was separately found to have stashed $7 million in [[gulf-cartel|Gulf Cartel]] drug profits, yet faced no legal consequence — protection the article attributes to the shadow government.[1]
