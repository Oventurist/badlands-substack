# Texas Commerce Bank

Texas Commerce Bank was a Houston-based bank that emerged from the 1964 merger of the National Bank of Commerce and [[texas-national-bank]]. In the Badlands Media article [[a-family-affair]], it is characterized as a CIA-controlled financial institution from its inception, later used for drug money laundering by the salinas family and the [[gulf-cartel]].

## Origins and CIA connections

According to a centenary history commissioned by bank president Ben Love in 1981 and cited via the [[wayne-madsen-report]], Texas Commerce Bank had a "cozy relationship" with [[george-hw-bush]] and his [[zapata-offshore]] Company. The bank was linked to the CIA's abortive Bay of Pigs invasion of Cuba in 1961. Its board members included Lady Bird Johnson and recently defeated President Gerald Ford after Ford had chosen Bush to lead the CIA in 1976. In the 1980s, [[enron]] founder [[kenneth-lay]]—described as CIA-connected—became a board member, and Howard Hughes's summa corporation used Texas Commerce Bank to purchase properties on the Las Vegas Strip.

## Jeb Bush as non-official cover officer

The article claims that [[jeb-bush]] was hired by the bank's international division as a CIA non-official cover (NOC) officer through his father's relationship with [[james-baker]], whose family founded the bank. The source argues that Texas Commerce Bank was an "optimal cover for CIA activities" and that its international division was the usual branch where CIA NOCs were placed within banks and investment firms.

## Drug money from the Gulf Cartel

According to *The Guardian* and U.S. grand jury testimony, [[mario-ruiz-massieu]] deposited nearly $10 million in cash at the bank's Houston branch between December 1993 and February 1995, in 26 cash deposits ranging from $98,000 to $800,000. His bagman, [[jorge-stergios]], carried the money in cardboard boxes and suitcases on commercial flights from Mexico. U.S. prosecutors alleged the funds were bribes paid by drug traffickers in exchange for immunity from prosecution. The article notes that Texas Commerce Bank was later discovered to have stashed $7 million in drug profits for the Gulf Cartel but faced no legal consequences.

## Acquisition and legacy

Texas Commerce Bank was bought by Chemical Bank in 1987 and is now part of jpmorgan chase. The article frames this lineage as evidence that the Bush family's CIA banking network was absorbed into the modern financial system rather than dismantled.
