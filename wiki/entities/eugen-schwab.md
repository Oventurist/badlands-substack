# Eugen Schwab

Eugen Schwab (also rendered Eugene Schwab) was a German industrial manager and the father of [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]], founder of the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]].[1] His wartime career forms a central strand of Badlands Media's account of the Schwab family history.[1]

## Escher-Wyss and Nazi Germany

In the 1930s, in the years leading up to the German annexation of Poland, Eugen Schwab managed the [[escher-wyss|Escher-Wyss]] factory in Ravensburg and was the town's largest employer.[1] While he was at the helm, [[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]]'s Nazi party awarded the Escher-Wyss Ravensburg branch the title of "National Socialist Model Company."[1] Some observers believe this amounted to an attempt by the Nazis to woo the Swiss firm into cooperating in the coming war.[1]

Ravensburg occupied a peculiar position during the conflict. The presence of the Red Cross and a rumored agreement involving various companies, including Escher-Wyss, reportedly led allied forces to publicly agree not to target the town, which was never classified as a significant military target and consequently retains many of its original features.[1]

## Wartime production and forced labor

Eugen Schwab continued to manage the "National Socialist Model Company" throughout the war, during which the Swiss firm aided the Nazi Wehrmacht in producing significant weapons of war as well as more basic armaments.[1] Badlands Media reports that Escher-Wyss also had involvement in the German atomic bomb project.[1]

Schwab additionally put forced laborers to work at his model Nazi company.[1] Nearly 3,600 forced laborers worked in Ravensburg during World War II, including at Escher-Wyss; according to Ravensburg city archivist Andrea Schmuder, the Escher-Wyss machine factory there employed between 198 and 203 civil workers and prisoners of war during the war.[1] Historian Karl Schweizer states that "Escher-Wyss maintained a small, special camp for forced laborers on the factory premises."[1]
