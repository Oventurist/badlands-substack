# Mark Kelly

Mark Kelly is a US senator and retired naval officer who became the subject of Defense Department administrative action over his participation in what Badlands and allied outlets call the Seditious Six scandal.[1]

## Pentagon demotion effort

The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] sought to demote Kelly and reduce his military retirement benefits for his role in the [[seditious-six|Seditious Six]] episode.[1] In litigation before the US District Court for the District of Columbia, Judge [[richard-leon|Richard Leon]] questioned Justice Department lawyers in early February 2026 about the legal precedent for the action, pressing the government to identify Supreme Court or D.C. Circuit authority supporting punishment for off-duty speech.[1]


## Censure and retirement grade review (January 2026)

Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] formally censured Kelly and launched [[pentagon|Pentagon]] proceedings that could demote his retired rank and reduce his military pension, officials said.[2] The action followed a November 2025 video in which Kelly and five other lawmakers — the group dubbed the [[seditious-six|Seditious Six]] — urged U.S. service members to refuse what they described as "illegal orders."[2] Hegseth called Kelly's participation "reckless misconduct" and "seditious," placed a letter of censure in his permanent military file, and initiated a retirement grade review; Kelly had 30 days to respond and the Navy up to 45 days to complete its review.[2]

Kelly, a former astronaut and 25-year Navy veteran, called the move politically motivated and "un-American" and vowed to contest the actions, while supporters argued his statements fell within protected speech.[2] Separately, the New York Post reported that Kelly declined to label recent U.S. military strikes in Venezuela and the arrest of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] as "illegal" in a television interview, though he criticized aspects of the operation's planning.[2] The dispute fueled debate over military discipline, free speech, and the limits of Pentagon authority to discipline retired service members who are also elected officials.[2]

## Hegseth announcement of January 5, 2026

On January 5, 2026, Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] publicly announced administrative actions against Kelly, a retired Navy captain and former astronaut.[3] The actions comprised a formal letter of censure — a permanent reprimand placed in Kelly's military record — for alleged "seditious statements" and "reckless misconduct," together with the initiation of a 45-day retirement grade determination that could reduce Kelly's retired rank and corresponding retirement pay.[3]

Hegseth said the actions rested on a pattern of public statements Kelly made between June and December 2025 that, in the secretary's view, characterized lawful military operations as illegal, and described the November video released with five other members of Congress as "reckless and seditious" and "clearly intended to undermine good order and military discipline."[3] The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] stated that retired officers remain subject to the [[uniform-code-of-military-justice|Uniform Code of Military Justice]] and that Kelly's status as a sitting US senator does not exempt him.[3]

Kelly responded with outrage, citing "over twenty-five years in the U.S. Navy, thirty-nine combat missions, and four missions to space," and said he had risked his life to defend the Constitution "including the First Amendment rights of every American to speak out."[3]

## Initial Department of War investigation (November 2025)

On November 24, 2025, the Department of War announced that it had opened a formal investigation into "serious allegations of misconduct" involving Kelly, an Arizona Democrat and retired Navy captain, over claims that he violated the [[uniform-code-of-military-justice|Uniform Code of Military Justice]] by appearing in the video urging service members to refuse orders they judged illegal.[4] Reporting noted that because Kelly is a retired regular-component officer he remains subject to the UCMJ, and that under Title 10 U.S. Code § 688 the Secretary of the Navy, with the approval of the Secretary of War, may recall certain retirees to active duty, exposing them to disciplinary action up to court-martial.[4]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that Kelly appeared to be the only member of the [[seditious-six|Seditious Six]] with meaningful military legal exposure, and argued that the others remained accountable under federal statutes such as 18 U.S.C. § 2387, which prohibits conduct intended to interfere with the loyalty, morale, or discipline of the armed forces.[4]

## "Seditious Six" video (November 2025)

Kelly appeared alongside Senator [[elissa-slotkin|Elissa Slotkin]] and Representatives [[chris-deluzio|Chris Deluzio]], [[maggie-goodlander|Maggie Goodlander]], [[chrissy-houlahan|Chrissy Houlahan]] and [[jason-crow|Jason Crow]] in the November 18, 2025 [[seditious-six-video|"Seditious Six" video]], which told U.S. service members and intelligence personnel to refuse illegal orders from the administration.[5] The video prompted pushback from GOP lawmakers and administration officials, with Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] describing it as a "politically-motivated influence operation" and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] calling the six "traitors"; the participants reported receiving threats afterward.[5]

## Reaction to Trump's December 2025 primetime address

Kelly joined [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] and other Democrats in criticizing President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s primetime address of December 17, 2025, with the group roasting the near-15-minute speech as "angry, desperate and defensive" after Trump used it to attack [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and tout his administration's record.[6]
