# Barak Ravid

Barak Ravid is an Israeli journalist who covers diplomacy and national security, described by Badlands as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s preferred Israeli journalist.[1]

## Reporting on the June 2026 Lebanon operation

Ravid affirmed reporting that [[israel|Israel]] had likely invaded Lebanon in an attempt to seize control of the Strait, in coverage published hours after Trump posted comments to Truth Social about the situation.[1] His confirmation was cited by Badlands alongside Times of Israel reporting that the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] had conducted a military operation overnight in southern Lebanon around the city of Nabatieh.[1]

## Trump's Netanyahu remarks (June 2026)

Ravid reported that [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] told him during a phone call, after Israel struck Beirut on the day the U.S.–[[iran|Iran]] agreement was expected to be signed: "Why did Bibi have to do a fucking attack? I was so pissed off. I let him know. He has no fucking judgement. I let him know that."[2] According to Ravid, Trump added that the U.S.–Iran peace deal was still happening, though the incident delayed the planned signing by several hours.[2] Badlands treated the quote as evidence of a widening rupture between Trump and Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[2]

## Access relationship with Trump
Badlands describes Ravid, along with Mark Caputo, as maintaining a direct-line relationship with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], routinely conducting phone interviews in which Trump reveals exclusive information.[3] Ravid is characterized as the only journalist to whom Trump has openly trashed [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], which he did during a December 2021 call whose audio was recorded and published in Israel but went unreported in American mainstream media.[3] During that call Trump also heavily implied Netanyahu played a role in the 2020 stolen election, with subtext concerning the prerecorded video Netanyahu released congratulating Biden "before the ink was even dry."[3]
The dynamic is described as transactional — Trump grants scoops, Ravid relays them accurately, and violating that arrangement would cost him access — while leaving open the strong possibility that Trump uses Ravid to broadcast mis- or disinformation.[3] Ravid's [[axios|Axios]] reporting of the June 1, 2026 call in which Trump halted a planned Israeli strike on Beirut was analyzed on those terms.[3] See [[june-2026-trump-netanyahu-lebanon-call|the June 2026 Trump–Netanyahu Lebanon call]].[3]


## Reporting on the Houthi announcement (2025)

Following President Trump's May 2025 [[us-houthi-ceasefire-announcement-2025|announcement]] that the United States would stop bombing the [[houthis|Houthis]], Ravid reported on his X account that Trump did not appear fazed by the prospect that Israel and the Houthis might continue fighting one another.[4]


## December 2021 Trump interview

Ravid's December 2021 interview with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] resurfaced in October 2024 Badlands commentary. In a clip discussing the interview with colleagues, Ravid recounted Trump saying that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had backed out of the air strike that killed [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]] the day before it was scheduled, and that Netanyahu also tried to back out of the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] the day before a major call was scheduled to finalize them for public signing.[5] Trump was quoted as saying, "Israel did not do the right thing. People will be hearing about that, at the right time."[5]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used Ravid's account to argue a speculative thesis: that Soleimani may have effectively served Netanyahu's interests by building regional proxy networks whose war against Israel would justify an Israeli war on Iran, and that Netanyahu's reported reluctance about the strike is best explained by that relationship.[5]

## December 2021 Trump interview

Ravid interviewed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on December 21, 2021, an exchange in which Trump revealed his true feelings about [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas.[6] Trump disclosed that while president he had quietly backed former Israeli general [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] against Netanyahu in the April 2019 Israeli election, and that he had stopped Netanyahu from building Israeli settlements in the [[west-bank-annexation|West Bank]] in May 2020.[6]

Ravid later explained on air that Trump's blockage of those settlements is exactly what leveraged Netanyahu into agreeing to the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]].[6]

## July 2024 reporting on Netanyahu and Trump

In July 2024, Ravid reported for Axios that Netanyahu's years-long effort to mend relations with Trump may have shown progress in the wake of the assassination attempt against Trump, citing a Netanyahu aide who described at least four meetings between Netanyahu allies and Trump over three years — including one in which an ally read passages from Netanyahu's book praising Trump at Mar-a-Lago.[6] Badlands commentary treated Ravid's byline as significant, noting that he was the same journalist who had drawn out Trump's candid 2021 remarks.[6]

## Reporting on the Trump–Netanyahu relationship (February 2025)

Axios correspondent Ravid reported in February 2025 on the strains beneath the public relationship between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], writing that Trump had realized at a relatively early stage that Netanyahu was dragging his feet on peace with the Palestinians but kept most of his bitterness private because the public "bromance" was good politics.[8] Ravid recounted that the relationship suffered a severe blow when Netanyahu congratulated Joe Biden on winning the 2020 election — "F-ck him," Trump told him of Netanyahu three months after leaving office — and that the two did not speak for almost four years, with Trump also criticizing Netanyahu after the October 7 attack before hosting him at Mar-a-Lago in summer 2024.[8] A U.S. source close to Trump told Ravid that although relations had improved, "Trump still does not like Bibi, does not trust Bibi."[8]

Ravid's reporting also documented the shock inside Washington at Trump's declaration that the United States could "take over" Gaza, noting that very few on Trump's team knew in advance and that Israeli far-right figures praised the displacement plan as a "green light" for full occupation and settlement building, which a source close to Trump said was not his intention.[8] He reported Saudi displeasure at Trump's claim that the kingdom would not condition normalization with Israel on Palestinian statehood, quoting a U.S. source that "the Saudi crown prince doesn't want to be seen as being part of what Trump is talking about," and another describing Trump as "a disruptor" who "wanted to challenge the discourse."[8]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that Ravid is "not just Trump's go-to Israeli journalist, but he's also former Israeli Military Intelligence," and treated his output as part of an active psywar battlefield.[8]

## Reporting on a US–Saudi security agreement (2024)

In November 2024, Ravid reported for Axios that the United States and Saudi Arabia were discussing a possible security agreement that would not involve a broader deal with Israel — a step back from the full defense treaty previously under negotiation, but one that Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and the White House hoped to conclude before [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] left office in January 2025.[9] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] regularly uses Ravid for interviews and to deploy his own narratives inside Israel, and treated the report as a narrative that had been "cooking for quite some time" without thawing.[9]
