# House Appropriations Committee

The House Appropriations Committee is the panel of the U.S. House of Representatives responsible for setting federal discretionary spending levels, including the annual defense budget.[1]

## June 2025 Ukraine aid testimony

On Tuesday, June 10, 2025, Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] appeared before the committee and disclosed that the administration would be slashing military funding for Ukraine, telling members, "It is a reduction in this budget," and adding that "this administration takes a very different view of that conflict."[1] Hegseth framed the cut as consistent with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s pursuit of a negotiated settlement, saying "a negotiated peaceful settlement is in the best interest of both parties and our nation's interests especially with all the competing interests around the globe."[1]

The testimony placed the committee at the center of the administration's "America First" pivot, under which foreign assistance has been significantly cut in favor of domestic spending.[1] Badlands commentary treated the announcement as another anticipated "rug pull" that legacy commentators had failed to foresee.[1]

## Renewal of NED funding (2025)

In August 2025 Badlands Media reported that Republicans on the House Appropriations Committee had voted out of session to renew full funding for the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]], an organization the outlet describes as deeply involved in undermining both Trump administrations.[2] The relevant vehicle was the National Security, Department of State, and Related Programs Appropriations Bill for fiscal year 2026, under which the House GOP proposed $315 million for the endowment despite President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s request that it receive nothing.[2] Rep. [[eli-crane|Eli Crane]] subsequently introduced an amendment to defund the NED, highlighting a split between traditional Republicans and Trump-aligned populists on the committee.[2]
