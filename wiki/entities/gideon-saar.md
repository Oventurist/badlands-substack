# Gideon Saar

Gideon Saar is the Foreign Minister of [[israel|Israel]].[1]

Amid the March 2026 war with [[iran|Iran]], Saar said that only the Iranian people can ultimately bring down their own government, while adding that outside assistance may be necessary to achieve it.[1] His remark accompanied Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s statement that he did not know whether Iranians would overthrow their leadership, and came as the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] reported striking dozens of targets in Tehran.[1]

## March 2026 remarks on regime change

In the Badlands Brief of March 13, 2026, Saar's position was restated as the war with Iran escalated: only the Iranian people can ultimately bring down their government, but outside assistance may be necessary.[2] Badlands commentary read the statement — alongside Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s admission that he did not know whether Iranians would revolt — as a public confession that the Iranian population had neither mobilized nor sought to overthrow its leadership, implying that regime change would require either renewed Israeli mobilization or American ground forces.[2]

## April 2026 statue desecration response

In April 2026, Sa'ar publicly confirmed that a widely circulated image showing an Israeli soldier smashing a statue of Jesus in the southern Lebanese village of Debl was authentic, describing the [[debel-statue-desecration|act]] as "grave and disgraceful" and saying he supported the military's investigation and disciplinary process.[3] Badlands commentary noted that the Israeli government routinely issues such condemnations when acts of terrorism are committed by the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] or the [[israeli-settler-movement|Israeli Settler Movement]], while those responsible face no consequences.[3]

## December 2024 reversal on Syria's new rulers

Weeks after Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had hailed [[abu-mohammad-al-jolani|al-Jolani]] as the liberator of Syria, Sa'ar publicly reversed course, calling the new leader's outfit a "gang" and describing his government as illegitimate.[4] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] paired the statement with the [[state-department|State Department]]'s simultaneous release of memos unmasking al-Jolani's Al-Qaeda and ISIS background, reading both as a sudden turn by Western and Israeli officials against jihadis they had backed to take Damascus — a turn he attributed to those same fighters signalling a desire for friendly relations with Moscow.[4]

## World Jewish Congress conference (May 2025)

Sa'ar attended a [[world-jewish-congress|World Jewish Congress]] meeting in Jerusalem in May 2025, where WJC president [[ronald-lauder|Ronald Lauder]] directed criticism of Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] to him, saying Smotrich's statements about starving and destroying Gaza were destroying Israel's reputation.[5] Lauder's remarks drew applause from many in the audience; Sa'ar responded simply that he "took note" of what had been said.[5]

## Appointment as Foreign Minister (November 2024)

On November 5, 2024, Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] fired Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] and named Foreign Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] to replace him, with Sa'ar — then a minister without portfolio — moving up to become foreign minister.[6] Sa'ar had been a political dissident of Netanyahu before being brought into the newly formed war cabinet alongside Netanyahu and Gallant, a step reportedly taken after Sa'ar publicly said that any entity attacking Israel should be erased from existence.[6]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Sa'ar had widely been expected to receive the defense portfolio instead of Katz, and argued that he would have been more favorable among [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] generals because he would have been more willing to enforce the Israeli Supreme Court's decision nullifying the draft exemption long enjoyed by the [[haredi-judaism|Haredi]].[6]
