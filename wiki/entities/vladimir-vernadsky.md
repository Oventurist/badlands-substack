# Vladimir Vernadsky

Vladimir Vernadsky (1863–1945) was a Russian academician described by Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as brilliant, and as the originator of a conception of the [[noosphere|Noosphere]] fundamentally distinct from that of the Jesuit [[pierre-teilhard-de-chardin|Pierre Teilhard de Chardin]].[1]

Ehret writes that Vernadsky was locked into his own parallel fight against the mechanists who were attempting to crush the soul of science in Russia, and that his work stands in contrast to the neo-Darwinian and [[eugenics|eugenical]] framework Chardin attached to the same term.[1]

## The noosphere and climate science

In his June 5, 2025 essay on climate, Ehret returned to Vernadsky as the authority for an open-system science of nature, noting that when humanity appeared a new phenomenon began expressing itself which Vernadsky described as the [[noosphere]], as distinct from the lithosphere and biosphere.[2] Vernadsky understood this new geological force to be driven by human creative reason and devoted his life to teaching that the law of humanity must accord with the law of nature, writing that "the noösphere is a new geological phenomenon on our planet. In it, for the first time, man becomes a large-scale geological force. He can, and must, rebuild the province of his life by his work and thought."[2]

Neither the noosphere nor the biosphere, in Vernadsky's view as summarized by Ehret, obeyed a law of mathematical equilibrium or stasis; both were governed by an asymmetrical harmony and progress from lower to higher states of organization, so that only by understanding the principles of nature does mankind become morally and intellectually fit to improve upon it — turning deserts green, harnessing the atom, and applying scientific progress to health and agriculture.[2] Ehret lists among his most important works Scientific Thought as a Planetary Phenomena (1938), Evolution of Species and Living Matter (1928), Some Words About the Noosphere (1943), and The Transition of the Biosphere to the Noosphere (1938), and laments that despite these contributions the world tolerates an unscientific policy of [[mass-decarbonization|mass decarbonization]].[2]
