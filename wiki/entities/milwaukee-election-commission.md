# Milwaukee Election Commission

The Milwaukee Election Commission is the municipal body responsible for administering elections in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, and operated the city's central count facility during the 2020 general election.[1]

Its former Executive Director, [[claire-woodall|Claire Woodall]], is reportedly a subject of examination in the [[fbi|FBI]]'s investigation into Wisconsin's 2020 election over an incident in which she briefly left a flash drive inside a voting machine.[1] Federal agents have also spoken with [[wisconsin-elections-commission|Wisconsin Elections Commission]] Deputy Administrator Robert Kehoe, attempted to question a top Milwaukee County election official at her home, and contacted at least two other individuals involved in administering Milwaukee's 2020 election.[1] Milwaukee's 2020 ballots still exist despite standard retention timelines, partly because of ongoing litigation brought by a New London man who sued state and local election officials over issues tied to that election.[1]

## 2026 federal inquiry

In May 2026 the commission's 2020 conduct returned to prominence when Townhall reported that the [[fbi|FBI]] was preparing to interview Milwaukee police officers about election-night ballot processing at the city's [[milwaukee-central-count-facility|Central Count facility]].[2] The reporting revisited a 2020 incident involving former commission director [[claire-woodall-vogg|Claire Woodall-Vogg]] and a flash drive containing absentee ballot tabulation data, which Woodall-Vogg said had been left at Central Count before being retrieved and transported by police.[2] It also cited a sworn affidavit attributed to Milwaukee County Election Commission employee [[david-j-bolter|David J. Bolter]] describing a November 4, 2020 announcement about the arrival of additional ballots.[2] Milwaukee County Executive [[david-crowley|David Crowley]] and Mayor [[cavalier-johnson|Cavalier Johnson]] denounced the inquiry as "an act of intimidation."[2]

## Leadership change ahead of the 2024 election

On Monday, May 6, 2024, Milwaukee Mayor [[cavalier-johnson|Cavalier Johnson]] announced that he would replace the commission's executive director, [[claire-woodall|Claire Woodall]], with her deputy, [[paulina-gutierrez|Paulina Gutierrez]], in a move the Associated Press described as a surprise.[3] The change came six months before Wisconsin's largest city would again draw national attention in a presidential swing state where four of the past six presidential contests were decided by less than a percentage point.[3] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary connected the shake-up to reporting on Wisconsin's "Vote at Home" practices and to resurfaced emails in which state election figures joked about margins for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[3]
