# North Sea Transition Authority

The North Sea Transition Authority (NSTA) is the United Kingdom regulator responsible for oil, gas and offshore energy activity in the North Sea, and its production and well-count data serve as the principal statistical basis for British debates over domestic energy security.[1]

## Role in the 2023 energy security debate

In October 2023, UK Energy Secretary [[claire-coutinho|Claire Coutinho]] cited new NSTA data at the annual Energy UK conference showing that the number of offshore UK oil and gas wells had fallen by 83 in 2022, leaving a total of 1,629 wells compared with 2,052 in 2019.[1] Extrapolating from the same dataset, Coutinho stated that UK production had fallen to a record low of 38 million tonnes in 2022 and would decline to 22 million tonnes by 2030 if no new drilling were permitted.[1]

Those figures underpinned the government's argument that North Sea output would halve by 2030 and that Britain could be forced to import as much as 80 percent of its oil and gas, leaving it "subservient to foreign regimes."[1] The same data framed the political contest between the governing Conservatives — who under [[rishi-sunak|Rishi Sunak]] approved [[equinor|Equinor]]'s Rosebank field in late September 2023 — and the [[labour-party|Labour Party]], which pledged to stop new offshore drilling in order to meet 2050 climate targets.[1]
