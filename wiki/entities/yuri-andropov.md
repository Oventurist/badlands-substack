# Yuri Andropov

Yuri V. Andropov was the chief of the KGB at the time of the [[soviet-union|Soviet Union]]'s December 1979 intervention in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]], and later Soviet leader.[1]

## The December 1979 memo

In a handwritten memo to Soviet leader [[leonid-brezhnev|Leonid Brezhnev]] in December 1979, Andropov wrote that Soviet intelligence had been receiving information about [[hafizullah-amin|Hafizullah Amin]]'s behind-the-scenes activities, which might indicate his political reorientation to the West, adding that Amin "keeps his contacts with the American chargé d'affaires secret from us."[1]

The memo became public in 1995 when Anatoly F. Dobrynin, the longtime Soviet ambassador to the United States, went to the Russian archives and transcribed documents for a project by the Norwegian Nobel Institute. At a meeting on December 8, 1979 — also transcribed by Dobrynin — Andropov and defense minister Dmitri F. Ustinov cited the dangers of American missiles being deployed in Afghanistan.[1]

Archivist Thomas Blanton is quoted describing the picture Andropov painted in early December: if Amin "did a flip," it would totally change the geopolitical balance in South Asia — an outcome he compared to Mexico becoming a base for Soviet short-range missiles.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands coverage

Badlands Media presents the Andropov memo as declassified evidence that the Soviets knew Amin was meeting secretly with the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and regarded their southern border as threatened, and that the Soviet Union therefore sent special forces into Kabul to kill Amin and seize the city as it invaded. The outlet argues that the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] reported this dynamic while stopping short of naming the CIA.[1]
