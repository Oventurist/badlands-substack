# William Johnson

William Johnson was an Irishman who moved to New York in 1738 and became the central intermediary between the British and the Six Nations, featured prominently in [[benjamin-west|Benjamin West]]'s 1770 painting *The Death of General Wolfe*.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Relations with the Six Nations

Johnson did not play by the typical stuffy British customs or rules of behaviour, instead organising treaties and working directly with the natives — the Six Nations, the Mohawks and the Iroquois — respecting their customs and languages and building sincere relationships with tribes that had been manipulated by the [[jesuits|Jesuits]] for generations.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Where other British soldiers and officers treated native populations as sub-human savages, Johnson integrated culturally with them and learned several native dialects.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) By 1742 he had been made a Mohawk civil chief, and he was made colonel of the Six Nation Mohawks; the Mohawks themselves requested that he serve as the official British representative to them because they could work with no one else.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Strategic significance

The article argues that if the Mohawks or Iroquois had allied with anyone during the war it would in all probability have been the Jesuit-run French, who controlled the French territories of the Americas and had built extensive native alliances while doing immense damage by turning groups against each other and against colonists.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Largely through Johnson's efforts the republican intelligentsia gained a creative hold over their French adversaries and built respectful relations with native groups, and when the Mohawks and Iroquois chose to fight alongside the English at the [[battle-of-quebec|Battle of Quebec]] the scales of the battle were tipped entirely.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

West depicts Johnson wearing native stockings and moccasins, pointing toward a soldier running to the cluster holding a fleur-de-lis and declaring victory — a figure the article calls very special both to West and to the history of the battle, and one most British historians chose to ignore.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)
