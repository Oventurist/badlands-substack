# TeleMessage

TeleMessage is an Israel-founded messaging archiving company whose modified version of the encrypted messaging app [[signal|Signal]] was used by senior United States officials, including then-National Security Advisor [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]], who was photographed using it during a Cabinet meeting in early May 2025.[1] The product is operated by Smarsh, a compliance-archiving vendor, and is marketed to clients that must retain copies of messages for regulatory and records-retention purposes.[1]

## 2025 reported breach

In May 2025 the company temporarily suspended service after it was reportedly hacked, a cyberattack first reported by the outlet 404 Media on Sunday, May 4.[1] According to that reporting, the hacker did not obtain the messages of Waltz or of the people he communicated with, but the incident nonetheless raised questions about whether an archiving-modified version of Signal in use by top government officials was secure.[1] A Smarsh spokesperson told CNBC that "TeleMessage is investigating a potential security incident," that the company "acted quickly to contain it and engaged an external cybersecurity firm to support our investigation," and that "out of an abundance of caution, all TeleMessage services have been temporarily suspended," while all other Smarsh products remained fully operational.[1]

## Security criticism

Security commentary quoted in the coverage argued that the underlying design was the problem rather than any single intrusion: "Taking a secure messaging application and changing a core functionality such as backing up messages essentially breaks the security model," creating a risk that users' sensitive information "could be, and has been, compromised."[1] Badlands commentary went further, with Chris Paul writing that a claim of "hacking" is "always a good cover story for a company/product whose entire purpose is compromising information security."[1]
