# Matthew Bunn

Matthew Bunn is a professor of energy, national security and foreign policy at [[harvard-university|Harvard University]], where he specializes in nuclear weapons policy and nonproliferation.[1]

## June 2024 nuclear war warning

In an editorial published in the scientific journal *Science* and released on Thursday, June 20, 2024, Bunn warned that the world was dangerously close to nuclear war at a moment when the leading experts responsible for preventing such conflicts were "aging out" of the field.[1] He pleaded with political leaders to urgently recruit a new generation of scientists and engineers into nuclear risk-reduction work.[1]

Bunn asserted that "the risk of nuclear war has not been so high since the Cuban Missile Crisis" in 1962, and wrote that "dark clouds loom on the nuclear horizon, with threats from all directions."[1] He cautioned that the world could soon face an unrestrained arms competition for the first time in over five decades — and a more complex one, involving more countries and more technologies than earlier arms races.[1]

Central to his argument was the status of the [[new-start-treaty|New START Treaty]] of 2010, which he described as the last remaining agreement limiting U.S. and Russian nuclear forces. Bunn noted that the treaty expires in 2026, that Russia was blocking the inspections it requires, and that no new negotiations were underway.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the Bunn editorial as part of a coordinated narrative deployment rather than as a neutral expert assessment.[1] Writing alongside coverage of reports that China was recruiting young hackers through state-sanctioned talent competitions for cyberattacks on the United States, he argued that the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] was conditioning what he termed the "Normie Layers of the Collective Mind" to fear and to expect a "New Axis" nuclear or infrastructure crisis.[1] He characterized the invocation of a "Harvard Expert" as an authority-laundering device, and closed with the observation that "nonlinear events" are only nonlinear if they are not predicted well in advance.[1]
