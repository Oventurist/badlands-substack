# We The People Arizona Alliance

We The People Arizona Alliance is an Arizona election-integrity organization that sued the election-services vendor [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]] over the company's refusal to comply with a public records request for surveillance footage of its ballot-handling facility.[1]

## Litigation against Runbeck

The Alliance submitted a lawful public records request seeking "exterior video of all loading dock locations which clearly shows any item(s) being delivered and/or picked up" and "interior video of all loading dock locations/warehouse space which clearly shows any item(s) being delivered and/or picked up" for the period after Election Day.[1] Runbeck refused to comply, and the dispute proceeded to court as We The People AZ v. Runbeck.[1]

The first hearing in the case took place on Wednesday, September 20, 2023, and was covered live by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] on Rumble.[1] The central legal question was whether Runbeck, acting as a government contractor, should be subject to public records requests at all.[1] The Alliance sought two days of loading dock surveillance footage, arguing that the video was critical to establishing ballot chain of custody.[1]

Attorneys for [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] and Runbeck argued in response that Runbeck was no different from a Staples or a UPS Store, and that the product of Runbeck's work was no different from the toilet paper stocked in the county's bathrooms.[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands]] correspondent [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], reporting from the hearing, noted that this argument was made in earnest.[1]

Ashe identified what she called the smoking-gun refutation of that position: the county's contract with Runbeck is a sole-source contract, meaning the legal requirements for competitive bidding with multiple vendors are waived precisely because Runbeck is deemed so critical to the election process that no other vendor can supply the service.[1] The plaintiff's attorney raised this point, but it was not the central thrust of the argument and its delivery was described as muddled.[1] The judge committed to ruling on the matter expeditiously.[1]
