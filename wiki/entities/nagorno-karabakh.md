# Nagorno-Karabakh

**Nagorno-Karabakh**, also known as Upper Karabakh, is a mountainous territory in the South Caucasus that is internationally recognized as part of [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]] but which was occupied by the Armenian military, together with seven adjacent regions, beginning in 1991 amid the collapse of the Soviet Union.[1] In the Badlands Media analysis by Joe Lange, Nagorno-Karabakh and the border districts attached to it are treated as far more than a frozen ethnic conflict: they are described as the physical hinge of a heroin corridor linking the Middle East to Europe.[1]

## Post-Soviet occupation

Relations between the former Soviet republics of [[armenia|Armenia]] and Azerbaijan have been tense since 1991, when Armenian forces took the territory.[1] The article argues that the breakup of the Soviet Union produced chaos across an entire region of Eastern European and Caucasus states, and that the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] helped foment that chaos in order to establish worldwide drug trafficking routes.[1] It treats it as significant that Armenia seized this gateway to Europe while [[george-hw-bush|George H. W. Bush]] was president, and that Bush — who had responded forcefully to Iraq's invasion of Kuwait — allowed the Armenian aggression to stand despite the international community's recognition of Azerbaijani sovereignty.[1]

## Strategic geography

Adjacent to the Karabakh territory runs an 81-mile (130-kilometer) section of the Azerbaijan–[[iran|Iran]] state border that was under Armenian control for roughly three decades.[1] The article emphasizes that this strip effectively divides Azerbaijan and contains the one main road through the area, making it vital for trade moving from Iran into Europe.[1] In the author's framing it was the "doorway" to Europe and a key segment of a major heroin trade route out of [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]], transiting Iran and Armenia before reaching European markets.[1]

## Recapture and aftermath

Azerbaijan retook the territory during the [[second-karabakh-war|Second Karabakh War]] in 2020, liberating several cities and nearly 300 settlements and villages after 44 days of fighting, with a Russian-mediated cease-fire signed on November 10, 2020.[1] Azerbaijani president [[ilham-aliyev|Ilham Aliyev]] subsequently reported that heroin seizures elsewhere along the Azerbaijan–Iran border doubled once the corridor was closed, which he presented as proof that the occupied territories had been used for narcotics trafficking and for the training of international terrorists.[1] Border clashes between the two countries continued after the war, including fighting in September 2022 that killed more than 200 people and prompted a visit to Armenia by U.S. House Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]].[1]
