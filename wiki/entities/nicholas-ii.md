# Nicholas II

Nicholas II — rendered "Tzar Nicholas II" in Badlands Media coverage — was the last emperor of Russia, deposed in 1917 during the upheaval that [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] characterizes as an early [[color-revolution|color revolution]].[1]

## Abdication

Carlson recounts that the Russian Revolution began in early 1917, with World War I going badly for Russia and unrest growing both within the Russian military and among the population at large.[1] The people no longer trusted that the Tzar could effectively run the empire during wartime, and he agreed to abdicate his power to a new government led by the Russian Parliament.[1]

## Death

In October 1917 the Bolsheviks, led by [[vladimir-lenin|Vladimir Lenin]], overthrew both the monarch and the newly created parliamentary government, producing a civil war between the "Reds" and the "Whites" — the Bolsheviks against those who supported the Russian monarch.[1] In 1918 the Tzar and his family were murdered by the same people who had carried out the [[bolshevik-revolution|October overthrow]].[1]

## Occult advisors and the Protocols

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes Nicholas II as a superstitious monarch surrounded by occult influence.[2] The French occultist [[gerard-encausse|Gerard Encausse]], known as Papus, became a trusted consultant to the Czar in 1901 and, with his colleague the Russian mystic [[yuliana-glinka|Yuliana Glinka]], introduced Nicholas and his wife Alexandra — a granddaughter of Queen Victoria — to [[protocols-of-the-elders-of-zion|*The Protocols of the Learned Elders of Zion*]] as proof of an evil Jewish conspiracy to take over the world.[2] Ehret treats this episode as the point at which a Theosophical forgery entered the highest levels of Russian state politics.[2]

## Support for the Bering Strait tunnel

In 1905, following the sage advice of [[sergei-witte|Sergey Witte]], Czar Nicholas II gave national support to the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait tunnel]] and funded a team of American engineers to carry out feasibility studies.[3] Russia had good reason to trust the United States at that time, as the [[trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]] built between 1890 and 1905 had benefited immensely from Baldwin Locomotives made in Philadelphia and from the support of hundreds of American technicians and engineers.[3]

That same year, however, Wall Street financier [[jacob-schiff|Jacob Schiff]] financed Japan's victory over Russia in the [[russo-japanese-war|Russo-Japanese War]], crippling the Russian state and military and opening the way to revolution.[3]

## Bering Strait tunnel patronage

In 1902 Nicholas II employed the French engineer [[loicq-de-lobel|Loicq de Lobel]], who predicted that through trains across the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait]] would run within five or six years, promising "a fast trip in palace cars with every convenience."[4] In 1906 the czar, with Prime Minister [[sergei-witte|Sergei Witte]], sponsored American engineering surveys for the tunnel; [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that two early color revolutions and the murder of the czar put a stop to that trajectory.[4]
