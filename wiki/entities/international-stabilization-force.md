# International Stabilization Force

The International Stabilization Force is the multinational force contemplated for Gaza under the U.S.-brokered ceasefire framework, drawing civilian and military personnel from participating countries.[1]

## Headquarters and composition

Personnel from the participating countries were to be hosted at the [[civil-military-coordination-centre|Civil Military Coordination Centre]], a headquarters established by the United States in October 2025 at Kiryat Gat, Israel.[1] Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] called in January 2026 for the headquarters to be dismantled so that Israel could remove representatives from "countries like Egypt and Britain."[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the force as a company of soldiers drawn from across the "Sovereign Alliance," led by [[turkey|Turkey]] and [[qatar|Qatar]], with Presidents [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] serving as its symbolic captains, and predicted that Israeli settlers might attack it.[1]

## Force composition and Gaza base (February 2026)

At the first [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]] meeting in Washington on February 19, 2026, the board set out plans to deploy an International Stabilization Force of 32,000 personnel — 12,000 police and 20,000 troops — drawn from countries including Indonesia, Morocco, Kazakhstan, Kosovo and Albania, with training support from [[egypt|Egypt]] and Jordan.[2] [[turkey|Turkey]] volunteered soldiers and police, a contingent President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has said he wants represented in the ISF, though [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] and other Israeli officials have emphatically stated it will never be allowed.[2] Trump also announced plans to build an ISF base in southern Gaza housing 5,000 soldiers and other personnel.[2]

## Phase Two disputes (January 2026)

When Special Envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] announced the start of Phase Two of the [[comprehensive-plan-to-end-the-gaza-conflict|20-Point Plan]] on January 14–15, 2026, Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the phase as the point at which Arab states take control of Gaza from the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]], making the composition of the stabilization force the central point of friction.[3] He wrote that most of the Israeli political establishment is opposed to [[turkey|Turkey]] or [[qatar|Qatar]] holding a leadership role in the ISF, while President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] specifically wants both involved.[3]

In his reading, Qatar antagonizes [[israel|Israel]] because of the internal Israeli scandal over Hamas funding before October 7, while Turkey does so through years of mutual threats — and Turkey's [[nato|NATO]] membership means an Israeli strike on Turkish forces could trigger [[article-5|Article 5]].[3] Witkoff credited [[egypt|Egypt]], Qatar and Turkey with finalizing the plan's particulars.[3]

## Security Council authorization (November 2025)

On November 17, 2025, the [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]] approved a U.S.-led resolution establishing the international stabilization force (ISF) to deploy to [[gaza|Gaza]] for at least two years, with thirteen members in favor, abstentions from Russia and China, and no votes against.[4] The United States sought to deploy the force as soon as January 2026, with troops contributed by several Arab and Muslim countries.[4]

The ISF is to operate under unified command acceptable to the [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]], train and work alongside a "newly trained and vetted Palestinian police force," and coordinate closely with [[israel|Israel]] and Egypt.[4] Its mission includes helping secure border areas, stabilizing Gaza's security environment, and facilitating humanitarian aid, using "all necessary measures to carry out its mandate consistent with international law, including international humanitarian law."[4] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Turkish participation places the force under the [[nato|NATO]] umbrella, so an IDF attack on ISF soldiers would put Israel at war with the United States and NATO.[4]
