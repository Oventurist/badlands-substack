# Kevin Warsh

Kevin Warsh is a former Governor of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], serving from 2006 to 2011, who in late January 2026 was widely regarded as the frontrunner to replace [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] as Fed Chair.[1] Warsh reportedly met with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the [[white-house|White House]] on January 29, 2026, the day before Trump said he would announce a nominee.[1]

Warsh appeared on a shortlist vetted by Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] that also included [[kevin-hassett|Kevin Hassett]], [[christopher-waller|Christopher Waller]], and [[rick-rieder|Rick Rieder]].[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that all of the candidates under consideration indicated support for cutting interest rates — a major point of contention between Trump and Powell, whose policies the President has claimed obstructed his economic strategy.[1]

## Expected nomination, March 2026

By mid-March 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was expected to nominate Warsh to succeed [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] as Chair of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], with tensions over interest-rate policy and Fed independence intensifying ahead of confirmation.[2] Powell's term was set to end on May 15, 2026, and Warsh's prior service as a Federal Reserve governor from 2006 to 2011 was cited as his central qualification.[2]

Republican Senator [[thom-tillis|Thom Tillis]] was reported by [[axios|Axios]] to be involved in discussions about the nomination and the confirmation dynamics surrounding Warsh.[2] Badlands commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized Tillis as effectively refusing to confirm Powell's successor in the Senate unless the administration dropped its criminal case against Powell, thereby entangling Warsh's confirmation with the broader standoff between the White House and the central bank.[2]

## June 2026 CPI surprise and the July rate decision

June 2026's inflation report delivered a sharp surprise that gave Warsh, as new [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] chair, room to delay potential interest-rate hikes.[3] The consumer price index fell 0.4 percent for the month — the largest drop since April 2020 — driven primarily by lower energy prices, while services inflation outside energy stayed flat; annual inflation eased to 3.5 percent from 4.2 percent in May, though it remained well above the Fed's 2 percent target.[3] The softer reading came after Fed officials, including board member [[christopher-waller|Christopher Waller]], had signaled openness to raising rates as soon as July if incoming data stayed hot.[3]

Warsh struck a cautious tone, warning against complacency: he said he did not want to "overread or cherry-pick data" and rejected any notion that it was "mission accomplished" on inflation.[3] Emphasizing that "inflation is a choice," he said the Fed "can and will deliver price stability" and that he wanted economic growth to be more broad-based while keeping price increases limited.[3] The combination of encouraging June data and fresh geopolitical risks left the Fed in a delicate spot: the immediate case for a July hike had weakened, but the inflation outlook remained fragile and closely tied to developments in the Middle East and energy markets.[3]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read Warsh's posture as the holding pattern he had predicted in his 2025 essay "Reality Wars," in which he argued the Fed had been caught in a game-theory pincer between raising rates — risking a true recession — and cutting too quickly, which would trigger another inflation wave and a faster crash.[3] He maintained that the stasis was temporary and that rate cuts were coming in 2026 despite financial media forecasts to the contrary, because the next liquidity wave was needed for a full-scale monetary transition and had to arrive only once it would benefit Main Street more than Wall Street, as Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] had promised in 2025.[3]

## Slipping odds for the Fed chair (August 2025)

By August 7, 2025, Warsh's standing as a front-runner to chair the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] had eroded on the prediction market [[kalshi|Kalshi]], where his implied probability stood at about 20 percent — behind [[kevin-hassett|Kevin Hassett]] at roughly 35 percent and Governor [[christopher-waller|Christopher Waller]], who had just crossed 50 percent.[4]
