# Robert Card

Robert Card was a 40-year-old man identified by law enforcement as the gunman in the October 2023 mass shootings in Lewiston, Maine, which killed 18 people.[1] He was found dead after a two-day manhunt, the Androscoggin County Sheriff's Office confirmed on a Friday, with the body located near a recycling plant in the Lisbon area according to multiple law enforcement sources.[1]

## Death and manhunt

During the initial overnight search, Maine State Police identified Card, 40, as the man they were seeking in connection with the two shootings at Sparetime Recreation and Schemengees Bar & Grille in Lewiston.[2] Card lived in Bowdoin and was a trained firearms instructor whom police believed served in the U.S. Army Reserve out of Saco.[2] State police said he had recently reported mental health issues, including hearing voices, had made threats to shoot the National Guard base in Saco, and had spent two weeks at a mental health facility during the summer of 2023; the public was warned that he should be considered armed and dangerous.[2] The white Subaru Outback police believed he was driving was found near the Lisbon boat dock on Frost Hill Avenue near Route 196, and officers knocked on the doors of nearby homes while helicopters circled overnight.[2] At an initial press conference at Lewiston City Hall, [[mike-sauschuck|Michael Sauschuck]] declined to confirm casualty figures and described Card only as a person of interest.[2]

Mike Sauschuck, commissioner of the Maine Department of Public Safety, said at a Friday night news conference that Card died of a self-inflicted gunshot wound.[1] The body was located at about 7:45 p.m. local time near the Androscoggin River in Lisbon, a town roughly eight miles southwest of Lewiston.[1] Card's vehicle, a white Subaru Outback, had earlier been found abandoned by a boat launch on the river.[1]

Officials said the following day that the body was found in the overflow parking lot of the Maine Recycling Corporation, in the back of a tractor-trailer.[1] Authorities had twice searched the business — where Card had worked at some point — but Sauschuck said they had not searched the overflow lot until the property owner contacted police and asked them to check.[1] Sauschuck also stated that the first three people to contact authorities to identify the man in the released photographs of the gunman were members of Card's own family.[1]

## Commentary and skepticism

### Thursday searches and the 24-hour mark

On Thursday, October 26, 2023, while the manhunt passed the 24-hour mark, law enforcement twice converged on Card's last known address in Bowdoin.[3] The second gathering that evening brought tense moments as officers surrounded the home and one was heard saying "come out with your hands up," but the episode ended uneventfully, with authorities departing two hours later and Maine State Police saying they were finished there for the night.[3] A law enforcement official said investigators had thought they detected something inside or near the home, prompting the activity, and Maine Department of Public Safety spokesperson Shannon Moss said authorities were doing their due diligence while trying to execute search warrants.[3] Maine State Police Col. William Ross announced at a Thursday news conference that an arrest warrant charging Card, 40, with murder had been issued, and officials repeated that he should be considered armed and dangerous.[3]

Writing at approximately the 24-hour mark, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Card had "all the classic earmarks of a mentally unstable person who was MK-Ultra'd into committing atrocities," and that the failure to capture him despite pervasive public surveillance in American communities was anomalous.[3] She contended that with Card still at large the public discussion focused solely on disarmament, messaging she said had been "locked and loaded" from the moment news of the shooting broke, and that "the usual suspects" were again blaming the gun for the actions of the gunman while proposing solutions that would produce more vulnerable, unarmed people.[3] She urged Americans to keep their guns, writing that "communists love to exploit tragedy to create the conditions for more tragedy," and closed by calling the loss of life tragic regardless of the shooter's inception and motivations.[3]

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] treatment of the case, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the episode fit a familiar and suspicious pattern: "Another mass shooting has concluded and, conveniently, the shooter has been found dead."[1] She noted that Card was known to the [[fbi|FBI]], that alleged imageboard posts attributed to him depicted a racist angered by unfettered immigration who broadcast an intent to act, that he should have been red-flagged based on his mental health evaluation yet continued working as a firearms instructor, and that he was reportedly hearing voices.[1]

Her commentary held that the official framing — a "radical far right extremist" who was "racist" and should have been disarmed — was conveniently useful to the prevailing narrative, providing cover on immigration policy, chilling criticism of mass migration, and amplifying calls for disarmament at a moment when American gun owners were characterized as the greatest remaining check on establishment power.[1] Because Card was dead, she wrote, he could not be questioned about "whose voices he was hearing or what his FBI handler's instructions were," adding that "false flags often have real victims, and the loss of life is a tragedy."[1] She concluded that low public trust in institutions, combined with modern technology and deepfake-driven reality collapse, has produced a skepticism-first approach to news analysis, and that "official narratives should never be believed without critical examination."[1] See [[false-flag-operation]] and [[lewiston-maine-mass-shooting]].
