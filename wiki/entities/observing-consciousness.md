# Observing Consciousness

Observing Consciousness is the pen name of a [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor who publishes on the Badlands Substack under a Substack profile of the same name.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features) The author is the writer of the ongoing [[hijacked-consciousness-series|Hijacked Consciousness]] series, described by Badlands as a series "on the Hijacking of the Human Operating System, and what's being done about it."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features)

## Feature work

Two installments of the series were highlighted in the August 15, 2025 Badlands News Brief roundup of recent features. "Hijacked Consciousness - The Silent War," published August 2, 2025, was the fourth article in the series, and "Hijacked Consciousness - Resonance Field," published August 9, 2025, was the fifth.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features) Both entries carried the standing editorial note directing new readers to begin with Part 1 of the series.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features)

## Themes

The series framing — the "human operating system" as something that can be hijacked, and the language of a "silent war" and a "resonance field" — situates the author's work within Badlands' broader interest in psychological and information warfare rather than conventional political reporting.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features)

## "Generational Dependence Syndrome" (February 2025)

On February 11, 2025, Observing Consciousness published "Generational Dependence Syndrome: Transitioning from Fear to Freedom in Fifth-Generation Warfare" through Badlands Media, an essay that received 136 likes on the platform.[2] In it the author proposed the concept of [[generational-dependence-syndrome|Generational Dependence Syndrome]] (GDS) to describe how patterns of trauma, fear and societal conditioning may reinforce dependence on centralized systems across generations, presenting it as a synthesis of [[intergenerational-trauma|intergenerational trauma]], [[learned-helplessness|learned helplessness]] and [[cultural-hegemony|cultural hegemony]].[2]

The piece drew on a wide range of outside authorities — [[mattias-desmet|Mattias Desmet]] on [[mass-formation-psychosis|Mass Formation Psychosis]], [[peter-levine|Peter Levine]] and [[carl-jung|Carl Jung]] on trauma and the shadow, [[martin-seligman|Martin Seligman]] on helplessness, [[antonio-gramsci|Antonio Gramsci]] on hegemony, [[david-hawkins|David Hawkins]] and [[joe-dispenza|Joe Dispenza]] on consciousness and frequency, [[walter-russell|Walter Russell]] on balanced systems, [[don-beck|Don Beck]] and [[chris-cowan|Chris Cowan]] on [[spiral-dynamics|Spiral Dynamics]], and [[ann-masten|Ann Masten]] on resilience.[2] It closed by presenting [[fifth-generation-warfare|Fifth-Generation Warfare]] as the counter-strategy and inviting readers to find more of the author's work at the Observing Consciousness Substack.[2]

## "Hijacked Consciousness - Field Builders" (August 2025)

On August 28, 2025 Observing Consciousness published the sixth installment of the series, "Hijacked Consciousness - Field Builders," subtitled "How Education Was Weaponized to Sever the Soul, Fracture Memory, and Re-write Reality"; the post drew 79 likes.[3] The essay describes the [[resonant-human|resonant human]] whose presence calibrates a room, the [[microfields|microfields]] such people build in homes, circles and online spaces, the return of [[sacred-education|sacred education]] as "soul remembering," and the [[grid-carriers|grid carriers]] holding the emerging field.[3]

## "Hijacked Consciousness - Sacred Origins" (July 2025)

On July 8, 2025 Observing Consciousness published the first installment of the series, "Hijacked Consciousness - Sacred Origins," which drew 170 likes and introduced the run as a new series "on the Hijacking of the Human Operating System, and what's being done about it."[4] The author described the project as concerning "memory, control, and the war for the human soul" rather than education alone, and promised readers they would not be told what to believe but might "start to remember what you've always known."[4]

The essay surveys [[ancient-sacred-learning-traditions|ancient sacred learning traditions]] in Sumer, Egypt, Vedic India, Mesoamerica, Greece and the Celtic and monastic worlds, invoking [[pythagoras|Pythagoras]] and the harmony of the spheres; it then attributes the loss of that order to [[the-enlightenment|the Enlightenment]] and the [[industrial-model-of-schooling|industrial model of schooling]], with [[harvard-university|Harvard]] and [[skull-and-bones|Skull and Bones]] named as the credentialing and ritual arms of an "Ivy obedience grid."[4] A section on [[pharmaceutical-compliance-in-schools|pharmaceutical compliance in schools]] argues that Ritalin, Adderall and SSRIs functioned as "chemical silencers" serving uniformity rather than clarity.[4] As in later entries, the article closes by directing readers to more of "OC's" free work at the Observing Consciousness Substack and carries the standard Badlands disclaimer that contributors' views are their own.[4]

## "Soul Governance" (July 2025)

On July 1, 2025 Observing Consciousness published "Soul Governance: Final Reclamation of Divine Authority from the Thrones That Never Owned You," which drew 148 likes and was signed simply "Michael."[6] The piece was framed as the capstone of the author's canon, addressed to readers who had "walked through crimson gates and golden thrones" and followed earlier work from Harvard's investments to Vatican vaults, biometric cages and spirit-cooked boardrooms.[6]

In an author's note, Observing Consciousness wrote that the piece "wasn't written to impress you, it was written to activate you," described his own passage "through collapse, through rage, through silence so loud it almost broke me," and told readers they were "not awakening into madness" but "awakening out of it."[6] As with the author's other contributions, the article closed by directing readers to more of "OC's" free work at the Observing Consciousness Substack and carried the standard Badlands disclaimer.[6]

## "The Bridge" (March 2025)

On March 1, 2025 Observing Consciousness published "The Bridge: Awakening, Healing, and Ascension," which drew 182 likes and was addressed directly to readers in the early stages of an [[spiritual-awakening|awakening]].[8] Departing from the geopolitical register of the author's other work, the essay is written in the second person as reassurance to people experiencing disorientation, heightened sensitivity, dizziness, ringing in the ears, restlessness, or shifts in taste and social preference.[8]

The piece argues that governments, corporations and "bad actors" have worked to keep people fragmented and fearful, and that when people feel unsafe they seek external authority, deepening the control structures.[8] It grounds this claim in psychology and neurobiology, citing [[bessel-van-der-kolk|Bessel van der Kolk]], [[peter-levine|Peter Levine]], [[joe-dispenza|Joe Dispenza]], [[bruce-lipton|Bruce Lipton]] and [[david-hawkins|David Hawkins]], and presents [[somatic-experiencing|Somatic Experiencing]], breathwork, time in nature, cold therapy and fasting as routes back to nervous-system regulation.[8] In an author's note the writer says the article "was written for all of us, for anyone who has ever felt that something is not quite right," and offers it as something to share with people beginning to see beyond the surface.[8] As with the author's other contributions, it closes by directing readers to more of "OC's" free work at the Observing Consciousness Substack and carries the standard Badlands disclaimer.[8]

## "The Liminal" (April 2025)

On April 5, 2025 Observing Consciousness published [[liminality|"The Liminal: Navigating the Emerging Space Between Selves"]], which drew 156 likes and was presented as a follow-up to the author's earlier feature "Walking the Path."[10] The essay addresses readers who have passed through initial awakening and emotional unraveling only to find themselves in an interval that is "not peaceful, necessarily, but eerily still."[10]

Written in the author's characteristic second person, the piece defines the liminal as "the pregnant pause between breaths" and supports the framing with citations to [[victor-turner|Victor Turner]] on sacred disorientation, [[andrew-newberg|Andrew Newberg]] on the deactivation of ego and spatial orientation, [[shamini-jain|Shamini Jain]] on energetic recalibration, [[lisa-miller|Lisa Miller]] on the "spiritual lull," [[dan-siegel|Dan Siegel]] on differentiation, [[lynne-mctaggart|Lynne McTaggart]] on [[morphic-resonance|morphic resonance]], [[konstantin-korotkov|Konstantin Korotkov]] on [[biofield-imaging|biofield imaging]], [[nora-bateson|Nora Bateson]] on dissolving connections in transforming systems, and the poet [[david-whyte|David Whyte]] on solitude.[10] It also grounds the experience mythically in [[joseph-campbell|Joseph Campbell]]'s [[heros-journey|Hero's Journey]] and in the Inanna, Buddha, Isis–Osiris and Odin narratives.[10]

The article's extended case study follows "Crystal," a VP of Sales who left a 17-year corporate career for a cabin at the edge of a wooded town and found not liberation but invisibility — a state the author reads as gestation rather than death.[10] As with the author's other contributions, the piece closes with an author's note addressed to those who sense "an undercurrent beneath reality," a pointer to more of "OC's" free work at the Observing Consciousness Substack, and the standard Badlands disclaimer.[10]

## "The Ivy Web" (2025)

"The Ivy Web," published on May 29, 2025, is an Observing Consciousness contribution to Badlands Media completing a trilogy with "Wilted Ivy" and "The Harvard Control Grid."[11] It argues that [[harvard-university|Harvard University]] operated as a global command node of soft power rather than a school, documenting its endowment liquidation, its training of sanctioned Chinese paramilitary officials, the conviction of [[charles-lieber|Charles Lieber]], and federal scrutiny of its undisclosed foreign funding.[11] Consistent with the author's wider body of work, the closing sections shift from institutional analysis to a spiritual register, describing the exposure as "not just political" but "biological" and "spiritual," and calling for decentralized education, ethical research networks, open source governance and "spiritual literacy" in place of the fallen academic order.[11]
