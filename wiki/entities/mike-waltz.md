# Mike Waltz

Mike Waltz is a former U.S. National Security Advisor in the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration. In Badlands Media commentary he is treated as an emblem of a political class deferential to foreign influence, particularly with respect to [[israel|Israel]].[1]

## The Danny Danon "list" episode

In June 2026 Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] highlighted a clip, reposted by Israel's Ambassador to the United Nations [[danny-danon|Danny Danon]], in which the suggestion was made that a list be compiled of Americans who criticize the Israeli government, with those Americans to be targeted for some form of retribution.[1] Waltz was described as nodding along in apparent agreement while sharing the stage.[1]

The Badlands writer compared the proposal to Nazi-era list-making and characterized Waltz's reaction as that of a traitor "fully prepared to support the targeted persecution of American citizens by a foreign government."[1] Because the conduct being punished would be criticism of a government — speech protected by the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] — the episode was presented as symbolic of a corrupt and irredeemable political class.[1] The commentary further faulted Waltz for remaining on stage after the remark and expressed relief that he had been removed from the cabinet, while calling for others in similar positions to be rooted out.[1]

## Assessment in Badlands coverage

Waltz functions in this coverage less as an individual policymaker than as a case study in what Badlands writers regard as the capture of U.S. national security officials by foreign patronage networks, a theme they also apply to figures such as [[john-bolton|John Bolton]].[1]


## Plan to Clear NSC Detailees (January 2025)

In January 2025 incoming National Security Adviser Mike Waltz told Breitbart News that every intelligence official detailed to the [[national-security-council|National Security Council]] at the [[white-house|White House]] under outgoing President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would be expected to vacate the premises by 12:01 p.m. Eastern on Inauguration Day.[2] "Everybody is going to resign at 12:01 on January 20," Waltz said, adding, "We're working through our process to get everybody their clearances and through the transition process now. Our folks know who we want out in the agencies, we're putting those requests in, and in terms of the detailees they're all going to go back."[2] He said he wanted it "crystal clear what the agenda is."[2] The detailees in question were career intelligence officials drawn from agencies including the [[fbi|FBI]], the [[cia|CIA]], the NSA and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], reporting up to political appointees atop each NSC portfolio.[2]

## UN ambassador confirmation (July 2025)

Waltz's nomination as United States ambassador to the United Nations advanced in late July 2025 after Senator [[jeanne-shaheen|Jeanne Shaheen]] (D-N.H.) agreed to support him in exchange for a commitment from the [[state-department|State Department]] to release $75 million in foreign aid for Haiti and Nigeria.[3] Shaheen argued that with figures such as Vice President JD Vance, Under Secretary Elbridge Colby and Secretary Pete Hegseth in the Situation Room favoring retreat from the world, the country was "better off having someone like Mike Waltz present," particularly "when you consider the alternatives."[3] The Senate Foreign Relations Committee reported Waltz out favorably by a 12–10 vote on Thursday, July 24, 2025, making confirmation by the full Senate likely.[3]

## Selection as national security adviser and transition remarks (November 2024)

President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] tapped Waltz, then a Florida congressman and the first Green Beret to serve in Congress, to be his national security adviser in the incoming administration.[4] In his first television appearance after being picked — on "Fox News Sunday" with Gillian Turner on November 24, 2024 — Waltz said he had met with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s national security adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] and that the two administrations were working collaboratively during the transition.[4] "For our adversaries out there that think this is a time of opportunity, that they can play one administration off the other — they are wrong," Waltz said, adding, "We are hand in glove. We are one team with the United States in this transition."[4] Politico noted that Waltz was at the time the member of Congress with the most Fox News appearances.[4]

Waltz also addressed the escalation of the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]], comparing the Biden administration's decision to let Ukraine use anti-personnel landmines to "World War I trench warfare."[4] "The President-elect has been very concerned about the escalation and where it's all going," he said, listing the sequence of moves by North Korea, the United States, Russia, Iran and South Korea, and allied extensions of missile range.[4] He concluded: "We need to bring this to a responsible end. We need to restore deterrence and peace and get ahead of this escalation later rather than responding to it."[4]

## Badlands reading of the Waltz remarks

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated Waltz's transition-period comments as narrative rather than policy, later summarizing that "the Mike Waltz comments on Ukraine were noise" while [[keith-kellogg|Keith Kellogg]]'s were "signal."[4] He framed the apparent contradictions between two Waltz clips recorded four days apart through a Game Theory and Sun Tzu lens, arguing that the Trump team was content to project an escalatory posture on Ukraine because it was in his view already aligned with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Russia on the other side of the battlefield.[4] The same commentary held that career diplomats such as [[amos-hochstein|Amos Hochstein]] were pressing [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] toward a ceasefire, freezing Israel into a holding pattern in expectation of a more hawkish Washington after January — a maneuver the writer likened to leaving a cornered enemy a narrow path of escape.[4]

## Vacated House seat

Waltz resigned his seat in Florida's 6th Congressional District when he became national security adviser to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], triggering an April 2025 special election contested by Republican [[randy-fine|Randy Fine]] and Democrat [[joshua-weil|Joshua Weil]].[5] See [[florida-6th-congressional-district-special-election]].

## Reported friction with Steve Witkoff (2025)

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] reported in April 2025 that Waltz, as national security adviser, was outraged that special envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] had arranged several face-to-face meetings with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], placing him among a cluster of administration figures — alongside [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], [[keith-kellogg|Keith Kellogg]] and [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] — described as resentful of Witkoff's expanding diplomatic role.[6]

## TeleMessage exposure (May 2025)

In early May 2025 Waltz, then national security advisor, was photographed using [[telemessage|TeleMessage]] — an Israel-founded modified version of [[signal|Signal]] that archives messages for compliance purposes — during a Cabinet meeting.[7] Days later the app's operator, Smarsh, temporarily suspended all TeleMessage services after a reported hack disclosed by 404 Media; the reporting indicated the hacker had not obtained the messages of Waltz or his interlocutors, but the incident raised questions about the security of an app widely used by senior government officials.[7]
