# Minneapolis

Minneapolis is the largest city in Minnesota and, together with neighboring [[saint-paul|Saint Paul]], the center of the metropolitan area that became the focus of the federal immigration enforcement campaign known as [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]] in the winter of 2025–2026.[1]

## Operation Metro Surge

Beginning in December 2025, the federal government deployed thousands of agents from [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] and [[customs-and-border-protection|CBP]] across the Minneapolis–Saint Paul metro area and statewide; by January 19, 2026, the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] reported more than 10,000 arrests.[1] Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] said the arrests targeted "criminal illegal aliens who were killing Americans, hurting children and reigning terror in Minneapolis," blaming Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] and Mayor [[jacob-frey|Jacob Frey]] for refusing to protect their own people.[1]

## Economic and civic impact

The surge affected business activity in the city, particularly in immigrant neighborhoods, where some establishments — notably day care and health transport businesses — reported revenue declines of up to 100%, and residents raised concerns about fear in the community and economic instability.[1] A coalition of faith leaders, community groups and unions organized an "ICE Out of Minnesota: Day of Truth and Freedom" for January 23, 2026, calling for a statewide economic blackout and general strike, with endorsements from local and regional [[afl-cio|AFL-CIO]], [[seiu|SEIU]] and [[american-federation-of-teachers|AFT]] affiliates.[1] The city joined Saint Paul and Minnesota Attorney General [[keith-ellison|Keith Ellison]] in a federal lawsuit filed January 12, 2026 against DHS and federal officials over the operation's tactics.[1]

## Local crime context

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] announcement that a Minneapolis gang member had been convicted of murder and RICO charges arising from a rivalry between two local gangs known as "The Highs" and "The Lows," and observed that no reports described American gangs warring with migrant gangs.[1] She argued that Minneapolis continued to dominate DHS activity and that Ellison's lawsuit followed grand jury indictments over the Minnesota government's alleged enablement of foreign criminal networks.[1]

## January 2026 ICE shooting and unrest

In mid-January 2026 an [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] agent shot and wounded a Venezuelan national during a traffic stop in north Minneapolis, with federal authorities stating that the man had attacked the agent with a shovel and that the shooting was in self-defense; the man was hospitalized in stable condition.[2] The incident followed the January 7 death of [[minneapolis-ice-shooting-of-renee-nicole-good|Renee Nicole Good]], who was fatally shot while attempting to ram an ICE agent with her car.[2]

Protests continued in the city the next day and federal vehicles were looted during the unrest, after which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he was considering invoking the [[insurrection-act|Insurrection Act]] in response to the situation in Minnesota.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the episodes had confirmed that no one is above the law, criticized what she called the migrants' "white Karen handlers," and predicted that immigration would be a central issue in 2026 — including through effects of deportations on housing demand and on voter rolls.[2]

## Pro-Palestinian rally incident (October 2023)

On Sunday, October 22, 2023, the Minneapolis Police Department received several reports of disturbances surrounding a pro-Palestinian demonstration near the intersection of Hennepin Avenue and Lyndale Avenue at about 3:23 p.m.[3] Witnesses reported that a vehicle drove through the crowd and one person reported hearing a gunshot; no injuries were reported, no victims came forward, and no arrests were made, with police stating that follow-up investigations were under way.[3] The rally was organized with help from the [[minnesota-anti-war-committee|Minnesota Anti-War Committee]], which announced a further protest for the following Wednesday outside Representative Betty McCollum's office in St. Paul.[3]

## Federal sanctuary lawsuit, September 2025

Minneapolis was named alongside the state of [[minnesota|Minnesota]] and [[saint-paul|St. Paul]] in a [[doj-lawsuit-against-minnesota-sanctuary-policies|Department of Justice lawsuit]] filed on 29 September 2025 challenging [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary city]] policies.[4] The city maintains an ordinance prohibiting cooperation with federal immigration enforcement, and federal prosecutors asked a judge to invalidate that code alongside state constitutional provisions and Hennepin County administrative orders.[4]
