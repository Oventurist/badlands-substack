# Jeanine Pirro

**Jeanine Pirro** is a United States Attorney serving in the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s Justice Department.[1]

## WHCA dinner case

Pirro attended the [[white-house-correspondents-association-dinner|White House Correspondents' Association dinner]] at which a man was accused of attempting to assassinate President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] In the resulting federal prosecution, the defendant's legal team sought to remove Pirro and Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] from involvement in the case, arguing that because both attended the event they could be considered victims or witnesses and thus present a conflict of interest.[1] Defense attorney Eugene Ohm indicated the request could expand into a demand for the recusal of Pirro's entire office; US District Judge [[trevor-mcfadden|Trevor McFadden]] did not immediately rule and asked the defense to explain the scope of the request further.[1] The matter is treated in detail at [[whca-dinner-assassination-attempt-prosecution|the WHCA dinner assassination attempt prosecution]].[1]

## January 6 seditious conspiracy dismissals (April 2026)

Pirro signed the April 2026 [[justice-department|Justice Department]] filing asking the US Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit to vacate seditious conspiracy convictions against leaders of the [[proud-boys|Proud Boys]] and [[oath-keepers|Oath Keepers]] and to permit prosecutors to permanently drop the indictments.[2] The filing states that the move follows standard practice when the government determines that dismissal is in the interest of justice and notes that similar motions are routinely granted.[2] The request covered [[stewart-rhodes|Stewart Rhodes]], who had not been pardoned earlier in the year, as well as Kelly Meggs, Kenneth Harrelson, Jessica Watkins, Ethan Nordean, Joseph Biggs, Zachary Rehl and Dominic Pezzola.[2]

## "Big Balls" assault sentencing (October 2025)

As U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia, Pirro called the October 2025 sentencing of two 15-year-olds to probation for their roles in the assault on former [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] staffer [[edward-coristine|Edward Coristine]] "shocking."[3] D.C. Superior Court judge [[kendra-biggs|Kendra Biggs]], a [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] appointee, imposed no jail time, stating that her role was to "rehabilitate" rather than punish; President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] separately said the decision was "terrible" and that "the judge should be ashamed."[3]

## Investigation into D.C. police crime data (2025)

As U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia, Pirro led a federal investigation opened in August 2025 into whether Washington, D.C. police officials manipulated crime statistics.[4] The New York Times first reported her role, with the Associated Press subsequently confirming it.[4] The inquiry arose during an escalating confrontation between the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration and the city over control of the [[metropolitan-police-department|Metropolitan Police Department]], and followed the placement of MPD commander [[michael-pulliam|Michael Pulliam]] on administrative leave over allegations he falsified crime data.[4] Reporting at the time noted that it was not immediately clear what federal laws could have been violated by the alleged manipulation.[4]

## Selection as U.S. Attorney (May 2025)

Pirro was named to the top federal prosecutor post in Washington, D.C. after the collapse of the nomination of [[edward-martin|Edward Martin]] in May 2025.[5] The surprise announcement of the Fox News personality as Martin's replacement triggered a unique and mixed wave of reaction from former prosecutors, critics of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and people involved in the U.S. Capitol riot prosecutions.[5] Her selection — described in coverage as that of an ardent Trump loyalist and cable news fixture who had spread claims of a rigged 2020 election — drew criticism from some Democrats, though the implosion of Martin's nomination drew celebratory statements from Trump's critics and potentially softened some of the backlash against her.[5] Badlands host [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] discussed the change on the Devolution Power Hour.[5]
