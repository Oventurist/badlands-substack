# American Bankers Association

The American Bankers Association (ABA) is the principal trade association and lobbying body of the United States banking industry. In A Seismic Shift, [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] casts it as the leading opponent of a public banking option and quotes its own policy analysis at length as an inadvertent confirmation of the threat such an option would pose to Wall Street.

## Position on FedAccounts

The ABA issue update quoted in the article notes that members of [[congress|Congress]] have proposed legislation requiring the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] to offer government-subsidised retail bank accounts in conjunction with the [[united-states-postal-service|U.S. Postal Service]] — [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]] — and to require banks with assets greater than $10 billion to offer basic "pass through" accounts, with the deposits resting at the Federal Reserve rather than on the bank's balance sheet. While stating that it strongly supports the goal of bringing more Americans into the banking system, the ABA calls the proposal impractical, "highly disruptive to the existing retail banking system," and unlikely to lead to long-term, wealth-building financial inclusion.

The association further argues that the United States has a healthy banking system of approximately 5,000 banks employing more than two million people, that FDIC-insured depositors have never lost a penny of an insured deposit, and that FedAccounts "would therefore draw deposits from banks of all sizes to the Federal Reserve balance sheet." It states plainly that banks "will nevertheless be unable to compete with a government agency offering deposit accounts with an unlimited balance sheet." On postal banking it warns that requiring the Postal Service to provide banking services would extend well beyond the agency's core competencies, raise serious regulatory and consumer protection questions, and could leave consumers less protected than at a regulated financial institution, concluding that "FedAccounts and Postal Banking would represent a seismic shift in our national banking infrastructure" — the phrase from which the article takes its title.

## The author's response

Lange reads the ABA's objections as an admission of vulnerability. He argues that the pass-through requirement would give bank customers the option of moving their deposits into safe, publicly owned and controlled accounts, removing those deposits from bank balance sheets, preventing their use in risky schemes and ending bailouts. He rejects the characterisation of FedAccounts as "subsidized," contending that it is private banks that have been subsidised for generations by [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|federal deposit insurance]], and that the industry fears losing FDIC protection because without it most Americans would choose the public option. He also disputes the claim of a healthy banking system in light of recent bank failures, and highlights that a public option would abolish the $250,000 limit on protected savings.
