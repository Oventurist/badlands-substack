# American Bankers Association

The American Bankers Association (ABA) is the principal trade and lobbying association of the U.S. private banking industry. In Badlands Media's account it is the leading opponent of the [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]] and postal banking proposals, applying pressure on Congress to reject them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Position on FedAccounts

The ABA notes that members of Congress have proposed legislation requiring the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] to offer government-subsidized retail bank accounts in conjunction with the [[united-states-postal-service|U.S. Postal Service]], and that the proposal would require banks with assets greater than $10 billion to offer basic "pass through" accounts with deposits resting at the Federal Reserve rather than on the bank's balance sheet.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) While stating that it strongly supports bringing more Americans into the banking system, the ABA calls the proposal impractical, highly disruptive to the existing retail banking system, and unlikely to lead to long-term, wealth-building financial inclusion.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

The association argues that the United States has a healthy banking system of approximately 5,000 banks employing more than two million people, that FDIC-insured depositors have never lost a penny of an insured deposit, and that FedAccounts would draw deposits from banks of all sizes onto the Federal Reserve balance sheet.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) It further states that banks would be unable to compete with a government agency offering deposit accounts with an unlimited balance sheet.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) On postal banking specifically, the ABA warns that requiring the Postal Service to provide banking services would extend well beyond its core competencies, raise serious regulatory and consumer protection questions, and could leave consumers less protected than at a regulated financial institution — concluding that FedAccounts and postal banking would represent a "seismic shift in our national banking infrastructure."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Badlands Media's reading

Badlands Media treats the ABA's objections as an inadvertent confession. The requirement that big banks offer the public option would let customers move deposits into safe, publicly owned accounts, removing them from bank balance sheets, preventing their use in risky schemes and ending bailouts.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The article disputes the ABA's use of the word "subsidized," arguing it is the private banks that have been subsidized for generations by [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|Federal Deposit Insurance]], and that the ABA's claim of a "healthy banking system" is undermined by recent bank failures.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The admission that banks cannot "compete" with a safe public option is presented as the industry's real fear: the end of private banking control over the U.S. government.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Support for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025)

In a letter published on Sunday, June 29, 2025, the association said it "strongly supports" many provisions within the [[one-big-beautiful-bill-act|One Big Beautiful Bill Act]] for the "much needed tax relief" they offer.[2] The endorsement came as the legislation — characterised by sweeping tax reforms and targeted incentives, and forecast to add to the federal deficit — advanced through the [[united-states-senate|US Senate]] on a narrow 51–49 vote and drew warnings from credit rating agencies.[2] The association's position aligned with that of some bank economists, including Nomura's chief economist for developed markets David Seif, who said the bill "would almost unquestionably be good for the US economy over the next couple of years compared to passing nothing."[2]
