# Libya

**Libya** is a North African state that, under [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]], became the wealthiest country in Africa before the 2011 US–NATO-backed intervention that ended his rule.[1] Badlands writer Joe Lange treats Libya as one end of a financial "bridge" running through [[moldova|Moldova]] and the [[podesta-group|Podesta Group]] back to Washington.[1]

## Pre-2011 condition

Before 2011, according to reporting in The Ecologist cited by the article, Libya had achieved economic independence: its own water, food, oil, currency and a state-owned bank.[1] Education and medical treatment were free, housing was treated as a human right, and Libyans participated in a system of local democracy.[1] The country built the world's largest irrigation system, the Great Man-Made River project, which carried water from the desert to the coastal cities; the $33 billion project was financed interest-free and without foreign debt through Libya's own state-owned bank.[1] Gaddafi was reportedly preparing to export that development model across Africa.[1]

## Gold and the pan-African dinar

A April 2, 2011 email from Sidney Blumenthal, released from [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s private server in late 2015, stated that Gaddafi's government held 143 tons of gold and a similar quantity of silver, accumulated before the rebellion and "intended to be used to establish a pan-African currency based on the Libyan golden Dinar," offering Francophone African countries an alternative to the CFA franc.[1] A source comment valued the bullion at more than $7 billion.[1] Badlands argues this — not humanitarian concern — was the operative motive for regime change, since a gold-backed African currency would have let other states escape the central-banking debt system.[1] A Q drop listing world central banks flagged the Central Bank of Libya as the network's "most recent conquest."[1]

## Aftermath and missing gold

Clinton's October 2011 visit to Libya was described in the press as a "victory lap," and she remarked "We came, we saw, he died!" in a CBS interview upon news of Gaddafi's capture and killing.[1] Human rights organizations questioned the atrocity reports used to justify the intervention, citing a lack of evidence.[1] Libya subsequently collapsed into civil war, destabilizing the region, fueling Europe's refugee crisis and allowing the Islamic State to establish a haven there.[1] US–NATO forces also bombed the irrigation infrastructure.[1] The World Gold Council recorded Libya's gold reserves falling from 143.82 tons in 2011 to 116.64 tons by 2014, a loss of about 27 tons, with some estimates that up to 20% of the stock — 28 to 30 tons — was stolen or disappeared during the revolution; none of it has been recovered.[1]

Lange contends that Gaddafi-era money and gold were funneled into European banks through Moldova, echoing testimony from a former chief investigator at Moldova's National Anti-Corruption Center that "Gadafi money was funneled into European banks through Moldova."[1] He further alleges the new post-Gaddafi government installed by Barack Obama, Clinton and [[john-mccain|John McCain]] was led by [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]], turning Libya into a terrorist training ground — an interpretive claim of the author.[1]
