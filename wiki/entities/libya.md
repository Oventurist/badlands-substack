# Libya

Libya is a country in North Africa that was ruled by [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] from 1969 until his overthrow and death in 2011. In the Badlands Media article "A Conflict of Interest," Libya is framed as the site of a U.S.-backed regime change operation that served as a cover for large-scale diversion of taxpayer funds to terrorist organizations.

According to the article, Libya had become the new "safe haven" training ground for Al-Qaeda after the original camps in Afghanistan were disrupted following 9/11. The source claims that Libya was providing the most foreign terrorists in Iraq based on population size, and that this was not a coincidence but the result of deliberate policy.

The article asserts that the 2011 NATO intervention in Libya was illegitimate and driven by financial interests rather than humanitarian concerns. It claims that [[john-mccain|John McCain]] visited [[benghazi|Benghazi]] in April 2011 to praise anti-Gaddafi rebels, whom the source identifies as members of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]], an [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] affiliate. The source further claims that the same playbook used in the [[iraq-war|Iraq War]]—diverting taxpayer money to enemies under the cover of military intervention—was replicated in Libya, only "much worse."

According to the article, after Gaddafi's fall, [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] became a billionaire businessman with a monopoly on air transport to Tripoli, and that his airline was used to fly [[isis|ISIS]] fighters from Libya to Syria through Turkey. It claims that [[mouza-moustafa|Mouza Moustafa]]'s [[libyan-emergency-task-force|Libyan Emergency Task Force]] was set up to channel U.S. taxpayer money to these same rebel groups. The source notes that in January 2019, during [[donald-trump|Donald Trump's]] first year as president, the Libyan government issued a warrant for Belhaj's arrest on war crimes charges, causing him to flee to Turkey.
