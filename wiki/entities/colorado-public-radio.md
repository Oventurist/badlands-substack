# Colorado Public Radio

Colorado Public Radio (CPR) is a local public media organization affiliated with the national public broadcasting system. Badlands writer Ashe in America identifies it as a direct antagonist in litigation involving Badlands personnel.[1]

## Allegations of false reporting

According to Ashe in America, the Colorado Public Radio apparatus published false reporting about Badlands during its case proceedings, which claimed to identify two witnesses against them in what she describes as the plaintiffs' frivolous case.[1] Those two "witnesses" were named in the CPR reporting, which was then picked up by [[npr|NPR]] and repeated by other outlets citing CPR; their names subsequently appeared in the plaintiffs' response to the defendants' motion for summary judgment.[1]

The judge cited the two witnesses as a reason for denying that motion, and the case continued for a year and a half. The witnesses never materialized and, in her account, "practically don't exist outside the reporting of local Colorado Public Radio and the court filings that reporting was ostensibly manufactured to bolster."[1]

## Role in the defunding argument

The episode is offered as evidence that the problem with public media "is most definitely not with just the national organizations," and that local public radio stations and sites function as progressive propaganda — supporting the conclusion "Defund it all. This is exactly what I voted for."[1] This argument was made in response to reporting on Congress's approval of $1.1 billion in clawbacks to [[corporation-for-public-broadcasting|Corporation for Public Broadcasting]] funding.[1]

## 2025 lawsuit over federal funding cuts

On May 27, 2025, Colorado Public Radio joined [[npr|NPR]] and other Colorado-based public radio stations, including outlets in Aspen and Ignacio, in filing suit against the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration to block planned federal funding cuts or restrictions on the broadcasters.[2] The complaint, filed in the U.S. District Court for Washington, D.C., cited President Trump's public remarks claiming that public broadcasting is biased against him as evidence that funding threats were being used to chill the stations' First Amendment rights.[2] Quoting prior court decisions, the suit stated, "It is not always obvious when the government has acted with a retaliatory purpose in violation of the First Amendment. 'But this wolf comes as a wolf,'" and argued that the executive order targeted NPR and [[pbs|PBS]] expressly because the President deemed their content not "fair, accurate, or unbiased."[2]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] criticized Colorado Public Radio for reporting on the merits of its own lawsuit, calling it "peak legacy media," and argued that Colorado had repeatedly served as a venue for legal campaigns against Trump.[2]
