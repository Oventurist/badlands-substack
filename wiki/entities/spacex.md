# SpaceX

SpaceX is the private space launch and satellite company founded and led by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], and one of the two core entities — alongside Tesla — around which Musk's industrial portfolio is organized.[1]

## Space Data Network Backbone contract

In May 2026 the [[united-states-space-force|US Space Force]] awarded SpaceX a $2.29 billion contract to rapidly build a new satellite communications network designed to securely move military data around the world at high speeds, creating what the service described as a critical "backbone" for future US military operations.[1] The program, known as the [[space-data-network-backbone|Space Data Network Backbone]], will use a constellation of low-Earth orbit satellites linked together with optical, or laser-based, communications technology, enabling US forces to share targeting information, sensor data, battlefield communications, and other intelligence globally even in contested environments.[1]

The contract requires SpaceX to deliver a fully operational prototype by the end of 2027, and the effort is to work alongside the [[space-development-agency|Space Development Agency]]'s Transport Layer program, another military satellite network already under development, with both systems expected to function together as part of a larger War Department communications architecture.[1] Space Force officials framed the award as part of a broader push to speed up military space acquisitions through rapid prototyping and commercial partnerships while expanding the industrial base supporting US national security space programs.[1]

## Interpretation in Badlands commentary

Writing in the Badlands Brief, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the contract is the latest confirmation of a long-running pattern in which the domestic alliance between [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Musk is expressed through contracts, integrations, and expanding partnerships between seemingly unrelated entities.[1] In that reading, secure, high-speed, resilient data movement in contested environments ties directly to the decentralization and autonomy themes running through Musk's portfolio while serving the reorientation of American defense priorities under Trump's [[pax-americana|Pax Americana]] doctrine.[1] Burning Bright further contended that speculation about an eventual consolidation between SpaceX and Tesla is not merely financial rumor but the logical extension of synergies compounding across a decade of cross-company integration.[1]

## Starship Flight 11 (October 2025)

On Monday, October 13, 2025, SpaceX launched the eleventh test flight of a full-scale Starship from the southern tip of Texas, flying halfway around the world and releasing mock satellites as on the previous mission.[2] The booster peeled away and made a controlled entry into the Gulf of Mexico as planned, while the spacecraft skimmed space before descending into the Indian Ocean; nothing was recovered.[2] "Hey, welcome back to Earth, Starship," SpaceX's Dan Huot announced as employees cheered.[2]

Starship, described as the biggest and most powerful rocket ever built, is intended by founder and CEO [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] to carry people to Mars.[2] [[nasa|NASA]]'s need is more immediate: the agency cannot land astronauts on the Moon by the end of the decade without the 403-foot (123-metre) reusable vehicle, which is meant to ferry crews from lunar orbit to the surface and back.[2]

## Dragon *Freedom* splashdown (March 2025)

On Tuesday, March 18, 2025 at 5:57 pm EDT, the SpaceX Dragon capsule *Freedom* splashed down off the coast of Tallahassee, Florida, returning [[nasa|NASA]] astronauts [[sunita-williams|Sunita Williams]] and [[butch-wilmore|Butch Wilmore]] to Earth after an eight-day stopover on the International Space Station had stretched into a nine-and-a-half-month mission.[3] Also aboard were NASA astronaut Nick Hague and Roscosmos cosmonaut Aleksandr Gorbunov.[3]

Conditions at splashdown were described as idyllic, with 78 degree Fahrenheit weather and a pod of dolphins swimming around the capsule as ground crews prepared to open it; the capsule was lifted from the water onto a recovery ship, where the astronauts took their first breath of fresh air in over nine months.[3]

## Crew-11 mission (July 2025)

[[nasa|NASA]] announced coverage plans in July 2025 for the agency's SpaceX Crew-11 mission to the International Space Station, with liftoff targeted for 12:09 p.m. EDT on Thursday, July 31, 2025, from Launch Complex 39A at Kennedy Space Center in Florida.[4] Docking with the station was targeted for approximately 3 a.m. on Saturday, August 2, and NASA said launch coverage would be streamed on NASA+, Netflix, Amazon Prime and other platforms.[4]

## FCC subsidy denial, December 2023

The Federal Communications Commission reaffirmed on December 12, 2023 its refusal to award SpaceX's Starlink unit $885.5 million in rural broadband subsidies, rejecting the company's challenge to the 2022 rescission of the funding.[5] Notably, the commission cited SpaceX's failure to successfully launch Starship, reasoning that "the uncertain nature of Starship's future launches could impact Starlink's ability to meet" its service obligations.[5] SpaceX responded that it was "deeply disappointed and perplexed," arguing that Starlink "is demonstrably one of the best options - likely the best option" for achieving the rural internet program's goals.[5] Dissenting Republican commissioners suggested the ruling reflected the Biden administration's hostility toward Elon Musk rather than a neutral technical judgment.[5]

## The Crimea Starlink refusal controversy

In September 2023 SpaceX was at the center of an international dispute after it emerged that [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] had earlier declined to extend [[starlink|Starlink]] coverage enabling a Ukrainian drone attack on the Russian fleet at anchor in Crimea.[6] Musk wrote that "the obvious intent being to sink most of the Russian fleet at anchor," and that "if I had agreed to their request, then SpaceX would be explicitly complicit in a major act of war and conflict escalation."[6] Kyiv's response, delivered by presidential aide [[mykhailo-podolyak|Mykhailo Podolyak]], accused Musk of enabling evil, and some commentators went so far as to call for the U.S. government to nationalize the SpaceX systems serving Ukraine.[6]
