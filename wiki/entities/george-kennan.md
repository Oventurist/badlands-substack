# George Kennan

George Kennan was an American journalist described in Badlands Media coverage as an asset of [[jacob-schiff|Jacob Schiff]] who played an instrumental role as perception manager of the Russian revolution.[1] He boasted of having converted 52,000 Russian soldiers imprisoned in Japan into Bolshevik revolutionaries.[1]

## The 1917 New York Times account

An interview recorded in the *New York Times* on March 24, 1917 described Kennan's work with the Friends of Russian Freedom. Kennan said that during the [[russo-japanese-war|Russo-Japanese War]] he was in Tokyo and was permitted to visit the 12,000 Russian prisoners held by Japan at the end of the war's first year, where he conceived the idea of putting revolutionary propaganda into the hands of the Russian army.[1] Japanese authorities favoured the plan and granted permission, after which he sent to America for all the Russian revolutionary literature available.[1]

Kennan stated that "the movement was financed by a New York banker you all know and love" — a reference to Schiff — and that "soon we received a ton and a half of Russian revolutionary propaganda."[1] He concluded that at the end of the war "50,000 Russian officers and men went back to their country ardent revolutionists," and that "the Friends of Russian Freedom had sowed 50,000 seeds of liberty in 100 Russian regiments."[1]
