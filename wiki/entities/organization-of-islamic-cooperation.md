# Organization of Islamic Cooperation

The Organization of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) is a 57-nation intergovernmental body representing Muslim-majority states in international affairs.[1]

## Role in the Huckabee controversy (February 2026)

In February 2026 the OIC was among the regional bodies that publicly condemned remarks by US Ambassador to Israel [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]], who told [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] that [[israel|Israel]] had a biblical right to territory "from the Nile to the Euphrates" and that "it would be fine if they took it all."[1] The OIC joined [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], Egypt, Jordan, other Arab and Muslim governments, and the League of Arab States ([[arab-league|Arab League]]) in condemning the statement.[1]

Badlands commentary emphasized that the joint statement circulated after Huckabee's remarks was co-signed by states drawn from NATO, BRICS, the European Union, the Arab League, the 57-nation OIC, and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization — an unusually broad cross-bloc alignment.[1]

## Response to Netanyahu at the 2024 UN General Assembly

At the [[united-nations-general-assembly]] in late September 2024, after [[benjamin-netanyahu]] displayed a map of his "Arab partners" under the [[abraham-accords]] and pledged to confront [[iran]] with their support, a group of foreign ministers claiming to speak for all fifty-seven OIC nations took the stage to contest that framing.[2] They expressed indignation and pleaded with the assembly to press Israel on its "endgame" — where the violence would end — with Saudi foreign minister [[faisal-bin-farhan]] among them.[2] Badlands commentary treated the exchange as evidence for [[douglas-macgregor|Colonel Douglas Macgregor's]] argument that the Abraham Accords were effectively dead despite Arab states having kept Israel provisioned and intercepted incoming missiles.[2]

## Doha emergency summit (September 2025)

The Organization of Islamic Cooperation convened an emergency summit in Doha in September 2025 together with the [[arab-league|League of Arab States]], in response to an Israeli airstrike on the Qatari capital that killed six people, including a Qatari security officer.[3] The joint final statement called on member nations to weigh the compatibility of [[israel|Israel]]'s [[united-nations|United Nations]] membership with the organization's charter and to coordinate efforts toward its suspension.[3] The leaders described the strike as a "dangerous escalation that exposes the extremist hostility of the Israeli government" and urged all states to review diplomatic and economic relations with Israel and adopt sanctions and arms and dual-use export suspensions.[3]
