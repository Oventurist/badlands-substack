# Steve Scalise

Steve Scalise is the Republican Majority Leader of the United States [[house-of-representatives|House of Representatives]], serving in the chamber's leadership alongside Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]].[1]

## June–July 2026 floor collapse

In late June 2026 Scalise was directly involved in leadership's failed attempt to contain a revolt by a small group of GOP hard-liners that froze most House floor business. The procedural rule needed to advance major legislation failed 224-198, with 14 Republicans joining Democrats in opposition, extending a week-long impasse rooted in frustration over the stalled [[save-america-act|SAVE America Act]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s top legislative priority on elections.[1]

Scalise and Johnson had planned to spend the following day-and-a-half resolving the disputes, but instead opted to cancel scheduled votes and send members home early for the July 4 recess.[1] The cancellation shelved work on the annual [[pentagon|Pentagon]] policy bill, [[state-department|State Department]] appropriations, and other pending measures.[1]

## Tabling the May 2024 motion to vacate

As House Majority Whip, Scalise moved on 8 May 2024 to table [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]]'s [[may-2024-motion-to-vacate-against-speaker-mike-johnson|motion to vacate]] against Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]].[2] Democrats joined Republicans in approving the shelving by a vote of 359 to 43, with 11 Republicans voting to proceed with the ouster attempt.[2]

## 2023 Speaker nomination

Scalise was the first Republican nominee for Speaker of the House after the removal of [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] in October 2023, but like [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] after him he failed to garner the support needed to win the gavel.[3] [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]] became the third nominee before withdrawing hours later, and [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] — the runner-up to Emmer — was elected with 220 votes on the first floor ballot, ending a three-week leadership void.[3]

## Withdrawal from the 2023 speakership race

### Nomination, October 11, 2023

Republicans voted on Wednesday, October 11, 2023 to nominate Scalise, then Majority Leader, as the next Speaker of the House, though his path to the gavel remained uncertain because he still had to secure the votes of nearly every member of his party.[6] He defeated Judiciary Committee Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] 113–99 in a secret ballot behind closed doors, having for years been viewed as the heir apparent to former Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]], who had been ousted in an internal GOP revolt a week earlier.[6] Because of the party's razor-thin majority, just five Republicans held the power to block him, meaning he needed at least 217 of 221 Republicans on the floor.[6]

"Obviously, we still have work to do. We're going to have to go upstairs on the House floor and resolve this and then get the House opened again," Scalise told reporters after the vote, adding, "We see how dangerous of a world it is and how things can change so quickly. We need to make sure we are sending a message to people all throughout the world that the House is open and doing the people's business."[6] He said the first action under a "Speaker Scalise" would be to pass a resolution in support of [[israel|Israel]].[6] Uncertainty hung over the conference immediately after the vote, with Rep. [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]] declaring "I'm voting for Jim Jordan on the floor."[6]

Scalise withdrew from consideration for the speakership on Thursday, October 12, 2023, after hard-line Republicans declined to rally behind the conference's chosen candidate, leaving the House leaderless.[4] Nominated the previous day in a closed-door secret ballot by a margin of 113–99, the Louisiana Republican — then the party's No. 2 leader — found himself far short of the 217 votes needed on the House floor, as supporters of his challenger [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]], endorsed by former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], refused to switch allegiance.[4] With 221 House Republicans, a Republican speaker could spare only four dissenters, and [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] reported that more than twenty had withdrawn support.[4]

"I just shared with my colleagues that I was withdrawing my name as a candidate for speaker-designee," Scalise said, adding that the conference "still has to come together, and it's not there. There are still some people that have their own agendas."[4] His abrupt exit returned Republicans to square one amid recriminations over the disarray.[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that all unofficial online polling pointed to Jordan as the grassroots choice, though Jordan had told reporters he was no longer whipping votes for himself after Scalise's selection, and recalled [[brian-lupo|Brian Lupo]]'s prediction that Republicans would ultimately reinstall [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]].[4] Commentators treated the vacancy as the basis of the [[congressional-paralysis|Congressional Paralysis]] thesis.[4]

Scalise nevertheless drew seven protest votes — the largest bloc among the twenty Republican defections — in the first ballot of [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]]'s speakership bid on October 17, 2023, ahead of [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s six and [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]]'s three.[5]

## Response to the Trump target letter

House Majority Leader Steve Scalise joined other Republican leaders in defending [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] after the July 2023 target letter from Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]], noting that the news broke as the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] prepared to hear testimony from two IRS whistleblowers who alleged that the [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] investigation had been stonewalled by prosecutors.[7] At a House GOP conference press conference he asked, "Now you see the Biden administration going after President Trump once again, it begs that question — is there a double standard? Is justice being administered equally?"[7]

## Confirmation of the December 2024 spending bill's collapse

As House Majority Leader, Scalise confirmed on Wednesday, December 18, 2024 that Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]]'s stopgap spending bill was dead after a Republican revolt over billions in added spending.[8] "There's no new agreement right now," Scalise said, with lawmakers facing a Friday deadline to avert a government shutdown in the [[2024-december-continuing-resolution-fight|December 2024 continuing resolution fight]].[8]

## September 2023 floor schedule

As House Majority Leader during the [[september-2023-government-shutdown-standoff|September 2023 government shutdown standoff]], Scalise told members on a Sunday-night conference call that the chamber would vote Wednesday on the Defense appropriations bill and Thursday on the [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]], with the Rules Committee marking up the CR on Monday.[9]
