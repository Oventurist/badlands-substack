# Aleksandar Vucic

Aleksandar Vučić is the President of Serbia, whose government faced months of swelling anti-government demonstrations that reached a peak in Belgrade in March 2025.[1]

## 2024–2025 protest wave

Tens of thousands of protesters poured into the central streets of the Serbian capital on Saturday, March 15, 2025, from all directions in "an almost unbroken tide of umbrellas and placards," defying relentless rain and unseasonably humid March air to march in demonstrations that had been building for months and that threatened Vučić's position.[1] Dinko Gruhonjić, a university professor from Novi Sad and one of the early supporters of the protests, told POLITICO that "fear has changed sides in Serbia," adding that average citizens were no longer afraid and now saw fear in the eyes of the government.[1]

The protests began the previous November after the canopy of the main railway station in Novi Sad, the center of Serbia's northern region, collapsed onto bystanders and killed 14 people on the spot, including children aged 9 and 5; another victim later died of injuries and several others underwent amputations.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the situation as one where it is hard to say which side is which, noting that Vučić is billed as a populist leader but that the label means little, and that mainstream outlets had branded him a ruthless autocrat.[1] He raised the possibility that the station awning collapse functioning as the catalyzing event could have been a false flag operation, and pointed to Vučić's accusations that foreign-funded NGOs had started the revolution, drawing parallels to campaigns launched against Robert Fico in Slovakia and Irakli Kobakhidze in Georgia.[1] Most significantly in that reading, Vučić had been calling for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to force a peace deal in Ukraine since before Trump won the election, suggesting to the commentator that the unrest was an operation designed to remove him from office — even while a worldwide crackdown on corruption would be welcome.[1]
