# Keith Ellison

Keith Ellison is the Democratic Attorney General of Minnesota.[1]

## Whistleblower allegations

In the course of the January 2026 congressional investigations into welfare fraud involving Minnesota's Somali immigrant community, whistleblowers including Minnesota state workers provided affidavits claiming they had warned Ellison and Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] about the issues, but that no action was taken.[1] Both officials deny the allegations.[1] The investigations, led by [[james-comer|James Comer]], [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] and [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]], estimated that up to half of $18–20 billion in federally funded welfare programs had been lost to fraud, including through the [[feeding-our-future|Feeding Our Future]] child nutrition scheme.[1]

## Lawsuit against DHS over Operation Metro Surge

On January 12, 2026, Ellison, as Minnesota Attorney General, joined the cities of [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]] and [[saint-paul|Saint Paul]] in filing a federal lawsuit (Case No. 0:26-cv-00190) against the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] and federal officials over [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]].[2] Defendants included DHS Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] as well as [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] and [[customs-and-border-protection|CBP]] officials, and the complaint alleged that the operation and its deployment of armed agents violated constitutional rights, seeking declaratory relief, a temporary restraining order and an injunction against certain enforcement tactics.[2]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Ellison's suit came on the heels of grand jury indictments concerning the Minnesota government's alleged enablement of foreign criminal networks, and asked pointedly whether state officials were "trying to sue their way out of being indicted."[2]

## Statements on Antifa (October 2025)

In October 2025 Ellison claimed that "nobody" knows what [[antifa|Antifa]] is and that it does not exist as an organization, despite having promoted an Antifa "handbook" in a 2018 social media post.[3] Reacting on MSNBC to remarks by U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi and Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem at a [[white-house|White House]] roundtable on political violence, he said, "There really is no Antifa as an institutional organization… Nobody even knows what it is."[3] He went on to claim that "the first person I ever heard use the word Antifa was Donald J. Trump when he was going on about Charlottesville."[3] A since-deleted X post made by Ellison in 2018, while he was a member of the U.S. House of Representatives, showed him holding *Antifa: The Anti-Fascist Handbook* by Rutgers University "antifascist historian" Mark Bray, with a caption stating that the book would "strike fear in the heart" of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[3]
