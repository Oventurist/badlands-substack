# G20

The G20 is the annual summit forum bringing together the leaders of the world's top economies. Badlands Media treated the September 2023 G20 summit in India as a case study in the declining authority of established instruments of global governance.[1]

## 2023 New Delhi summit and Xi's absence

Following several days of widespread reports that Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] planned to skip the summit, [[joe-biden|President Joe Biden]] told reporters during a trip to Delaware that "I am disappointed, but I am going to see him."[1] Biden offered no timeline for a future meeting, and his remarks constituted the highest-level confirmation from a G20 country leader that the Chinese leader would not attend.[1]

The absence unfolded against a backdrop of escalating rhetoric. China's foreign ministry accused the United States of comprehensively "containing" China through wars of tariffs, trade, tech, chips and rules, with spokesman [[wang-wenbin|Wang Wenbin]] saying "What the US is doing is not competition but enforcing its zero-sum cold war mindset" and warning that suppression in the name of competition "will only push two countries towards confrontations and divide the world with the new cold war."[1] Biden officials nonetheless sounded a note of optimism that tensions would not spiral further amid tit-for-tat export controls affecting technology.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the snub as depicting "a new world order, emerging as decades of America Last policies rapidly arrive at their inevitable ends."[1] She linked the summit's diminished standing to the failure of Commerce Secretary [[gina-raimondo|Gina Raimondo]]'s recent mission to Beijing, the anti-western realignment of Africa, the rise of [[brics|BRICS]], Russia and [[wagner-group|Wagner]], and a wave of coups d'état.[1] In her framing, Xi was "snubbing the G20 as if to signal that the old, established instruments of global control aren't worth his time. Maybe they aren't."[1] She concluded that the accelerating pace of disclosure across pandemic, election and geopolitical fronts favored public awakening, with those who see through the operations comprising "two-thirds of the nation and growing."[1]

## New Delhi summit and the EU's Africa push (September 2023)

Ahead of the New Delhi summit, both [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] confirmed they would stay away, with China's new premier [[li-qiang|Li Qiang]] attending in Xi's place and Foreign Minister [[sergei-lavrov|Sergei Lavrov]] representing Russia — absences that reportedly relieved Western heads of state, since sensitive agenda items could more easily reach consensus without them.[2] [[european-union|EU]] officials were expected to endorse the [[african-union|African Union]]'s bid to become a permanent member of the G20 while presenting Europe as the more stable partner for African investment and engagement, a goal Bloomberg reported would drive a high-level meeting with African leaders on the summit's sidelines.[2] The overture came at a sensitive moment for Europe following the coup in Niger and a run of junta governments voicing anti-colonial sentiment aimed particularly at France.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the episode as part of a continuing tug of war for Africa, calling the continent "the jewel of the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] to the [[new-world-order|New World Order]]" and the EU's outreach an afterthought next to the groundwork already laid by China and Russia; she predicted African peoples would reclaim their sovereignty from "the two terrible suitors."[2]
