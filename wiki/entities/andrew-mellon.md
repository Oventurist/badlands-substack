# Andrew Mellon

Andrew Mellon was an American financier who served as Secretary of the Treasury during the 1920s.[1] In Badlands Media's account of the manufactured collapse of 1929, Mellon — alongside "JP Morgan's President Coolidge" — deregulated the banks, imposed austerity on the country, and "cooked up a scheme for Broker loans," allowing speculators to borrow 90 percent of the value of their stock.[1]

Under this regime Wall Street was deregulated, investment into the real economy was halted through the 1920s, and speculative "insanity became the norm."[1] Broker loans totaled $1.5 billion in 1925, grew to $2.6 billion in 1926, and reached $5.7 billion by the end of 1927; by 1928 the stock market was overvalued fourfold.[1] The bubbles of that decade are described as having been unleashed by the early death of President [[warren-harding|William Harding]] in 1923.[1] When the bubble was sufficiently inflated, a coordinated mass "calling in" of the broker loans triggered the [[stock-market-crash-of-1929|1929 crash]].[1]
