# Yale University

Yale University is a private university in New Haven, Connecticut, described by Badlands commentators as the headquarters of the Skull and Bones society.[1]

## April 2025 Beinecke Plaza encampment

On the night of 22 April 2025 pro-Palestinian protesters erected eight tents on Beinecke Plaza to protest an upcoming talk near the campus by Israeli national security minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]].[1] A crowd of about 100 began forming a circle on the plaza around 8 p.m.; by 9:30 p.m. the tents were up and the crowd had grown to roughly 200, and an organizer announced by megaphone, "We're here, and we're staying the night."[1] Shortly before 11:30 p.m. organizers announced that the encampment would disband, citing threats of "retribution" from administrators, and urged students to rally at a further protest the following day.[1] A university spokesperson said the protest was not affiliated with any recognized student organization and that administrators had issued final warnings to disperse at 11:00 p.m.[1]

## Context and commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] linked the protest to reports of a possible endowment adjustment at Yale and confirmed cuts to federal funding, including National Institutes of Health support for public health equity research.[1] She characterized the episode acidly, noting that "the most privileged offspring of the most privileged people in the world" ended their "overnight" encampment at 11 p.m., and paraphrased the student newspaper's headline as: "We made some demands and they said no. Then they gave us a bedtime and we left. It was promising." — concluding that "it's almost like the resistance does as they're told."[1]

## Intelligence recruitment and the OSS

Yale's prestigious faculty and alumni, steeped in a culture of privilege and intellect, were naturally drawn into the [[office-of-strategic-services|Office of Strategic Services]] during World War II, and the university was used not only for recruitment but as a middleman in operations abroad.[2] One of the earliest prototypes of the scholar-to-spook pipeline was [[norman-holmes-pearson|Norman Holmes Pearson]], a Yale literature professor who led counterintelligence efforts in London and later helped recruit [[james-jesus-angleton|James Jesus Angleton]] at the outset of the Cold War.[2]

After declining a post at the [[state-department|State Department]], Pearson co-founded and headed Yale's American Studies program, created to foster a cohesive understanding of American identity during the ideological competition with the Soviet Union.[2] The historian [[robin-winks|Robin Winks]], in *Cloak and Gown: Scholars in the Secret War*, argues that Pearson used the program as a recruiting tool for U.S. intelligence agencies, particularly the newly formed [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]].[2]

In the present day Yale is cited alongside [[arizona-state-university|Arizona State University]] and Cambridge as an institution hosting [[pentagon|Pentagon]]-, State Department- or CIA-funded programs that analyze and target "disinformation."[2]

## Role in the Ivy Dominion thesis

The Badlands Media essay "The Ivy Dominion" assigns Yale the role of covert recruitment center within an alleged supranational network of [[ivy-league|Ivy League]] institutions, claiming that its deep ties to [[skull-and-bones|Skull and Bones]] and the intelligence community groom covert operatives, "economic hitmen" and globalist state actors.[3] The article states that Yale supplies CIA recruits, [[state-department|State Department]] officials and transnational deal-brokers, and describes its influence as "global, silent, and surgical."[3]

Skull & Bones is called "the ur-club" and a direct pipeline to the CIA and global economic networks, with alumni including [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]], [[john-kerry|John Kerry]] and [[austan-goolsbee|Austan Goolsbee]]; [[scroll-and-key|Scroll & Key]] is said to perform similar functions for strategic intelligence work.[3] The article traces a three-step relay from Bones to the CIA, from the CIA to the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]], and onward to the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]], [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]] and [[world-bank|World Bank]].[3] It cites George H.W. Bush's service as CIA Director before the presidency and [[william-bundy|William Bundy]]'s role in Vietnam-era psychological operations as evidence that "Bones is not a club, it is a covert network."[3]

In the essay's collapse narrative, Yale is described as more insulated than its peers but facing increasing pressure from its association with intelligence agencies and the resurfacing of Skull and Bones affiliations in podcasts, documentaries and lawsuits tied to Cold War-era trauma programs.[3]

## Founding and the East India connection

"The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 8" traces Yale's beginnings to a would-be charter called "An Act for Liberty to Erect a Collegiate School," passed at a meeting in New Haven by the General Court of the Colony of Connecticut on 9 October 1701.[4] In 1718 several men involved with the college approached the Boston-born businessman [[elihu-yale|Elihu Yale]] for financial help in constructing a new building, while Isaac Newton — alleged in the article to have been grand master of the Order of Sion at the time — donated books and money.[4] Ryan DeLarme asserts that both men had ties to the secret societies operating behind the [[british-east-india-company|East India Company]], Elihu Yale having risen from clerk to President of the Company's settlement at Fort St George, Madras before losing the post on corruption charges, and concludes that "from its very inception, Yale had connections to the moving forces of the world."[4]

## Recruitment and grooming thesis

The article presents Yale as the best-documented site of the deep state's recruitment and grooming process, noting that as of 2020 the university had graduated five U.S. presidents, ten Founding Fathers, nineteen Supreme Court justices, thirty-one living billionaires and fifty-four college founders and presidents, along with hundreds of members of Congress, many heads of state, cabinet members, governors and diplomats, 78 MacArthur Fellows, 263 Rhodes Scholars, 123 Marshall Scholars, 102 Guggenheim Fellows and nine Mitchell Scholars.[4] DeLarme adds a disclaimer that graduating from Yale is not itself a damning indictment, while maintaining that a subversive recruitment and grooming process takes place behind closed doors and that the names involved have been implicated in everything from war crimes to depopulation.[4] Yale's three pre-eminent senior societies are named as [[wolfs-head|Wolf's Head]], [[scroll-and-key|Scroll and Key]] and [[skull-and-bones|Skull and Bones]], the last incorporated in 1856 as the [[russell-trust-association|Russell Trust Association]] and, according to the article, funded from the Far East opium trade.[4]
