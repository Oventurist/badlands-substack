# Carl Gershman

Carl Gershman is a longtime American foreign-policy figure who served as president of the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] (NED), and who is described in Badlands Media coverage as a U.S. Deep State actor central to the postwar "democracy promotion" apparatus.[1]

## Role in the NED's founding rationale

Gershman is frequently quoted for a candid 1986 explanation, given to the *New York Times*, of why the NED was created as an ostensibly private body rather than an arm of the intelligence community: "It would be terrible for democratic groups around the world to be seen as subsidized by the C.I.A. We saw that in the 60's, and that's why it has been discontinued. We have not had the capability of doing this, and that's why the endowment was created."[1] The remark is treated by critics as an admission that the endowment was designed to perform, openly and with political cover, work that the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] had previously conducted covertly.[1]

## Opposition to Donald Trump

Critics argue that the NED, originally bipartisan in presentation, grew increasingly partisan after the 2016 election, and Gershman is cited as a leading example.[1] He published a 2016 *Washington Post* article suggesting that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had collaborated with Vladimir Putin to win the presidency, and later characterized Trump's victory as evidence that American democracy was "broken."[1] These statements are presented in Badlands coverage as part of a broader pattern of hostility from NED-affiliated figures toward Trump and the national-populist movements he is said to have inspired.[1]
