# Michael Dreeben

Michael Dreeben is an American appellate lawyer who argued on behalf of the Department of Justice before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] in the April 2024 oral argument over whether a former president enjoys criminal immunity for official acts.[1] He appeared opposite D. John Sauer, who argued for former [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]].[1]

## The April 25, 2024 immunity argument

Dreeben carried the government's side of the question of [[presidential-immunity|presidential immunity]] in the prosecution brought by special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] over Trump's efforts to contest the 2020 election.[1] Much of the more than two and a half hours of argument turned on the distinction between "official" and "private" acts, and on whether a sitting or former president can be criminally prosecuted at all.[1] Both the justices and the attorneys converged on the proposition that a president enjoys no immunity for private criminal conduct; the disputed question was the treatment of official acts.[1]

Badlands Media commentary characterized the case law and binding precedent in this area as sparse, attributing that sparseness to a long-respected understanding of immunity that, in the writer's view, was disrupted only when the Biden administration sought to prosecute a political opponent during an election year.[1] The same commentary noted that the justices signaled a result that could require lower courts to spend months sorting which alleged acts were sufficiently official to be shielded — an outcome that would make a pre-election trial nearly impossible even if Dreeben's side ultimately prevailed on the law.[1]
