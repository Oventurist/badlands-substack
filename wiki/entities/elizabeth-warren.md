# Elizabeth Warren

Elizabeth Warren is a Democratic United States Senator from Massachusetts, identified in Badlands Media coverage as the leading legislative proponent of a federal wealth tax on high-net-worth American households.[1]

## Ultra-Millionaire Tax Act

On Tuesday, March 19, 2024, Warren reintroduced her [[ultra-millionaire-tax-act|'Ultra-Millionaire' Tax Act]], legislation under which the federal government would levy 2 percent annually on households with net worth between $50 million and $1 billion, and 3 percent on households worth more than $1 billion.[1] Representatives [[pramila-jayapal|Pramila Jayapal]] (D-WA) and [[brendan-boyle|Brendan Boyle]] (D-PA) introduced a companion measure in the House of Representatives.[1]

Warren defended the proposal in populist terms, stating: "As President Biden says, no one thinks it's fair that Jeff Bezos gets enough tax loopholes that he pays at a lower rate than a public school teacher. All my bill is asking is that when you make it big, bigger than $50 million dollars, then on that next dollar, you pitch in two cents, so everyone else can have a chance."[1]

## Criticism

Warren maintains that the bill would reach only the wealthiest roughly 100,000 households in the United States — about 0.05 percent of the population.[1] Critics quoted by [[zerohedge|Zero Hedge]] countered that Democrats "routinely lie to sell voters on their plans," and argued that normalizing the confiscation of assets at the top of the wealth distribution would open the door to applying similar levies to lower wealth brackets over time.[1] Badlands Media framed the measure as an effort by Warren "and socialist pals" to normalize asset confiscation as a tool of federal policy.[1]

## Origins of the CFPB

Badlands commentary accompanying President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s February 2025 firing of [[rohit-chopra|Rohit Chopra]] noted that the [[consumer-financial-protection-bureau|Consumer Financial Protection Bureau]] was Warren's brainchild, and that the agency is funded not by Congress but by the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[2]

## Pentagon AI contracting letter (May 2025)

On May 28, 2025, Warren, a senior member of the Senate Armed Services Committee, wrote to Secretary of Defense [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] urging the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] to ensure competitive artificial-intelligence contracting.[3] The letter followed reporting that [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[doge|DOGE]] team was expanding use of the [[grok|Grok]] chatbot to analyze federal data, potentially violating conflict-of-interest laws and putting sensitive information on millions of Americans at risk.[3] "I seek to ensure that the DoD's procurement decisions encourage competition and avoid consolidation that can lead to higher prices, concentration of risk, and the stifling of innovation," Warren wrote.[3] Her office asked the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] to respond by June 11 on its AI acquisition practices, how it would avoid lock-in with a single contractor, and its safeguards for data collection, asking: "How does DoD plan to ensure government data is not used to illegally train commercially available AI algorithms?"[3]

## "Anti-crypto army"

Warren is a vocal critic of cryptocurrency who has pushed to clamp down on the nascent industry.[4] In a re-election advertisement posted to X in March 2023 she said she was building an "anti-crypto army," a phrase the [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaign seized on in May 2024 when it announced it would begin accepting cryptocurrency donations through Coinbase Commerce.[4] The campaign cast Warren as a surrogate for President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and framed her stance as an attack on "Americans' right to make their own financial choices," declaring that "MAGA supporters, now with a new cryptocurrency option, will build a crypto army moving the campaign to victory on November 5th!"[4]

## Bitcoin criticism

A January 2024 Badlands Brief essay on the fifteenth anniversary of the [[bitcoin-genesis-block|Bitcoin Genesis Block]] cited Warren — referred to derisively as "Senator Pocahontas" — together with [[jamie-dimon|Jamie Dimon]] as examples of figures for whom [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] "lives rent-free" in the mind because it cannot be stopped and steadily erodes what the author called their abstract power hierarchy.[5] The essay grouped both under the adage that "those who scream the loudest" reveal the most about what threatens them.[5]

## 2020 primary and the DNA test episode

During the [[2020-democratic-presidential-primary|2020 Democratic presidential primary]], Warren surged in popularity and fundraising — particularly through small donations — at a point when [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was slipping, but faced significant doubt among Democratic voters that she could defeat [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[6] [[npr|NPR]] reporting cited by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] framed her rise around precisely that electability question.[6]

Contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] attributed much of the doubt to Trump, who had pinned the nickname "Pocahontas" on Warren over her claim of partial Native American ancestry — a claim Lange said was made to secure a position at Harvard Law School when the school sought to hire more minorities.[6] At a Montana rally Trump challenged her to take a DNA test; the challenge dominated the news cycle, Warren took the test, and the result showing she was only 1/1024th Native American drew nationwide mockery.[6] The Republican Party followed with political advertising, a talking-points memo and a talk-show push around the line "1/1024th Native American, 100% Liar," damage Lange described as serious and lasting — though he noted Biden continued losing ground to her and other candidates even afterward.[6] See [[elizabeth-warren-dna-test|Elizabeth Warren DNA test episode]].
