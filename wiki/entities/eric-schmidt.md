# Eric Schmidt

Eric Schmidt is the former chief executive officer of [[google|Google]] and, as of 2023, the chair of the [[bilderberg|Bilderberg Group]] — a combination that Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] treated as emblematic of the fusion between Silicon Valley and transnational policy-setting bodies.[1] DeLarme argued that Schmidt "seems to be filling the vacant role of the classic 'baddies' that conspiracy researchers of yesteryear spent their lives exposing," placing him in a lineage with David Rockefeller, [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]], Zbigniew Brzezinski and Herman Kahn.[1]

## Government role and the AI Commission

Citing a February 2021 report by The Last American Vagabond, DeLarme wrote that the Biden administration assigned Schmidt to head the [[national-security-commission-on-artificial-intelligence|National Security Commission on Artificial Intelligence]], commonly referred to as the "A.I. Commission."[1] A December 2022 Politico report cited in the article stated that Schmidt was funding the salaries of more than two dozen employees inside the Biden administration, and earlier Politico reporting from March 2022 described Schmidt's "fingerprints" as being "all over" the administration's science and technology apparatus.[1]

DeLarme used this record to rebut the claim that the Bilderberg Group has become a spent force: "the industriousness of Eric Schmidt as detailed above suggests that the Group still has its tendrils in the various halls of power, whether it be the board room or the Oval Office."[1]

## Relationship with Henry Kissinger

Schmidt's association with [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] is central to the article's argument. Kissinger has said his interest in artificial intelligence began after Schmidt persuaded him to attend a lecture on the subject at the 2016 Bilderberg conference.[1] The two men co-authored the 2021 book *The Age of AI: And Our Human Future*.[1] DeLarme concluded that Schmidt's combined roles — former Google CEO, Bilderberg member and chair, Biden administration financier, and Kissinger collaborator — were "more than enough reasons to be concerned with the direction the A.I. conversation is headed."[1]

## Alleged CIA computer installations abroad (Badlands analysis)

Badlands contributor Joe Lange identifies Eric Schmidt, cofounder of [[google|Google]] — which he describes as a company created as a CIA spy operation — as one of two Americans shown in [[qanon|Q]] drop imagery assisting [[north-korea|North Korea]] with its computer systems, alongside [[bill-richardson|Bill Richardson]].[3] Lange asks what kind of system the founder of Google would install in North Korea: one to censor and control the population, one to enable hacking of Western businesses and governments, or one built to serve the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] directly.[3] He argues Schmidt helped establish CIA [[snow-white-supercomputers|supercomputer systems]] in both North Korea, where three machines operated, and [[armenia|Armenia]], where four unique computer identifiers appear in the Q material.[3] Given Google's alleged status as a CIA-controlled company, Lange writes, Schmidt's involvement in building machines used for the narrative war should surprise no one.[3]

## The superyacht auction and the North Korea photograph

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] opened his July 2023 essay "Does the CIA Still Control Iran?" with the news that a confiscated superyacht belonging to a Russian oligarch — which Carlson prefers to call stolen — had been auctioned for $67.6 million, and that the winning bidder was Eric Schmidt.[2] He identified Schmidt as the former CEO and later executive chairman of [[google|Google]] and subsequently of Alphabet, with a net worth estimated at around $25 billion, and noted that Schmidt announced he would step down from his position on 21 December 2017.[2]

Carlson attached significance to that date: [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had issued [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]], blocking the property of persons involved in serious human rights abuse, on 20 December 2017 — literally the day before, which Carlson called "kind of suspicious."[2] He also recalled the photograph of Schmidt circulated in the Q drops showing him standing over North Korean military personnel busy at their computers, asking what Schmidt would be doing in a country foreigners were said to be barred from, and reading the image as implying that Schmidt and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] were training the North Koreans and that the agency in fact controlled [[north-korea|North Korea]].[2]
