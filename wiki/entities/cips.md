# CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System)

CIPS, the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System, is China's clearing and settlement network for cross-border transactions denominated in yuan, and is described in Badlands Media coverage as Beijing's alternative to [[swift|SWIFT]].[1]

## Background and function

Backed by the [[peoples-bank-of-china|People's Bank of China]], CIPS was launched in 2015 to internationalise the use of the yuan.[1] It allows global banks to clear cross-border yuan transactions directly onshore rather than routing them through clearing banks in offshore yuan hubs.[1]

## Scale of use

CIPS processed roughly 80 trillion yuan (about $12.68 trillion) in 2021, a 75 percent increase over the prior year according to the state-backed Jiefang Daily.[1] As of the end of January of the following year, CIPS reported that about 1,280 financial institutions in 103 countries and regions had connected to the system, including 30 banks in Japan, 23 in Russia and 31 from African nations receiving yuan funds tied to infrastructure projects under the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]].[1]

## Significance

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that CIPS has achieved far wider international adoption than Russia's equivalent messaging system precisely because Belt and Road financing gives partner states a practical reason to settle in yuan, making it a central instrument of [[de-dollarization|de-dollarization]] and of the [[brics|BRICS]] effort to build a parallel financial architecture outside the reach of Western [[sanctions]].[1]
