# International Tin Supply Chain Initiative (ITSCI)

The **International Tin Supply Chain Initiative (ITSCI)** is a UN-connected mineral traceability and certification body operating in the Great Lakes region of Africa.[1]

## Allegations

A report published by the law firm [[amsterdam-and-partners|Amsterdam & Partners]] on behalf of the government of the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] accused major technology companies of using UN-connected organizations such as ITSCI and the Responsible Minerals Initiative to legitimize mineral supply chains through fraudulent certifications.[1]

According to the report's allegations, rare earth minerals were smuggled out of the Congo into neighbouring countries — primarily [[rwanda|Rwanda]] — where they could be laundered into the global supply chain as "clean," meaning certified as not having been mined with child slavery, a practice described as common in the deep jungles of the eastern Congo.[1] Companies named in the report included [[apple|Apple]], Intel, Sony, and Lockheed Martin.[1]

## Context

The certification question sits at the centre of a broader contest over Congolese mineral wealth, estimated in excess of $24 trillion, involving [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]]'s Rwanda, sanctioned mining figure [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]], and President [[felix-tshisekedi|Félix Tshisekedi]]'s effort to offer Western states a stake in the sector.[1]
