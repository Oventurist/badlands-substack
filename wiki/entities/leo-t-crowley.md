# Leo T. Crowley

Leo T. Crowley served as President Franklin Roosevelt's Alien Property Custodian during the Second World War.[1] On 17 November 1942 he signed Vesting Order Number 248, seizing the property of [[prescott-bush|Prescott Bush]] under the Trading with the Enemy Act.[1]

As recounted in *George Bush: The Unauthorized Biography* and quoted by Ryan DeLarme, the order was published in obscure government record books and kept out of the news; it explained nothing about the Nazis involved, stating only that the [[union-banking-corporation|Union Banking Corporation]] was run for the "Thyssen family" of "Germany and/or Hungary" — "nationals ... of a designated enemy country."[1] By deciding that Bush and his fellow directors were legally "front men for the Nazis," the government avoided the larger historical question of how [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]]'s Nazis had themselves been hired, armed and instructed by the New York and London clique of which Bush was an executive manager.[1] A five-paragraph *New York Times* item of 16 December 1944 mentioned only that the Union Banking Corporation had received authority to move its principal place of business from 39 Broadway to 120 Broadway, omitting both the seizure and the fact that 120 Broadway was the address of the Alien Property Custodian.[1]
