# Alaska-Alberta Railway Development Corporation

The **Alaska-Alberta Railway Development Corporation**, known as A2A, is the private company created in 2015 to advance the [[alaska-canada-railway|Alaska-Canada Railway]] after decades in which the Canadian federal government had committed to blocking the plan.[1] Its formation followed a 2015 feasibility study funded by the Province of [[alberta|Alberta]], which [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes as sitting on the edge of total economic despair under the weight of decarbonization initiatives being pushed into law by Ottawa technocrats; that study in turn followed a $6 million feasibility study undertaken by the Alaskan government in the year 2000.[1]

## Statements and objectives

A2A's chief executive, [[sean-mccoshen|Sean McCoshen]], described the undertaking as "a world-class infrastructure project that will generate more than 18,000 jobs for Canadian workers at a time when they are most needed, provide a new, more efficient route for trans-Pacific shipping and thereby link Alberta to world markets."[1] Ehret regards this concept of linking North America into the Pacific — a region increasingly shaped by China's [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] and the multipolar alliance — as vital.[1]

## Context

Both the Albertan and Alaskan governments had made several small efforts to encourage the plan over the years, but Ehret writes that very little headway occurred because of the monetarist rules underpinning globalization, which place supposedly "free markets" on the throne and nation states in the dungeon of economics.[1] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s September 26, 2020 announcement of federal support for the rail connection was, in his account, the decisive break with that pattern.[1]

## Alaska summit essay

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] repeated in his August 2025 Alaska summit essay that A2A was created in 2015 as a private initiative to advance the Alaska–Alberta rail plan after the federal government had committed to blocking it, and quoted CEO [[sean-mccoshen|Sean McCoshen]]'s claim that the "world-class infrastructure project" would generate more than 18,000 jobs for Canadian workers and provide a more efficient route for trans-Pacific shipping, linking [[alberta|Alberta]] to world markets.[2]
