# Katie Hobbs

Katie Hobbs was the Democratic Secretary of State of Arizona and her party's candidate for governor in the [[2022-arizona-general-election|2022 Arizona general election]], a dual role that drew criticism because it placed her in charge of the office overseeing certification of her own contest.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

Badlands Media contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] noted that under Article V, Section 6 of the Arizona Constitution the Secretary of State stands first in the line of succession to the governorship, ahead of the Attorney General.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) When Attorney General [[mark-brnovich|Mark Brnovich]] was described as "acting governor" in November 2022 — apparently because Governor [[doug-ducey|Doug Ducey]] was travelling out of state, creating a "temporary disability" — Gillespie asked why the role had not fallen to Hobbs, speculating that Hobbs was "TOO BUSY trying to certify her OWN ELECTION," and complained that no journalist in Arizona had pressed the question.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

Statewide unofficial results published by the [[arizona-secretary-of-state|Arizona Secretary of State's office]] under Hobbs formed the basis of Gillespie's undervote analysis, in which the Secretary of State contest recorded 1,320,059 votes against 1,389,215 for State Treasurer.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Under A.R.S. § 16-642(A) [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] was required to submit its official [[canvassing|canvass]] to her office on or before November 28, 2022.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## Certification and the December 2022 lawsuits

Hobbs certified the Arizona general election on Monday, December 5, 2022, an act [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] characterized as Hobbs certifying her own election and as "a total clown show."[3] Certification opened the five-day statutory window for election contests, and on Friday, December 9 three separate Republican filings named her as a defendant.[3]

[[kari-lake|Kari Lake]]'s 70-page [[kari-lake-election-contest-2022|election contest]] named Hobbs both personally as contestee and in her official capacity as Secretary of State, alongside [[stephen-richer|Stephen Richer]], [[scott-jarrett|Scott Jarrett]] and the [[maricopa-county-board-of-supervisors|Maricopa County Board of Supervisors]].[3] It alleged that Hobbs and Richer had colluded with the federal government to censor Americans and infringe their [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] rights, noting that she had complained even about a "private" Facebook post asserting that Trump won.[3] The suit stressed that Hobbs was overseeing an election in which she herself was a candidate trailing by ten points, and concluded that maladministration and illegal votes in [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] caused the state wrongfully to name her the winner of the governor's race.[3]

A parallel suit by [[mark-finchem|Mark Finchem]] and [[jeff-zink|Jeff Zink]], which also named [[adrian-fontes|Adrian Fontes]] and [[ruben-gallego|Ruben Gallego]], alleged that Hobbs abused her power by failing to have the tabulation machines properly certified, by threatening the boards of supervisors in Mohave and Cochise counties with criminal charges if they did not certify, and by having her office flag misinformation on a Twitter account in January 2021; the plaintiffs argued she should have recused herself as Secretary of State while running for governor.[3]

## Governorship and the 2023 big tech censorship hearings

As governor, Hobbs remained a focus of Arizona legislative scrutiny over censorship.[4] The [[arizona-house-ad-hoc-committee-on-oversight-accountability-and-big-tech|Arizona House Ad Hoc Committee on Oversight, Accountability, and Big Tech]] was formed after reporting that Hobbs, while Secretary of State, had been caught censoring her critics and accurate reports of fraud in the 2020 election on Twitter, and it held a hearing on September 5, 2023, on free speech violations by big tech companies and government officials.[4]

Badlands Media writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Hobbs had presided over "Drawer Three" and the inexplicable Arizona 2022 midterm elections before her coordination with big tech was exposed, and argued that as governor she had likely grown worse.[4] Ashe described Hobbs as "unqualified, incapable," inclined toward tyranny in her methods of governance, and "the perfect mascot for 2023's weaponized government and the persecution of political opponents," noting that the courts had already helped her escape accountability for the conduct.[4]

## Operation Desert Guardian (2025)

On Tuesday, February 25, 2025, Hobbs signed an executive order "to combat the cartels, stop drug smuggling, and secure Arizona's border," establishing a joint task force named [[operation-desert-guardian|Operation Desert Guardian]].[5] The order directs the Arizona Department of Public Safety, the Arizona Department of Homeland Security, and the Department of Emergency and Military Affairs to focus on the identification, deterrence, interdiction, and dismantling of transnational criminal organizations, with the state homeland security department convening the task force alongside county sheriffs and local and federal law enforcement.[5] Hobbs said her administration had been in contact with the federal government and local sheriffs about the operation and a shared commitment to keeping criminals and drugs out of Arizona communities.[5] Badlands contributor Chris Paul dismissed the announcement with the line "Fake governors don't have choices."[5]
