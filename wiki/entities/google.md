# Google

Google is the American technology company co-founded by [[larry-page|Larry Page]], best known for its search engine and its artificial-intelligence research operations.[1]

In April 2023, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] told [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] that Google's "end game" is to create a "digital super intelligence," which Musk equated with a "[[digital-god|digital god]]," and said Page had once described such a plan to him directly.[1] Musk added that he had repeatedly warned Page about the dangers of [[artificial-intelligence|AI]] while staying at Page's Palo Alto home, and that Page dismissed the warnings and called him a "speciesist."[1] The exchange was circulated in the *[[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]]* as evidence that the company's AI ambitions carry a theological as well as a technological dimension.[1]

## October 2024 nuclear investment

In mid-October 2024, Google announced an investment in [[small-modular-reactor|small nuclear reactors]], two days before a comparable announcement by [[amazon|Amazon]], as both technology giants sought carbon-free electricity to meet surging data-center and [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] demand.[2] The moves followed the announcement that the shuttered [[three-mile-island-nuclear-power-plant|Three Mile Island]] plant would restart to supply [[microsoft|Microsoft]] data centers.[2] The International Energy Agency forecast at the time that data centers' total electricity consumption could exceed 1,000 terawatt hours in 2026, more than double the 2022 figure.[2]

## 2023 antitrust trial

In September 2023 Google went to trial in a Washington, D.C. federal court in the largest U.S. antitrust case since the government's action against [[microsoft|Microsoft]] a quarter century earlier, with the government targeting the dominance of its search engine as the internet's main gateway.[3] The ten-week trial before U.S. District Judge Amit Mehta was expected to feature testimony from top Google and [[alphabet|Alphabet]] executives, including chief executive Sundar Pichai, who succeeded co-founder [[larry-page|Larry Page]] in 2019, and possibly [[apple|Apple]] executive Eddy Cue.[3] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that the investigation began under [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[justice-department|Department of Justice]] and continued under the Biden administration, framing it as Big Tech having a rough time under regime control.[3] See [[united-states-v-google-antitrust-trial]].[3]

## Epstein and the JPMorgan private-banking relationship

August 2023 court filings by the [[us-virgin-islands|U.S. Virgin Islands]] alleged that [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] served as an investment "advisor" to Google and co-founder [[sergey-brin|Sergey Brin]], helping [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan]] secure their business.[4] Internal 2006 emails, including one from [[mary-erdoes|Mary Erdoes]] under the subject line "google," showed the bank staffing the relationship from New York to accommodate Epstein rather than from San Francisco where the company and its founders were based, and referenced GRATs terminating for the Google founders.[4]

## Incognito browsing settlement (April 2024)

In a settlement filed on Monday, April 1, 2024 in San Francisco federal court, Google pledged to destroy a trove of data reflecting millions of users' web-browsing histories, resolving a 2020 class action alleging that the company tracked people without their knowledge in Chrome's private "Incognito" mode.[5] The suit alleged that Google's marketing and privacy disclosures did not properly inform users about the kinds of data collected, including which websites they viewed.[5] Under the agreement Google will destroy billions of data points it allegedly collected improperly, update its private-browsing disclosures, and let users disable third-party cookies in that setting.[5] The settlement includes no damages for individual users but permits individual claims, with plaintiff attorneys having already filed 50 in California state court.[5] Attorney [[david-boies|David Boies]] said the deal requires deletion and remediation "in unprecedented scope and scale" and called it "an historic step in requiring honesty and accountability from dominant technology companies"; Google did not immediately comment.[5] See [[google-incognito-privacy-settlement]].[5]

## Gemini bias controversy (2024)

Google's artificial intelligence tool [[google-gemini|Gemini]] drew widespread criticism in early 2024 for delivering results with clear progressive ideological slant on political, historical, racial and gender topics, including refusing to produce information or images favorable to white people.[6] Senior Google officials publicly described the bias as a "mistake," but former employees told The Free Press that the design was intentional and stemmed from a corporate culture prioritizing diversity, equity and inclusion ideology "over excellence and good business sense."[6] Shaun Maguire, a partner at Google Ventures from 2016 to 2019, said the episode "revealed how broken Google's culture is in such a visually obvious way to the world" and was "a symptom of a larger cultural phenomenon that has been taking over the company for years."[6]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] contrasted this with Google's origins, when accurate search results were the foundation of its brand equity and its name became a verb, arguing that by 2015 the company was visibly manipulating results and that its willingness to compromise its product for ideology disproved the assumption that markets punish such behavior.[6]

## DOJ breakup deliberations (August 2024)

Following [[amit-mehta|Judge Amit Mehta]]'s August 5, 2024 ruling that Google had illegally monopolized the online search and search text advertising markets, the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] began considering measures to restore competition, including a potential breakup of the company.[7] [[bloomberg|Bloomberg]] reported that the DOJ was exploring dismantling Google, which would mark the first significant attempt to break up a company for monopolization since the failed effort against [[microsoft|Microsoft]] two decades earlier.[7] Less drastic options under consideration included requiring Google to share more data with competitors and imposing safeguards against the company leveraging an unfair advantage in AI, while the government was expected to push for a ban on the exclusive contracts central to its case regardless of approach.[7] If a breakup were pursued, the DOJ was seen as likely to target the Android operating system and the Chrome browser for divestment, and possibly to force the sale of AdWords, Google's text advertising platform.[7] Google planned to appeal, but Mehta directed both parties to begin preparing for the remedies phase.[7]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued against a breakup under the sitting administration, calling Mehta "an authoritarian who imagines the Judiciary as an extension of the Executive," warning that the Biden DOJ would be deciding which parts of Google stayed and which were divested, and noting that Search, AdWords, Play and Cloud constitute societal institutions that would be "core enablers of tyranny in the hands of a criminal regime."[7] She concluded that while Google might need to be broken up, it should not be done with [[amit-mehta|Mehta]] and [[merrick-garland|Garland]] at the helm.[7] See [[united-states-v-google-antitrust-trial|United States v. Google]].[7]

## Chinese antitrust probe (February 2025)

On Tuesday, February 4, 2025, China announced it would launch an investigation into Google over alleged antitrust violations, with the State Administration for Market Regulation stating that it would initiate the probe because of alleged violations of China's anti-monopoly law.[8] The announcement followed closely on the heels of Beijing's additional tariffs on select U.S. goods: China's finance ministry said it would levy 15% tariffs on coal and liquefied natural gas imports from the United States starting February 10, and impose 10% higher duties on American crude oil, farm equipment, and certain cars and trucks.[8] Google had stopped its internet and search engine services in China in 2010 but continued some operations, including helping Chinese businesses advertise on Google platforms abroad.[8]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright read the move as coordinated rather than adversarial, arguing that Trump and Xi were "executing a pincer against the globalist Deep State" with the trade war providing cover, and noting that the U.S. Justice Department was already embroiled in its own antitrust suit against Google.[8]

## Iranian phishing disclosure (August 2024)

In a research report published in August 2024, Google said its [[google-threat-analysis-group|Threat Analysis Group]] had detected efforts by Iranian hackers to target both the Trump and Biden-Harris campaigns in May and June, part of a larger email phishing operation that persisted at the time of publication.[9] The disclosure added credence to the Trump campaign's claim that it had been hacked as part of an [[iran|Iranian]] interference campaign.[9] Google said it blocked numerous login attempts against targeted personal accounts, secured the Gmail account of a high-profile political consultant that was accessed in July, and referred the matter to law enforcement without identifying the consultant.[9]

## April 2025 advertising technology antitrust ruling

On Thursday, April 17, 2025, US District Judge [[leonie-brinkema|Leonie Brinkema]] found that Google had illegally monopolized certain online advertising technology markets, marking the latest antitrust setback for the company and a challenge to its main source of revenue.[10] She held that the Alphabet Inc. unit violated antitrust law in the markets for advertising exchanges and for ad servers — the tools websites use to sell ad space — but ruled that the company did not meet the definition of a monopoly in a third market covering tools advertisers use to buy display ads.[10] Alphabet shares sank as much as 3.2% before paring losses, trading down 1.1% at $153.78 in early-afternoon New York trading.[10]

The decision was the second time within a year that a court found Google to be an illegal monopolist, following [[amit-mehta|Judge Amit Mehta]]'s August 2024 ruling that the company had unlawfully secured default search engine status on devices.[10] A remedies trial in the search case was set to begin the following Monday in Washington, where the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] sought to force Alphabet to sell off its Chrome browser.[10]

Badlands commentator Ashe in America argued that both rulings punished Google not for harming consumers but for dominating competition, describing the notion that software and hardware providers cannot engage in joint ventures as "mind-boggling" and characterizing the proposed structural remedies — including breaking off Chrome and Android — as drastic for anyone who believes in free-market innovation.[10] She noted that Google's ad tech position was built through innovation and strategic acquisitions such as DoubleClick, that the company planned to appeal, and warned that the government's overreach risked setting a dangerous precedent.[10]

## Admission of political censorship under Biden (2025)

In September 2025, Republicans on the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] declared victory after an investigation into political censorship under the Biden administration produced admissions from Google.[11] Committee chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] (R-Ohio) posted a letter from Google acknowledging that it had been asked by the Biden administration to violate free speech rights, with the resulting policy changes affecting both Google and its subsidiary [[youtube|YouTube]].[11] Jordan wrote that Google had committed to offer all creators previously removed from YouTube over political speech violations the opportunity to return to the platform, and that YouTube had admitted the censorship pressure from the Biden administration was "unacceptable and wrong."[11] According to Jordan, YouTube had changed its policies under White House pressure during the Biden-Harris years to censor political debate on COVID and elections.[11]

Badlands commentary was skeptical that reinstatement was sufficient. [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] disputed the framing that the censorship was done to help Democrats, arguing instead that it was done to help the "Uniparty election system" survive, and asked how a war could be won without properly identifying the enemy.[11] [[zak-paine|Zak Paine]] observed that the limited amnesty did not cover accounts deleted late in the first Trump administration for covering the Hunter Biden laptop story — accounts he said were directly targeted by Media Matters for America and terminated under pretextual non-political "violations" that conveniently fall outside the scope of the remedy.[11]

## Gemini image bias controversy (February 2024)

In February 2024 Google faced sustained criticism after its [[google-gemini|Gemini]] AI image generator was documented producing historically inaccurate "diverse" outputs, including black Vikings, and appearing to exclude white people from image results.[12] [[jack-krawczyk|Jack Krawczyk]], Senior Director of Product Management for Gemini Experiences, told Fox News Digital the company was "working to improve these kinds of depictions immediately," conceding the product was "missing the mark here" while defending the broader goal of generating "a wide range of people."[12] Critics questioned whether the statement amounted to an apology at all, and treated the episode as evidence of ideological bias deliberately encoded into Google's consumer AI products.[12]

## DOJ breakup proposal (November 2024)

In November 2024, U.S. regulators asked a federal judge to break up Google to prevent it from continuing to suppress competition through its dominant search engine, after a court found the company had maintained an abusive monopoly over the previous decade.[13] The proposed remedy, floated in a 23-page filing by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], called for the sale of Google's Chrome web browser and restrictions preventing Android from favoring Google's own search engine.[13] DOJ lawyers argued that a sale of Chrome "will permanently stop Google's control of this critical search access point and allow rival search engines the ability to access the browser that for many users is a gateway to the internet."[13] Regulators stopped short of demanding a sale of Android but asserted the judge should make clear that divestiture of the smartphone operating system could still be required if an oversight committee continued to see evidence of misconduct.[13] The proposal followed an August ruling by U.S. District Judge [[amit-mehta|Amit Mehta]] branding the company a monopolist.[13]

Badlands commentary opposed the action as federal overreach, arguing that market dominance does not automatically equal monopoly, that Chrome is one of many browsers available, and that default-installation agreements with hardware makers closely resemble the Microsoft/Windows claims long ago rejected by U.S. courts.[13] The commentary allowed that AdWords practices or search manipulation and suppression might involve genuinely criminal conduct, but held that a superior product stack producing market dominance is not itself a monopoly, and looked to the incoming Trump DOJ to take a liberty-preserving approach.[13]

## Stock decline over Apple AI search testimony (May 2025)

Google's stock fell sharply in May 2025 after an [[apple|Apple]] executive testified in court that the iPhone maker was considering adding artificial intelligence search engines to its web browser as search traffic declined.[14] The share price tumbled 9 percent on the Wednesday that [[eddy-cue|Eddy Cue]], Apple's senior vice president of services, took the stand, before recovering by nearly 2 percent the following day.[14] Cue testified that Safari search traffic had fallen for the first time the prior month because of AI, and said Apple would likely add AI search providers such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity to the browser, though "they probably won't be the default."[14]

## Pause of Gemini image generation (February 2024)

On February 22, 2024, [[alphabet|Alphabet’s]] Google announced on X that its artificial intelligence model [[google-gemini|Gemini]] would pause the image generation of people, after the model produced numerous images of black and Asian people when prompted but declined to do the same for white people.[15] “We’re already working to address recent issues with Gemini’s image generation feature. While we do this, we’re going to pause the image generation of people and will re-release an improved version soon,” the company wrote, adding that it was “aware that Gemini is offering inaccuracies in some historical image generation depictions.”[15] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] responded by calling the product’s head, [[jack-krawczyk|Jack Krawczyk]], “a racist douchenozzle” over his past postings on X.[15] Badlands contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] treated the episode as a case of “garbage in, garbage out,” attributing the outcome both to companies deliberately embedding critical race theory in their systems and to models trained on decades of mainstream news reproducing existing cultural bias.[15]


## August 2024 monopoly ruling

On Monday, August 5, 2024, U.S. District Judge [[amit-mehta|Amit Mehta]] ruled that Google's search engine had been illegally exploiting its dominance to squash competition and stifle innovation, in a decision described as capable of shaking up the internet and hobbling one of the world's best-known companies.[18] Mehta's 277-page opinion concluded that "Google is a monopolist, and it has acted as one to maintain its monopoly," and found that the company "enjoys an 89.2% share of the market for general search services, which increases to 94.9% on mobile devices."[18] The ruling followed a ten-week trial featuring testimony from top executives at Google, Microsoft and Apple, and came three months after closing arguments in the [[justice-department|Justice Department]]'s case.[18] Badlands Media commentary characterised Google's control over information as conferring an advantage over the collective mind with few historical precedents, and judged the ruling "MUCH more than simply a shot across the bow" even if it would not dismantle the company overnight.[18] See [[united-states-v-google-antitrust-trial|United States v. Google]].[18]

## Fusion power purchase (2025)

In June 2025 Google struck a deal to buy 200 megawatts of [[fusion-power|fusion power]] as the company struggled to reach its sustainability goals amid the growing energy demands of artificial intelligence.[19] It announced a partnership with [[commonwealth-fusion-systems|Commonwealth Fusion Systems]] to offtake power from that firm's first commercial plant in Virginia, which is set to bring 400 megawatts online starting in the early 2030s.[19] Michael Terrell, Google's head of advanced energy, wrote that fusion is "clean, abundant and inherently safe," while cautioning that "commercializing fusion is immensely challenging, and success is not guaranteed."[19]

## Alleged CIA origin and foreign installations (Badlands analysis)

Badlands contributor Joe Lange describes Google as a company that was created as a CIA spy operation, and argues that cofounder [[eric-schmidt|Eric Schmidt]] used that relationship to help install [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] computer systems abroad — three in [[north-korea|North Korea]] and four in [[armenia|Armenia]].[21] He treats Google search statistics as corroborating evidence, noting that Armenia ranked as the top country in the world for searches on "qanon" and attributing the anomaly to CIA operators using the Armenian machines to wage a narrative war against [[qanon|Q]] and the anon movement rather than to genuine Armenian public interest.[21]

## Kennedy restraining order denied (August 2023)

## Antitrust remedies ruling (September 2025)

On September 2, 2025, District Judge [[amit-mehta|Amit Mehta]] refused to break up Google over its monopolization of the online search and advertising markets, instead imposing lesser restrictions on the roughly $2 trillion company.[17] Mehta rejected the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s request to force Google to spin off its Chrome browser and Android products, but agreed with the government that Google must allow competitors to gain a foothold.[17] The order requires Google to share some of its search data with rivals and bars it from deals making its products — including its artificial intelligence offerings — the default tools on mobile devices.[17] Politico described the opinion as the most consequential antitrust decision on Big Tech business practices since a federal judge's failed bid to break up Microsoft in the early 2000s.[17]

The Badlands Media article "Dark Equation" recounts that Google acquired the UK-based artificial-intelligence research lab [[deepmind|DeepMind]] around the time of the falling-out between [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and co-founder [[larry-page|Larry Page]], with Musk saying Google's approach to AI had "great potential for good, but there's also potential for bad."[20] The article also reported that the so-called "Godfather of A.I." quit Google in 2023 so that he could speak openly about the dangers posed by AI-generated deepfakes.[20]

Former Google chief executive [[eric-schmidt|Eric Schmidt]] is described as chairing the [[bilderberg|Bilderberg Group]], heading the [[national-security-commission-on-artificial-intelligence|National Security Commission on Artificial Intelligence]], and funding the salaries of more than two dozen Biden administration employees — a combination [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] cited as reason for concern about the direction of AI policy.[20]
