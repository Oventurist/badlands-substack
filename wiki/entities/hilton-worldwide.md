# Hilton Worldwide

Hilton is a global hotel company operating brands including Hampton Inn by Hilton through a mix of owned and franchised properties.[1]

## Lakeville, Minnesota franchise termination (2026)

In January 2026 Hilton terminated its franchise agreement with the independently owned Hampton Inn by Hilton in Lakeville, Minnesota, following allegations that the hotel had refused accommodations to [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] and [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] agents conducting deportation operations.[1] The incident surfaced when agents' reservations — made using official government email addresses and government rates — were canceled, with hotel staff citing the guests' "immigration work."[1]

A DHS post on X condemned the cancellations as a "coordinated campaign" by Hilton to impede law enforcement, triggering widespread conservative backlash, boycott calls and comparisons to the Bud Light controversy, including public cancellations of Hilton credit cards and reservations.[1] Investigative journalist Nick Sortor released video showing the hotel continuing to deny service despite earlier assurances that the matter had been resolved.[1]

Hilton, emphasizing that its hotels are "welcoming places for all," investigated, determined the franchisee was not meeting brand standards, and immediately removed the property from its systems.[1] The hotel's management company, Everpeak Hospitality, had claimed a commitment to non-discrimination and to resolving the dispute, but Hilton proceeded with termination.[1] Shareholder Bill Ackman praised Hilton CEO Chris Nassetta's swift response in addressing what he called a "rogue franchisee."[1]

The episode highlighted tensions over corporate neutrality amid heightened immigration enforcement.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] linked the refusal of service to a broader pattern of local resistance to federal immigration enforcement associated with the [[democratic-socialists-of-america|Democratic Socialists of America]], framing such resistance as sedition and arguing the property would decline without Hilton's brand support.[1]
