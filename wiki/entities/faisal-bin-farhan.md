# Faisal bin Farhan

Faisal bin Farhan is the Foreign Minister of [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]]. Badlands commentary points to public reports of his meetings with Iranian counterparts in the run-up to the 2026 war, arguing that media accounts wrongly framed the encounters as Saudi threats against [[iran|Iran]] when they were in fact warnings delivered in good faith.[1]

That reading is offered as corroboration for Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]]'s account of a heated pre-war dialogue with an unnamed Gulf prince — a description [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] considers congruent with the conduct of Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[1] The broader point advanced is that the Saudis are playing an ever-expanding role in regional diplomacy, plausibly on behalf of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[1]


## Call to Lift Syria Sanctions (January 2025)

At a press briefing following high-level meetings on [[syria|Syria]] in Riyadh on Sunday, January 12, 2025, Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan called for the lifting of [[syria-sanctions|sanctions on Syria]], saying the curbs hindered the country's rebuilding and development.[2] "We stressed the importance of lifting unilateral and international sanctions imposed on Syria, as their continuation hinders the aspirations of the Syrian people to achieve development and reconstruction," he said.[2] The meeting drew representatives from Syria, Jordan, Lebanon and Türkiye as well as the UK and US, with officials from the GCC, EU and UN also taking part.[2] Prince Faisal said the gathering sought to advise Syria's new leadership in a way that respects its independence, and stressed continuing humanitarian and economic support, stability, reconstruction, and conditions for the return of Syrian refugees.[2]

## Riyadh diplomacy, September 2024

At the annual [[gulf-cooperation-council|Gulf Cooperation Council]] regional meeting held in Riyadh on September 9, 2024, Faisal bin Farhan — referred to in Badlands commentary by the initials "FBF" — scheduled stacked Joint Ministerial Meetings of Strategic Dialogue with both [[russia|Russia]] and [[india|India]], held in immediate sequence.[3] Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] attended, as did India's External Affairs Minister.[3] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the convergence of Russia and India in Riyadh as connected to Indian Prime Minister [[narendra-modi|Narendra Modi]]'s recent meeting with Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]].[3]

A widely circulated photograph from the meeting showed Faisal bin Farhan seated beside Kuwaiti Crown Prince [[mishal-al-sabah|Mishal Al-Sabah]].[3] During the same gathering, Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] publicly called for an emergency meeting of all Islamic world leaders; Faisal bin Farhan then departed the Riyadh meeting, went directly to the airport, and flew to [[egypt|Egypt]].[3] The Badlands commentary dubbed him "The Peacemaker," presenting his shuttle diplomacy as part of a broader healing of the Middle East and the formation of a strong alliance across the Islamic world that would frustrate efforts to set Sunni against Shia.[3]

## Postponed Washington trip (February 2025)

In February 2025 the Financial Times reported that Prince Faisal had postponed a scheduled trip to Washington after the US president announced his plan for Gaza, according to a person familiar with the matter.[4] The postponement came amid a ferocious [[saudi-arabia|Saudi]] state media campaign against Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[4]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], who described Faisal as a capable and congenial diplomat who had earned friends for Saudi Arabia around the world, called the cancellation "a little alarming" and read it as a signal that the Saudis had lost patience with Tel Aviv — even while assuming the kingdom remained aligned with [[donald-trump|Trump]] ahead of an anticipated peace summit with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[4]

## UN two-state solution conference (July 2025)

Speaking on Monday, July 28, 2025, at the opening of a high-level international conference at the [[united-nations|United Nations]] in New York on the peaceful settlement of the Palestinian issue, Prince Faisal said that implementation of a [[two-state-solution|two-state solution]] in the Israel-Palestine crisis was "the key to regional stability."[5] "The Kingdom believes that the two-state solution is the key to regional stability," he said, adding that "the New York conference is a pivotal milestone toward implementing the two-state solution."[5] He reiterated that peace in the region must begin with securing the legitimate rights of the Palestinian people, and welcomed French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]]'s stated intention to formally recognize a state of Palestine in September.[5] See [[2025-un-two-state-solution-conference|2025 UN two-state solution conference]].[5]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the Saudis had been planning the meeting since at least December, describing it as part of a broader strategy in which Arab states leverage financial power to win European support bilaterally, with results visible at the United Nations and European Union.[5] He noted that [[state-department|State Department]] spokeswoman [[tammy-bruce|Tammy Bruce]] issued a statement condemning the meeting and, ostensibly, the concept of a two-state solution itself.[5]

## Recruitment by MBS

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] recruited Faisal bin Farhan, an accomplished Dubai-based developer, to serve as his foreign minister and help sell [[vision-2030|Vision 2030]] to the world's investors.[6]

## Riyadh two-state solution meeting (October 2024)

On Wednesday, October 30, 2024, Faisal bin Farhan chaired the first meeting of the [[international-alliance-to-implement-the-two-state-solution|International Alliance to Implement the Two-State Solution]] in Riyadh, telling attendees that nearly 90 "states and international organizations" were participating in the two-day gathering.[7] He said, "A genocide is happening with the goal of evicting the Palestinian people from their land, which Saudi Arabia rejects," calling the humanitarian situation "catastrophic" and denouncing the "complete blockade" of northern Gaza.[7] Israel rejected the genocide accusation, saying its operation aimed to eliminate [[hamas|Hamas]] and recover the 251 hostages taken on October 7, 2023.[7] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Faisal as "putting together a little team" and speculated that the grouping might come to be known as the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[7]

## Regional diplomacy assessment (July 2024)

In the July 2, 2024 Badlands News Brief, contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that "it's Faisal bin Farhan's show now," describing the Saudi foreign minister as working every diplomatic channel and speaking daily — per Foreign Ministry press releases — with members of what Badlands calls the Sovereign Alliance, including Iran, China, and Russia.[8] The commentary placed his statecraft at the center of a regional dynamic in which Arab states, aligned by the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]], restrained escalation against Israel while the broader Muslim world stayed its hand, a posture the author predicted would eventually yield peace after a period of heightened conflict.[8]

## April 2024 ceasefire call and Badlands profile

On a visit to Islamabad in April 2024, bin Farhan issued a renewed Saudi call for an immediate ceasefire in Gaza and for uninterrupted delivery of humanitarian aid, saying that international efforts toward a ceasefire between [[israel|Israel]] and [[hamas|Hamas]] had been "wholly insufficient."[9] He warned that "we are now actively discussing the potential for famine in Gaza, and it means people are starving to death because humanitarian assistance is not getting to them," calling the situation unacceptable.[9] Without directly naming the Iranian attack on Israel that weekend, he added that "we are already in an unstable region, and the humanitarian catastrophe in Gaza is already inflaming the region," concluding that "de-escalation must be everybody's priority."[9]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called bin Farhan "the most important diplomat in the world right now" and offered an extended profile of his background.[9] By that account, bin Farhan was born in Frankfurt, West Germany in 1974, spent part of his childhood there before schooling in the United States, and returned to the Kingdom to attend business school at King Saud University in Riyadh, later serving as vice chairman of the Saudi Company for Maintenance and Operations, which manages the trades — plumbers, electricians and carpenters — in Saudi Arabia.[9]

In 2003 he founded Shamal Investment Group, a Dubai-based investment house whose portfolio of "extraordinary investments" focused on tourist-centric real estate development and entertainment, and which the commentary credits with a role in Dubai's transformation under [[mohammed-bin-zayed|Mohammed bin Zayed]], appointed Crown Prince the same year — implying a relationship between the two men going back more than twenty years.[9] He led the group until 2017, when Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] asked him to serve as ambassador to Germany, an appointment that reportedly angered Saudi dissidents living there, and in 2019 MBS elevated him to foreign minister.[9]

The Badlands account speculates that bin Farhan used his regional contacts to broker meetings for [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] and facilitate the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]], and notes that around their formal signing in 2020 MBS tasked him with expanding the Accords to include both Saudi Arabia and Palestine, reviving the ten points of the [[saudi-peace-initiative|Saudi Peace Initiative]] presented at the 2002 Arab League summit in Beirut by King Abdullah Al Saud.[9] Both the Bush and Obama administrations admired the initiative's spirit but rejected its terms restoring Palestine's 1967 borders.[9]

Contrasting bin Farhan's cosmopolitan formation with MBS's upbringing entirely inside the Kingdom, the commentary describes the pair as a unique pairing of proud Arab nationalists with personal admiration for Western culture, suggests bin Farhan's West German childhood made him alert to totalitarianism and to efforts to destabilize Europe through weaponized migration, and argues that their shared desire for peace is driven by a vision of transforming the Middle East into the geographic center of the world through ambitious tourist-centric development — including turning the Red Sea coast into "the next French Riviera" — none of which can be achieved amid war and chaos.[9]
