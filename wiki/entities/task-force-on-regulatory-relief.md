# Task Force on Regulatory Relief

The Presidential Task Force on Regulatory Relief was the body established by [[ronald-reagan|President Ronald Reagan]] in 1981 to carry out the [[deregulation|deregulation]] pillar of his economic recovery agenda, chaired by Vice President [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]].[1]

Announcing it, Reagan said the task force would review pending regulations, study past regulations with an eye toward revising them, and recommend appropriate legislative remedies, adding that he intended it to be "more than just another Presidential task force that files a report and is soon forgotten."[1] He described the job as requiring a vigorous leader, talented administrator and superb diplomat, and named Bush as chairman to coordinate the interagency effort to end excessive regulation.[1] Alongside the task force Reagan issued an executive order barring promulgation of any regulation whose potential costs outweighed its benefits and put the newly created OIRA in charge of reviewing new rules.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats the chairmanship as the pivotal fact of the era: putting Bush in charge of revising and reforming past regulations opened the door to events that would devastate many people and produce one of the biggest scandals in United States history.[1] Federal banking regulators who might have looked askance at [[silverado-savings-and-loan-association|Silverado]] instead saw the vice president's son and knew the vice president himself chaired the task force deregulating the banking industry — so, in the Orlando Sentinel's account, the regulators did nothing.[1] The task force's banking work is therefore identified as the proximate cause of the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan crisis]].[1]
