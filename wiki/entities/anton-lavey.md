# Anton LaVey

Anton LaVey was the founder and leader of the [[church-of-satan|Church of Satan]] and, according to the testimony of [[mark-passio|Mark Passio]] recounted in the Badlands Media essay "False Flags and the Occult," the man who personally elevated Passio within the organization.[1]

## Appointment of Mark Passio

Passio states that after being recruited as a teenager circa 1995, LaVey himself appointed him to the position of high priest and granted him authority over a Grotto in the Philadelphia area.[1] In that role Passio was tasked with attending and overseeing midnight meetings with members of the satanic group, an experience that exposed him to a membership drawn from politicians, lawyers, teachers, policemen and priests rather than the countercultural youth he had anticipated.[1] It was this exposure that ultimately led Passio to break with the organization and dedicate his life to exposing what he calls the dark occult agenda.[1]



## Aquino, Polanski and the Manson murders

Matthew Ehret records that [[michael-aquino|Michael Aquino]] was initiated into a Satanic California coven led by LaVey in the early days of the Vietnam War and joined the [[church-of-satan|Church of Satan]] in 1966, before breaking away in the 1970s to found the [[temple-of-set|Temple of Set]] with 28 of LaVey's members as founding priests.[2] Despite the split, both organizations supported one another and promoted [[aleister-crowley|Crowley's]] Thelemic doctrine.[2] LaVey worked closely with director [[roman-polanski|Roman Polanski]], who hired him to stage-manage the satanic cult scenes of *Rosemary's Baby*.[2] When Polanski's pregnant wife [[sharon-tate|Sharon Tate]] was murdered by followers of [[charles-manson|Charles Manson]], it emerged that Susan Atkins, who stabbed Tate, had been employed by LaVey as a topless vampire in his Witches' Sabbath ceremony, and that former Church of Satan follower Jay Sebring was among the five victims.[2]
