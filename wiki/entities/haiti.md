# Haiti

Haiti is a Caribbean nation whose state collapse and gang violence drew U.S. military intervention at the embassy level in March 2024.[1]

## Embassy airlift, March 2024

The U.S. military sent forces to Haiti to airlift nonessential staff from the U.S. embassy and to help bolster security amid gang violence plaguing the country.[1] [[united-states-southern-command|U.S. Southern Command]] said the operation was conducted at the request of the [[state-department|State Department]] to bolster security of the U.S. Embassy at Port-au-Prince.[1] Nonessential personnel were airlifted and no Haitians were aboard the aircraft; the embassy had already ordered the departure of nonessential staff and all family members the previous July.[1] The neighborhood around the embassy in the capital was largely controlled by gangs.[1]

## Cannibalism claims and viral video

"Cannibalism" trended on X on Sunday, March 10, 2024, amid reports of cannibal gangs roving Haitian streets.[1] A widely circulated video of a man known as "Barbeque" apparently eating a human arm was, as commentator Stormy Joe pointed out, more than two years old and depicted a battle between the Gran Grif and Ti Mepri gangs in the Artibonite Valley.[1]

## Foreign aid and NGO questions

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the cannibalism story likely distracted from questions about the volume of U.S. "aid" dollars sent to Haiti over prior decades, suggesting the money may have been laundered through NGOs such as the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]].[1] She also cited alleged celebrity crimes in the country, noting that [[pras-michel|Pras Michel]] of the Fugees was found guilty on ten counts in a conspiracy case linking Haiti, China, and the United States through an illegal foreign lobbying scheme, and had been accused of stealing charitable funds from his native homeland.[1] Less than a year after that verdict, she wrote, the nation had collapsed, raising the question of how much the outcome cost taxpayers and whether any aid money reached its intended recipients.[1]

## 2024 gang takeover and blackout

In March 2024 Haiti descended into darkness overnight as gangs attacked electrical stations, prompting former Prime Minister [[laurent-lamothe|Laurent Lamothe]] to describe a "total void of law and order" and to call for order to be restored on the island.[2] The national power company announced that four substations in the capital and elsewhere "were destroyed and rendered completely dysfunctional," leaving swaths of Port-au-Prince without power — including the entrance to the U.S. embassy, the Cité Soleil slum, the Croix-des-Bouquets community, and a hospital.[2]

Nearly 1.5 million Haitians were on the brink of famine as armed gang leader [[jimmy-cherizier|Jimmy "Barbecue" Cherizier]], a former police officer turned gangster, attempted to seize power.[2] Cherizier claimed responsibility for the surge of attacks and said his goal was to capture Haiti's police chief and government ministers and to prevent outgoing Prime Minister [[ariel-henry|Ariel Henry]] from returning to the country; his reputation for burning people alive, which he denies, preceded him.[2]

Lamothe also said that "the international community promised $9 billion in aid but Haiti only received half of it, and out of the half most of it went to the US military," a claim Badlands Media commentary treated as an opening onto unexamined questions about aid flows, speculating whether the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] was implicated.[2]
