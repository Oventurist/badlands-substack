# The Tragically Hip

The Tragically Hip were a Canadian rock band, enormously popular in Canada during the 1990s and reasonably successful in Europe, whose limited American radio presence is used in Badlands Media commentary as evidence of industry gatekeeping.[1]

## Career and American reception

The band's debut album *Up to Here* was released in 1989 and, according to an October 2024 essay by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]], received substantial airplay on American rock radio at the time.[1] Their subsequent records — which Carlson considers arguably better — received no US airplay, and the band "seemed to have fallen into an abyss," a disappearance the essay attributes to music executives' stock explanation that they "just didn't hear a single."[1] Carlson wrote that in the 1990s he had to drive to Canada to buy their CDs, since releases came to the United States months later, and that he saw the band play stadiums and large festivals in Canada but only small clubs in the US.[1]

## Interpretation

Carlson concluded that the disparity meant "the band chose not to sell their souls," making them a central example of his claim that success in the American entertainment industry is granted by a [[cabal|Cabal]] rather than earned.[1] He drew a direct parallel to the American musician [[chris-whitley|Chris Whitley]], whose career followed the same arc.[1]

## Other references

A four-part documentary about the band was released on Amazon Prime shortly before the essay was written.[1] Carlson also remarked that, in his view, former Canadian prime minister [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]]'s "only redeeming quality" is being a Tragically Hip fan.[1] Badlands Media notes that its articles reflect the opinions of contributing authors.[1]
