# Cuba

Cuba is a Caribbean island nation governed since 1959 by a revolutionary state long associated with the Castro family, and in March 2026 the subject of publicly acknowledged negotiations with the United States.[1]

## 2026 negotiations with the Trump administration

President [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Diaz-Canel]] — the first non-Castro to lead Cuba since Fidel Castro took power in 1959 — convened an emergency press conference in March 2026 and announced that Cuba was in open negotiations with the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]], offering no additional detail.[1] He appeared alongside Raulito Castro, grandson of Raul Castro, nicknamed "El Cangrejo."[1]

Badlands analysis treated the appearance as confirmation of reporting by Axios's Marc Caputo that Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and Castro had held secret talks about Castro assuming the leadership.[1] The commentary noted the reversal of a long-standing neoconservative demand that the Castro family be expelled or prosecuted for war crimes, suggesting instead that [[donald-trump|Trump]] intended to install a Castro as America's preferred Cuban leader, mirroring the template used in Venezuela.[1]

## 2026 "accelerationism" pressure campaign

By May 2026 the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] had shifted to applying sustained economic pressure on Cuba's regime through expanded sanctions, a strategy described internally by a senior official as [[accelerationism|"accelerationism"]] — hastening societal collapse in stages.[2] On May 1, 2026, President Trump signed an executive order imposing secondary sanctions on companies doing business with [[gaesa|GAESA]], Cuba's military-industrial conglomerate.[2] The order prompted foreign firms including Canadian miner Sherritt International and European shipping companies CMA CGM and Hapag-Lloyd to suspend operations on the island, with further pullouts expected from banks and hotel operators.[2]

Additional measures included the cutoff of Venezuelan oil shipments to Cuba, a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] indictment of [[raul-castro|Raúl Castro]] over the 1996 Brothers to the Rescue shootdowns, and the termination of a GAESA executive's U.S. green card.[2] The administration also enforced existing sanctions more aggressively while conducting multiagency planning for potential unrest or chaos on the island.[2]

Senior officials said the goal was to methodically constrict Cuba's economy and create conditions for a peaceful transition away from the current regime without immediate regime elimination, with limited humanitarian assistance routed through non-government channels such as the Catholic Church and charities to demonstrate alternatives to the existing system.[2]

## Sanctions enforcement against U.S. activists (2026)

In May 2026 federal investigators subpoenaed streamer [[hasan-piker|Hasan Piker]] and activist [[medea-benjamin|Medea Benjamin]] over their participation in the March 2026 [[nuestra-america-convoy|Nuestra America Convoy]], which delivered roughly 20 tons of goods to Havana with organizing help from [[codepink|CodePink]].[3] More than 40 Americans came under scrutiny by the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury]]'s [[office-of-foreign-assets-control|Office of Foreign Assets Control]] for potential violations of U.S. sanctions law.[3]

The probe followed heightened [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration pressure on Cuba, including a tightened blockade implemented in 2025, Cuba's designation as a state sponsor of terrorism, and a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] indictment of former Cuban president [[raul-castro|Raúl Castro]].[3]

## Energy crisis and Ratcliffe talks (May 2026)

By mid-May 2026 Cuba was facing worsening blackouts, fuel shortages and broad economic deterioration as the Trump administration tightened sanctions and pursued regional isolation of Havana.[4] Reports indicated the island's energy infrastructure was under severe strain, with dwindling fuel imports and recurring nationwide outages intensifying public frustration and placing new pressure on the Communist government.[4]

The deterioration coincided with reporting that [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] had met with President [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Díaz-Canel]] and senior regime officials for talks, signaling that backchannel communication between Washington and Havana remained active despite escalating pressure.[4] Analysts viewed the combination of economic pressure and quiet diplomatic engagement as a sign the administration might be attempting to force concessions from Cuba while preventing broader regional instability.[4]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that President [[donald-trump|Trump]] was queuing Cuba up as the next domino to fall, a move that would drain inertia from the Middle East and animus toward [[iran|Iran]], and asked whether the Castro family would return to power as a partner of Trump.[4] The Cuba chapter is also treated as a stage in the [[multipolar-margin-call|Multipolar Margin Call]] sequence of sovereign disentanglement.[4]

## Reported regime change planning (March 2026)

In March 2026 a Russian oil tanker, the *Anatoly Kolodkin*, approached the Caribbean carrying crude oil to Cuba as the US Navy increased efforts to restrict fuel shipments to the island.[5] Cuba was facing a deepening economic crisis marked by widespread blackouts, shortages and migration, while its government acknowledged ongoing discussions with the United States over potential reforms.[5]

About 10 percent of Cuba's population has left the country since COVID-19, and according to [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] officials familiar with internal planning said the United States was considering legal and strategic measures that could escalate pressure on Cuban leadership, including possible indictments and broader contingency plans.[5] Anonymous officials told the outlet that regime change in Cuba is "lined up."[5] See [[cuba-regime-change-planning-2026|Cuba regime change planning 2026]].[5]

## April 2026 economic outreach

In late March 2026 Cuba's senior diplomat to the United States, [[lianys-torres-rivera|Lianys Torres Rivera]], proposed expanded economic cooperation with the Trump administration and offered "mutually beneficial discussions," while a senior US official said the outreach "sounded like the regime is getting desperate."[6] Torres Rivera said there is "a huge range of issues that we can discuss with the US" that would serve both countries' national interests, and that Havana is open to American involvement in the island's economic transformation without compromising sovereignty.[6]

The remarks offered an early glimpse into Cuba's position in ongoing talks led on the American side by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], following a US oil blockade imposed roughly two months earlier.[6] Cuba is seeking sanctions relief including eased embargo restrictions, a waiver of Title III of the [[helms-burton-act|Helms-Burton Act]], and removal from the state sponsors of terrorism list; President [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Díaz-Canel]] confirmed the largely undisclosed talks in mid-March.[6] Torres Rivera also raised potential cooperation on Alzheimer's research and drug-trafficking enforcement, while US officials said any deal would require major political and economic changes in Cuba, including privatization and greater financial independence for citizens.[6]

## Collapse conditions and Lavrov's intervention (February 2026)

During an Air Force One gaggle in February 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said, "Cuba is right now a failed nation, and they don't even have jet fuel for airplanes to take off, clogging up their runway."[7] Reports described worsening blackouts and accumulating waste on the island, tied to large-scale power outages in recent months and strain on its electrical grid and generation facilities.[7] Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] referred to Cuba as a "brotherly nation" in public remarks while criticizing US sanctions.[7]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] flagged as the most interesting element of the narrative the allegation that Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] was withholding information from President Trump, adding that although the story is "probably nonsense," if true it would fit the pattern of the [[state-department|State Department]] frustrating the president's major policy decisions.[7]

## Cayo Falcones shooting (February 2026)

In February 2026 Cuban border guards killed four people aboard a Florida-registered speedboat in Cuban territorial waters near Cayo Falcones off Villa Clara province.[8] Cuba's Interior Ministry said the vessel entered its waters and that guards returned fire after the boat opened fire on soldiers.[8] Cuba stated that ten people were aboard, six were wounded, and that the group carried assault rifles, handguns, Molotov cocktails or other incendiary devices, body armor or homemade ballistic vests, telescopic sights, and camouflage gear, describing them as Cuban nationals residing in the United States attempting an infiltration to "unleash terrorism."[8] US authorities did not independently confirm who fired first, the number aboard, the reported wounded, or the identities of the dead, and Florida Attorney General [[james-uthmeier|James Uthmeier]] ordered an investigation while declaring that "the Cuban government cannot be trusted."[8] See [[cayo-falcones-boat-incident|Cayo Falcones boat incident]].[8]

## State Department report on Cuban-backed activism (2026)

In July 2026 the [[state-department|State Department]] previewed a report alleging that Cuba's communist government has spent decades supporting and cultivating radical left-wing movements in the United States and across the Western Hemisphere, providing ideological, organizational, and political support to networks influencing activism beyond Cuba's borders.[9] The report is expected to examine [[codepink|Code Pink]] and the [[peoples-forum|People's Forum]] and to discuss funding from [[neville-roy-singham|Neville Roy Singham]], alleging these groups promoted positions aligned with Havana and other authoritarian regimes opposed to US interests.[9] Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] had made countering transnational left-wing political influence a foreign policy priority.[9]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] linked the Cuba angle to accounts in which Venezuela's corruption and election fraud were a product of Fidel Castro's influence over Nicolás Maduro and Colombia's Gustavo Petro, and noted that [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]], once a Cuban-trained Marxist radical, is now Mayor of Los Angeles and at the center of the biggest alleged fraud scandal of the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]].[9] Her position was to sanction foreign nations if desired but to prioritize prosecuting the Americans committing what she called treason, along with their Cuban-trained foot soldiers.[9]

## Intensified pressure campaign (May 2026)

Pressure on Cuba intensified in tandem with U.S. action in [[venezuela|Venezuela]], with surged U.S. surveillance flights since February, new sanctions imposed in May 2026, and sharp rhetoric from President [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]].[10] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that media coverage of Cuba betrayed an inverted logic and a fear that the same process underway in Venezuela would soon play out on the island, characterizing frenzied speculation about military signaling as concern not over destabilization but over Cuba's disentanglement from what he called the proxy architecture of the Hegemon and its renormalization within Trump's hemispheric reformation.[10]

## State Department subversion report (July 2026)

In July 2026 the [[state-department|State Department]] released a report accusing the Cuban regime of a nearly seven-decade campaign of subversion, espionage, and ideological warfare aimed at undermining the United States from within.[11] The [[state-department-cuba-subversion-report-2026|report]] described Havana as a sophisticated intelligence and influence machine whose espionage network penetrated the State Department, the National Security Council, and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], and which used front groups to recruit and radicalize Americans.[11] It linked Cuban networks to the George Floyd riots, the rise of [[antifa|Antifa]], and pro-terror campus activism, and stated that Cuba hosts military, intelligence, and terrorist installations belonging to [[russia|Russia]], China, and Iran.[11] The document simultaneously described the regime as keeping its own population in hunger and conditions akin to modern slavery amid a chronically failing economy.[11]

Badlands commentary rejected the report's framing, arguing it projected the [[cia|CIA]]'s own record onto Havana and noting the implausibility of casting a small island under US embargo since 1960 as capable of dismantling the American value system unopposed.[11] Commentators cautioned, however, against the opposite reflex of assuming Fidel Castro's government was itself a CIA proxy, calling the question a worthwhile subject of genuine inquiry rather than assumption.[11]

## July 2026 negotiation offer and grid collapse

In an interview published on or about July 6, 2026, [[raul-guillermo-rodriguez-castro|Raúl Guillermo Rodríguez Castro]] — grandson, aide, and bodyguard to [[raul-castro|Raúl Castro]] — said he was prepared to negotiate directly with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over the future of US–Cuba relations, and indicated Cuba could consider measures such as releasing political prisoners if broader talks with Washington took place.[12] He holds no formal government position, and the remarks came as the Trump administration maintained economic and diplomatic pressure on Havana.[12]

Hours after the interview was published, Cuba experienced another nationwide electrical grid collapse, leaving much of the island without power.[12] Cuban authorities said the cause was not immediately known and began restoring electricity while prioritizing hospitals and other critical infrastructure.[12] It was the country's eighth nationwide grid failure since October 2025, underscoring persistent energy and infrastructure problems amid broader economic difficulties.[12]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] criticized online calls of "CUBA IS NEXT!!!", argued for testing whether negotiation with the younger Castro is possible, and framed the US trade embargo in place since 1958 — rather than communism alone — as a candidate explanation for Cuban poverty.[12]

## Trump ultimatum (January 2026)

In January 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly urged Cuba to reach an agreement with the United States, warning that time was running out.[13] Cuban officials responded that they were not currently involved in negotiations with Washington.[13] The appeal came during the same week in which the [[state-department|State Department]] maintained its Level 4 "Do Not Travel" advisory for [[venezuela|Venezuela]] and Washington pressed its Arctic claims over [[greenland|Greenland]], a sequence Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized as a hemispheric demonstration that "power projects rules" rather than the reverse.[13]


## Trump ultimatum (January 2026)

[[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] escalated pressure on the Cuban government, issuing a high-stakes ultimatum that Havana must "make a deal" with the United States or face unspecified consequences as its access to Venezuelan oil and financial support was cut off.[14] On Truth Social he declared that "there will be no more oil or money going to Cuba — ZERO!" and urged Cuban leaders to negotiate with Washington "before it's too late."[14] The remarks followed the U.S. military operation that ousted [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] and resulted in the death of Cuban security personnel, developments that had already strained Cuba's oil-dependent economy.[14]

Trump separately reposted a message suggesting that Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], a Cuban-American and key architect of his Latin America policy, could become president of Cuba, adding "Sounds good to me!"; the repost sparked commentary but did not reflect an official policy proposal.[14] Cuban officials responded by asserting their nation's sovereignty and condemning the U.S. approach.[14]

## "Friendly takeover" remarks (February 2026)

On February 27, 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told reporters outside the [[white-house|White House]] that the United States "could very well" have a "friendly takeover" of Cuba, a phrase he did not define further.[15] He said Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] was involved in high-level discussions with Cuban officials and described the island as experiencing severe economic difficulties and seeking American assistance.[15] The remarks came amid continuing tensions following recent U.S. actions in neighboring [[venezuela|Venezuela]] and pressure on Cuba's oil supply, though Trump outlined no formal plan or mechanism for such a takeover; the Cuban government denied engaging in high-level talks with Washington.[15]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the comments as pointing to a "Venezuela 2.0 situation with the Castro regime, minus the 'arrest'/extraction," and anticipated a rugpull for audiences hoping for a hardcore regime-change operation, while advocating negotiation with [[raulito-castro|Raulito Castro]].[15]

## 2024 economic crisis and Santiago protests

Cuba fell into a near-unprecedented economic crisis following the COVID-19 pandemic, marked by vast shortages of food, fuel and medicine and a record-breaking exodus in which upwards of 400,000 people migrated to the United States.[16] In March 2024 hundreds of residents of Santiago, the island's second-largest city, staged a rare public protest chanting "power and food" as blackouts in some areas ran 18 hours or more per day.[16] President [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Díaz-Canel]] called for dialogue in an "atmosphere of tranquility and peace" while accusing "terrorists" from the United States of seeking to foment further uprisings; the U.S. embassy in Havana urged the government to respect protesters' human rights, and foreign minister Bruno Rodríguez demanded Washington refrain from interfering in internal affairs.[16] See [[2024-santiago-de-cuba-protests|2024 Santiago de Cuba protests]].[16]
