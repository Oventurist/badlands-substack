# Cuba

Cuba is an island nation in the Caribbean governed since the 1959 revolution by the Communist Party of Cuba. The Badlands corpus treats it as a live theater of the Trump administration's hemispheric policy, alongside [[venezuela|Venezuela]].

## The 2026 negotiations with Washington

The March 16, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that on a Thursday evening, sitting president [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Diaz-Canel]] announced an emergency press conference for the following morning to disclose something important. He appeared the next day alongside [[raulito-castro|Raulito Castro]] — grandson of [[raul-castro|Raul Castro]] and known by the nickname "El Cangrejo," the Crab — and said that Cuba was in open negotiations with the Trump administration. No further details were provided.

Badlands commentator [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the announcement "all but confirms" [[marc-caputo|Marc Caputo]]'s reporting in [[axios|Axios]] that Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and Castro had been in secret negotiations over Castro potentially taking over as leader. The Brief notes that Diaz-Canel is the first person outside the Castro family to lead Cuba since [[fidel-castro|Fidel Castro]] took power in 1959.

## Interpretation

The commentary observes an apparent reversal of long-standing exile and neoconservative demands. Where the neoconservative position had been that the Castro family be expelled from Cuba and possibly prosecuted for war crimes, the reported arrangement would install a member of that family as Washington's preferred Cuban leader — a template the Brief compares to the approach taken in Venezuela.

## Reported regime-change planning (March 2026)

The March 24, 2026 Badlands Brief, Alternative Annexations & New(ish) Regime Change, reports that a Russian oil tanker, the *Anatoly Kolodkin*, was approaching the Caribbean carrying crude to Cuba as the [[united-states-navy|US Navy]] increased efforts to restrict fuel shipments to the island. Cuba was described as facing a deepening economic crisis of widespread blackouts, shortages, and emigration, with roughly 10 percent of the population having left since COVID-19, while its government acknowledged ongoing discussions with the United States over potential reforms. Citing [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]], the brief reports that officials familiar with internal planning say Washington is weighing legal and strategic measures to escalate pressure on Cuban leadership, including possible indictments and broader contingency plans, with anonymous officials stating that regime change in Cuba is "lined up."
