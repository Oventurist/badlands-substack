# LiHyTech (Lithium Infinity)

LiHyTech, also known as Lithium Infinity, is a Saudi Arabian startup spun out of [[king-abdullah-university-of-science-and-technology|King Abdullah University of Science and Technology]] that commercializes direct lithium extraction technology.[1] The company was named as the lead operator of a commercial pilot program for direct lithium mining announced by Saudi Arabia's deputy minister of mining affairs, [[khalid-al-mudaifer|Khalid bin Saleh Al-Mudaifer]], following the discovery of lithium reserves within Saudi oil fields.[1]

## Technology

The underlying process traces to a 2021 announcement by researchers at King Abdullah University of Science and Technology, who developed what they described as an economically viable system to extract high-purity lithium from seawater.[1] Development was funded with investment from [[saudi-aramco|Saudi Aramco]], the kingdom's national oil company, and [[maaden|Ma'aden]], its national mining company, as part of an effort to cultivate a domestic lithium mining sector supplying the critical material used in advanced battery technology.[1] Two persistent challenges were identified: the method is relatively expensive compared with traditional lithium mining, and the lithium concentration in ordinary brine or seawater is very low.[1]

## Oilfield brine pilot

Saudi Aramco established a pilot program allowing LiHyTech to test the technology at an oilfield Aramco was already drilling.[1] Oilfield brine — a mixture of water, salts, oil, and injected sterile water — is the largest byproduct of oil and natural gas extraction, and because the brine was already a sunk cost of Aramco's operations, LiHyTech's extraction costs were minimized.[1] The pilot found that the oilfield was rich in lithium, generating a substantially higher yield rate than typical brine, a result that reportedly prompted other major oil companies including [[exxon-mobil|Exxon Mobil]] to plan similar programs at their own oilfields in hopes of extracting "white gold" from brine.[1]

## Strategic significance

The venture is presented as evidence supporting Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]]'s [[saudi-vision-2030|Saudi Vision 2030]] program of diversification away from oil dependence.[1] Beyond oilfield brine, Saudi Arabia's position as the world's leading desalination nation offers a second feedstock: desalination produces a highly salinized wastewater considered potentially toxic if returned to the ecosystem, which could instead be farmed for lithium.[1]
