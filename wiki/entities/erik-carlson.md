# Erik Carlson

Erik Carlson is a contributing writer for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] whose commentary appears under the recurring column heading "What's Really Going On?".[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) His June 5, 2023 piece "A Communist Plot to Take Over America" is written in an explicitly introspective register, opening with the admission that he was "more confused than ever before with what is going on in the world" and that he had never claimed to have all the answers.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## Method and epistemic stance

Carlson frames his writing as observation and question-asking rather than settled conclusion, arguing that "coming to a hard conclusion on most anything is foolish" because new evidence may at any time overturn an earlier view.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) He recommends examining every person or situation "from every possible angle," asking whether an actor is being straightforward, whether they are being used willingly or unwillingly, and whether multiple things may be happening at once rather than accepting the single explanation that appears most obvious.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

He distinguishes between confusion about "day-to-day operations, the details" and clarity about "the bigger picture," stating that he believes God will never abandon the country and that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and "the Patriots" retain some measure of control and are attempting to take the country back.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) He contrasts his present confusion with the period when he was, in his framing, "asleep" — a time when mainstream narratives made enough sense that he willingly accepted them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## Positions taken

Carlson had previously argued, in an article titled "Get Woke, Go Broke," that American corporations are trapped between alienating customers with "woke" policies and losing their place in large investment portfolios if they decline to adopt them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) After watching a video by [[andy-frisella|Andy Frisella]], he publicly revised that analysis to admit a possibility he had not considered — that consumer boycotts of [[americana-brands|Americana brands]] may themselves serve the aims of the [[deep-state|Deep State]] — and said he was "slightly embarrassed" the theory had not occurred to him.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

He also revisited his reading of [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s speakership after the 2023 [[debt-ceiling-agreement-2023|debt limit agreement]], and speculated about the true purpose of [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]]'s presidential campaign.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) On [[artificial-intelligence-manipulation|artificial intelligence]] he argues the technology's sudden prominence is "all an act" and that AI has been used on the public for decades.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

Carlson closes the article with an appeal to unity across political lines — conservatives, moderates and liberals alike — and the exhortation, "Be brave. Be courageous. God bless you, and God bless America."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) Badlands Media notes that articles by contributing authors represent the opinions of those authors and not necessarily the views of the outlet itself.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) Carlson maintains additional free work at his own Substack.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## "A New Hollywood"

In "A New Hollywood," Carlson writes as a full-time worker and father who produces content on the side, noting that most criticism of his writing concerns typos, misspellings and bad grammar, followed by pushback for not home-schooling his children and for consuming [[hollywood|Hollywood]] entertainment.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) As a member of Generation X he recalls the theaters and even the seats where he watched *Star Wars*, *The Empire Strikes Back*, *Return of the Jedi*, *Road Warrior*, *ET*, *Rocky III*, *Rambo*, *Top Gun*, *The Terminator*, *Ghostbusters*, *Raiders of the Lost Ark*, *Die Hard* and *Back to the Future*, and concludes that all of them featured brave heroes standing against corrupt systems.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

He argues that children should be dreamers rather than pragmatists — contrasting himself with Vivek Ramaswamy — and says young boys should aspire to be Batman, not an accountant.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He recounts once saving a man from being beaten to death by five other men, emerging almost unscathed by the grace and protection of God, and credits the movies of his youth with the "dumb courage and need for justice" behind the act.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

Carlson traces the ownership of [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]], [[cbs|CBS]] and [[showtime|Showtime]] to argue that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s allies have already infiltrated Hollywood, and closes by asserting that Hollywood, Big Tech, the financial system and Big Medicine can all be transformed to do good.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He writes for Badlands Media and publishes additional work at his own Substack.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## "A Virus, or a Metaphor?"

In his July 17, 2023 article *A Virus, or a Metaphor?*, subtitled "Decoding Donald Trump's COVID Comms," Carlson sets out a theory that [[donald-trump|Trump]] uses the word "virus" as a metaphor for Communism.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) He opens by cataloguing the ways MAGA and ex-MAGA audiences read Trump — as a genius, as an exhausting communicator, as a man who pushed the "jab" — and answers the critics by asking why Trump and his family would endure such abuse, and why his net worth fell in office, if he were himself part of the [[deep-state-cabal|Deep State Cabal]].[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

He defends Trump's vaccine posture as a wartime compromise: Trump forced [[big-pharma|Big Pharma]] to accelerate development so the [[covid-19-lockdowns|lockdowns]] could end, had to appear to support [[covid-19-vaccines|vaccines]] or be blamed for millions of deaths, and signalled his true position by insisting vaccination be a choice rather than a requirement.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) He then reads Q's Tom Clancy references — [[the-hunt-for-red-october|The Hunt for Red October]] and [[the-sum-of-all-fears|The Sum of All Fears]] — as coded messages about Communism and about modern-day Nazis attempting to set Russia and the United States against each other.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

On origins, Carlson writes that a bioweapon was released from a lab in [[wuhan-lab|Wuhan]] but originated in North Carolina, alleging that [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] funded and ran the program and moved it overseas when Trump took office, with further biolabs in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and possibly Taiwan.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) He reads Trump's "Chyna virus" as a double meaning about the [[chinese-communist-party|CCP]] infiltrating America, and Trump's repeated dating of the [[spanish-flu|Spanish Flu]] to 1917 as a pointer to the [[bolshevik-revolution|Bolshevik uprising]].[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) He closes by promising a follow-up post identifying who the Nazis really are, and by insisting Communism is only a tool — an axe swung by an axe-murderer — with the Cabal the real enemy.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor) The article again carries the Badlands Media disclaimer that contributing authors' views are their own, and directs readers to more of his free work at his own Substack.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-virus-or-a-metaphor)

## "A Week to Remember"

Carlson's July 25, 2024 article *A Week to Remember*, subtitled "But What Set This Chaos Into Motion?", was largely written between [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s debate performance and the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Donald Trump]] in Butler, Pennsylvania.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He notes that Q had posted the phrase "a week to remember" on July 14, 2019, five years and one day before the shooting, and catalogues the events that followed: [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s selection of [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] as running mate, the Republican National Convention, and Biden's exit from the race.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

His central argument is that the debate did not cause the [[deep-state-cabal|Deep State]] to abandon Biden; rather, the decision to replace him had already been taken, and the debate was the pretext chosen to justify it — which he says explains why the media turned on Biden universally and immediately, without waiting for their 4am talking points.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He speculates that the drug regimen that had made Biden semi-coherent at his most recent State of the Union was deliberately withheld before the debate to expose his condition.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He believes Biden fought the decision "kicking and screaming," submitted only after having his assets frozen and fearing for his life, and had to go before the public began asking who was actually running the country.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

Carlson proposes that the true trigger for the Cabal's panic was Trump's June 10, 2024 pledge to make tips tax-free, read as a first step toward abolishing the [[federal-income-tax|federal income tax]] and then the money supply system itself.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) The bulk of the article is a survey of American monetary history — the 1913 creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], income tax and the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] in a single year; the gold dispute with France; the [[petrodollar|Petrodollar]]; and [[inflation|inflation]] as the largest scam of all.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) He closes with a timeline linking the assassination attempt, the death of [[sheila-jackson-lee|Sheila Jackson Lee]], Biden's withdrawal and the resignation of Secret Service Director [[kimberly-cheatle|Kimberly Cheatle]], and four unanswered questions for readers.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) The article carries the standard Badlands Media disclaimer and directs readers to Carlson's own Substack.[[4]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## "Are We Being Distracted?"

In his August 1, 2024 article *Are We Being Distracted?*, subtitled "Obviously ... But What Are We Being Distracted From?", Carlson describes a personal heuristic developed over the preceding years: when it appears peak craziness has been reached, and when every other post in his social media feed carries the same strange narrative, he immediately asks what he is being distracted away from.[5] He observes that his community is very good at finding things to be outraged over but rarely does anything about it, with most people simply getting upset and moving on to the next thing.[5]

Referring back to *A Week to Remember*, he restates his thesis that the chaos of mid-2024 was set in motion not by the debate but by [[donald-trump|Trump]] signalling to the [[deep-state|Deep State]], via [[tax-free-tips|tax-free tips]], that he intended to tear the financial system down to its foundation — a chain running from capped income tax to abolition of the [[federal-income-tax|income tax]] to the destruction of the financial system as presently known.[5] He argues that Trump's team already knows exactly who was responsible for the shooting and who controls them, so there is no reason for readers to spend months chasing an answer the patriots already possess.[5]

Carlson invokes [[bread-and-circus|bread and circus]] and the claim that lukewarmness is the most controllable political state, then applies the [[distraction-operations|distraction]] framework to the [[paris-olympics-2024-opening-ceremony|Paris Olympics opening ceremony]], arguing that the offense it generated was intended and that [[transgender-athletes|transgender participants]] are being used by the [[cabal|Cabal]] rather than championed by the [[international-olympic-committee|Olympic Committee]].[5] He identifies the concealed story as Trump's simultaneous appearance at the [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] Conference, where Trump said Bitcoin would be used to strengthen the US Dollar and that the government should stockpile it, and points to [[jd-vance|JD Vance]]'s connections to Big Tech and cryptocurrency as a further clue.[5] He is careful to disclaim any investment advice, saying he is not telling anyone to buy cryptocurrency.[5]

The article closes on the assurance that the patriots are in control, are neither distracted nor manipulated, that the dismantling of the old guard is underway and cannot be stopped, and that while God is mocked today he will get the last laugh — in the end, God wins, and we win.[5]

## "America's Greatest Threat" (April 24, 2024)

In this essay Carlson argued that the greatest danger to the United States has never been foreign but domestic — the American government itself — and that the [[us-constitution|Constitution]] was written to protect citizens from an American government infiltrated by enemies rather than from those enemies directly.[6] He traced the loss of that protection to 1913, when the [[sixteenth-amendment|Sixteenth Amendment]] was ratified on February 3 alongside the formation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], a convergence he insists was no coincidence.[6]

The piece develops an extended argument about [[debt-slavery|debt slavery]], estimating that taxes and [[inflation]] leave roughly twenty-five cents of each dollar earned, and describes Americans as suffering from [[stockholm-syndrome|Stockholm Syndrome]] toward their captors.[6] Carlson noted that he pays his taxes despite believing them theft, admitting he is "afraid to leave the plantation" given the enforcement power of the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]].[6] Pointing to the Covid era, he stressed that it was not Putin, Xi, Iran, China, or ISIS who locked Americans down or checked vaccine cards, but Americans themselves acting for their own government.[6] He closed by naming [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as the figure capable of draining the swamp from 2025 onward, while cautioning that the underlying disorder must also be cured.[6] Carlson's work also appears on his personal Substack, and Badlands Media notes that contributor articles represent the opinions of the authors rather than the outlet.[6]

## "America Has a CIA-Run Economy" (January 18, 2025)

In this essay Carlson argued that the five largest American companies by market capitalization — [[apple|Apple]], [[nvidia|Nvidia]], [[microsoft|Microsoft]], [[alphabet|Alphabet]] and [[amazon|Amazon]] — all answer to the same master, the [[cia|CIA]], and are used to propagandize and control Americans and the world.[7] He built the case first on market structure, noting that the top five companies accounted for 33% of the [[sp-500-index|S&P 500 Index]] weighting in 2025 against 11% in 2010, that only 1% of American businesses are publicly traded, and therefore that the stock market does not represent the economy and the S&P 500 does not properly represent the stock market.[7]

Carlson attributed the consolidation to design rather than chance, recalling that during Covid smaller companies went under while mega-corporations grew — some calling it "the Amazon pandemic" — and offering [[starbucks|Starbucks]]' pre-lockdown pivot to drive-throughs, order-ahead and removed seating as evidence of foreknowledge and of a deliberate program to minimize human interaction, ending in [[15-minute-cities|15-minute cities]].[7] He proposed the 1911 Supreme Court breakup of [[standard-oil|Standard Oil]] into 34 entities as a possible template for limiting the power of today's top 1%.[7]

The mechanism he described runs through [[darpa|DARPA]] and the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]]: taxpayer-funded military research passes into consumer technology behind manufactured garage-startup origin stories, and the CIA then creates companies with installed public faces to gather what Americans would never surrender directly.[7] He anchored the pattern in the 1975 [[church-committee|Church Committee]] disclosure of [[operation-mockingbird|Operation Mockingbird]], argued the agency lost control when the internet arrived and needed social media and search engines to regain it, and pointed to the cancellation of [[lifelog|LifeLog]] on 4 February 2004 — the day Facebook launched — as the hinge.[7]

On the politics of 2025, Carlson read the [[trump-inaugural-fund-donations|million-dollar inaugural donations]] from Google, Microsoft, [[meta-platforms|Meta]], Amazon, Apple and [[openai|OpenAI]] as "the real pardoning" in America, with [[donald-trump|Trump]] letting valuable firms kiss the ring.[7] He conceded that these CIA-created companies will remain a large part of the American economy, that the cure cannot be worse than the disease, and that the economy cannot be collapsed to obtain justice — but predicted that some corporations will be broken up, that Deep State strings will keep being cut, and that if the CIA's strings have been cut he will "take the win. Gladly."[7]
