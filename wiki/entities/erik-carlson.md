# Erik Carlson

Erik Carlson is a contributing writer for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] whose commentary appears under the recurring column heading "What's Really Going On?".[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) His June 5, 2023 piece "A Communist Plot to Take Over America" is written in an explicitly introspective register, opening with the admission that he was "more confused than ever before with what is going on in the world" and that he had never claimed to have all the answers.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## Method and epistemic stance

Carlson frames his writing as observation and question-asking rather than settled conclusion, arguing that "coming to a hard conclusion on most anything is foolish" because new evidence may at any time overturn an earlier view.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) He recommends examining every person or situation "from every possible angle," asking whether an actor is being straightforward, whether they are being used willingly or unwillingly, and whether multiple things may be happening at once rather than accepting the single explanation that appears most obvious.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

He distinguishes between confusion about "day-to-day operations, the details" and clarity about "the bigger picture," stating that he believes God will never abandon the country and that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and "the Patriots" retain some measure of control and are attempting to take the country back.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) He contrasts his present confusion with the period when he was, in his framing, "asleep" — a time when mainstream narratives made enough sense that he willingly accepted them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## Positions taken

Carlson had previously argued, in an article titled "Get Woke, Go Broke," that American corporations are trapped between alienating customers with "woke" policies and losing their place in large investment portfolios if they decline to adopt them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) After watching a video by [[andy-frisella|Andy Frisella]], he publicly revised that analysis to admit a possibility he had not considered — that consumer boycotts of [[americana-brands|Americana brands]] may themselves serve the aims of the [[deep-state|Deep State]] — and said he was "slightly embarrassed" the theory had not occurred to him.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

He also revisited his reading of [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s speakership after the 2023 [[debt-ceiling-agreement-2023|debt limit agreement]], and speculated about the true purpose of [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]]'s presidential campaign.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) On [[artificial-intelligence-manipulation|artificial intelligence]] he argues the technology's sudden prominence is "all an act" and that AI has been used on the public for decades.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

Carlson closes the article with an appeal to unity across political lines — conservatives, moderates and liberals alike — and the exhortation, "Be brave. Be courageous. God bless you, and God bless America."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) Badlands Media notes that articles by contributing authors represent the opinions of those authors and not necessarily the views of the outlet itself.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america) Carlson maintains additional free work at his own Substack.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-communist-plot-to-take-over-america)

## "A New Hollywood"

In "A New Hollywood," Carlson writes as a full-time worker and father who produces content on the side, noting that most criticism of his writing concerns typos, misspellings and bad grammar, followed by pushback for not home-schooling his children and for consuming [[hollywood|Hollywood]] entertainment.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) As a member of Generation X he recalls the theaters and even the seats where he watched *Star Wars*, *The Empire Strikes Back*, *Return of the Jedi*, *Road Warrior*, *ET*, *Rocky III*, *Rambo*, *Top Gun*, *The Terminator*, *Ghostbusters*, *Raiders of the Lost Ark*, *Die Hard* and *Back to the Future*, and concludes that all of them featured brave heroes standing against corrupt systems.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

He argues that children should be dreamers rather than pragmatists — contrasting himself with Vivek Ramaswamy — and says young boys should aspire to be Batman, not an accountant.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He recounts once saving a man from being beaten to death by five other men, emerging almost unscathed by the grace and protection of God, and credits the movies of his youth with the "dumb courage and need for justice" behind the act.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

Carlson traces the ownership of [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]], [[cbs|CBS]] and [[showtime|Showtime]] to argue that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s allies have already infiltrated Hollywood, and closes by asserting that Hollywood, Big Tech, the financial system and Big Medicine can all be transformed to do good.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He writes for Badlands Media and publishes additional work at his own Substack.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

Referring back to *A Week to Remember*, he restates his thesis that the chaos of mid-2024 was set in motion not by the debate but by [[donald-trump|Trump]] signalling to the [[deep-state|Deep State]], via [[tax-free-tips|tax-free tips]], that he intended to tear the financial system down to its foundation — a chain running from capped income tax to abolition of the [[federal-income-tax|income tax]] to the destruction of the financial system as presently known.[5] He argues that Trump's team already knows exactly who was responsible for the shooting and who controls them, so there is no reason for readers to spend months chasing an answer the patriots already possess.[5]

The article closes on the assurance that the patriots are in control, are neither distracted nor manipulated, that the dismantling of the old guard is underway and cannot be stopped, and that while God is mocked today he will get the last laugh — in the end, God wins, and we win.[5]

In this essay Carlson argued that the greatest danger to the United States has never been foreign but domestic — the American government itself — and that the [[us-constitution|Constitution]] was written to protect citizens from an American government infiltrated by enemies rather than from those enemies directly.[6] He traced the loss of that protection to 1913, when the [[sixteenth-amendment|Sixteenth Amendment]] was ratified on February 3 alongside the formation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], a convergence he insists was no coincidence.[6]

The piece develops an extended argument about [[debt-slavery|debt slavery]], estimating that taxes and [[inflation]] leave roughly twenty-five cents of each dollar earned, and describes Americans as suffering from [[stockholm-syndrome|Stockholm Syndrome]] toward their captors.[6] Carlson noted that he pays his taxes despite believing them theft, admitting he is "afraid to leave the plantation" given the enforcement power of the [[internal-revenue-service|IRS]].[6] Pointing to the Covid era, he stressed that it was not Putin, Xi, Iran, China, or ISIS who locked Americans down or checked vaccine cards, but Americans themselves acting for their own government.[6] He closed by naming [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as the figure capable of draining the swamp from 2025 onward, while cautioning that the underlying disorder must also be cured.[6] Carlson's work also appears on his personal Substack, and Badlands Media notes that contributor articles represent the opinions of the authors rather than the outlet.[6]

Carlson attributed the consolidation to design rather than chance, recalling that during Covid smaller companies went under while mega-corporations grew — some calling it "the Amazon pandemic" — and offering [[starbucks|Starbucks]]' pre-lockdown pivot to drive-throughs, order-ahead and removed seating as evidence of foreknowledge and of a deliberate program to minimize human interaction, ending in [[15-minute-cities|15-minute cities]].[7] He proposed the 1911 Supreme Court breakup of [[standard-oil|Standard Oil]] into 34 entities as a possible template for limiting the power of today's top 1%.[7]

On the politics of 2025, Carlson read the [[trump-inaugural-fund-donations|million-dollar inaugural donations]] from Google, Microsoft, [[meta-platforms|Meta]], Amazon, Apple and [[openai|OpenAI]] as "the real pardoning" in America, with [[donald-trump|Trump]] letting valuable firms kiss the ring.[7] He conceded that these CIA-created companies will remain a large part of the American economy, that the cure cannot be worse than the disease, and that the economy cannot be collapsed to obtain justice — but predicted that some corporations will be broken up, that Deep State strings will keep being cut, and that if the CIA's strings have been cut he will "take the win. ## "Believe Half of What You See" (November 2023)

In a November 17, 2023 essay titled "Believe Half of What You See," Carlson built an extended argument for treating all media and social media output as suspect until independently verified, opening with the adage "Believe half of what you see, and none of what you hear."[9] He urged readers to look for patterns in the narratives being pushed, warning that lies circulate from both sides, that some material carries dual meanings, and that some participants in narrative operations are willing while others are unwitting.[9]

The piece surveyed the Republican presidential primary debates, the [[gal-gadot|Gal Gadot]] October 7 footage screening in Los Angeles, the killing of two anti-mining protesters in [[panama|Panama]] by [[kenneth-darlington|Kenneth Darlington]], and the earlier media focus on violence against Asian Americans, using each as an example of framing rather than straightforward reporting.[9] Carlson set out a three-part taxonomy: when mainstream media presents a story it is either a total lie, a half-truth, or a manipulation of the truth, the last consisting of real events selectively presented or stripped of context to serve a narrative.[9]

Carlson closed on a personal and spiritual note, describing a darkness he felt had hung over the truth-seeker community for roughly a month — anxiety and a depression producing feelings of inadequacy — and reporting that he sensed the cloud recently lifting.[9] He advised readers to step back from the news cycle when overwhelmed, to share their feelings with other truth seekers, and to pace themselves for the long haul, framing the effort as a spiritual war and invoking the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]] and the need to hold on to hope.[9]
