# National Park Service

The National Park Service (NPS) is the United States federal agency, housed within the [[department-of-the-interior|Department of the Interior]], that administers national parks, monuments and other federal park land, including many of the public spaces and memorials of Washington, D.C.[1]

## Washington fountain restoration

In 2026 the NPS was the lead agency in a broad repair effort for the capital's fountains and water features. The Washington Post reported on January 2 that, according to procurement records it reviewed, the Park Service was spending at least $54.2 million to repair fountains and water features at seven locations in the city, including a $10.68 million project at [[meridian-hill-park|Meridian Hill Park]].[1]

The Park Service said the cascading fountain and reflecting pool at Meridian Hill Park reopened to visitors on May 14, 2026 after years of work at the lower plaza.[1] It said the fountain had been turned off in 2019 during rehabilitation and that additional repairs were later required to replace critical components and to fix damaged concrete.[1] On May 18 the Interior Department released video showing water running at the fountain, which the station WJLA reported is a 13-basin structure and one of the longest in North America.[1]

## Context

The restoration work was folded into commentary about the wider effort by the administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to improve the appearance of the federal capital, an effort that also included [[executive-order-14344|Executive Order 14344]], "Making Federal Architecture Beautiful Again."[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] welcomed the improvements while arguing that the more urgent restoration was of governmental legitimacy, and expressed hope that mayors and governors elsewhere would be shamed into cleaning up their own cities.[1]
