# Axios

Axios is an American news organization whose Middle East coverage, largely through correspondent [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]], served as the primary conduit for reporting on the June 2026 rupture between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1]

## Role in the June 2026 Iran–Israel escalation

Axios, citing a senior U.S. official and an Israeli source, reported that Trump urged Netanyahu not to retaliate against [[iran|Iran]] after an Iranian missile attack.[1] Israeli officials cited by Axios said Iran launched 11 ballistic missiles toward [[israel|Israel]] on June 8, the first direct Iranian attack since an April 8 ceasefire.[1] A U.S. defense official told Axios that American forces did not participate in the retaliatory Israeli Air Force strikes on military targets in central and western Iran.[1]

Because Axios's access flowed through repeated Trump phone calls to Ravid, the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] treated the outlet's reporting less as neutral observation than as an intentional messaging channel in the information war surrounding the U.S.–Israel relationship.[1]

## June 2026 Lebanon call scoop
Axios reported the June 1, 2026 phone call in which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] unleashed an expletive-filled rebuke of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] over escalation in [[lebanon|Lebanon]], and reported that Trump intervened to halt a planned Israeli strike on Beirut.[2] The outlet separately reported that the Trump administration was preparing to eliminate the Justice Department [[weaponization-fund|weaponization fund]], citing two anonymous sources — reporting [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed as "statist wishful thinking" and said Trump should shame Axios for publishing.[2] Badlands commentary pushed back on blanket "it's Axios, it's probably fake news" dismissals, noting that [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]] and Mark Caputo maintain direct phone access to Trump and routinely receive exclusive information.[2]

## Framing of the 2026 Gallup morality poll

In June 2026 Axios reported on a new [[gallup|Gallup]] survey showing fewer Americans saying that birth control, having a baby outside marriage, and gambling are morally acceptable, writing that "Americans' more puritanical view of personal liberty coincides with a Republican resurgence, young men's rebounding religiosity and conservative backlash to LGBTQ+ advancements."[3] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] singled out that framing, noting that a decline from 90 to 83 percent still leaves most Americans "pretty sin tolerant," and arguing that what the outlet labeled puritanism is simply reduced tolerance of sin rather than an assault on liberty.[3]

## Coverage of the 2024 Biden debate crisis

Over the weekend of June 29–30, 2024, Axios published two front-page political stories on the fallout from the [[2024-presidential-debate|June 2024 presidential debate]].[5] The first, headlined "Behind the Curtain: Biden oligarchy will decide fate," argued that pundits, New York Times editorials and social media were irrelevant and that the only way President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would step aside was if the small group of lifelong loyalists who enabled his run decided it was time.[5] It named Dr. [[jill-biden|Jill Biden]], his sister [[valerie-biden|Valerie Biden]], 85-year-old adviser [[ted-kaufman|Ted Kaufman]] and a small band of [[white-house|White House]] advisers as "the only Biden deciders," describing this kitchen cabinet as an extended family, council of elders and "governing oligarchy."[5] Citing the [[new-york-times|New York Times]]' Peter Baker, Axios reported that Biden engaged in no organized process outside his family in deciding to run again, and that he alone made the decision; it added that if he stayed in, it was because he and the oligarchy believed he had a better chance of beating [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] than [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] did.[5]

The second piece, "Two Joe Bidens: The night America saw the other one," reported on aides' stage-management of the president and on his time-of-day cognitive variability.[5] Badlands contributor Ashe in America noted that Axios used the word "oligarchy" five times, observed that placing both stories at the top of the site on a Saturday morning signalled that "Axios editorial leadership is on Team Replacement," and argued the outlet was applying to Biden the same anonymous-source method it had long applied to Trump after years of covering up his decline.[5]

## Media-industry scoops

Axios also breaks corporate news in the media sector: in December 2023 it was the first outlet to report that [[warner-bros-discovery|Warner Bros. Discovery]] and [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]] had held talks about a potential merger, a story subsequently confirmed by *[[variety|Variety]]*.[4]

## Coverage of the 2024 post-debate crisis

In July 2024 Axios published a pair of pieces that Badlands Media analysed together: "Behind the Curtain: Trump's new 2024 plan," describing how [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was adjusting his agenda, the GOP platform, his vice-presidential plans and even his debate style to win over persuadable voter groups across seven swing states, and "Biden's incredible shrinking path to victory," reporting that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had for the moment stalled an internal Democratic revolt at potentially massive cost.[6] The Trump piece laid out a swing-state hierarchy from hardest to easiest — Pennsylvania, Wisconsin, Michigan, Georgia, North Carolina, Nevada and Arizona — and reported that the running mate's first job would be to camp in Pennsylvania to deny Biden the largest of his Blue Wall states.[6] The Biden piece cited the [[cook-political-report|Cook Political Report]] moving six states toward Trump in its Electoral College ratings.[6] Axios also reported on Biden's dual mission at the [[2024-nato-washington-summit|NATO Washington summit]] and on CIA director [[william-burns|Bill Burns]]'s Doha talks with Qatari, Egyptian and Israeli officials over a Gaza hostage and ceasefire deal.[6]

Badlands commentary argued that Axios had been "firmly on Team Replacement" since the debate, publishing disparagement resting solely on anonymous sources, and had pivoted once it became clear Biden was not withdrawing.[6] The critique held that Axios criticised both candidates but not symmetrically — attacking Biden's age and feebleness while recycling "convicted felon" lines and abortion warnings against Trump, in line with Democratic politicians and NGOs casting Heritage's [[project-2025|Project 2025]] as a dystopian sequel.[6] The central charge was that both articles resolved to the same takeaway, that the candidates remained "neck and neck," which the writer framed as defence of a "50/50 country" lie underpinning perceived national division and legitimising narrow governing majorities.[6]

## Coverage of the Hunter Biden license suspension

Axios reported on June 25, 2024 that the [[dc-court-of-appeals|D.C. Court of Appeals]] had suspended [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s law license following his conviction on three federal gun charges, framing it as the latest fallout from a trial that "aired some of the Biden family's darkest moments."[7] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] singled out that phrasing as "subtle gaslighting to reinforce the regime narrative," arguing the case had barely scratched the surface of the First Family's conduct.[7]

## "The big media era is over" (October 2024)

On October 28, 2024, Axios published a "Behind the Curtain" column declaring that mainstream media dominance in narrative- and reality-shaping in presidential elections had shattered in 2024, and dubbing the contest the "shards of glass" election and news era.[8]
The piece argued that how and where Americans get informed had broken into scores of pieces — from young men on [[joe-rogan|Joe Rogan]]'s podcasts to suburban women following Instagram influencers — with both campaigns targeting small, little-appreciated shards through unorthodox long-form appearances and precision ad targeting.[8]
Axios noted that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s three-hour Rogan conversation drew 33 million views over a weekend, reaching more potential male voters than a dozen or more appearances on Fox News, CNN and MSNBC combined, all of which skew old with median viewer ages of 67 to 70; [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] similarly reached more young women on Alex Cooper's "Call Her Daddy" than on CBS' "60 Minutes" and ABC's "The View" combined.[8]
Memes, prediction markets and long-form podcast interviews, it argued, now shape the conversation as surely as any front page, and this fragmented reality is the future not only of elections but of how America learns about business, products, technology and culture.[8]
Badlands cited the column, alongside Bloomberg's description of a media "doom loop," as an admission that the info war had been lost by legacy outlets.[8]

## Musk–Putin and ballot box fire coverage

Axios also reported in late October 2024 on the national security implications of [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s alleged conversations with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], framing the government's dilemma as continuing its reliance on SpaceX despite the liability or severing ties and losing needed services.[8]
A separate Axios piece covered the fires set in ballot drop boxes in Washington and Oregon on October 28, 2024.[8]

## Coverage of Trump's 2025 plans

Axios published a survey of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s plans for a hypothetical second term, which Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] responded to at length in June 2023 and characterized as a Trump-targeted hit piece.[9] Deschamps described Axios as a left-wing outlet founded by former Politico journalists and argued that it works hand-in-glove with establishment media to propagandize American audiences.[9]

The Axios reporting catalogued Trump's proposals on the federal workforce, education, law enforcement and intelligence, gender issues, crime, housing, cities, and guns, quoting [[norman-ornstein|Norman Ornstein]] of the [[american-enterprise-institute|American Enterprise Institute]] describing the program as "the trappings of a democracy" but in substance "a Potemkin village."[9] Deschamps treated the article as inadvertently useful, arguing that each item Axios framed as alarming identified an institution already captured by [[globalism|globalist]] interests, and that the hostility of the framing was itself evidence the agenda ran against those interests.[9]
