# Reuven Rivlin

Reuven Rivlin is a former President of Israel whose ceremonial powers included designating which party leader would receive the mandate to form a governing coalition after national elections.[1]

## Role in the 2019–2020 coalition deadlock

Following the April 2019 Israeli election, [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]]'s Blue and White party tied with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s [[likud|Likud]], each winning 35 seats; because Likud had won the popular vote, it was given the first chance to form a government.[1] Rivlin set Netanyahu a deadline of May 29, after which Gantz would receive the opportunity to rally support among elected leaders for a coalition.[1]

Netanyahu failed to form a government, but avoided handing the mandate to Gantz by convincing enough members of the [[knesset|Knesset]] to dissolve the legislature on May 30, triggering new elections in September 2019.[1] In that vote Blue and White took more seats than Likud, and Gantz initially succeeded in assembling the necessary support — until a far-left party rescinded its backing at the last minute, prompting Rivlin to grant Netanyahu another chance to form a government.[1] Netanyahu failed again, and when Gantz also failed for a second time, the Knesset was dissolved once more and elections were scheduled for March 2020.[1]

Badlands commentary cited this sequence as an illustration of Netanyahu's political subversion and of the frustrations inherent to a multi-party parliamentary system.[1]
