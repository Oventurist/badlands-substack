# Dennis Meadows

Dennis Meadows is one of the two named leaders — with [[jay-forrester|Jay Forrester]] — of the MIT team whose two-year study produced the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]]'s 1972 report [[limits-to-growth|*Limits to Growth*]].[1]

## Limits to Growth

The book became the bible and blueprint of the anti-humanist movement that, in [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s account, birthed today's [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]] agenda, and holds the record as the most widely read book on ecology, having sold 30 million copies in 32 languages.[1] The Club of Rome–MIT study was not begun in the United States but at Montebello, Quebec in 1971, when Club of Rome backer [[pierre-trudeau|Pierre Trudeau]] allocated taxpayer money to start the project; when the Canadian funds had served their role, funding continued from the Volkswagen Foundation, whose Nazi-supporting past Ehret suggests should have made some of the MIT statisticians uncomfortable.[1]

## Criticism

[[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]] charged that the study was conspicuously fraudulent on two counts: that in attempting to prove industrial society was rapidly using up its remaining natural resources, Meadows and Forrester greatly understated the known quantities of those resources; and, more importantly, that they projected consumption rates using systems of simultaneous linear equations, which builds into the computer projections the assumption that absolutely no technological progress is occurring.[1] LaRouche countered that technological progress, including fundamental redefinitions of what "natural resources" means, had been the outstanding feature of European civilization for five hundred years, and that *Limits to Growth* depended on the assumption that such progress had come to a sudden, absolute stop.[1]

## Later statements

Ehret notes that while Forrester died in 2016, Meadows is still alive, and recently shared what Ehret calls a sick vision of a future in which at most one to two billion people will be granted permission to exist by a ruling oligarchy.[1]
