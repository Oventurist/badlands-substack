# Robert F. Kennedy Jr.

Robert F. Kennedy Jr. ("RFK Jr.") serves as Health Secretary in the second administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and has pursued a broad restructuring of federal vaccine policy, portions of which were blocked by a federal court in March 2026.[1]

## Vaccine policy overhaul

Kennedy's tenure has been defined by efforts to revise federal vaccine guidance, including a plan to reduce the list of recommended childhood immunizations.[1] On January 5, the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] cut the list of routinely recommended childhood vaccines to eleven and lowered the recommendation status for several illnesses, including rotavirus, influenza, and hepatitis A.[1]

Kennedy also removed all seventeen members of the [[advisory-committee-on-immunization-practices|Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices]] in the prior year and appointed new experts to the panel, which helps determine vaccine recommendations and the insurance coverage that follows from them.[1]

## Litigation and the Boston ruling

A federal judge in Boston, U.S. District Judge Brian Murphy, halted parts of Kennedy's effort in a ruling issued on a Monday in March 2026, siding with the [[american-academy-of-pediatrics|American Academy of Pediatrics]] and other medical organizations that had challenged the changes.[1] The plaintiffs argued that federal health officials acted outside legal procedures while advancing the broader restructuring of national immunization policy.[1] The ruling stayed the appointments of Kennedy's replacement advisers and any votes on further policy changes, although the panel was still permitted to hold a scheduled meeting that week.[1]

## 2026 FDA vaccine study withdrawals

The FDA's blocking of several studies supporting the safety of COVID-19 and shingles vaccines came amid broader policy shifts under Kennedy as Secretary of Health and Human Services, a role in which his long record of vaccine criticism framed reporting on the decision.[2] Badlands commentary read the withdrawals as confirmation that the underlying data contradicted official safety claims.[2]


## Allegations against Anthony Fauci (July 2026)

In late July 2026, coinciding with Senator [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]]'s release of the [[fauci-files|Fauci Files]], Kennedy accused [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] of concealing a personal COVID vaccine injury — a pulmonary infarction suffered five months after Fauci received the vaccine in January 2021.[3] Kennedy claimed that Fauci continued promoting the shots and dismissing risks while receiving elite medical care for the condition, and further charged that Fauci's policies produced the highest COVID mortality rate among major countries.[3]

## Campaigning for Trump in Arizona (September 2024)

In September 2024 Kennedy campaigned for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in Glendale, Arizona, returning to the city where he had boosted the GOP nominee at a rally the previous month.[4] He appeared alongside former Democratic Rep. [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] of Hawaii at Arizona Christian University, in a conversation moderated by Substack writer [[jessica-reed-kraus|Jessica Reed Kraus]].[4] The event came weeks after Kennedy ended his own independent presidential campaign and endorsed Trump at an Arizona event.[4]

## Selection of a running mate (March 2024)

In March 2024, Kennedy was reported to be preparing to name Nicole Shanahan — a California-based attorney and entrepreneur once married to Google co-founder Sergey Brin — as the running mate for his independent presidential bid.[5] NFL quarterback Aaron Rodgers, whom Kennedy had been considering, was reported to be out of the running after the prospect of his selection prompted concerns among campaign donors.[5] Shanahan had been behind Kennedy's Super Bowl commercial earlier that year.[5] A source close to the campaign said the pair aligned on numerous issues and that the campaign was seeking a candidate who could help finance the ballot access initiative crucial to Kennedy's chances, adding that Shanahan "might be infusing millions of dollars into the campaign" but "lacks the qualifications to actually do the job."[5]

## Food dye phase-out, April 2025

As U.S. health secretary, Kennedy made synthetic food colourings a signature target, fixating in particular on tartrazine, or Yellow 5, which he warned in a YouTube video was among the "poisons" many Americans were unknowingly eating.[6] On 22 April 2025 the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]], which he oversees, announced the [[2025-fda-synthetic-food-dye-phase-out|phase-out]] of Yellow 5 along with Red 40, Blue 1 and Green 3 by the end of the following year; Kennedy said at a press conference that he had reached an "understanding" with food companies rather than imposing a formal ban, offering scant detail.[6] Badlands contributor [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] praised the initiative alongside Kennedy's campaigns against fluoride and vaccines, but argued that genetically engineered corn, soy, canola and alfalfa remained "the big toxic elephant in the room," a fight complicated by agribusiness dominance and farmers' dependence on those products.[6]

## Media reaction to the 2024 primary campaign

Kennedy was attacked by the national press immediately upon announcing that he would run against President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in 2024, a reaction [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] described in June 2023 as a "media hysteria typhoon."[7] [[cbs|CBS News]] characterized his views as "misleading" and "dangerous," the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] called him a "threat to democracy," and [[npr|NPR]] devoted a segment to attacking him as a person, saying he made debunked and misleading claims that undermine trust in vaccines and offered moral support to "crazed extremists" who rally under the banner of liberty or freedom.[7]

People Magazine, Carlson said, reported none of Kennedy's actual remarks and instead ran a story about his relatives' dislike of him.[7] Kennedy also faced censorship on Instagram and YouTube for raising questions about rises in allergies, asthma, autism and other conditions he associated with vaccines, and was labeled a lunatic, a Nazi and an extremist supporter by the media and medical establishment.[7] Carlson defended his right to ask such questions, concluding that "Bobby Kennedy won't stop asking, and that's why they hate him."[7] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that he did not claim to know which side Kennedy was on, but judged the net effect to be that reachable audiences were learning that questions are what the establishment most fears.[7]

## Libertarian convention debate challenge (May 2024)

In May 2024 Kennedy, running as an independent presidential candidate, challenged [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to a debate at the [[libertarian-party|Libertarian Party]]'s national convention, exploiting their overlapping appearance commitments and describing the venue as "perfect neutral territory."[8] His invitation opened by thanking the former president for spotlighting major media's "rigged polling," saying his campaign shared that concern and that Democrat-aligned pollsters used deceptive methods to pretend Kennedy was languishing in the single digits.[8]

Kennedy cited his campaign's own Zogby polling, which he called the largest and most accurate of the cycle, reporting that Trump beat President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] handily, that Kennedy beat Biden by more, and that Kennedy edged Trump in a head-to-head "nail-biter"; in a three-way race Trump led while Kennedy was closing.[8] He added that two new polls from CNN and Quinnipiac placed him above the 15 percent debate threshold, that an Activote survey had him at 26 percent among young voters, and that he and Trump were tied among America's 70 million independents.[8] The message was not purely flattering: Kennedy said many of Trump's former supporters were backing him because they were upset that Trump "blew up the deficit, shut down their businesses during Covid, and filled your administration with swamp creatures."[8]

## Impeachment infighting among House Democrats (December 2025)

In December 2025 House Democrats split over attempts to impeach Trump administration Cabinet members, after articles of impeachment were filed against Health Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] and Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]].[9] House Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] dismissed the effort, telling Axios: "You know what I'm focused on? Making sure that the American people don't have their health care ripped away from them."[9] Representatives [[pete-aguilar|Pete Aguilar]] of California and [[katherine-clark|Katharine Clark]] of Massachusetts joined Jeffries in a joint statement denouncing the moves, arguing the process would require an immense amount of work to succeed.[9] Badlands contributor [[canncon|CannCon]] mocked the episode with a quip about aliens and lizard-people living among us.[9]

## Children's Health Defense and Reform Pharma (2023)

At the second annual conference of [[childrens-health-defense|Children's Health Defense]], the organization Kennedy founded, the group announced the debut of [[reform-pharma|Reform Pharma]], an initiative aiming to restore the integrity of healthcare by identifying and dismantling the avenues that permit corruption to dominate the healthcare system.[10] Its stated goals include the separation of pharma and state, the prohibition of gain-of-function research, accountability, vaccine manufacturer liability, and a restoration of medical freedom.[10] Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of November 10, 2023, [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] said that while he supported [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for 2024, he was glad to see a candidate advancing concrete, much-needed measures rather than merely nodding along to talking points as had been seen at that week's debate.[10]

## June 2025 dismissal of the CDC vaccine panel

In June 2025 Kennedy fired all 17 experts serving on the [[advisory-committee-on-immunization-practices|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention vaccine advisory panel]], announcing the decision in a *Wall Street Journal* op-ed in which he wrote that "a clean sweep is needed to re-establish public confidence in vaccine science."[11] Former Biden White House COVID-19 response coordinator [[ashish-jha|Ashish Jha]] criticized the move in an interview with CNN's Wolf Blitzer, calling the op-ed "a series of nonsense" about experts "who shape what vaccines, if any, are going to be available to the American people," and describing the purge as "a step in the wrong direction."[11] Jha said he would watch closely to see whom Kennedy appointed next.[11]

## New Hampshire Ballot Access (January 2024)

On the same day President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] learned he had won the New Hampshire Democratic primary as a write-in candidate, Kennedy — running as an independent presidential candidate — announced that his campaign had gathered the required number of signatures to appear on the state's general election ballot in November 2024.[12] His team collected more than 3,000 signatures in a single day in the Granite State, according to a campaign statement.[12] Kennedy thanked "our dedicated supporters and volunteers," adding that "democracy is much more than voting" and that the enthusiasm for collecting signatures, creating new political parties and rallying for change would "get us onto the ballot in every state and fuel our voter registration and GOTV operation."[12]

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] treated the milestone as evidence that Kennedy was becoming a live possibility in 2024, arguing that voters were weary of engineered political polarization.[12] While crediting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with a deeper awareness of the information war — grounded in the Q drops and the [[devolution|Devolution]] research — Esler argued that Kennedy embodies a distinct asset: family legacy, historically an Achilles heel for the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[12] He contrasted the Kennedy line with the Bush, Rockefeller and Rothschild legacies, which he described as multigenerational vehicles for war propaganda, the United Nations and Agenda 2030, and centuries of banking power respectively, concluding that a victory by an "America First family legacy" — whether as president or as Trump's running mate — would carry major symbolic weight.[12]

## March 2025 scandal allegations

In late March 2025 Kennedy, as Health and Human Services Secretary, ousted [[peter-marks|Peter Marks]] from the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]], a move that coincided with a wave of adverse press coverage directed at Kennedy himself.[13] Reports described him as involved in a complex scandal combining allegations of sexual misconduct with political intrigue, drawing attention to his political alliances and potential vulnerabilities.[13] [[candace-owens|Candace Owens]] criticized Kennedy's focus on antisemitism, suggesting it distracted from other issues including an alleged affair and the influence of blackmail on his political decisions.[13]

Badlands contributor [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] framed the coverage as a media operation, asking why "individuals" would not be activated across social media to run the same kind of PSYOPs against cabinet members that were being run against President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s White House.[13] [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] argued the campaign would fail as previous attempts had, citing failed takedowns of [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]], Pam Bondi, and Kash Patel.[13]

### Third-party speculation (September 2023)

In a 8 September 2023 interview with Forbes, Kennedy indicated he might pursue "alternatives" rather than continue contesting the 2024 presidential race solely as a Democrat.[14] The remark prompted commentary from [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]], who argued on 9 September that a Kennedy third-party candidacy would complicate election manipulation because a three-way field makes ballot composition unpredictable.[14] Badlands Media writers cast the episode as part of a broader unravelling of the two-party system.[14]

## Nomination as HHS Secretary (November 2024)

### Post-election comments on the FDA

On Wednesday, November 6, 2024, the day after Trump's win, Kennedy said the bureaucracy of the [[food-and-drug-administration|Food and Drug Administration]] should be winnowed down, telling MSNBC that "there are entire departments, like the nutrition department at the FDA … that have to go—that are not doing their job. They're not protecting our kids."[26] Asked whether he would remove any health agencies, he said, "to eliminate the agencies, as long as it requires congressional approval, I wouldn't be doing that," adding, "I can get the corruption out of the agencies."[26] In a separate Fox News interview he said, "we don't know what I'm going to do. I talked to the president about it yesterday, and he asked me what I wanted, and I said, we're developing a proposal now."[26] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the remarks as strategic rather than conciliatory, arguing Kennedy and Trump had no intention of waiting for congressional procedures before beginning to dismantle the [[deep-state|Deep State]], citing advice given by Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]] in an interview with [[zak-paine|Zak Paine]].[26]

On Thursday, November 14, 2024, President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that he had picked Kennedy to serve as secretary of the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]], a selection CNN framed as adding to a list of provocative picks whose confirmation processes would test the loyalty of Senate Republicans.[15] Trump said in a post on X that "The Safety and Health of all Americans is the most important role of any Administration," and that HHS would help ensure Americans were protected from "harmful chemicals, pollutants, pesticides, pharmaceutical products, and food additives that have contributed to the overwhelming Health Crisis in this Country."[15] He added that Kennedy would "restore these Agencies to the traditions of Gold Standard Scientific Research, and beacons of Transparency, to end the Chronic Disease epidemic, and to Make America Great and Healthy Again!"[15]

Kennedy accepted the offer that Thursday, having spent several days in Palm Beach, Florida following the election.[15] CNN described him as one of the nation's most prominent anti-vaccine conspiracy theorists, saying he had frequently spread false claims about vaccine safety and efficacy.[15]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that floating Kennedy as a potential remedy to one of the most subversive systems comprising the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s power projection apparatus — and its profit sector — was one thing, but actualizing the appointment was "something else entirely."[15] He restated his view that the sum total of Trump's second-term appointments should be treated as narrative deployments, and argued that installing the son of Robert Kennedy and the nephew of John F. Kennedy was "one of the most emphatic war-like moves Trump has made on the public stage against the Deep State."[15]

## Reported independent presidential run (October 2023)

In early October 2023, Kennedy — who had launched his campaign for the Democratic presidential nomination in April of that year — was reported to be preparing to run as an independent instead.[16] Mediaite reported that he planned to make the announcement on October 9 in Philadelphia, citing a text message indicating the campaign would run "attack ads" against the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]] to "pave the way" for the switch.[16] A campaign insider told the outlet that "Bobby feels that the DNC is changing the rules to exclude his candidacy so an independent run is the only way to go."[16]

The New York Times separately reported that Kennedy had met [[libertarian-party|Libertarian Party]] chair Angela McArdle in July at a Memphis conference; McArdle said he "emphasized that he was committed to running as a Democrat but said that he considered himself very libertarian," and that the two were "aligned on a lot of issues," including the threat of the "[[deep-state|deep state]]."[16] Matt Bennett, co-founder of the Democratic group Third Way — which was coordinating efforts to block the No Labels third-party ticket — said it "would be very bad" if Kennedy ran on the Libertarian line.[16]

Badlands contributor Paul Fleuret placed the move within a broader pattern of Democratic officeholders abandoning the party mid-term, calling Kennedy the biggest name yet to leave the Democrats, with the possible exception of [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], and predicting the exodus would eventually register in the 2024 polls.[16] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued an independent run pre-empted the expected scenario in which Kennedy would be cheated out of the nomination, and speculated about a 2024 cycle in which establishment candidates ([[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and, hypothetically, [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]]) were eclipsed by two populists — [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Kennedy — as part of a "controlled demolition" of the two-party system.[16]

## Reversal on the border wall (August 2023)

A trip to the U.S.–Mexico border in Arizona changed Kennedy's position on the border wall long advocated by his political rival [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[17] Speaking on 3 August 2023 to a crowd of more than 300 at the Saban Theatre premiere of his documentary *Midnight at the Border*, Kennedy said: "I went down to the border feeling that Trump has made a mistake on the wall, but I feel like people need to be able to recalibrate their worldview when they're confronted with evidence."[17] At the press conference that followed, asked about rumours of a Trump–Kennedy ticket in 2024, he ruled out the Republican–Democrat pairing, quipping that "a lot of the stuff that you read in the mainstream news and the corporate news is what I would call conspiracy theories" before stating flatly, "No, I will not be Donald Trump's vice president."[17]

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the reversal as consistent with the theory of a narrative and political pincer movement executed jointly by Trump and Kennedy.[17] While acknowledging that many in the MAGA and America First movement found other Kennedy policy positions distasteful, he argued that reaching undecided and middle-ground voters — people who had voted for Trump in neither 2016 nor 2020 — first requires getting them "in the door."[17] Kennedy's adoption of pro-wall rhetoric, in this reading, was a catastrophic turn for establishment talking points, since it aligned the populist candidates of both major parties in the public mind on national sovereignty and border security.[17]

## Formation of the 2024 alliance with Trump

Reporting by [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] in September 2024 detailed how Kennedy's partnership with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took shape.[18] About three hours after Trump was nearly assassinated on a Saturday evening in mid-July 2024, Kennedy received a phone call from [[calley-means|Calley Means]], a health care entrepreneur who had advised him on chronic disease policy, asking whether he would consider joining forces with Trump or serving as his running mate.[18] Means suggested the moment called for unity because Trump had just narrowly escaped the same fate that had befallen Kennedy's father and uncle; Kennedy, then running an independent presidential campaign, said he was not interested in the vice presidency and ended the call, before ringing back a short while later to say he would speak with Trump.[18]

The calls set off what the Times described as a frenzy of calculations and soul-searching inside the Kennedy camp over whether an alliance with Trump would give Kennedy more power to address chronic disease, censorship, corporate reach into government agencies and the war in Ukraine, or whether it would tear apart his coalition and his family.[18] Trump was not at that point seriously considering adding Kennedy to the ticket, but Means's efforts presented an opening to bring Kennedy into the fold and remove him as a potential drain on Trump's votes.[18]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] used the story to revisit her earlier prediction that [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] would be Trump's running mate, a call [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] had challenged her on.[18] She wrote that her logic — the pursuit of a unity ticket — had been sound but too small in scope: uniting the Republican Party was ambitious, but uniting the nation by capturing "the neglected middle of the American People" was more so, and the unity ticket with Kennedy had the potential to change the course of history.[18] In her framing, Trump was building a new party founded on the liberty ideals of the American founding, and a November 5 victory would drive a stake through the corrupted dark-money [[uniparty|uniparty]] system.[18]

## Guardrails from the Trump Transition (January 2025)

Months after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] promised to let Kennedy "go wild" on health care as a member of his Cabinet, some of the president-elect's advisers were quietly working to box him in, according to January 2025 reporting.[19] Transition officials planned to install several longtime GOP allies in senior roles across the health department, filling key positions well before Kennedy could be confirmed as Health and Human Services secretary.[19] The stated aim was to surround him with conservative policymakers who could compensate for his lack of government experience and MAGA credentials, while ensuring the White House could keep close tabs on a nominee many Trump aides did not fully trust — an account attributed to a half-dozen Republicans granted anonymity to discuss private deliberations.[19]

## MAHA report and Ecuador delegation (May 2025)

An expected report from Kennedy on Thursday, May 22, 2025, assessing the causes of chronic disease in children, was described as a test of whether Republicans in [[congress|Congress]] could get along with a health secretary keen on regulating farm and food companies.[20] Republican lawmakers from agriculture and food-manufacturing districts had warned him to lay off, fearing the [[make-america-healthy-again-commission|Make America Healthy Again Commission]] report would point to pesticides and food dyes as causes of childhood disease and propose profit-cutting regulation, or otherwise dampen demand for their constituents' products.[20] Most congressional Republicans had endorsed [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s decision to let Kennedy "go wild" on health care, but had lately asked pointed questions about the report's contents, given Kennedy's repeated claim that food companies make people sick for profit.[20] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] defended the focus, arguing that pursuing Big Food and Big Ag and pursuing the pharmaceutical cartel are not mutually exclusive.[20]

On the same day, Trump announced that Kennedy, as U.S. Secretary of Health and Human Services, would lead the Presidential Delegation to Quito for the inauguration of [[daniel-noboa|Daniel Noboa]] as President of the Republic of [[ecuador|Ecuador]] on May 24, 2025.[20]

## Criticism of Trump ballot removal efforts (December 2023)

Facing a monumental challenge to get on the ballot in all 50 states and the District of Columbia, Kennedy continued to criticise legal efforts to prevent former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from appearing on the ballot in multiple states.[21] The [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] had on 19 December 2023 declared Trump ineligible in that state under Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]], which bars from office those who have engaged in insurrection.[21] Kennedy, who announced on 9 October that he would run in 2024 as an independent rather than as a Democrat because the [[democratic-national-committee|Democrat National Committee]] was "rigging the primary," expressed his disapproval of the decision several times.[21]

On X he wrote that the ruling "makes America look like a Banana Republic," asking why every American did not understand that if such a thing could be done to a former US president, "EVERYONE is vulnerable to punishment for crimes with which they have never been convicted," leaving democracy "a total shambles."[21] In a further post he warned that if Trump were kept out of office "through judicial fiat rather than being defeated in a fair election, his supporters will never accept the result," and that "this country will become ungovernable."[21] The remarks were published under the headline framing that America was "headed to a system where the elites pick our leadership."[21]

## Independent announcement and family denunciation (October 2023)

Kennedy launched his independent bid on October 9, 2023, speaking in front of the National Constitution Center in Philadelphia — within view of where the first and only independent candidate to win the American presidency helped establish the new country's government — and telling supporters he believed he would take a similar path to the White House.[22] "Today, we are turning a new page in American politics," he said, adding, "There have been independent candidates before. But this time is different. This time, the independent is going to win."[22] He argued that "three-fourths of Americans believe President [[joe-biden|Biden]] is too old to govern effectively" while "President [[donald-trump|Trump]] faces multiple civil and criminal trials," with both carrying favorability ratings "deep in negative territory," and said the country needed "to pry loose the hammerlock of corrupt power over Washington D.C. and make this nation ours again."[22]

He acknowledged the cost of leaving the [[democratic-party|Democratic Party]], calling it "painful for me to let go of the party of my uncles, my father, of my grandfather, and of both of my great-grandfathers—John 'Honey Fitz' Fitzgerald, Boston's first Irish Catholic mayor, and Patrick Kennedy, a Boston ward boss—who, together, launched my family's political dynasty more than a century ago," while comparing his own sacrifice to the risk taken by the founders who signed the Declaration of Independence 247 years earlier nearby at Independence Hall.[22] "What really terrifies the elites, though, is not me. It is what I represent—a populist movement that defies the left-right division," he continued.[22]

His siblings responded immediately. In a statement posted on Twitter/X, Kerry Kennedy, Rory Kennedy, Joseph P. Kennedy II, and Kathleen Kennedy Townsend said that while their brother has the [[kennedy-family|Kennedy]] name, he does not share the Kennedy "values, vision, or judgement," called the announcement "deeply saddening," and stated that they "denounce his candidacy" while endorsing Biden.[22] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the rebuke as a play to "Kennedy Democrat" illusions designed to paint Kennedy as crazy, doubted it would work given disillusionment with the party, and argued that for Trump supporters "a Kennedy that's not a Kennedy" amounted to the best endorsement he could have received — concluding that on net the move to independent status helps Trump.[22]

## Delay of the "healthy" food labeling rule (February 2025)

In February 2025 Kennedy delayed a 2024 [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] rule redefining which foods may be labelled "healthy," a move framed as a potential setback for the [[make-america-healthy-again|Make America Healthy Again]] movement.[23] The rule had been scheduled to take effect that week; Kennedy signed off on a delay until April 28 in order to review its terms and make any changes, as it was introduced before his confirmation as secretary of the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]].[23] Under the rule, foods must contain certain nutrients such as protein and whole grains and minimal harmful ingredients like sugar and saturated fat, disqualifying high-sugar cereals, protein bars, and yogurts, while newly qualifying olive oil, salmon, canned vegetables, and — for the first time — water and coffee.[23]

Badlands commentator Jordan Sather used the episode to warn against pendulum swings between extremes, from veganism to carnivore diets and from liberal-atheistic to religiously fundamentalist politics, arguing the movement needs balance and continued questioning of official narratives from political, scientific, media, and religious authorities alike.[23]

Kennedy's arrival at HHS also coincided with the unexplained cancellation of the March 2025 meeting of the [[vaccines-and-related-biological-products-advisory-committee|Vaccines and Related Biological Products Advisory Committee]], reported amid a flu season that [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] data suggested was on track to be the most severe in at least a decade.[23]

## Autism root-cause studies timeline (May 2025)

In a May 2025 interview on *The Source with Kaitlan Collins*, Kennedy said studies to determine the root cause of autism — an effort led by his federal agency as part of Making America Healthy Again — could be completed around March.[24] He explained that some studies would be totally completed by September and would mainly be replication studies of work already done, adding that new teams comprising 15 groups of scientists were being deployed and that grants would go out to bid within three weeks; other studies would conclude six months after September.[24] "We're going to begin to have a lot of information by September. We're not going to stop the studies in September. We're going to be definitive. And the more definitive you are, the more it drives public policy," he said.[24]

The pledge followed Kennedy's April promise to get to the root of the autism epidemic, citing CDC data showing 1 in 31 American children have autism — another rise in what he has described as an epidemic.[24] Badlands contributor [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] offered his own two main causes: toxins from vaccines, fluoride, GMO food, artificial dyes, sweeteners, microplastics and household chemicals; and lack of nutrition from poor diet.[24]

## Bipartisan opposition to his HHS nomination (January 2025)

In late January 2025, pressure mounted on Kennedy as critics squeezed senators from both parties to oppose President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s pick to be the nation's top health official.[25] His bipartisan opponents included liberal advocates and an organization founded by former Vice President [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]], all arguing that the nominee to lead the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] was not fit to serve as secretary.[25] Liberal critics pointed to Kennedy's longtime advocacy against vaccines and his role as founder of the prominent anti-vaccine organization [[childrens-health-defense|Children's Health Defense]].[25]

## Voter fraud complaint, January 2025

With less than two weeks remaining before [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s inauguration, Kennedy — Trump's pick to lead Health and Human Services — faced a new legal challenge over alleged voter fraud.[27] The left-leaning watchdog group [[accountable-us|Accountable.US]] filed a complaint on the morning of January 8, 2025, shared exclusively with Politico, alleging that Kennedy committed felony election fraud when he voted in the 2024 election.[27] Filed with the [[new-york-state-board-of-elections|New York State Board of Elections]], the complaint called on the agency's Division of Election Law Enforcement to investigate Kennedy for "registering for and voting" from "a New York residence at which he does not legally reside."[27]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] questioned the sudden interest of leftist watchdog groups in voter integrity, asking whether they were also examining non-citizens on the voter rolls, while allowing that Kennedy should be held accountable if he did in fact commit voter fraud.[27]

## Weaponization subcommittee testimony (July 2023)

Kennedy, then chairman on leave of [[childrens-health-defense|Children's Health Defense]], testified before the [[house-weaponization-committee|Select Subcommittee on the Weaponization of the Federal Government]] in a hearing marred by contentious interruptions and by attempts from House Democrats to remove him as a witness.[28] The hearing was convened to examine the federal government's role in censoring Americans, Big Tech's collusion with government agencies, and the ongoing [[missouri-v-biden|Missouri v. Biden]] lawsuit.[28] Fellow witnesses included [[d-john-sauer|D. John Sauer]], counsel for the Missouri v. Setting aside his prepared testimony to respond to a letter signed by 102 people urging that he not be heard, Kennedy told the committee that the signatories were "itself evidence of the problem that this hearing was convened to address," calling it "an attempt to censor a censorship hearing."[28] He also argued: "We need to be able to talk. And, the First Amendment was not written for easy speech. It was written for the speech that nobody likes you for."[28]

The [[harvard-harris-poll|Harvard CAPS/Harris]] survey released the following Sunday — with data gathered July 19–20, during the hearing — found Kennedy the most favorable public figure at 47 percent, up two points and ahead of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at 45 percent, though it showed him trailing [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] 22 percent to 44 percent among Democratic primary voters.[28]

## Restrictions on pediatric gender-affirming care

On Thursday, December 18, 2025, Kennedy and Dr. [[mehmet-oz|Mehmet Oz]], who leads Medicaid and Medicare, announced at the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]] headquarters in Washington, D.C. a set of measures that would effectively ban gender-affirming care for transgender young people even in states where it remains legal.[29] "So-called gender affirming care has inflicted lasting physical and psychological damage on vulnerable young people," Kennedy said. "This is not medicine. It is malpractice."[29] The measures take the form of two proposed rules barring federal Medicaid reimbursement for such care to patients under 18 and blocking all Medicaid and Medicare funding for any services at hospitals that provide pediatric gender-affirming care.[29] The [[american-academy-of-pediatrics|American Academy of Pediatrics]] objected that the policies "misconstrue the current medical consensus."[29]

## 2023 Los Angeles campaign event security incident

On Friday, September 15, 2023, an armed man was taken into custody at a Los Angeles campaign event for Kennedy, then running for the Democratic presidential nomination, according to a spokesperson for the [[lapd|Los Angeles Police Department]].[30] Police identified the suspect as [[adrian-paul-aispuro|Adrian Paul Aispuro]], 44, who was held on felony charges and $35,000 bail.[30]

Kennedy wrote on X, formerly known as Twitter, that the man had worn shoulder holsters with loaded pistols and spare ammunition magazines and carried a U.S. Marshal badge on a lanyard along with a belt-clip federal ID.[30] According to Kennedy's post, his private security detail detained the suspect until LAPD arrived, after the man allegedly attempted to approach him while claiming to be part of his security detail.[30]

The same day Kennedy reiterated his request to the [[white-house|White House]] for [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]] protection, writing that he was "still entertaining a hope that President Biden will allow me Secret Service protection" and that he was "the first presidential candidate in history to whom the White House has denied a request for protection."[30] The Secret Service states on its website that major presidential and vice presidential candidates and their spouses are protected within 120 days of a general presidential election.[30]

Badlands Media commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Kennedy and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were effecting a "narrative pincer" against the establishment, and that the episode exposed both the establishment's disregard for Kennedy's safety and its attempts to conflate threats against him with the MAGA, America First and Q movements.[30]

## Cabinet overtures to Kamala Harris (August 2024)

In August 2024, while still running as an independent presidential candidate, Kennedy sought a meeting with Democratic nominee [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] to discuss the possibility of serving in her administration — perhaps as a Cabinet secretary — if he threw his support behind her campaign and she won, according to Kennedy campaign officials.[31] Harris and her advisers did not respond with an offer to meet or show interest in the proposal, according to people familiar with the conversations.[31]

The outreach, made through intermediaries, followed a meeting in Milwaukee the previous month between Kennedy and Republican nominee [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to discuss a similar policy role and endorsement, which produced no agreement; in those discussions Kennedy spoke about advising Trump in a second term on health and medical issues.[31] Kennedy was exploring multiple options for the future of his presidential effort, which had declined in national polling after President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] dropped out of the race.[31] His campaign produced polling, which it tried to share with Democrats, claiming that both Harris and Trump would gain public support across 31 states if either announced publicly that Kennedy would have a Cabinet role in their administration.[31]

## June 2025 ACIP replacements and press criticism

Following his removal of all 17 members of the [[advisory-committee-on-immunization-practices|CDC's Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices]] — justified with the statement that "a clean sweep is necessary to reestablish public confidence in vaccine science" — Kennedy drew a warning from editorial critics that restoring Americans' trust in federal public-health decisions required hiring "truly fair-minded, science-driven folks — not anti-vax crackpots," and that his agenda risked endangering the entire [[donald-trump|Trump]] reform drive.[32] Critics noted that most of the dismissed members had been appointed only the previous year to terms running through 2028, and that Joe Biden had set a precedent for removing nominally nonpartisan advisory-board members.[32] The same commentary judged several of the eight new ACIP members Kennedy announced later that week to be solid scientists, including biostatistician Martin Kulldorff, a co-author of the [[great-barrington-declaration|Great Barrington Declaration]], and pediatrician Cody Meissner.[32]

## Transition to Health and Human Services (November 2024)

Named by [[donald-trump|Trump]] to lead the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]], Kennedy found his advisers rebuffed when they attempted repeatedly to open coordination with the agency in November 2024, a casualty of the transition's unsigned ethics and transparency agreements.[33] A CBS/YouGov poll released the same month found that an overwhelming majority of self-identified Trump voters supported his cabinet picks, Kennedy among them.[33]

## Leaked Trump phone call (July 2024)

On July 16, 2024 a leaked video surfaced of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] speaking to Kennedy by phone, which the *New York Times* framed as an effort to coax the independent candidate out of the presidential race and into Trump's camp.[34] On Kennedy's speakerphone, Trump — by then the Republican nominee — offered up the kind of anti-vaccine message Kennedy was known for, saying he wanted "to do small doses," revealed that his call with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had been "very nice," and told Kennedy: "I would love you to do something. And I think it'll be so good for you and so big for you. And we're going to win."[34] Trump also marveled on the call that he had been shot with an AR-15-type rifle of the sort Biden wanted to ban, and described the wound to his ear as feeling "like the world's largest mosquito."[34]

Kennedy apologized almost immediately on X, where the video had appeared, writing that he had been taping with an in-house videographer when Trump called, that he "should have ordered the videographer to stop recording immediately," and that he was "mortified that this was posted."[34] Badlands contributor Ashe in America argued the recording did not explicitly show Trump trying to recruit Kennedy, since the request was unspecified, and defended the vaccine remarks as an unremarkable case for smaller infant dosing schedules.[34]

## Secret Service protection (July 2024)

Following the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Trump]], President Biden directed the [[us-secret-service|Secret Service]] to protect Kennedy, an announcement made by Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] at a White House press briefing on Monday, July 15, 2024: "In light of this weekend's events, the president has directed me to work with the Secret Service to provide protection to Robert Kennedy Jr."[34] The Kennedy campaign had been requesting protection long before the attempt on Trump's life, and the family was described as painfully aware of the risk, both Kennedy's father Robert F. Kennedy and his uncle John F. Kennedy having been assassinated in the 1960s.[34] At the time Kennedy was still working to appear on ballots across the country, and while his candidacy was considered a long shot, his events continued to draw crowds.[34]

## September 2025 Senate Finance Committee hearing

At a contentious Senate Finance Committee hearing in September 2025, Kennedy, as Health Secretary, rejected his own department's Covid-19 statistics, telling senators "I don't know how many died," and adding, "I don't think anybody knows, because there was so much data chaos coming out of the C.D.C."[35] The [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] put the U.S. death count from Covid-19 at 1.2 million.[35]

When Senator [[elizabeth-warren|Elizabeth Warren]] said that it was becoming harder for people to obtain Covid shots at pharmacies, Kennedy denied it, retorting: "Everyone can get access to them."[35] He also claimed that mRNA vaccines "cause serious harm, including death, particularly in young people," although the CDC had found "no increased risk of death" from the shots.[35] Critics said his defiant performance illustrated what happens when someone without medical or scientific training and with a conspiracy-minded approach takes charge of national public health.[35]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] host [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded under the heading "Propaganda, Pressure, and Personal Responsibility," arguing that "parents don't consent because the danger is real - they consent because they've been systematically scared into it," and that children were being injected with experimental substances against diseases that could not kill them.[35] He concluded that resisting institutional pressure and exercising discernment were individual responsibilities, warning that "as long as we let authority tell us what's true, we'll keep being deceived."[35]

## Senate confirmation as HHS Secretary (February 2025)

On Thursday, February 13, 2025, the [[us-senate|Senate]] confirmed Kennedy as Secretary of Health and Human Services by a vote of 52-48, giving the longtime vaccine skeptic — who had vowed to take on "big pharma" and ultra-processed food — authority over the nation's food and health care systems.[36] Kennedy, then 71 and a longtime environmental lawyer, drew a no vote from Senator [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]] (R-Ky.), who survived polio before Jonas Salk's vaccine breakthrough in the 1950s and had raised concerns about Kennedy's position on vaccination.[36]

The confirmation coincided with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s executive action establishing the President's [[make-america-healthy-again|Make America Healthy Again]] Commission, which cited lagging US life expectancy, widespread chronic disease, and sharply rising rates of childhood conditions as justification for aggressive federal action.[36] Badlands contributor [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] highlighted Kennedy's argument that America "can't be a strong nation if we have a weak citizenry" and that the country needed someone with "the spine and the guts to challenge orthodoxies and break institutions that have turned against our democracy."[36]

## Impeachment calls after September 2025 Senate hearing

Calls for Kennedy's impeachment and demands for his resignation escalated in early September 2025 following a contentious hearing with Senate lawmakers.[37] Conservative commentator [[william-kristol|Bill Kristol]] declared "I've seen enough," joining the calls.[37]

Senators criticized Kennedy over recent moves including his firing of [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] Director [[susan-monarez|Susan Monarez]], a [[donald-trump|Trump]] appointee, and his dismissal of the full panel of the federal vaccine advisory board.[37] Democrats accused him of putting the public in great danger by spreading misinformation and gutting evidence-based vaccine research, while a growing number of GOP critics suggested Kennedy could become a liability as Republicans headed into the midterm elections.[37]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the episode as part of a 2025 information-war battlespace in which Trump's opponents include "intra-MAGA pretenders," the "Con Inc. Doom Brigade" and former mainstream-media figures turned podcasters, arguing that according to Lt. Gen. Steve Kwast this pressure does not trouble Trump.[37]

## Push to ban television drug advertising

As [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s pick to lead the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]], Kennedy — a known critic of the pharmaceutical industry — repeatedly called for a ban on television advertising of prescription drugs, an effort the administration was expected to push but which Quartz reported would not be easy, as Big Pharma would not go down without a fight.[38] Past efforts to regulate such ads have faced legal challenges, often over First Amendment concerns, and prescription drug advertising on U.S. television exploded in the late 1990s after the Food and Drug Administration relaxed its guidelines, allowing drug companies to emphasize a drug's benefits with only a brief summary of side effects.[38]

## ACIP dismissals and industry response

As Health and Human Services Secretary, Kennedy dismissed all members of the CDC's Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices over conflict-of-interest concerns.[39] In response, vaccine industry groups with ties to Merck, Sanofi, and Pfizer — joined by pharmacists, medical associations, state health officials, and a new advocacy group — launched a private coalition working with the University of Minnesota's Vaccine Integrity Project to build a "nongovernmental vaccine system" that would order vaccines directly from manufacturers and follow medical-association recommendations instead of ACIP's.[39]


## Autism causes initiative

The [[2025-white-house-tylenol-autism-announcement|September 22, 2025 White House announcement]] of a wide-ranging federal effort to study the causes of autism came as the [[make-america-healthy-again|Make America Healthy Again]] movement pressed for answers, with AP News noting that the coalition of supporters of Health Secretary Kennedy includes several anti-vaccine activists who have long spread debunked claims that immunizations are responsible.[40]

## 2025 HHS confirmation hearing

On Wednesday, January 29, 2025, Kennedy appeared before the [[senate-finance-committee|Senate Finance Committee]] as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s nominee for Secretary of Health and Human Services, the first of a series of executive branch confirmation hearings held on Capitol Hill that day.[41] During the heated hearing, Democratic Senator [[sheldon-whitehouse|Sheldon Whitehouse]] of Rhode Island told Kennedy that he frightens people, referring specifically to his stance on vaccines, while Senator [[ron-wyden|Ron Wyden]] of Oregon argued that "the receipts show" Kennedy embraces anti-vaccine "quacks" and "charlatans."[41] The hearing was disrupted by protesters, six of whom were ultimately arrested.[41] Confirmation hearings for [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]], Trump's pick to lead the [[department-of-commerce|Department of Commerce]], and [[kelly-loeffler|Kelly Loeffler]], nominated to head the [[small-business-administration|Small Business Administration]], also took place that day.[41]

## Supreme Court denial of the Children's Health Defense COVID appeal (January 2025)

On Monday, January 13, 2025, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] blocked Kennedy's [[childrens-health-defense|Children's Health Defense]] from stopping investigations of doctors in Washington state over possible COVID-19 misinformation.[43] The ruling came after Justice [[elena-kagan|Elena Kagan]] had denied the bid in November; Kennedy's group and the doctors then asked Justice [[clarence-thomas|Clarence Thomas]] to review their application for an injunction pending appeal, and Thomas referred the matter to the full court.[43] None of the justices noted any disagreement with the denial.[43] Kennedy, then President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s nominee to lead the Department of Health and Human Services, had founded CHD, which joined other plaintiffs in arguing that investigations seeking to sanction doctors for their views on COVID would violate free speech rights under the First Amendment.[43]
