# Equestrian Order of the Holy Sepulchre

The Equestrian Order of the Holy Sepulchre of Jerusalem, also called the Knights of the Holy Sepulchre, is a papal knighthood established in 1098 — twenty years before the founding of the [[knights-templar|Templars]] by Bernard of Clairvaux.[1]

## Structure and mission

The Jerusalem crosses became affiliated with the Templars before that order was dissolved, at least publicly, and appeared on the insignia of the Equestrian Order.[1] The article states the Order currently has some 30,000 members under a structure "not too dissimilar" to that of the Jesuit Generals, and that in Freemasonic fashion it is organized around a Grand Master with a chain of command of obedience down to the lower degrees.[1] According to the sect's own website, the Knighthood is devoted to "absolute fidelity to the Popes" and seeks to "sustain and aid … the Catholic Church in the Holy Land."[1]

In a footnote, the article describes the Order as a Catholic order of knighthood under the protection of the Holy See, with the pope as its sovereign, creating both canons and knights with the primary mission to "support the Christian presence in the Holy Land," numbering roughly 30,000 knights and dames across 60 lieutenancies worldwide, with Fernando Filoni as cardinal grand master since 2019 and the Latin patriarch of Jerusalem as grand prior; its headquarters are at the Palazzo Della Rovere in Rome.[1]

## Present-day priorities and imagery

Among the Order's priorities today are the funding and maintenance of religious schools across Palestine, Israel and the broader Middle East.[1] The article displays an image of a Good Friday ritual celebrated by Knights of the Sepulchre in Bolivia and remarks sardonically on its similarity to the Ku Klux Klan, which it says emerged out of the Masonic Knights of the Golden Circle under [[albert-pike|Albert Pike's]] command in the 19th century.[1]
