# Israel Defense Forces

The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) are the armed forces of [[israel|Israel]].[1]

## 2026 Iran campaign

According to reporting collected in March 2026, U.S. and Israeli forces launched coordinated strikes on [[iran|Iran]] in late February 2026, prompting retaliatory attacks by Tehran across the region.[1] The IDF said it had struck dozens of targets in Tehran, describing the operation as part of a campaign to deepen the blow against the Iranian regime.[1]

The strikes accompanied statements by Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] that he did not know whether Iranians would overthrow their leadership and that Israel could target Iran's Supreme Leader if escalation continued, and by Foreign Minister [[gideon-saar|Gideon Saar]] that only the Iranian people could bring down the government but might need outside help.[1] Iranian official [[ali-larijani|Ali Larijani]] responded that the war would not end until Iran's enemies were made "sorry for their grave miscalculation."[1]

## Operations in southern Lebanon (March 2026)

By March 2026 the IDF had been operating inside [[lebanon|Lebanon]] for months, having broken the ceasefire with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] when the Iran war began, according to Badlands commentary.[2] Fighting intensified after March 2, when Hezbollah launched missiles into Israel and Israel responded with evacuation orders, expanded strikes, and ground activity; Lebanese authorities reported more than 1,070 deaths and over one million displaced.[2] On March 24, Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] announced that Israeli forces would occupy territory up to the [[litani-river|Litani River]] and control bridges and a broader "security zone."[2]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that the IDF was bombing Christian villages in the south in the hope of driving out their populations, and that Hezbollah had come to those communities' defense with aid.[2] He also reported that the IDF accidentally killed Father Pierre al-Rahi that month with a tank round mistakenly fired in Qlayaa, and characterized the force as "the invading imperial army of Greater Israel."[2]

## April 2026 posture

Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] declared the IDF ready for both defense and offense against [[iran|Iran]], with new targets marked, as Israeli leadership awaited a US green light to resume strikes and intensified intelligence work and missile restocking.[3] The IDF also formally established an "Advanced Defense Zone" on the Lebanese side of the Israel–Lebanon border, warning that anyone found inside it would be shot.[3] Its pattern of strikes on Lebanese journalists, including the bombing of their homes, prompted the Lebanese Prime Minister to accuse [[israel|Israel]] of war crimes.[3]

Badlands commentary questioned whether the IDF could prevail against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] without American assistance, noting that it had failed to annihilate [[hamas|Hamas]] in Gaza even with US support, and asked which would break first: the IDF or Israeli public support for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]].[3]

## Attrition thesis in the 2026 Iran war

Commenting on the April 2026 decision by [[planet-labs|Planet Labs]] to withhold satellite imagery of [[iran|Iran]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] advanced the possibility that Israel and Iran are engaged in an actual conflict while [[donald-trump|Trump]] merely pretends to or exaggerates U.S. involvement for narrative purposes.[4] The stated goal would be to wear down the IDF while it is stretched thin across multiple fronts and to exhaust the Israeli public's appetite for warmongering and empire building, potentially collapsing what he called the Jewish supremacy political movement and producing a secular state detached from Zionism.[4] He further speculated that the [[4th-psychological-operations-group|4th Psyop Group]] may be running an information operation against the IDF and [[mossad|Mossad]] themselves, leaving them convinced the U.S. is fighting an earnest war while they continue supplying intelligence to Washington.[4]

## Sde Teiman abuse case and dropped charges

In March 2026, Israeli military prosecutors dropped charges against several IDF soldiers accused of sexually assaulting a male Palestinian detainee at the [[sde-teiman|Sde Teiman]] detention facility during the Gaza war, citing evidentiary issues after months of investigation.[5] The investigation had been opened after video footage of the alleged abuse surfaced, leading to arrests and prompting public debate inside [[israel|Israel]] about the treatment of Palestinian detainees.[5] Badlands Media reported that IDF military police documentation, hospital medical records, and security-camera footage recorded the assault, in which the detainee was sodomized with objects including a knife, causing internal injuries requiring surgery.[5] Military lawyers who leaked the footage were later identified, fired, and prosecuted for what critics in Israel called a betrayal of Israeli values.[5]

The arrests triggered demonstrations — later dubbed the "Right to Rape" protests — in which settlers led by [[knesset|Knesset]] members and National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] stormed the base holding the accused, forcing IDF soldiers to fight back physically.[5] One of the accused, [[meir-shitrit|Meir Shitrit]], appeared unmasked on Israel Channel 14.[5]

## March 2026 strikes on Tehran

The IDF said it struck dozens of sites in Tehran as part of a campaign to deepen the blow against the Iranian regime.[5] Iranian official [[ali-larijani|Ali Larijani]] responded that the war would not end before Iran's enemies were made "sorry for their grave miscalculation."[5] Badlands analysis paired the strikes with [[haaretz|Haaretz]] reporting that Iranian missiles had penetrated the [[iron-dome|Iron Dome]] and struck central Israel, arguing that Israeli defenses had failed.[5]

## Warning on settler violence (2026)

A senior IDF general commanding in the West Bank warned in 2026 that rising extremist violence by Israeli civilians could spark a broader Palestinian uprising, a concern raised amid assaults, trespassing cases, and a widely reported attack on a French nun at a Jerusalem biblical site.[6] Israeli authorities made arrests in several cases, but officials acknowledged gaps in enforcement capacity — notably in the Negev, where a comptroller report tied rampant crime to police manpower shortages.[6] The warning signaled growing concern in Israel's security establishment that internal instability, not only external threats, could drive the next phase of conflict.[6]

The IDF was also at the center of a domestic confrontation over conscription: settlers stormed the home of the Chief of Military Prisons in anger at the arrest of Ultra-Orthodox students refusing to comply with conscription orders, after the Supreme Court found their long-standing exemption had never been codified into law.[6] See [[jewish-terrorism|Jewish terrorism]] for fuller treatment.[6]

## Manpower crisis warning (March 2026)

In March 2026, IDF Chief of Staff Lt.-Gen. [[eyal-zamir|Eyal Zamir]] warned a security cabinet meeting that the IDF "could soon collapse if there is no solution to the shortage of manpower," saying, "I am raising 10 red flags before the IDF collapses into itself," as confirmed by *The Jerusalem Post*.[7]

Badlands analysis described the underlying structure: the IDF is a relatively small fighting force that has never had to fight a protracted war lasting longer than about a month, and its shortfall of enlisted soldiers has been offset by mobilizing reservists, who remain liable for service for years after their mandated two years of active duty.[7] Because reservists constitute the bulk of the Israeli workforce, mobilization sustained over roughly two and a half years has generated significant economic pressure, with small businesses unable to stay open or find labor and consequent breakdowns in supply chains, logistics, and services - compounded by citizens fleeing war-zone conditions.[7]

The force has reportedly been short between 10,000 and 15,000 men since October 7, and over-reliance on reserves has pushed some reservists through as many as six or seven combat tours.[7] Roughly 375,000 reservists had been mobilized since October 7, and the [[knesset|Knesset]] approved the mobilization of an additional 400,000.[7] With Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] announcing a large invasion of southern Lebanon to destroy [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and annex territory south of the Litani River, and possible escalation with [[iran|Iran]] including a ground invasion, Badlands commentary judged the IDF stretched too thin to sustain a multi-front war and warned that collapse or mutiny against military or government leadership was becoming more likely.[7]

## April 2026: Lebanon deployment line and abuse allegations

In April 2026 the Israeli military published a map showing a new deployment line in southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]] that placed dozens of villages inside an Israeli-controlled zone extending about 5 to 10 kilometers from the border, with five divisions backed by naval forces operating there to dismantle [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] infrastructure and address threats to northern Israel.[8] Badlands analysis read the resulting [[forward-defense-zone|Forward Defense Zone]] as a vehicle for annexation, including a push northeast toward [[mount-hermon|Mount Hermon]] and a maritime-boundary shift enabling seizure of the [[qana-gas-field|Qana Gas Field]].[8]

The IDF verified the authenticity of an image showing one of its soldiers smashing a statue of Jesus with a sledgehammer in the Christian village of Debl, saying it viewed the [[debel-statue-desecration|incident]] with "great severity" and would work with the local community to restore the statue.[8] Badlands placed the act alongside the destruction of the Tomb of Saint Peter, described as accidental, within a broader pattern of [[jewish-terrorism|Jewish terrorism]].[8]

[[haaretz|Haaretz]] published reporting by Tom Levinson documenting an explosion in suicide, depression, and PTSD among IDF reservists conscripted after October 7th, many of whom served six or seven tours — including soldiers already diagnosed with PTSD who should have been disqualified, and who were arrested if they refused.[8] Israeli journalist [[shaiel-ben-ephraim|Shaiel Ben-Ephraim]] separately compiled interviews with IDF prison guards alleging torture, abuse, and rape of Palestinian detainees, claims Badlands connected to a 2024 leaked video of IDF soldiers gang-raping a Palestinian prisoner.[8] Badlands concluded that the force overall appeared to be "an army of bandits, thieves, and cut-throats" operating on a "might makes right" ethos.[8]

## Nabatieh operation and Lebanon Security Zone (June 2026)

In June 2026, hours after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted comments to Truth Social warning [[israel|Israel]] over Lebanon, reports flooded social media of a major IDF operation in southern Lebanon concentrated around the city of Nabatieh.[9] Accounts described vicious close-quarter combat and armor columns that included armored bulldozers moving through the area; the IDF was said to have sustained heavy losses, with at least four Merkava tanks destroyed.[9] Israeli media confirmed that at least four IDF soldiers had been killed, including a senior officer, and the Times of Israel reported the IDF's confirmation that it had conducted the operation overnight.[9] The IDF said it had struck [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] targets, while Lebanese reports cited sixteen killed.[9]

The IDF published a map showing the Security Zone it intended to establish in southern Lebanon.[9] Badlands noted that while the objective had been announced before, this appeared to be the first published map in which the Security Zone included areas north of the Litani River, and argued that the concurrent operation against Nabatieh made clear the map did not show the full extent of the territory intended.[9] The strikes caused the postponement of the formal [[us-iran-memorandum-of-understanding|US–Iran]] signing ceremonies in Switzerland.[9]

## June 2026 strikes on Iran

Following an Iranian barrage of 11 ballistic missiles toward [[israel|Israel]] on June 8, 2026, the Israeli Air Force carried out strikes against military targets in central and western [[iran|Iran]], according to the IDF.[10] Israeli Ambassador [[yechiel-leiter|Yechiel Leiter]] said the operation focused on missile launch sites and related infrastructure and did not target energy facilities, and explosions were reported in multiple Iranian cities.[10] A U.S. defense official said American forces did not participate in the strikes, which were carried out despite President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s private urging that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] not retaliate — a position the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] noted was at odds with statements from the Israeli military.[10]

## Lebanon operations (2026)

Badlands commentary paired aerial before/after footage of IDF attacks on the Lebanese town of Khiyam between March 3 and April 13, 2026 with historical context on the IDF's 1982 bombing of Beirut, which preceded Hezbollah's existence.[11] The subsequent 18-year occupation of southern Lebanon, including the establishment of settlements, is described as having provoked Hezbollah's formation, first as a political movement and then as a counterinsurgency, until the IDF was driven out in 2000.[11] In April 2026 Israeli forces also fired warning shots at Italian UNIFIL troops in southern Lebanon, damaging a vehicle and prompting Italy to suspend its defence memorandum with Israel.[11]

## Reservist strain and economic impact (July 2026)

Badlands commentary in July 2026 argued that repeated reserve mobilisations were degrading both the IDF and the Israeli economy: the producing class in its twenties, thirties and forties, all on reserve status, had been called up for multiple tours since the October 7 attack, which was described as disruptive to the economy and as driving a proliferation of PTSD among reservists, many of whom could no longer function or be productive after what they witnessed in Gaza.[12] The same commentary reported that the highest 10–15% of income earners had left [[israel|Israel]] out of fear of the war or of conscription into the IDF, and questioned whether neighbouring countries would eventually be forced to take action against the force.[12]

## Manpower crisis and pressure to withdraw (July 2026)

In July 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] pressed Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] to redeploy the IDF out of southern [[syria|Syria]] and southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]], part of a broader U.S. push for phased withdrawals including from designated "pilot zones" in southern Lebanon under a framework envisioning Lebanese army deployment and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] disarmament.[13] Netanyahu resisted, insisting on border security zones to prevent a repeat of October 7.[13]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the force was on the verge of collapse from manpower shortages and waning morale, compounded by the [[haredi-conscription-exemption-law|Haredi conscription exemption law]] that removed the threat of arrest for draft-dodgers and effectively excused more than 80,000 able-bodied ultra-Orthodox men from service.[13] He predicted the IDF might "bottom out" and even mutiny, contending it was built for quick skirmishes lasting weeks rather than a multi-year, multi-front war of attrition.[13]

## Discipline crisis in Lebanon (April 2026)

In April 2026 IDF Chief of Staff Lt. Gen. [[eyal-zamir|Eyal Zamir]] publicly condemned a series of unethical incidents by Israeli soldiers, warning that discipline had eroded during the multi-front wars since the October 7, 2023 [[hamas|Hamas]] attack.[14] Speaking at Ramat David Airbase, he cited looting in southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]], the destruction of a statue of Jesus in a Lebanese Christian village that drew international outrage, and unauthorized Velcro patches bearing inciteful or messianic slogans — including one reading "Stop the hatred. It is time for violence" — as a "rebellion against IDF values."[14]

Two soldiers involved in smashing the Jesus statue on April 19 were dismissed from combat service and jailed, and the IDF replaced the statue.[14] Zamir vowed full investigations into alleged looting of homes in depopulated southern Lebanese areas and into troops' misuse of social media, warning that "the erosion of norms could be no less dangerous than operational threats" while noting the force must stay ready for intense fighting through 2026.[14] Badlands commentary tied the indiscipline to the psychological toll of prolonged deployments on hundreds of thousands of reservists, reported spikes in reservist suicide and depression, and Ultra-Orthodox violence against military leadership after the end of the yeshiva conscription exemption.[14]

## October 7, 2023 and its aftermath

Following the [[october-7-attacks|October 7, 2023 attacks]], Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] declared that the IDF "will use all its strength" to "destroy" [[hamas|Hamas]] and warned [[gaza|Gaza]] residents to leave.[15] Former IDF international spokesperson [[jonathan-conricus|Jonathan Conricus]] told [[cnn|CNN]] that "the entire system failed... it's the entire defense architecture that evidently failed to provide the necessary defense for Israeli civilians," calling it a "Pearl Harbor type of moment for Israel."[15] Two days on, Hamas and [[palestinian-islamic-jihad|Palestinian Islamic Jihad]] reportedly still controlled swathes of southern territory and even IDF outposts, with town-by-town fighting underway.[15]

## April 2024 northern Gaza escalation

Israeli strikes intensified across [[gaza|Gaza]] on April 23, 2024 in some of the heaviest shelling in weeks, with residents describing almost 24 hours of non-stop bombardment by air and tank shelling in central and southern areas.[16] IDF spokesperson Avichay Adraee urged residents of four zones in Beit Lahiya on Gaza's northern edge to relocate to two designated areas, saying the military "will work with extreme force against terrorist infrastructure and subversive elements," while a later statement asserted the military "follows international law and takes feasible precautions to mitigate civilian harm."[16] The renewed campaign came almost four months after the IDF announced a troop drawdown in the north on the grounds that [[hamas|Hamas]] no longer controlled those areas.[16]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the offensive's effect, and possibly its purpose, was to grow rather than eradicate Hamas, invoking former [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]] head [[ami-ayalon|Ami Ayalon]]'s view that only Palestinian hope for the future can bring Israel security.[16]

## Friendly-fire deaths on October 7, 2023

An Israeli police investigation into the Hamas attack on the [[nova-music-festival-friendly-fire-admission|Nova music festival]] near the Gaza border on October 7, 2023 found that an Israeli attack helicopter had killed some of the attendees, Haaretz reported on November 18, 2023.[17] According to a police source, a combat helicopter dispatched from the Ramat David base fired on Hamas fighters and other Palestinians who had crossed the border fence from Gaza, but also fired on some of the Israelis attending the festival; police put the total death toll at the site at 364.[17] The Israeli military and rescue services had previously stated that 260 Israelis were killed at the festival, all by Hamas and Palestinians in a deliberate massacre, making this the first acknowledgement that Israeli forces killed some of their own.[17]

Earlier reports in Israeli media had described a comparable episode at Be'eri, a settlement also near the Gaza border, where Hamas fighters held Israelis captive in homes and arriving Israeli forces opened fire — including with tank shells — killing both Israeli captives and Hamas fighters.[17]

## October 7 accountability and the Haliva resignation (April 2024)

In April 2024 Maj. Gen. [[aharon-haliva]], chief of the IDF's Military Intelligence Directorate, announced his resignation over his role in the failures that led to the [[hamas]] onslaught of October 7, saying he would step down once a replacement was appointed.[18] The move was coordinated with IDF Chief of Staff Lt. Gen. [[herzi-halevi]] and approved by Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant]], making Haliva the first senior IDF officer to resign over the attack; another top intelligence general who had planned to step down instead quit after a cancer diagnosis.[18] Other senior defense officials, including the head of the [[shin-bet]] and the chief of staff, said they bore responsibility without announcing resignations, with many expected to depart once the security situation stabilized.[18]

Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief]] observed that the IDF appeared to have shifted toward a raid-based warfare strategy of smash-and-grab operations in which territory is not captured and held, with raids concentrated in the West Bank and airstrikes launched against Lebanon, Syria, and Iran while Gaza had already been heavily bombed.[18] The same commentary noted that the United States was poised to sanction multiple IDF battalions over alleged human rights abuses and immoral misconduct, amid Israeli street protests against the government of [[benjamin-netanyahu]].[18]

## Rafah operations and the crossing seizure (May 2024)

In early May 2024 the IDF conducted airstrikes and limited ground incursions in eastern [[rafah|Rafah]] after dropping thousands of leaflets telling civilians to leave the area.[19] An IDF tank unit took control of the [[rafah-border-crossing|Rafah border crossing]] with Egypt — reportedly with some level of coordination with Washington, intended to prevent weapons reaching [[hamas|Hamas]] through the crossing — and by 8 May the IDF announced it had reopened the crossing while retaining full control of it.[19] With that seizure the IDF controlled all of Gaza's border crossings for the first time in nearly twenty years.[19]

Badlands commentary was sharply critical, describing armour columns and bulldozers driving over tent cities, questioning the purpose of special operations forces, armour and 2,000-pound bombs against an encampment containing over 600,000 children, and asserting that the IDF had routinely failed to exercise restraint.[19] It also argued that continued guerrilla activity in northern Gaza contradicted IDF claims that those areas had been cleansed of Hamas fighters, and that IDF operations in Samaria and Judea were generating new support for Hamas militancy in the West Bank.[19]

## October 2023 Khan Younis engagement

In late October 2023 the IDF began conducting what it described as "limited" ground incursions into [[gaza|Gaza]] ahead of a major offensive.[20] The [[al-qassam-brigades|Al-Qassam Brigades]] claimed to have ambushed one such force near Khan Younis, destroying two military bulldozers and a tank, in what was reported as the first direct ground clash of the war inside Gaza.[20] The IDF did not confirm the Hamas account, saying only that "shots were fired at IDF soldiers operating west of the Gaza Strip security fence, in the area of Kissufim" and that "An IDF tank struck the terrorist cell who fired at the soldiers," while confirming that its forces were inside Gaza at the time.[20] A subsequent IDF statement cited by the *Times of Israel* appeared to corroborate the claim that the tank and bulldozers had been repelled, and observers noted the IDF was taking casualties before the main ground offensive had begun.[20]

## Neutra announcement and hostage-narrative skepticism (December 2024)

On Monday, December 2, 2024, the Israeli military announced that [[omer-neutra|Omer Maxim Neutra]], a 21-year-old American-Israeli tank platoon commander previously believed to be alive in captivity, had in fact been killed during the Hamas-led attack of [[october-7-attacks|October 7, 2023]], and that his body was still held in Gaza.[21] The IDF did not explain what prompted the announcement; in previous cases a hostage had been declared dead on the basis of new intelligence and evidence.[21] Foreign Minister Israel Katz said Neutra "fell in a heroic battle near Nir Oz," a kibbutz where roughly one in four residents was murdered or kidnapped.[21]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] disputed the plausibility of the account, questioning how [[hamas|Hamas]] could have retained a body through more than a year of bombardment that Israel had stated was intended to collapse underground tunnels, and noting the absence of even unverified supporting intelligence.[21] He connected the episode to reports that the IDF implemented the [[hannibal-directive|Hannibal Directive]] on October 7, under which Israelis being taken captive may be targeted and killed, and raised the possibility that many presumed hostages died that day.[21]


## September 2024 Lebanon bombing campaign

Following the remote detonation of pagers and other communications devices believed to be held by [[hezbollah]] members — not all of which were, with children reportedly killed by several devices — the IDF immediately began a bombing campaign against southern Lebanon.[22] On September 20, 2024, an airstrike on the Dahiyeh district of Beirut killed senior Hezbollah commander [[ibrahim-aqil|Ibrahim Aqil]] and other senior figures, with Lebanon's health ministry reporting at least 14 dead.[22] The campaign appeared to culminate in the release of the largest single munition the IDF had ever dropped.[22]

Badlands analyst [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the operation was intended either to soften Hezbollah defenses ahead of a ground invasion or to provoke an overtly aggressive Hezbollah response, and read the record-size bomb as [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s defiant answer to General [[michael-kurilla|Michael Kurilla]]'s warning that the US would not support an Israeli-instigated ground war.[22] Colonel [[lawrence-wilkerson|Lawrence Wilkerson]] added that Kurilla's warning carried the nuance that the US military would know if the IDF began the provocation.[22]

## Advance knowledge of the October 7 raid plan

In June 2024 a newly surfaced document revealed that the IDF and Israeli intelligence systems had detailed knowledge of [[hamas|Hamas]]'s plan to raid Israel and kidnap 250 people weeks before the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 massacre]].[23] The document, compiled in the Gaza Division and titled "Detailed End-to-End Raid Training," was distributed on September 19, 2023 and described in detail exercises conducted by Hamas's elite units, including raids on military posts and kibbutzim, the kidnapping of soldiers and civilians, and the maintenance of hostages inside the Gaza Strip.[23] Security sources told Kan News that the document was known to the intelligence leadership, at the very least within the Gaza Division.[23]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] combined this revelation with the circumstances of the [[nova-music-festival|Nova music festival]] — relocated two days beforehand to a site near the Gaza border with no enhanced IDF security and no quick reaction force on standby — and alleged that when IDF forces arrived seven hours later, their helicopters and tanks "accidentally" killed hundreds of Israeli citizens.[23] He argued these facts converted a suspicion of a setup into an unsettling certainty.[23]

## Gaza City operations (November 2023)

On November 7, 2023, Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] announced that IDF troops were for the first time "in the heart" of Gaza City and that the force had [[hamas|Hamas]] leader [[yahya-sinwar|Yahya Sinwar]] surrounded in a bunker, with fighting taking place in residential areas.[24] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said the IDF had been reaching deeper into Gaza than Hamas ever imagined and that Gaza City was surrounded, adding that the force was "deepening the pressure on Hamas every hour, every day."[24]

## December 2023: planned third phase and Lebanon strikes

In late December 2023 the Israeli Public Broadcasting Corporation reported that the IDF was preparing to move to a [[gaza-war-third-phase|third phase]] of the fighting in Gaza within weeks, in accordance with operational achievements — ending the ground operation, reducing forces and demobilizing reserves, shifting to air strikes, and establishing a buffer zone along the Gaza border.[25] The report claimed the army had taken control of most of the northern Gaza Strip while facing great difficulty advancing in the south.[25]

Simultaneously on Israel's northern front, the IDF said fighter jets had struck a [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] military headquarters in Lebanon in response to attacks on northern Israel that day, one of which — against the Manara area — left a soldier moderately wounded.[25] The IDF also carried out artillery shelling near the border, presumably to foil planned Hezbollah attacks, and said it struck the launch sites of several projectiles fired from Lebanon.[25]


## Lebanon invasion preparations (September 2024)

In late September 2024 the head of Israel's military, along with Israeli media and other officials, warned repeatedly that a ground invasion of southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]] was possible and might begin at any moment, and the IDF called up reserve brigades for a potential operation.[26] The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] said the US military was not providing intelligence for Israel's operations against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and assessed that an invasion did not appear imminent.[26] Former CIA and Army intelligence officer [[phil-giraldi|Phil Giraldi]] argued that the preceding week's pager attack may have been timed for the invasion's onset but was triggered early once the IDF feared Hezbollah had detected the plot.[26]

Badlands commentary noted that Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s warnings for Lebanese civilians to evacuate the south mirrored a tactic the IDF used in 1967 when Israel claimed large portions of the West Bank, and more recently in Gaza.[26] Rabbi [[yitzchak-ginsburgh|Yitzchak Ginsburgh]] of the [[chabad|Chabad]] movement publicly urged the IDF to conquer and annex southern Lebanon, expel the hostile population and establish Jewish settlement there as part of the [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] project.[26]

## Warning shots at foreign diplomats in Jenin (May 2025)

On Wednesday, May 21, 2025, Israeli soldiers fired shots near a group of foreign diplomats visiting the Jenin refugee camp in the occupied West Bank, prompting representatives from more than 20 countries and accompanying journalists to scramble for cover.[27] The tour, organized by the Palestinian Authority, included delegates from the United Kingdom, Canada, France, Italy, Spain, China, Japan, Mexico, Egypt and others; no injuries were reported, but video footage showed diplomats fleeing in panic as gunfire erupted around 2 p.m. local time.[27] The IDF claimed the delegation had deviated from its pre-approved route and entered an unauthorized area it described as an "active combat zone."[27]

The same week, the IDF's expanded ground offensive in Gaza, [[operation-gideons-chariots|Operation Gideon's Chariots]], was launched; [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] said its forces were "landing powerful blows" on [[hamas|Hamas]] strongholds and that all Gazan territory would come under Israeli security control by its end.[27]

## Invoked in US protest rhetoric (January 2024)

During the [[pro-palestinian-airport-caravan-protest|pro-Palestinian caravan protest]] at New York's JFK and LaGuardia airports at the turn of 2024, demonstrators chanted "NYPD, KKK, IDF they're all the same!", equating the Israeli military with American police.[28] In the same period the Israeli military announced a [[israeli-troop-withdrawal-from-gaza-2024|partial withdrawal]] of several thousand troops from [[gaza|Gaza]], with spokesman Rear Admiral [[daniel-hagari|Daniel Hagari]] insisting the move did not alter the aim of destroying [[hamas|Hamas]].[28]

## 'New phase' toward Lebanon, September 2024

After the September 2024 [[pager-attack|pager attacks]] on [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] told troops at the Ramat David Airbase near Haifa that "a new phase" of the war had begun and that "the center of gravity is moving north," with forces, resources and energy being diverted from Gaza toward Lebanon.[29] Israel intensified bombing of southern Lebanon and reports circulated that a ground invasion was mobilizing.[29]

Badlands analysis argued that the IDF had signalled months earlier — by moving most of its armor north to the Lebanese border — that its intended strategy was a blitzkrieg, and that Russian military forces and the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] embedded with Hezbollah were prepared to defeat it.[29] [[centcom|CENTCOM]] commander [[michael-kurilla|Michael Kurilla]] reportedly visited Israel twice in the same week to warn that a ground war with Lebanon, Syria or [[iran|Iran]] would leave the IDF on its own.[29]

## Khan Younis fighting and ground-op toll (December 2023)

On Monday, December 11, 2023, the IDF announced the deaths of seven more soldiers, six of whom were killed in fighting in the southern Gaza Strip the previous day, bringing the toll of slain troops in the ground offensive against [[hamas|Hamas]] to 104.[30] The announcement came amid heavy fighting in and around Khan Younis, a Hamas stronghold in southern Gaza, and as Gaza terror groups fired rocket barrages into Israel, including a midday salvo at the center of the country in which a Holon man was injured by shrapnel.[30] Five of the fallen soldiers belonged to the 5th Brigade's 8111st Battalion and were killed when an explosive device was detonated near their force as they battled a terror cell near a school in the Khan Younis area.[30] The IDF said helicopter gunships and fighter jets were called in to strike the militants who had opened fire from the school, killing the Hamas operatives and destroying infrastructure in the area.[30]

## Golan Heights and southern Syria operations (December 2024)

In December 2024, Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said he had ordered the IDF to begin securing territory in the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]], territory the force has occupied since the Six Day War in 1967, and declared the occupation permanent.[31] Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] said Israel would establish a "sterile zone" in southern Syria to prevent any "terrorist threat," following the collapse of the [[bashar-al-assad|Assad]] government.[31]

## Lebanon ground-maneuver debate (September 2024)

In September 2024, following IDF preemptive strikes on targets deep inside [[lebanon|Lebanon]], former Commander of the IDF General Staff Corps Maj.-Gen. (Res.) [[gershon-hacohen|Gershon Hacohen]] declared that "the IDF is ready" and that "it is entirely possible that the IDF will need to enter Lebanon on the ground."[32] He framed the objective as returning evacuated northern residents to their homes, which continual fighting with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] had prevented, concluding that Israel "must change tack."[32] He acknowledged American pressure for restraint out of concern that escalation could destabilize the region and prompt Iranian intervention.[32]

## July 2024 Gaza City hospital dispute

On Tuesday, July 9, 2024 the IDF rejected an accusation by Anglican Church authorities that it had ordered the closure of the [[al-ahli-arab-anglican-hospital|Al-Ahli Arab Anglican Hospital]] in Gaza City, insisting it had made clear there was no need to close hospitals or medical centres in an area where it had ordered evacuations for military operations against terror groups.[33] The [[episcopal-church-of-jerusalem-and-the-middle-east|Episcopal Church of Jerusalem and the Middle East]] said it was protesting the hospital's closure as a result of the evacuation of several residential districts, and a statement in the name of the Diocese of Jerusalem said the hospital had been "compelled to close by the Israeli army" after heavy drone fire in its immediate vicinity.[33] The medical centre's own director said patients had fled of their own accord while others were moved to alternative hospitals.[33]

## Haredi recruitment and internal strain (2024)

In July 2024 senior Sephardic rabbis instructed yeshiva students to ignore IDF recruitment orders and banned them from reporting to recruitment offices even to seek an exemption, in protest at the state's plan to begin drafting ultra-Orthodox men and at proposed legislation setting recruitment targets — a confrontation examined at [[ultra-orthodox-conscription-in-israel|ultra-Orthodox conscription in Israel]].[33] Around the same time, reports described potential mutinies within the IDF, with the officer corps said to believe [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had placed it in an impossible position to declare victory and to favour a truce even if it left [[hamas|Hamas]] in power; former IDF leaders spoke out publicly against Netanyahu, claiming he had been baited into invading Gaza.[33]

## American volunteers and the 2024 IDF benefits bill

In May 2024, Representatives [[guy-reschenthaler|Guy Reschenthaler]] and [[max-miller|Max Miller]] introduced the [[protecting-americans-in-the-idf-act|Protecting Americans in the IDF Act]], which would treat service by U.S. citizens in the IDF the same as service in the U.S. uniformed services for purposes of employment and financial protections.[34] Reschenthaler stated that over 20,000 American citizens were then defending Israel from Hamas.[34] Critics, including Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], questioned whether the bill was intended to encourage U.S. citizens with Israeli dual citizenship to enlist, and whether it conflicted with U.S. Code § 2387 on activities affecting the armed forces.[34]

## February 2024 airstrikes deep into Lebanon

On February 19, 2024, the IDF conducted its first airstrikes near a large city deep inside Lebanon, hitting the region of Ghaziyeh just south of Sidon, roughly 45 to 60 kilometers from the Israeli border.[35] Spokesman Rear Adm. [[daniel-hagari|Daniel Hagari]] confirmed the military's responsibility, stating the strikes targeted [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] weapons depots and answered an explosive-laden attack on northern Israel earlier that day.[35] The operation broke the pattern of tit-for-tat exchanges that had, since early October 2023, remained within a few kilometers of the border on either side.[35]

## Jit settler rampage investigation (August 2024)

An IDF investigation released in late August 2024 into a deadly attack carried out by roughly 100 Israeli settlers on the West Bank village of Jit concluded that troops who arrived first on the scene did not act as expected to stop the assailants.[36] The military dismissed two members of a nearby outpost's local security team for acting "outside the scope of their authority" during the incident.[36] According to the probe, the IDF had received an alert from the [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]] on the evening of August 15 warning that a group of Israelis in vehicles was heading to commit a "nationalistic crime" in the Yitzhar area, and a large number of troops and Border Police officers were dispatched in an attempt to prevent it.[36]

At around 8 p.m. some 100 masked settlers entered Jit, set fire to three cars and two buildings, and hurled Molotov cocktails and stones.[36] The IDF concluded that "the first force was unable to fully understand the situation. It tried to disperse the rioters and prevent harm to the Palestinians, but it should have acted with greater determination."[36] A 23-year-old man, Rashid Mahmoud Abdul Qader Sada, was shot and killed and another man wounded; although no arrests were made initially despite troops being present, four Israelis were later detained and charged with terrorism.[36]

Badlands Media linked the episode to a wider crisis of discipline, citing the arrest of IDF soldiers by military police over the alleged gang rape of a male Palestinian detainee, the storming of two IDF bases by religious zealots demanding their release, and a televised [[knesset|Knesset]] debate in which one lawmaker defended the abuse of detainees identified as Nukhba operatives.[36]

## Claimed loss of control over units (2024)

In June 2024, several months after commentators began predicting a "strategic defeat" for Israeli forces in Gaza, Israel's military high command claimed it had lost control over various units in the armed forces.[37] [[the-cradle|The Cradle]] argued that the claim served to scapegoat individual soldiers in order to provide plausible deniability for their superiors and to dissociate the leadership from war crimes charges, and that the body of evidence on these alleged "rogue Israeli units" could instead produce a damning indictment of Tel Aviv's military leadership.[37]

The Israeli newspaper [[haaretz|Haaretz]] reported that "Halevi and the military high command woke up too late. The General Staff lost control over the units, especially reserve units, months ago. In Gaza, in the West Bank and in bases in Israel, soldiers record themselves destroying Palestinian property and civilian infrastructure, blowing up homes without permission and spreading political messages identified with the far right."[37] Haaretz had run a January 2024 headline warning that "The Israeli Army Must Act Before Some of Its Soldiers Turn Into Lawless Gangs."[37] Israeli military leaders subsequently instructed soldiers to stop filming their conduct, with military police opening a probe after soldiers filmed themselves burning a Quran and other books in Gaza; the instructions were reportedly disregarded.[37]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the IDF, or portions of it, had "become the [[azov-battalion|Azov Battalion]]," calling the situation "Ukraine-Crimea all over again."[37] He cited Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]], a former military advisor to the Trump administration, who told Judge [[andrew-napolitano|Andrew Napolitano]] that western media was not reporting actual events on the ground in Gaza or the West Bank and that western audiences were being propagandized into a fictitious reality; Macgregor also asserted that [[hamas|Hamas]] was winning the war, having previously driven the IDF out of Gaza in 2006.[37] General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] brought Macgregor onto a live stream after the Trump verdict to discuss the geopolitical chessboard including Israel.[37] The context included the [[international-court-of-justice|ICJ]]'s call for Israel to halt operations in [[rafah-invasion|Rafah]] and an [[international-criminal-court|ICC]] arrest warrant application against Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], who remained resolute in his vow to invade.[37]

## US sanctions on the Netzah Yehuda Battalion (April 2024)

In April 2024 the United States was reported to be poised to sanction the [[netzah-yehuda-battalion|Netzah Yehuda Battalion]], an IDF unit of over 900 soldiers, over alleged human rights abuses in the occupied West Bank — the first time Washington would sanction an Israeli military unit.[38] Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] was expected to announce measures barring the battalion and its members from receiving US military assistance or training.[38] Separately, an IDF drone strike on Iran early on April 19, 2024 was thwarted by Tehran and derided as "lame" by Israeli National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]].[38]

## Friction with the Netanyahu family, June 2024

During the June 2024 coalition crisis, [[sara-netanyahu|Sara Netanyahu]] reportedly told hostage families that IDF leadership was "staging a coup" against her husband, while [[yair-netanyahu|Yair Netanyahu]] publicly blamed the IDF brass for wartime failures, naming individual officers and demanding investigations.[39] In the same period Israel's Supreme Court struck down the long-standing exemption of ultra-Orthodox men from IDF conscription, requiring the government either to draft them or to legislate a formal exemption.[39]

## December 2023 killing of three hostages in Gaza

On Friday morning, December 15, 2023, Israeli troops shot and killed three Israeli hostages in the Shijaiyah neighborhood of Gaza; IDF officials said the following day that the men had been holding a stick with a white piece of cloth and had shouted "help" in Hebrew before being shot.[40] IDF chief of staff [[herzi-halevi|Herzi Halevi]] stated that "the shooting at the hostages was done in violation of the IDF rules of engagement," adding that "it is not allowed to shoot at a person who is holding a white flag and wants to surrender," while noting that the shooting "was conducted under stress and on a battlefield."[40] IDF spokesperson [[daniel-hagari|Daniel Hagari]] disclosed the details of the incident at a press conference later that Friday.[40]

It was the first time since the start of the Israel–[[hamas|Hamas]] war that the IDF acknowledged accidentally killing hostages during its ground operation in Gaza; Hamas had previously claimed that some hostages were killed in Israeli airstrikes.[40] The incident increased pressure on Israel to reach a new deal to release the more than 130 hostages still held in Gaza.[40]


## Reported use of Palestinian civilians in explosive-laden tunnels

Reuters reported in November 2025, citing former US officials with knowledge of intelligence, that IDF troops employed the tactic of sending Palestinian civilians into [[hamas|Hamas]] tunnels that they knew might be rigged with explosives, a practice of which the country's leadership was aware.[41] Information about Israeli officials discussing such practices was shared with the administration of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] by US intelligence agencies in the final months of 2024.[41] According to two sources, this prompted questions within the [[white-house|White House]] and the US intelligence community over how extensively Israel had used civilians as human shields during the Gaza operation; such tactics violate international law and are considered a war crime.[41] The IDF said in a statement that it "prohibits the use of civilians as human shields or coercing them in any way to participate in military operations," and the Israeli military police told Reuters it was investigating "suspicions involving Palestinians in military missions."[41] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] commented that the more that emerges about the culture of the IDF, the more apparent it becomes that Israel is incompatible with western values, which he identified as Christian values.[41]

## October 2023 Gaza church compound strike

On Thursday, October 19, 2023, the Hamas-controlled interior ministry in Gaza said that several displaced people sheltering at the compound of a Greek Orthodox church in the Gaza Strip had been killed and injured in an Israeli strike, reporting a "large number of martyrs and injured."[42] There was no immediate comment from the IDF, which told AFP it was looking into the potential strike, while witnesses said the strike appeared to have been aimed at a target close to the place of worship where many Gaza residents had taken refuge.[42] The same day's reporting noted that the IDF was threatening [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] on Israel's northern border while the United States said it was in talks with Israel over Gaza "safe zones."[42] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that with two US carrier groups in the region, Israel heading into Gaza and Iranian retaliation under way, the United States was already engaged in kinetic conflict without any declaration of war or even a sitting speaker of the House.[42]
