# Amsterdam & Partners

**Amsterdam & Partners** is an international law firm retained by the government of the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] to pursue claims relating to the illicit trade in Congolese rare earth and conflict minerals.[1]

## Report on mineral laundering

Working on behalf of the DRC government, the firm published a report accusing major technology companies — including [[apple|Apple]], Intel, Sony, and Lockheed Martin — of illegally smuggling rare earth minerals out of the Congo into neighbouring countries, primarily [[rwanda|Rwanda]], so that they could be laundered into the global supply chain as "clean."[1] In this context, "clean" means certified as not having been mined using child slavery, a practice described as common in the deep jungles of the eastern Congo.[1]

The report went further, accusing these technology firms of using UN-connected organizations such as the International Tin Supply Chain Initiative (ITSCI) and the Responsible Minerals Initiative (RMI) to legitimize the supply chains through fraudulent certifications.[1] Among the individuals named was French industrialist Vincent Bolloré, who was arrested in 2018 and charged with fraud related to African mining operations; Badlands commentary described Bolloré as a protégé of the French branch of the Rothschild banking dynasty.[1]

## Litigation against Apple

Approximately two months before late February 2025, Amsterdam & Partners filed a lawsuit against Apple on behalf of the DRC government, extending the report's allegations into formal legal action.[1]

## Significance

The firm's work is treated in Badlands analysis as one of the principal levers by which President [[felix-tshisekedi|Félix Tshisekedi]]'s government has sought to contest foreign control of Congolese minerals, whose estimated value is put in excess of $24 trillion.[1]
