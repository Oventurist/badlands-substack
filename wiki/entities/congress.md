# United States Congress

The United States Congress is the bicameral national legislature, consisting of the Senate and the House of Representatives, and holds the constitutional power of the purse over federal spending, including foreign aid and military appropriations.

## Role in the Badlands corpus

The Badlands Media article [[a-conflict-of-interest|A Conflict of Interest]] treats Congress as the institutional mechanism through which, in the author's account, American taxpayer money is directed to hostile foreign actors. The article states flatly that "Congress was giving taxpayers money to ISIS," describing the [[syrian-emergency-task-force|Syrian Emergency Task Force]] run by [[mouza-moustafa|Mouza Moustafa]] as a lobbying operation whose real beneficiary was [[isis|ISIS]], and its Libyan predecessor, the [[libyan-emergency-task-force|Libyan Emergency Task Force]], as having performed the same function for anti-Gaddafi militias.

Lobbying is identified in the article as one of the principal channels of what the author calls taxpayer theft, alongside wartime cash transfers. The argument is that registered lobbying groups established in Washington on behalf of foreign insurgencies can obtain congressional appropriations and political recognition for those insurgencies, and that members of Congress who sponsor them supply the legitimacy needed to move the money.

[[john-mccain|John McCain]], described in the article as the most senior Republican on the [[senate-armed-services-committee|Senate Armed Services Committee]] and, in a cited Politico report, as ranking Republican on the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, is presented as the pivotal congressional figure. The article recounts his April 2011 trip to [[benghazi|Benghazi]], where he pressed the United States and [[nato|NATO]] allies to recognise the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]] as the legitimate voice of the Libyan people and to supply "command and control support, battlefield intelligence, training and weapons." The article argues that formal recognition of the council was sought precisely because it would make the flow of appropriated funds to the rebels administratively straightforward.

The article also notes that when [[barack-obama|President Obama]] initiated military action in Libya with limited congressional consultation, McCain defended the decision, saying the president could not wait even a few days for Congress to debate the use of force.

## Postal savings and postal banking legislation

[[a-seismic-shift|A Seismic Shift]] (January 12, 2024) assigns Congress a recurring role in the history of American [[public-banking|public banking]]. [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] notes that Congress created the [[postal-savings-system|Postal Savings System]] — dated in the article both to a 1910 act of Congress and to the system's 1911 launch — and that Congress limited the amount Americans could deposit in postal savings accounts, with the original $500 cap raised to $2,500 by 1918. He further argues that private bankers, unable to compete for depositors in hard times, "got Congress to create Federal Deposit Insurance," which made private deposits appear as secure as postal ones and helped kill the public option in 1967.

The article also reports that members of Congress have proposed legislation requiring the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], in conjunction with the [[united-states-postal-service|U.S. Postal Service]], to offer retail [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]], and to require banks with more than $10 billion in assets to offer basic "pass through" accounts whose deposits rest at the Fed. The [[american-bankers-association|American Bankers Association]] is described as lobbying Congress to reject the proposal.

## The 2022 midterm result

The essay [[all-assets-deployed|All Assets Deployed]] (November 16, 2022) discusses control of Congress as the immediate stake of the [[2022-united-states-midterm-elections|2022 midterm elections]]. Its author, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], writes that he would have preferred "a crushing Red Wave that soundly overwhelmed [their] systems of fraud en route to taking Congress" as a prelude to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2024 announcement, but counts "real wins, even amidst the fraud," specifically "the projected reclaiming of the House and a potentially-interesting deadlock in the Senate, where a few key 'Democrat' votes never seem to quite go the way the powers that would be intend."
