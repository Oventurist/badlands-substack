# Sandinista National Liberation Front

The **Sandinistas** are the Central American revolutionary movement that Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] uses as her principal illustration of how the word "communist" has functioned as a pretext in [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]] operations.[1]

## The "communist" label

Americans, Watkins writes, were told repeatedly by the Reagan and Bush administrations that the [[nicaraguan-contras|Contras]] — funded with millions upon millions of dollars — were the good guys, and that the Sandinistas were the communists.[1] In her account neither claim was true.[1]

## Conflict with United Fruit and the CIA

The Sandinistas, she argues, were fighting a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]-installed dictator, installed because the prior government had decided that the [[international-syndicate|International Syndicate]]'s [[united-fruit-company|United Fruit]] should not own 42% of the country's farmable land — land that had been acquired by paying off government officials to confiscate it, primarily from middle-class coffee farmers.[1] Once the Sandinistas removed the CIA-installed dictator, the United States, acting on behalf of the syndicate, sent in Special Forces, Gladio-trained Cuban exiles and mercenaries to overthrow the new government.[1] Watkins presents this sequence as the template for how nationalization or contract renegotiation triggered the [[nato|NATO]] paramilitary capability of Gladio.[1]
