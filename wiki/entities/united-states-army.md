# United States Army

The United States Army is the land warfare service branch of the U.S. armed forces and the largest component of the Department of Defense by active-duty personnel. In April 2025 it became the focus of reporting on a potential force-structure contraction of historic scale.[1]

## Proposed 2025 force reduction

According to a Military.com report relayed by Fox News, the Army was quietly considering cutting as many as 90,000 active-duty soldiers, a reduction that could bring the total force down to roughly 360,000 troops.[1] The proposal was described as part of a broader Pentagon budget retrenchment, with Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] pressing for an 8 percent cut to the department's budget.[1]

The prospective cuts were framed as consistent with defense leaders' stated goal of reshaping the Army into a smaller, faster and more high-technology force better suited to future conflicts.[1] Reporting tied the shift to a reorientation of the military away from Europe and counterterrorism missions and toward the Pacific theater, where China is treated as the principal emerging threat.[1]

## Recruitment crisis and the 2023 "TikTok mutiny"

In December 2023 the Army was reported to be facing what the Daily Mail called a "TikTok mutiny," as Generation Z recruits took to social media to complain about low pay, poor food quality and fitness testing.[2] The posts, made by uniformed troops on U.S. bases, were characterized as an audacious challenge to senior leadership occurring amid a recruitment crisis in which the Army had fallen 25 percent short of its enlistment target the previous year.[2]

One widely circulated post by military influencer Anthony Laster criticized Army life for "No Privacy, The Pay Sucks, Sh***y Food, Disrespectful Leadership, NO SLEEP!" and was viewed more than 600,000 times.[2] Laster, from Chicago, had more than a million [[tiktok|TikTok]] followers and made the comments in uniform while on mission in the desert; in another post he claimed to have spent an entire day watching TikToks while ostensibly fighting the Taliban.[2] Commentators argued the posts gave a poor impression of America's fighting forces to potential recruits and were likely to intensify political animosity toward TikTok, whose links to China and alleged promotion of subversive anti-U.S. content had drawn bipartisan concern.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that while Chinese use of TikTok to demoralize U.S. troops was plausible, the complaints were also legitimate and heartfelt, contending that the U.S. government was itself degrading service-member morale.[2] She cited young people who regretted enlisting, some departing after vaccine mandates, and others deterred from serving under current leadership, and predicted that the Pentagon would discipline the posting service members rather than address root causes.[2]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the United States does not require a large standing army because it is ending its role as "World Police," and dismissed the personnel likely to be shed as unfit or ideologically selected recruits.[1] The commentary situated Army downsizing within the broader Badlands framing of American retrenchment from global policing commitments.[1]
