# Alexander Lukashenko

Alexander Lukashenko is the President of [[belarus|Belarus]] and, in the Badlands Media corpus, the source of a June 2026 claim that a "Jewish lobby" in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] misled the Russian leadership at the outset of the Ukraine war.

## The Al Arabiya interview (June 2026)

The Badlands Brief of June 16, 2026 reports, citing RT, that Lukashenko said in an interview with the Saudi-owned broadcaster *Al Arabiya* that Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] was given faulty information during the early stages of the Ukraine conflict. According to Lukashenko, a "Jewish lobby" in Ukraine convinced Moscow that Russian forces would be welcomed as liberators — an assumption that proved incorrect once the invasion began.

The remarks came in a broader interview in which Lukashenko argued that neither [[russia|Russia]] nor Ukraine can achieve a decisive military victory and that the conflict must ultimately be resolved through negotiation and compromise. He described the war as a prolonged stalemate and warned that continued escalation could worsen conditions for all parties. The Brief situates the comments within continuing discussion of pathways to ending what it describes as a more than four-year war.

## Record of controversial statements

The Brief notes that Lukashenko has previously drawn criticism from Israeli officials and Jewish organizations over comments widely characterized as antisemitic. In past speeches he claimed that the world "bows" to Jews because of Holocaust remembrance and made other controversial remarks regarding Jewish communities, prompting diplomatic protests from [[israel|Israel]].

## Use in the Badlands argument

Contributor [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treats the interview as "quite the revelation" and uses it to revisit the 2022 question of who persuaded Russian forces to withdraw from Kiev. Where Britain's Boris Johnson had in May 2022 been credited with orchestrating the deception that led to the Russian pullback and allowed Ukraine to regroup, the commentary argues that it was in fact [[israel|Israel]] — then Prime Minister [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] — who served as mediator between Putin and [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], and who therefore "most likely orchestrated the ruse."

From that premise the commentator poses a chain of open questions rather than assertions: what Israel's interest is in perpetuating the Russia–Ukraine war; whether whispered rumors of a new Jewish homeland or ethnostate in Ukraine are true; whether perpetuating a Europe–Russia war was a calculated scheme to draw the West into conflict with Russia's emerging ally [[iran|Iran]]; whether American money sent to Ukraine and purportedly diverted to Africa to fund ISIS relates to rare earth mineral regions lost to what he calls "the Israeli oligarchy" when [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] was sanctioned by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in 2017; and where the Vatican fits in. The author explicitly frames these as questions, writing that "more important than pinpointing answers is asking the right questions." The claims are speculative and are presented as such.
