# Alexander Lukashenko

Alexander Lukashenko is the President of Belarus.[1]

## Board of Peace

Lukashenko confirmed Belarus's acceptance of an invitation to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]] in January 2026, joining Hungary under [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orban]], Kazakhstan under [[kassym-jomart-tokayev|Kassym-Jomart Tokayev]], and Argentina, Vietnam and Morocco among the confirmed participants.[1] Belarus was one of roughly 60 countries invited to the signing ceremony at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] meeting in Davos, an invitation list whose inclusion of Russia and Belarus contributed to European hesitancy — Poland in particular objected to the inclusion of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[1]

## "Jewish lobby" remarks (June 2026)

In a June 2026 interview with Al Arabiya, Lukashenko said that Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had been given faulty information during the early stages of the Ukraine conflict, alleging that a "Jewish lobby" in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] convinced Moscow that Russian forces would be welcomed as liberators.[2] According to Lukashenko, those assumptions proved incorrect once the invasion began.[2]

In the same interview Lukashenko argued that neither Russia nor Ukraine can achieve a decisive military victory and that the conflict must ultimately be resolved through negotiation and compromise, describing the war as a prolonged stalemate and warning that continued escalation could worsen conditions for all parties.[2] He has previously drawn criticism from Israeli officials and Jewish organizations over comments widely characterized as antisemitic, including past claims that the world "bows" to Jews because of Holocaust remembrance, which prompted diplomatic protests from [[israel|Israel]].[2] The remarks added to a series of high-profile statements by the Belarusian leader as discussion continued over pathways to ending the more than four-year war.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the interview as a revelation, contrasting it with the May 2022 account crediting Britain's Boris Johnson with the deception that led Russian forces to pull back from Kiev, and arguing that then-Israeli Prime Minister [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]], who mediated between Putin and Zelensky, most likely orchestrated the ruse.[2] The commentary posed further questions about Israeli interests in perpetuating the war, rumored plans for a Jewish homeland in Ukraine, diverted Ukraine aid allegedly funding ISIS in African rare-earth regions, the 2017 sanctioning of Dan Gertler, and the role of the Vatican.[2]

## Warning that Ukraine could cease to exist (October 2025)

In an interview with Russian journalist Pavel Zarubin published in October 2025, Lukashenko warned that [[ukraine|Ukraine]] could cease to exist as a state if no diplomatic settlement were reached and Russian forces continued to advance.[3] "Russia is moving forward on the front line… and this could result in the disappearance of Ukraine as a state," he said, arguing that meaningful negotiations must begin "urgently."[3] His warning followed statements by President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] that Russian forces were advancing almost everywhere along the front and had taken nearly 5,000 square kilometres of territory and more than 210 settlements since the start of the year.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded that Ukraine had historically seldom been a country, with different portions belonging to other nations and empires, and suggested that whatever remained after the war should be called "The Ukraine."[3]

## Wagner remarks on Poland (July 2023)

During a meeting with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] on Sunday, July 23, 2023, Lukashenko said that the [[wagner-group|Wagner]] mercenary group, exiled to Belarus after its apparent botched mutiny against Putin, was "itching" to march on Poland, and that he was "stressed" because he was having to restrain the fighters.[4] In video of the exchange posted to [[twitter|Twitter]] by [[anton-gerashchenko|Anton Gerashchenko]], an adviser to Ukraine's minister of internal affairs, Lukashenko recounted the fighters asking to "go to the West" and proposing, "let's go on an excursion to Warsaw and Rzeszow" — a reference to the Polish capital and to a key military logistics hub.[4] Wagner forces were based less than 10 kilometres from the Polish border at the time.[4]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that, true or not, the narrative seeded by the Putin ally furthered escalatory rhetoric depicting a world balanced on a knife's edge, and framed the episode as setup for a peacemaker role — "Peacemaker was always the endgame."[4]

## "Big deal" with the United States (October 2025)

At a meeting with senior officials in Minsk on Tuesday, October 14, 2025, Lukashenko announced that he was ready to secure a "big deal" with the United States, saying that after recent contacts with envoys of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Belarus was willing to meet certain "global proposals" from Washington.[5] "But our interests must also be taken into account. And everything must be fair," he stressed, adding that the US had moved to reopen diplomatic relations with Belarus for purely "pragmatic purposes."[5]

Lukashenko noted that, according to the Belarusian national airline Belavia, US "sanctions have not yet been fully lifted" despite an earlier agreement to loosen restrictions, and that the carrier still faced limits on destinations and repairs.[5] The [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] had moved the previous month to thaw relations with Belarus and lift Biden-era sanctions on Belavia as part of a deal that freed 52 prisoners; Washington had imposed the restrictions in 2023 after accusing Lukashenko of election fraud and "complicity" in the Ukraine conflict.[5]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that the deal had likely been in the works or effectively in place for a long time, reasoning that given Belarus's strategic relationship with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[russia|Russia]], Lukashenko would not consider an agreement with the United States without Putin's encouragement — and that an imminent US-Belarus announcement implies a US-Russia deal is not far behind.[5] He credited the "vodka diplomacy" Lukashenko employed in negotiations with Trump lawyer-turned-envoy John Coale during the summer of 2025, which led to the release of 14 American hostages.[5]
