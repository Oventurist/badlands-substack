# Zhang Youxia

Zhang Youxia is a senior Chinese general and vice chairman of the Central Military Commission. In late January 2026 he became the central figure in widely circulated reports of an alleged military coup and subsequent purge inside the Chinese Communist Party.[1]

## Reported arrest

Social media reports amplified by General Michael Flynn, sourced to a Chinese-language account, claimed that Zhang Youxia, Liu Zhenli and Zhong Shaojun had been arrested simultaneously and that all of Zhang's family members had also been taken into custody.[1] Circumstantial indicators cited in the reports included Zhang's absence from the opening ceremony of a seminar for provincial and ministerial-level cadres on the spirit of the Fourth Plenary Session of the 20th Central Committee, his absence from a scheduled annual event at the National Defense University, and the removal of a funeral wreath he had sent following the death of former Deputy Chief of the General Staff Wei Fulin.[1]

A follow-up post claimed that Wang Xiaohong's special service bureau had carried out the arrests, that Zhong Shaojun had been detained again shortly after being released, and that seventeen people were taken in a single sweep.[1] The same source cautioned that the information could not be verified and suggested watching whether Zhang appeared at the Spring Festival group meeting.[1]

## Significance

Commentators framed a genuine arrest of Zhang as evidence that [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] and the "red families" — descendants of the founding families of the CCP, who control much of the state-owned natural resource and infrastructure base — had broken with each other irreparably.[1] The Wall Street Journal was reported to have said Zhang stood accused of leaking classified intelligence on China's nuclear weapons technology to the United States.[1] Badlands commentary read the episode through a "Good Twin / Evil Twin" frame in which Xi and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] represent the reformist faction in their respective countries while the purged Chinese military leadership parallels the American Uniparty and Deep State.[1]

The broader purge context is documented at [[china-military-purge-2026|the 2026 Chinese military purge]].

## Formal investigation, January 2026

Over the weekend of January 25–26, 2026, Chinese authorities publicly announced investigations into Zhang, then 75, and Gen. [[liu-zhenli|Liu Zhenli]] for "serious violations of discipline and law," a formulation conventionally indicating corruption.[2] The *People's Liberation Army Daily* accused the pair of "seriously betraying" the Communist Party's trust, "trampling on and undermining" the [[central-military-commission|Central Military Commission]], and threatening the authority of its chairman, President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]].[2]

A longtime Xi ally with family ties dating to China's revolutionary era and rare combat experience, Zhang had previously been regarded as untouchable, and stood as the highest-ranking military officer beneath Xi.[2] Unverified reports cited by The Wall Street Journal alleged that he leaked core nuclear weapons data to the United States and accepted bribes, while other speculation pointed to factional infighting or the accumulation of excessive personal power.[2] The removals reduced the CMC to Xi and one other member, leaving the senior ranks of the [[peoples-liberation-army|People's Liberation Army]] in disarray.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Western alarm over the purge inverted its meaning, presenting it instead as the culmination of Xi's [[tigers-and-flies-campaign|Tigers and Flies campaign]] and as consolidation rather than fragility.[2]

## Confrontation with Jake Sullivan (August 2024)

As a vice chairman of the Central Military Commission, Zhang met US national security adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] in Beijing in late August 2024, immediately before Sullivan's meeting with [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]].[3] Zhang confronted Sullivan over expanding US military support for [[taiwan|Taiwan]] and told him that reunification of Taiwan with the mainland is "the mission and responsibility" of the [[peoples-liberation-army|People's Liberation Army]], which would not be deterred.[3] Chinese defense ministry messaging accompanying the meeting demanded the United States stop arming Taiwan and cease "spreading false narratives" about the island.[3] Badlands Media described Zhang as confident and beaming throughout the exchange while Sullivan appeared nervous, casting the encounter as a public demonstration of Chinese contempt for the Biden administration's standing.[3]
