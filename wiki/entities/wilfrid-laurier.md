# Wilfrid Laurier

Sir Wilfrid Laurier was Prime Minister of Canada whose dream of a North American customs union based on industrial cooperation and protective tariffs was, according to Badlands Media contributor Matthew Ehret, sabotaged in 1911 by the forces of Lord [[alfred-milner|Alfred Milner]].[1] A soft coup removed Laurier from office, after which Canada was dragged into World War I as a pawn of British interests, and the [[round-table-movement|Round Table]]'s grip on Canada tightened.[1]

Laurier's removal followed Milner's 1909 warning that the greatest danger to British power was not Canadian nationalism but Canadian union with the United States, a danger the network countered by fostering a synthetic nationalism that was loudly "not-American" yet quietly subordinate to London.[1]

Amid the carnage of World War I, Laurier wrote that "Canada is now governed by a junta sitting at London, known as 'The Round Table', with ramifications in Toronto, in Winnipeg, in Victoria, with Tories and Grits receiving their ideas from London and insidiously forcing them on their respective parties."[1]
