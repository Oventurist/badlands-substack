# Madhu Gottumukkala

Madhu Gottumukkala is a US cybersecurity official who served as acting director of the [[cisa|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]].[1] By March 2026 he had been replaced in that role on an acting basis by [[nick-andersen|Nick Andersen]].[1]

During his tenure, conflicting reports circulated over whether Gottumukkala had tried to push CISA chief information officer [[robert-costello|Robert Costello]] out of his position.[1] House Homeland Security Chairman [[andrew-garbarino|Andrew Garbarino]] said in February 2026 that it was "good" that an earlier reported attempt to move Costello out of the CIO role had failed.[1] Costello announced his own departure from the agency the following month.[1]

## The July 2025 polygraph episode

At least six career staffers at CISA were suspended with pay in the summer of 2025 after organizing a polygraph test that Gottumukkala, then acting director, failed.[2] The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] opened an investigation into whether those staff had provided "false information" about the need for the test, which had been scheduled after Gottumukkala sought access to certain highly sensitive cyber intelligence shared with the agency.[2] Politico's account was based on interviews with eight current and four former US cybersecurity officials, including multiple [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration appointees, granted anonymity for fear of retribution.[2] The July incident and its fallout — previously unreported — angered career staff, alarmed fellow administration appointees, and raised questions about Gottumukkala's leadership of the nearly $3 billion cyber defense agency.[2]

The material at issue was designated a controlled access program, meaning circulation was to be tightly restricted to need-to-know personnel, and the agency furnishing it to CISA additionally required that such employees first pass a counter-intelligence polygraph.[2] Those interviewed could not say definitively why Gottumukkala did not pass, and several cautioned that people fail such examinations for innocuous reasons such as anxiety or technical error; polygraph results are not reliable enough to be admitted as evidence in most US courts.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the episode as evidence that a spy agency had been subverting the chain of command within the executive branch and had been caught doing so over the summer, and suggested that the reporting reflected an attempt to set a narrative against Gottumukkala.[2]
