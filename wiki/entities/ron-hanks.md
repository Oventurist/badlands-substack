# Ron Hanks

Ron Hanks is a United States Air Force veteran and former [[colorado|Colorado]] state representative who, at the start of 2024, announced a run for the third congressional district seat abandoned by [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]].[1]

## Oath and January 6

Hanks swore the oath of office twice, in two different official capacities — as a military officer and as a state legislator — a fact that matters under the disqualification clause of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]], which applies to prior oath-takers.[1] It is broadly known that he attended January 6 in Washington, D.C., as did thousands of other Coloradans.[1]

## Election-denier list

In September 2021, Hanks was named the sixth "Most Dangerous Election Denier in Colorado" by Colorado Newsline, an article for which the outlet subsequently won an award; Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who disclosed being number five on the same list, cited Hanks' inclusion in describing the environment facing Colorado election-integrity activists.[1]

## Test case for lawfare

Ashe in America proposed Hanks' candidacy as an empirical test of the theory that the January 6 insurrection finding in [[anderson-v-griswold|Anderson v. Griswold]] would be used to bar veteran January 6 attendees from office, writing that it remained to be seen whether anyone would bring a Fourteenth Amendment claim against his candidacy.[1] She described Hanks as "an honorable man and an excellent choice for Colorado's third congressional district."[1]

Writing in January 2024, Ashe reiterated that if the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] ruled only on the Fourteenth Amendment's applicability to the presidency and left the insurrection finding undisturbed, Hanks' third-district candidacy would be "ripe for a 14th Amendment claim," making him one example of how the decision would reach candidates well beyond Trump.[3]

## 2022 US Senate campaign

Hanks, then the state representative for House District 60, sought the Republican nomination for United States Senate in 2022.[2] At the [[2022-colorado-republican-state-assembly|Colorado Republican State Assembly]] he took 39% of the delegate vote and was the only candidate to qualify for the primary ballot from a crowded field.[2] In the primary he faced [[joe-odea|Joe O'Dea]], who had bypassed the assembly by petitioning onto the ballot; O'Dea won the nomination and lost the general election, a pattern [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited as evidence that candidates who refuse to face party voters at assembly nonetheless prevail in Colorado primaries.[2]
