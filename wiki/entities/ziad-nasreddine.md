# Ziad Nasreddine

**Dr. Ziad Nasreddine** is a Canadian neurologist who developed the [[montreal-cognitive-assessment|Montreal Cognitive Assessment]], one of the most widely used screening tools for cognitive impairment and often described in press coverage as the "gold-standard" cognitive test.[1]

In February 2024, Nasreddine told the New York Post that it may be a good idea for aging politicians to take his test, after being asked about President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] He sidestepped politics and declined to comment on Biden, then 81, directly.[1] Asked about the mounting public calls for Biden and former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], then 77, to take such a test, he replied: "It's a good thing that we assess cognition the minute there's any suspicion about cognitive function."[1]

Nasreddine also stressed the reassurance value of a normal result: "If the test shows normal results, the likelihood of impairment is very low. Then everybody is reassured that this is maybe just normal aging — that we can all be forgetful and make mistakes. But it doesn't mean that there's a significant cognitive disorder happening."[1] His comments came as scrutiny of Biden's mental acuity intensified following special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s report, which described Biden as a "well-meaning elderly man with a poor memory."[1]
