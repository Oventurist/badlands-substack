# Sam Altman

Sam Altman is the chief executive officer of [[openai|OpenAI]].[1]

## Pentagon contract backlash

In March 2026, Altman responded to intense internal and public backlash over OpenAI's rushed [[pentagon|Pentagon]] artificial intelligence contract by calling the initial rollout "sloppy" and lacking clear communication.[1] The agreement, which would supply OpenAI models for classified U.S. military operations, drew employee protests, chalk graffiti at company headquarters, and an open letter signed by nearly 900 workers at OpenAI and Google over fears it would enable domestic mass surveillance.[1] Under Altman's direction the company rewrote the contract to add explicit language prohibiting intentional use of its systems for domestic surveillance of U.S. persons and nationals.[1]

## Open-source AI dispute (July 2026)

By July 2026 Altman had become a principal target of Trump-aligned technology investors in the fight over [[open-source-artificial-intelligence|open-source artificial intelligence]], with OpenAI and [[anthropic|Anthropic]] generally supporting stronger safeguards for frontier systems while open-source advocates argued such rules would entrench incumbents.[2] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] contended that the concerted effort by Altman and Anthropic's [[dario-amodei|Dario Amodei]] to obtain government regulation was an attempt to slow and control the industry rather than accelerate it, placing AI in collectivist and oligarchic hands.[2] Former White House AI and Crypto Czar [[david-sacks|David Sacks]] similarly stated that he believed the two companies sought regulation in order to stave off competitors.[2]

## Musk lawsuit verdict (2026)

In May 2026 a federal jury rejected [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s claims against Altman and [[openai|OpenAI]] after less than two hours of deliberation.[3] U.S. District Judge [[yvonne-gonzalez-rogers|Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers]] adopted the advisory jury's finding that Altman and OpenAI were not liable, and dismissed claims of breach of charitable trust and unjust enrichment as untimely.[3] Musk's 2024 suit had alleged that Altman abandoned OpenAI's founding nonprofit mission, and sought as much as $134 billion, Altman's removal alongside that of president [[greg-brockman|Greg Brockman]], and reversal of the company's 2025 restructuring; see [[musk-v-openai|Musk v. OpenAI]].[3]

## Stargate and the clash with Musk (January 2025)

Altman was named alongside [[softbank|SoftBank]]'s [[masayoshi-son|Masayoshi Son]] and [[oracle|Oracle]]'s [[larry-ellison|Larry Ellison]] as a principal of Stargate, the $500 billion artificial-intelligence infrastructure project announced by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in January 2025 and funded by [[openai|OpenAI]], Oracle and SoftBank.[4] Trump described the venture as "a resounding declaration of confidence in America's potential," with an initial $100 billion private investment.[4]

When OpenAI posted on X that it would begin deploying $100 billion immediately, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] responded "They don't actually have the money," later adding that "SoftBank has well under $10B secured. I have that on good authority."[4] Altman replied first diplomatically, then sharply: "Wrong, as you surely know. Want to come visit the first site already under way? This is great for the country. I realize what is great for the country isn't always what's optimal for your companies, but in your new role, I hope you'll mostly put [the United States] first."[4]

Badlands commentator [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] noted that Trump had put Altman and Ellison in the spotlight and that the internet had begun digging into both, while [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] argued that conservatives souring on Trump over the exchange were falling victim to an anti-Trump psyop.[4]

## Worldcoin

Altman is a co-founder of [[worldcoin|Worldcoin]], a venture that offers free crypto tokens worth roughly $50 to people willing to have their eyeballs scanned by a device called the Orb.[5] In August 2023 the Kenyan Ministry of the Interior suspended Worldcoin and similar entities from operating in [[kenya|Kenya]], opening inquiries into the legality of the scheme and the safety of the biometric data being harvested, with Cabinet Secretary [[alfred-mutua|Alfred Mutua]] objecting to Kenyans being used as "guinea pigs."[5]

## Return as OpenAI CEO (November 2023)

Five days of turmoil at [[openai|OpenAI]] ended early on Wednesday, 22 November 2023, when the company announced on X that it had "reached an agreement in principle for Sam Altman to return to OpenAI as CEO," alongside a new initial board of Bret Taylor as chair, [[larry-summers|Larry Summers]] and Adam D'Angelo.[6] Altman wrote that everything he had done over the preceding days was "in service of keeping this team and its mission together," that joining [[microsoft|Microsoft]] the prior Sunday evening had seemed the best path at the time, and that with the new board and Satya Nadella's support he looked forward to returning.[6] The reinstatement was expected to satisfy the more than 700 employees who had threatened to resign and join Microsoft unless the directors stepped down.[6] See [[2023-openai-leadership-crisis|2023 OpenAI leadership crisis]].[6]

## Chip and hardware fundraising before the ouster

In the weeks leading up to his ouster from [[openai|OpenAI]], Altman was actively working to raise billions of dollars from some of the world's largest investors for a new chip venture, according to people familiar with the matter.[7] He had been travelling to the Middle East to fundraise for the project, which was code-named [[tigris|Tigris]], and planned to spin up an AI-focused chip company producing semiconductors to compete with those of [[nvidia|Nvidia Corp.]], which then dominated the market for artificial intelligence tasks.[7] The venture had not yet been formed and talks with investors were in early stages, the people said, asking not to be named because the discussions were private.[7]

Altman was also seeking money for an AI-focused hardware device he had been developing in tandem with former [[apple|Apple Inc.]] design chief [[jony-ive|Jony Ive]], holding talks about both ventures with [[softbank|SoftBank Group Corp.]], Saudi Arabia's [[public-investment-fund|Public Investment Fund]], [[mubadala-investment-company|Mubadala Investment Company]] and others as he sought tens of billions of dollars for the new companies.[7] Many details of the scale and focus of his chip ambitions, as well as the project's codename, had not previously been reported.[7]
