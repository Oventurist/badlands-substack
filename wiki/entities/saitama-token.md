# Saitama Token

Saitama Token, also known as Saitama Inu, is a cryptocurrency whose contract was created on the [[ethereum|Ethereum]] network on May 30, 2021 with an initial supply of 100 quadrillion tokens.[1] Badlands Media presented the project as a case study in low-level crypto fraud and the failure of accountability in the [[decentralized-finance|DeFi]] sector.[1]

## Token distribution

Of the initial 100 quadrillion tokens, 47.04 quadrillion were burned and removed from circulating supply, 46.06 quadrillion were added to [[uniswap|Uniswap]] as tradable supply, and 5.76 quadrillion were reserved as "team tokens" for the small handful of people running the project.[1] According to Sky, a retail investigator operating under the handle Krypto_Research who spoke to Badlands, the developers never disclosed the team tokens at launch and instead claimed on their website to have conducted a [[fair-launch|fair launch]].[1] That website remained online at the time of publication.[1]

## Allegations

Sky characterized Saitama as a meme coin "intended to rug since the beginning."[1] The team was said to number around eight people; the original developer, known as "Mr. Saito," allegedly handed the project to the later embattled dev team.[1] The contract was renounced on June 5, 2021, less than a week after launch, and the story circulating in the community is that the Saitama team took the money and ran.[1]

According to Sky, "the original deployer began moving the undisclosed reserve tokens from four main wallets through hundreds of sub wallets — which had already sold a lot — and some still hold close to 3 quadrillion. At least 1 quadrillion ended up directly on Gate.io and several more trillions on Bitmart, Lbank, and other exchanges."[1] These transfers were allegedly used to hide the trace of the team tokens, though KYC limitations at the exchanges forced some funds into private wallets, from which they were sold on Uniswap.[1] Investigators tracking the wallets say they caught the pattern.[1]

## Legal exposure and status

The article argued that advertising a fair launch while secretly reserving team tokens amounts to deliberately misleading investors, a violation of [[securities-exchange-act-of-1934|Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934]], and noted the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|SEC]]'s position that "victims in these cases have the right to recover their investment losses they suffered as a result of the fraud."[1] As of publication there had been no accountability for Saitama and no recovery of losses, though Sky expressed optimism, saying authorities were "100%" looking into the matter given the project's market capitalization — close to $8 billion in November 2021 — and the deployer's connection to multiple [[rug-pull|rug pulls]].[1]
