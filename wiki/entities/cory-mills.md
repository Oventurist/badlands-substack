# Cory Mills

Cory Mills is a Republican member of the U.S. House of Representatives from Florida.[1]

## Position on mail-in voting

Quoted in a February 2026 Politico report on Republican reluctance to embrace President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s election reform agenda, Mills took a middle position: "We have to be very cautious about mail-in-ballot voting," he said. "But I think that if we get the registration process correct, then that'll fix a lot of this."[1]

The [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] cited his comments as part of a pattern of congressional Republicans hedging on the [[election-reform-campaign|election reform campaign]] during an election year.[1]

## Criticism of the Afghanistan withdrawal

Mills, a veteran, was one of four veterans whose criticism of the 2021 [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] withdrawal prompted a September 2024 Fox News Digital inquiry to the [[white-house|White House]].[2] The veterans accused [[national-security-council|National Security Council]] communications adviser [[john-kirby|John Kirby]] of providing "cover" for the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration at a press conference held on the anniversary of 9/11.[2] Kirby responded in an errant "reply all" email that there was "obviously no use in responding" to "a 'handful' of vets indeed and all of one stripe."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that because the disparaged group included a sitting U.S. congressman, Republicans should "exploit the hell out of" the episode as an indication of the administration's private sentiment toward veterans.[2]

## "Sniper's paradise" comment (July 2024)

Following the July 13, 2024 attempted assassination of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Mills — a former Army sniper — described the rooftop a little over 400 feet from the stage as a "sniper's paradise," so obvious a firing position that he wondered aloud whether the [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]]'s failure to secure it was "intentional."[3] His remarks, delivered in an exchange that triggered a CNN host, accompanied a vow that a J13 committee would investigate the security failures.[3] Secret Service Director [[kimberly-cheatle|Kimberly Cheatle]] subsequently attributed the absence of an agent on the building to its "sloped roof," an explanation critics said did not add up.[3]
