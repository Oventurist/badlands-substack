# Max Boot

Max Boot is an American columnist and foreign-policy commentator described in Badlands coverage as a leading propagandist of the "Trump Russia Hoax," a self-described enthusiast of "forever wars" who laundered Trump–Russia conspiracy theories through the pages of the *Washington Post*.[1]

Boot is married to [[sue-mi-terry|Sue Mi Terry]], a former [[cia|CIA]] analyst and senior fellow at the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] who was indicted as an unregistered foreign agent acting on behalf of South Korea in an indictment revealed in July 2024.[1] According to the reporting, Terry allegedly began spying for South Korea in October 2013 — five years after leaving the CIA, and three years before Boot began publicly describing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as a Russian asset.[1] The juxtaposition of Boot's Russia-collusion advocacy with his wife's alleged decade-long covert relationship with a foreign intelligence service was the focus of the ZeroHedge item carried in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of 19 July 2024.[1]

## Assessment of the 1980 October surprise

In a [[politico|Politico]] magazine essay published on October 15, 2024 and excerpted by Badlands Media, Boot's definitive biography of [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] was cited on the question of the original [[october-surprise|October surprise]].[2] Reviewing new documents about the movements of Reagan campaign manager Bill Casey and the subsequent dealings between the Reagan administration and Iran, Boot asserted that the evidence for the theory that the campaign persuaded Tehran to delay releasing the 52 hostages until after the election was "substantial and credible if still circumstantial" — a contrast with the 1993 House conclusion that there was "little or no credible evidence of communication between the 1980 Reagan campaign and the government of Iran."[2]
