# National Intelligence Council

The **National Intelligence Council (NIC)** is the strategic analytic body of the United States Intelligence Community, reporting to the Director of National Intelligence. It produces National Intelligence Estimates and other community-coordinated assessments, and hosts National Intelligence Officers responsible for particular subject areas such as cyber, Russia, or economics.

## January 2020 election-infrastructure memo

A memo dated January 15, 2020, prepared by then-National Intelligence Officer for Cyber [[christopher-porter|Christopher Porter]], assessed that foreign adversaries possessed the capability to compromise American voting infrastructure ahead of the [[2020-united-states-presidential-election|2020 presidential election]]. It identified [[russia|Russia]], [[china|China]], [[iran|Iran]], North Korea, and non-state actors as potential threats able to disrupt voting, steal data, or undermine public confidence, and singled out centralized voter registration databases, electronic pollbooks, and election websites as most vulnerable because they were internet-connected. Data alterations, the memo warned, could prevent voting, cause delays, or force provisional ballots. President Trump was briefed on these concerns in February 2020.

The memo was declassified in 2026 by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]]. Post-election evidence cited alongside its release indicated that China had gained access to voter registration data in multiple states and that Iran had compromised election-related systems, breaches reportedly suppressed for years. The Intelligence Community's inspector general opened a full investigation into the alleged suppression. The declassification sat in tension with the public declaration by [[chris-krebs|Chris Krebs]] of the [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]] that the November 2020 election was "the most secure in U.S. history."

## Badlands treatment

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing in the Badlands Brief, treated the memo as vindication: known vulnerabilities that officials had publicly dismissed as conspiracy theory were documented internally before the vote. She linked the memo to the [[colorado-bios-password-scandal|Colorado BIOS password scandal]] as evidence that official assurances of election security have repeatedly outrun what officials could actually verify.
