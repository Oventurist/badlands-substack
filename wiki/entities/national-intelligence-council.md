# National Intelligence Council

The National Intelligence Council (NIC) is the U.S. Intelligence Community body responsible for producing strategic assessments spanning multiple agencies. It became a focus of Badlands coverage in April 2026 after the declassification of a January 2020 NIC memo concerning election infrastructure vulnerabilities.[1]

## The January 2020 memo

The memo, dated January 15, 2020 and prepared by then–National Intelligence Officer for Cyber [[christopher-porter|Christopher Porter]], warned that foreign adversaries had the capability to compromise America's voting infrastructure ahead of the 2020 presidential election, with particular emphasis on vulnerabilities in voter registration databases.[1] It assessed that Russia, China, [[iran|Iran]], North Korea and non-state actors could disrupt voting, steal data, or undermine public confidence.[1]

The assessment identified centralized databases, electronic pollbooks, and election websites as the most vulnerable components because they were internet-connected, potentially allowing data alterations that would prevent voting, cause delays, or force the use of provisional ballots.[1] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was briefed on the concerns in February 2020.[1]

## Aftermath and declassification

Post-election evidence cited in the reporting showed that China gained access to voter registration data in multiple states, and that Iran also compromised election-related systems — breaches reportedly suppressed for years.[1] Despite the pre-election warnings, [[chris-krebs|Chris Krebs]], then director of the [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]], and other officials publicly declared the November 2020 election "the most secure in U.S. history."[1]

The memo was declassified by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], and the Intelligence Community's inspector general opened a full investigation into the alleged suppression.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the memo as a revelation about vulnerabilities that the government had publicly dismissed as conspiracy theory after November 3, 2020, arguing that the falsity of the 2020 talking points extends to every election since.[1] She linked the memo to the [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]] BIOS password scandal in [[colorado|Colorado]] as a concrete illustration of official denial outrunning actual investigation.[1]


## April 2025 Tren de Aragua assessment

In April 2025 the council produced a memo, released publicly by the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]], assessing the relationship between the Venezuelan government and the [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] gang.[2] The memo found that some Venezuelan officials "facilitated" illegal migration of gang members into the United States, but concluded that while Venezuela's "permissive environment enables TDA to operate," the [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] regime "probably is not systematically directing Venezuelan outflows."[2] The assessment watered down an earlier [[fbi|FBI]] judgment attributing direction of TdA activity to the Maduro government, and Badlands commentary noted that the underlying claims rest on unverifiable sources.[2]


## May 2025 firings

In May 2025, Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] fired two officials at the council: [[michael-collins-nic|Michael Collins]], its acting chair, and [[maria-langan-riekhof|Maria Langan-Riekhof]], who served as Collins' deputy.[3] A spokesperson for Gabbard's office said the move was part of the Trump administration's goal to "end the weaponization and politicization of the intelligence community," stating that "the Director is working alongside President Trump to end the weaponization and politicization of the Intelligence Community."[3]

The dismissals came shortly after the council published a report suggesting that the [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] regime in [[venezuela|Venezuela]] plays no role in [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] gang members illegally immigrating to the United States.[3]

## 2025 whistleblower disclosure

The whistleblower whose testimony DNI Tulsi Gabbard declassified in 2025 served as a Deputy National Intelligence Officer at the National Intelligence Council from 2015 to 2020 and helped produce the Intelligence Community Assessment on cyber threats to the 2016 election.[4] He alleged he was pressured to change his findings to conform to the conclusion that Russia acted to help Donald Trump.[4]
