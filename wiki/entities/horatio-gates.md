# Horatio Gates

General Horatio Gates was the newly appointed commander of the American [[continental-army|Continental Army]] in the South in 1780, and the officer to whose camp [[francis-marion|Francis Marion]] first brought his militia band.[1]

## Character and outlook

Gates is described as the epitome of a British officer: English-born, trained at a military academy in the art of open-field warfare with set pieces, a former major in the British army, and a devoted observer of the European rules of engagement.[1] Any deviation from those conventions struck him as an affront to civilized society, and yet he found himself commanding an army of farmers and peasants in open rebellion against the [[british-empire|British Empire]].[1] To Gates, guerrilla warfare was not only an inferior form of combat but evidence of inferior intelligence — "the blunt blade of undisciplined savages."[1]

## Marion's arrival at Buffalo Ford

On 25 July 1780, at Gates's camp near Buffalo Ford, North Carolina, roughly twenty militia volunteers rode in on horseback: some white, some black, with a Catawba Indian or two among them and a few soldiers barely in their teens.[1] One of Gates's officers, noting the "wretchedness of their attire," described the newcomers' appearance as "so burlesque" that the Continentals had to restrain themselves from laughing.[1] At their head was a small man with fire in his eyes, wearing a tattered Continental uniform whose presence kept the laughter at bay, and who walked with a pronounced limp yet moved with quickness.[1] Shortly afterward, the Continental Army that had marched south to relieve Charleston was decimated and captured at the [[battle-of-camden|Battle of Camden]].[1]
