# National Film Administration

The **National Film Administration (NFA)** is the [[china|Chinese]] government body that regulates the domestic film market, including the quota of foreign films permitted for theatrical import.[1]

## Restriction of Hollywood imports (April 2025)

On April 10, 2025 the NFA announced that China would immediately restrict imports of [[hollywood|Hollywood]] films in retaliation for President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s escalation of U.S. tariffs on imported Chinese goods.[1] For three decades China had annually imported ten Hollywood movies; the agency said the tariff increase would further sour domestic demand for U.S. cinema after years of decline.[1] "We will follow market rules, respect the audience's choices, and moderately reduce the number of American films imported," the NFA said on its website.[1]

Chris Fenton, author of *Feeding the Dragon: Inside the Trillion Dollar Dilemma Facing Hollywood, the NBA, and American Business*, described the move as a "super high-profile way to make a statement of retaliation with almost zero downside for China," noting that Hollywood films account for only about 5 percent of overall box office receipts in the Chinese market.[1] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] framed the story around Hollywood's disappointment at reduced access to the Chinese market for "American propaganda."[1]
