# Joni Ernst

Joni Ernst is a Republican United States Senator from Iowa and a member of the Senate Armed Services Committee. In December 2024 she became the first and most prominent test case in what allies of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] described as a campaign to eliminate the last pockets of Republican resistance to the president-elect within the [[us-senate|Senate]].[1]

## December 2024 Hegseth confirmation controversy

Ernst's initial hesitance toward [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]], Trump's embattled nominee for Secretary of Defense, drew a substantial wave of criticism from Republicans in her home state of Iowa.[1] As a member of the Armed Services Committee — the panel with jurisdiction over the Defense Secretary nomination — Ernst was the first senator placed under the spotlight by MAGA activists conducting what they described as a hunt for [[rino|RINOs]], or Republicans-In-Name-Only.[1]

Steve Deace, an Iowa-based conservative radio talk show host who was publicly weighing a primary challenge against Ernst, told Politico that "the pressure valve is now pointed in the direction of the Senate," adding that "the tea party was not nearly as organized as the Trump movement is."[1]

## Context

Ernst's predicament formed part of a broader December 2024 pattern in which Republican senators judged insufficiently loyal to Trump faced organized pressure. Senator [[bill-cassidy|Bill Cassidy]], one of four remaining senators who voted to impeach Trump in 2021, drew a formal primary challenge, while moderates [[susan-collins|Susan Collins]] of Maine and [[lisa-murkowski|Lisa Murkowski]] of Alaska began catching heat from party activists.[1] Trump's unorthodox nominees, including [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] for FBI director and [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] for intelligence chief, tested the Senate's willingness to defer to the incoming administration.[1]

Badlands Media commentary framed the episode differently, arguing that the notion of the Senate as an authority over the president is a fiction, and that "advise and confirm is not veto power" — confirmation being, in that reading, a ministerial record-keeping act rather than an approval right.[1]

## Role in the Hegseth confirmation hearing (January 2025)

Ernst, a veteran and sexual assault survivor, was widely seen as a key Republican vote on the Senate Armed Services Committee and critical to the confirmation chances of [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] as defense secretary.[2] At the January 14, 2025 hearing she appeared satisfied with Hegseth's responses to her questions about the role of women in the military and about sexual assault prevention, and spent much of her allotted time focusing on an area of agreement between them: that the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] should be audited.[2] Her posture helped Hegseth emerge from the more than four-hour hearing with Republican support intact.[2]
