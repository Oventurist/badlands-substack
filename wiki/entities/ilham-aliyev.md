# Ilham Aliyev

Ilham Aliyev is the President of [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]].[1]

## Zangezur corridor agreement (August 2025)

On Friday, August 8, 2025, Aliyev signed an agreement in Washington alongside Armenian Prime Minister [[nikol-pashinyan|Nikol Pashinyan]] and US President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to open the [[zangezur-corridor|Zangezur transport corridor]].[1] The route links mainland Azerbaijan to its exclave of Nakhichevan through a narrow strip of land in southern [[armenia|Armenia]] running along the border with [[iran|Iran]].[1] Under the terms of the deal, the United States would manage the corridor under Armenian sovereignty via a 99-year land lease, subletting it to a consortium for construction and operations.[1]

Tehran condemned the plan, warning that a US-controlled corridor threatened regional stability and undermined Iranian security interests; Iran said it welcomed peace agreements between Yerevan and Baku but "vehemently" opposed placing the corridor under American control.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the deal was designed in part to sever the geographical connection between Russia and Iran, and recalled that Aliyev and Pashinyan had both worked to entrap [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in a quagmire when Russia deployed peacekeeping troops to the same corridor in 2020 to end fighting between the two countries.[1] The commentary framed NATO's years-long effort to pull the Caucasus into Europe's orbit via Turkey as parallel to its posture on Ukraine, because the region sits on Russia's doorstep — and also on Iran's.[1]

The contributor suggested that Aliyev and Pashinyan appeared "giddy" because they believed the corridor threatened Iran, and noted allegations that Azerbaijan had already been used by the IDF to launch drone strikes on Tehran, which made the claim that Armenia would suddenly become an attack platform feel redundant.[1] He proposed watching how both leaders behaved after the [[2025-trump-putin-alaska-summit|Trump–Putin summit in Alaska]] scheduled for that same week.[1]

## Support for Ukraine and the 2025 rift with Moscow

In July 2025 Aliyev urged Ukraine to "never agree to occupation," speaking at the Shusha Global Media Forum and encouraging Kyiv to consider Azerbaijan's approach to its territorial dispute with Armenia over Nagorno-Karabakh — a conflict that ended in 2023 after Azerbaijan retook the region by force, prompting the departure of its remaining ethnic Armenian population.[2] The remarks came amid a broader deterioration in relations with Moscow, in which the [[kremlin|Kremlin]] detained the leader of the Azerbaijani diaspora in Russia during a crackdown on Azeri criminal organizations and Baku responded by imprisoning several Sputnik journalists as alleged Kremlin agents.[2] Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] said Russia hoped the disagreement would not obstruct the restoration of ties.[2] See [[2025-russia-azerbaijan-rift|2025 Russia–Azerbaijan rift]].[2]
