# U.S. Department of the Treasury

The U.S. Department of the Treasury appears in the article as the federal body whose own public record contradicts [[john-mccain|John McCain]]'s 2011 claim that the Libyan rebels were not Al-Qaeda.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

On 30 October 2008 the Treasury designated three members of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] under Executive Order 13224, which targets terrorists and the financial, technological or material support networks of terrorists.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) Adam Szubin, director of Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control, stated that "LIFG, along with other al Qaida affiliates, seeks to exploit our globalized world, raising funds in Europe for transfer to terror cells operating in North Africa."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

The accompanying Treasury release set out LIFG's history: its 1995 formation among Libyans who had fought Soviet forces in Afghanistan and the Gaddafi regime; its believed participation in planning the May 2003 Casablanca suicide bombings and its links to the 2004 Madrid attacks; the flight of many members from Libya after the late 1990s, particularly to the United Kingdom; Ayman al-Zawahiri's announcement in early November 2007 that LIFG had become an official affiliate of [[al-qaeda|al Qaida]]; and the group's designation as a Specially Designated Global Terrorist in the annex to E.O. 13224 on 23 September 2001 and its addition to the UN 1267 Committee Consolidated List on 6 October 2001.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

The article uses this documentary record — dated three years before McCain's Benghazi trip — to argue that neither McCain nor [[barack-obama|Obama]] could credibly have been unaware that the men they backed were Al-Qaeda.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Role in the FedAccounts proposal

A separate Badlands Media essay places the Treasury at the centre of a proposed transformation of American banking.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) The [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]] concept was posed in 2018 by three researchers, two of whom worked in the Treasury Department, among them [[morgan-ricks|Morgan Ricks]]; the article notes that [[donald-trump|Trump]] was president at the time and treats the timing as significant rather than coincidental.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Badlands Media urges readers to substitute "Treasury" for "[[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]]" in descriptions of how FedAccounts would hold "pure sovereign money," arguing that the Federal Reserve is unconstitutional and that the Treasury, not a private central bank, is properly in charge of the people's money.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) In that framing the return to a redeemable [[gold-standard|gold standard]] and to Treasury-issued sovereign money would remove any need for [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|Federal Deposit Insurance]] and end taxpayer bailouts of private banks.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Cuba convoy probe (2026)

In May 2026 the Treasury Department's [[office-of-foreign-assets-control|Office of Foreign Assets Control]] was reported to be investigating more than 40 Americans for potential violations of U.S. sanctions law arising from the March 2026 [[nuestra-america-convoy|Nuestra America Convoy]] to [[cuba|Cuba]].[3] Federal investigators subpoenaed streamer [[hasan-piker|Hasan Piker]] and activist [[medea-benjamin|Medea Benjamin]], seeking financial, logistical and communications records related to the trip.[3]

The probe came amid heightened [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration pressure on Cuba, including a tightened blockade, the state-sponsor-of-terrorism designation, and the indictment of [[raul-castro|Raúl Castro]].[3]

## Operation Economic Fury (2026)

In April 2026 the Treasury imposed sanctions on 35 individuals and entities tied to [[iran|Iran]]'s "shadow banking" network, a system used to move tens of billions of dollars globally to evade sanctions and fund military and proxy operations.[4] The action formed part of a broader pressure campaign described as [[operation-economic-fury|Operation Economic Fury]], aimed at cutting off Iran's access to international finance, including networks linked to the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]].[4] It came alongside the [[naval-blockade-of-iran|US naval blockade of Iran]] begun that month.[4]

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] credited Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] with the sanctions salvo and argued that the [[shadow-banking-empire|shadow banking]] architecture being dismantled was not primarily Iranian but the financial rails of the wider globalist order.[4]

## Disrupting political-terrorism finance (July 2026)
Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] said in July 2026 that the department would intensify efforts to investigate and disrupt funding networks supporting violent far-left political organizations, as part of the administration's [[global-crackdown-on-far-left-political-terrorism|global crackdown on far-left political terrorism]] launched at a Washington summit of more than 65 countries.[5] The financial tools were presented alongside diplomatic and law enforcement measures, including terrorist designations, sanctions and visa restrictions.[5]

## Iran shadow fleet sanctions (February 2026)

In February 2026 the Treasury imposed new sanctions targeting vessels, companies, and financial networks linked to [[iran|Iran]]'s so-called [[iran-shadow-fleet|shadow fleet]], accusing them of helping Tehran evade sanctions by transporting oil and generating revenue for missile and weapons programs.[6] The designations named shipping facilitators and associated entities alleged to support Iran's military-linked industries, including missile development, and came amid rising tension over possible Israeli strikes on Iran.[6]

In the same period, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] recounted [[doge|DOGE]] findings that Treasury payment approval officers had been instructed to always approve payments, with zero denials across entire careers, even for known fraudulent or terrorist-linked recipients — a practice she cited as emblematic of the plausible-deniability architecture the [[war-on-fraud|War on Fraud]] was meant to dismantle.[6]

## Overtime deduction data, 2026 filing season

Internal Treasury Department data reported in early 2026 showed that nearly 20 million Americans had claimed the [[overtime-pay-tax-deduction|overtime pay tax deduction]] enacted in the Republican 2025 tax cuts, with the break appearing on roughly 23 percent of returns filed that season and outpacing other new provisions covering tipped income, seniors and car-loan interest.[7] The figures became the basis for Republican claims of a political win ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]] and for Democratic debate over whether to extend, replace or scrutinize the provision.[7]

## Iranian oil-laundering designations

Sanctions issued under Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] identified a network run by the Iraqi-British national [[salim-ahmed-said|Salim Ahmed Said]], which included the shipping company [[vs-tankers-fze|VS Tankers FZE]], as selling Iranian oil falsely declared as Iraqi oil since at least 2020.[8] The designation described the use of ship-to-ship transfers and other obfuscation techniques, with Iranian oil blended into Iraqi oil and sold to Western buyers via [[iraq|Iraq]] or the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] under forged documentation, allowing [[iran|Iran]] to evade international sanctions on its oil exports.[8] Treasury further asserted that Said had bribed many members of key Iraqi government bodies, including parliament, paying millions of dollars in kickbacks for forged vouchers.[8] Said was designated pursuant to Executive Order 13902 for operating in the petroleum sector of the Iranian economy.[8]

Badlands commentary linked the designation to the June 2026 Iranian strikes on tankers in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and to the concurrent [[green-zone|Green Zone]] anti-corruption sweep in Baghdad, and drew a parallel to the 2018 sanctions [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] imposed on Iranian central bankers accused of laundering U.S. cash through Iraqi and UAE banks.[8]

## FinCEN whistleblower tip portal (2026)

On February 13, 2026, the Treasury announced a new website to accept confidential whistleblower tips on fraud, money laundering and sanctions violations, expanding federal efforts to combat financial crime.[9] Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] said the portal, run through the [[financial-crimes-enforcement-network|Financial Crimes Enforcement Network]]'s Office of the Whistleblower, would take reports of suspected violations of the [[bank-secrecy-act|Bank Secrecy Act]], U.S. sanctions programs and other financial laws, with awards available to tipsters whose information leads to successful enforcement.[9] The department encouraged submitters to provide detailed documentation, and framed the initiative as strengthening enforcement tools amid broader federal scrutiny of financial abuses.[9]

Badlands commentary linked the program to the department's earlier 2025 sanctioning of Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]], whose designation accused him of causing Colombia's financial intelligence unit to be suspended from the [[egmont-group|Egmont Group]].[9]

## Venezuela oil-sector sanctions (December 2025 – January 2026)

At the turn of 2026 the Treasury's [[office-of-foreign-assets-control|Office of Foreign Assets Control]] sanctioned four companies — Corniola Limited, Krape Myrtle Co LTD, Winky International Limited, and Aries Global Investment LTD — for operating in [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s oil sector and helping the country evade sanctions, and designated four oil tankers as blocked property in connection with the action.[10] The designations were made under [[executive-order-13850|Executive Order 13850]], "Blocking Property of Additional Persons Contributing to the Situation in Venezuela," dated November 2, 2018; transactions by U.S. persons involving the blocked or designated persons are generally prohibited, and violations can lead to civil or criminal penalties.[10]

One day earlier, on December 30, 2025, OFAC updated its Specially Designated Nationals list to include ten individuals and entities based in Venezuela and [[iran|Iran]] for involvement in the Iran–Venezuela weapons trade and related military-industrial activity.[10] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Treasury added Iranian entities while removing four women and two men — persons from Russia, Switzerland, Cyprus, and Israel — from the list, joking that the department's staff worked through the holiday and "checked their sanctions list twice."[10] Under Secretary for Terrorism and Financial Intelligence [[john-k-hurley|John K. Hurley]] said Treasury was "holding Iran and Venezuela accountable for their aggressive and reckless proliferation of deadly weapons around the world" and would act swiftly to deprive enablers of Iran's [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]] of access to the U.S. financial system.[10]

## Release of Biden family suspicious activity reports (2023)

In March 2023 the Treasury Department agreed to comply with a request from the [[united-states-house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] and release [[suspicious-activity-report|suspicious activity reports]] generated by the business transactions of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s family and their associates.[11] The disclosure was characterized as a major development in the committee's investigation into the Biden family business, which was examining nine alleged violations including wire fraud and money laundering and sought to determine whether the president was compromised by communist China.[11] United States banks had flagged more than 150 such reports involving [[hunter-biden|Hunter]] and [[james-biden|James Biden]] covering "large" amounts of money tagged for further Treasury review.[11]

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] expressed skepticism about material produced by a Treasury Department under the Biden administration, warning that documents could prove "missing, altered or incomplete" and questioning whether the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] would act on incriminating evidence even if it were fully released.[11]

## Chinese state-sponsored breach (December 2024)

In a letter sent to Congress on Monday, December 30, 2024, the Treasury Department disclosed that it had been hacked by a Chinese state-sponsored actor through a third-party software service provider, describing the intrusion as a "major cybersecurity incident" because it was attributed to a state-sponsored actor.[12] Treasury said it was notified on December 8 by third-party software provider BeyondTrust Inc. that a hacker had gained access "to a key used by the vendor to secure a cloud-based service used to remotely provide technical support for Treasury Departmental Offices (DO) end users."[12] The department was assisted by the [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]], the FBI, the intelligence community and third-party forensic investigators, and advanced hackers tied to [[china]] were assessed as responsible.[12] China denied the accusation, with its Washington embassy calling the claims baseless smear attacks, while Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] suggested the US government itself was the more likely culprit.[12] The episode is covered in detail at [[us-treasury-department-hack-2024]].

## Yellen–He Lifeng economic talks (November 2023)

The Treasury Department announced on November 6, 2023 that Secretary [[janet-yellen|Janet Yellen]] would hold economic talks with Chinese Vice Premier [[he-lifeng|He Lifeng]] on November 9 and 10, ahead of a potential meeting between the leaders of the world's two largest economies.[13] The talks were scheduled to precede the [[asia-pacific-economic-cooperation|Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation]] summit in San Francisco, where Yellen was to host finance officials and ministers from APEC's 21 member states on November 11.[13] China's Foreign Ministry confirmed the meeting, with spokesman Wang Wenbin saying He would visit the United States from November 8 to 12.[13] The announcement followed state-media reports that He had been named head of two Chinese Communist Party committees created by [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] in March 2023 to bring the financial system under direct Party control.[13]

## July 2025 cash balance rebuild

In July 2025 the Treasury announced plans to rebuild its cash balance to $500 billion by the end of the month, following passage of a sweeping $5 trillion tax and spending bill that included a significant debt ceiling hike.[14] The department's cash balance had dropped to $372 billion, according to a JPMorgan research note, well below the operational target range, and to address the shortfall the Treasury said it would sharply increase issuance of short-term Treasury bills at 4-, 6-, and 8-week maturities.[14] It stated that it did not expect to issue additional cash management bills to rebuild reserves — signaling confidence in the T-bill market's ability to absorb the increased supply — and noted that the CMB series announced on June 24 had been completed.[14] At the May refunding the Treasury had targeted an $850 billion cash balance by the end of September, suggesting further issuance would continue in line with internal cash balance policy.[14] Because buyers pay for bills from commercial bank accounts and the proceeds move into the [[treasury-general-account|Treasury General Account]] at the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], the operation drains bank reserves, a dynamic Badlands commentary linked to the parallel rise of [[private-credit|private credit]].[14] Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] simultaneously reported roughly $100 billion in 2025 tariff revenue collected to date, projecting as much as $300 billion by December.[14]

## Withdrawal from the NGFS (2025)

In early February 2025 the Treasury's Federal Insurance Office (FIO) notified the [[network-of-central-banks-and-supervisors-for-greening-the-financial-system|Network of Central Banks and Supervisors for Greening the Financial System]] (NGFS) that it was withdrawing its membership.[15] Treasury described the withdrawal as part of implementing President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s executive orders Putting America First in International Environmental Agreements and Unleashing American Energy, noting that the NGFS had been organized to help meet the goals of the Paris Agreement, from which the United States had withdrawn.[15] The department said the network's initiatives were inconsistent with the administration's priorities of growing the US economy and American jobs, and that important parts of its scope — including monetary policy frameworks — went beyond the FIO's core duties.[15]

## DOGE access to the federal payment system

Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] granted representatives of the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] access to the federal payment system late on Friday, January 31, 2025, handing [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and his team a powerful tool to monitor and potentially limit government spending.[15] The move followed a standoff with [[david-lebryk|David Lebryk]], a career civil servant and top Treasury official who had resisted allowing DOGE into the system; he was placed on leave and abruptly retired that Friday.[15] Reporting noted that control of the system, which disburses money on behalf of the entire federal government, could give the administration another mechanism to attempt to unilaterally restrict disbursement of funds appropriated by Congress.[15]

## Russia sanctions (March 2023)

In early March 2023 the Treasury Department announced a new wave of sanctions against Russia's metals and mining sector, intended to hinder the country's resupply efforts in its ground war against Ukraine.[16] The measures were coordinated with U.S. allies and [[g7|G7]] partners with the expressed purpose of isolating Russia from the global economy, and were credited with contributing to losses of hundreds of billions of dollars in Russia's financial sector.[16] Since the start of the war, Treasury actions taken with a coalition of more than 30 allies and partners had included sanctions, export controls and price caps, blacklisting thousands of individuals and entities tied to the Russian regime — including senior officials and military manufacturing firms — while more than 1,000 foreign companies scaled back or halted operations in Russia.[16] Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] questioned whether these Treasury and [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] actions truly targeted Russia and its enablers or instead a transnational "Swamp" spanning Russia, Europe and the United States.[16]

## Gold disclosure under the Gold Standard Restoration Act (April 2023)

Under H.R. 2435, the [[gold-standard-restoration-act|Gold Standard Restoration Act]] introduced in April 2023 by Reps. [[alex-mooney|Alex Mooney]], [[andy-biggs|Andy Biggs]] and [[paul-gosar|Paul Gosar]], the Treasury and the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] would be given 24 months to publicly disclose all gold holdings and gold transactions, after which the Federal Reserve note "dollar" would be repegged to a fixed weight of gold at its then-market price.[17]

## Deposit guarantee review (March 2023)

Bloomberg reported in March 2023 that Treasury Department staff were reviewing whether federal regulators had enough emergency authority to temporarily insure deposits greater than the $250,000 cap on most accounts, without formal consent from a deeply divided [[us-congress|Congress]], as a coalition of banks pressed for a blanket [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|FDIC]] guarantee.[18] Authorities did not yet view the step as necessary, particularly after regulators had acted earlier that month to help banks meet withdrawal demands, but were developing a strategy out of due diligence in case conditions worsened.[18] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] asked "what does the Treasury know that we don't," applying to the department the same question hawks posed about a [[federal-reserve|Fed]] pause.[18] The Treasury had also joined the Fed and FDIC in shoring up the sector after the failures of [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] and [[first-republic-bank|First Republic Bank]].[18]

## Merger with the Federal Reserve thesis (March 2020)

Badlands commentary has focused heavily on the March 2020 emergency lending architecture as a moment of institutional fusion between the Treasury and the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[21] Under that arrangement the Fed financed a special purpose vehicle for each emergency facility while the Treasury, drawing on the [[exchange-stabilization-fund|Exchange Stabilization Fund]], made an equity investment in each SPV and took the "first loss" position — meaning in substance that the Treasury, not the Fed, was buying the securities and backstopping the loans, with the Fed acting as banker and provider of financing and [[blackrock|BlackRock]] hired to execute the trades and administer the vehicles on the Treasury's behalf.[21] Contemporaneous commentary described this as the federal government nationalizing large swaths of the financial markets and as a scheme that essentially merged the Fed and Treasury into one organization.[21]

Because the Treasury sits within the executive branch and the Exchange Stabilization Fund is controlled by the Treasury and the president and exempt from Congressional approval, [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argued in "Master and Commander" that this made [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] the effective Fed chairman and placed the most powerful central bank in the world under presidential control.[21] He added that Trump directed the fund toward bailing out American companies struggling during the pandemic rather than distressed foreign central banks, its customary modern use, and concluded that the Federal Reserve and the Treasury were thereafter "working for the American people, not the central banks."[21]


## Platinum coinage authority

The Treasury holds a statutory authority to unilaterally mint platinum coins, a power that gave rise to the [[trillion-dollar-coin|trillion-dollar coin]] proposal — under which the department would strike a coin denominated at $1 trillion and deposit it in its account at the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] to fund operations without new borrowing under the [[debt-ceiling|debt ceiling]].[22] The idea began in 2011 as a suggestion for Treasury Secretary Timothy Geithner and resurfaced in 2013 and again in May 2023.[22] As a 2013 Quartz discussion summarized the underlying premise, "a coin can be any size. The Treasury gets to decide a coin's worth, and the value of the metal that comprises the coin doesn't have anything to do with its value in currency."[22]

Treasury Secretary [[janet-yellen|Janet Yellen]] rejected the maneuver in January 2023 as a "gimmick," emphasizing that the Federal Reserve is not required to accept such a coin.[22] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the episode illustrates the limits of Treasury's nominal authority, since the decision ultimately rests with the central bank rather than the department.[22]

## Twitter payment-processor registration (2022)

A March 2023 Badlands Media article by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] cited Washington Examiner reporting that [[twitter|Twitter]] filed paperwork with the Treasury Department to enter the payment processing business, expanding [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s vision for the company.[24] The registration was filed with the department's [[financial-crimes-enforcement-network|Financial Crimes Enforcement Network]], a step required of any business that wants to be involved in money transfers, currency and exchanges.[24] Lange treated the filing, alongside Musk's repurchase of the [[x-com|X.com]] domain from [[paypal|PayPal]], as evidence that Musk intends to revive his original digital-banking plan and compete with the legacy banks.[24]

## The COVFEFE Dig

Badlands Media's "The COVFEFE Dig" (March 2023) presents the Treasury as the institution that, in the author's reading, ultimately absorbed the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[26] [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]]'s [[executive-order-11110|Executive Order 11110]] is described as an effort to transfer power from the Fed to the Treasury by replacing Federal Reserve Notes with silver certificates, the distinction being that a United States Note carries no interest while a Federal Reserve Note does.[26]

During the 2020 COVID interventions the Treasury, using the [[exchange-stabilization-fund|Exchange Stabilization Fund]], made equity investments in each Fed-financed special purpose vehicle — the CPFF, PMCCF, TALF, SMCCF and MSBLP.[26] [[gmoney|GMONEY]] concludes that in substance the Treasury bought the securities and backstopped the loans while the Fed acted as banker and financier, with [[blackrock|BlackRock]] hired to execute purchases and administer the vehicles on the Treasury's behalf.[26]

## The 2016 Iran currency license and the Judgment Fund

"Panic in DC" identifies the Treasury as the instrument through which the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration allegedly circumvented its own public commitments on [[iran|Iran]].[23] Citing Investor's Business Daily and a report by the Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, the article states that Obama officials pushed the Treasury to permit Iran to convert the equivalent of $5.7 billion held in Oman's Bank of Muscat from rials into dollars and then into euros, which required a special Treasury license granted in February 2016 and never disclosed to [[congress|Congress]] or the public.[23]

The department also administers the [[judgment-fund|Judgment Fund]], the permanently appropriated account from which the $1.3 billion in interest paid to Iran in January 2016 was drawn, and CBS News reporting quoted in the article noted there was no explanation for the Treasury keeping the individual transactions just under $100 million each.[23] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues this structuring was intended to avoid triggering a reporting flag.[23] The same fund had been used for $278 million in Iran-related claims in 1991.[23]

## The nineteenth-century revolving door with Citibank

In "The Battle for Control," [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] describes the Treasury Department as the original source of [[citibank|Citibank]]'s political protection.[25] Bank president [[james-stillman|James Stillman]] recruited former Treasury officials to work for Citibank, ensuring the bank's increased wealth and political influence — a template Lange says shielded the institution from prosecution and secured its future bailouts.[25] Anticipating the revolving door between Citi and Washington, Stillman also recruited his eventual successor [[frank-vanderlip|Frank Vanderlip]] from the department; Vanderlip secretly accepted the offer and then spent another year at Treasury in what the cited account calls a patent conflict of interest, before becoming one of the architects of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve System]].[25]
