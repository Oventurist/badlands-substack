# Culper Spy Ring

The **Culper Spy Ring** was an intelligence network organized in 1778 by Major [[benjamin-tallmadge|Benjamin Tallmadge]] to gather information on British troop movements, fortifications, and military plans in the New York area during the American Revolution.[1]

## The Culper Code Book

Informants used fake names and a numerical code book consisting of seven hundred and sixty-three numbers representing words, names, and places.[1] The Culper Code Book was essential in protecting the vital communications and the identities of the group.[1] The Badlands Brief article "Kekistani Code Talkers" reproduces portions of the book, including its mappings of the English alphabet and base-10 digits, and extracts the words assigned three-digit numbers composed of the digits 1, 3, and 5 to illustrate how [[george-washington|Washington]] mapped hundreds of unique words to numbers.[1]

## Context

Washington, struggling to win a war with an army that was perpetually undermanned, undertrained, and undersupplied, relied increasingly on this unseen weapon of a secret intelligence network.[1] The story of the network is described as replete with intrigue: letters written in invisible ink, a rare female agent known only as Agent 355, and the execution of the spy [[nathan-hale|Nathan Hale]].[1] Washington and his allies were insurrectionists inside British colonies and traitors to the crown; despite geographical advantages, the threat of discovery and punishment by loyalists was constant.[1]

The ring is the third of five historical case studies in the article and the author's primary inspiration for it.[1]
