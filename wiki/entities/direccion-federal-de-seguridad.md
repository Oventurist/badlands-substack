# Dirección Federal de Seguridad

The Dirección Federal de Seguridad (Federal Security Directorate, DFS) was a Mexican intelligence agency and secret police force created in 1947 under president Miguel Alemán Valdés with the assistance of U.S. intelligence agencies — namely the [[cia|CIA]] — as part of the [[truman-doctrine|Truman Doctrine]] of Soviet containment, charged with preserving Mexico's internal stability against subversion and terrorist threats.[1] It was merged into the Centro de Investigación y Seguridad Nacional (CISEN) in 1985, though Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] notes that many accounts describe the DFS as simply disbanded that year.[1]

During the period from 1968 to the late 1970s, known as the Mexican Dirty War, the DFS was accused of illegal detentions, torture, assassinations and forced disappearances; at least 347 complaints concerning Mexican state crimes between 1960 and 1980 were received by the United Nations.[1] Lange observes that this record resembles the methods taught with CIA training manuals at the [[school-of-the-americas|School of the Americas]].[1]

## Cartel origins

The article argues that the agency's dissolution owed nothing to its civil-rights abuses. Multiple agents were suspected and later confirmed to have links to criminal organizations, including senior figures such as Miguel Nazar Haro and Arturo "El Negro" Durazo Moreno.[1] Other former agents included Rafael Aguilar Guajardo, a founding member of the Juárez Cartel, and Juan José Esparragoza Moreno, who became one of the leaders of the [[sinaloa-cartel|Sinaloa Cartel]], named in 2021 as the most powerful drug-trafficking organization in the world.[1] Lange treats it as no coincidence that a CIA-trained Mexican intelligence service produced the founders of the country's largest cartels.[1]

Julián Slim, the elder brother of the billionaire [[carlos-slim|Carlos Slim]], was a member of the DFS — another connection the article declines to read as accidental.[1]
