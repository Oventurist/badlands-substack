# Hank Johnson

Hank Johnson is a Democratic member of the United States House of Representatives from Georgia, widely known in political commentary for remarks during a congressional hearing suggesting that the island of Guam might capsize.[1]

## Badlands reference

In November 2024 commentary on Representative [[wiley-nickel|Wiley Nickel]]'s [[shadow-cabinet-proposal-2024|shadow cabinet proposal]], Badlands Media wrote that it never expected to see the day Johnson would lose his informal title as the least intelligent member of [[congress|Congress]], adding sardonically that Johnson's misunderstanding of marine topography at least made a kind of sense to very young children.[1] The reference is used as a rhetorical yardstick rather than a substantive claim about Johnson's record.[1]

## The Guam remark and the "balance" motif

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s February 14, 2023 essay "Balance, Necessary for Control" builds its central metaphor around Johnson, describing him as "a big proponent of balance."[2] Carlson recounts that in 2010 Johnson famously expressed concern over the island of [[guam|Guam]], fearing that if the United States built a military base on one side of the island the added weight could cause the island to tip over, and notes that some readers may assume the anecdote is a joke, "but he actually said it, and he's still a US Congressman."[2]

Carlson writes that Johnson had been comparatively quiet since 2010 and was "released from 'time out'" to tell America he believed the classified documents discovered in [[joe-biden|Biden]]'s possession had in fact been planted there — a set-up.[2] He asks why Democrats would have a man widely considered a joke deliver that theory, and concludes that they do not actually want the public to believe the documents were planted; they merely want to appear to be fighting for Biden, since it likely suits them for Biden to take the fall as the illusion that he is running the country becomes harder to maintain.[2]

Johnson's remark then supplies the essay's running image for the [[cabal|Cabal]]'s need to preserve a near-even partisan split: the perception of a slight majority must be maintained because, as Carlson puts it in Johnson's voice, it might otherwise "tip the island."[2] The piece closes by asking whether it is time to lose the balance, destroy the system, and tip the island.[2]
