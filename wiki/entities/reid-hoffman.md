# Reid Hoffman

Reid Hoffman is the founder of LinkedIn and a major Democratic Party donor. In July 2024 he was linked to the rapid spread of the claim that the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempt on Donald Trump's life]] had been staged.[1]

## Connection to the 2024 assassination-attempt narrative

Hoffman had joked about assassinating [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the week before the July 13, 2024 shooting.[1] His adviser, Democratic powerbroker [[dmitri-mehlhorn|Dmitri Mehlhorn]] — who had visited the [[joe-biden|Biden]] [[white-house|White House]] at least ten times — sent a memo to reporters within hours of the shooting urging them to frame it as a false-flag operation modeled on [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s tactics.[1] That memo was later identified as the origin point of the theory that a [[morning-consult|Morning Consult]] poll found 34 percent of Democrats believed, and which critics labeled [[blueanon|BlueAnon]].[1]

## Reported plans to leave the United States (November 2024)

In late November 2024, the New York Times reported — citing three people with knowledge of private conversations, who insisted on anonymity — that Hoffman was weighing a move overseas following [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s victory and Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s defeat.[2] Hoffman, who has spent hundreds of millions of dollars on politics in recent years, was said to be "worried about retribution" from Trump after helping bankroll efforts such as the E. Jean Carroll lawsuit against him.[2] Trump has said he is not seeking retribution or revenge, telling a February 2024 town hall that his "revenge will be success."[2] A spokeswoman for Hoffman declined to respond to the Times.[2]

Breitbart's account also noted [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s October 2024 remarks to Tucker Carlson describing Hoffman as "terrified of a Trump victory," with Musk attributing the fear partly to "the Epstein situation" rather than to ideology alone.[2] Other prominent figures, including [[ellen-degeneres|Ellen DeGeneres]], had already reportedly relocated abroad after the election.[2]

## Withdrawal of Nikki Haley funding (January 2024)

Hoffman was also a donor to [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]]'s 2024 Republican presidential campaign. Following her loss to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the [[2024-new-hampshire-republican-primary|New Hampshire Republican primary]], a person familiar with the matter told CNBC that Hoffman did not plan to give any more money to the campaign.[3] He was among the first of Haley's wealthy donors to head for the exit after she said on January 23, 2024 that she would remain in the nomination contest despite failing to beat Trump in either New Hampshire or the Iowa caucuses; the source declined to be named in order to speak freely.[3]

## Funding of the E. Jean Carroll litigation (2023)

In April 2023 The National Pulse reported that Hoffman — who became a billionaire by founding the business networking website LinkedIn — was bankrolling [[e-jean-carroll|E. Jean Carroll]], the former *Elle* magazine advice columnist who accused [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] of raping her in a New York department store nearly thirty years earlier.[4] Hoffman, then 55, told *New York Magazine*'s Kara Swisher: "The short answer is I will spend as much as I possibly can and it takes and is effective [to beat Trump]."[4]

Carroll had previously denied receiving outside funding for her litigation, but her attorney [[roberta-kaplan|Roberta Kaplan]] conceded to Trump lawyer [[alina-habba|Alina Habba]] that Carroll "remembered just recently that she had been told at some point about outside funding by a nonprofit and that it was irrelevant to her claim anyway."[4] Habba consequently wrote to Judge [[lewis-kaplan|Lewis Kaplan]], who was overseeing the lawsuit, raising significant concerns as to "the plaintiff's bias and motive in commencing the litigation."[4] Habba argued that Hoffman is "one of the largest donors to the Democratic party — reportedly 'one of the most influential Democratic donors of the Trump era' — and a vocal critic of Defendant and his political policies," and noted that since 2017 he had reportedly been "funding groups to create a bulwark against Mr. Trump's agenda."[4]

Habba further noted that Hoffman had previously contributed more than $600,000 to the legal defense fund of Bean LLC — otherwise known as [[fusion-gps|Fusion GPS]], the company responsible for the creation of the [[steele-dossier|Steele Dossier]] — and had been the primary source of funding for an organization that launched an "elaborate false flag" operation spreading misinformation about a Republican senatorial candidate in hopes of costing him his election.[4]

## Consideration of anti-Trump Republican primary spending (2023)

Puck News reported that Hoffman's anti-Trump strategy could involve aiding other GOP primary candidates, including [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]], to the point where Hoffman and his cohorts were investigating state primary rules as well as the idea of supporting anti-Trump, right-leaning media; the subject was discussed at length in a late-March 2023 podcast produced by the Washington-based news site.[4]

## Ties to Jeffrey Epstein

A January 2020 report by Vox on a new [[massachusetts-institute-of-technology|MIT]] review of the institution's dealings with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] identified Hoffman as a Silicon Valley figure who had apologized over Epstein.[4] Hoffman sat on the advisory board of the MIT Media Lab and helped raise money for it, activity that involved fundraising solicitation meetings at which the convicted sex offender was present.[4] "By agreeing to participate in any fundraising activity where Epstein was present, I helped to repair his reputation and perpetuate injustice. For this, I am deeply regretful," Hoffman said.[4]

Hoffman said he had vouched for Epstein because Media Lab director Joi Ito told him Epstein had cleared MIT's vetting process, and in 2015 he brought Epstein to Palo Alto for a dinner with figures including [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]] — by which point Epstein had been a convicted child prostitution offender for seven years.[4] That was the last time Hoffman saw Epstein, yet as late as August 2019 he was still publicly defending Ito's handling of the matter, apologizing only after Ronan Farrow's September story.[4] The MIT report documented additional interactions, including a July 2013 Epstein visit to the MIT campus to meet Hoffman and others, and a July 2016 episode in which Ito sought Hoffman's advice about whether to allow Epstein to attend a conference with "lots of people" who might "see him and maybe know he's involved"; what Hoffman advised was not disclosed.[4]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] remarked on the pattern that "so many former Epstein associates are also anti-Trump," and suggested keeping tabs on Hoffman's donations to both Democratic and Republican candidates in the coming election season.[4]

## Trump call for investigation (November 2025)

On November 18, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly called for Hoffman to be investigated over his ties to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] and his travels to Epstein's Little Saint James island.[5] The remarks came during an Oval Office event with Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], in response to a press question about Epstein and the [[us-house-of-representatives|House]] vote ordering release of the federal [[epstein-files|Epstein files]].[5] Trump initially referred to Hoffman as a "creep fund guy" before recalling his name: "That creep fund guy was with him all the time. What's his name? Reid Hoffman. I don't know Reid Hoffman, but I know he spends a lot of money on the radical left. Reid Hoffman, in my opinion, should be under investigation," adding that "he's a sleaze bag" and telling the questioner to "go back and learn how to be a reporter."[5] The National Pulse, which had long pushed for a federal investigation into Hoffman and his financial network benefiting Democratic candidates, noted that Hoffman had been a key donor and adviser to [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s failed presidential campaign.[5]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] connected the call for investigation to the broader question of political money laundering, citing her long-running work with [[canncon|CannCon]] on [[smurfing|smurfing]] and the [[actblue|ActBlue]] fraud findings, and framing Hoffman as a probable node in that network — "Whose money are they laundering? We might be close to no longer speculating."[5]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that many of "the nation's worst people" had promised to leave the country if Trump were elected, and that DeGeneres appeared to be the only one who had followed through.[2] Describing Hoffman as another reported visitor to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s island who now feared "receiving his just deserts," she argued he had "bankrolled the worst impulses of the left" in ways likely worse than the publicly known lawfare and weaponized-government operations.[2] She closed by asking how extradition would function in a realigned, multipolar world — "Is there actually anywhere to run and hide?"[2]
