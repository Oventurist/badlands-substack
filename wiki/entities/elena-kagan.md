# Elena Kagan

Elena Kagan is an Associate Justice of the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] and a member of the Court's liberal bloc.[1]

## Department of Education dissent (2025)

On July 14, 2025, when the Court granted the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s request to pause a district-court order requiring the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] to reinstate nearly 1,400 fired employees, Kagan joined the 19-page dissent authored by Justice [[sonia-sotomayor|Sonia Sotomayor]] and also joined by Justice [[ketanji-brown-jackson|Ketanji Brown Jackson]].[1] The dissent called the majority's decision "indefensible," warning that it "hands the Executive the power to repeal statutes by firing all those necessary to carry them out" and that "the threat to our Constitution's separation of powers is grave."[1]

## Birthright citizenship argument (May 2025)

In May 2025 Kagan questioned Solicitor General [[d-john-sauer|John Sauer]] on the practicalities of ending [[nationwide-injunctions|universal injunctions]], the central issue in a closely watched case arising from the Trump administration's executive order on [[birthright-citizenship|birthright citizenship]].[2] She pressed Sauer on how the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] would manage the flow of lower-court challenges if universal injunctions ended, and observed that the administration had been "losing uniformly" in lower courts on the substantive question.[2] "If I were in your shoes, there's no way I'd approach the court with this case!" she quipped, adding, "This is not a hypothetical — this is happening out there. Every court is ruling against you."[2] The justices had agreed in April to hear the case, which arose from three lower courts that issued national injunctions blocking [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s executive order.[2] Badlands commentators criticized her reference to "all the law this court has ever made" as an admission that the Court legislates, and contrasted her posture with a 2022 statement, made while [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was president, that "it CAN'T be right that one District Judge can stop a nationwide policy in its tracks!"[2]
