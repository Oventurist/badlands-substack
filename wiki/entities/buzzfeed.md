# BuzzFeed

BuzzFeed is an American digital media company whose news division became, in the Badlands Media account, both a symbol of "fake news" and an early casualty of the collapse of the digital media ecosystem.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## The Steele Dossier

BuzzFeed was the outlet responsible for publishing the Steele Dossier — described by Badlands as "one of the most notorious instances of fake news."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The publication of unverified allegations concerning [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s supposed ties to Russia is treated in the article as emblematic of how entangled the press had become with establishment political interests during and after the 2016 campaign.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Shutdown of BuzzFeed News

In 2023 the company completely abolished its news division.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]], in her February 2024 assessment of the industry, listed BuzzFeed News among the "major media companies" that had "completely shuttered their news operations," part of the hollowing out of the digital media ecosystem in which she and other millennial journalists had built their careers.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Reporting on the 2009 Clinton–Kim memo

"America for Sale — Part 3" relies on BuzzFeed News reporting for its account of [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]]'s 2009 trip to [[north-korea|North Korea]].[2] BuzzFeed reported that a memo attached to a hacked email showed the visit went so well that [[kim-jong-il|Kim Jong Il]] asked Clinton to "return often, officially and unofficially," and invited him back on holiday.[2] The outlet also reported that the memo was attached to an email sent to [[john-podesta|John Podesta]], whose account [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]] was publishing and which researchers believed had been hacked by Russian hackers.[2] The Badlands author treats this mainstream reporting as inadvertent confirmation that the hostage negotiation was a cover story.[2]

## Asset sales amid the digital media contraction (January 2024)

By January 2024 BuzzFeed had lost more than 97% of its value since going public in 2021 and was looking to sell its food properties, Tasty and WeFeast.[3] Tasty had been an attempt by the company to generate revenue streams beyond advertising through direct sales of kitchenware.[3] The Wall Street Journal reported the effort alongside [[fortress-investment-group|Fortress Investment Group]]'s attempt to sell [[vice-media|Vice]]'s [[refinery29|Refinery29]], framing both as evidence that digital media was contracting sharply.[3]

The two former darlings of digital media joined a broader wave of retrenchment that included the shuttering of Jezebel by G/O Media in November 2023, an 8% staff cut at [[business-insider|Business Insider]], 120 layoffs at the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]], and 30 layoffs at [[time-magazine|Time Magazine]].[3]
