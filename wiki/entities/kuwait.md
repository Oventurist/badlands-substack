# Kuwait

Kuwait is a Gulf Arab state that became a target of infrastructure strikes during the 2026 [[iran-war|Iran war]].[1]

## 2026 strike on power and desalination facility

Kuwait's electricity and water ministry said a power and water desalination facility was hit in an Iranian attack in late March 2026, damaging a service building and killing an Indian worker.[1] [[iran|Iran]]'s military denied responsibility and, via the outlet *The Cradle*, characterized the incident as an [[israel|Israeli]] "false flag," a claim that has not been independently verified.[1] The episode is detailed at [[kuwait-desalination-plant-attack|Kuwait desalination plant attack]].[1]

Because Kuwait and its neighbors depend heavily on desalinated water, analysts warned that a cycle of retaliation against Gulf desalination facilities could produce a humanitarian crisis.[1] Commentary in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] suggested that strikes on Kuwait and Saudi Arabia — which Iran denies while claiming others — could be intended to bait the Gulf Arab states into deeper conflict with Tehran.[1]

## Historical origins of the Al-Sabah rule

Badlands commentary on the September 2024 [[gulf-cooperation-council|Gulf Cooperation Council]] meetings in Riyadh sketched Kuwait's dynastic history at length.[2] The Bani Utbah tribe settled in Kuwait in the 1700s, when the territory was "nothing more than a small fishing village"; among the tribe were the Al-Sabah and Al-Kalifa families, who became the rulers of Kuwait and of Bahrain respectively.[2] The Bani Utbah are credited with bringing horses to the Middle East from the Steppe region of [[russia|Russia]], the ancestors of the Arabian stallions now regarded among the world's finest racing horses.[2]

Under the Al-Sabahs, Kuwait became a refuge for those seeking shelter — including Iraqi merchants during the period of the American Revolution, who established a shipbuilding industry that made Kuwait a small maritime power, and the [[house-of-saud|House of Saud]], who sought refuge there in 1890 after the Al Rashid clan burned their village and killed nearly all of their family.[2] It was during this era that the Sheikhdom became a British protectorate, an association that introduced the British to the teenage Abdulaziz bin Saud.[2] The account also holds that Kuwait "has also always been a safe haven for all three of the religions of Abraham."[2] Kuwaiti Crown Prince [[mishal-al-sabah|Mishal Al-Sabah]] attended the Riyadh meetings alongside Saudi Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]].[2]


## The Bush family and the Gulf War

"Profiting From War" presents Kuwait as central to the [[bush-family|Bush family]]'s Middle Eastern dealings.[3] As early as 1964, [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] was labeled by his Senate opponent Ralph Yarborough a hireling of the sheik of Kuwait, for whom his company drilled offshore oil wells.[3] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues that Bush wanted [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]] to invade Kuwait so that evidence of family corruption there would be destroyed and public attention diverted from the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] scandal to the [[gulf-war|Gulf War]].[3] Kuwait was devastated and its people suffered, while reconstruction contracts are said to have enriched firms including [[halliburton]] and the [[carlyle-group|Carlyle Group]].[3]

From 1993 to 2000, [[marvin-bush|Marvin Bush]] was a major shareholder — alongside Mishal Youssef Saud al Sabah of the Kuwaiti royal family — in the [[kuwait-american-corporation|Kuwait-American Corporation]], which held stakes in several U.S. defense, aviation and industrial security companies.[3] In 1993 [[neil-bush|Neil Bush]] travelled to Kuwait with his parents, his brother Marvin and former Secretary of State [[james-baker|James A. Baker III]], staying behind to lobby for contracts.[3]
