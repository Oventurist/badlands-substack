# Yair Lapid

Yair Lapid is an Israeli politician, a former news anchor, and the leader of the opposition to the government of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1] He is described in Badlands coverage as one of the favorites to win the Israeli premiership in the election scheduled for October 2026.[1]

## Position on Iran and Greater Israel (February 2026)

Amid the controversy over remarks by US Ambassador [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]], Lapid declared that he would stand with Netanyahu in a war against [[iran|Iran]] — a stance Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited as evidence that even nominally "center-left" Israeli politicians favor war with Iran and would give Netanyahu the support needed to carry it out.[1] Lapid also went on, speaking in Hebrew, to express sympathy for the [[greater-israel-project|Greater Israel]] concept that Huckabee articulated in his interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]].[1]

Badlands paired Lapid's remarks with those of rival contender [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]], who said "the world will never love Israel, so it should learn to fear it," presenting both as illustrations of an Israeli political spectrum unified around confrontation rather than the peace track pursued by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Opposition to the US-Iran deal (June 2026)

After President Donald Trump announced a framework agreement to end the war with [[iran|Iran]] in June 2026, Lapid said the deal was "terrible for Israel," argued that it erased Israeli military achievements, and called it "the culmination of many years of failure."[2] His objection placed him alongside National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]] and Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] in an Israeli political consensus opposing the agreement, even as the three men remained rivals domestically.[2] Naftali Bennett, described as a Lapid ally, simultaneously warned that Israel faced "an existential moment" and promoted his [[octopus-doctrine|Octopus Doctrine]] for accelerating the collapse of Iran's government.[2]

## Beyahad alliance with Bennett (April 2026)

In April 2026 Lapid and former prime minister [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] announced they would run together in Israel's upcoming national election in a bid to unseat [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], forming a new party called [[beyahad|Beyahad]] — "Together" — under Bennett's leadership.[3] Lapid described Bennett as "a right-wing politician, but an honest one," said the two had built trust, and framed the alliance as unifying what he called the "Repair Bloc."[3]

Bennett said that if elected he would establish a national commission of inquiry into the failures preceding the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack, an inquiry the current government has rejected.[3] The pair previously led a coalition government formed in June 2021 that ended in 2022, after which Netanyahu returned to power.[3] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that during Lapid's 2022 stint as prime minister Netanyahu orchestrated the collapse of their support in the [[knesset|Knesset]] while allying with radical figures who now sit in his Security Cabinet, and argued that this time the actual merger of parties into a single ticket makes a co-premiership arrangement likely should they win in October.[3]

## March 2026 complaints over Iranian strikes

In March 2026, as Iranian missiles struck Tel Aviv without air-raid alarms sounding, Lapid — described in Badlands coverage as an Israeli [[knesset|Knesset]] member — publicly accused the Iranians of targeting civilians.[4] Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] dismissed the complaint as whining, replying that [[israel|Israel]] had chosen to attack [[iran|Iran]] first and unprovoked, and citing a reported early Israeli strike on a school that killed 175 girls under the age of 10, an incident the [[white-house|White House]] and [[pentagon|Pentagon]] were said to have confirmed and to be investigating.[4]

## Opposition to a US-Saudi enrichment deal (2025)

On April 14, 2025, Lapid rejected any possible nuclear deal between the United States and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] that might allow Riyadh to enrich uranium.[5] "Israel must demand from the US, our closest friend, that any nuclear cooperation agreement with Saudi Arabia explicitly prohibits uranium enrichment on Saudi soil," he wrote on X, noting the Israeli government had taken the same position in 2021 and 2022 when he was prime minister.[5] "I expressed to the US administration our firm opposition to any attempt to allow uranium enrichment in the Middle East. This is how it should be done now," he added.[5] The statement followed Saudi state media reports, citing US Energy Secretary [[chris-wright|Chris Wright]], that Washington was edging closer to an energy partnership with Riyadh that could pave the way for commercial nuclear development.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that even the leader of the opposition to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] insisted Saudi Arabia may not have a nuclear energy program, and argued the position was inconsistent with Saudi conduct during the Red Sea shipping crisis, when Riyadh organized food caravans and supply convoys between Abu Dhabi and Tel Aviv after Houthi attacks cut Israeli supply lines.[5] He also called for scrutiny of [[israeli-nuclear-program|Israel's own undeclared nuclear program]].[5]

## April 2025 criticism of Netanyahu over Iran

On Thursday, April 17, 2025, commenting on a report that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had blocked an Israeli-proposed strike on Iran's nuclear facilities, Lapid accused Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] of scuttling opportunities to hit Iran in the past.[6] "Back in October, I proposed attacking Iran's oil fields," Lapid said, but "Netanyahu was afraid, and stopped it."[6]

Badlands commentary framed Lapid's intervention, alongside that of former prime minister [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]], as a political double envelopment — a pincer move against Netanyahu from both the Israeli left and right, with the novel attack vector that Netanyahu was not aggressive enough as a warmonger.[6] Lapid and Bennett had served as co-Prime Ministers for 18 months between June 2021 and December 2022 in an effort to force Netanyahu from power.[6] Lapid was raised by left-wing liberals, and while his party [[yesh-atid|Yesh Atid]] brands itself as centrist and has attracted centrist defectors from [[likud|Likud]], it has also drawn members of the Labor Party.[6]

## Withdrawal from Zionist institution appointments, 2025

In November 2025 Lapid announced that [[yesh-atid|Yesh Atid]] would no longer take part in negotiations over leadership appointments at Israel's large Zionist institutions, decrying a system steeped in "a culture of corruption and political appointments."[7] The move collapsed a long-negotiated agreement that divided posts at the [[world-zionist-organization|World Zionist Organization]] and the [[jewish-national-fund-kkl|Jewish National Fund-KKL]] among the political factions, and in doing so reopened the possibility that [[yair-netanyahu|Yair Netanyahu]], the elder son of Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], would receive a senior executive role at the WZO.[7] Lapid said he would instead advance legislation to nationalize the Jewish National Fund-KKL, arguing that the only way to "clean up this organization and the other national institutions is to bring them under state law, state audit, and the strictest transparency regulations."[7]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] mockingly framed Lapid's criticism as "coming in HOT with the antisemitism," and used the episode to argue that the Zionist national institutions function as a self-serving patronage machine sustained by social and political incentives.[7]
