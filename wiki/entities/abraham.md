# Abraham

Abraham is the patriarch of the monotheistic religion practiced by the ḥanīf, an ancestor of [[hashim-ibn-abd-manaf|Hashim]] and of the Prophet [[muhammad|Muhammad]], and — in Islamic tradition as recounted in the article — the builder of the Kaaba at Mecca.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Conflict with Nimrod

According to the texts of all three major religions as well as Greek and Roman historians, [[nimrod|Nimrod]] — the great-grandson of Noah — was a wicked king of ancient Babylon who claimed to be God, promoted idolatry in the form of polytheism and blood magic, and built the Tower of Babel.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) The Quran records that Nimrod set himself against the will of God.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Told by his astrologers that a baby descended from Noah would be born and end idolatry, Nimrod ordered the slaughter of all newborns in Mesopotamia; that baby was Abraham, whose mother escaped into the wilderness, where he was born.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

Abraham eventually confronted Nimrod to end idolatry, and Nimrod ordered him burned at the stake — fire being central to Nimrod's godless worship.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Witnesses saw Abraham emerge from the fire unscathed and began to believe he was a prophet of God.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Fearing for his rule, Nimrod challenged Abraham to a public debate, expecting to disprove monotheism.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Asked what his God could do that Nimrod could not, Abraham answered that God gives life and death; Nimrod had two prisoners brought out, executed one and pardoned the other, and claimed the same power.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Abraham replied, "Well, my lord God makes the sun rise from the East. Can you make it rise from the West?"[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Confounded and humiliated, Nimrod banished Abraham from his Mesopotamian kingdom.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Mecca and the Kaaba

Abraham is believed to have settled in Mecca, constructing a stone sanctuary on the same foundation where his ancestor Adam had built one to worship the one God.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) That structure is the Kaaba, which Islam considers mankind's first House of Worship, and which Abraham built with his son Ishmael.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Muslims read Abraham's conflict with Nimrod as the divine triumph of logic and reason over self-indulgent narcissism, and as the quintessential contest of monotheism versus polytheism — Good versus Evil, a recurring theme in Islamic history and tradition.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)

## Hospitality

All three major religions record that Abraham and his wife Sarah, childless, were visited at age 86 by three angels who told them they would bear a son.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Before hearing their news, Abraham received them with warm hospitality, washing their feet and feeding them his best bread, milk, and finest calf — paying forward the kindness he had received from strangers while traveling after his exile by Nimrod.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights) Hashim and all Islamic scholars since have held this behavior up as exemplary.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/arabian-nights)
