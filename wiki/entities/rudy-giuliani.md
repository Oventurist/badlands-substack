# Rudy Giuliani

Rudy Giuliani, the former mayor of New York City frequently referred to as "America's Mayor," is a longtime ally of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and a recurring voice in American foreign-policy debate.[1]

## Anti-regime-change statement (January 2026)

In early January 2026, amid renewed argument over US involvement in Venezuela and Iran, Giuliani posted what Badlands described as an anti-regime-change rant, calling the son of the former Shah of Iran "a bum" and denouncing attempts to overthrow the Iranian government.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] highlighted the post approvingly, contrasting it with the interventionist rhetoric of figures such as Senators [[tom-cotton|Tom Cotton]] and [[rick-scott|Rick Scott]], who had spoken of "fixing" Cuba, Nicaragua and Colombia after the Venezuela operation.[1]

## Interstate 93 crash and Medal of Freedom (September 2025)

Over the weekend of August 30–31, 2025, Giuliani and his driver, Theodore Goodman, were traveling on Interstate 93 in New Hampshire when they were flagged down by a woman who told them she had been involved in a domestic violence incident.[2] Goodman called New Hampshire State Police and remained at the scene until troopers arrived; while merging back onto I-93, their vehicle was struck from behind.[2] The former mayor was taken to hospital and treated for a serious back injury, and was released on Monday, September 1, 2025.[2] State police said the driver who hit Giuliani's car had no connection to the initial domestic violence report, and said dashboard and body-worn camera video was being reviewed as part of the ongoing investigation.[2]

On the same day, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced on Truth Social that he would award Giuliani the [[presidential-medal-of-freedom|Presidential Medal of Freedom]], calling him "the greatest Mayor in the history of New York City, and an equally great American Patriot," with details of a date and location to follow.[2]

Badlands commentator Ashe in America noted that Giuliani had been disbarred in Washington, D.C. on September 26, 2024, a couple of months after being disbarred in New York, and argued he was punished for being an "election denier" and deserved restoration as well as the Medal of Freedom.[2] She described him as "America's Mayor" during the September 11 attacks, crediting him with guiding New York City through its darkest hours with strength, compassion and resilience, giving regular updates on World Trade Center rescues at a time when institutions urged fear.[2] She added that he had earlier stood up to organized crime and later stood up for free and fair elections, and recommended the Badlands documentary [[codex-911|Codex 9/11]].[2]


## Disbarment in Washington, D.C. (2024)

On September 26, 2024, a three-judge panel of the District of Columbia Court of Appeals ordered Giuliani's disbarment following a disciplinary board's criticisms of his response to the 2020 presidential election, an outcome documented in [[disbarment-of-rudy-giuliani-in-washington-dc|the D.C. disbarment proceeding]].[3] The order cited his earlier disbarment in New York, where an appeals court had accused him of making "demonstrably false and misleading statements to the courts, lawmakers, and the public at large."[3] The D.C. Bar's Board on Professional Responsibility had concluded in June 2023 that Giuliani "claimed massive election fraud but had no evidence of it," and that although it weighed his conduct after the September 11 attacks, his service in the Justice Department, and his tenure as Mayor of New York City, "all of that happened long ago."[3]

## Commentary on the Adams indictment

Days later, Giuliani appeared on Newsline urging the public not to convict Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] before trial, in what Badlands writer Burning Bright characterized as a "narrative deployment" that placed the spotlight firmly on the [[southern-district-of-new-york|Southern District of New York]] and emphasized past over present.[3] Whatever the truth regarding Adams, Burning Bright argued, Giuliani "knows where the bodies are buried in the SDNY better than anyone" — and, more importantly, knows who buried them, among district attorneys, commissioners, mayors, and governors.[3]

## New York disbarment (July 2024)

On Tuesday, July 2, 2024, Giuliani lost his license to practice law in New York, his home state, when a state appeals court ordered his immediate disbarment and directed that "his name stricken from the roll of attorneys and counselors-at-law."[4] The ruling deepened the fall from grace of the 80-year-old former New York City mayor and mob prosecutor, once hailed as "America's Mayor" after the September 11 attacks.[4] Giuliani's New York law license had already been suspended in 2021, and the court found that he had repeatedly made false claims about the 2020 presidential election while representing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his campaign.[4]

Badlands writer Ashe in America placed the disbarment in the context of the Supreme Court's [[presidential-immunity|presidential immunity]] ruling issued a day earlier, arguing that if Trump were immune from prosecution for official acts, "the bloodthirst of the regime will come for his team."[4] The commentary grouped Giuliani with John Eastman, Jeffrey Clark "and countless others in the club of collateral damage," predicting that the asymmetrical, politically motivated application of law being established as precedent would ultimately boomerang.[4]

## Georgia election workers defamation verdict (December 2023)

On Friday, December 15, 2023, a federal jury ordered Giuliani to pay more than $148 million to two Georgia election workers for falsely claiming they had committed ballot fraud in the 2020 presidential election.[5] The award comprised $75 million in punitive damages, $20 million to each plaintiff for emotional distress, and more than $16 million each for defamation.[5]

Giuliani was in court as a federal judge read the verdict aloud; after adjournment the plaintiffs embraced while Giuliani, standing alone, packed a bag without looking up, according to NBC News.[5] Outside the courthouse he called the damages figure "absurd" and vowed to appeal.[5] The judgment was the latest in a series of legal blows tied to his service as top campaign lawyer for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in efforts to reverse the 2020 result.[5]

## 2025 New Hampshire car accident

On the evening of August 30, 2025, Giuliani, then 81, was severely injured in a motor vehicle accident in New Hampshire.[6] According to [[michael-ragusa|Michael Ragusa]], the head of his security detail, Giuliani had been flagged down shortly before the crash by a woman who was the victim of a domestic violence incident; he rendered assistance, contacted 911, and remained on scene until responding officers arrived to ensure her safety.[6] While subsequently traveling on the highway, Giuliani's vehicle was struck from behind at high speed.[6] He was taken to a nearby trauma center and diagnosed with a fractured thoracic vertebrae, multiple lacerations and contusions, and injuries to his left arm and lower leg.[6] Badlands host [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] observed that while he did not know whether the crash was a targeted attack, the framing of the statements around it suggested those involved were conscious of that possibility.[6]

## Hunter Biden laptop lawsuit (2023)

On 26 September 2023, [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] sued Giuliani in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California, alleging that Giuliani had violated his privacy rights by illegally disseminating data taken from his laptop.[7] The complaint calls Giuliani "primarily responsible" for the "total annihilation" of Biden's digital privacy and names his former attorney [[robert-costello|Robert Costello]] as a co-defendant.[7] Biden's lawyers characterised the defendants' conduct as hacking, tampering with and disseminating data taken from his devices, and disputed that the material came from a "laptop" at all, describing it instead as an "external drive."[7] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the suit represented a shifting of the goalposts, in that Hunter Biden was now conceding the data existed while contesting how it was obtained.[7] See [[hunter-biden-v-giuliani|Hunter Biden v. Giuliani]].[7]
