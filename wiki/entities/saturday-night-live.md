# Saturday Night Live

Saturday Night Live (SNL) is the long-running late-night sketch comedy program produced at 30 Rockefeller Plaza in New York and broadcast by NBC, a unit of [[nbcuniversal|NBCUniversal]].[1]

## 2024 election coverage

SNL opened its 2024–2025 season with sketches that repeatedly targeted Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], whose character has famously been played by [[maya-rudolph|Maya Rudolph]].[1] In the mid-October 2024 episode, the show mocked Harris in a Family Feud–style sketch that played on her trademark mannerisms, including her widely memed "word salad" delivery, her alleged Glock, [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] as a knucklehead white guy, and [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s decline; the punchline for the Trump campaign side was that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has dementia.[1]

Social media reaction treated the mockery as a bad sign for the Democratic ticket, with commentators declaring "It's over. The Democrats have lost SNL."[1] [[robby-starbuck|Robby Starbuck]] wrote on X that "when SNL is doing skits like this, you know that they think Kamala's cooked," adding that he saw "zero on the ground excitement for her."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that none of the week's sketches were genuinely funny, describing the writing as safe, narrative-reinforcing, and far from pushing the comedic envelope, and speculated that the strategy was to be playful with Harris criticisms while still portraying her as preferable to Trump.[1] She emphasized that "SNL is NBC," rejecting the framing by "Con Inc" that the show's shift represented independent creative judgment, and noted a third consecutive week of swipes at Harris.[1] She also criticized the season opener's Ariana Grande sketch, in which Grande played Celine Dion mocking the UFC.[1]

## Epstein, Diddy, and the SDNY frame

Ashe in America placed the show — described as a New York institution — in the context of the explosive legal landscape in the Southern District of New York, asking whether SNL was "signal" and what overlap existed between [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], [[sean-combs|Diddy]], and Saturday Night Live.[1] She noted that the prior week's "Weekend Update" tied all three together and that she found parts of it genuinely funny.[1]

Researching Diddy's SNL appearances, she surfaced a 2006 sketch titled "Diddy Kiddies," described as three multi-ethnic middle-schoolers "solving mysteries, doing the bad thing, they out there going for the brass ring," whose central mystery was what exactly Diddy does for a living.[1] The video had been wiped and the Internet Archive was down at the time of writing, prompting a public request for copies.[1] The commentary closed by asking who is calling the shots at 30 Rock.[1]
