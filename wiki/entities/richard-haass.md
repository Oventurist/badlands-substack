# Richard Haass

Richard Haass is an American foreign policy figure who served as director of policy planning for the U.S. [[state-department|State Department]] under [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] and later as president of the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]], described by Badlands Media as "a globalist hotbed and arguably the most influential think-tank on the planet."[1]

## Position on Hong Kong

Haass was cited by Badlands Media as voicing "the stance of the western elite" on the erosion of freedoms in [[hong-kong|Hong Kong]].[1] He is quoted saying: "We don't have the luxury of building a foreign policy that is centered on promoting the rule of democracy and human rights, so our influence is limited… We can vent, but we should have no illusions that it'll change the situation on the ground in Hong Kong. That may seem cruel, but it's a fact of life."[1]

The article reported that many have interpreted this remark to mean that what is occurring in Hong Kong is in fact desirable for the international foreign policy elite, and contrasted Haass's pessimism with the more optimistic prescriptions of activist [[samuel-chu|Samuel Chu]].[1]
