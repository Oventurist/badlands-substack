# Kamala Harris

Kamala Harris was the Democratic presidential candidate whose [[60-minutes|60 Minutes]] interview became the basis of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s $10 billion lawsuit against [[cbs|CBS]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

During the campaign, it was quickly discovered that the interview had been edited to make Harris look better, with the producers removing as much unintelligible word-salad as they could.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) Trump viewed the edit as election tampering.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] uses the episode to argue that the resulting litigation gave Trump leverage over [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]] during its merger with [[skydance-media|Skydance Media]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## Succession to Biden, 2024

Harris's elevation follows [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s withdrawal from the 2024 race, which Badlands Media commentary treats as a decision imposed on Biden rather than taken by him.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] asks which [[deep-state-cabal|Cabal]] families back Harris and which might back a different candidate, and whether the Deep State would succeed in creating unity within what he calls the cartel or whether the power struggle would continue.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## As a source of division and fear

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] included Harris's elevation in his catalogue of mid-2024 events designed to distract and divide the public.[3] He asserted that most Americans do not find Harris likeable or competent, and treated this perception as itself a generator of division and anger.[3] He further argued that Harris introduced fear into the equation: Americans already knew how bad things would be under [[joe-biden|Biden]], but Harris was a new unknown, and unknown situations are the scariest.[3] Being distracted by what he called the unlikability and incompetence of Harris was, in his framing, one of several diversions from the restructuring of the financial system.[3]

## 2028 Democratic power rankings

In July 2026 Axios' Democratic "power rankings" placed Harris atop the party's early 2028 presidential field alongside California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] and Representative [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]].[4] The rankings drew on interviews with Democratic strategists, donors, elected officials, and party insiders, weighing political influence, fundraising strength, and national profile, and reflected early positioning rather than declared candidacies in a field Axios described as fluid.[4] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the list as evidence that the party had no new talent, dismissing the three front-runners as punchlines rather than serious contenders.[4]

## Renewed donor outreach (July 2026)

Reports in July 2026 indicated that Harris had renewed outreach to Democratic donors and allies as she weighed her next political moves amid the party's evolving leadership landscape.[5] The reporting coincided with turmoil among [[house-of-representatives|House Democrats]], who were reassessing their political strategy after Rep. [[diana-degette|Diana DeGette]] of Colorado lost her primary to [[justice-democrats|Justice Democrats]]-backed challenger [[melat-kiros|Melat Kiros]].[5]

## 2024 campaign: taxes and veterans

Harris backed the plan to eliminate taxes on tips that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had proposed earlier in the 2024 campaign, a copy Badlands treated as evidence that Trump's tax strategy was effective; the open question posed by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] was whether she would also adopt his [[no-tax-on-overtime|no-tax-on-overtime]] pledge.[6]

She was also implicated by association in the September 2024 controversy over [[national-security-council|National Security Council]] communications adviser [[john-kirby|John Kirby]]'s errant email dismissing veterans critical of the [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] withdrawal as "a 'handful' of vets indeed and all of one stripe."[6] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that because Harris was expected to retain much of the same team, "Kirby insulting veterans is effectively Kamala insulting veterans," and urged Republicans to brand her with it.[6] Polling at the time showed Harris and Trump in a close race before a bitterly divided electorate, a tension cited in the decision to treat the January 6, 2025 vote certification as a [[national-special-security-event|national special security event]].[6]

## October 2024 closing-weeks difficulties

In mid-October 2024, Harris made [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "enemy from within" remarks a centerpiece of a campaign rally, arguing that his rhetoric about using the military against domestic opponents was dangerous.[7] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized her Pennsylvania delivery of the theme as shrill and deteriorating, and argued that those who had for years described Trump and his supporters as a threat to democracy were simultaneously insisting that domestic enemies are a conspiracy theory.[7]

On Wednesday, October 16, 2024, Harris appeared in a [[fox-news|Fox News]] interview with [[bret-baier|Bret Baier]] in which, according to ZeroHedge's account, she alternated between deflection and blame before reacting angrily to a clip of Trump.[7] Asked whether she would take responsibility for the surge in illegal immigration after she and [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] rescinded Trump's immigration executive orders on their first day in office, Harris returned to the argument that Trump had killed a bipartisan border bill; Baier pushed back, and Fox correspondent Bill Melugin noted that the Democratic bill "would have given pathway to amnesty to millions of illegal immigrants and it failed despite Democrats controlling the White House & Congress."[7]

Her Pennsylvania operation also drew internal criticism. Top state Democrats said the campaign was poorly run in the largest battleground state, faulting aides for lacking relationships with Philadelphia-area party figures, for ineffective surrogate deployment, and for insufficient turnout work among voters of color; several named state campaign manager [[nikki-lu|Nikki Lu]] specifically.[7]

## Loss of security protection (2025)

Harris lost Los Angeles Police Department protection in September 2025 after the arrangement drew criticism from officers who had been pulled from crime prevention duties to staff it.[8] The move followed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s cancellation of her Secret Service protection the previous month, consistent with the standard practice of ending vice-presidential Secret Service coverage six months after leaving office; Harris's detail had been extended for an additional year by a directive from then-President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], which Trump rescinded.[8] Officers from the [[lapd|LAPD]]'s Metropolitan Division had been assisting the California Highway Patrol in protecting Harris after the federal detail ended, in a roughly twelve-officer arrangement funded by the city of Los Angeles.[8] The Los Angeles Police Protective League suggested that Harris pay for her own private security, as other influential people without mandated government protection do, or that Governor Gavin Newsom cover the cost.[8]

## 2024 presidential campaign and the Walz pick

Harris became the presumptive Democratic presidential nominee after [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] stepped out of the 2024 race, a withdrawal that followed weeks of mounting calls from Democrats — including [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] — citing concerns over his age, mental fitness, and ability to recapture the White House.[9] In early August 2024 she selected Minnesota Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] as her running mate, a decision former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly welcomed as a "shocking pick," saying, "I could not be more thrilled."[9] Trump described Walz as a "smarter version" of Harris and comparable to [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]], and said Pennsylvania Governor [[josh-shapiro|Josh Shapiro]] would have been the stronger choice.[9]

On their first full day of joint campaigning, Harris and Walz travelled through Wisconsin and Michigan, courting the younger, diverse, labor-friendly voters instrumental to Biden's 2020 win.[9] At a rally in Eau Claire she declared, "As Tim Walz likes to point out, we are joyful warriors," contrasting the ticket's optimism with what she portrayed as Trump's backward-looking and confrontational politics.[9] The campaign said it raised $36 million in the first 24 hours after the announcement, and the pair narrowly avoided a tarmac confrontation with Republican vice-presidential nominee [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] in Wisconsin.[9]

Badlands commentary argued the pairing was a liability, noting that Harris had been rated the most radical senator during her Senate tenure and that adding a running mate credibly compared to Sanders risked alienating middle-of-the-road voters.[9]


## Biden Retrospective on 2024 (January 2025)

In January 2025 President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] said he believed both he and Vice President Kamala Harris could have defeated [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], telling reporters, "I think I would have beaten Trump, could have beaten Trump, and I think that Kamala could have beaten Trump."[10] Harris had been the Democratic nominee in 2024 and lost to Trump, who repeatedly called for the prosecution of his perceived enemies.[10]

## No tax on tips and the 2024 campaign (August 2024)

In August 2024, Harris embraced eliminating federal taxes on tips for service and hospitality workers, roughly two months after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] first proposed the idea in June, with both campaigns targeting Nevada and its large tipped workforce.[11] Economists across the political spectrum warned the policy could worsen the federal deficit, erode Social Security and Medicare revenue, and create a loophole for high earners.[11] Trump, returning to [[x-corp|X]] the same month, attacked her in campaign videos as "San Francisco Radical Kamala Harris," while his return came amid Harris' rise in the polls.[11] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] mocked reports that Harris rallies were invitation-only, calling it a telling example of "modern democracy."[11] See [[tax-free-tips|tax-free tips]].[11]

## September 2024 debate challenge and the uncommitted vote

On Saturday, September 21, 2024, Harris accepted an invitation from [[cnn]] to debate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on October 23, with campaign chair Jen O'Malley Dillon saying she was "ready for another opportunity to share a stage with Donald Trump" and that Trump "should have no problem agreeing to this debate."[12] Harris posted that she "will gladly accept a second presidential debate" and hoped Trump would join her; Trump countered at a rally in Wilmington, North Carolina, that it was "too late" because voting had already started.[12]

The same weekend, Hamtramck Mayor [[amer-ghalib|Amer Ghalib]] — a leader of the [[uncommitted-movement|uncommitted movement]] — described a "very productive" meeting with Trump and weighed an endorsement, which Badlands treated as another blow to Harris.[12] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that ending Israel's war in Gaza was the paramount issue for uncommitted voters and that the war had commenced and escalated on Harris's watch, making a drift of those voters toward Trump logical.[12]

## September 2024 campaign context

By late September 2024 polls showed Harris in a close race with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], a dynamic cited when Trump urged Pennsylvania supporters to vote early even while calling the practice "stupid."[13] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing on Trump's tariff threat against [[john-deere|John Deere]], referred to "Harris' inflation recipe" as having already pushed the company close to the price thresholds of its farming customer base.[13]

## Succession speculation, July 2024

As pressure mounted on [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to leave the 2024 race in July 2024, Axios reported a movement among some Democratic officials and operatives to bypass Harris as his successor, or at least to make the nomination a contest rather than a coronation, floating [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]], [[josh-shapiro|Josh Shapiro]], and [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]].[14] The outlet listed five reasons that effort was unlikely to succeed: the Biden campaign's $91 million war chest could be transferred to Harris but to no other candidate; as a Black and Asian American woman she could become the first woman president; party heavyweights including [[jim-clyburn|Jim Clyburn]] had said they would back her; senior Democrats feared a multi-candidate fight a month before the convention; and the Biden–Harris ticket's 14 million primary votes conferred legitimacy.[14]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized Harris as "the least popular politician of the last three election cycles" and described the party as desperately attempting to crown her while Biden was "being hunted by his own in full view of the normie hivemind."[14]

## Puerto Rico protest incident (March 2024)

During a March 2024 visit to [[puerto-rico|Puerto Rico]], Harris was filmed clapping along to a song performed by musicians at the Goyoco community center in the Santurce neighborhood of San Juan, before an aide informed her that the musicians were in fact protesting her.[15] Her motorcade had already been confronted by dozens of loud demonstrators, one of whom held a sign reading "Kamala Harris war criminal."[15] The lyrics asked, "We want to know, Kamala, what did you come here for?... Long live Free Palestine and Haiti too!", and the performers also demanded, "We want to know what you think of the colony."[15] Badlands commentary called the episode a national embarrassment, listing it alongside her border remarks, her description of artificial intelligence, and her widely mocked word salads and laugh, and cited Australian commentary that she "hasn't done women any favors in that role."[15]

## Securing the 2024 nomination

On Friday, August 2, 2024, [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]] chair [[jaime-harrison|Jaime Harrison]] announced that Harris had secured enough delegate votes to become the party's presidential nominee, ahead of the close of online voting the following Monday.[16] The pace reflected a campaign eager to preserve momentum after President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] ended his reelection bid and endorsed her less than two weeks earlier.[16] Harris, poised to be the first woman of color atop a major party ticket, told supporters she was "honored to be the presumptive Democratic nominee" and added, "It's not going to be easy. But we're going to get this done. As your future president, I know we are up to this fight."[16]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that to "save democracy," Democrats had redefined it, listing four features of the process: that Harris got the nod largely because she was the only candidate who could retain Biden's campaign funds; that no candidate challenged her nomination; that the party claimed unanimity while ignoring contrary evidence; and that Democrats were gaslighting the nation about her popularity.[16] The planned state-by-state roll call was described by party officials as "purely ceremonial," which the commentary read as an admission that the outcome had already been decided.[16]

## September 2024 debate agreement

Later that week [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced he had agreed with [[fox-news|Fox News]] to debate Harris on September 4, 2024 in Pennsylvania, moderated by [[bret-baier|Bret Baier]] and [[martha-maccallum|Martha MacCallum]], with a full arena audience.[16] Trump said the Democrats had "Unconstitutionally taken a Candidate, who was acknowledged to be defeated, and unceremoniously replaced him," calling it a threat to democracy while saying he was prepared to accept the results of the "coup" and face "Crazy Kamala Harris" on stage.[16] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized her campaign as taking every bit of bait Trump laid out, citing the resurfaced birth-certificate controversy that pulled [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] back into the news cycle and Trump's remarks on Harris's racial identity.[16]

Ashe in America separately tied Harris's platform to what she called a project to end private automobile ownership — pointing to ride-share promotion, the normalization of electric-vehicle limits, and municipal measures such as the [[denver-city-council|Denver City Council]]'s gas-station spacing bill — and argued that the regime's agenda would not change regardless of who headed the ticket.[16]

## Retrospective on the 2024 nomination

In December 2024 Democratic mega-donor [[john-morgan|John Morgan]] wondered aloud whether [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had deliberately forced Harris onto the ticket — endorsing her within minutes of pulling out of the race — to spite [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] and [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], both of whom had worked to push Biden out and both of whom held serious reservations about Harris's capabilities.[17] Morgan noted that Obama did not endorse Harris for five days.[17] A Harris campaign adviser told the Washington Post that "it's almost Shakespearean that Biden didn't just kill his own campaign, he killed hers, too."[17] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that he liked the idea that Harris had also been on the outside of the operation, believing she would actually be allowed to become president.[17]

## Late October 2024 campaign developments
In the closing weeks of the 2024 campaign, Harris was reported to be a liability for Senate Democrats in the "blue wall" states critical to the election's outcome, with candidates such as Pennsylvania's Bob Casey distancing themselves from her and the Biden administration and, in Casey's case, embracing Donald Trump's tariff policies.[18]

Her campaign was also drawn into the leak of internal documents from the Center for Countering Digital Hate, whose founder Morgan McSweeney — a British political operative — was reported to be advising the Harris campaign while his organization planned in writing to "kill Musk's Twitter" and to strengthen ties with the Biden/Harris administration.[18]

Harris additionally featured in coverage of the Israel–Iran intelligence leak, where Badlands writer Burning Bright described her as positioned in the middle of the "Narrative Kobayashi Maru" of the Middle East conflict, and argued that the campaign's visible panic stemmed from the prospective exposure of U.S. Deep State connections to Iranian networks rather than from the horse race itself.[18] Like Trump, Harris made numerous podcast stops during the campaign, including an appearance on "Call Her Daddy" to discuss women's issues.[18]

## Post-defeat marriage reporting (January 2025)

After Trump was sworn into office in January 2025, the Daily Mail reported that Harris — who had been publicly affectionate toward her "beloved" second gentleman [[doug-emhoff|Doug Emhoff]] in the immediate aftermath of her election defeat — had turned to assigning blame, with Emhoff characterized as "dead weight" whose scandals were faulted for the loss.[19] A source told DailyMail.com that "there's plenty of blame to go around as far as Kamala is concerned and Doug has his share."[19] The report tied the shift to Harris weighing a 2026 run for Governor of California or another presidential bid, and to whether Emhoff was an asset or a liability to those plans.[19] Badlands commentator [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] called the coverage "a total rewriting of history, brought to you by fake elections."[19]

## Republican national security endorsement (September 2024)

On September 18, 2024, more than 100 Republican former national security officials and ex-members of Congress signed an open letter endorsing Harris for president, citing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "demonstrated chaotic and unethical behavior" and stating that "she possesses the essential qualities to serve as President and Donald Trump does not."[20] The signatories praised Harris for having "consistently championed the rule of law, democracy, and our constitutional principles," for agreeing to sign a bipartisan border security bill, and for expressing support for U.S. allies while condemning adversaries.[20] See [[2024-republican-national-security-endorsement-of-harris|2024 Republican National Security Endorsement of Kamala Harris]].

## September 2024 polling and Hogan remarks

By early September 2024 Harris had closed the polling gap with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and even surpassed him nationally and in some battleground states, leading nationally by nearly two points and leading slightly or tying Trump in all seven battlegrounds after replacing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on the ticket on July 21.[21] Republican Party analysts told the Washington Times that polling methodology gave Harris a "phantom advantage" because many polls sampled far fewer Republicans than the share of Republican voters who participated in the 2020 election according to exit polls; critics argued the oversampling of Democrats might be deliberate, intended to generate enthusiasm and boost fundraising.[21] The Harris campaign announced it had raised $540 million in July, more than four times what Trump raised in the same period.[21]

Separately, wrestling figure [[hulk-hogan|Hulk Hogan]] joked at a promotional event for his new beer about body slamming Harris, asking a jeering crowd "Do you want me to body slam Kamala Harris?!" and "Do you want me to drop the leg on Kamala?!", and made comments questioning her racial identity and her Indian heritage — remarks that came amid calls to curb violent political rhetoric after the assassination attempt on Trump.[21]

## "Vice-president Trump" gaffe (July 2024)

At his press conference closing the [[2024-nato-washington-summit|NATO Washington Summit]] on July 11, 2024, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] mistakenly referred to Harris as "vice-president Trump" while expressing confidence in her, a slip that came in his first unscripted answer and recurred at the end of the session.[22] The gaffe landed amid mounting calls for Biden to step aside in favor of another candidate, including Harris herself, following his faltering debate performance against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[22] Biden insisted he would remain in the race.[22]

## October 2024 "trifecta of crises"

In early October 2024, a compounding series of challenges converged on the [[white-house|White House]] with Election Day roughly a month away: the [[2024-east-and-gulf-coast-dockworker-strike|dockworker strike]], escalating fighting in the Middle East, and the devastation of [[hurricane-helene|Hurricane Helene]] across the Southeastern United States.[23] Coverage framed the events as putting Harris to the test as she spent the final weeks of her campaign trying to convince voters that the current administration should not be punished at the polls in November.[23] At the time she and former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were nearly tied nationally and across the pivotal swing states, with Decision Desk HQ/The Hill's poll aggregation showing Harris ahead by 4.6 percentage points nationally.[23]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued, following an observation by editor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], that the near-simultaneous appearance of matching headlines across legacy outlets reflected "four am talking points" originating with the Harris campaign rather than independent reporting.[23] She noted that although the framing appeared on its surface to throw shade on Harris, it functioned as a campaign contribution: The Hill quoted strategists calling her handling of the three crises "a critical opportunity for voters to see her at work," and a former Obama campaign manager saying "there's no better argument for her capability than proving she's already handling the toughest challenges of leadership under intense scrutiny."[23] Business Insider quoted another Obama-era figure, Peter Loge, advising that the Biden-Harris administration "project calm competence" while the Trump campaign promoted Trump as the candidate of strength.[23] Ashe concluded that Team Obama was framing domestic policy, foreign policy and crisis management for readers, and questioned where new support for Harris could be coming from given her losses across key demographics.[23]

## Iranian hack disclosure and the Teamsters snub

In September 2024 the [[fbi|FBI]] revealed that Iranian hackers had passed stolen [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaign material to what was then the Biden–Harris campaign in June and July, and the Harris campaign responded by issuing a statement describing itself as a "victim" of the hack.[24] Badlands commentary argued that because [[russia|Russia]] and [[iran|Iran]] had just formalized their [[comprehensive-strategic-partnership-treaty|Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Treaty]], the alleged Iran–Harris connection could be recast as a Russia–Harris connection, inverting the Russiagate narrative against her.[24]

The same week, the [[teamsters|Teamsters]] declined to endorse any presidential candidate for the first time in nearly three decades after internal polling showed roughly 60% of members backing Trump against 34% for Harris.[24] Harris had reportedly met with the union that Monday, and Badlands writers described the abstention as a devastating blow to a campaign that had been positioning itself as a labor movement.[24] Harris also welcomed the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]]'s 50 basis-point rate cut, calling it "welcome news for Americans," and was scheduled to meet Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] in Washington the following week.[24]


## Emergence as presumptive nominee, July 2024

After President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] ended his reelection bid on Sunday, July 21, 2024, Harris was thrust into what the Associated Press called "the most scrutinizing of spotlights," becoming the leading candidate to succeed him as the presumptive Democratic presidential nominee and her party's main hope of defeating [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[25] Democrats promised an "orderly process" to replace Biden on the ticket, and Harris vowed to "earn and win" the nomination.[25] Reporting emphasized that none of the potential replacements, Harris included, could match Biden's five decades of personal relationships with foreign leaders at a moment of wars in Ukraine and Gaza, a more assertive China and a rising European far right.[25]

## Battleground standing and immigration pivot (October 2024)

In early October 2024 Democrats leaned on [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] as a closing surrogate for Harris, with the former president due to stump for her in Pennsylvania and to cut ads in key Senate and House races.[26] Badlands rejected the framing of a tight contest, arguing the race could not be close absent any demographic in which Harris was gaining, while [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was reportedly picking up Black, Latino, male, and union voters at outcome-determinative levels.[26]

While mainstream polling showed Trump and Harris essentially tied across seven battleground states, betting markets showed a pronounced late surge for Trump: according to [[polymarket|Polymarket]], he began climbing in Michigan late in the prior week and picked up a Monday bump in Arizona and Pennsylvania.[26] The Hill reported that Harris's lack of traction with unionized blue-collar workers had emerged as one of her biggest challenges in states such as Michigan and Pennsylvania, with progressive activist Bob Borosage noting that Trump's tariffs and immigration message appealed to workers who feel their jobs went abroad.[26]

On immigration, Axios reported that although Trump might lose the election, Harris was largely conceding he had won the argument on the border, with both candidates pledging some of the most restrictive immigration, asylum, and border policies in decades.[26] Harris shifted from presenting herself as an advocate for the undocumented to touting her record as a former border-state prosecutor, pledging to curtail asylum eligibility, pursue felony charges for illegal border crossings, and continue building a border wall.[26] "I will protect our nation's sovereignty, secure our border and work to fix our broken system of immigration," she told a crowd in Nevada, while a campaign ad declared "we need a leader with a real plan to fix the border."[26] Some immigration advocates on the left argued the election-year embrace of harsh rules weakened her leverage to push for pathways to citizenship, and some Democrats were frustrated that she and the Biden administration had adopted asylum restrictions resembling Trump policies they once opposed.[26]

## Post-elevation polling (late July 2024)

Harris's elevation to the top of the Democratic ticket after [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s July 21, 2024 withdrawal jolted the presidential race and blunted the momentum [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] might otherwise have carried out of the [[2024-republican-national-convention|Republican convention]] and the assassination attempt that preceded it, according to [[politico|Politico]]'s survey of fresh polling.[27] Trump's post-debate advantage over Biden largely evaporated against Harris: new surveys from the [[new-york-times|New York Times]]/Siena College (Trump +1), the [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]] (Trump +2) and [[cnn|CNN]] (Trump +3) all represented tightening from six-point Trump leads in the same three polls after the debate.[27] Politico noted it was difficult to separate any post-assassination-attempt shift in views of Trump from a convention bounce or from his selection of Senator [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] as running mate, but that crosstabs showed Harris performing more strongly with traditionally Democratic groups among whom Biden had badly lagged.[27]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed the coverage as "a donation in kind" — an unlabeled advertorial for the Harris campaign — observing that even Politico's own numbers still showed Trump ahead, which she took to mean he was well ahead.[27] She characterized Harris as unlikable and lacking meaningful accomplishments, argued that the post-announcement bump still left her losing, and predicted voters would reject her the more they saw of her.[27] She contrasted the media's earlier insistence that Biden was a strong and competent statesman with the same outlets' report that more than three in four likely voters in the New York Times/Siena poll were enthusiastic or satisfied that Biden had dropped out — 78 percent approval for the withdrawal in [[fox-news|Fox News]]'s Pennsylvania poll — and quoted Orwell's line that "the Party told you to reject the evidence of your eyes and ears."[27]

## Closing weeks of the 2024 campaign

In the final two weeks before Election Day 2024, Harris appeared with former Wyoming Representative [[liz-cheney|Liz Cheney]] in the Milwaukee suburbs at an event explicitly aimed at moderates and onetime Republicans, but Politico judged the pair to be preaching to the converted, with rhetoric better suited to an audience wearing Lincoln Project t-shirts.[28] The two criticized [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s lack of decency, his disregard for the Constitution and the rule of law, and recounted his conduct on January 6 — issues Politico argued had long ago pushed voters away from Trump's GOP and were not what animated the remaining undecideds.[28] The magazine concluded the discussion was "all about yesterday and him rather than tomorrow and her," and that the clock was ticking on her campaign.[28]

The Hill reported growing fear in Democratic circles that the race was slipping away, with battleground polling appearing to move toward Trump over the preceding two weeks.[28] Cracks were showing in the "blue wall" of Michigan, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin, with Senate incumbents in Pennsylvania and Wisconsin in toss-up races according to the Cook Political Report; Harris was losing ground particularly in Wisconsin, while doubts among Arab American voters in Michigan worried Democrats.[28]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that legacy media panic was giving way to anger as Harris failed to make her case, with activists calling out the nominee for failing to define a platform beyond "Not Donald Trump."[28] She characterized Harris as a weak candidate who never won a primary and was "overtly inauthentic in every appearance," and predicted that after Election Day Democrat efforts to prevent Trump from taking office would intensify, noting that Representative Jamie Raskin had been working on this since December 2023.[28]

## Gender framing of a potential defeat

In an October 2024 interview in Washington, D.C. with Hallie Jackson of NBC, Harris was pressed at length on gender, with a good ten minutes of the interview devoted to the subject and four separate questions about her sex, each of which she politely deflected.[28] Asked whether the country was ready for a woman and a woman of color to be president, Harris answered "Absolutely," adding that it was important to be "not only turning the page, but closing the chapter on an era that suggests that Americans are divided," and that voters of any race, gender or age want a president focused on their needs.[28] The Federalist framed the segment as Democrats whining about nonexistent sexism to obscure Harris's incompetence.[28]

Ashe in America rejected the emerging narrative that a Harris loss would prove American misogyny, listing [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], Michelle Bachmann, [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], Carly Fiorina, Elizabeth Warren and Amy Klobuchar as women whose defeats were attributed to bigotry rather than to their candidacies.[28] She argued that voters were choosing Trump publicly and proudly because "Trump is not the regime," and that the establishment's best closing argument — "Trump is Hitler and voters are misogynistic racists" — was a narrative that no longer held meaningful social power.[28]

## Democratic doubts about her vice presidency (February 2023)

A February 2023 New York Times report found that Democratic Party officials had lost faith in Harris and regarded her as a lose-lose proposition for the 2024 presidential election.[29] Critics quoted anonymously from the White House, Capitol Hill and around the country argued that if [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] ran again her presence on the ballot could cost the party the election, and that if he did not run she was not a strong enough candidate to win on her own.[29] Democratic donor [[john-morgan|John Morgan]] told the paper that this would be "one of the most hard-hitting arguments against Biden."[29]

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] answered sarcastically that Harris's critics must be racist and sexist to overlook her "razor-sharp wit and boundless charisma," before characterizing her career as riding the coattails of Bay Area political boss [[willie-brown|Willie Brown]] and covering up criminal activity — including crimes against children by the Catholic Church — as California Attorney General.[29]

Writing separately, [[just-human|Just Human]] parsed the sourcing of the Times piece: a Clinton associate said Harris "has not distinguished herself" and could not name one thing she had done except "stay out of the way," while two unnamed Democrats recalled private conversations in which [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] lamented that Harris lacked the political instincts to clear a primary field, even as a Clinton spokesperson insisted the two women "have built and maintained a strong bond."[29] Counting three Clinton-aligned figures throwing shade with her own spokesperson providing cover, Just Human concluded: "Hillary is running in 2024."[29]

## October 2024: SNL mockery and Biden-team friction

In mid-October 2024, Saturday Night Live devoted a Family Feud–style sketch to mocking Harris's trademark mannerisms, including her signature "word salad" delivery and her alleged Glock, in what commentators counted as a third consecutive week of swipes at her.[30] Social media reaction treated the mockery as ominous for the Democratic ticket, with Robby Starbuck writing that "when SNL is doing skits like this, you know that they think Kamala's cooked."[30] Badlands contributor Ashe in America read the sketches as safe, narrative-reinforcing writing designed to concede Harris's incompetence while still framing her as preferable to Trump.[30]

Relations between Harris's team and Joe Biden's White House also grew fraught in the final weeks before Election Day, according to ten people familiar with the situation.[30] Biden's team wanted Harris to win, but many senior aides remained wounded by the president's ouster from the re-election bid and were adjusting to a supporting role; one close Harris ally said of them, "They're too much in their feelings."[30] Some on the Harris team complained that top White House aides were not sufficiently coordinating Biden's messaging and schedule with the vice president's campaign — citing an impromptu Biden press conference in the briefing room that stepped on a Harris event in Michigan, and Biden praising Ron DeSantis as "gracious" and "cooperative" shortly after Harris criticized him for not returning her hurricane call.[30]

Harris and Trump both sat for interviews on popular podcasts during the 2024 cycle.[30]

## Defense of withholding the Epstein files

In a Wednesday, December 17, 2025 interview with talk show host Jimmy Kimmel, Harris defended the Biden administration's decision to keep files relating to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] sealed, framing it as a necessary separation of interests between the White House and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[33] Asked about the imminent release of classified Epstein documents that the Trump administration had until that Friday to deliver, Harris said: "To give you an answer that will not satisfy your curiosity, I will tell you, we, perhaps to our damage, but we strongly and rightly believed that there should be an absolute separation between what we wanted as an administration and what the Department of Justice did. We absolutely adhered to that and it was right to do that."[33] She added that the Justice Department "would make its decisions independent of any political or personal vendetta or concern that we may have," a framing critics said obscured the fact that an overwhelming majority of Americans want the files released.[33]

## Control of Biden campaign funds (July 2024)

In the days after the June 2024 debate, reporting examined what would happen to President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s campaign war chest if he stepped aside, concluding that Harris as vice president would be in line for first dibs on the tens of millions of dollars involved.[34] The question was raised during a tense July 2024 call between the campaign's aides and donors, in which campaign manager [[julie-chavez-rodriguez|Julie Chavez Rodriguez]] said that in the event of the president dropping out — which she emphasized he had no intention of doing — Harris would control most of the money in their campaign account, which stood at $91.2 million as of May 31.[34] Campaign finance experts broadly agreed, though noted this likely held only if she then became the Democratic presidential nominee.[34]

## RFK Jr. Cabinet approach and 2024 campaign standing (August 2024)

In August 2024 independent presidential candidate [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] sought a meeting with Harris through intermediaries to discuss serving in her administration, possibly as a Cabinet secretary, in exchange for endorsing her campaign; Harris and her advisers did not respond with an offer to meet or show interest in the proposal.[35] The Kennedy campaign produced polling claiming both Harris and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would gain support across 31 states if either publicly promised him a Cabinet role.[35] At the same time Harris led Trump in fundraising and in some swing-state polling, prompting the Trump campaign to add senior advisers including [[corey-lewandowski|Corey Lewandowski]] amid Republican complaints that Trump had struggled to find a consistent line of attack against her.[35]

## Polling anxieties and labor politics (September 2024)

In late September 2024, The Guardian reported that Democrats were increasingly worried that pollsters were undercounting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s support and rated his chances of winning higher than headline polling suggested.[36] While most national surveys showed consistent though moderate leads for Harris, supporters were unnerved by narrow margins in the three northern battlegrounds of Pennsylvania, Michigan and Wisconsin deemed must-wins for her path to the White House; some Pennsylvania polls showed leads of four to six points while others showed far smaller deficits for Trump, and Harris's lead in Michigan and Wisconsin stood at just one or two percent.[36] Underpinning the anxiety was Trump's overperformance relative to forecasts in all three states in 2016 and again in 2020.[36]

Badlands Media additionally argued that Democratic reliance on labor was a strategic misstep, pointing to the [[teamsters|Teamsters]]' refusal to endorse Harris after members broke for Trump and to the looming East and Gulf Coast dockworker strike, and blaming the party's economic policy for wage erosion that forced workers into second and third jobs.[36] It also criticized the 2024 [[farm-aid|Farm Aid]] headliners for supporting Harris and "the status quo of the global regime."[36]

## "I am ready to serve" (February 2024)

Interviewed by [[wall-street-journal|The Wall Street Journal]] aboard Air Force Two in early February 2024 while detailing her campaign priorities, Harris was asked whether voters' concerns about President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s age meant she had to convince them she was ready to serve.[37] "I am ready to serve. There's no question about that," she responded bluntly, adding that everyone who sees her on the job "walks away fully aware of my capacity to lead."[37] The interview took place two days before the release of the [[hur-report|Hur report]], which described the 81-year-old president as displaying "diminished faculties" and as an "elderly man with a poor memory," amplifying concerns about his mental acuity.[37]

The findings intensified scrutiny on Harris, then 59 and the first woman and Black vice president, whose tenure had been marked by criticism of her political skills; what had been quiet talk of whether she could step into the presidency spilled into the open.[37] Democratic strategist [[jennifer-palmieri|Jennifer Palmieri]] observed that "the special counsel's report has sort of accelerated that moment."[37] Harris herself called the Hur report "politically motivated," while polling showed 86% of Americans believed Biden was too old to serve another term.[37]

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] contended that Harris's comments contributed to a conditioning of the public mind: that removing Biden from office or from the ballot for "simply being an old man" could later be mirrored and projected onto [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], stocking narrative ammunition for the campaign ahead.[37]

## POLITICO/Morning Consult poll, June 2024

A POLITICO/[[morning-consult|Morning Consult]] poll released in June 2024 found that only a third of voters thought it likely Harris would win an election were she to become the Democratic nominee, with just three of five Democrats and a quarter of independents believing she would prevail.[38] Forty-two percent of voters described her as a strong leader, including three-quarters of Democrats but only a third of independents, and her favorability numbers closely tracked [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s — Biden at 43 percent favorable against 54 unfavorable, Harris at 42 percent favorable against 52 unfavorable.[38] The survey was framed against voter concern over Biden's age and the question of whether Harris, as next in line, could perform the job if she inherited it.[38]

Badlands contributor Ashe in America argued that POLITICO's attempt to find a silver lining — noting that Harris outperformed Biden among Black voters, a shift from the 2020 primary — was a fatal unforced error, since it reminded readers that Biden had been more popular than Harris with Black voters four years earlier and simultaneously confirmed that Biden's support in the demographic was collapsing rather than that Harris had gained ground.[38] She added that pitting two candidates on the same ticket against each other confounds any effort to demonstrate either's popularity, and concluded that "white leftist women are the only people still pretending Kamala is a competent world leader or even remotely electable," floating the possibility that [[politico|POLITICO]] was deliberately publishing self-owning storylines.[38]

## Putin's mock endorsement and capital gains triangulation (September 2024)

On September 5, 2024, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said in a teasing comment at the [[eastern-economic-forum|Eastern Economic Forum]] that Russia wanted Harris to win the US presidential election, citing her "infectious" laugh as a reason to prefer her over [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[39] The remark came a day after the US Justice Department charged two Russian media executives over an alleged illegal scheme to influence the November election with pro-Russian propaganda.[39] Putin had earlier said, in another comment widely seen as not to be taken at face value, that he preferred [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] over Trump as a more predictable "old school" politician; US intelligence agencies believed Moscow actually wanted Trump to win because he was less committed to supporting Ukraine.[39]

Days earlier Harris had chosen the top rate for capital gains as her first big move toward the center of the 2024 political conversation, calling for Americans making $1 million a year to pay a 28% capital gains rate — a symbolic departure from Biden, who preferred 39.6%, against a current top rate of 20% plus a 3.8% Medicare surcharge.[39] Having spent the first six weeks of her campaign embracing Biden's tax positions as her own, she used a New Hampshire event to signal to the business community that "Comrade Kamala" she isn't.[39]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] read the moment through accelerationism, arguing that Harris was "running away with polling, while rapidly hemorrhaging support" while her campaign alternated between full-scale communist messaging and aping Trump campaign promises.[39] He noted that Putin's rhetoric on the Biden-to-Harris candidacy shift mirrored Trump's own statement that Biden was his first option and Kamala "would be my second choice," suggesting the two were "saying the same thing, in the same manner, because they're on the same side."[39] In his framing the Harris candidacy was no more a threat to patriot plans than the Biden presidency, and was necessary for purposes of exposure, disclosure and awakening.[39]

## Aftermath of the 2024 defeat

[[donald-trump|Trump]]'s November 2024 victory over Harris, in which he won the Electoral College for a second time and the popular vote for the first, was followed by reports that the [[state-department|State Department]] had arranged counseling for distressed employees — a step Representative [[darrell-issa|Darrell Issa]] attacked as catering to officials "personally devastated by the normal functioning of American democracy" because Harris was not elected.[40]

### February 2025 CAA signing
In February 2025, Harris reportedly signed once more with Creative Artists Agency, following former President Joe Biden, whose partnership with the entertainment company was announced on February 3.[41] Variety reported that Harris's agreement with CAA covered "all areas," with an emphasis on speaking engagements and publishing; she had a deal with the agency before becoming vice president, and Michelle and Barack Obama also work with CAA.[41] NBC News had reported the previous month that she was working on a book that could lay groundwork for another presidential run in 2028.[41] The 60-year-old California native, who campaigned as the first woman of color to capture a major party's nomination, lost to Donald Trump by 1.5 percent of the popular vote in 2024 and had kept a relatively low profile since her defeat.[41] Badlands commentary treated the signing as evidence that "you are watching a movie."[41]

## 2024 general election flashpoints

In the closing weeks of the 2024 campaign, Harris's contest with [[donald-trump]] was close enough in several battleground states that lawmakers in both parties braced for a protracted count and a contested certification.[42] Republicans meanwhile pursued more than 120 lawsuits across 26 states, which the Harris campaign said amounted to "scheming to sow distrust in our elections and undermine our democracy so they can cry foul when they lose."[42] See [[2024-republican-election-litigation-campaign]].[42]

At the end of September 2024, [[hillary-clinton]] warned of an October surprise that would "distort and pervert" Harris, invoking the [[pizzagate]] episode from her own 2016 campaign as a template for how such narratives spread.[42] Badlands commentary read the warning as foreshadowing, linking it to the [[sean-combs-federal-trial|Sean Combs]] scandal then breaking into mainstream awareness and to Harris's alleged connections to and electoral support from the hip hop world.[42]

Trump's campaign also invoked Harris directly when defending his call for "one real rough, nasty" and "violent day" of policing to end crime: communications director [[steven-cheung]] said the alternative was "all-out anarchy, which is what Kamala Harris has created in some of these communities across America," particularly during her tenure as California Attorney General when, in his account, she emboldened criminals.[42]

## Positioning during the 2024 replacement crisis

Badlands Media commentary in July 2024 argued that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had absorbed nearly half a billion dollars in establishment campaign support that could legally be transferred only to Harris — the one presidential candidate the writer contended the political establishment wanted nothing to do with.[43] Reporting that month also recorded Democratic representatives calling on Biden to step aside and throw his support behind Harris in the aftermath of his June 27 debate performance.[43]

## Policy platform release before the 2024 debate

Less than 48 hours before her September 10, 2024 debate against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Harris's presidential campaign posted a list of her policy positions on its website, following sustained criticism that she had been vague and thin on proposals since launching her run in July.[44] The list was organized into four main sections focused on the economy, "fundamental freedoms," safety and crime, and national security.[44] The release came as new polling showed a tight race: a New York Times/Siena College national poll found Trump up one percentage point over Harris, with many voters saying they wanted to know more about the vice-president's policies, while a CBS/YouGov survey showed Harris narrowly ahead in Michigan and Wisconsin and tied in Pennsylvania.[44]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued the framing was inverted — designed to imply that only Trump and his supporters expected the nominee to have a defensible platform — and characterized Harris as "merely the latest in a long line of proxy candidates who have no platform other than that which the machine funnels through them."[44] Where earlier chapters of the information war allowed the machine to "flaunt this platform flagrantly," he wrote, it was now "silent-running," which he read as retreat: "Vampires and sunlight don't mix."[44]

Separately, former California State Senator [[gloria-romero|Gloria Romero]], announcing her departure from the [[democratic-party|Democratic Party]], criticized Harris's record as California Attorney General, saying she had done "nothing to change the schools" and had once sought to pass a bill "to jail mothers" of truant students.[44] Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] also coined "Kamalanomics" in the same issue to describe grocery inflation under the Biden–Harris administration.[44]

## October 2024: 60 Minutes interview and hurricane feud

In an interview with CBS's [[60-minutes|60 Minutes]] released on Monday, October 7, 2024, correspondent [[bill-whitaker|Bill Whitaker]] asked Harris whether democracy had been best served by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] stepping down and "basically handing" her the nomination without a primary process.[45] Harris answered that Biden had made a decision "rare among leaders, which was to put country before self," and said she was "proud to have earned the support of the vast majority of delegates and to have been elected the Democratic nominee."[45] Told that the truncated process was why voters said they did not know who she was, she replied that she had been in the race for 70 days and was travelling from state to state because "it is my responsibility to earn the vote."[45] The network later edited portions of her answers out of the broadcast.[45]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the interview a disaster of "word salads and unintentional admissions," and singled out the claim that Harris had been "elected" as nominee, arguing it was the third rigged Democratic primary in a row and that gaslighting voters about it would backfire among Bernie Sanders supporters and other defecting constituencies.[45] She argued the campaign was no longer contesting the middle but trying to convince its own base — black, Latino, feminist and union voters — that the party remained viable, and that Harris lost support every time she spoke even as the campaign scheduled far more appearances.[45]

Days later Harris drew bipartisan criticism for accusing Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] of "political gamesmanship" and selfishness after he did not take her calls ahead of [[hurricane-milton|Hurricane Milton]].[45] DeSantis called the charge "delusional," noting he was coordinating with Biden and [[fema|FEMA]]; CNN commentator [[scott-jennings|Scott Jennings]] asked "Is she crazy?"; and Biden publicly praised DeSantis's performance.[45] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the episode as establishment figures hedging against the Harris campaign ahead of the election.[45]

## August 2024 CNN interview

On August 29, 2024, Harris sat for her first televised interview since accepting the Democratic presidential nomination, appearing on CNN alongside her running mate [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] in a session moderated by [[dana-bash|Dana Bash]].[46] Coverage of the interview emphasized that Harris repeatedly invoked President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s record to justify the policy reversals she had made since her 2019 presidential campaign, presenting herself as open to fracking, willing to crack down on the southern border, and dismissive of Medicare for All.[46] In doing so she effectively occupied the centrist Democratic position that Biden vacated when he withdrew from the race.[46]

Harris praised Biden as possessing "the intelligence, the judgment and the commitment and the disposition to serve the American people" during his final months in office, and said she had "no regrets" about publicly defending him after the debate that derailed his campaign.[46] Asked about [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s questioning of her racial identity, she replied only, "Same old tired playbook, next question please."[46]

Badlands Media characterized the interview as a rehabilitation exercise conducted with largely softball questioning.[46] The outlet noted that Harris declined to explain why she had reversed her opposition to fracking, saying instead that she had "been very clear," and that her stated support for consequences against illegal border crossers contradicted the position she had held as border czar.[46] Harris credited her "root causes of migration" initiative with generating US corporate investment in Central and South America and reducing migration from the region, then blamed Trump and congressional Republicans for the failure of the bipartisan border bill, and cited her record prosecuting cartels.[46] Pressed on her many issue changes, she answered that "my values haven't changed," adding language about "holding ourselves to deadlines around time."[46] Walz remained largely silent apart from defending his own record with the remark, "People know who I am."[46]

Badlands also tied Harris to the fiscal machinery of the migration system, noting that the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] under the Biden-Harris administration was distributing hundreds of millions of dollars in Shelter and Services Program grants to sanctuary cities and NGOs, and that a federal judge had ruled the administration could not grant illegal farm workers collective bargaining rights unavailable to citizen farm workers.[46]

## 2024 election defeat

Harris lost the [[2024-us-general-election|November 5, 2024 presidential election]] to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who appeared to win decisively on election night.[47] AP VoteCast exit polling of more than 110,000 voters found that about 80 percent of voters wanted at least "substantial change" in how the country is run and roughly 25 percent wanted "complete and total upheaval" — figures Badlands and Breitbart both read as damaging to Harris as the second-ranking official in the incumbent administration.[47]

A New York Times/Siena College poll released on October 25 had found 52 percent of likely voters trusted Trump to lead the economy against 45 percent for Harris, while the economy and jobs were the top issue for 40 percent of voters.[47] Just under half of respondents said they were at least somewhat concerned that a Harris administration would move the US closer to authoritarianism, compared with just under six in ten who said the same of Trump.[47]

Harris also featured in Israeli commentary on the race: a [[jerusalem-post|Jerusalem Post]] opinion piece acknowledged she was "not an ideal candidate for the presidency – neither from an American perspective nor from an Israeli perspective" while still arguing that a Trump victory would be worse, and noted that Israeli polling showed only around 20 percent support for Harris against well over 60 percent for Trump.[47]

## Israel and antisemitism charges, July 2024

Within days of becoming the presumptive Democratic nominee, Harris was caught between Republicans accusing her of embracing antisemitic protesters and progressives urging her to challenge Israel's conduct in the Gaza war, making Israel an early and volatile test of her foreign policy footing.[48] Rupert Murdoch's New York Post attacked her positioning after she skipped [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s address to Congress, running the cover headline "ISRAEL LEFT BEHIND" above her photo.[48] Pro-Palestinian organizers, including Hatem Abudayyeh of the U.S. Palestinian Community Network, had for months paired the epithets "Genocide Joe" and "Killer Kamala."[48] Harris sought a middle course, affirming her defense of Israel while condemning the treatment of civilians in Gaza, and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told a North Carolina rally that "she's running away from Israel" and was "totally against the Jewish people."[48] The episode is covered in detail at [[harris-israel-antisemitism-controversy|the Harris Israel antisemitism controversy]].[48]

## Democratic anxiety over her candidacy

Behind the public jubilation over Harris's swift rise, Democratic lawmakers were privately anxious about her prospects against Trump, acknowledging that she was largely untested as a candidate and faced serious challenges.[48] Much of the anxiety had been set aside out of relief that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], then 81, had dropped his reelection bid, and lawmakers hoped Harris would energize donors along with young and minority voters.[48] Concerns nonetheless surfaced about her ability to connect with white working-class and union voters in Michigan, Pennsylvania, and Wisconsin — the three states critical to defeating Trump in 2020.[48] One Democratic senator said of her 2020 primary run, in which she withdrew before the Iowa caucuses, "she wasn't a great candidate," adding that "she may not be as a political campaigner as good as Biden was in his prime."[48]

Separately, reporting that former President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] told Biden "we have Kamala's approval to invoke the 25th Amendment" placed Harris at the center of the [[pressure-campaign-for-bidens-withdrawal|pressure campaign for Biden's withdrawal]].[48]

## Closing week of the 2024 campaign

In the final week of the campaign, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] devoted a Mar-a-Lago press conference to "the character of Kamala Harris," describing her run as a "campaign of destruction" and a "campaign of hate"; Badlands framed the event as his answer to a brief gaggle Harris had held the previous week in which she spent five minutes comparing Trump to Hitler.[49]

At the same time, Democrats were reported to be frustrated that Harris had not done more to sell her economic message, worrying that Trump retained a sizable advantage on the issue voters most often named as their top concern.[49] Having focused on attacking Trump, Harris lost ground in the polls as voters indicated they were less motivated by additional criticisms of a figure whose flaws had been in the national spotlight for more than eight years.[49] Her campaign said it would put more emphasis on the economy in the closing week, even though abortion rights and attacks on Trump's character drew louder applause at rallies featuring [[michelle-obama|Michelle Obama]], Bruce Springsteen and Maggie Rogers.[49] Some Democratic strategists defended the scathing criticism as necessary and effective while conceding she could speak better about the economy — a difficulty that had also vexed [[joe-biden|President Biden]] before he abandoned his reelection bid; strategist Steve Jarding complained that Trump "gets away with saying, 'The economy is the worst it's ever been, there's more unemployment, inflation is the highest it's ever been.' None of that is true."[49]

National polling in the same period, including the New York Times–Siena College survey, showed the race a dead heat, prompting [[politico|Politico]] to argue for abolishing the [[electoral-college|Electoral College]] and Badlands commentators to predict Trump would win the popular vote.[49] Badlands also asserted that the notion of Harris and Biden being against Israel was "pure WWE theater," pointing to her husband [[doug-emhoff|Doug Emhoff]] publicly advocating for the Zionist lobby.[49]

## Allegations of concealing Biden's incapacity (July 2024)

Speaking at a National Conservatism Conference event in Washington, DC on July 8, 2024, [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]] charged that Harris had violated her oath of office and endangered the nation by helping hide President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s "brain dead" status.[50] Miller said Harris knew Biden was "cognitively impaired to the point of being nonfunctional" for three and a half years, until his condition was exposed by his performance in the [[2024-presidential-debate|June 2024 debate]] against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[50] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] amplified the charge, arguing that Harris, the cabinet, congressional leaders, bureaucrats, the intelligence community and the news media had jointly conspired to conceal an incapacitated president and govern without oversight — conduct she framed as a criminal conspiracy rather than lawful governance, and as precisely the kind of scenario the [[twenty-fifth-amendment|25th Amendment]] was written to address.[50]

## Border wall reversal (August 2024)

In late August 2024 Harris, after presiding over what ZeroHedge called the worst illegal immigration crisis in U.S. history as "border czar," pledged to spend hundreds of millions of dollars to build a wall at the southern border — a plan she had called "un-American" during the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[51] Axios described the shift as the latest example of Harris flip-flopping on past liberal positions, noting she was embracing a more hawkish immigration policy while the Trump campaign spent tens of millions of dollars on attack ads over the Biden-Harris border record.[51]

At the Democratic National Convention she said she would sign the bipartisan border security bill negotiated by Senators [[james-lankford|James Lankford]] and [[chris-murphy|Chris Murphy]], which calls for hundreds of millions of dollars in unspent funds to be used to continue Trump's wall.[51] "It requires the Trump border wall," Lankford told Axios. "It is in the bill itself that it sets the standards that were set during the Trump administration: Here's where it will be built. Here's how it has to be built, the height, the type, everything during the Trump construction."[51] In 2017, then-Senator Harris had called the wall project a "stupid use of money" and committed to blocking funding for it.[51]

The Trump campaign responded by asking how much longer the mainstream media would allow Harris "to hide and use staff to speak on her behalf," saying it was "DAY 37 of ZERO interviews" and that the anonymous-sourced claim she supported the wall was "a preposterous and false claim," adding that "Kamala's ACTIONS speak much louder than the WORDS of the anonymous staff she is cowering behind."[51]

## NABJ panel dispute over her race (July 2024)

At a July 31, 2024 conference of the [[national-association-of-black-journalists|National Association of Black Journalists]], [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] made a combative appearance in which the questioning at times focused squarely on Harris's race.[52] Trump claimed he had not known until a few years earlier that Harris, who is Indian American and Black, was Black, and baselessly suggested she had decided to "turn Black" recently for political gain, saying she "was always of Indian heritage, and she was only promoting Indian heritage."[52] He asked, "Is she Indian or is she Black?"[52] ABC News' Rachel Scott, one of three moderators, noted that Harris — the de facto Democratic nominee — had attended Howard University, a historically Black college; Trump did not directly respond.[52]

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that Trump had laid out bait that the media took entirely, and that the resulting argument over whether Harris campaigned as "actual" or "political" Black showed neither the press nor Harris had substantive arguments; he predicted a swift backlash and argued that racial "communities" exist only in a constructed central narrative.[52]

## Border vulnerability and the Venezuelan election

Polling in mid-2024 suggested the border remained one of Harris's top vulnerabilities, and Republicans made clear they intended to highlight her role in addressing the "root causes" of migration, particularly as unrest following [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro's]] disputed re-election in [[venezuela|Venezuela]] raised fears of a new migrant surge.[52] Illegal border crossings had plunged after President Biden issued an executive order cracking down on asylum.[52]

## Male-voter problem and the Rogan approach (October 2024)

In mid-October 2024, Newsweek reported that Harris was planning an appearance on [[joe-rogan|Joe Rogan]]'s podcast as polls suggested the vice president needed a boost among male voters, with Reuters reporting that her campaign had discussed plans with Rogan's team for an appearance before Election Day though nothing had been confirmed.[53] Rogan's program is the most popular podcast in the United States and regularly attracts millions of predominantly male listeners; Harris, seeking to become the first female US president, had recently embarked on a media blitz of talk shows and podcasts over traditional news outlets, including *Call Her Daddy*, popular with young women.[53]

Badlands contributor Ashe in America called the plan "a terrible idea," arguing that the long-form, winding and often irreverent format would expose the inauthenticity of Harris's 2024 repositioning.[53] In her account, Harris could have handled the format in 2019 when she ran on what Ashe called her authentic Marxist ideology, and the reason the 2024 version comes across as "cringe" is that she is attempting to credibly convince voters she holds her current policy positions — which is why she flubs talking points, produces word salads, and deploys the laugh.[53] "Middle of the road moderate" is unnatural for a career politician who, Ashe wrote, previously claimed the worldview that Americans are evil colonialists, borders are fascist, and society is post-capitalist.[53] She cited Holly at Altitude's line that "Kamala may be drunk, but that's cope for the fact that she's cracking under the pressure," and summarized: "She's a passable politician, but a terrible actress."[53]

Harris also declined to appear at the Economic Club of Chicago event where [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was interviewed by Bloomberg Editor-in-Chief [[john-micklethwait|John Micklethwait]] on October 15, 2024; Micklethwait noted at the outset that she had refused to discuss her economic vision.[53] In separate commentary on the [[october-surprise|October surprise]] phenomenon, Ashe listed allegations that Harris plagiarized her book and faked Black relatives among the vulnerabilities a late-breaking event could activate.[53]

## Front-runner status in a hypothetical open convention (July 2024)

In the week after the June 2024 [[2024-presidential-debate|presidential debate]], as Democratic governors quietly positioned themselves as possible replacements for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], reporting noted that even if Biden stepped aside and opened a nominating contest at the August convention, Harris would be the clear front-runner.[54] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called this the funniest element of the episode for the ambitious governors, while observing that Harris's polling usually ran several points behind Biden's.[54]

## Response to Biden's "garbage" remark (October 2024)

On Wednesday, October 30, 2024, Harris gave her first public response to the fallout from President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s apparent characterization of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s supporters as "garbage" the previous evening.[55] "First of all, he clarified his comments. But let me be clear, I strongly disagree with any criticism of people based on who they vote for," she told reporters before boarding Air Force Two.[55] She added: "You heard my speech last night and continuously throughout my career. I believe that the work that I do is about representing all the people, whether they support me or not. And as president of the United States, I will be a president for all Americans, whether you vote for me or not. That is my responsibility, and that's the kind of work that I've done my entire career. And I take it very seriously."[55]

Trump responded to the broader controversy by campaigning in a garbage truck in a hi-vis vest, telling supporters the truck was "in honor of Kamala [Harris] and Joe Biden."[55] Days later, the Pennsylvania ABC affiliate [[wnep|WNEP]] mistakenly aired test presidential "results" showing Harris beating Trump in the state 52 percent to 47 percent, an error the station publicly apologized for.[55]

## Democratic panic in October 2024

With less than a month until Election Day 2024, The Hill reported that Democratic nerves were at an all-time high, a sharp reversal from the bullishness of a month or two earlier about Harris's prospects of defeating [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[56] Party figures grumbled about her interview on CBS's "60 Minutes," about static poll numbers, about her messaging, and about her standing with men — not only white men but Black and Hispanic men as well.[56] Democratic strategist Jamal Simmons, who had served as Harris's communications director until the previous year, said "everything is deadlocked and the composition of the electorate is unknowable," listing the unprecedented features of the cycle: an African American woman on the ticket, a former president running again, a campaign with two assassination attempts, and a candidate switched out two months before Election Day.[56] "If you're not nervous, you're not paying attention," he concluded.[56]

Newsweek reporting in the same period held that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s valedictory tour was hurting rather than helping Harris by tethering her to his record, a view Karl Rove had voiced in an op-ed and that the Trump campaign openly welcomed.[56] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] grouped these stories — Harris engendering panic among the Democratic establishment, Trump being "everywhere" in the campaign and in American culture, and Biden working effectively for the MAGA movement — as converging headlines in "the first Act of October."[56]

## SNL cameo and equal-time controversy

Days before the November 2024 vote, Harris made a surprise cameo on [[saturday-night-live|Saturday Night Live]], appearing in a sketch with [[maya-rudolph|Maya Rudolph]] — who has portrayed her on the show's 50th season — and poking fun at [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] after flying to New York City en route to campaign in Michigan.[57] [[brendan-carr|Brendan Carr]], a Republican commissioner on the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]] nominated to the panel by both Trump and [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], said the appearance may have violated the agency's "equal time" rule.[57]

## Russian commentary on a Harris presidency

In a Telegram post days before the election, Russian Security Council deputy chairman [[dmitry-medvedev|Dmitry Medvedev]] argued the result would change nothing for Russia because both candidates shared a consensus that "Russia must be defeated," and described Harris as "dumb, inexperienced, and easy to control," predicting that real decisions would be made by senior ministers and advisors and, indirectly, the Obamas.[57]

## August 2024 debate-rules dispute

In late August 2024, Harris's campaign sought to alter the format of the September 10 debate on ABC, asking that the candidates be seated, be allowed written notes, and give opening statements rather than debating standing and extemporaneously.[58] Trump campaign adviser [[jason-miller|Jason Miller]] told Politico Playbook that the terms had already been agreed — "We accepted the ABC debate under the exact same terms as the CNN debate" — and that the campaign would permit "no changes to the agreed upon rules."[58] Miller argued the request fit a pattern of insulating Harris, who at that point had not held a press conference, committed to a long-form interview, or regularly taken reporters' questions, and had agreed to only one debate.[58]

Badlands commentator Ashe in America wrote that the episode inverted days of punditry claiming Trump was trying to avoid the ABC event, and observed that debate had been "DeSantis' Achilles heel and Biden's death rattle" and had never been a strength for Harris.[58] The commentary also noted that [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] was reportedly assisting Trump with debate preparation.[58]

## Republican establishment endorsements

Also in late August 2024, more than 200 Republicans who had worked for George H. W. Bush, [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]], Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] and Senator [[mitt-romney|Mitt Romney]] endorsed Harris, warning that democracy would be "irreparably jeopardized" by another Trump administration.[58] Their letter, first reported by USA Today, acknowledged "plenty of honest, ideological disagreements with Vice President Harris and Gov. Walz" while calling the alternative "simply untenable," and urged moderate Republicans and independents in battleground states "to take a brave stand once more."[58] The group had issued a similar letter in 2020, and the announcement followed speeches by Republicans at the [[2024-democratic-national-convention|Democratic National Convention]].[58] Badlands contributors read the endorsements as confirmation of the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] thesis, with Burning Bright arguing that the convention's parade of "Old Guard" figures amounted to a final illusion cast to present Harris as "a real candidate deposing a real president in a real election."[58]

## Democratic anxiety over a risk-averse campaign (October 2024)

In early October 2024, Democratic operatives — including some of Harris's own staffers — were reported to be growing increasingly concerned about her relatively light campaign schedule, which had her holding fewer events than [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and avoiding unscripted interactions with voters and the press almost entirely.[59] In interviews with POLITICO, nearly two dozen Democrats described Harris as running a do-no-harm, risk-averse race they feared could hamper her in the final 30-day stretch, with the party still haunted by 2016.[59]

With early voting by mail and in person already under way in more than half the country, Harris spent just three days of the last week of September in battleground states; on September 28, while Trump gave a speech in Wisconsin before flying to Alabama for the Georgia–Alabama football game, Harris attended a fundraiser in San Francisco.[59] Democrats argued she would benefit from venues allowing a more authentic introduction to voters — town halls, sit-down interviews and unscripted exchanges.[59]

[[david-axelrod|David Axelrod]], the longtime Democratic operative who helped lead Barack Obama's presidential campaigns and an early critic of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s campaigning style, said, "There's a time at which you just have to barnstorm these battlegrounds... These races are decathlons, and there are a lot of events, and you have to do all of them because people want to test you."[59] He added that the campaign was "the most difficult oral exam on the plan for the most difficult job," listing town halls, all kinds of interviews and off-the-record voter interactions as valuable, and said, "I would be doing them if I were her."[59]

Separately, polling showed Harris likely to win no more than 58 percent of Latino voters — far below historic Democratic margins — a weakness illustrated by the rightward drift of places like [[starr-county|Starr County]], Texas.[59]

## October 2024: Fox interview fallout, Al Smith absence, and slipping polls

Harris skipped the 79th [[alfred-e-smith-memorial-foundation-dinner|Alfred E. Smith Memorial Foundation Dinner]] on October 17, 2024, sending a pre-recorded video message instead, and became the target of extended ribbing from [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who joked that she would have attended if told the proceeds were going to bail out looters and rioters in Minneapolis and that the audience could not "get enough of hearing her beautiful laugh."[60]

A day earlier, her much-anticipated [[fox-news|Fox News]] interview with [[bret-baier|Bret Baier]] ended in disarray when four Harris staffers waved their arms to signal that it had to stop, leaving Baier with questions unasked.[60] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that this, together with [[cbs-news|CBS]]'s editing of Harris's answers before broadcast, would in normal times have been disqualifying, and advanced the view that the Harris campaign's decisions were so uniformly ill-advised that internal sabotage was the most plausible explanation — the Obama-aligned apparatus being too skilled to be merely incompetent with the "first black female president."[60] One theory floated in a post-interview discussion held that the self-destruction was cover for a coming market and currency collapse.[60]

Polling in the same week showed Harris's five-point national lead collapsing to a 48–48 tie, historically low support among Black men aged 18 to 44 (41 percent), 51 percent of Latino men aged 18 to 34 in Arizona backing Trump, a general shift of Black voters away from the Democratic Party, and the erosion of her advantage with white women into a two-point Trump lead.[60] Decision Desk HQ data showed her down four percentage points over the prior month, with Wisconsin moving from a two-point Harris lead to a tie, Michigan from plus-one to minus-one, and Pennsylvania to even; the firm's data science director, Scott Tranter, called it significant movement in an otherwise static race.[60] [[the-view|The View]] co-host [[joy-behar|Joy Behar]] said publicly she was "starting to worry" about the polls.[60]

At [[ethel-kennedy|Ethel Kennedy]]'s memorial service in Washington on October 16, an off-mic exchange between President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and former President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] — deciphered by a forensic lip reader for the New York Post — had Biden saying of Harris, "She's not as strong as me," with Obama replying, "I know … that's true."[60] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the episode alongside [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s re-emergence as evidence of an intra-[[deep-state|Deep State]] struggle unfolding as Harris's campaign disintegrated.[60]

## National Strategy to Counter Islamophobia (2023)

On Wednesday, November 1, 2023, Harris announced in a video address that the administration would develop the first-ever [[national-strategy-to-counter-islamophobia|National Strategy to Counter Islamophobia]].[61] She said the plan would be "a comprehensive and detailed plan to protect Muslims and those perceived to be Muslim from hate, bigotry, and violence," and would "address the concern that some government policies may discriminate against Muslims."[61] Press secretary [[karine-jean-pierre|Karine Jean-Pierre]] said the strategy would be led by the Domestic Policy Council and the National Security Council, and cited the October 14 killing of six-year-old [[wadea-al-fayoume|Wadea Al-Fayoume]] outside Chicago as part of its impetus.[61]

## October 2024: reduced campaigning and the CNN town hall

In late October 2024 [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] claimed at a Georgia rally that Harris was taking days off the campaign trail because she knew something about the election that the American public did not, asking, "This is a woman who took yesterday off, how do you take yesterday off?" and adding, "You've got 13 days, you're running for the presidency, she takes time off."[62] He contrasted this with his own schedule of 52 consecutive campaign days and concluded, "maybe she knows something we don't know, that's bad, right?"[62] Harris had no speeches or public appearances scheduled that Tuesday, her campaign noting only recorded interviews with NBC's Hallie Jackson and Telemundo, while respondents on X speculated the remark alluded to Democratic cheating or to the election not occurring at all.[62]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] canvassed possible explanations — that Harris believed the election was already secured, that a new Trump scandal was imminent, or that her own scandals were closing in — and judged the last most plausible given how defeated she appeared, noting that a press conference calling Trump "Hitler" and a swiftly debunked #MeToo allegation had failed to land.[62]

The same week Harris appeared at a [[cnn|CNN]] town hall moderated by [[anderson-cooper|Anderson Cooper]], who pressed her on the Biden administration's immigration record and her support for a bipartisan deal containing $650 million in border wall funding despite having criticized the wall more than fifty times as "stupid," "useless" and "a medieval vanity project."[62] Harris pivoted to Trump, disputing that Mexico paid for the wall and asserting he had built only about 2 percent of it.[62] She also called Trump a fascist, echoing [[john-kelly|John Kelly]]'s claim that Trump had praised Adolf Hitler's generals, which Trump's team denies; Trump responded on Truth Social that Harris was "a Threat to Democracy."[62] Polling for Newsweek by Redfield & Wilton Strategies found 53 percent of Democrat-leaning voters favored a stronger approach to immigration.[62]

Harris' transition team, unlike Trump's, had signed the memoranda of understanding with the [[general-services-administration|General Services Administration]] and the White House governing the presidential transition process.[62]

## The July 2024 succession

After [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] used a July 24, 2024 Oval Office address to frame his exit from the presidential race as passing "the torch to a new generation," Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the ensuing elevation of Harris as a "rapid installation" carried out with a speed and coordination she found infuriating.[63] She dismissed the narratives about Harris's "fitness" for duty as laughable and argued that Hollywood and the music industry were trying to manufacture her into a pop-culture icon through the "Brat Summer" meme — a reference that required explanation to the public and whose originating artist was British, which she called "spot on, globalists."[63] Both national and global media, she wrote, were working overtime on the Harris campaign with countless articles aimed at reshaping the vice president's image into that of a viable candidate, amounting to in-kind donations that would go unpunished.[63] Ashe characterised the effort as an attempt to gaslight voters with possibly the least popular Democratic candidate in history on the eve of an election.[63]
