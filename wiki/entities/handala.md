# Handala

Handala is a hacking group believed to be linked to Iran that came to prominence in November 2024 for publishing private photographs apparently stolen from the personal devices of senior Israeli politicians, military officials, and nuclear scientists.[1]

## November 2024 leak

The group published photographs of [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]], the former Israeli deputy prime minister and defence secretary, showing him relaxing with friends; in one image he appears topless on a bed alongside a woman.[1] Personal photographs of [[natan-sharansky|Natan Sharansky]], another former deputy prime minister, were also published, including an image of him on a gondola in Venice.[1] The leak further included personal material belonging to a serving Israeli ambassador and a former military attaché posted in the United States.[1]

Handala also released roughly thirty photographs it claimed had been taken inside the [[soreq-nuclear-research-centre|Soreq Nuclear Research Centre]], together with screenshots purporting to reveal the names of nuclear scientists working on the facility's particle accelerator project.[1] Responding on behalf of the Israel Atomic Energy Commission, the office of the Prime Minister of Israel denied that the images had been taken inside the nuclear facility, stating that "following a thorough examination, the images and blueprint do not belong to any of its facilities."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that, as with the Hunter Biden laptop, the salacious photographs would capture public attention while the more consequential detail — imagery said to depict Israeli nuclear facilities — went comparatively unexamined.[1] He noted that Israel has never signed the [[nuclear-non-proliferation-treaty|Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty]] and that its possession of nuclear weapons is treated in Washington as one of the worst-kept secrets in the capital.[1] He linked the leak's implications to the [[glenn-amendment|Glenn Amendment]], under which a formal Israeli admission of nuclear weapons possession would jeopardize continued U.S. aid.[1]
