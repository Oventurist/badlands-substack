# Susan Monarez

Susan Monarez is a public health official who served as Director of the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] (CDC) as a [[donald-trump|Trump]] appointee before being fired by Health and Human Services Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] in 2025.[1]

Her dismissal became a central point of criticism during a contentious Senate hearing in early September 2025, where lawmakers pressed Kennedy over the firing as well as his removal of the full panel of the federal vaccine advisory board.[1] Democrats accused Kennedy of endangering the public by spreading misinformation and gutting evidence-based vaccine research, and the episode fed escalating calls for his impeachment or resignation — including from conservative commentator [[william-kristol|Bill Kristol]], who said "I've seen enough."[1]

## Firing and CDC exodus

The [[white-house|White House]]'s firing of Monarez as CDC Director in late August 2025 was described as the latest fallout from the agency's controversial handling of longstanding vaccine policy.[2] Her removal was followed by the resignations of several top CDC officials, including chief medical officer [[debra-houry|Debra Houry]], [[daniel-jernigan|Daniel Jernigan]], director of the National Center for Emerging and Zoonotic Infectious Diseases, and [[demetre-daskalakis|Demetre Daskalakis]], director of the National Center for Immunization and Respiratory Diseases.[2]

The shakeup coincided with tightened restrictions on eligibility for the COVID-19 vaccine, prompting [[cvs-health|CVS]] and [[walgreens|Walgreens]] to restrict vaccinations, with CVS saying the vaccine was available in only 13 states plus Washington, D.C., to people with a prescription, citing "the current regulatory environment."[2] Positive COVID-19 diagnoses were reported up 11.2% for the week ending August 23.[2] Senator [[bill-cassidy|Bill Cassidy]], a physician and chair of the Senate health committee, called for the September meeting of the [[advisory-committee-on-immunization-practices|Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices]] to be delayed to ensure "significant oversight," while Senator [[susan-collins|Susan Collins]] said she was "alarmed" by the firing, which she said had "triggered the immediate resignation of four long-time experts at the agency."[2] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed the senators' objections as driven by pharmaceutical lobbies rather than constituents.[2]
