# Rusal

**Rusal** (later **UC Rusal**) is a Russian aluminum producer that was for years the world's largest by production, a distinction it lost in 2015 to the China Hongqiao Group.[1] It remains the second-largest aluminum company globally.[1]

## Founding and consolidation

Rusal was founded in 2000 through a merger between Sibirsky Aluminum and [[roman-abramovich|Roman Abramovich]]'s Millhouse Capital.[1] Its principal founder, Russian oligarch [[oleg-deripaska|Oleg Deripaska]], is regarded as the victor of the so-called "aluminum wars" of the 1990s — effectively a turf war among former Soviet elites over control of state assets after the collapse of the USSR.[1]

In 2007, Rusal merged with the aluminum producer SUAL Group and with Glencore International AG to form **UC Rusal**, with Deripaska named chairman.[1] That same year, [[glencore|Glencore]] entered into business dealings with Israeli mining magnate [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]].[1] Deripaska is a close associate of Nathaniel Rothschild, a major investor in Glencore.[1]

## Sanctions

In June 2018, Treasury Secretary [[steven-mnuchin|Steve Mnuchin]] announced that Rusal, along with Deripaska's other company En+ Group, was being targeted by the U.S. government for evading sanctions imposed on Russian companies.[1] Mnuchin accused Deripaska of instigating violence in eastern Ukraine and implied accusations of money laundering and bribery.[1] The action resulted in the resignation of Rusal's top executives, including Deripaska himself.[1] Deripaska was later targeted with sanctions by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] after criticizing the 2022 decision to invade Ukraine.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] traced Rusal's history while analyzing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s February 2025 imposition of 25% steel and aluminum tariffs, noting Deripaska's alleged role in the Russiagate scandal — including a reported $10 million contract negotiated in 2017 with Paul Manafort to promote Russian interests in the Trump administration — and asking whether "the oligarchs who control the world's resources" were being taken down.[1]
