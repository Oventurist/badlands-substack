# Stellantis

Stellantis is the multinational automaker that owns Chrysler and is counted among the Detroit Three alongside [[ford-motor-company|Ford Motor]] and [[general-motors|General Motors]].[1]

During the [[2023-united-auto-workers-strike|2023 United Auto Workers strike]], Stellantis was one of the three companies whose U.S. assembly plants were targeted by the union's coordinated walkout, and its representatives resumed negotiations with the [[united-auto-workers|UAW]] on September 16, 2023.[1] Company officials said the same weekend that a proposal to resume work at an idled Illinois factory had fallen through.[1]

## Response to the 2025 tariffs

Stellantis was among the first multinational manufacturers to react operationally to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[liberation-day-tariffs|"Liberation Day" tariffs]] in April 2025, announcing that it would temporarily lay off US workers and close plants in Canada and Mexico as businesses raced to adjust to the new duty schedule.[2] Its retrenchment contrasted with [[general-motors|General Motors]], which said it would instead increase US production.[2]

## Layoffs during the 2023 UAW strike

Stellantis, the maker of Chrysler, Jeep and Dodge vehicles, announced on September 20, 2023 that it was laying off 68 workers in Ohio, with another 300 layoffs in Indiana potentially to follow.[4] The company said it was cutting employment at its Toledo machining plant in Perrysburg, Ohio because of "storage constraints" and expected to do the same at its transmission and casting facilities in Kokomo, Indiana.[4] The reductions came after the United Auto Workers struck Stellantis, Ford and General Motors when the previous contract expired, sending some 12,700 workers off the job.[4]

## RAM 1500 plant strike (October 2023)

On Monday, October 23, 2023, the [[united-auto-workers|UAW]] struck Stellantis' RAM 1500 truck plant in Michigan, one day before president [[shawn-fain|Shawn Fain]] escalated further with a surprise 5,000-worker walkout at [[general-motors|General Motors]]' Arlington Assembly plant.[3] The back-to-back actions marked an acceleration of the union's rolling strike strategy against Detroit's Big Three in the sixth week of the dispute.[3] See [[2023-united-auto-workers-strike]].[3]
