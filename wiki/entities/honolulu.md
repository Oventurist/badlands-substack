# Honolulu

Honolulu is the capital city of Hawaii and the plaintiff in one of the most consequential climate-change lawsuits filed against the American energy industry. In 2020 the city sued several major fossil fuel companies, including [[exxonmobil|Exxon]] and [[chevron|Chevron]], claiming that the companies' products cause greenhouse gas emissions and global warming and that the companies failed to warn consumers about those risks.[1]

## Legal theory

Rather than proceeding under federal environmental statutes, Honolulu employed a series of state-law causes of action, including public nuisance and trespass measures, and argued that the defendant companies should pay billions of dollars to the state to abate the effects of climate change — including extreme weather events, sea level rise, heat waves, flooding and global warming generally.[1] This state-tort framing was designed to keep the case in Hawaii's courts, and the [[hawaii-supreme-court|Hawaii Supreme Court]] ruled that the case could proceed to trial.[1]

## Significance

Critics of the suit argue that its success would set a precedent for Democrat-run states across the nation, allowing them to overwhelm energy companies with litigation until those companies accept [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]]-like parameters and apply them in every state — meaning that conservative states opposing such measures would have them imposed anyway.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], argued that the labeling theory at the heart of Honolulu's claim was incoherent, since no consumer is unaware of the claimed link between fossil fuels and warming, and that if warning labels were desired the blame belongs with [[congress|Congress]] and the [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]], which possess the regulatory authority to require them.[1]
