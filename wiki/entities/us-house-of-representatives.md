# U.S. House of Representatives

The U.S. House of Representatives is the lower chamber of the United States Congress.[1]

## 2026 letter on Israel's nuclear arsenal

In May 2026, 30 Democratic members of the House, led by Rep. [[joaquin-castro|Joaquin Castro]] of Texas, sent a letter to Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] demanding that the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] disclose information about the [[israeli-nuclear-program|Israeli nuclear program]].[1] The lawmakers criticized the United States for fighting "side by side with a country whose potential nuclear weapons program the United States government officially refuses to acknowledge," and pressed for an end to nuclear ambiguity in service of a coherent nonproliferation policy in the region.[1]

Badlands commentary characterized the effort as opportunistic — an attempt to damage [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] politically given his close alignment with [[israel|Israel]] — rather than principled.[1]

## Vote against the D.C. noncitizen voting law (February 2023)

On February 9, 2023, the GOP-led House voted 260–173 to overturn the [[dc-noncitizen-voting-law|District of Columbia law]] that would have allowed noncitizen residents to vote in municipal elections, with 42 Democrats joining Republicans.[2] The Constitution grants Congress authority to block laws passed in the District, which is not a state.[2] The repeal still required passage in the Democratic-led Senate and the President's signature; [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] said he opposed the D.C. law but did not say whether he would veto the repeal.[2] Jason Snead of [[honest-elections-project-action|Honest Elections Project Action]] applauded the House for ensuring "that foreign spies and illegal immigrants are not voting in the nation's capital."[2]

## November 2023 funding impasse

Ten days before a shutdown deadline of November 17, 2023, House Republicans had not coalesced around a plan to keep federal agencies open, meeting behind closed doors to debate the structure of a [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]].[3] The conference held a slim 221-212 majority. Representative Brian Babin summarised the state of play: "Everybody's got a little bit different position, different opinions. Right now, we're cogitating about it."[3] Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] called the debate "positive" and promised to reveal a plan "in short order," while members said they feared being forced to back a rival funding measure from the Democratic-led [[us-senate|Senate]].[3] Representative Dusty Johnson predicted the speaker would "make a play call" backed by the overwhelming majority of the conference.[3]

## July 2025 rule vote on the One Big Beautiful Bill

Late on the evening of Wednesday, July 2, 2025, House Republicans began a key procedural vote on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s massive domestic policy bill, the [[one-big-beautiful-bill-act|One Big Beautiful Bill]], with the outcome uncertain.[4] Republican leadership and Trump spent much of the day trying to shore up support from skeptical members ahead of a self-imposed July 4 deadline to send the measure — which had squeaked through the [[us-senate|Senate]] the previous day — to the president's desk.[4] Before final passage the chamber first had to adopt a resolution setting the rules of debate; after hours of delay that vote began at around 9:30 p.m.[4]

As of 1 a.m. Thursday, five House Republicans had voted no, theoretically enough for the rule to fail, but the vote remained open and members could switch; eight Republicans had not yet voted, and the party could afford only three defections with all members present and voting.[4] Representative [[chip-roy|Chip Roy]] of Texas, who had voted against the rule in committee, said he was still reviewing the bill.[4]

## Inquiry into X's actions in Brazil

In April 2024 the House sent an inquiry to [[x-corp|X]] concerning "actions taken in Brazil that were in violation of Brazilian law," according to [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], who said the platform had been asked to suspend accounts belonging to sitting members of the Brazilian parliament and many journalists.[5] The inquiry came after Brazilian Supreme Court judge [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]] opened a probe into Musk over his refusal to keep certain accounts blocked.[5]

## April 2024 foreign aid votes

On Saturday, April 20, 2024, the House passed $61 billion in fresh aid for Ukraine on a 311 to 112 bipartisan vote, ending a six-month impasse, along with more than $14 billion in military aid to Israel and $9 billion in humanitarian assistance and a Taiwan aid measure.[6] Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] pushed the legislation through with Democratic support over fierce objections within his own conference, and Democrats and some Republicans waved Ukrainian flags on the floor in a rare moment of bipartisanship in a bitterly and narrowly divided chamber.[6] House Foreign Affairs Chairman [[michael-mccaul|Michael McCaul]] and Democratic leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] both defended the vote publicly.[6] Details are covered at [[april-2024-foreign-aid-package|the April 2024 foreign aid package]].[6]
