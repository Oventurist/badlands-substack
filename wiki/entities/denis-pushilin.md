# Denis Pushilin

Denis Pushilin is the head of the Donetsk People's Republic (DPR), the formerly Ukrainian region annexed by Russia, and in February 2025 he emerged as the principal Russian voice contesting Ukrainian offers of mineral wealth to the United States.[1]

## The minerals dispute

Pushilin argued that a large portion of the mineral wealth that Ukrainian leader Vladimir Zelensky was touting to Washington in exchange for military assistance is in fact Russian, lying inside territories that have since joined the Russian Federation.[1] He pledged that the region's rich reserves of lithium and titanium would benefit its own people and Russia as a whole rather than American investors.[1]

Zelensky had offered the United States privileged access to Ukrainian natural resources in order to incentivize continued military assistance, and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] claimed that Kiev had agreed to relinquish $500 billion worth of rare-earth minerals to compensate for aid previously provided under President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] Ukraine's backers have argued that the country could supply trillions of dollars' worth of raw materials, including coal, iron ore, lithium, titanium, graphite and uranium.[1]

In an interview on Wednesday, February 12, 2025, Pushilin stated: "Among what they [Zelensky's team] are offering, the majority of reserves are located in Donbass... Most of the lithium and titanium is within our territory, which has already been liberated."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands hosts framed the exchange as a bargaining position rather than a dead end, speculating that the ownership question would become part of a broader negotiation with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] — possibly with Russia relinquishing some mineral rights in return for American help against what the hosts described as the entrenched political apparatus in Kiev.[1] Commentators also compared the scramble over post-Soviet resource control to Russia's "Aluminum Wars" of the 1990s, when Soviet-era elites fought over state assets after the collapse of the USSR.[1]
