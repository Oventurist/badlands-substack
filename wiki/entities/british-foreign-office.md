# British Foreign Office

The British Foreign Office is the United Kingdom's department of state for external affairs. In Badlands Media's revisionist account of the Anglo-American relationship it is cast not as an ordinary diplomatic ministry but as the institutional centre of a long-running effort to direct American foreign policy from outside — and, at times, from inside.[1]

## The Darroch cables

In the summer of 2019, leaked emails from British ambassador [[kim-darroch|Kim Darroch]] were said to reveal that the Foreign Office had been conspiring to "flood the zone" around [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in order to influence his presidency and push it toward a war footing with Russia, Iran, and other Middle Eastern nations.[1]

## Historical pattern

The article situates that episode in a much older pattern. [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] complained to his son that career diplomats in the American [[state-department|State Department]] repeatedly concealed, delayed, or held up messages to him because they disagreed with his views, adding that "they should be working for Winston" and that in many cases they in fact were.[1] Roosevelt concluded that the State Department had become "like the British Foreign Office."[1]

[[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] later made the reverse admission from the American side. Speaking in May 1981 at London's Royal Institute for International Affairs, he said the British had been "so matter-of-factly helpful that they became a participant in internal American deliberations, to a degree probably never practiced between sovereign nations," and that in his White House years he kept the British Foreign Office better informed and more closely engaged than he did the American State Department.[1] Kissinger characterised this as "symptomatic."[1]
