# Jochen Kopelke

Jochen Kopelke is the chairman of the German police union ([[gewerkschaft-der-polizei|Gewerkschaft der Polizei]], GdP). In August 2024 he drew international attention for proposing that criminals and other knife holders who surrender their weapons receive substantial material rewards, including a year's subscription to the Netflix streaming service — a plan discussed at [[german-knife-amnesty-proposal|the German knife amnesty proposal]].[1]

## Knife amnesty proposal

Kopelke expressed concern about what he described as a rising knife-crime epidemic in German cities and urged the federal left-wing administration to "think outside the box" to tackle the issue effectively.[1] He proposed an amnesty for all those in possession of a knife who were willing to hand in their weapons, arguing that a material reward was necessary to incentivize young people in particular to participate.[1]

"For this measure to be effective, the federal government must create serious incentives for those who donate," Kopelke said, adding: "Specifically, that could mean a year of Netflix for the delivery of a banned butterfly knife."[1] German law already prohibits possession of a knife with a blade length of 12 cm or greater, but butterfly knives had become a widely used weapon and knife crime had spiraled in recent years.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the proposal as a window into how far disarmament campaigns extend once authoritarian governance gains a foothold, predicting that "hammers and garden tools are likely next."[1] The commentary framed the underlying principle as one of capability rather than weaponry — "it's not about the people having arms; it's about the people being capable of defense" — and contrasted the European situation with the United States' Second Amendment protections.[1]
