# Michael Rogers

Admiral Michael S. Rogers served as the 17th Director of the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]] and is treated in the Badlands Media [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series as one of the clearest examples of a [[white-hats|white hat]] inside the intelligence community.[1]

## Role in the Q drops

Drop #120, covered in Chapter 19 of the series, asks why Admiral Rogers was not replaced by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] when Trump took office.[1] The Badlands author answers that the reason is simple: Rogers is a white hat who gave Trump advance warning that he was being spied on, and that Trump's decision to retain him "speaks volumes" about the trust between them.[1]

The chapter states that Rogers was so effective at rooting out and identifying corruption that [[james-clapper|James Clapper]] was among the officials who approached Barack Obama seeking to have him fired.[1] The reason offered for that hostility is that Rogers "has it all" — that he knows where the bodies are buried and most likely knows the who, why, when, where, what and how of every clandestine operation run against the United States by its internal enemies.[1]

The author also notes as a "fun side note" that Rogers was the 17th Director of the NSA, a numerological detail carrying significance within [[qanon|Q]] lore.[1]

## Relationship with Snowden

The chapter advances the author's belief that [[edward-snowden|Edward Snowden]] was working with Rogers to expose the corruption and mass surveillance being conducted by the NSA, and cites Snowden's disclosures as a reason not to regard him as a black hat.[1]

## Q drop #20

Chapter 3 of [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] identifies Admiral Rogers as the answer to the question posed in [[qanon|Q]] drop #20 of November 1, 2017 — who President [[donald-trump|Trump]] met with.[3] The author states that the purpose of the meeting was to warn Trump about further spying that was taking place, and connects this to the renovation of certain rooms in the [[white-house|White House]] during Trump's first administration, which he says was ostensibly carried out to remove listening devices planted to spy on the President, his family and his administration.[3]

The drop's follow-up questions about where the meeting was held and why phones were not allowed in the room are read as a direct nod to a [[sensitive-compartmented-information-facility|SCIF]], implying discussions of a highly sensitive nature bearing on national security and on [[deep-state|Deep State]] attempts to remove Trump from office.[3]

## Chapter 21 reference

Chapter 21 of [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] invokes Rogers at the close of its discussion of Q drop #129, which [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] reads as indicating that the [[national-security-agency|NSA]] had taken over operations of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]].[2] Having argued that the NSA stood on the patriot side of the conflict, Fleuret ends the section with the reminder that Rogers was the 17th Director of the agency — the number 17 corresponding to the letter Q.[2]
