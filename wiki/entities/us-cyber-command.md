# United States Cyber Command

United States Cyber Command is the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] combatant command responsible for military cyberspace operations. In Badlands Media coverage it appears principally as a named defendant in litigation over government-linked media ratings and speech suppression.[1]

## Consortium News lawsuit

In October 2023, [[consortium-news|Consortium News]] sued [[newsguard|NewsGuard]] and the Biden administration and specifically named the Pentagon's Cyber Command for "contracting with NewsGuard to identify, report and abridge the speech of American media organizations that dissent from U.S. official positions on foreign policy."[1] The complaint alleged that NewsGuard was "acting jointly or in concert with the United States to coerce news organizations to alter viewpoints" on Ukraine, Russia and Syria, amounting to a form of "censorship and repression of views" diverging from U.S. policy and that of its allies.[1]

Badlands commentary treated the contracting relationship as the crucial element: it converts a nominally private ratings NGO into an instrument of state action, supplying what contributor Justin Deschamps described as a clear-cut and provable example of collusion to suppress free speech rather than the suspicions and anecdotes alternative media previously had to work with.[1]

## February 2024 command change

Cyber Command underwent a change of command on February 2, 2024, presided over by Deputy Secretary of Defense [[kathleen-hicks|Kathleen Hicks]], alongside a leadership change at the [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]] the same day.[2] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] quoted the command's stated mission to "Direct, Synchronize, and Coordinate Cyberspace Planning and Operations - to Defend and Advance National Interests - in Collaboration with Domestic and International Partners," and argued that the timing — days before Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]]'s hospitalization left Hicks atop the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] — was notable amid ubiquitous discussion of cyber vulnerabilities in U.S. critical infrastructure.[2]
