# Glenn Youngkin

Glenn Youngkin is a Republican politician who served as Governor of Virginia.[1]

In September 2023 Youngkin's office announced that he had pardoned Scott Smith, the Loudoun County father convicted after protesting his daughter's rape in a school bathroom by a transgender student and the public school's attempted cover-up of the incident.[1] The pardon followed several investigations into the matter.[1] Smith had been charged with obstruction of justice and disorderly conduct in June 2021 and convicted in August 2021 after a viral video showed him raging at school officials.[1] The episode is described in detail at [[loudoun-county-school-bathroom-assault-case]].[1] The pardon was reported by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] and carried as a bonus item in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[1]

## Antisemitism speech measure

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] identified Youngkin as, to his knowledge, the first U.S. governor to adopt a state measure classifying criticism of the government of [[israel|Israel]] as hate speech, dating the action to 2022.[2] The contributor placed Virginia's action at the head of a wave in which roughly 40 states — most controlled by the [[republican-party|Republican Party]] — installed comparable measures, each citing the [[international-holocaust-remembrance-alliance|International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance]] as the authority defining antisemitism, and argued that the pattern amounted to a coordinated foreign-influenced assault on the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[2]

## Executive Order 35 (2024)

On August 7, 2024, Youngkin issued [[virginia-executive-order-35|Executive Order 35]], directing that all votes in Virginia's 2024 presidential election be cast by paper ballot and codifying the election security protocols established under his governance for all future elections.[3] The order's central requirement is the exclusive use of paper ballots with a traceable chain of custody, alongside the use of Department of Motor Vehicles data to verify voter identity and offline counting machines.[3] "The Virginia model for Election Security works. This isn't a Democrat or Republican issue, it's an American and Virginian issue," Youngkin said, adding that "every legal vote deserves to be counted without being watered down by illegal votes or inaccurate machines. In Virginia, we don't play games and our model for election security is working."[3] He further stated that Virginia uses "100% paper ballots with a strict chain of custody," counting machines rather than voting machines that are tested before every election and never connected to the internet, no mass mailing of ballots, and drop boxes monitored around the clock.[3]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the order bold and "a slap in the face to a regime that prioritizes election access over accuracy," while treating the never-connected-to-the-internet claim as unproven and predicting litigation from [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]].[3] She noted that the August 7 issuance came exactly 90 days before the November 5 election, clearing a timeliness hurdle that had defeated similar efforts elsewhere, and suggested Virginia could function as a control case against jurisdictions with less rigorous procedures.[3]
