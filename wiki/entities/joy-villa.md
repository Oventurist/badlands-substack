# Joy Villa

Joy Villa is an American singer known for making political statements through her outfits at awards shows.[1] At the [[68th-annual-grammy-awards|68th Annual Grammy Awards]] in February 2026, her protest outfit took aim at Scientology, continuing her established pattern of red-carpet political expression.[1]

## Departure from Scientology

Villa left Scientology after 15 years. Writing in *Evie* magazine, she said that "from the outside, my life inside Scientology looked like a success story. Inside, it was slowly destroying me."[1] She described being visible, successful, and influential while the organization took credit for all of it, attributing every achievement "not to God, not to talent, not to perseverance, but to auditing, donations, and loyalty to the organization," so that "my success became propaganda. My life became marketing."[1]

Explaining her decision to speak publicly, Villa said that "silence protects systems of abuse" and that "no one should be interrogated for praying or punished for seeking a church."[1] She described herself as fully surrendered to Jesus Christ and rebaptized, and committed to helping others recover from cult abuse and coercive control through her nonprofit, The Fearless Joy Foundation, which supports survivors in rebuilding their lives, reclaiming their faith, and finding peace.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited Villa's protest as one of three encouraging moments at the ceremony.[1]
