# Lawrence Walsh

Lawrence Walsh was the Special Counsel appointed to investigate and prosecute the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra affair]].[1] His report, cited by Joe Lange from Volume One, Chapter 29, records that during the Vietnam War [[donald-gregg|Donald Gregg]] supervised CIA officer [[felix-rodriguez|Felix Rodriguez]], that the two kept in contact after the war, and that Gregg introduced Rodriguez to Vice President [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] in January 1985, with further meetings in Washington in May 1986 and in Miami on May 20, 1986.[1] The report also notes that Rodriguez had participated as a teenager in the [[bay-of-pigs|Bay of Pigs]] invasion and remained an ardent anti-communist afterward.[1]

Lange, who says he has read a great deal of the Walsh report, argues that it contains statements that are not true — probably because the scope Walsh was limited to prevented him from examining subjects that would have exposed those statements as false.[1] In Lange's assessment almost all of the CIA operatives involved in Iran-Contra gave false testimony, hiding something and protecting someone, in keeping with the maxim "admit nothing and deny everything."[1]

Walsh's office won convictions against [[oliver-north|Oliver North]] and John Poindexter, but both were overturned on appeal because [[congress|Congress]] had granted the two men immunity to testify — over Walsh's pleas that it not do so.[1] [[richard-secord|Richard Secord]], who ran the enterprise channeling money from the Iran arms sales to the Contras, ultimately pleaded guilty to a single felony count of misleading Congress in a plea bargain that dismissed eleven other charges.[1]
