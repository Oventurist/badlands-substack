# Neville Roy Singham

Neville Roy Singham is a China-based technology entrepreneur whose multimillion-dollar funding network became the subject of a federal grand jury investigation in Manhattan in 2026.[1]

## Grand jury investigation

The grand jury is investigating Singham for potential financial crimes tied to the funding of socialist, communist, and Marxist organizations across the United States over roughly the past decade.[1] The probe was initiated by US Attorney [[jay-clayton|Jay Clayton]] for the Southern District of New York and authorized by Acting US Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]], focusing on possible wire fraud, bank fraud, money laundering, and related violations.[1] Prosecutors have issued subpoenas for bank records and financial documents from organizations in the network.[1]

The grand jury action followed a mid-March [[fox-news|Fox News]] Digital investigation reporting that Singham routed hundreds of millions of dollars from his Shanghai base into US nonprofits, media outlets, and activist groups promoting identity politics, sectarian division, and socialist causes.[1]

## Alleged money flows

Singham allegedly moved approximately $278 million from Shanghai through three primary channels: $164,040,000 to Mutod LLC, a now-defunct shell company established in 2017 in Chicago; $110,376,701 to the GS Donor Advised Philanthropy Fund For Wealth Management Inc., [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]]' philanthropy arm in New York City; and $3,500,000 to Likewise Conceptions LLC, another now-defunct shell set up in 2017 in Crystal Lake, Illinois.[1]

Those entities are said to have directed funds into six key nonprofits: $167,540,000 to People's Support Foundation Ltd., a Chicago 501(c)(3) with Singham's wife on the board; $68,748,701 to Justice and Education Fund Inc., a New York 501(c)(3) with self-described communists on the board; $22,440,000 to [[peoples-forum|People's Forum Inc.]]; $16,760,000 to [[tricontinental|Tricontinental Ltd.]], a Massachusetts 501(c)(3) founded by Marxist activist [[vijay-prashad|Vijay Prashad]]; $1,330,000 to [[codepink|CodePink Women For Peace]], founded by Singham's wife and ally [[medea-benjamin|Medea Benjamin]]; and $1,098,000 to Breakthrough BT Media Inc., a pro-communist outlet linked to the network of longtime activist [[brian-becker|Brian Becker]].[1]

These nonprofits reportedly funnelled at least $223 million, plus additional support, into a broader global network including the People's Welfare Association — a 501(c)(4) channeling grants worldwide — the [[answer-coalition|ANSWER Coalition]], the [[party-for-socialism-and-liberation|Party for Socialism and Liberation]], and various unidentified groups.[1]

## Treasury pressure on Goldman Sachs

Sources indicated that Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] met with Goldman Sachs Chairman and CEO [[david-solomon|David Solomon]] earlier in the year in New York, reportedly delivering a direct message urging cooperation with investigators and warning of potential scrutiny over the role of the firm's philanthropy fund in moving Singham's money.[1]

## State Department report on Cuba (2026)

A [[state-department|State Department]] report previewed in July 2026 is expected to discuss Singham's financial backing for several left-wing groups, including [[codepink|Code Pink]] and the [[peoples-forum|People's Forum]], as part of an allegation that [[cuba|Cuba]]'s communist government has spent decades supporting radical left-wing movements in the United States and the Western Hemisphere.[2] The report alleges those organizations promoted positions aligned with the Cuban government and other authoritarian regimes opposed to US interests, and it arrived as Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] made countering transnational left-wing political influence a priority.[2]
