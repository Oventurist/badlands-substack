# Neville Roy Singham

Neville Roy Singham is a China-based technology entrepreneur described in the Badlands Media corpus as the principal financier of a multimillion-dollar network funding socialist, communist and Marxist organizations in the United States.

## Federal grand jury investigation (2026)

[[badlands-brief-169|"Condemnation, Calibration, & Constitutional Boxes"]] reports that a federal grand jury in Manhattan is investigating Singham for potential financial crimes tied to the funding of such organizations over the preceding decade. The probe was initiated by U.S. Attorney [[jay-clayton|Jay Clayton]] for the Southern District of New York and authorized by Acting U.S. Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]]. It focuses on possible wire fraud, bank fraud, money laundering and related violations, and prosecutors have issued subpoenas for bank records and financial documents from organizations across the network. The grand jury action followed a [[fox-news|Fox News]] Digital investigation in mid-March that described how Singham routed hundreds of millions of dollars from his Shanghai base into U.S. nonprofits, media outlets and activist groups promoting identity politics, sectarian division and socialist causes.

## The alleged funding architecture

According to the figures presented in the Brief, Singham moved approximately $278 million from Shanghai through three primary channels: $164,040,000 to Mutod LLC, a now-defunct shell company established in Chicago in 2017; $110,376,701 to the GS Donor Advised Philanthropy Fund For Wealth Management Inc., the New York philanthropy arm of [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]]; and $3,500,000 to Likewise Conceptions LLC, another now-defunct shell established in 2017 in Crystal Lake, Illinois.

Those entities in turn directed money to six nonprofits: $167,540,000 to People's Support Foundation Ltd., a Chicago 501(c)(3) with Singham's wife on the board; $68,748,701 to Justice and Education Fund Inc., a New York 501(c)(3) described as having self-described communists on its board; $22,440,000 to [[peoples-forum|People's Forum Inc.]]; $16,760,000 to [[tricontinental|Tricontinental Ltd.]], founded by [[vijay-prashad|Vijay Prashad]]; $1,330,000 to [[codepink|CodePink Women For Peace]], founded by Singham's wife and ally [[medea-benjamin|Medea Benjamin]]; and $1,098,000 to Breakthrough BT Media Inc., linked to the network of [[brian-becker|Brian Becker]]. The Brief states that these nonprofits passed at least $223 million onward into a broader global network including the People's Welfare Association, a 501(c)(4) channeling grants worldwide, the [[answer-coalition|ANSWER Coalition]] and the [[party-for-socialism-and-liberation|Party for Socialism and Liberation]].

## Treasury pressure on Goldman Sachs

The source further reports that Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] met with Goldman Sachs Chairman and CEO [[david-solomon|David Solomon]] earlier in 2026 in New York, reportedly delivering a direct message urging cooperation with investigators and warning of potential scrutiny of the firm's donor-advised philanthropy fund for its role in moving Singham's money.
