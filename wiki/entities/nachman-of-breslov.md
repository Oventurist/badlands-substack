# Nachman of Breslov

Rebbe Nachman of Breslov (1772–1810) was a Hasidic leader in the Pale of Settlement and the great-grandson of [[baal-shem-tov|Israel ben Eliezer, the Baal Shem Tov]], the father of Hasidic Judaism.[1] He founded the movement that became [[breslov-hasidism|Breslov Hasidism]].[1]

## Teachings

Nachman was known for his parables, which drew on well-known Eastern European folk stories and were deeply infused with [[kabbalah|Kabbalistic]] mystical imagery.[1] The Badlands account credits him above all with connecting Kabbalah to the Torah — the step by which, in that telling, a mystical tradition originating in Central Asia among the Turkmen became attached to the Biblical stories of the ancient Israelites, a fusion the writer dates to after the American Revolution.[1]

Although Nachman taught his followers that they should pray only to God, for a period he claimed to be the Messiah, unlike his great-grandfather, who never did.[1] He was said to have suffered intense mood swings and is believed to have had severe chronic depression and possible bipolar disorder.[1] His teachings led to his exile from his home in Breslov by fellow Jews.[1]

## Uman gravesite

Nachman died in Uman, Ukraine, in 1810, and his gravesite is now regarded as a holy site.[1] Throughout his life he told his followers that the most important thing they could do was to spend Rosh Hashanah, the Jewish New Year, in his physical presence — a teaching that today draws tens of thousands of Israeli citizens on annual pilgrimage from Israel to Uman.[1] The Badlands News Brief notes that the pilgrimage has repeatedly created friction between the Israeli and Ukrainian governments given the ongoing conflict with Russia, citing the September 2022 case in which Ukrainian authorities turned away the convicted Breslov leader [[eliezer-berland|Eliezer Berland]] at the border.[1]
