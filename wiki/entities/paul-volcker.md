# Paul Volcker

Paul Volcker was head of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] when [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] came into office in 1981.[1]

Reagan inherited an economy struggling with high inflation, and Volcker was battling it by raising interest rates — a situation Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]], writing in 2023, compares to conditions in his own time.[1] One industry in particular struggled badly under those high rates: the savings and loan sector, which was then restricted to residential lending funded by depositors.[1] That squeeze became the stated justification for the banking [[deregulation|deregulation]] pursued by the [[task-force-on-regulatory-relief|Task Force on Regulatory Relief]], allowing thrifts to make riskier commercial investments — the change Lange identifies as the catalyst of the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan crisis]].[1]

## "Controlled disintegration of the US economy"

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that the great liberalization of world commerce, begun in waves through the 1970s, moved into high gear with Volcker's interest rate hikes of 1980–82.[2] Their effects annihilated much of the small and medium-sized entrepreneurial base, opened the speculative gates into the savings and loan debacle, and helped cartelize mineral, food and financial institutions into ever greater behemoths.[2] Ehret quotes Volcker as describing this process as the "controlled disintegration of the US economy" upon becoming Fed chairman in 1978.[2] Raising interest rates to 20–21% not only shut down the lifeblood of much of the US economic base but also threw the third world into greater debt slavery as nations had to pay usurious interest on US loans.[2] The hikes form part of the sequence Ehret traces from the end of [[bretton-woods-system|Bretton Woods]] through the 1986 [[big-bang-deregulation|Big Bang]] to the destruction of [[glass-steagall|Glass-Steagall]].[2]

## Trilateral Commission ties

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 2" reports that [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]]'s selection of Volcker to head the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] further fanned speculation about Trilateralist control of U.S. policy, because Volcker was reportedly appointed on instructions from [[david-rockefeller|David Rockefeller]].[3] Volcker had served as North American chairman of the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] and was also a member of the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] and the [[bilderberg-group|Bilderberg group]].[3] He was replaced as Federal Reserve chairman during the [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]] administration by [[alan-greenspan|Alan Greenspan]], who belonged to the same three organizations.[3]
