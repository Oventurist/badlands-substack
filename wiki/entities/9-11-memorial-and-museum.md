# 9/11 Memorial and Museum

The National September 11 Memorial and Museum, located at the ground zero site in New York City, is the institution that hosts the annual rite of remembrance for the victims of the [[september-11-attacks|September 11 attacks]].[1]

## Annual commemoration

On September 11, 2025 — the 24th such gathering — survivors and victims' families assembled at the memorial for an hours-long recitation of nearly 3,000 names.[1] The ceremony features bagpipes, the national anthem, and moments of silence, with relatives of the dead taking turns in the recitation.[1] Children are among those who read names; to take part, family members enter their names into a lottery managed by the museum.[1]

The New York Times reported that some readers are the children of children who lost parents on September 11.[1] In 2024, ten-year-old Kylie Corrigan recited the name of her grandfather, James J. Corrigan, 60, a retired New York fire captain who was director of fire safety at the twin towers, saying "We love you so much and we miss you so much" and adding that she and her sisters "wish we got to meet you."[1] Two of Kylie's sisters, Keira and Megan, then 21 and 19, had served as readers in earlier years, and their father and his brother became firefighters after the attacks.[1]

## Generational framing

The [[new-york-times|New York Times]] observed that students today see September 11 as history, belonging to a post-Cold War generation for whom terrorism may be a more familiar threat than nuclear annihilation, and whose defining event has been the coronavirus pandemic.[1]
