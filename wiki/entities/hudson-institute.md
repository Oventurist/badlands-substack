# Hudson Institute

The Hudson Institute is a Washington-based foreign policy think tank. Badlands contributor Ryan DeLarme described it as "notorious" and characterized it as one of "a college of think tanks comprising the international deep political milieu" — a network of nominally independent policy institutes that, in his reading, shape state behavior on behalf of entrenched transatlantic interests.[1]

## Platform for European hawks

The Institute served as the venue for remarks by [[kaja-kallas|Kaja Kallas]], the former Estonian prime minister and the European Union's High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, in which she argued that Europe must continue to confront Russia through Ukraine even if the United States chose to withdraw its support.[1] Kallas rejected American commentary suggesting Russia could not be beaten, and tied credibility on Russia to credibility on China, asking how Washington could claim it would defend Asian partners against a larger Chinese military if it conceded that the West collectively could not pressure Russia effectively.[1]

DeLarme presented the appearance as evidence that the transatlantic think-tank circuit functions as a conveyor belt for war advocacy, arguing that "the international elite thrive on endless war" and that the [[european-union|EU]], [[nato|NATO]], and the American CIA–Pentagon–State Department nexus had long been paving the way toward conflict with both Russia and China.[1]

## Herman Kahn and Cold War research

In *The Men Behind the Curtain — Part 7*, Badlands Media describes the Hudson Institute under the direction of [[herman-kahn|Herman Kahn]] — covered earlier in the series as one of the men behind the rise of [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]] — as having done more to shape how Americans react to political and social events, think, vote and generally conduct themselves than perhaps any other entity apart from the five major media conglomerates.[2] Hudson specialized in defense policy research and relations with the USSR and was, in the article's framing, an instrumental cog in the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]], with most of its military work classified "SECRET."[2] Early papers included "Stability and Tranquility Among Older Nations" and "Analytical Summary of U.S. National Security Policy Issues."[2]

The article states that Hudson prides itself on its diversity, having helped NASA with its space programs and having promoted new youth fashions and ideas, youth rebellion and alienation, ostensibly funded by Coca-Cola; it classifies the institute as one of the cabal's "brainwashing establishments."[2] Among its nuclear war scenarios the author recommends "The 6 Basic Thermonuclear Threats," "Possible Outcomes of Thermonuclear War," and a paper titled "Israeli-Arab Nuclear War."[2] Hudson also performed corporate advising for firms including Rank, Xerox, General Electric, IBM and General Motors, though its largest client remained the U.S. Department of Defense on matters of civil defense, national security, military policy and arms control.[2] The institute is likewise named in the article's catalogue of [[tax-exempt-foundations|tax-exempt foundations]] tied to the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[2]
