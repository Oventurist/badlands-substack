# Robin DiAngelo

Robin DiAngelo is an American author and academic best known for the book *[[white-fragility|White Fragility]]*, a text that became a fixture of corporate diversity programming and antiracist reading lists in the United States.[1] In August 2024 she was accused of plagiarising minority writers in her doctoral work, an allegation reported by Breitbart and covered in the Badlands News Brief.[1]

## Plagiarism allegations

The *Washington Free Beacon* obtained a complaint filed with the University of Washington outlining 20 instances of alleged plagiarism in DiAngelo's 2004 doctoral thesis, *Whiteness in Racial Dialogue: A Discourse Analysis*.[1] Among the examples cited were two paragraphs reproduced almost entirely from Northeastern University scholar Thomas Nakayama — who is Asian-American — and his coauthor Robert Krizek, in which DiAngelo was said to have failed to provide adequate attribution.[1]

Another example in the complaint concerned a paragraph written by Asian-American professor Stacey Lee of the University of Wisconsin-Madison; rather than clearly delineating that Lee had summarised the work of scholar David Theo Goldberg, the material was allegedly presented in such a way as to suggest DiAngelo herself was providing the summary.[1]

Peter Wood, president of the National Association of Scholars, said DiAngelo's work rises to the level of "forgery" in certain circles, adding that "it is never appropriate to use the secondary source without acknowledging it, and even worse to present it as one's own words. That's plagiarism."[1] University of Washington spokeswoman Dana Robinson Slote told the *New York Post*, "We are committed to the integrity of research conducted at the University of Washington. All complaints are carefully reviewed."[1]

## Rise to prominence

As the *Free Beacon* noted, DiAngelo remained a relatively obscure professor after the publication of her book until the death of [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] in 2020 propelled her to national stardom.[1] Reporting cited in the brief described her as "a staple of teacher trainings, corporate affinity groups, fundraisers, and antiracist book clubs."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for Badlands, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] recalled that in the summer of 2020, as "Racial Justice Task Force" affinity groups spread through her firm and her clients' companies, *White Fragility* became required reading, and that declining to read it was itself treated as evidence of fragility and fear of confronting one's own whiteness.[1] She argued that DiAngelo, celebrated as a heroine of racial justice, had allegedly stolen the intellectual property of minority writers and presented it as her own, speculating that DiAngelo may have assumed the words would carry greater impact coming from a privileged white woman than from their original authors — describing the dynamic as a "white savior complex."[1]

Ashe predicted that defenders would rationalise the alleged plagiarism as theft undertaken to help its victims, and characterised DiAngelo as "one of the key architects of weaponized empathy as a means for social change" who, on the new allegations, "is a fraud."[1]
