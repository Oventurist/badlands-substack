# Small Business Administration

The Small Business Administration (SBA) is the U.S. federal agency responsible for supporting small business lending, contracting and disaster assistance, and is treated as a Cabinet-level post in the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1]

## 2025 leadership

Former Georgia Senator [[kelly-loeffler|Kelly Loeffler]] was Trump's pick to serve as SBA administrator; the [[united-states-senate|Senate]] invoked cloture on her nomination in February 2025 alongside that of Commerce nominee [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]], setting up confirmation votes after the Presidents Day recess.[1] The agency had previously been led during Trump's first term by [[linda-mcmahon|Linda McMahon]], later nominated to head the [[department-of-education|Education Department]].[1]

## 2024 Michigan voter-registration controversy

Under the Biden administration the SBA became the focus of congressional scrutiny over the use of federal resources for voter registration.[2] A House subcommittee hearing on June 4, 2024, titled "Weaponizing Federal Resources — SBA Voter Registration," examined a "first of its kind" agreement between the agency and the state of Michigan carried out under a March 2021 executive order directing federal agencies to "get out the vote."[2] Badlands Media covered the hearing, which was largely overshadowed the same day by Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]]'s testimony before the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] and by a heavily attended abortion hearing.[2]

The administration insisted the initiative was a bipartisan endeavor, but House Republicans spent the hearing arguing the opposite.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported that the Michigan agreement targeted must-win areas for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] while apparently not pursuing registration in redder parts of the state, and that to the subcommittee's knowledge the administration had approached no jurisdiction that was must-win for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[2] Her conclusion was that the executive order and its execution in Michigan violated the [[hatch-act|Hatch Act]] by turning the federal government into campaign infrastructure, and that Republicans had multiple avenues to take the matter to court.[2] She highlighted the questioning by Representative [[celeste-malloy|Celeste Malloy]] of Utah as the clearest treatment of the issue.[2]

## PPP fraud and suspect Social Security numbers

In an alert issued on January 30, 2023, the [[pandemic-response-accountability-committee|Pandemic Response Accountability Committee]] estimated that roughly $5.4 billion in coronavirus relief may have gone to small businesses whose applications carried potentially ineligible Social Security numbers, an outcome attributed to the speed with which the SBA processed aid early in the pandemic.[3] Reporting noted that the agency had spared millions of firms from financial ruin but that its haste produced costly errors, and that it had already granted full or partial forgiveness to more than 93 percent of Paycheck Protection Program recipients.[3] The finding surfaced as House Republicans prepared their first hearing into the roughly $5 trillion in federal stimulus aid approved since spring 2020.[3] Badlands Media contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that the funds frequently flowed to organizations implementing destructive COVID policies and described the programs as payoffs to those willing to advance the agenda.[3]

## Disaster loan funding strain (October 2024)

As [[hurricane-milton|Hurricane Milton]] approached Florida in October 2024, less than two weeks after [[hurricane-helene|Hurricane Helene]], the SBA's loan programs for stricken businesses and homeowners were identified — alongside [[fema|FEMA]]'s disaster fund and flood insurance program — as among the federal disaster programs facing financial instability and potentially within weeks of running out of cash.[4] Lawmakers of both parties urged Congress to return early to approve additional disaster funding, but Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] said he had no plans to recall members before the November election.[4]
