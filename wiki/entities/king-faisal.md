# King Faisal of Saudi Arabia

Faisal bin Abdulaziz Al Saud was King of Saudi Arabia from 1964 until his assassination in 1975. In Badlands Media's telling he is a pivotal figure linking the Kennedy presidency, the birth of the petrodollar, and the modern Saudi state under his great-nephew [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]].[1]

## Meeting with John F. Kennedy

While still Crown Prince, Faisal met in secret with President [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] in 1961. During that meeting Kennedy persuaded Faisal to see the Saudi people as his nation's most precious commodity, and therefore as the proper object of the royal family's vast wealth.[1]

## Reign and domestic reform

Faisal became King the year after Kennedy's assassination, and Badlands holds that he honored the late president by following his guidance: abolishing slavery in Saudi Arabia and imposing a ten-point plan that included funding free health care among other programs for the population.[1]

## Oil embargo, the petrodollar, and assassination

Faisal waged an oil embargo against the West as punishment for its support of Israel in the Yom Kippur War. Two years after Secretary of State [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] coerced him into signing the agreement that created the petrodollar, Faisal met the same fate as Kennedy: in 1975 he was assassinated by a young nephew who had reportedly just returned from a long trip to the United States and who was said to be mentally ill.[1]

## Legacy

Faisal's son, Prince [[turki-al-faisal|Turki Al Faisal]], remains a senior Saudi figure and a top advisor to MBS.[1]
