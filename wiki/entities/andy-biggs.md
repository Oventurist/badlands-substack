# Andy Biggs

Andy Biggs is a Republican U.S. Representative from Arizona.[1]

In April 2023 Biggs joined Rep. [[alex-mooney|Alex Mooney]] (R-WV) and Rep. [[paul-gosar|Paul Gosar]] (R-AZ) as an original cosponsor of H.R. 2435, the [[gold-standard-restoration-act|Gold Standard Restoration Act]], a bill to repeg the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] note to a fixed weight of gold bullion following a 24-month disclosure of federal gold holdings and transactions.[1] The measure was presented as a response to inflation, bank failures, and long-term monetary instability.[1]

## January 5 pipe bomb oversight

Biggs, who chaired the Subcommittee on Crime and Federal Government Surveillance, joined [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] and Representative [[bill-posey|Bill Posey]] in a May 2023 letter to [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] concerning the [[january-5-2021-pipe-bombs|pipe bombs placed at the DNC and RNC]] on January 5, 2021.[2] The letter documented briefing requests dating back to September 2021 that the bureau had ignored for more than a year and a half, and referenced a whistleblower disclosure questioning the "unusual" nature of the FBI's investigation.[2]

Reporting on the introduction identified Biggs and Gosar as the two Arizona Republicans joining Mooney on the measure, which was framed as a sound-money answer to inflation and bank failures.[3]

## January 2023 speakership concessions

Biggs was one of the twenty-one holdouts whose votes forced the [[january-2023-mccarthy-concessions|January 2023 McCarthy concessions]].[4] He kept his places on the powerful [[house-judiciary-committee|Judiciary]] and [[house-oversight-committee|Oversight]] committees and was named chairman of Judiciary's subcommittee on Crime and Federal Government Surveillance.[4]
