# Nikol Pashinyan

Nikol Pashinyan is the Prime Minister of [[armenia|Armenia]] and, as of 2026, a close diplomatic partner of the United States under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

In a Truth Social post in May 2026, Trump described Pashinyan as "a great friend and Leader" who is making his country "strong, wealthy, and very secure," and said Pashinyan completely shared his vision of peace and prosperity for Armenia and the entire South Caucasus region.[1] Trump noted that Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] had recently travelled to Armenia to advance several deals between the two countries, and announced that the United States and Armenia would soon break ground together on the [[trump-route-for-international-peace-and-prosperity|Trump Route for International Peace and Prosperity]].[1]

Trump gave Pashinyan his "COMPLETE and TOTAL Endorsement" for re-election on June 7, 2026, closing with the slogan "Make (Armenia) Great Again — MAGA!"[1] Badlands commentary read the endorsement and the associated corridor project as part of a wider US move to plug into Eurasian rail and energy networks running through the South Caucasus and Central Asia.[1]

## Washington corridor agreement (August 2025)

On Friday, August 8, 2025, Pashinyan joined Azerbaijani President [[ilham-aliyev|Ilham Aliyev]] and US President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in Washington to sign an agreement opening the [[zangezur-corridor|Zangezur transport corridor]], a route linking mainland [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]] to its exclave of Nakhichevan through a narrow strip of southern [[armenia|Armenia]] along the Iranian border.[2] Under the terms, the United States would manage the corridor under Armenian sovereignty via a 99-year land lease, subletting it to a consortium for construction and operations.[2] [[iran|Iran]] condemned the arrangement as a threat to regional stability, saying it welcomed peace between Yerevan and Baku but "vehemently" opposed placing the corridor under US control.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that both Pashinyan and Aliyev had earlier worked to entrap [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in a quagmire when, in 2020, Russia deployed peacekeeping troops to the same corridor to halt fighting between the two countries.[2] The commentary argued that both leaders appeared "giddy" because they believed the deal threatened Iran, while noting that allegations of [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]] already being used by the IDF to launch drone strikes on Tehran made claims about Armenia becoming a new attack platform feel redundant.[2] The contributor said the corridor's fate would be clarified by the behavior of Pashinyan and Aliyev after the [[2025-trump-putin-alaska-summit|Trump–Putin meeting in Alaska]] scheduled for that Friday.[2]
