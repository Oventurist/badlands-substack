# Tesla

Tesla is the American electric vehicle manufacturer led by chief executive [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]. By late 2023 the company was described as "officially thinking past electric vehicles," expanding into artificial intelligence infrastructure and custom silicon rather than confining itself to automobile production.[1]

## Dojo and in-house silicon

Tesla was reported in October 2023 to be constructing a "bunkerlike" structure in Texas to house part of a new supercomputer the company calls [[dojo-supercomputer|Dojo]], a machine intended to run the artificial intelligence software behind the self-driving capabilities in its vehicles.[1] The project was characterized as "an audacious plan by Musk to take greater control over the technology it needs to run the AI software at the heart of its products," and reporting indicated the resulting compute capacity could eventually be sold as cloud services to other companies.[1]

Musk stated he planned to invest "well over" $1 billion in the effort, which is built around an in-house chip designed by Tesla and known as the D1.[1] At the time, Tesla's vehicles relied on artificial intelligence chips supplied by [[nvidia|Nvidia]]; a primary motivator for developing the D1 was to decrease that dependency, a sentiment echoed by other technology giants including [[amazon-web-services|Amazon Web Services]] and Microsoft.[1] Musk acknowledged that Tesla would have stayed with Nvidia had the chipmaker been able to meet Tesla's growing demands, and the stated plan going forward was to use both Nvidia and D1 chips within Dojo.[1]

Glenn O'Donnell, a semiconductor researcher at the technology research firm Forrester, framed the industry-wide constraint driving these decisions: "Everybody who is trying to do this is complaining that they can't get enough of these chips."[1] Tesla's move toward a "robotaxi" product line was cited as the reason the company had concluded it would need significantly more computing power.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the Dojo buildout as a "fairly fascinating development," describing a proprietary, highly secure data center with in-house chips, and argued that while transhumanist concerns about such projects are sometimes understandable, Musk has "consistently been a thorn in the side of the Globalist Establishment."[1] The commentary framed the project as a possible longer-term play to keep highly valuable production corridors — including semiconductors and computer chips, characterized as the most-traded commodity in the modern world — outside the control of a globalist oligarchy beholden to ESG and DEI initiatives.[1]

## Exposure to the 2025 tariffs

In April 2025 White House trade adviser [[peter-navarro|Peter Navarro]] argued that Tesla was particularly vulnerable to the administration's new tariff regime, noting that although Teslas are manufactured in Texas factories, "they get a lot of their content from China, Mexico, Japan and Taiwan and elsewhere."[2] Navarro made the remarks while accusing chief executive [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] of "simply protecting his own interests" in criticizing the tariffs, after Musk lost roughly $11 billion in the ensuing market selloff.[2]

## We, Robot event (October 2024)

Tesla held its long-awaited robotaxi event, branded "We, Robot," in Burbank, California, on October 10, 2024, unveiling the Cybercab robotaxi, the futuristic Robovan concept, and the latest version of the Optimus humanoid robot.[3] The company's shares stumbled in premarket trading following the presentation.[3]

Wall Street analysts praised the product lineup as potentially revolutionary for transportation and other areas of the economy while criticizing the lack of technical detail.[3] A Goldman Sachs team led by Mark Delaney and Will Bryant said Tesla "demonstrated very strong progress with the Optimus humanoid robot, and we think the Cybercab looked attractive," but noted that limited Full Self Driving data, thin robotaxi business-plan details, and the absence of a lower-cost consumer vehicle "may be disappointing for some market participants."[3]

Badlands commentary framed the reaction as part of a pattern of premature media dismissal of Elon Musk's timelines, citing the Model Y's path from a missed 2019 target to becoming the world's bestselling vehicle by 2023, and noting that robotaxis were slated to enter production in 2025.[3] Commentary also highlighted decentralized household power packs as the most scalable element of Tesla's business plan.[3]

## Share slide after the America Party announcement (July 2025)

Tesla shares fell after chief executive [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] announced over the weekend of July 5–6, 2025 that he was forming a new political party, the [[america-party|America Party]], to contest House and Senate seats over the following 12 months.[4] The stock slid 6.8% on Monday, July 7 in New York, wiping $15.3 billion from Musk's net worth according to the Bloomberg Billionaires Index, in what reporting described as a political pursuit that had already been a drag on his most valuable business.[4]

## 2024 shareholder vote on Musk's pay package

At Tesla's annual meeting on Thursday, June 13, 2024, the company announced it had won shareholder approval for [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s compensation package after weeks of campaigning.[5] Musk celebrated with a victory dance on stage, but the vote resolved only the first battle: Tesla had to take the matter back to court before the package could be restored, because a Delaware judge struck down the 2018 plan in January 2024 following a shareholder lawsuit alleging the agreement was "beyond the bounds of reasonable judgment."[5] See [[elon-musk-tesla-pay-package-vote]].

## Attack on Gigafactory Berlin (May 2024)

In May 2024 hundreds of demonstrators described as "anticapitalist" and "woke Marxist extremists" stormed Tesla's [[gigafactory-berlin-brandenburg|Gigafactory Berlin-Brandenburg]] near Grünheide, Germany.[6] Commentators on X noted that most of the protesters appeared to come from outside the area and objected to capitalism in general rather than Tesla specifically, and asked why factories producing luxury vehicles such as the BMW 7 Series, Mercedes S-Class and Maybach were not targeted.[6] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] responded, "Something super weird is going on, as Tesla was the *only* car company attacked!"[6]

## Manufacturing as the product

In December 2024, Launch i/o CEO Jeff Lutz — a former executive at [[google|Google]] and Motorola — argued that one of the most significant and under-discussed developments at Tesla was occurring not in its design studios or on the road but in its factories.[7] Lutz contended that Tesla's true innovation was not the electric vehicles or robots it builds but how those products are made, describing a first-principles approach to manufacturing that departs radically from industry norms by rethinking the entire production process rather than relying on cheap labor or existing models.[7] In this account Tesla treats its factories as the product itself, designing, testing and perfecting every element just as it does with its cars, a focus on manufacturing efficiency that Lutz believed would dramatically reduce production costs — potentially toward zero — and set a new standard for the wider manufacturing world.[7]
