# Amy G. Elliott

Amy G. Elliott was the [[citibank|Citibank]] private banker who handled the accounts of [[raul-salinas-de-gortari|Raúl Salinas de Gortari]], brother of Mexican president [[carlos-salinas-de-gortari|Carlos Salinas de Gortari]].[1] According to the New York Times, Salinas "was introduced to the folkways of sophisticated money management" by Elliott, a Cuban-American who started in Citibank's human resources department in 1967 and worked her way up to head the New York-based unit that advised wealthy Mexicans.[1]

Under the strategy her colleagues devised, Salinas no longer needed aliases: money was moved to European banks and deposited under the names of untraceable Cayman Islands shell corporations established for him with Citibank's help, ultimately shifting more than $80 million out of Mexico into Swiss accounts.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats her biography as evidence rather than coincidence, noting that she was a Cuban exile who began working at Citibank in 1967 — the period he associates with [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]]'s worldwide drug-trafficking operations — and asking whether she was among the roughly 15,000 Cuban exiles trained and employed by the [[cia|CIA]] out of its Miami station under [[ted-shackley|Ted Shackley]].[1] His stated bet is that she was a CIA operative and "an untouchable," and that the agency was training not only accountants for the [[medellin-cartel|Medellín cartel]] but bankers to launder the money privately.[1]
