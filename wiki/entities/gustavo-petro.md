# Gustavo Petro

Gustavo Petro is the President of Colombia. In January 2026 he publicly criticized United States military actions in Latin America following Washington's operation involving Venezuela, warning about the regional consequences of expanded US force.[1]

## Coverage in the Badlands Brief

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry has repeatedly characterized Petro as "the righteous actor" in the regional drug war, arguing that Petro spent the three years preceding January 2026 actively targeting the cartels rather than accommodating them.[1] Petro posted a video to X recapping Colombian counter-narcotics operations, which Badlands cited as evidence for that reading.[1]

On the Wednesday night following [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]]'s cartel strike remarks, Trump posted to Truth Social that he had spoken with Petro by telephone and had invited him to the [[white-house|White House]] for a face-to-face meeting — a reversal that Badlands read as confirmation that the preceding public friction had been "kayfabe."[1]

## The ELN disclosure

A year before the January 2026 brief, Petro drew attention by disclosing that the [[national-liberation-army|National Liberation Army (ELN)]] — active in the region since 1963 and historically linked to [[pablo-escobar|Pablo Escobar]] and the [[medellin-cartel|Medellín Cartel]] — functioned as a proxy of the Mexican [[sinaloa-cartel|Sinaloa Cartel]], dispatched to secure cocaine smuggling routes through Central America.[1] Badlands contrasted this account with claims by what it called NeoCons and Zionists that [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] headed the ELN, asserting that no evidence had been offered for those claims.[1]

## Alleged 2026 assassination attempt

In February 2026, Petro announced that he had evaded an assassination attempt after intelligence revealed a plot to fire on his helicopter, forcing an abrupt route change.[2] Speaking at a government meeting broadcast live on the presidential administration's YouTube channel, he said the aircraft — carrying him along with his children — was rerouted to fly for four hours over the open sea before landing at an unplanned location: "I didn't land where I was supposed to because there were fears that the helicopter, in which my children were also located, would be fired upon."[2] Petro stated that he had "avoided being killed."[2] The report, originating from Buenos Aires and published by TASS on February 10, 2026, provided no additional details on the alleged perpetrators, investigation status, or specific threats involved.[2]

## Cartel proposal and confrontation with Colombian finance

The week before the disclosure, Petro met President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the White House and afterward held a press conference announcing that he had proposed an international joint operation to pursue the most powerful figures at the top of organized crime — bankers, financiers, and corporate executives — rather than only cartel foot soldiers.[2] At the same press conference he called out Colombian billionaire banker [[luis-carlos-sarmiento|Luis Carlos Sarmiento]], telling him he could "go F yourself" if he intended to obstruct a pipeline project with Venezuela that Petro had discussed with Trump.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cast Petro as "our guy," the most flippantly defiant world leader since Nicolas Maduro, and framed the sequence — cartel-finance proposal followed by an assassination attempt — as evidence that a "Sovereign Alliance" of benevolent strongmen willing to risk their lives is the foundation of the coming Golden Age.[2]

## Claims of joint operations with Trump

In March 2026 Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry recalled Colombian President Petro's claim that he had spoken with [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] about joint operations against the cartels, noting that U.S. Southern Command had just announced new operations against narco-terrorists in Ecuador — the precise location Petro had long described as the new hub of cartel operations.[3] Petro had also said that the real cartel leaders were not in the jungles but in major cities working for banks and corporations, and that he and Trump would be working together to target those entities as well.[3]

## Joint anti-cartel framework (2026)

In a post on X dated January 24, 2026, Petro laid out his position on transnational criminal networks; roughly a week later he met President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the [[white-house|White House]], proposing joint operations against the cartels not only in South America but worldwide.[4] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Petro had been pursuing these networks since 2022 and argued that US action in Mexico and [[ecuador|Ecuador]] — the two theaters Petro consistently identified as the core of the problem — indicated Trump was earnestly working with other world leaders to dismantle the [[global-cartel|cabal]].[4] The March 3, 2026 joint [[united-states-southern-command|SOUTHCOM]]-Ecuadorian operations against narco-terrorists were presented as an early product of that approach.[4]

## White House meeting, February 3, 2026

Petro met President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the [[white-house|White House]] on February 3, 2026 for their first in-person encounter, a private Oval Office session lasting more than two hours.[5] The American side included Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], and Senator [[bernie-moreno|Bernie Moreno]]; the Colombian delegation comprised Foreign Minister Rosa Villavicencio, Defense Minister Pedro Sánchez, and Ambassador Daniel García-Peña.[5] The tone and visuals were unexpectedly positive after a year of public tensions, threats, and insults between the two leaders.[5]

Trump said "we got along very well" and noted their prior unfamiliarity despite past friction, while Petro called himself "optimistic and positive" and reported discussions of "concrete problems and joint pathways."[5] Gifts were exchanged: Petro presented a colorful jaguar sculpture, and Trump gave a modified MAGA hat reading "Make Americas Great Again" along with a signed copy of *The Art of the Deal* inscribed "You are great" and "I love Colombia," which Petro promptly shared on X.[5] Talks covered counternarcotics cooperation, economic development near [[colombia|Colombia]]'s borders with [[venezuela|Venezuela]] and [[ecuador|Ecuador]], energy and oil, the Venezuelan border situation following the U.S. capture of [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], and potential guerrilla threats; general agreements were reached on counternarcotics and "sanctions" were mentioned, though Petro denied any discussion of personal sanctions against him.[5]

The meeting followed [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Treasury]] sanctions imposed in October 2025 on Petro, his family, and associates over alleged drug ties, which he denied, as well as his criticism of Maduro's capture as a sovereignty violation.[5] A late-January phone call facilitated by Senator [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] paved the way for the invitation.[5] Afterward Petro held a press conference stating that the next step in fighting the cartels is to target the major financiers and international structures operating outside Colombia.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] lamented that the meeting was not televised, calling Petro one of the more vibrant and entertaining figures in "TrumpaMania lore" and nicknaming him "Pepe Le Pew" for his self-description as a passionate romantic.[5] He also noted that both leaders independently said in interviews that they agreed to collaborate against the cartels, with Trump looking into lifting the recently imposed sanctions.[5] Reading the Treasury sanctions document, GhostofBasedPatrickHenry highlighted Colombia's suspension from an international financial-intelligence body — headquartered in Canada — that tracks financial-crime data shared between governments and forwards suspicious activity to the [[fbi|FBI]] and [[department-of-justice|DOJ]], triggered by Petro's disclosure of privileged information about the group; he treated the Canadian location as reinforcing "[[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] lore as the king of all central bankers."[5]

## Vindication in the 2026 Mexican indictments

When US prosecutors indicted the governor of Sinaloa and other Mexican officials for alleged collaboration with the [[sinaloa-cartel|Sinaloa Cartel]] in 2026, Badlands treated the case as confirmation of Petro's analysis and of the theory that he is a "white hat" working with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to dismantle the transnational cartel.[6] Petro had described Sinaloa as the "head of the snake" of the Colombian drug trade and disclosed that he and [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] had discussed a joint military operation against the cartels.[6] During a February visit to the White House he urged Trump to have the US lead operations targeting not only the Sinaloa Cartel and jungle militant groups but also the bankers laundering their proceeds.[6]

## Role in the cartel war narrative (February 2026)

Writing after the Jalisco raid that killed [[el-mencho|El Mencho]], Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that much of the cartels' infrastructure had already been severely hampered by the efforts of Petro, Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] and El Salvador's Nayib Bukele, among others.[7] He noted that Petro was calling for [[donald-trump|President Trump]] to lead a crusade against the bankers and financiers who support the cartels, even as the Mexican military moved against the cartels themselves inside [[mexico|Mexico]].[7]

## Challenge to the June 2026 election result

Following [[colombia|Colombia]]'s June 21, 2026 presidential election, won by [[abelardo-de-la-espriella|Abelardo de la Espriella]], Petro moved to challenge the certified outcome, reportedly claiming the real winner was [[ivan-cepeda|Iván Cepeda]] and alleging that the result had been manipulated using technology from California.[8] On July 10, 2026 the United States and twelve regional partners issued a joint statement through the [[shield-of-the-americas|Shield of the Americas]] coalition voicing alarm at his attempts, rejecting any effort to delegitimize the mandate or obstruct Colombia's institutional transition, and urging officials to respect the certified results.[8] Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] shared the statement publicly.[8]

## Ben-Gvir "true Nazi" remark (2026)

Petro accused Israeli National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] of acting "like a true Nazi" after Ben-Gvir posted video of detained [[global-sumud-flotilla|Global Sumud Flotilla]] activists, including Colombians, kneeling and handcuffed following [[israel|Israel]]'s interception of the convoy.[9] The flotilla reportedly carried about 430 international volunteers attempting to reach Gaza with humanitarian aid; Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] defended the interception but distanced himself from Ben-Gvir's treatment of the detainees and ordered deportations expedited.[9]

Badlands commentary credited Petro with showing considerable courage in his public statements on Israel in recent years, noting he has purportedly survived at least one attempt on his life.[9]

## Sinaloa allegations recounted (July 2026)

Badlands coverage in July 2026 recalled that in February 2025 Petro held a press conference with his generals at which he announced that the Colombian militias operating in the jungles against the Colombian government since the early 1960s were in fact proxy forces of the [[sinaloa-cartel|Sinaloa cartel]], which operates out of [[mexico|Mexico]].[10] According to Petro, the Sinaloa cartel is the head of the snake in the cocaine trade — a claim treated as prefiguring the Trump administration's subsequent indictment of ten Mexican officials over alleged cartel ties.[10]

## Mossad and California servers allegations (July 2026)

By early July 2026 Petro had escalated his challenge to the presidential result by refusing to recognize the legitimacy of president-elect [[abelardo-de-la-espriella|Abelardo de la Espriella]] and alleging that the vote was compromised by foreign interference.[11] In a series of posts on X he claimed Colombia's election systems were manipulated through servers located in California and alleged that Israel's intelligence agency, [[mossad|Mossad]], played a role in influencing the outcome.[11] He called for a comprehensive forensic audit, a recount, and an independent review of the election infrastructure before the results were treated as final.[11]

The allegations followed a closely contested runoff in which Petro-backed candidate [[ivan-cepeda|Iván Cepeda]] narrowly lost.[11] Petro has not publicly released evidence supporting the claims; Colombian electoral authorities defended the integrity of the vote and Israeli officials did not publicly respond.[11] In one widely circulated post Petro wrote that from a phone conversation with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] he confirmed Trump did not know he opposed de la Espriella "because of his old ties to drug trafficking and the genocide in Colombia," urging Trump to ask his intelligence agencies who de la Espriella is, why he is a US citizen, and who his partners are, including an associate under the alias "Boliche."[11] He went on to accuse Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Mossad directly of being behind the operation, and to allege that Netanyahu and bad actors in the US government were aiding the [[clan-del-golfo|Clan del Golfo]]'s attempt to return to power.[11]

Petro also accused de la Espriella of planning to restore the tax and monetary policies of former president [[ivan-duque|Iván Duque]].[11] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the story as potentially one of the most explosive narratives of the year, capable of unravelling the election-fraud apparatus in the United States, and noted that fifty minutes before Petro's long post Trump had published a photograph of the restored [[simon-bolivar|Simón Bolívar]] statue to Truth Social.[11]

## Trump threats after the Maduro capture (January 2026)

Following the US raid that captured [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], President Trump extended similar threats to [[colombia|Colombia]], calling Petro a "sick man" who enables the cocaine trade and suggesting a comparable raid "sounds good."[12] Trump also said in a widely noted remark that Petro had better "watch his ass."[12] Petro vowed he would "take up arms" in defense of Colombia, demanded loyalty from Colombian officers, and rallied Colombians against what he called American imperialists, calling Trump a coward and telling him "Come and get me" in an exchange one Badlands writer likened to a professional wrestling soap opera.[12]

At a rally held after Maduro's arrest, Petro claimed that "a clan of pedophiles" — specifically "friends of [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]]" — were attempting to take over Colombia and Venezuela and had to be stopped.[12] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Petro is in fact at the tip of the spear of the war against the cartels, contrary to Trump's characterization of him as a cartel drug lord, and folded the theatrics into the [[trumpamania|TrumpaMania]] frame.[12]

## Egmont Group suspension allegation

Petro was sanctioned by the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Department of the Treasury]] in 2025, and the sanctioning document accused him of getting Colombia's financial crimes intelligence unit suspended from the [[egmont-group|Egmont Group]] for allegedly sharing information he had collected from Egmont servers.[13] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that both Petro and Nicolás Maduro have accused the most powerful banks in the United States and Europe of literally being the cartels, with Petro arguing that institutions like the Egmont Group allow that criminality to continue and prosper because they cover for it.[13]

## "Bosses of bosses" remarks (February 2026)

In a press conference delivered entirely in Spanish following his meeting with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Petro said he had spoken with Trump specifically about joining forces to go after the bankers who serve as the "bosses of bosses" in charge of the cartels.[14] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] obtained and translated a transcript of the remarks and read through them on his show, presenting them alongside disclosures about [[ubs|UBS]] and [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]] as evidence of a coordinated move against the financial layer of organized crime.[14]

## Opposition to Machado's Nobel Peace Prize (October 2025)

Over the weekend following the announcement of the [[2025-nobel-peace-prize|2025 Nobel Peace Prize]], Petro posted on X about a December 2018 letter that laureate [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] had sent to Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] through the Argentinian embassy in Venezuela seeking help in overthrowing [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[15] Petro asserted that the letter disqualified her from the prize and accused her of attempting to bring "the Zionist genocide" to South America, while also noting that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] appeared to be distancing himself from Netanyahu.[15] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], who had previously reported Petro's assertion that Trump's war appeared to be aimed at Colombia rather than Venezuela, took the intervention as further evidence that Petro is "a [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] bro and coordinating with Putin," and observed that Petro was now joining the Trump White House in blasting Machado as unfit for the award.[15]

## February 2025 remarks on cocaine

In February 2025, Petro claimed that cocaine is no worse than whiskey, arguing that the drug's illegality derives from its Latin American origin rather than its effects.[16] Speaking during a cabinet meeting, he said, "Cocaine is illegal not because it's worse than whiskey, but because it's produced in Latin America," a comparison apparently chosen because the United States is a leading producer and exporter of whiskey.[16] Colombia has long been associated with cocaine production, with significant portions of the global supply originating there from the coca plant native to Latin America.[16]

The remarks came amid tensions with the administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over deportation policy and threatened trade tariffs, following a public dispute over the repatriation of Colombian nationals deported from the United States.[16] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the statement as notable mainly for its comic value in the context of that spat.[16]
