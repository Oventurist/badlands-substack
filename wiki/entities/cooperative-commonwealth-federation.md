# Cooperative Commonwealth Federation

The Cooperative Commonwealth Federation (CCF) was a Canadian political party established in 1933 by the [[league-for-social-reconstruction|League for Social Reconstruction]], the formal name of what Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] calls the Fabian Society of Canada.[1]

## Founding and the Haldeman connection

When the organization created the CCF in 1933, [[joshua-haldeman|Joshua Haldeman]] — the future grandfather of [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] — was assigned to establish the Saskatchewan branch in the Assiniboia federal constituency, where Ehret notes he would have interfaced with some or all of the five Oxford Rhodes Scholars who founded the organization.[1] Ehret's footnote identifies the Canadian Rhodes Trust network behind the League as centring on [[escott-reid|Escott Reid]], Frank Underhill, Eugene Forsey, F. R. Scott and David Lewis, who founded a self-described "Fabian modeled think tank" for Canada, and records that the group selected J. S. Woodsworth, an Oxford-trained Fabian and avowed eugenicist who endorsed Alberta's 1927 sterilization laws, to head the new CCF.[1]

## The Regina Manifesto

The CCF published the [[regina-manifesto|Regina Manifesto]] in Haldeman's hometown of Regina in 1933, while he ran the Saskatchewan branch.[1] Ehret reads the document as a call for a technocratic order managed by experts to emerge from the Great Depression, quoting its aim "to replace the present capitalist system… by a social order from which the domination and exploitation of one class by another will be eliminated, in which economic planning will supersede unregulated private enterprise and competition," and its declaration that "in every industrial country some form of planning will replace the disintegrating capitalist system."[1]

## Haldeman's departure

Disappointed that Canadians were not embracing "scientifically managed governance" under the CCF, Haldeman changed course in 1936 and joined [[technocracy-inc|Technocracy Inc.]], an organization he judged better placed to overthrow capitalism.[1] Ehret argues that the CCF and Technocracy Inc. were in any case managed by the same transatlantic agency that ran [[howard-scott|Howard Scott]] in New York from 1919 onward through the [[new-school-for-social-research|New School for Social Research]].[1]
