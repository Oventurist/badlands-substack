# Geert Wilders

Geert Wilders is a Dutch politician who, as of September 2025, was serving as an opposition leader in the parliament of the [[netherlands|Netherlands]].[1] He is described in Badlands Media reporting as the sponsor of a parliamentary motion targeting the [[antifa|Antifa]] movement.[1]

## Antifa terrorist designation motion

On Thursday, 18 September 2025, the Dutch parliament adopted a motion introduced by Wilders calling on the Dutch government to designate Antifa as a terrorist organization.[1] The motion followed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s decision to pursue the same designation in the United States, which Trump announced on Wednesday, 17 September 2025, in the aftermath of the assassination of conservative activist [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[1]

In the text of his motion, Wilders asserted that Antifa cells are active within the Netherlands, and accused them of threatening politicians, disrupting public events, and intimidating students and journalists.[1] Because the measure took the form of a parliamentary motion rather than binding legislation, the Dutch government was required to formally respond and to decide independently whether to implement the request.[1] Reporting at the time noted that, if carried out, such a designation could broaden law enforcement powers relating to surveillance, monitoring, and prosecution of suspected Antifa activity in the country.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor Burning Bright placed the Dutch vote within a longer arc, noting that Trump had first signaled an intention to designate Antifa a terrorist organization in 2020, and arguing that the 2025 push represented the cultivation of political mandate rather than a reaction to the Kirk assassination.[1]

## 2023 election victory

In the [[2023-dutch-general-election|November 2023 Dutch parliamentary elections]], Wilders' [[party-for-freedom|PVV (Freedom Party)]] — routinely labelled "far-right" by the media — came out on top, and was expected to take between 35 and 37 of the 150 seats in the Dutch House of Representatives, up from 16.[2] The party has promised to ban mosques and calls for a far more restrictive immigration policy.[2] Commentary published with the result argued that the "far-right" label had been broadened to cover anyone dissenting from mainstream positions on war, taxation, climate policy and gender ideology, citing [[martin-armstrong|Martin Armstrong]] of Armstrong Economics.[2]

Contemporaneous reporting described Wilders as a politician who built his name on anti-Islam and anti-European Union rhetoric, having called Islam a "backward religion," named the prophet Mohammad a "pedophile," and campaigned to ban mosques.[3] Declaring victory at a café in The Hague on the Wednesday evening, the 60-year-old said, "The Dutch voter has spoken. We will ensure that the Netherlands will return to the Dutch."[3] Early results showed his [[party-for-freedom|Party for Freedom]] winning 37 of the 150 parliamentary seats, twelve ahead of its nearest rival, the Labor-Green Alliance led by EU climate policy veteran Frans Timmermans.[3] With fewer than a quarter of the seats, Wilders had the first opportunity to approach other parties about forming a coalition, though most had vowed not to govern with him because of his views.[3] His inflammatory comments on Islam have prompted death threats, forced him to live under police protection for years, sparked protests in countries with large Muslim populations, and led a religious leader in Pakistan to issue a fatwa against him.[3]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] linked the Dutch result to the near-simultaneous victory of an anarcho-capitalist in [[argentina|Argentina]] endorsed by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], arguing that both campaigns ran on nationalism, free trade, decentralization and strong borders, and that the worldwide sovereignty movement Trump began in 2016 was accelerating.[3]
