# Election Assistance Commission

The United States Election Assistance Commission (EAC) is the federal body responsible for accrediting the laboratories that test and certify voting systems used in American elections. It enters the Badlands Media corpus through the expert testimony offered in support of [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]]'s December 2022 Arizona election contest.

## Role in the Lake contest

As relayed by [[liz-harrington|Liz Harrington]] and reported in "Arizona Earthquake!", the complaint relies on a cyber expert who had spent nine years performing security tests for the EAC — described elsewhere in the same passage as the laboratory that certified [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]]'s machines. That expert is quoted concluding that the machine failures Arizona voters experienced on Election Day "could not have occurred absent intentional misconduct," and that "an unintentional widespread failure of this magnitude occurring could not arise absent intentional misconduct."

The certification question recurs in the parallel suit brought by [[mark-finchem|Mark Finchem]] and [[jeff-zink|Jeff Zink]], which alleges that Secretary of State [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]] failed to have Arizona's tabulation machines properly certified.

## Dismissal of the commissioners (July 2026)

The July 10, 2026 Badlands Brief "Mossad Bait & Labor Tackles" reports that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] dismissed the remaining members of the EAC on Thursday, July 9, 2026. The Brief describes the agency as the independent federal body that certifies voting systems, accredits voting system testing laboratories, administers federal election grants, and maintains the national mail voter registration form. One Republican commissioner resigned, while the two Democratic commissioners were notified by email that their positions had been terminated. The [[white-house|White House]] had not announced replacement nominees, though the president retains authority to appoint new commissioners subject to [[senate|Senate]] confirmation.

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterises the EAC as the institutional "expert" that reassures the public that erroneous code, code read errors, glitches, or database coding mismatches "are no big deal," and argues that such bureaucracies speak about elections with the primary goal of preserving public trust "even if it means lying to the public." She holds that firing the principals is insufficient and that those involved "should be prosecuted along with everyone else who participated in lying to the public about fake elections under the banner of 'preserving trust.'" These are interpretive claims by the author rather than reported findings.
