# Increase Mather

Increase Mather was a Boston Puritan minister and the father of [[cotton-mather|Cotton Mather]], described in the Badlands Media essay "In Defense of Benjamin Franklin" as having played, with his son, a dual role during the New England witch panics: exposing genuine satanic covens while restraining the fanatical zealots who threatened to turn the fight against evil into a paranoia-led terror.[1]

In *Cases of Conscience*, published in 1693, Increase Mather attacked the use of "spectral evidence" and wrote that "It were better that ten suspected witches should escape, then that one innocent person should be condemned."[1] The essay credits the relatively small number of executions and the quick end of the [[salem-witch-trials|Salem trials]] by 1693 with settling the storm for a period, though it maintains that the underlying threat did not disappear.[1]

Quoting [[graham-lowry|Graham Lowry]], the article states that Increase Mather was "entirely accurate" when in 1722 he charged [[james-franklin|James Franklin]] with promoting a "Hell-Fire Club" in Boston, on the grounds that James had published the claim that man cannot distinguish between the work of God and the work of the Devil.[1]
