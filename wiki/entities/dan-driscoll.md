# Dan Driscoll

Dan Driscoll is the U.S. Army Secretary in the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1] On Thursday, November 20, 2025 he presented the 28-point [[trump-ukraine-peace-plan|Ukraine peace plan]] to Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], a draft of which was obtained by [[axios|Axios]].[1]

Alongside the 28-point document — which states only that "Ukraine will receive reliable security guarantees" — the U.S. delegation presented the Ukrainians with a separate draft agreement containing an [[article-5|Article 5]]-style commitment treating a future Russian attack as an attack on the transatlantic community.[1] Driscoll's role placed a service secretary rather than a State Department official at the center of the most consequential U.S.–Ukraine diplomatic exchange of the period.[1]

## Geneva mission and the 28-point plan (November 2025)

Driscoll, described by Politico as an unusual choice to play diplomat, spent two days in late November 2025 delivering a tough message to Ukraine and its European allies: accept the offered 28-point peace proposal by Thanksgiving, or else.[2] Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] then arrived in Geneva on Sunday, November 23 to join him and resume discussions with senior Ukrainian officials, after which the administration backed away from its firm Thursday deadline and, according to European and Ukrainian participants, began giving more weight to their positions.[2]

## Rescission of the Easterly appointment (2025)

On Wednesday, July 30, 2025, as Secretary of the Army, Driscoll issued a memorandum abruptly ending the United States Military Academy's appointment of [[jen-easterly|Jen Easterly]] to the Robert F. McDermott Distinguished Chair in [[west-point|West Point's]] Department of Social Sciences, a position announced only the day before.[3] Driscoll announced in a post on X that the position would be rescinded and that a full review of the academy's hiring practices would be conducted.[3]
