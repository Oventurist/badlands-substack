# Canada

**Canada** is the northern neighbor of the United States and, in July 2026, the target of a renewed tariff escalation by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] In Badlands Media commentary, Canada is framed not merely as a trading partner in dispute but as the "final stand of Globalism" on the North American continent.[1]

## 2026 tariff escalation

Trump announced new 50% tariffs on nearly $20 billion worth of selected Canadian imports, citing what the administration described as Canada's discriminatory treatment of American automobiles, dairy products, and alcoholic beverages.[1] The duties were issued under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 and scheduled to take effect on August 19, 2026.[1] The measures apply to a range of Canadian goods but exempt energy, potash, fish, critical minerals, and certain other products.[1]

Canadian Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] criticized the move as inconsistent with the [[usmca|USMCA]] trade agreement and said Canada remained willing to negotiate, while Canadian officials indicated they were considering retaliatory measures.[1]

## Badlands framing

Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the trade war with wry approval, calling it "The Great North American Richard Measuring Contest" and saying she liked America's chances.[1] The Brief tied the tariff dispute to other grievances raised in prior editions, including wildfire smoke drifting into the American northeast and waste shipped into the American Midwest.[1]

The commentary went further, arguing that Canada "arguably represents the final stand of Globalism as it sits on our US northern border" and describing it as, in the author's opinion, the greatest national security threat to the United States.[1] Two features were emphasized: that Canada, through its Prime Minister, swears allegiance to the English Crown, and that the Canada–US border runs 5,525 miles including Alaska — raising, in the author's telling, the question of Alaska's exposure in any escalation.[1] The author noted a measure of reassurance that [[russia|Russia]] sits on the far side of the "Canadian-Globalist divide," while warning readers not to underestimate the "Maple Marxists."[1]

The piece was explicit that the critique was not directed at Canadians generally — invoking the hashtag "#notallcanadians" and naming Canadian figures the author admires, including Matthew Ehret, Wesley Huff, and Ryan Reynolds, while singling out Carney and Neil Young for criticism.[1] The concluding formulation was that Canadians "will be fine in the golden age, so long as they pledge allegiance to the flag instead of the crown."[1]

## US-Canada tensions and the 2026 invasion war game

Amid heightened tensions with the Trump administration — tariff threats, the dispute over [[greenland|Greenland]], and remarks framing Canada as a potential 51st state — the [[canadian-armed-forces|Canadian Armed Forces]] quietly developed a conceptual war game simulating a U.S. military invasion and Canada's response, the first exercise of its kind in over a century.[2] The model assumes American forces would overwhelm Canada's conventional defenses within days to a week and contemplates asymmetric insurgency, a volunteer reserve exceeding 400,000, and reinforcement from allies including Britain, France, Germany, Japan and South Korea.[2]

Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] used his address to the January 2026 [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in Davos to describe a "rupture, not a transition" in the [[rules-based-international-order|rules-based international order]] and to call on "middle powers" to unite against great-power coercion.[2] Canada was among the countries publicly confirming participation in [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]], though it declined to pay the membership contribution.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed the war-game story as further provocation, writing that "Canada isn't real" and calling for the whole of the north to be made "red, white, and blue."[2]
