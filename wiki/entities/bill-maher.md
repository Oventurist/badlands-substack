# Bill Maher

**Bill Maher** is a comedian and longtime Democratic voter who, in July 2026, intensified his public criticism of the Democratic Party's leftward shift.[1]

## Criticism of the DSA and 2028 posture

Maher warned that the growing influence of the [[democratic-socialists-of-america|Democratic Socialists of America]] could drive him to support Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] in 2028.[1] In an interview on ABC's *This Week*, he said Democrats had become "cowardly" and argued the party was increasingly being shaped by "very radical" figures.[1]

Asked whether he could see himself voting for Vance if the party continued in its current direction, Maher replied, "Yes. If this is the Democratic side, yes."[1] He added, "I'm not living in Communist America," arguing that proposals in the DSA platform — including public ownership of key industries — amounted to communism rather than democratic socialism.[1] Maher emphasized that his vote is "in play," while reiterating that he would not support [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] himself.[1]

His remarks came amid a broader debate within Democratic circles following a series of victories by DSA-backed candidates in recent primaries; some prominent Democratic strategists and former party officials publicly expressed concern that the party was moving too far left, while progressive leaders argued the DSA reflected growing grassroots support for economic reforms and expanded social programs.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read Maher's statements as "abject panic" at the state of his party's public branding, and cautioned readers not to lose sight of the fact that Maher had de facto endorsed Vance — not Trump — as preferable to the Marxist vision he described as overtaking leftist politics.[1] Burning Bright noted that trust in Trump does not automatically transfer to those around him, citing Mike Pence as precedent.[1]

## Criticism of celebrity virtue signaling (2026)

A *Real Time* monologue from January 30, 2026 went viral in the wake of the [[68th-annual-grammy-awards|68th Annual Grammy Awards]], in which Maher criticized celebrity politicization and said of ICE, "I don't need to wear a pin about it."[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Maher enjoys triggering his own core audience and was doing so during a peak virtue-signaling moment on immigration, noting that he had called their pain over Renee Good "stupid" — something she contended he could never have done in 2020 about a comparable cause.[2]

## Trump's Valentine's Day rebuke (2026)

On Valentine's Day 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] used his Truth Social account to deliver a highly critical message aimed at Maher, eschewing a traditional holiday greeting in favor of a lengthy personal rebuke.[3] Trump described a 2025 White House dinner with Maher as "a total waste of time," writing that the comedian had been "extremely nervous" and lacking confidence during their meeting and had asked for a vodka tonic to calm himself.[3]

Trump also attacked Maher's HBO program *Real Time with Bill Maher*, calling it "very boring" and "anti-Trump," and referred to Maher as "a highly overrated lightweight" while suggesting he was slightly more talented than other late-night hosts.[3] The message went beyond Maher to include jabs at Canada and China, reflecting a sharp deterioration in tone between the president and the long-time media figure; representatives for Maher and the White House did not immediately respond to requests for comment.[3]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] called the post "signal-soaked," noting that Trump called Maher out amid the comedian's seeming public about-face on the establishment, during which he continued attacking the MAGA agenda while praising [[qanon|'QAnon']] adherents who have long considered him a member of the "red shoe club."[3] Burning Bright found the statement gratifying chiefly because Trump assigned blame to Republicans for "using [Maher] to show how the Left is coming over our way," quoting Trump's line that "Our Base, the Greatest of All Time, laughs at your weakness when you do it!"[3] He extended the lesson to Q decoders who had latched onto [[fox-news|FOX News]] as secretly full of "white hats," arguing Trump engages such outlets only because portions of the awakened audience still consume them, and that lending them attention perpetuates the Media Industrial Complex the movement once opposed.[3]

## "Sanctuary city hypocrites" monologue (2023)

In an October 2023 opening monologue on "Real Time," Maher blasted President [[joe-biden|Biden]] and "ultra-radical progressives" over their policy U-turn on the southern border, saying that border-state Republicans who bused migrants to [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary]] metro areas in the Northeast "called the Democrats' bluff about sanctuary cities" and that the Democrats "look like sanctuary city hypocrites."[4]

"They've been busing the migrants to the cities now... and they said oh, you love them so much? Here you go. And the cities don't like it so much," Maher said, noting that New York Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] had warned the influx would "destroy our city" and had traveled to Mexico, Ecuador and Colombia to tell prospective migrants personally that the city had no room.[4] Maher mocked the reversal of the city's own mythology: "Don't you know our catchphrase? We're the Little Apple. And our theme song, if you can make it here, you can make it anywhere, so go anywhere, don't come here."[4]

## Defense of Harrison Butker (2024)

In May 2024 Maher defended Kansas City Chiefs kicker [[harrison-butker|Harrison Butker]] over a [[benedictine-college|Benedictine College]] commencement speech in which Butker said one of the "most important" jobs a woman can have is being a homemaker.[5] Described by ZeroHedge as a "90s Democrat," Maher said during a Friday night "Real Time" panel discussion that he did not see "what the big crime is," and mocked Butker's critics for trying to make him out to be "history's greatest monster."[5]

"I can't express how much this guy is not like me," Maher said. "He's religious. He loves marriage. He loves kids… And he's now history's greatest monster."[5]

## 2024 remarks on transgenderism

During a May 2024 appearance on Fox News, Maher asserted that in some cases transgenderism is just 'a TikTok challenge that got out of hand.'[6] He argued that the United States had become an 'outlier' compared with other Western nations, noting that European countries were moving away from measures such as puberty blockers for children.[6] Maher also drew a distinction between liberalism and 'woke,' saying: 'Liberalism is not the same thing as woke, woke they would like to think they're an extension of liberalism, they're not, I'm an old school liberal, it's very often the opposite,' adding that 'they think they're going so far in the right direction and it actually turns out that they're in the wrong direction and they've actually reversed it.'[6]

## Commentary on the Stormy Daniels testimony (2024)

Maher was unimpressed by the testimony of adult film actress [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]] in the New York criminal trial of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], declaring that "She's a bad witness!"[7] During a Friday night panel discussion he reiterated his frustration at how the criminal cases against Trump had stalled, citing developments in the Georgia case and the classified documents case, and quipped, "So it's Stormy or bust."[7]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the remarks as significant, describing Maher as a "canary in the coal mine" among the "Culture Critics" subset of what he termed Mind-Movers in the Information War.[7] He argued that the truth community too often dismisses late-night figures as insufficiently "legitimate" to warrant scrutiny, and that Maher and his peers are arguably more influential over mainstream audiences than cable news hosts precisely because viewers' political filters are lowered by his softer, entertainment-framed rhetoric.[7] When such critics drift toward common-sense positions, Burning Bright wrote, it should be read not as enlightenment but as "a final attempt to re-brand ahead of the backlash he sees coming."[7]
