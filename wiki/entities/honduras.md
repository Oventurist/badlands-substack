# Honduras

Honduras is a Central American nation whose November 2025 general election became the subject of a US visa-restriction action and of allegations of foreign interference in the vote count.[1]

## Disputed 2025 election and US visa restrictions

In December 2025 the [[state-department|State Department]] restricted the visas of two Honduran officials over what it described as national security concerns, claiming they had tried to impede a special vote count following the election held the previous month.[1] The [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] revoked the visa of Mario Morazán, a magistrate of the Electoral Justice Tribunal, and denied a visa to Marlon Ochoa, a member of the Honduras National Electoral Council.[1] Both men belong to the [[libre-party|LIBRE party]], which holds the legislative majority in the country.[1]

The department cited Section 221(i) of the [[immigration-and-nationality-act|Immigration and Nationality Act]], which officials said "generally bars entry to those whose entry or proposed activities have potentially serious adverse U.S. foreign policy consequences."[1] Its statement read: "The United States will not tolerate actions that undermine our national security and our region's stability. We will consider all appropriate measures to deter those impeding the vote count in Honduras."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[canncon|CannCon]] wrote that "there appears to be an attempt at regime placement in Honduras," pointing to the travel of congressmen from the [[house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence|House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence]] to the country to "monitor" the election.[1] He noted that HPSCI Chairman [[rick-crawford|Rick Crawford]] had also monitored elections in [[guyana|Guyana]], Suriname and Barbados in person in April of that year — countries he characterized as key narcotrafficking hubs — and observed that three weeks after the Honduran vote a winner had still not been certified.[1]
