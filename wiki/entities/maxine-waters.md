# Maxine Waters

Maxine Waters is a Democratic member of the United States House of Representatives from California, long a prominent and combative voice in national partisan debate. Badlands Media coverage in May 2024 focused on remarks she made in a televised interview warning of organized right-wing violence should Donald Trump lose the 2024 presidential election.[1]

## May 2024 "training in the hills" remarks

In an interview broadcast on Sunday, May 5, 2024, Waters said she feared "right-wing" organizations that she described as "training up i the hills somewhere" in the event that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is not elected in 2024.[1] The comments were made during an [[msnbc|MSNBC]] appearance in which the host asked Waters about Trump's interview with TIME magazine, in which Trump had said there was "a definite anti-white feeling in the country."[1] The exchange was reported by [[the-post-millennial|The Post Millennial]].[1]

## Record of confrontational rhetoric

Coverage of the remarks placed them against Waters' own history of calling for confrontation with political opponents.[1] In 2021, during the trial of Derek Chauvin, Waters encouraged Black Lives Matter protesters to escalate: "We've got to stay on the street and we've got to get more active, we've got to get more confrontational... We've got to make sure that they know that we mean business."[1] In 2018 she had urged Democrats and activists to confront Republicans and Trump administration officials when they were seen in public.[1] Reporting on Waters has also noted campaign-funds payments made to her daughter, including a figure of $192,000 in 2022.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing for Badlands Media, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Waters' warning was part of a deliberate effort to "hard sell the civil war narrative," and that "Mad Max as the messenger is a perfect red pill for normies" because her earlier calls for confrontation were widely remembered.[1] The commentary characterized the remarks as projection — Waters attributing to her opponents the conduct she had herself encouraged — and contrasted them with contemporaneous left-wing street activity, including protesters in Denver who on Monday, May 6, 2024 stopped traffic and harassed truck drivers and other motorists as demonstrations moved off university campuses.[1] Ashe in America framed the question as whether Waters was concerned about opposition to what the piece called her "handlers' ongoing color revolution," and predicted that whether another summer of unrest was tolerated would depend on jurisdiction and venue.[1]

## Stablecoin legislation and Badlands criticism (2025)

During the March 2025 [[house-financial-services-committee|House Financial Services Committee]] hearing on the [[stable-act|STABLE Act]], Badlands contributor GMONEY singled Waters out as "an employee of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]]" attempting "to protect the banking class through permissioned control and issuance of human energy."[2] Prefacing his remarks with the quotation attributed to Mayer Amschel Rothschild — "Give me control of a nation's money supply, and I care not who makes its laws" — he argued that the dollar had been "a centralized shitcoin for over 100 years" and that crypto was exposing the fraud behind it, concluding that "[[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] fixes this."[2]

## Q drop #19

Chapter 3 of [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] identifies Waters as the subject of the closing portion of [[qanon|Q]] drop #19 of November 1, 2017, describing Q as "again calling out a corrupt member of Congress for the gigantic disparity in their income and net worth" and aiming, in the author's phrase, "the proverbial informational reticle" at her.[3] The passage parallels the treatment of [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] in the preceding drop.[3]
