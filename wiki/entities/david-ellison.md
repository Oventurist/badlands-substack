# David Ellison

David Ellison runs [[skydance-media|Skydance Media]] and is the son of [[larry-ellison|Larry Ellison]], cofounder of the technology company [[oracle|Oracle]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) In "A New Hollywood," [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] identifies him as the executive who will eventually control [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]] once the Skydance merger completes, placing [[cbs|CBS]], [[showtime|Showtime]] and Paramount+ under a family closely allied with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

Under Ellison, Skydance produced *Top Gun: Maverick*, the *Mission Impossible* films and *Jack Reacher*, along with the television series *Reacher* and *Jack Ryan* — a slate Carlson reads as evidence of a pro-American, intelligence-and-military-themed turn in [[hollywood|Hollywood]] output.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## Paramount and CBS News takeover

As founder and chief executive of [[skydance-media|Skydance]], the 42-year-old Ellison was positioned in July 2025 to become chief of [[paramount-global|Paramount]] and of CBS, once the premier institution of American television news built by Edward R. Murrow, Mike Wallace, and Walter Cronkite.[2] Commentators noted that the cachet such a role would once have conferred had faded, and that Ellison — the son of Oracle billionaire [[larry-ellison|Larry Ellison]], a MAGA supporter of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] — was unlikely to be dwelling on the CBS News legacy as he prepared to complete the roughly $8 billion combination.[2] CBS management had shortly beforehand settled a lawsuit filed by Trump in order to remain in the White House's good graces and secure approval of the Skydance–Paramount merger from Trump's FCC regulators.[2] Badlands contributor Burning Bright framed the transaction as part of a hostile Hollywood takeover in which Skydance, the Ellisons, and Paramount sat at the center of a controlled demolition and transformation — Hollywood being not destroyed but "gutted and restored."[2]

## Hostile bid for Warner Bros. Discovery (December 2025)

As chief executive of [[paramount-global|Paramount]], Ellison led a hostile takeover bid for [[warner-bros-discovery|Warner Bros. Discovery]] announced on Monday, December 8, 2025, bypassing the target's board to put an all-cash offer of $30 per share directly to shareholders days after [[netflix|Netflix]] reached an $83 billion agreement for part of the company.[3] The offer valued Warner Bros. Discovery at roughly $108 billion including debt and covered the entire business, including the movie studio, HBO Max and cable channels such as CNN.[3] "We believe our offer will create a stronger Hollywood," Ellison said in a press release. "It is in the best interests of the creative community, consumers and the movie theater industry."[3]
