# Thierry Breton

**Thierry Breton** is a French politician and businessman who served as the European Commissioner for the Internal Market, a portfolio that included enforcement of the European Union's online content rules.[1]

## Remarks on the German election

In January 2025, Breton drew international attention after appearing in European media and stating, in a translation from the French, "We did it in Romania, and we will do it in Germany if necessary" — remarks widely read as an admission that European authorities had engineered the annulment of the Romanian presidential election and were prepared to do the same in Germany.[1] Reporting by the French broadcaster BFMTV, relayed by Romania Journal, indicated Breton believed that if [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] "breaks the law, especially in Germany, by supporting the 'extremist' [[alternative-for-germany|AfD]] party," authorities would need to act as they did in Romania, where the presidential election was cancelled.[1]

Breton also asserted that "freedom of expression is a fundamental element in Europe," while adding that noncompliant platforms faced "fines and the possibility of a ban," saying, "We are equipped to enforce these laws to protect our democracies in Europe."[1] Musk responded that this is what a tyrant sounds like.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized Breton's stance as "we must destroy democracy to protect democracy," arguing that he effectively criminalized Musk's political support for a major party in the EU's largest member state and that the reaction reflected an existential fear within the EU of decentralization and national sovereignty.[1] The episode was tied to [[2025-romanian-presidential-election-ban|the annulment of Romania's first-round presidential vote]] won by [[calin-georgescu|Călin Georgescu]], whose TikTok campaign was cited as evidence of alleged foreign interference.[1]

## Twitter's exit from the disinformation code

In late May 2023, as EU Internal Market Commissioner, Breton announced that [[twitter|Twitter]] had withdrawn from the bloc's voluntary [[eu-code-of-practice-on-disinformation|Code of Practice on Disinformation]], writing that "Twitter leaves EU voluntary Code of Practice against disinformation. But obligations remain."[2] He added: "You can run but you can't hide. Beyond voluntary commitments, fighting disinformation will be legal obligation under #DSA as of August 25. Our teams will be ready for enforcement," referring to the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]].[2] Badlands contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] read the statement as a display of fear and argued that Breton and the [[european-union|EU]] were underestimating both European citizens and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[2]

## Resignation from the European Commission (September 2024)

On Monday, September 16, 2024, Breton resigned as the [[european-union|European Union]]'s internal market chief, leaving the [[european-commission|European Commission]] and saying that France had been asked to provide a different nominee for the next Commission.[3] In a letter to Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]], he said her request was "further testimony to questionable governance."[3] According to Breton, von der Leyen asked France to withdraw his name as a nominee "for personal reasons that in no instance have you discussed directly with me," with France to receive a more influential role in the EU's executive arm in return.[3] The resignation was reported by [[bloomberg|Bloomberg]] and carried in the Badlands News Brief bonus items.[3]

## Announcement of the X infringement proceedings (December 2023)

On Monday, December 18, 2023, Breton announced that the [[european-union|EU]] had opened infringement proceedings against [[x-corp|X]] — the first probe under the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]] — in response to suspected breaches of the platform's transparency obligations and its duties to counter illegal content and disinformation, as well as what the bloc called a "deceptive" design of its user interface relating to blue checks.[4] X replied that it remained committed to complying with the Act and urged that the process remain "free of political influence."[4]


## Digital COVID certificate advocacy

As EU Commissioner for Internal Market, Breton championed the [[eu-digital-covid-certificate|EU Digital COVID-19 Certificate]], stating in 2023 that "with 80 countries and territories connected to the EU Digital COVID-19 Certificate, the EU has set a global standard."[5] He said the certificate had been an important tool in the pandemic response and had facilitated international travel and tourism, and expressed satisfaction that the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] would build on its "privacy-preserving principles and cutting-edge technology" to create a global tool against future pandemics.[5] His remarks were cited in Badlands coverage critical of the emerging [[digital-vaccine-passport|digital vaccine passport]] system.[5]

## Named among censorship proponents (2025)

Writing in February 2025, Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] named Breton, alongside Brazil's [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]], as a leading proponent of censorship whose reaction to the [[rumble-and-trump-media-v-moraes|Rumble and Trump Media lawsuit]] remained to be seen.[6] He suggested it would be instructive to watch such figures attempt to fight back against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at what he judged to be the height of Trump's strength, and situated Breton's [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]] among the principal fronts of the [[censorship-war|censorship war]].[6]

## Stress test of X's California headquarters (June 2023)

As the internal market commissioner responsible for enforcing the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]], Breton led a team of EU officials to [[x-corp|X]]'s California headquarters in June 2023 to "stress test" the company's moderation and compliance systems, after which the platform — which had quit the EU's voluntary code of practice — said it would comply with the rules.[7] Breton welcomed [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s change of heart but warned there would be "no half measures" when it came to sanctions for online crimes.[7]

Following the visit, Breton said the EU would be extremely vigilant heading into the following year's European elections, stating: "I told Elon Musk and Linda Yaccarino [Twitter's chief executive] that Twitter should be very diligent in preparing to tackle illegal content in the European Union," and that "fighting disinformation, including pro-Russian propaganda, will also be a focus area in particular as we are entering a period of elections in Europe."[7] Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] treated this warning as evidence that the DSA's enforcement machinery was aimed at political speech ahead of the 2024 election cycle.[7]
