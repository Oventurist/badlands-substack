# Amer Ghalib

Amer Ghalib is a Democrat politician who serves as mayor of [[hamtramck|Hamtramck]], Michigan, a Wayne County community with a large Arab-American and Muslim population. He helped lead the "uncommitted" protest movement during the 2024 Democratic presidential primary.[1]

## Meeting with Donald Trump

In September 2024 Ghalib met with former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in Flint, Michigan, and afterward told Breitbart News Washington Bureau Chief Matthew Boyle on SiriusXM's *Breitbart News Saturday* that the roughly 20-minute meeting "was very productive" and had been well received in his community, which traditionally votes Democrat.[1] He said the meeting materialized in part because many residents of Hamtramck, Dearborn, and neighboring Wayne County cities supported Trump and "the shifting dynamics" he was leading.[1] Ghalib was reported to be weighing a formal endorsement.[1]

Ghalib said the blowback to a colleague's endorsement of [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] ran 90–95% negative, compared with a 90–95% positive response to his own decision to open lines of communication with Trump.[1] He was quoted as saying that "Trump doesn't want wars."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the meeting as another blow to Harris and the Democrats, arguing the [[uncommitted-movement|uncommitted movement]] — popularized by Congresswoman [[rashida-tlaib|Rashida Tlaib]] — appeared anecdotally to be leaning toward Trump, since ending the war in Gaza was the movement's paramount issue and that war had escalated on Harris's watch.[1] She did not expect widespread public endorsements, but predicted that uncommitted Free Palestine voters casting ballots in their own self-interest would break for Trump.[1]

## Endorsement of Trump (September 2024)

In late September 2024, Ghalib — the Arab-American mayor of Hamtramck, Michigan — issued a full endorsement of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for president, a move Badlands Media cited as evidence that Trump was gaining across every demographic and that the country was "nowhere near a 50/50" split.[2]
