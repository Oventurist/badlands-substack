# Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington

Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington (CREW) is a Washington, D.C.-based legal advocacy organization described in Badlands Media coverage as the "liberal group" that spearheaded the effort to disqualify [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the Colorado presidential primary ballot.[1]

## Colorado ballot litigation

CREW brought the Colorado case on behalf of a group of Republican and unaffiliated Colorado voters, arguing that Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]] barred Trump from holding office again.[1] District Court Judge [[sarah-wallace|Sarah Wallace]] found that Trump had "engaged in insurrection" on January 6, 2021, but ruled he could stay on the ballot because the provision was not clearly intended to reach the presidency.[1]

CREW appealed that portion of the ruling to the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] on the night of Monday, November 20, 2023, contending Wallace erred in concluding that the disqualification clause's application to presidents was unclear.[1] Trump filed a parallel appeal challenging the insurrection finding.[1] The organization's Colorado suit was one of a broader wave of Section 3 challenges that had produced a string of defeats in other states.[1]

## Named as a potential Trump administration target (April 2025)

In the Oval Office on Thursday, April 17, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] hinted at future actions against universities and nonprofits beyond [[harvard-university|Harvard University]], naming Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington (CREW) specifically.[2] The article described CREW as a watchdog group suing the administration over records-law violations and other issues.[2] CREW responded that it had examined politicians from both parties and would continue its work.[2] The remarks came in the context of the administration's request that the [[internal-revenue-service|Internal Revenue Service]] cancel Harvard's tax exemption.[2]

## Colorado ballot litigation

CREW brought the Colorado case that resulted in the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]]'s 4–3 ruling of December 19, 2023 barring [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the state's Republican primary ballot, and publicly celebrated the outcome.[3] Its president and CEO [[noah-bookbinder|Noah Bookbinder]] said in a press release that "our Constitution clearly states that those who violate their oath by attacking our democracy are barred from serving in government," adding that CREW looked forward to ensuring the ruling stood.[3] Reporting by the Daily Caller drew a direct line between the organization and the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], noting Bookbinder's seat on the [[homeland-security-advisory-council|Homeland Security Advisory Council]] advising DHS Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]].[3] Badlands writer [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] cited the connection as evidence that the case amounted to lawfare rather than a genuine judicial proceeding.[3]

## Funding and network ties

Writing on January 1, 2024, [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterized CREW as one of a cadre of surrogate institutions propping up the Biden administration, alongside the organizations making up the [[soros-shadow-network|George Soros shadow network]] of NGOs, foundations, charities and advocacy groups.[4] Citing the research site [[influencewatch|InfluenceWatch]], he noted that CREW is a legal advocacy group with previous connections to political strategist [[david-brock|David Brock]] that describes itself as a "nonpartisan" watchdog directing litigation against government corruption, and that it forms part of Brock's network of organizations including the Democratic-aligned opposition research super PAC [[american-bridge-21st-century|American Bridge 21st Century]] and the media criticism organization [[media-matters-for-america|Media Matters for America]].[4] CREW has received funding from left-of-center foundations including [[george-soros|George Soros]]' [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]] and singer [[barbra-streisand|Barbra Streisand]]' Streisand Foundation.[4]

Journalist [[glenn-greenwald|Glenn Greenwald]] served on CREW's board more than a decade earlier and resigned publicly in 2010 after the group's president condemned [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]].[4] Greenwald said he had joined believing it was a nonpartisan group devoted to transparency and clean government, and quit in protest over the condemnation of [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]] following its publication of secrets showing U.S. war crimes under Presidents Bush and Obama.[4]

DeLarme described CREW as one of many liberal advocacy groups leading the effort to remove [[donald-trump|Trump]] from ballots in more than a dozen states, an effort he characterized as anti-democratic election interference dressed up as protecting democracy.[4]
