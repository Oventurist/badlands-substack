# Darren Beattie

Darren Beattie is a former [[donald-trump|Trump]] speechwriter who was appointed acting Under Secretary for Public Diplomacy and Public Affairs at the [[state-department|State Department]] in early February 2025.[1] He was already serving in a senior role within the department and was expected to assume the new position on a Monday.[1]

## Background

Beattie previously worked as a speechwriter for Trump but was fired in 2018.[1] In 2020, Trump appointed him to the Commission for the Preservation of American Heritage Abroad.[1] He has been an outspoken critic of US foreign policy, which made his elevation to a senior public diplomacy post a notable move.[1]

## The position

As described by the State Department, the Under Secretary for Public Diplomacy and Public Affairs "leads America's public diplomacy outreach, which includes messaging to counter terrorism and violent extremism."[1] The post also oversees the bureaus of Educational and Cultural Affairs and Global Public Affairs and plays a role in foreign policy development.[1]

## Commentary

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] praised the appointment, describing Beattie as a reformed Democrat long outspoken against US foreign policy, and expressed hope that he would revitalize US diplomatic relations abroad.[1] The commentary argued that the State Department had for too long treated other nations as feudal vassals, using USAID, the threat of sanctions, and even the implied threat of the CIA and US military to coerce foreign governments into compliance — behavior it characterized as textbook tyranny and the antithesis of America's founding principles.[1]

## Termination of the R/FIMI hub (2025)

As Acting Under Secretary for Public Diplomacy, Beattie joined Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] in March 2025 in terminating more than 100 contractors tied to the [[counter-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-hub|Counter Foreign Information Manipulation and Interference]] (R/FIMI) hub at the [[state-department|State Department]].[2] Badlands Media characterized R/FIMI as a sly continuation of the [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]], created by the outgoing Biden administration after Congress refused to reauthorize GEC funding, and staffed by the same personnel.[2]

The article credited Beattie and Rubio with thwarting that continuation and described the episode as one of the clearest wins to date in the effort to dismantle the U.S. government's portion of the [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship-industrial complex]].[2] It noted the particular offence that the apparatus had been financed with the tax dollars of the very people it was built to silence.[2] Badlands nonetheless argued that with U.S. elements being cut back, the centre of the censorship effort had shifted to the European Union's [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]].[2]

## Leadership of the U.S. Institute of Peace

Beattie — identified as the founder of Revolver News and a former speechwriter for [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Matt Gaetz — ultimately assumed the presidency of the [[united-states-institute-of-peace|United States Institute of Peace]] after the Trump administration removed acting president [[george-moose|George Moose]] in 2025, a post initially filled by a [[doge|DOGE]] staffer.[3] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that he was glad Beattie was "now at the helm" while cautioning that USIP's networks and personnel remained active.[3] The article also relayed that [[mike-benz|Mike Benz]] and Beattie were said to be contemplating throwing a party in the contested USIP headquarters building.[3]

## USIP appointment as a reform model (2025)

In July 2025 Darren Beattie, a State Department official, was named to the top post at the [[united-states-institute-of-peace|United States Institute of Peace]].[4] Badlands Media cited the appointment as a template for reforming the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]]: installing someone who has been vocal about the institution's abuses to take the reins and lead its reformation, rather than abolishing soft-power projection altogether.[4]
