# Trey Yingst

**Trey Yingst** is a correspondent for [[fox-news|Fox News]] covering the Middle East.[1]

## Interview with Trump on Iran (June 2026)

In June 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told Yingst that the United States would resume major military strikes against [[iran|Iran]] if ongoing peace negotiations failed, saying he would "bomb the shit out of them" if Tehran did not immediately agree to a deal.[1] The exchange followed a fresh round of U.S. strikes on Iranian military targets and renewed fighting despite months of ceasefire negotiations, and became the most widely quoted element of that news cycle.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read Trump's remarks to Yingst as part of a deliberate narrative performance rather than a straightforward policy statement, arguing that Trump was testing how far he could push a wrestling-style promo while reporters treated it with complete seriousness.[1] He highlighted Trump's claim in the same period of speaking directly with Iranian leaders who were begging him to stop the bombing as a further test of the credibility of media-constructed reality.[1]

## Reporting on the Haniyeh assassination (2024)

On "Fox and Friends," Yingst delivered a segment on the killing of [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]] that Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] called surprisingly journalistic, laying out the gravity of the situation without the typical whitewashing to make [[israel|Israel]] look good.[2] Yingst recounted that CIA director [[william-burns|William Burns]] had met Qatari and Egyptian negotiators in Rome on a Sunday amid hopes that a ceasefire between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]] was imminent, and that within hours Hamas said the Israelis were reneging on their promises.[2] He then noted that Haniyeh, whom he described as the organization's head negotiator, was killed days later in a targeted assassination in Tehran, an indication that ceasefire talks were "basically off the table."[2] Yingst quoted the Qatari prime minister asking, "How can mediation succeed when one party assassinates the negotiator on the other side?" and observed that more than 100 hostages remained inside Gaza with no diplomatic solution in sight and tens of thousands of Israelis displaced in the north.[2] DeLarme seized on Yingst's characterization of Haniyeh as a negotiator to argue that the [[assassination-of-ismail-haniyeh|assassination]] called into question whether the talks had ever been serious.[2]
