# Islamic State

The Islamic State (ISIS) is a jihadist organization whose West African affiliates were the target of a joint Nigerian–U.S. counterterrorism operation announced in May 2026.[1]

## West Africa operations

[[nigeria|Nigeria]]'s State House said that Nigerian and U.S. forces killed the Islamic State figure [[abu-bilal-al-manuki|Abu-Bilal Al-Manuki]] and several lieutenants at a compound in the Lake Chad Basin, with the Nigerian Army placing the strike in Metele, Borno State in coordination with U.S. [[africom|Africa Command]].[1] A U.S. official told the Associated Press that Al-Manuki was involved in Islamic State organizing and finance and had been plotting attacks against the United States and its interests.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands commentary argues that ISIS and its predecessors have operated in the Sahel for more than twenty years, that their original purpose was to necessitate the French intervention of 2013, and that they are sustained by funding channelled through the [[state-department|State Department]], the [[cia|CIA]], the [[united-nations|United Nations]], [[nato|NATO]] and affiliated NGOs.[1] In this account, [[russia|Russia]] has assisted Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] and the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]] in reclaiming sovereignty from ISIS-linked groups, while Russia and the AES have repeatedly accused [[ukraine|Ukraine]] of funding, arming and training those groups using money [[congress|Congress]] appropriated for Kyiv.[1] Four U.S. soldiers were killed by ISIS in the [[tongo-tongo-ambush|Tongo Tongo Ambush]], which the commentary describes as a set-up by bad actors in the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] and CIA.[1]

## December 2025 attacks

Australian federal police commissioner Krissy Barrett said the [[bondi-beach-mass-shooting|Bondi Beach mass shooting]] of December 14, 2025, in which 15 people were killed at a Hanukkah celebration in Sydney, was "a terrorist attack inspired by Islamic State," with Prime Minister [[anthony-albanese|Anthony Albanese]] citing Islamic State flags found in a seized vehicle as part of the supporting evidence.[2] The same week, an ISIS affiliate was blamed for the [[palmyra-attack-on-iowa-national-guard-members|Palmyra ambush]] in Syria that killed two Iowa National Guard members and a civilian U.S. interpreter and wounded three other Guard members.[2]

## Damascus church bombing (2025)

Syria's interior ministry said that the attacker who killed at least 25 people and wounded 63 at the Greek Orthodox Church of the Prophet Elias in the Dweila neighbourhood of Damascus was affiliated with Islamic State, though the group issued no immediate claim of responsibility.[3] The assailant opened fire during an evening service before detonating an explosive vest, leaving a heavily damaged altar and pews covered in broken glass.[3] See [[2025-damascus-church-suicide-bombing|the 2025 Damascus church suicide bombing]].[3]
