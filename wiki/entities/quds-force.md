# Quds Force

The Quds Force is the elite foreign espionage and paramilitary arm of [[iran|Iran]]'s [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]].[1]

## 2024 Damascus embassy strike losses

On April 1, 2024, suspected Israeli warplanes bombed Iran's embassy compound in Damascus, [[syria|Syria]].[1] The IRGC said seven Iranian military advisers died in the strike, among them [[mohammad-reza-zahedi|Mohammad Reza Zahedi]], a senior Quds Force commander.[1] Three of the dead were senior commanders, and the attack was the first time [[israel|Israel]] had hit the embassy compound itself, after years of targeting Iranian military installations and proxy assets in Syria.[1] See [[2024-israeli-strike-on-iranian-embassy-in-damascus]].[1]

## Alleged narcotics kingpin designation (Badlands analysis)

Citing the Jerusalem Post, Badlands contributor Joe Lange notes that in March 2012 the U.S. Department of the Treasury designated Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Brigadier General Gholamreza Baghbani, commanding officer of the Quds Force, as a kingpin of heroin exports to the West.[2] The Quds Force is described as the IRGC branch assigned to national, regional and international special operations and unconventional warfare, given priority in funding, training and equipment, and numbering an estimated 10,000 to 15,000 men; it enables [[iran|Iran]] to conduct unconventional warfare overseas through foreign proxies such as [[hamas|Hamas]] and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[2] Because Iran sits geographically between the poppy fields of [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] and the West, it forms the starting point of the world's major heroin trade routes into Russia, Western Europe and the United States, and the Quds Force controlled Iran's ports and border security, which Lange presents as the reason for its deep involvement in trafficking.[2]

## Funding through the Iran cash transfers

"Panic in DC" places the Quds Force among the designated entities that made dollar transactions illegal for banks, alongside [[qasem-soleimani|Qasem Soleimani]], the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard]], [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and Hamas — the reason, [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues, that value was moved to [[iran|Iran]] in physical foreign banknotes rather than by wire.[3] Washington Times reporting quoted in the article states that the U.S. government traced portions of the $1.7 billion released to Iran under the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration to the Quds Force, described as Iran's main foreign intelligence and covert action arm and an element of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, as well as to Hezbollah and Houthi rebels in Yemen.[3] The article also alleges that the [[us-department-of-justice|Justice Department]] declined a [[project-cassandra|Project Cassandra]] request to charge a central player in a U.S.-based Quds Force cell.[3]
