# Jigar Shah

Jigar Shah is the director of the Loan Programs Office at the United States Energy Department, the federal financing arm that underwrites large-scale energy projects.[1]

## Role in the US nuclear restart

In 2024, Shah emerged as a central figure in an unexpected reversal of American nuclear policy: the revival of shuttered commercial reactors. A month after the United States offered $1.5 billion to restart one closed nuclear power plant, Shah said that "there are a couple of nuclear power plants that we probably should, and can, turn back on," reflecting a growing sense among industry and government officials that the first restart would not be the last.[1]

In March 2024, Shah's office approved a loan to [[holtec-international|Holtec International Corp.]] to reopen the [[palisades-nuclear-plant|Palisades nuclear plant]] in Michigan. Holtec had purchased the facility when it closed in 2022 with the intention of decommissioning it, but officials including Governor Gretchen Whitmer began calling almost immediately for the plant to reopen. Holtec subsequently shifted course and planned for the plant to resume producing power in late 2025, a step observers expected might spur other companies to follow suit.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed the loan office's nuclear financing within a broader thesis he calls "The Switch" — the argument that while narratives about the energy transition appear catastrophic for the existing establishment, the underlying actions being implemented point toward a brighter outcome. He predicted that restarting closed plants would generate new energy jobs and curtail the push toward what he described as inefficient solar and wind projects, and asserted that the framing of nuclear energy as an existential threat had always been a psychological operation.[1]
