# Mason Locke Weems

Reverend Mason Locke Weems, usually called Parson Weems, was an American traveling preacher, publisher and biographer whose first-person life of [[francis-marion|Francis Marion]] is one of the primary sources for the Swamp Fox legend.[1]

## Life and work

Weems was only seventeen when the Declaration of Independence was signed and, rather than fight, became a traveling preacher who distributed Bibles to the illiterate — including girls and blacks — and taught them to read.[1] He later moved into publishing and bookselling, a market previously dominated by the British, and when he began writing his own books his stated intention was to promote patriotism, morality and shared cultural values.[1]

Weems maintained personal friendships with the Founding Fathers, Marion among them.[1] He is best known as the first biographer of [[george-washington|George Washington]], who publicly endorsed the work; that book was also the origin of the cherry-tree fable, which is why some scholars treat Weems as an unreliable source.[1] He additionally wrote biographies of [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]], William Penn and Francis Marion; the Marion volume, written in the first person after interviewing Marion's militiamen, is credited in the article with rescuing the Swamp Fox from historical obscurity.[1] Weems was publicly rebuked by a prominent Virginia bishop for promoting the work of the atheist Thomas Paine.[1]

The character of the reverend who enlists in Ghost's militia in [[the-patriot-2000-film|The Patriot]] was inspired by Weems.[1]
