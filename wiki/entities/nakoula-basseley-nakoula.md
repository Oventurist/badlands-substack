# Nakoula Basseley Nakoula

Nakoula Basseley Nakoula is a Coptic Christian filmmaker and American resident whose short video [[innocence-of-muslims|"The Innocence of Muslims"]] was publicly blamed by the Obama administration for the September 11, 2012 [[benghazi-attack|attack on the U.S. diplomatic compound in Benghazi]].[1] The video trailer, posted online under the pseudonym "Sam Bacile," mocked the Islamic prophet Mohammad, depicting him as everything from a bozo and womanizer to a predator and homosexual.[1]

## Role in the Benghazi narrative

In the aftermath of the Benghazi attack, President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and then-Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] seized on the anti-Islamist film as the cause of a spontaneous protest that turned violent — a narrative that Badlands Media argues was designed in advance rather than improvised.[1] Clinton reportedly told Charles Woods, the father of slain former Navy SEAL [[tyrone-woods|Tyrone Woods]], at Andrews Air Force Base on September 14, 2012 that "we are going to have the person responsible for the video arrested," a remark Woods said he found odd given the tone of the event.[1]

## Arrest and imprisonment

Nakoula had previously been convicted on charges relating to bank and credit fraud. Federal prosecutors determined that his use of the Internet to post the video violated the terms of his probation, and he was charged with eight counts of probation violation, jailed without bail, and deemed a "danger to the community."[1] Badlands Media argues that designation is significant: the administration was making a public example of an American citizen for constitutionally protected speech, and would have found charges against him whether or not a technical probation violation existed.[1]

Kenneth Timmerman, author of *Deception: The Making of the YouTube Video Hillary and Obama Blamed For Benghazi*, described Nakoula as "the first victim of Islamic [[sharia-law|Sharia]] blasphemy laws in the United States," and said the actors and actresses in the film became collateral damage subject to death threats and fatwas that cannot be rescinded.[1]

## Aftermath

After serving prison time, being publicly shamed by the White House and threatened with death, Nakoula was reported to be living in a homeless shelter run by the First Southern Baptist Church in Buena Park, California, in poverty and fear.[1] Badlands Media places his prosecution alongside U.S. adoption of [[un-resolution-16-18|UN Resolution 16/18]] as an opening move in what it calls a coordinated effort to introduce blasphemy-style speech restrictions into American law as part of the [[sixteen-year-plan|sixteen year plan]].[1]
