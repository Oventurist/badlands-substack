# Carnegie Foundation

The **Carnegie Foundation** was, alongside the [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller]] philanthropies, the funder of the 1910 survey of North American medical education submitted by [[abraham-flexner|Abraham Flexner]] and published as the [[flexner-report|Flexner Report]].[1]

When the [[american-medical-association|American Medical Association]] ran out of money in 1910 and could not finish ranking the country's medical schools, the Rockefeller and Carnegie foundations created an education fund to complete the project begun by [[george-h-simmons|George H. Simmons]]. Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] notes that the Rockefellers and the Carnegies had traditionally worked together in furtherance of mutual goals and that the medical-education project was no exception.[1]

Flexner submitted his report directly to the Carnegie Foundation. Schools that failed to conform to its recommendations were denied funds and prestige and were generally forced out of business, an outcome the article treats as the intended result of the joint Rockefeller–Carnegie plan. The related philanthropic vehicle [[andrew-carnegie|Andrew Carnegie]] established remains a standard reference point in Badlands coverage of tax-exempt foundations.[1]
