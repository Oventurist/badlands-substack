# Raul Salinas

Raul Salinas de Gortari is the older brother of former Mexican President [[carlos-salinas]]. In the Badlands Media article [[a-family-affair]], he is identified as the "intellectual author" of the September 1994 assassination of José Francisco Ruiz Massieu, secretary general of the [[institutional-revolutionary-party]] (PRI), and as a key financial operator for the [[gulf-cartel]].

## José Francisco Ruiz Massieu murder

According to Mexican court records cited in [[the-washington-post]], Federal Judge Ricardo Ojeda Bohorquez concluded that evidence "logically and judicially interwoven" proved Raul Salinas was the intellectual author of the September 1994 killing of José Francisco Ruiz Massieu, the second-ranking official in Mexico's ruling PRI. Ruiz Massieu had previously been married to Adriana Salinas, sister of Raul and Carlos.

## Cover-up through Mario Ruiz Massieu

The article describes an elaborate cover-up in which President Carlos Salinas appointed the victim's brother, [[mario-ruiz-massieu]], as special prosecutor to investigate the assassination. Mario resigned claiming party leaders were blocking the investigation, but later evidence suggested he had altered witness statements to delete references to Raul Salinas. According to the source, this was a deliberate move to protect Raul, funded by the Gulf Cartel.

## Gulf Cartel connections

The article links Raul Salinas to the [[gulf-cartel]] and its leader [[juan-garcia-abrego]], stating that he provided "green light protection" for the cartel's drug trafficking operations through Mexico. According to the source, the Salinas family was paid substantial sums from cartel revenues, and the Gulf Cartel's drugs were moved across the border with the protection of the Salinas network. The article further claims that [[nafta]] was designed in part to facilitate this trafficking.

## Arrest and imprisonment

Raul Salinas went to prison for his role in the Ruiz Massieu murder and for drug trafficking-related charges. The article frames his imprisonment as a rare instance of accountability, arguing that the Bush family and CIA network have historically operated above the law.
