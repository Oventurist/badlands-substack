# John Lydon

John Lydon, better known by his stage name Johnny Rotten, is the frontman of the British punk band [[sex-pistols|the Sex Pistols]] and of the post-punk group Public Image Ltd.[1] He is cited in Badlands Media commentary as a leading example of an original [[punk-rock]] figure whose politics diverge sharply from the progressive assumptions attached to the genre by contemporary pop culture.[1]

## Support for Donald Trump

In a 2020 interview, Lydon unequivocally confirmed his support for [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]], saying he would be "daft as a brush" not to back him and describing Trump as "the only sensible choice now that Biden is up — he's incapable of being the man at the helm."[1] Lydon also framed Trump's outsider status as the source of his appeal, remarking that "'Assume the position' is the democrat party attitude, now Trump, he's not a politician, he's never claimed to be. How unusually exceptionally wonderful is that for people like me?"[1] Those remarks positioned him in opposition to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], whom he judged unfit for the presidency.[1]

Lydon paired that endorsement with an insistence on intellectual flexibility, stating: "If I'm wrong, I'm highly capable of correcting that... I've done that many times and I've had to. That's why I love debate and conversation, because you learn from it. Don't become entrenched in one opinion and get stuck there forever."[1]

## Brexit and Nigel Farage

Lydon has also expressed sympathy for [[nigel-farage|Nigel Farage]] and, by extension, for [[brexit]], grounding his position in class solidarity rather than party loyalty: "And where do I stand on Brexit? Well, here it goes… the working class has spoke, and I'm one of them and I'm with them."[1]

## Relationship with the record industry

Badlands Media notes Lydon's extensive and contentious history with the record label EMI, using it to illustrate the long-standing friction between punk artists and the major-label system that dominates commercial music distribution.[1]
