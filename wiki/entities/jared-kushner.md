# Jared Kushner

**Jared Kushner** is a businessman who served as senior adviser to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and is Trump's son-in-law. In the Badlands Media article "Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?", [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] identifies Kushner as the driving force behind the push for a [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currency]] by the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], describing him as Trump's most trusted advisor, the man tasked with the biggest behind-the-scenes developments in the administration, and someone who never leaks.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-cbdcs-the-path-to-fear-or-freedom)

## Advocacy for a digital dollar

Citing CNBC, the article reports that in 2019 — during a multi-year crypto bear market — Kushner was quietly advocating for a U.S. digital dollar issued by the Fed.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-cbdcs-the-path-to-fear-or-freedom) The claim rests on a 250-page release of Treasury Secretary [[steven-mnuchin|Steven Mnuchin's]] crypto-related email correspondence spanning his four years at Treasury, obtained by CoinDesk through a Freedom of Information Act request.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-cbdcs-the-path-to-fear-or-freedom)

In one email Kushner wrote to Mnuchin: "Steven – Would you be open to me bringing a small group of people to have a brainstorm about this topic?" adding, "My sense is it could make sense, and also be something that could ultimately change the way we pay out entitlements as well saving us a ton in waste fraud and also in transaction costs…"[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-cbdcs-the-path-to-fear-or-freedom)

## Assessment

CNBC characterized Kushner's take on the CBDC phenomenon as ultimately prescient: few countries were seriously exploring national digital currencies in mid-2019, but at least 87 countries representing over 90% of global GDP were doing so by the time of the reporting, according to the [[atlantic-council|Atlantic Council]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-cbdcs-the-path-to-fear-or-freedom) Lange treats Kushner's early, private advocacy as evidence that the American CBDC effort originated inside Trump's inner circle rather than with the [[cabal|cabal]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-cbdcs-the-path-to-fear-or-freedom)


## 2026 Doha mission
In June 2026 the [[white-house|White House]] said Kushner would travel with Special Envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] to Doha, [[qatar|Qatar]], for talks aimed at advancing implementation of the US-[[iran|Iran]] memorandum of understanding.[2] Tehran denied that any negotiations were scheduled, though it confirmed a delegation would attend technical discussions in Doha.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the episode as a game of chicken whose outcome would be tested by the fact that Witkoff and Kushner were ostensibly already en route.[2]

## February 2026 conflict-of-interest coverage

In February 2026 the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] published a report on President Trump's peace team, Kushner and [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], suggesting the pair faced conflicts of interest in their foreign negotiations.[3] "Supporters of the administration see Mr. Witkoff and Mr. Kushner as ideal negotiators in part because their personal wealth, they say, makes them more resistant to corrupting influences," the report stated, before noting that Witkoff's son Zach is chief executive of [[world-liberty-financial|World Liberty Financial]], the Trump family's cryptocurrency company, that an investment firm tied to the United Arab Emirates purchased nearly half the company for $500 million, and that Kushner raised several billion dollars from overseas investors before Trump's second term.[3]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the coverage as part of a coordinated operation against Trump's closest allies, describing Kushner as the architect of the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] and a consistent driver of Middle East peace initiatives who had "earned the seething enmity of extreme Zionist factions precisely because his efforts bypassed the perpetual conflict machine they thrive on."[3] He noted the timing arrived while Kushner was traversing the globe forging deals with nations long designated adversaries by that same supranational order.[3]

## Geneva nuclear talks (February 2026)

Kushner was part of the U.S. delegation to the second round of indirect United States–Iran nuclear negotiations, which concluded in Geneva in February 2026 alongside special envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]].[4] The Oman-mediated round produced what Iranian officials described as an understanding on core principles, with Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] promising detailed proposals within two weeks, though Tehran refused to give up uranium enrichment or discuss its ballistic missile program.[4]

## Iranian rejection (March 2026)

In March 2026 Iran rejected negotiations with Kushner and fellow US envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], branding them "backstabbers" over strikes that followed February talks, and signaled that it preferred to deal with Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] instead.[5] The rebuff came while Washington was pressing a one-month ceasefire proposal delivered through Pakistani intermediaries.[5]

## Saudi sovereign wealth fund investment

In February 2024, Kushner publicly defended his post-government business dealings with Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], who was implicated in the 2018 killing of Washington Post columnist [[jamal-khashoggi|Jamal Khashoggi]].[6] Kushner had worked on a wide range of issues in the Trump administration, including Middle East peace efforts, and developed a relationship with the crown prince, who oversaw social and economic reforms as well as a far-reaching crackdown on dissent in the kingdom.[6] After leaving the White House, Kushner started a private equity firm that received a reported $2 billion investment from the sovereign wealth fund controlled by Prince Mohammed, drawing scrutiny from Democrats.[6]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] acknowledged Kushner as "one of the more controversial names in the Trump orbit even among the MAGA base," but noted that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] himself cited Kushner as the lead architect of "some of the biggest peace agreements of the modern age," agreements orbiting the gravitational center of Saudi Arabia.[6] He argued Saudi power tipped "in the direction of sovereignty" only after Mohammed bin Salman's rise, and framed fresh media scrutiny of Saudi ties as a fear-mongering campaign by the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]].[6]

## Senate Finance Committee investigation, 2024

In June 2024, Senator [[ron-wyden|Ron Wyden]] (D-Ore.), chair of the [[senate-finance-committee|Senate Finance Committee]], launched an investigation into [[affinity-partners|Affinity Partners]], the Saudi-backed private equity firm Kushner leads.[7] Reporting noted that Wyden, as a member of the Senate majority, could potentially secure enough votes to subpoena documents that House Democrats had unsuccessfully sought.[7] Kushner had formed Affinity shortly after leaving the White House and by the end of 2021 had raised $3 billion for a debut fund, including a $2 billion commitment from Saudi Arabia's [[public-investment-fund|Public Investment Fund]] made over reported red flags from PIF staff; nearly 99 percent of the fund's capital came from non-U.S. sources.[7] The firm had by then invested in at least eight companies, including an Israeli auto services group and a California solar energy fintech.[7] The central question raised was whether Kushner had discussed his plans for Affinity with foreign governments while still serving as senior adviser to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], or whether the arrangement constituted a retroactive quid pro quo.[7]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that nothing Kushner and Affinity were doing was illegal or unprecedented, and that Kushner would have followed every conventional norm given the scrutiny on the Trump family and the fact that his own father had been sentenced to two years in prison in 2005 for white-collar crimes.[7] He framed the Wyden letter as evidence of hypocrisy by what he called the DC criminal cartel, whose outrage reflected resentment of competition rather than genuine concern about impropriety.[7] A second reading held that the pairing of Saudi capital under [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] with a targeting of the Israeli technology sector — an economy strained by wartime reservist call-ups and investor flight — suggested foreknowledge of the coming crisis and an intent to stabilize the Israeli economy while its leadership was publicly branded by MBS as war criminals.[7]

## October 2025: "integration" message to Israel

In an interview with CBS's [[60-minutes|60 Minutes]] broadcast on October 19, 2025 alongside U.S. special envoy to the Middle East [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], Kushner said Israel must start aiding the Palestinians and improving their quality of life if it wants to become fully integrated into the Middle East "now that the war is over."[8] He described the message conveyed to Israeli leadership: "if you want to integrate Israel with the broader Middle East, you have to find a way to help the Palestinian people thrive and do better," adding that he and Witkoff were "just getting started" in relaying it.[8]

Asked what allowing Palestinians to "thrive" would look like, Kushner said the United States is "focused on creating a situation for joint security and economic opportunity for Israelis and Palestinians so that they can live side by side in a durable way," and on the question of a path to statehood said, "What you end up calling it over time, we'll allow the Palestinians to determine that themselves."[8]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the interview as a vindication of Kushner against years of accusations that he was beholden to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and the Zionist diaspora, arguing that Kushner had revealed the administration's lost patience with Netanyahu and its view that the Israelis were "out of control."[8]

## Comment on Middle East coexistence (November 2024)

In November 2024 Kushner posted an observation on X that Jews, Muslims and Christians had lived in relative harmony in the Middle East for centuries prior to the Second World War.[9] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], endorsed the claim as historically accurate and said British tax records from Palestine dating to the 1850s bore it out.[9]

## Electronic Arts buyout, September 2025

[[affinity-partners|Affinity Partners]], the investment firm founded and led by Kushner, was named in September 2025 as one of three buyers in a $55 billion agreement to acquire the video game publisher [[electronic-arts|Electronic Arts]].[10] Its partners in the deal were [[silver-lake-partners|Silver Lake Partners]] and Saudi Arabia's [[public-investment-fund|Public Investment Fund]], and the transaction — described as the largest private equity-funded buyout in history — required regulatory approval because of Saudi involvement.[10]

## January 6 grand jury subpoena

In February 2023, Kushner and his wife [[ivanka-trump|Ivanka Trump]] were subpoenaed by special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] to testify before a federal grand jury about [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s efforts to remain in power after the 2020 election and his role in the pro-Trump mob's attack on the Capitol on January 6, 2021.[11] Reporting on the subpoenas described the move as underscoring how deeply into Trump's inner circle Smith was reaching, and as the latest sign that no potential high-level witness was off limits.[11] The disclosure came two weeks after it emerged that Smith had subpoenaed former Vice President [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]], who planned to fight his subpoena by invoking his role as president of the Senate under the Constitution's "speech or debate" clause.[11]

It was unclear whether Trump would seek to block Kushner and Ivanka Trump from testifying on executive privilege grounds, as he had attempted with other witnesses; both had served as White House officials in his administration, and Trump had declined to try to stop them from testifying before the [[january-6-committee|House select committee]] that investigated the attack.[11] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] argued that Smith's investigation would ultimately clear Trump and his team while gathering evidence of 2020 election fraud, and predicted indictments of figures from both parties.[11]

## December 2025 Moscow talks

Kushner accompanied special envoy Steve Witkoff to Moscow in early December 2025 for talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin on ending the Ukraine conflict.[12] European leaders, on a phone call the previous day, voiced fears that the United States would betray Ukraine on the territorial question without clarity on security guarantees.[12]

## Early contact with MBS

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] made contact with Jared Kushner through back-channels before [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was even inaugurated, ensuring that the ascending president would receive a king's welcome when he visited Riyadh in the months ahead.[13]


## Kremlin talks on the Ukraine peace plan (December 2025)

On Tuesday, December 2, 2025, Kushner — described as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s son-in-law and unofficial diplomat — joined US envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] at the Kremlin for high-level talks with President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], at which the American side presented Trump's Ukraine peace plan in its current form.[14] The meeting followed a high-stakes session in Miami with the Ukrainian delegation focused on ceding territory and the shape of future boundaries in the Donbass.[14] Russian presidential aide [[yuri-ushakov|Yury Ushakov]] called the five hours of talks constructive, very useful and substantive, while noting that no compromises had yet been found.[14]

## Miami talks with Russia, December 2025

Trump's son-in-law was expected to join special envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] in representing the United States at talks with Russian officials in Miami in mid-December 2025, part of the administration's push to end nearly four years of war between Kyiv and Moscow.[15] The Russian delegation was expected to include sovereign wealth fund head [[kirill-dmitriev|Kirill Dmitriev]], and both sides' plans remained in flux as Moscow held to its demands.[15]

## Intervention for Mohammed Ibrahim (2025)

In late 2025, [[israel|Israel]] agreed to release [[mohammed-ibrahim|Mohammed Ibrahim]], a Palestinian-American teenager detained for over nine months, after Kushner intervened on the boy's behalf, according to a U.S. official and a second source cited by [[times-of-israel|The Times of Israel]].[16] Ibrahim, a Florida resident, had been held under [[administrative-detention|administrative detention]] without charge since February over allegations he threw stones at Israeli troops in the West Bank; his family lobbied U.S. lawmakers, and the case eventually reached Kushner, who contacted senior Israeli officials demanding his release.[16] Washington took particular issue with a U.S. citizen being held without charge by an allied country.[16] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Kushner "seems like a good man who takes a lot of flak from various segments of the MAGA coalition," and that the episode — likely a favor requested by Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas — showed both his clout among Israelis and his willingness to use it for good.[16] Kushner was also named as a contributor, alongside Steve Witkoff and Marco Rubio, to Trump's 28-point Ukraine peace plan.[16]

## Contacts with Sergey Kislyak and the Russia back channel

In "Setting the Stage," Joe Lange examines reporting by The Independent that Kushner had at least three previously undisclosed contacts with Russian ambassador [[sergey-kislyak|Sergey Kislyak]] during and after the 2016 campaign, including two phone calls between April and November of that year, and that he had become a focus of the FBI's collusion inquiry after investigators began scrutinising [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]]'s Russian contacts.[18] Crucially, the same reporting stated that Kushner was not a target of the investigation — a detail Lange stresses as evidence that [[james-comey|Comey]]'s FBI had nothing on either man despite extensive surveillance.[18]

Before the election, Kislyak's undisclosed discussions with Kushner and Flynn reportedly focused on counterterrorism and improving U.S.–Russian economic relations; after the November 8 election, the two discussed with Kislyak creating a back channel between [[donald-trump|Trump]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] that would bypass diplomats and intelligence agencies.[18] Lange treats this as evidence of a [[sovereign-alliance|sovereign alliance]] against the cabal and its personnel in the State Department and intelligence community, and compares it to Kushner's earlier back channel with Chinese President Xi Jinping, which he argues allowed both leaders to circumvent the deep state in their own countries.[18]

On this reading the leak of Kushner's Kislyak communications to the press was not counterintelligence but an attempt to shut the channel down, and Lange raises the possibility that Kushner or Trump himself asked Kislyak to call Flynn about the new Obama sanctions in order to bait the trap that forced Comey to move.[18] Lange describes Kushner as Trump's most trusted aide, routinely tasked with important work behind the scenes.[18]

## The Xi back channel and the Abraham Accords (Lange, 2023)

In "The Master Builder" (February 2, 2023), [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presented Kushner as the private channel through which Beijing chose to communicate with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], citing NPR reporting that ambassador [[cui-tiankai|Cui Tiankai]] said China had to "find a channel through which to communicate with Trump" and found one in Trump's son-in-law, who helped arrange the April 2017 summit between [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] and Trump at [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]].[19] Lange stressed that Mar-a-Lago is outfitted with a [[sensitive-compartmented-information-facility|SCIF]], allowing the two leaders to speak in private and, in his reading, beyond the reach of the [[deep-state|Deep State]].[19]

The article treats concealed effectiveness as the defining pattern of Kushner's career, noting that his role running the 2016 campaign only became public after the election.[19] It quotes Forbes reporting in which Peter Thiel said that "if Trump was the CEO, Jared was effectively the chief operating officer," and Eric Schmidt, the former Google chief executive who helped design the Clinton campaign's technology system, called Kushner "the biggest surprise of the 2016 election" and said that as best he could tell Kushner "actually ran the campaign and did it with essentially no resources."[19] Forbes credited Kushner with running the campaign's secret data operation like a Silicon Valley startup and tipping the states that decided the election.[19]

Lange extends the same pattern to the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]], which Kushner spearheaded and which surprised the world when announced.[19] Quoting [[aryeh-lightstone|Aryeh Lightstone]] in Newsweek, the article notes that there were no public negotiations and no strategic leaks; everyone involved held a high-level security clearance so that discretion was mandatory, and the participants preferred that discretion to selective leaking — a factor Lightstone considered crucial to success.[19] Kushner was subsequently nominated for a Nobel Peace Prize for his role as negotiator.[19]
