# Manhattan Company

The Manhattan Company was a nominal water utility created and run by [[aaron-burr|Aaron Burr]] from April 1799 onward which, in the account of [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], was transformed into a speculative bank and became the seed of Wall Street and ultimately of [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]].[1]

## The regulatory dodge

Ehret describes the company as among the most destructive acts of sabotage against the young republic's financial order.[1] The fatal poison in Burr's water scheme was his insertion of a clause giving the company's directors the right to spend its capital on any business endeavor they chose, having nothing to do with water projects.[1] The company built hardly any actual water infrastructure during its forty years of existence.[1] By September 1799 it had opened a "house of certificate and deposit at 40 Wall Street" under the name The Manhattan Company Bank.[1]

## Legacy

This was the seed which grew, through more than 1,200 subsequent mergers and acquisitions, into Chase Manhattan and JP Morgan.[1] Writing on May 21, 2025, Gary Leff noted that JP Morgan Chase "was founded by Aaron Burr… it was a water company, a regulatory dodge to circumvent protectionist legislation," encompassing over 1,200 predecessor banks and owning the pistols used in the Hamilton–Burr duel.[1] Those pistols — the same weapons used in 1802 at the same New Jersey location in the duel that killed [[alexander-hamilton|Hamilton]]'s son Philip at the hands of an agent of Burr — are displayed on the executive conference floor of the JP Morgan Chase building at 277 Park Avenue in New York City.[1]

Ehret contrasts this speculative lineage with Hamilton's [[first-bank-of-the-united-states|national bank]], whose dominant owner was always meant to be the US government, whose directors all had to live in the USA, and 70% of whose state branch directors were obliged to live in their respective states — unlike the later JP Morgan-owned [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] established as an independent entity in 1913.[1]
