# Chabad-Lubavitch

Chabad-Lubavitch is an Ultra-Orthodox Jewish movement with a substantial community in New York City.[1]

In March 2026 the movement re-entered American political controversy when [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] published a podcast episode highlighting Chabad-Lubavitch and what Badlands writers characterized as its "dubious history."[1] The episode prompted Florida politician [[randy-fine|Randy Fine]] to call Carlson a "cancer" on March 5, 2026.[1]

Badlands coverage recalled that two years earlier the movement's headquarters had been raided by the New York Police Department after "secret tunnels" were discovered beneath the synagogue facility.[1] The episode is presented as one of the proximate triggers of the wider campaign to have Carlson silenced or prosecuted, and thus part of the broader dispute over the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[1]

## Carlson's Iran war claim (March 2026)

In March 2026 [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] claimed that Chabad-Lubavitch was responsible for the ongoing war involving [[iran|Iran]], asserting that the movement was a "hidden hand" behind the conflict and was pushing for a broader religious war connected to rebuilding the Jewish Temple in Jerusalem.[2] Chabad-Lubavitch is a global Hasidic Jewish religious movement known for operating Jewish community centers and outreach programs worldwide.[2]

Critics described Carlson's claim as a conspiracy theory, and Jewish security organizations warned that such rhetoric could contribute to an increase in antisemitic narratives online.[2] Carlson has been among the commentators criticizing US and Israeli military actions against Iran, arguing that the conflict serves Israeli strategic interests rather than American ones and that it was initiated at the urging of Israeli leadership.[2] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] welcomed the [[third-temple|Third Temple]] question reaching a mass audience, framing it as a theological dispute over [[christian-zionism|Christian Zionism]] rather than a purely geopolitical one.[2]
