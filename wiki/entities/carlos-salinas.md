# Carlos Salinas

Carlos Salinas de Gortari (born 1948) served as the 60th President of Mexico from 1988 to 1994. In the Badlands Media article [[a-family-affair]], he is portrayed as a central figure in a corruption network linking the salinas family to drug trafficking, political cover-ups, and the [[nafta]] agreement, which the source argues was designed to facilitate drug smuggling across the southern border and increase [[cia]] profits.

## Presidency and NAFTA

Carlos Salinas negotiated [[nafta]], the North American Free Trade Agreement, which the article frames as a mechanism for easing drug trafficking across the Mexican border. The source claims NAFTA was "all about increasing drug trafficking across the southern border in order to increase the CIA's profits," and that the salinas family was the Mexican partner in this arrangement.

## Connection to the Gulf Cartel

The article links Carlos Salinas and his brother [[raul-salinas]] to the [[gulf-cartel]], led by [[juan-garcia-abrego]]. According to the source, the Salinas family provided "green light protection" for Gulf Cartel drugs moving through Mexico. The [[gulf-cartel]] is described as having transformed from smugglers into cocaine suppliers through a deal with the [[cali-cartel]], securing 50 percent of every Colombian shipment as payment for delivery.

## José Francisco Ruiz Massieu murder cover-up

In September 1994, José Francisco Ruiz Massieu—secretary general and second-ranking official of the [[institutional-revolutionary-party]] (PRI)—was assassinated. According to [[the-washington-post]] and Mexican court records cited in the article, [[raul-salinas]] was convicted as the "intellectual author" of the murder. José Francisco Ruiz Massieu had previously been married to Adriana Salinas, sister of Raul and Carlos.

The source describes a bizarre twist: Carlos Salinas appointed the victim's brother, [[mario-ruiz-massieu]], as special prosecutor to investigate the assassination. Mario resigned claiming party leaders were blocking the investigation, but later evidence suggested he had altered witness statements to delete references to Raul Salinas's role. The article argues this was a deliberate cover-up orchestrated by the Salinas family.

## Post-presidency and exile

After leaving office, Carlos Salinas went into self-imposed exile, similar to [[carlos-andres-perez]] of Venezuela, who is also described in the source as a corrupt leader installed with CIA assistance who later fled to miami. The article draws a parallel between the two leaders, suggesting a shared "CIA template" for corrupt governance and escape.
