# Michel Barnier

Michel Barnier is a French politician who served as Prime Minister of France, leading a minority government that faced near-certain defeat in a no-confidence vote in December 2024.[1]

## The December 2024 no-confidence crisis

Barnier's administration, which took office only in September 2024, faced a no-confidence vote on Wednesday, December 4, that could dramatically intensify the political crisis in one of the European Union's key member states.[1] Were the vote carried, his government would be the first in France to be ousted by a motion of no confidence since 1962, and its fall — at the hands of far-right and leftwing parties — would be a significant blow to Europe weeks before [[donald-trump|Donald Trump's]] return to the White House.[1]

Barnier told MPs on Tuesday that the vote risked making "everything more difficult and more serious," adding that France's situation was already "difficult in budgetary and financial terms" and "very difficult in economic and social terms."[1] In a television interview late Tuesday he warned there was "a lot of tension in France" but argued that for the country to regain political stability he should remain in office, adding: "The moment is serious. It is difficult, but the stakes are not impossible."[1]

Hardline interior minister Bruno Retailleau said that toppling the government would "throw France and the French into an insufferable situation," accusing those backing the motion of playing "Russian roulette" with the country's future.[1] A parliamentary debate was due to start at 4 p.m. local time, followed by a vote roughly three hours later; two separate no-confidence motions had been tabled, by the far-left and far-right opposition, with the former widely predicted to pass.[1] President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]], on a visit to Saudi Arabia, was expected to return to France for what media described as a "moment of truth" risking "plunging France into the great political and financial unknown."[1]

The crisis coincided with Macron's hosting of the [[notre-dame-cathedral|Notre Dame]] reopening, an event [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would attend.[1]
