# Trevor McFadden

**Trevor McFadden** is a United States District Judge presiding over the federal prosecution of the man accused of attempting to assassinate President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the [[white-house-correspondents-association-dinner|White House Correspondents' Association dinner]].[1]

At a May 2026 hearing at which the defendant pleaded not guilty, McFadden considered a defense motion to remove Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] and US Attorney [[jeanine-pirro|Jeanine Pirro]] from the case on conflict-of-interest grounds.[1] He did not immediately rule, instead asking the defense to further explain the scope of its request, which counsel indicated could extend to the recusal of Pirro's entire office.[1] The case is described at [[whca-dinner-assassination-attempt-prosecution|the WHCA dinner assassination attempt prosecution]].[1]

## Ruling on the immigrant registration rule (April 2025)

McFadden, appointed by [[donald-trump|Trump]], ruled in favor of the administration in its defense of a mandatory registration requirement for non-citizens, accepting the argument that the mandate was not new but rather an enforcement of existing federal law applying to all non-citizens.[2] He did not weigh in on the merits of the policy itself, instead ruling that the organizations challenging it lacked legal standing to bring the case.[2] The measure took effect that Friday; the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] said individuals in the country more than 30 days had to register, and administration estimates put the affected population at between 2.2 million and 3.2 million people.[2] See [[2025-alien-registration-requirement|2025 alien registration requirement]].[2]

## Denial of the AP's emergency access request (February 2025)

On February 24, 2025, McFadden denied the [[associated-press|Associated Press]]'s request to immediately restore its reporters' access to White House events and to Air Force One after they were banned over the outlet's refusal to adopt "Gulf of America" in its style guide.[3] He allowed the ban to remain in place while the litigation proceeds, saying the case differed from past disputes because it concerned specific areas rather than broad White House access, and put the matter on an expedited track while promising to keep an "open mind."[3] At that early stage, he held, the AP had not met its burden for immediate judicial intervention.[3] See [[associated-press-white-house-access-ban|Associated Press White House access ban]].[3]
