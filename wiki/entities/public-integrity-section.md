# Public Integrity Section

The Public Integrity Section (PIN) is an office of the United States [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] originally established in the aftermath of Watergate to oversee investigations into corruption among lawmakers.[1] Badlands Media commentary argues that over the intervening decades the section devolved from a corruption watchdog into a protective bottleneck for the political class, since prosecutions of federal officials generally required its sign-off.[1]

## 2025 restructuring

In March 2025, Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] launched an internal initiative to restructure PIN.[1] Under her plan, U.S. Attorneys may now indict federal lawmakers without prior sign-off from PIN; DOJ whistleblower protection protocols were expanded so that sealed indictments can proceed even when internal consensus is blocked; and a [[continuity-of-government|COG]]-authorised task force was activated under FISA-secured oversight to pursue cases involving election fraud, misuse of classified information, and financial bribery.[1]

Bondi framed the reform in terms of prosecutorial neutrality, stating at an April 2025 hearing that "the discretion afforded Department attorneys… does not include latitude to substitute personal political views or judgments for those that prevailed in the election."[1]

## Criticism

Former federal prosecutor Barbara McQuade warned that Bondi's associated "weaponization working group" could undermine public confidence by targeting political opponents.[1] Civil liberties organisations argued more specifically that bypassing PIN could increase the incidence of politically motivated prosecutions, since the section had functioned as an internal check on charging decisions against elected officials.[1] As of the article's publication no specific indictments of judges or lawmakers had been publicly reported under the new procedures.[1]
