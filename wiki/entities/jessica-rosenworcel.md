# Jessica Rosenworcel

Jessica Rosenworcel is an American telecommunications regulator who served as Chair of the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]] during the 2024 election cycle.[1]

Badlands Media identified Rosenworcel as a Democrat and as the chair of the five-member commission when the [[center-for-american-rights|Center for American Rights]] filed its news-distortion complaint against [[cbs-news|CBS News]] in October 2024.[1] The commission's composition at the time was described as 3–2 in favor of Democratic appointees: Rosenworcel and Geoffrey Starks and Anna Gomez as Democrats, with [[brendan-carr|Brendan Carr]] and Nathan Simington as Republicans.[1]

Because FCC commissioners are appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate and the agency rules by committee, Badlands argued that the Democratic majority under Rosenworcel made it unlikely the CBS complaint would advance beyond rhetoric before January 20, 2025.[1] The same commentary contrasted the commission's aggressive posture toward regulating the internet and independent creators with what it characterized as reluctance to hold legacy broadcasters accountable.[1]

## Reaffirming the Starlink subsidy denial

As FCC Chair, Jessica Rosenworcel defended the commission's December 2023 decision to reaffirm its denial of $885.5 million in rural broadband subsidies to SpaceX's Starlink unit, saying, "The FCC followed a careful legal, technical and policy review to determine that this applicant had failed to meet its burden."[2] The commission concluded Starlink had failed basic program requirements and could not demonstrate it would deliver the promised service, citing among other factors SpaceX's failure to successfully launch Starship.[2] The two Republican commissioners dissented, framing the ruling as part of a Biden administration pattern of regulatory action against Elon Musk's businesses.[2]
