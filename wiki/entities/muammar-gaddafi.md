# Muammar Gaddafi

Muammar Gaddafi was the leader of Libya until he was overthrown and killed during the 2011 insurgency. The article notes that his name is transliterated many ways and adopts the spelling "Gaddafi."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) He was killed on approximately 20 October 2011, five days before CNBC published its account of the Iraq cash airlift.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Revisionist portrayal

The article inverts the conventional depiction of Gaddafi as a state sponsor of terrorism. It argues that Gaddafi was in fact fighting the terrorists of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] inside his own country, arresting large numbers of them and driving many others to flee Libya from the late 1990s onward.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The image of Gaddafi as a terror sponsor, comparable to Saddam Hussein, is attributed to a CIA-controlled media practice of painting agency targets as the world's worst enemies.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Cooperation with Washington

After the 9/11 attacks Gaddafi took President [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]]'s war on terror as a serious threat, voluntarily abandoned his weapons of mass destruction programmes, and made peace with Western nations including the United States.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) Under a deal with the Bush administration he accepted custody of [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] and other captured LIFG members, imprisoning Belhaj for several years.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) In 2009, with [[barack-obama|Obama]] newly in office and Gaddafi seeking to maintain good relations with the new administration, Belhaj's release was negotiated via the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] preacher Ali Sallabi and Gaddafi's son Saïf al-Islam.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Overthrow

In 2011 an insurgency swept Libya from [[benghazi|Benghazi]]. [[john-mccain|John McCain]] called for U.S. military intervention in February 2011, weeks before the UN Security Council authorised action and a no-fly zone, and in April visited Benghazi to praise the anti-Gaddafi rebels as "heroes" and to demand recognition of the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) With NATO's help the uprising toppled Gaddafi, and Belhaj — commanding the [[february-17-brigade|February 17 Brigade]] — led the force that brought him down.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) The article argues that post-Gaddafi Libya became a money-laundering operation "much worse" than Iraq and a regional hub for training Islamist extremists.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Aftermath and the arms pipeline

Badlands Media's follow-up feature restates that removing Gaddafi did not bring peace and freedom to the Libyan people as Obama, Hillary Clinton and McCain had promised, but the opposite, because Gaddafi had not been a dictator killing his own people so much as a ruler fighting [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] within his borders.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) With his government toppled, Libya became the perfect staging ground for lucrative weapons deals arming the proxy force in Syria.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

Under the Gaddafi regime the Libyan armed forces had acquired sophisticated Russian-made Grinch SA-24 missiles capable of downing a warplane at up to 11,000 feet, and a Western official working on Libyan weapons recovery estimated that of a pre-revolution stock of some 25,000 surface-to-air missiles, fewer than 5,000 units were accounted for.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Human Rights Watch documented widespread looting of thousands of SA-24 and SA-7 missiles.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Badlands notes that the militia which ultimately rescued the Americans trapped at the CIA annex in Benghazi was a Libyan force made up of ex-Gaddafi soldiers.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)
