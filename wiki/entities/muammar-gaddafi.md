# Muammar Gaddafi

Muammar Gaddafi was the leader of Libya until his death on 20 October 2011, five days before CNBC published its report on the Iraq cash airlift.[1] The Badlands Media article "A Conflict of Interest" presents a revisionist account of Gaddafi, arguing that the standard media portrayal of him as a sponsor of terrorism was a CIA-shaped narrative comparable to the treatment of Saddam Hussein.[1]

## Fighting the LIFG

The article contends that Gaddafi was in fact waging war against terrorists inside his own country, arresting many members of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]], which is why Treasury records note that many LIFG members fled Libya for other countries from the late 1990s.[1]

## Post-9/11 cooperation

After the 9/11 attacks Gaddafi treated President George W. Bush's war on terror as a serious threat, voluntarily gave up his weapons of mass destruction programs and made peace with Western nations including the United States.[1] Under a deal with the Bush administration he took custody of [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] and other captured LIFG members, imprisoning Belhaj for several years.[1] In 2009 his son Saïf al-Islam negotiated Belhaj's release with [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] preacher [[ali-sallabi|Ali Sallabi]]; the article suggests Gaddafi complied with an Obama administration request in order to maintain goodwill with the new president.[1]

## Overthrow

In 2011 [[john-mccain|John McCain]] visited [[benghazi|Benghazi]], praised the anti-Gaddafi rebels as "heroes," and lobbied for recognition of the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]] and for arming and training the insurgents.[1] Belhaj, whom Gaddafi had released two years earlier, led the [[february-17-brigade|February 17 Brigade]] that toppled and killed him.[1] The article characterizes the resulting Libya as a money-laundering operation "much worse" than Iraq and a regional hub for Islamist training.[1]
