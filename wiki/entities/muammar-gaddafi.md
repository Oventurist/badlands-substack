# Muammar Gaddafi

Muammar Gaddafi was the longtime ruler of Libya, in power from 1969 until his death in 2011. In the Badlands Media article "A Conflict of Interest," Gaddafi is framed as a target of a deliberate U.S.-backed regime change operation that served as a cover for large-scale diversion of taxpayer funds to rebel groups, some of which the source identifies as terrorist organizations.

According to the article, Gaddafi had been fighting the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] (LIFG) within Libya for years, and LIFG members had fled the country in the late 1990s to escape his crackdown. The source claims that despite this, the CIA-controlled U.S. media portrayed Gaddafi as a "big sponsor of terrorism," using the same framing tactics applied to Saddam Hussein in Iraq.

The article asserts that Gaddafi took the post-9/11 "war on terror" seriously, voluntarily gave up Libya's weapons of mass destruction programs, and made peace with Western nations including the United States. It claims that the U.S. under [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] struck a deal with Gaddafi to turn over captured LIFG members, including [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]], who then spent years in Libyan prison.

According to the source, Gaddafi was overthrown and killed in October 2011 following a NATO military intervention that the article frames as illegitimate and driven by financial and geopolitical interests rather than humanitarian concerns. The article specifically links his fall to the ambitions of [[john-mccain|John McCain]], who visited Benghazi in April 2011 to praise anti-Gaddafi rebels, and to [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], whose administration the source claims had strong ties to the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]]. The piece notes that five days after Gaddafi's death, a CNBC report revealed that Basel—the man who had handled $40 billion in Iraqi cash shipments—was negotiating a new contract to transport billions of dollars into Libya, which the source interprets as evidence that the same theft mechanism was being replicated.
