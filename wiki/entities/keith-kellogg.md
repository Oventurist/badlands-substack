# Keith Kellogg

Keith Kellogg is President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s special envoy for [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and Russia in the second Trump administration.[1]

## February 2025 Ukraine diplomacy

Kellogg said on Saturday, February 15, 2025, that while Kyiv would be involved in peace talks, European nations would not — a position contradicted publicly by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] on CBS's "Face the Nation" the following day.[1] Rubio styled the upcoming Riyadh meeting as a follow-up to Trump's phone call with Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], cautioning that "one phone call does not make peace. One phone call does not solve a war as complex as this one."[1] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] asked whether the mixed messaging amounted to a good cop, bad cop routine.[1]

Several Western media outlets expressed surprise at Kellogg's absence from the U.S. delegation to the [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] talks, which was instead represented by Rubio, national security adviser [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]] and Middle East special envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]]; neither Ukrainian nor EU diplomats attended.[1]


## Call for Maximum Pressure on Iran (January 2025)

Speaking at an Iranian opposition event in Paris on Saturday, January 11, 2025, Keith Kellogg — [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s incoming special envoy on the Ukraine conflict — called for reviving a [[maximum-pressure-campaign|"maximum pressure" campaign]] against [[iran|Iran]], stressing the need for comprehensive measures to encourage democratic reforms in the Islamic Republic.[2] The event was organized by the [[national-council-of-resistance-of-iran|National Council of Resistance of Iran]] (NCRI); Kellogg, a retired US Army lieutenant general, had attended more than one NCRI event, which analysts said suggested ties between the group and the incoming administration.[2] He has repeatedly accused Iran of aiding [[russia|Russia]] in the Ukraine conflict, which Moscow has denied.[2]

"These pressures are not just kinetic, not just military force, but they must be economic and diplomatic as well," Kellogg said, adding that action "to change Iran for the better" should be taken without delay and that "we must exploit the weakness we now see."[2] Tehran did not immediately comment, but the Iranian Foreign Ministry condemned France for hosting the NCRI gathering, which Iran considers a terrorist organization.[2]

## Appointment as special envoy for Ukraine and Russia (November 2024)

On November 27, 2024, President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] picked Kellogg to serve as special envoy for [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and Russia, a newly conceived role created in response to the ongoing war between the two countries.[3] Kellogg, an 80-year-old retired U.S. Army lieutenant general, would take up the post as Russia's invasion entered its third year.[3] "Keith has led a distinguished Military and Business career, including serving in highly sensitive National Security roles in my first Administration. He was with me right from the beginning! Together, we will secure PEACE THROUGH STRENGTH, and Make America, and the World, SAFE AGAIN!" Trump wrote in a Truth Social post.[3]

Kellogg had previously served as national security adviser to Vice President [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]] during Trump's first presidency, and later became acting national security adviser to Trump himself after [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] resigned in 2017.[3] Describing his plan to end the war, Kellogg told Reuters in June 2024 that he would emphasize bringing both parties to the negotiating table: "We tell the Ukrainians: 'You've got to come to the table, and if you don't come to the table, support from the United States will dry up,'" he said, adding that Moscow would be told that if [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] refused to negotiate, "then we'll give Ukrainians everything they need to kill you in the field."[3] Early reactions to Kellogg's potential confirmation were tepid; one anonymous Washington-based security analyst told The Hill it was "a gulp – not horrible, not amazing."[3]

## Badlands assessment of the appointment

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] contrasted the appointment with contemporaneous remarks by [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]], writing that "the Mike Waltz comments on Ukraine were noise. The Keith Kellogg comments on Ukraine are signal."[3] He described Kellogg as one of the more important figures in [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]]'s theory of [[devolution|Devolution]], and argued that Trump's creation of an entirely new diplomatic position intended to thwart a third world war, with Kellogg at its helm, was "not at all insignificant."[3] According to reports cited in the Brief, it was Kellogg who wrote the June 2024 proposal for the Ukraine peace plan Trump touted on the campaign trail, and who had gained a reputation during the [[iraq-war|Iraq War]] as an "expeditor" skilled at cutting through bureaucratic red tape.[3]

The same commentary questioned whether the objective was truly to end the war as quickly as possible, speculating that if a legitimate threat and trafficking victims remained in parts of Ukraine, allowing the Russian military to continue its advance might be preferable — a rationale offered for why Kellogg and others had telegraphed continued limited arming of Ukraine.[3] Kellogg's appointment was nonetheless characterized as "a major indicator of positive things to come."[3] In a lighter aside, the writer noted that Kellogg shares a name with Will Keith Kellogg, inventor of Corn Flakes, and connected this to Chuck Grassley's "harvesting the corn" posts and to reports that corn was Ukraine's top crop in 2024.[3]

**Another Take:** [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] called Kellogg "one of the most signal names in the Info War," tying the [[q-operation|Q Op]] to Devolution theory while hinting at how compromised the Deep State was heading into Trump 2.0, concluding: "They lost the game some time ago. Panic in DC."[3]

## Comments on the Kirillov assassination (December 2024)

As President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s incoming Ukraine envoy, Kellogg was asked on Fox News in December 2024 about the assassination of Russian general [[igor-kirillov|Igor Kirillov]] in Moscow.[4] He said he did not really think it was a setback for peace talks, but cautioned that "there are rules for warfare and there are certain things you just don't do," citing prohibitions on killing wounded soldiers and non-combatants.[4] "When you kill commanders, senior officers, admirals, or generals in their hometowns, it's kind of like you extended it and I don't think it's really smart to do it. It's just kind of not the rules of war," he said.[4]
