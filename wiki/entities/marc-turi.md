# Marc Turi

Marc Turi is an American private arms dealer whose attempted 2011 weapons deal for Libya, subsequent federal prosecution in Arizona, and connections to [[john-mccain|Senator John McCain]], the [[state-department|State Department]] and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] made him a central figure in the Badlands Media account of the [[benghazi-attack|Benghazi]] arms-trafficking story.[1]

## Early life and identity

According to reporting by *AZ Central*, Turi was born Marc Chapman. In March 1987, as an Arizona State University sophomore, he took roughly $10,000 worth of computer equipment from Old Main, a historic building on the Tempe campus, and within months was a convicted felon stripped of civil rights and barred from possessing firearms.[1] He has also acknowledged stealing his roommate's car and writing bad checks, including one for $100,000, and served time in an Arizona jail.[1] He was discharged from the United States Navy "other than honorable," a fact he says he has been "fighting ever since to get that honor back."[1] The Badlands article treats the name change from Chapman to Turi, combined with his later career, as evidence that he was given a new identity and functioned as a CIA asset specializing in weapons sales in war zones.[1]

## Arms dealing career

Turi entered the arms business in the 1990s, describing his work as "transportation, logistics, and liaising with those foreign governments."[1] He was one of several thousand U.S. arms contractors licensed by the State Department to sell and move weapons internationally and became, in Fox Business's phrasing, a "go to guy" for the U.S. government, most recently in Iraq and [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]].[1] By 2010 his firm [[turi-defense-group|Turi Defense Group]] had supplied $14 million in arms to Afghanistan's CIA-supported National Directorate of Security.[1] In a March 2011 message to a McCain staffer, Turi described his company as "the single largest private supplier of weapons and ammunition into Afghanistan."[1]

## The Libya deal

In March 2011, amid the Arab Spring, Turi Defense Group applied to the State Department for a permit to ship "$195 million worth of Eastern European light and heavy armament and ammunition" to Libya, including mortars, machine guns, anti-tank rockets, combat rifles and millions of rounds of ammunition.[1] While the request was pending, [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and [[christopher-stevens|Chris Stevens]] met with the leader of the rebels' [[national-transitional-council|National Transitional Council]], and [[barack-obama|President Obama]] authorized air attacks on Gaddafi's military.[1] The application was denied on March 22, 2011, because of the U.S. and United Nations arms embargo on Libya.[1]

Turi then submitted an identical weapons list listing [[qatar|Qatar]] as the recipient — a transshipment route to Libya through a state with close CIA ties.[1] One week before Stevens was sworn in as ambassador, Turi exchanged emails with him seeking help with the permit; Stevens replied that he would "keep it in mind and share it with my colleagues in Washington."[1] That same month Clinton messaged a State Department colleague that "the idea of using private security experts to arm the opposition should be considered."[1] The Qatar application was approved, with the price tag set at $276 million "because the U.S. Dept of State denied Libya," against an overall deal worth $534 million.[1]

## Raid, prosecution and dismissal

On July 22, 2011, roughly twenty heavily armed Department of Homeland Security and Department of Defense agents raided Turi's Scottsdale residence with a search warrant. The raid drew no news coverage, no charges were filed at the time, and Turi remained free; he says the State Department letter authorizing the Qatar shipment arrived two days after the raid, by which point the deal was dead.[1] In December 2012 the *New York Times* reported that the Obama administration had "secretly gave its blessing to arms shipments to Libyan rebels from Qatar," noting Turi Defense Group was under investigation although it never actually sent weapons to Libya; Turi said his pipeline was shut down "because he was getting in the way of the Obama administration's dealings with Qatar."[1]

After 31 months of inaction, a federal grand jury in Phoenix returned a four-count indictment on February 11, 2014, accusing Turi of lying to the State Department and violating U.S. arms-export laws, on the theory that the Qatar deal was intended to evade the Libyan embargo.[1] His attorney, Phoenix lawyer Jean-Jacques Cabou, built a "public authority defense," asserting Turi's plan was government-authorized, that Turi was a CIA contractor, and that the United States "did in fact covertly arm the Libyan rebels using a plan remarkably similar to the one at issue here."[1] A May 2016 defense slideshow for Justice Department lawyers stressed that Turi had pursued the deal openly, received the Qatar permit, and had contacted McCain's office for support.[1]

Prosecutors attempted to invoke the [[state-secrets-privilege|state-secrets privilege]] with limited success. With trial scheduled to begin on Election Day 2016, U.S. District Judge David Campbell ordered prosecutors to hand over key documents by October 5; with two days to spare, the Justice Department agreed to throw out its five-year criminal case.[1] The Badlands account reads this as the DOJ being forced to drop the prosecution rather than produce evidence implicating Clinton.[1]

## Interpretation in the Badlands account

Joe Lange's article argues Turi's Arizona base, Navy discharge and criminal-to-contractor trajectory point to a direct relationship with McCain, noting that within weeks of Turi's March 2011 message to McCain's staffer, the senator became a leading public voice for arming Libya's anti-Gadhafi forces and urged Western powers to "facilitate" weapons and training for the rebels.[1] In this telling, McCain and Turi expected to profit from arming [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]]-linked fighters in Benghazi, but Clinton cut her own deal routing benefit through the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] via [[sidney-blumenthal|Sidney Blumenthal]] and [[osprey-global-solutions|Osprey Global Solutions]], leaving Turi cut out and targeted to keep him quiet.[1]
