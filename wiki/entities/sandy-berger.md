# Sandy Berger

Samuel R. "Sandy" Berger was President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]]'s National Security Adviser, and is discussed in the article for his removal of classified documents from the National Archives.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

According to the Washington Post account quoted in the article, on the evening of October 2, 2003 Berger stashed highly classified documents taken from the National Archives beneath a construction trailer at Ninth Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW so he could retrieve them later and take them to his office.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) The documents detailed how the Clinton administration had responded to the threat of terrorist attacks at the end of 1999; Berger removed five copies of the same document without authorization and later used scissors to destroy three before placing them in his office trash, according to a November 4, 2005 National Archives inspector general report.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty) Confronted, he told investigators he "tried to find the trash collector but had no luck," and initially claimed he had removed the documents by mistake rather than admitting he had stuffed them into his suit pockets on multiple occasions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)

The article frames this as a panicked act tied to the [[nine-eleven-commission|9/11 Commission]] that Congress had established to examine government failures, arguing Clinton feared exposure of the memorandum that had barred killing [[osama-bin-laden|Osama Bin Laden]] and the blame that would follow.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-dereliction-of-duty)
