# McGeorge Bundy

**McGeorge Bundy** was a United States National Security Advisor and later president of the [[ford-foundation|Ford Foundation]], cited in the Badlands Media essay "The Ivy Dominion" as an exemplar of the traffic between intelligence, government and philanthropic institutions.[1]

The article identifies Bundy as a [[skull-and-bones|Skull & Bones]] alumnus who "transitioned from National Security Advisor to president of the Ford Foundation, continuing covert foreign influence through academia."[1] He is invoked alongside his brother [[william-bundy|William Bundy]] to support the claim that Yale's secret society network seeded leadership across the CIA, the [[state-department|State Department]] and intelligence-adjacent financial and philanthropic institutions — and that foundations such as Ford and [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller]] serve as "laundromats for psychological operations" inside the alleged [[ivy-dominion|Ivy Dominion]].[1]

## Founding of the Trilateral Commission

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 2" lists Bundy — former National Security Advisor to both John F. Kennedy and Lyndon B. Johnson, and a member of the purportedly elite Bundy family — among the attendees of the private meeting at [[kykuit|Kykuit]] on July 23–24, 1972 at which the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] began organizing.[2] Those present were hand-selected by [[david-rockefeller|David Rockefeller]] and [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]].[2]

## Bones lineage

"The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 8" places McGeorge Bundy in the Yale class of 1940 as a member of [[skull-and-bones|Skull and Bones]], following his father [[hollister-bundy|Hollister Bundy]] and joined by his brother [[william-bundy|William Bundy]].[3] The brothers are described as having exercised significant impact on the flow of information and intelligence during the Vietnam War from posts in the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] and the [[state-department|State Department]], and as Special Assistants to Presidents Kennedy and Johnson; McGeorge subsequently became president of the [[ford-foundation|Ford Foundation]].[3] [[antony-sutton|Antony Sutton]] cited the Whitney–Stimson–Bundy sequence as the exemplary "vertical chain" of the Order's influence.[3]
