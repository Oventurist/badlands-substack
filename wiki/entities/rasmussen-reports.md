# Rasmussen Reports

**Rasmussen Reports** is an American polling organization whose survey data on public attitudes toward the press is cited in alternative-media commentary.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-hypnotists-take-on-israel-palestine)

## Cited polling on media trust

An October 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] essay cites Rasmussen for the finding that 58% of Americans believe the media is deliberately misleading them — reported by the pollster under the headline that 58% of voters agree the media are the "enemy of the people."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-hypnotists-take-on-israel-palestine) The essay's author, [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], argues that this number is reasonably expected to climb sharply in the following months, while cautioning that many of those same respondents likely believe *their own* preferred outlets could not be deceiving them, because tribal loyalty keeps audiences tethered to old programming.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-hypnotists-take-on-israel-palestine) The figure is used as evidence that the [[mockingbird-media|Mockingbird media]] edifice is cracking.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-hypnotists-take-on-israel-palestine)

## Election-integrity polling (2026)

Rasmussen Reports said a survey of 1,098 likely U.S. voters conducted 8–10 March 2026 found 63% supported the [[save-act|SAVE Act]] and 32% opposed it.[2] The firm also published question wording for a 10–12 May 2026 election-integrity survey of 1,060 likely voters.[2]

## October 2023 US–Israel relations survey

In October 2023 Rasmussen shared with the Washington Examiner a survey on American attitudes toward [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s handling of Israel, conducted hours before he boarded Air Force One for the Middle East.[3] It found 43 percent of voters believed US–Israel relations had gotten "worse" under Biden, 17 percent "better," and 29 percent that they had remained the "same" — a margin of more than two to one that relations had soured.[3] The same poll found 63 percent of respondents regarded Israel as an ally, 7 percent as an enemy, and 20 percent "somewhere in between."[3]

## January 2025 Trump approval tracking

Rasmussen's Presidential Tracking Poll at the outset of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second term recorded a net approval of +14, described by Newsweek as a historic high that broke previous records set during his first term.[4] The poll, conducted by the conservative-leaning firm as a two-day average of approximately 829 U.S. likely voters, found 54 percent approving of Trump's job performance and 40 percent disapproving, with a three-day rolling average due for release on January 23, 2025.[4]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cautioned that "polls didn't suddenly become real," but argued the result showed the gap between the institutions and the people widening, adding: "Of course Trump is popular. He was always popular. It's now okay for them to admit it."[4]

## Poll on illegal foreign campaign donations (July 2025)

A Rasmussen Reports national telephone and online survey released in July 2025 found that 81% of likely U.S. voters suspect political campaigns have used illegal foreign donors, and that voters overwhelmingly favor cracking down on such contributions.[5] The survey found that 86% of likely U.S. voters believe it is important to enforce the federal law prohibiting foreigners from making campaign contributions to U.S. politicians, including 69% who consider it very important.[5] Similarly, 85% considered it important to enforce the law prohibiting the use of "straw donors" by political campaigns, including 65% who called it very important.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the results as evidence against the narrative of a bitterly divided 50/50 country, and predicted the numbers would rise further as the [[actblue|ActBlue]] story developed.[5]

## Economic Index, February 2025

The Rasmussen Reports Economic Index rose to 111.3 in February 2025, nearly eight points higher than in January.[6] Rasmussen noted that enthusiasm about the economy had surged during President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term, reaching as high as 147.8 in January 2020 before falling after the coronavirus lockdown threw Americans out of work and closed many businesses.[6] By November 2020 the index had recovered to 126.4, but dropped sharply after Joe Biden was elected, reaching a low of 78.6 in July 2022.[6]

Badlands contributor Ashe in America cited the February reading as evidence that consumer confidence was rising fast only one month into Trump's second term, attributing it to his record of doing what he said he would do, and predicted that confidence would break records before the term was finished.[6]

## 2023 polling cited by Badlands Media

Badlands commentary in July 2023 drew on a series of Rasmussen findings to argue that a supermajority of Americans rejected official narratives: an April 2023 poll finding 65% of likely US voters thought undercover government agents likely helped provoke the January 6 riot (46% "very likely"); 63% saying on July 17 that the country was heading in the wrong direction; 62% expressing distrust of elections on April 20; 88% on May 22 rejecting the claim that the economy was strong; and a June survey showing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] roughly ten points underwater on approval.[7]
