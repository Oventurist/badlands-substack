# Office of War Information

The Office of War Information (OWI) was the United States government's wartime propaganda agency, created in 1942 by President Franklin D. Roosevelt.[1] In the account given by Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]], the OWI was a mid-level agency that joined a host of other wartime bodies — including the War and [[state-department|State]] Departments — in the dissemination of war information and propaganda to the American public.[1]

## Methods

Officials at the OWI used numerous tools to communicate with Americans, among them Hollywood movie studios, radio stations and printing presses.[1] Carlson summarises the agency's purpose in blunt terms: propaganda was created to inspire Americans to join and support America's involvement in the Second World War, and was also used to create and amplify hatred toward the enemy through exaggerated and false claims of the enemy's cruelty and barbarism.[1]

## Legacy and the postwar firewall

The OWI is central to Carlson's argument because it is the clearest example of a period in which the U.S. government openly aimed propaganda at its own citizens.[1] Together with the military's wartime [[psychological-operations|psychological operations]] capability, the OWI's effectiveness convinced postwar policymakers that such tools should continue to be used against foreign enemies or potential enemies but never again against the American people themselves — the reasoning behind the [[smith-mundt-act|Smith-Mundt Act]] of 1948.[1]
