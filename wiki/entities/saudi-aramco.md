# Saudi Aramco

Saudi Aramco is the Saudi state oil company and, as of January 2025, the world's largest oil company.[1] Alongside its core hydrocarbons business, it has been enlisted in the kingdom's effort to build out a critical-minerals industry as part of its long-term diversification away from oil dependence.[1]

## Lithium Joint Venture with Ma'aden (January 2025)

In January 2025 Saudi Arabia announced a joint venture between Aramco and the state-owned mining company [[maaden|Ma'aden]], described as a significant step in the kingdom's push to dominate critical minerals.[1] The partnership aims to extract lithium — essential to electric vehicles and electronic devices — from high-concentration deposits, while exploring cost-effective direct extraction technologies; commercial lithium production is expected to commence by 2027.[1] Aramco's president of exploration and production, Nasser al-Naimi, said the companies expected the partnership to "leverage the world's leading upstream enterprise . . . with a view to meeting the kingdom and potentially the world's projected lithium demand."[1]

The venture was presented as aligned with [[vision-2030|Vision 2030]], the Saudi initiative to reduce dependence on oil by developing the mining sector and other industries.[1]

## Criticism of ESG investing (February 2023)

In February 2023, Aramco publicly warned that [[esg|environmental, social and governance]] investment, if outright biased against the oil and gas industry, is a threat to energy affordability and energy security.[2] Speaking at the Saudi Capital Markets Forum 2023, the company's president and chief executive [[amin-nasser|Amin Nasser]] said ESG was clearly a rising trend in capital markets and that increased emphasis on it was "a move in the right direction," but cautioned that ESG-driven policies implemented with an automatic bias against any and all conventional energy projects would produce underinvestment with serious implications for the global economy, energy affordability and energy security.[2] Nasser said the cost of capital for the oil and gas sector had risen because of higher perceived risks driven by ESG policies.[2]

The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] contributor [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]] noted that the intervention carried weight because Aramco is the world's largest oil firm, and that with Saudi Arabia likely to join [[brics|BRICS]] in 2023 — further threatening the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]]'s global reserve status — the statements were all the more intriguing.[2]
