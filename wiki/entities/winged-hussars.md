# Winged Hussars

The Winged Hussars were the heavy shock cavalry of Poland, a force that became dominant on the plains of Eastern Europe and is best remembered for its role in the 1683 [[battle-of-vienna|Battle of Vienna]].[1]

## Origins

Badlands traces the Hussars' equestrian tradition to the [[lipka-tatars|Lipka Tartars]], an all-Muslim unit maintained by Poland whose lineage ran back to the Mongolian Golden Horde.[1] Exiled from Hungary in the 1500s, they formed a mercenary group in Poland and shared their mastery of horses with the Poles, a transfer that produced the Winged Hussars.[1] Europeans referred to the Mongols as "Tatars," sometimes spelled "Tartars," and to the Mongolian Empire as "Tartaria"; Crimea, where many Mongols settled, is known throughout Russian history as "Little Tartaria."[1]

## Vienna and America

At Vienna in 1683 the Hussars formed part of an 18,000-strong cavalry charge — described as the largest in recorded history — led by Polish King John III against an [[ottoman-empire|Ottoman]] host of 150,000 besieging the Austrian capital.[1] Badlands suggests the episode most likely inspired J.R.R. Tolkien's "Ride of the Rohirrim" at the end of the *Lord of the Rings* trilogy.[1]

After the Partition of Poland began in 1772, the Hussars were left without a country and had stopped wearing their iconic wings.[1] [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] recruited their leader, Count [[casimir-pulaski|Casimir Pulaski]], to bring the horsemen to America to fight the British Empire, some 90 years after Vienna.[1] Badlands concludes that the American Revolution was "the last ride of the [Winged] Hussars," a force that effectively became the U.S. Cavalry.[1]
