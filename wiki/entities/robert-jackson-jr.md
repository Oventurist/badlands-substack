# Robert J. Jackson Jr.

Robert J. Jackson Jr. is a law professor at [[new-york-university|New York University School of Law]] and a former commissioner of the U.S. [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]] who, with [[joshua-mitts|Joshua Mitts]], co-authored a widely publicized 2023 study of trading activity preceding the [[october-7-attacks|October 7, 2023 Hamas attack]] on Israel.[1]

## Study of pre-October 7 trading

The 66-page paper concluded that unidentified individuals were cognizant of the impending operation and intended to profit illicitly, as evidenced by a "significant spike" in short selling of listed Israeli companies.[1] Jackson and Mitts found that short interest in the [[msci-israel-etf|MSCI Israel Exchange Traded Fund]] "suddenly, and significantly, spiked" on October 2 based on data from the [[financial-industry-regulatory-authority|Financial Industry Regulatory Authority]], and that short selling of Israeli securities on the [[tel-aviv-stock-exchange|Tel Aviv Stock Exchange]] increased dramatically just before the attack.[1]

The authors reported that the volume of short selling on Israeli companies in the days before October 7 "far exceeded short selling that occurred during numerous other periods of crisis, including the recession following the financial crisis, the 2014 Israel-Gaza war, and the COVID-19 pandemic."[1] They calculated that [[bank-leumi|Leumi]], Israel's largest bank, was the subject of 4.43 million new shares sold short between September 14 and October 5, associated with profits of 3.2 billion shekels (about $862 million).[1] While they saw no aggregate increase in shorting of Israeli companies on U.S. exchanges, they identified a sharp and unusual increase just before the attacks in trading of risky short-dated options on those companies expiring just after the attacks.[1]

Their central conclusions were that "traders informed about the coming attacks profited from these tragic events," that such trading occurs in gaps in U.S. and international enforcement of prohibitions on informed trading, and that "it is extremely unlikely that the volume of short selling on October 2nd occurred by random chance."[1] They further identified similar patterns in April 2023, when Hamas was reportedly preparing a comparable attack timed to the eve of Passover that was called off after Israeli intelligence obtained advance notice.[1]

## Aftermath

Jackson and Mitts urged the SEC and FINRA to investigate further in order to understand why and how financial markets may have anticipated the events of October 7.[1] Israeli authorities subsequently opened an inquiry, with the [[israel-securities-authority|Israel Securities Authority]] stating that the matter was known to it and under investigation.[1] The findings became a central pillar of the broader [[israel-october-7-foreknowledge-allegations|Israel October 7 foreknowledge allegations]].[1]
