# Steve Sadow

Steve Sadow is the Atlanta criminal defense attorney representing former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|Georgia election interference case]] prosecuted by Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]].[1]

## February 2024 disqualification hearing

Sadow was described by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] as "the star of the hearing" held Tuesday, February 27, 2024, on the motion to disqualify Willis over her romantic relationship with special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]].[1] Sadow conducted an aggressive cross-examination of witness [[terrence-bradley|Terrence Bradley]], Wade's former law partner and divorce attorney, attacking his prior testimony and repeatedly demanding to know why Bradley would speculate to defense lawyer [[ashleigh-merchant|Ashleigh Merchant]] about allegations against Wade — a man Bradley considered a friend — while knowing Merchant was preparing to file those allegations against Wade and Willis.[1]

Bradley offered no persuasive answers, and the judge eventually required Sadow to stop that line of questioning, but according to the Badlands account the absurdity of Bradley's testimony had already been established beyond doubt.[1] The hearing was scheduled to resume on the record the following Friday at 1 p.m. ET.[1]

## First Amendment motion to dismiss

On March 28, 2024, Sadow appeared before Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] to argue that the Georgia indictment against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] should be dismissed because the charges sought to criminalize political speech and advocacy conduct protected by the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[2] "There is nothing alleged factually against President Trump that is not political speech," Sadow told the judge, adding that a sitting president expressing concerns about an election is "the height of political speech" and remains protected even if what was said turned out to be false.[2] Prosecutor [[donald-wakeford|Donald Wakeford]] responded that the statements were integral to criminal activity and therefore unprotected.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the motion was framed as an "as applied" constitutional challenge to the facts of the case.[2]

## Response to the McAfee ruling (April 2024)

After Fulton County Superior Court Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] rejected the defense's First Amendment challenge in April 2024, Sadow — [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s lead lawyer on the Georgia case — said in a statement: "President Trump and other defendants respectfully disagree with Judge McAfee's order and will continue to evaluate their options regarding the First Amendment challenges."[3]
