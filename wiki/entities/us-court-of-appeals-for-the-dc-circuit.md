# U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit

The United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit is the federal appellate court with jurisdiction over most litigation against federal agencies, including Freedom of Information Act disputes involving the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]].[1]

In July 2026 the court denied an emergency request by former president [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] for an injunction blocking release of the audio recordings and transcripts of his 2016–2017 conversations with ghostwriter [[mark-zwonitzer|Mark Zwonitzer]], material gathered during Special Counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s classified-documents investigation.[1] The panel held that Biden had not shown a likelihood of success on the merits, citing a substantial public interest in allowing the public to assess whether the government had gone easy on a former vice president, and quoting precedent that where a high-profile investigation ends in a decision not to charge, the public has a strong interest in evaluating "whether the government had the evidence but nevertheless pulled its punches."[1] The court observed that redactions would protect sensitive personal details and that much of the content was already public through Hur's report and Biden's memoir, and it extended an administrative stay until August 3 to allow for further review.[1]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the decision as the appellate court "surprisingly acknowledging the rights of the People" and as a step toward accountability for what she describes as the autopen scandal — the contention that Biden's signature was used on executive orders and pardons while he was cognitively impaired, with unnamed handlers as the real culpable parties.[1]

## Boasberg contempt ruling (2026)

In April 2026 a divided panel of the court ordered U.S. District Judge James Boasberg to immediately end his criminal contempt probe against Trump administration officials over the disputed 2025 deportation flights to El Salvador.[2] Judges Neomi Rao and Justin Walker, both Trump appointees, wrote that Boasberg's inquiry into "high-level Executive Branch deliberations about matters of national security and diplomacy" was "a clear abuse of discretion" that had reached "a legal dead end," while Biden appointee Michelle Childs dissented.[2] It was reportedly the second time the appeals court had blocked Boasberg's contempt efforts.[2]
