# Ed Bastian

Ed Bastian is the chief executive officer of [[delta-air-lines|Delta Air Lines]].[1] He became a prominent public voice in the fallout from the July 2024 global information technology outage caused by a faulty software update from the cybersecurity firm [[crowdstrike|CrowdStrike]].[1]

## Response to the CrowdStrike outage

On Wednesday, July 31, 2024, Bastian announced that the global IT outage sparked by CrowdStrike had cost Delta approximately $500 million.[1] Speaking to CNBC's "Squawk Box," he said the carrier would seek damages from the disruptions, stating flatly, "We have no choice."[1]

Bastian framed the dispute as one of vendor accountability for critical systems: "If you're going to be having access, priority access to the Delta ecosystem in terms of technology, you've got to test the stuff you got. You can't come into a mission critical 24/7 operation and tell us we have a bug."[1] He added that the airline had an obligation to "protect our shareholders... our customers, our employees, for the damage, not just to the cost of it, but to the brand, the reputational damage and the physical channel."[1]

The faulty CrowdStrike update led Delta to cancel more than 4,000 flights during July 2024.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the airline-industry angle was the least interesting element of the CrowdStrike affair, but that Bastian's expected lawsuit usefully kept the company in the headlines and created an opening to educate the public about vulnerabilities in US critical infrastructure.[1] The suit was also described as a potential test of the contractual terms governing public-private partnerships within critical infrastructure.[1]
