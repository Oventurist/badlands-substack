# William Gilpin

William Gilpin (1813–1894) was an American statesman, ally of [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] and the first Territorial Governor of Colorado during the American Civil War. In the Badlands Media corpus he is a central figure of the anti-slavery, pro-development wing of nineteenth-century American politics and an early theorist of a world land-bridge.

## Anti-slavery politics and Anglophobia

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s Avoiding WWIII lists Gilpin among the wiser anti-slavery voices — with [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]], John Jay, [[john-quincy-adams|John Quincy Adams]], Lincoln, Charles Sumner and William Seward — whom an Anglophile [[deep-state|Deep State]] repeatedly subverted. Gilpin developed a reputation as an Anglophobe suspicious of British Imperial intrigue, working closely with patriots seeking to reclaim the nation from the oligarchical slave power.

## The Cosmopolitan Railway

Gilpin was among the most vocal proponents of a trans-continental railway extending beyond North America into Eurasia. He published *The Cosmopolitan Railway* in 1890, by which time the United States had laid over 163,000 miles of railway. The article treats this work as the ancestor of contemporary land-bridge projects such as the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait tunnel]] and China's [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]].

## On Indian reservations

Gilpin is quoted in the article as describing Native American reservations as "like blocks of stone in the wall of a jail against the frontier line" — a formulation Ehret uses to link the nineteenth-century reservation system to twentieth-century [[eco-colonialism|eco-colonialism]] and the freezing of federal lands against infrastructure development.
