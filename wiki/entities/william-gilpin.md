# William Gilpin

**William Gilpin** was an American statesman, Colorado governor during the Civil War and ally of [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]], described by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as one of the most vocal proponents of a trans-continental railway extending into Eurasia.[1]

## Anti-slavery and anti-imperial career

Gilpin is listed among the "wiser anti-slavery voices" of the nineteenth century who were subverted by the anglophile [[deep-state|Deep State]] parasite class running Wall Street and the southern slave power.[1] He became a leading figure of anti-slavery America and developed a reputation as an Anglophobe suspicious of British Imperial intrigue, working closely with patriots who wished to take back the nation from the oligarchical slave power.[1]

## The Cosmopolitan Railway

Gilpin published *The Cosmopolitan Railway* in 1890, at a point when the United States had over 163,000 miles of railway and many other nations were experiencing parallel growth through the same model of political economy.[1] His vision anticipated the later Eurasian land-bridge concepts embodied in the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait Tunnel]] proposals.[1]

## On reservations

Gilpin astutely identified Native American reservations as being "like blocks of stone in the wall of a jail against the frontier line," a formulation Ehret uses to introduce his critique of [[eco-colonialism|eco-colonialism]] and the ghettoization of First Nations.[1]

## The Cosmopolitan Railway

In 1890 Gilpin, a friend of [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] and the first governor of Colorado, published [[cosmopolitan-railway|The Cosmopolitan Railway]], outlining his dream of a world of rail networks, industrial progress and cooperation.[2] He wrote that "the cosmopolitan railway will make the whole world one community," reducing "the separate nations to families of our great nation," and that from extended intercommunication would arise "a wider intercourse of human ideas… for in the track of intercommunication, enterprise and invention invariably follow, and whatever facilitates one stimulates every other agency of progress."[2] The article compares this win-win philosophy to Xi Jinping's New Silk Road.[2]

Gilpin noted the common cultural and geographic properties of American and Russian societies, with Russia's Manifest Destiny calling forth a pioneering spirit to "go east" while America's envisioned westward expansion.[2] He argued that Russia and the United States, "each having broad, uninhabited areas and limitless undeveloped resources, would by the expenditure of 2 or 3 hundred millions apiece for a highway of the nations through their now waste places, add a hundredfold to their wealth and power and influence."[2] History unfolded in another direction from the vision laid out by Gilpin, Lincoln and [[alexander-ii|Czar Alexander II]], as bankers' wars, anarchist assassinations and economic turmoil disrupted the age of cooperation among civilizational states.[2]

## Bodyguard to Lincoln and Bering Strait advocacy

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes Gilpin as [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]]'s personal friend, bodyguard and the first governor of Colorado, who fought hard against what Ehret calls the Anglo-American Deep State and recognized earlier than most the strategic value of the U.S.–Russia alliance to build the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait tunnel]].[3] Gilpin's vision was most clearly laid out in his 1890 magnum opus [[cosmopolitan-railway|The Cosmopolitan Railway]], which featured designs for development corridors across all continents united by a "community of principle."[3] Ehret invokes this lineage in his analysis of the [[2025-trump-putin-alaska-summit|2025 Trump–Putin Alaska summit]] and the prospect of an Arctic economic strategy revived along the Bering Strait.[3]

## Family, rail advocacy and Civil War service

Gilpin (1813–1894) hailed from a patriotic family of nation builders whose patriarch Thomas Gilpin was a close ally of Benjamin Franklin and a leading member of Franklin's Philosophical Society.[4] He was famous for advocating America's transcontinental railway as early as 1845; the line was finally begun by [[abraham-lincoln|Lincoln]] during the Civil War and completed in 1869 as the world's first transcontinental railway, serving as a model abroad, with American-made railcars sent to Russia and American engineers assisting in the construction of the [[trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]].[4]

With Lincoln's 1861 victory Gilpin became the President's bodyguard and ensured that Lincoln survived his first assassination attempt en route to Washington from Illinois.[4] Appointed Colorado's first governor during the war, he stopped the southern slave power from opening a western front — applying Lincoln's greenback system to finance his army at the state level — and won the Battle of Glorieta Pass of 1862, which Ehret credits with helping save the Union.[4]

## Sinophilia and the American System abroad

In his thousands of speeches and writings, Gilpin held that America's destiny was inextricably tied to both Russia and the ancient civilization of China — not to impose opium as the British did, but to learn from and even emulate.[4] In 1852 he wrote that "salvation must come to America from China," praising the "patriarchal democracy of the Celestial Empire" and arguing that a railroad to the Pacific was of vast importance because Chinese trade, and with it Chinese civilization, would then cross the North American continent.[4] He later wrote that "the ancient Asiatic colossus, in a certain sense, needed only to be awakened to new life."[4]

After the war Gilpin became a leading advocate of internationalizing the [[american-system|American System of political economy]], citing Lincoln's success: "No amount of argument will make America adopt old world theories… to manufacture everything that can possibly be manufactured within her territory — this is and has been the policy of the USA from the time of Alexander Hamilton to that of Henry Clay and thence to our own days."[4] Against British control of maritime choke points he advocated internal improvements, rail development and the harmonization of diverse cultures through scientific and technological progress, predicting that a global system of mutual development would "largely supersede the ocean traffic of Great Britain."[4] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents Gilpin as the direct antithesis of [[alfred-thayer-mahan|Alfred Thayer Mahan]], and argues that today's [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] and the Russia–China strategic friendship have re-awakened Gilpin's forgotten vision.[4]
