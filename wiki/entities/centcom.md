# United States Central Command

United States Central Command (CENTCOM) is the unified combatant command responsible for U.S. military operations across the Middle East, including the Persian Gulf and the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[1]

## Lifting of the Iranian maritime blockade

Following the entry into force of the [[us-iran-memorandum-of-understanding|US–Iran memorandum of understanding]] in June 2026, CENTCOM announced force posture and operational updates for the region.[1] The command stated that U.S. forces had "lifted the blockade on all maritime traffic entering and exiting Iranian ports and coastal areas, in accordance with the President's direction," that American forces were no longer impeding the transit of vessels to or from Iranian ports, and that all U.S. military blockade enforcement efforts had ceased.[1] The statement added that U.S. naval ships would "remain in the general area to make sure that all aspects of the agreement are adhered to, obeyed and in full force and effect."[1]

Badlands reported that CENTCOM's announcement corresponded with the official lifting of the blockade on the Strait of Hormuz, and noted that [[iran|Iran]] had moved over five million barrels of oil since signing the electronic agreement with the Trump administration that Sunday.[1]


## Iran maritime blockade (April 2026)

In April 2026 US Central Command announced that a maritime blockade would be enforced in the Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Sea, targeting vessel traffic east of the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], after negotiations with [[iran|Iran]] collapsed in Islamabad.[2] CENTCOM guidance stated that any unauthorized ship entering or leaving the restricted zone could face interception, diversion, or capture, while neutral transit through the strait and humanitarian shipments would continue to be permitted under inspection.[2] The measure formed the operational core of the [[naval-blockade-of-iran|naval blockade of Iran]].[2]

## Role in the 2024 Israel–Hezbollah escalation

Badlands Media reported that the September 2024 pager attacks against [[hezbollah]] were flanked by visits to Israel from CENTCOM Combatant Commander General [[michael-kurilla|Michael Kurilla]], who warned Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] that the US military would not help the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] if Israel instigated a ground war with Hezbollah or anyone else.[3] Colonel [[lawrence-wilkerson|Lawrence Wilkerson]] said the warning included the nuance that the US military would be able to tell if the IDF started the provocation.[3] Commentators cited Kurilla's intervention as a template when asking whether comparable [[pentagon]] officials had blocked the release of long-range missiles to Ukraine at the September 13 [[joe-biden|Biden]]–[[keir-starmer|Starmer]] meeting.[3]

## Claims of usurped foreign-policy authority (2024)

In September 2024 Colonel [[lawrence-wilkerson|Lawrence Wilkerson]] said in an interview with Judge [[andrew-napolitano|Andrew Napolitano]] that CENTCOM's Combatant Commander, General [[michael-kurilla|Michael Kurilla]], had usurped the authority of the [[white-house|White House]] and [[state-department|State Department]] and was effectively conducting US foreign policy.[4] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that contemporaneous reports of a US troop surge to the Middle East described only routine rotations and a long-scheduled carrier deployment, and therefore did nothing to contradict Wilkerson's account of combatant-commander primacy.[4]

## Raid on a senior ISIS financier in Syria (August 2025)

On Thursday, August 21, 2025, CENTCOM announced that American forces had killed a senior [[isis|ISIS]] official during a raid in northern Syria earlier that week.[5] The Defense Department did not reveal the official's name but said the raid took place on Tuesday, August 19, and identified the target as a key financier who helped orchestrate attacks in Syria and Iraq and who posed a threat to American forces and to the new Syrian government because of his ISIS contacts in the region.[5] CENTCOM Commander Admiral Brad Cooper said in a statement, "We will continue to pursue ISIS terrorists with unwavering determination, throughout the region," adding that together with partners and allies CENTCOM "remains steadfast in our commitment of ensuring the lasting defeat of ISIS and the protection of the U.S. homeland."[5]

## Attacks on US positions, October 2023

In late October 2023, US Central Command disclosed that during the preceding week American troops and installations in Iraq and Syria had come under more than a dozen drone attacks, resulting in two dozen troop injuries that were officially described as "minor."[6] The disclosure coincided with the Pentagon's rush to place additional air-defense systems across the region and was read as evidence that Washington expected significant escalation.[6]

## Southern Syria ISIS cache operation (November 2025)

From 24 to 27 November 2025, CENTCOM forces and the Syrian Ministry of Interior located and destroyed more than 15 sites containing [[isis|ISIS]] weapons caches in southern [[syria|Syria]].[7] Personnel from Combined Joint Task Force – Operation Inherent Resolve worked with Syrian forces to identify and eliminate the storage facilities across Rif Dimashq province through multiple airstrikes and ground detonations, destroying over 130 mortars and rockets, assault rifles, machine guns, anti-tank mines, improvised explosive device materials and illicit drugs.[7] CENTCOM commander Admiral Brad Cooper said the operation ensured that gains against ISIS would be lasting and that the group could not regenerate or export attacks.[7] Badlands commentary noted the irony of the U.S. partnering with a Syrian government led by a former Al Qaeda commander.[7]

## September 2024 warnings to Israel

In the week after the Lebanon [[pager-attack|pager attacks]], CENTCOM commander General [[michael-kurilla|Michael Kurilla]] travelled to [[israel|Israel]] twice to warn [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] that if they initiated a ground war with Lebanon, Syria or [[iran|Iran]], they would be on their own.[8] Badlands writers, drawing on retired Colonel [[lawrence-wilkerson|Lawrence Wilkerson]]'s remarks to Judge [[andrew-napolitano|Andrew Napolitano]], framed this as a combatant commander effectively usurping the [[white-house|White House]], [[state-department|State Department]] and [[cia|CIA]] to take control of U.S. Middle East diplomacy — a pattern they associate with [[devolution|Devolution]].[8]

## December 2024 strikes on ISIS camps in central Syria

On December 8, 2024, CENTCOM forces conducted dozens of precision airstrikes against known [[isis|ISIS]] camps and operatives in central [[syria|Syria]], striking more than 75 targets with multiple U.S. Air Force assets including B-52s, F-15s, and A-10s.[9] The command said the strikes were part of the ongoing mission to disrupt, degrade, and defeat ISIS and to prevent the group from exploiting the collapse of the Syrian government to reconstitute in central Syria.[9] Battle damage assessments were under way and CENTCOM reported no indications of civilian casualties.[9]

"There should be no doubt — we will not allow ISIS to reconstitute and take advantage of the current situation in Syria," said commander General [[michael-kurilla|Michael Erik Kurilla]], adding that "all organizations in Syria should know that we will hold them accountable if they partner with or support ISIS in any way."[9] CENTCOM said it would continue operations with regional allies and partners to degrade ISIS capabilities throughout the transition period.[9]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the operation as one arm of a "Sovereign Alliance pincer move" against Deep State forces in Syria with analogues to the 2021 Kabul withdrawal, noting the coincidence of the strikes being launched amid chaos that he argued could not plausibly have gone unanticipated by the major players.[9]
