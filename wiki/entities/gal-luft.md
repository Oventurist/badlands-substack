# Gal Luft

Dr. Gal Luft is an Israeli think-tank co-director who became known in 2023 as the "missing witness" in the congressional investigation into the business dealings of the family of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] Badlands Media covered his indictment in the July 12, 2023 edition of the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], framing it as evidence of selective prosecution by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[1]

## Allegations against the Biden family

Speaking to the New York Post in early July 2023, Luft said he had been arrested in Cyprus in order to prevent him from testifying before the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]].[1] His planned testimony, he said, would have alleged that the Biden family received payments from individuals linked to Chinese military intelligence, and that the family's benefactors were served by an FBI mole who passed classified information to them from the China-controlled energy company [[cefc-china-energy|CEFC]].[1]

Luft stated: "I told the DOJ that Hunter was associated with a very senior retired FBI official who had a distinct physical characteristic—he had one eye."[1] That official is widely believed to be former [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[louis-freeh|Louis Freeh]], who in 2016 gave $100,000 to a trust for two of then-Vice President Joe Biden's grandchildren shortly before telling [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], "I would be delighted to do future work with you."[1]

## Indictment

Days after Luft detailed his allegations publicly, the Biden administration's Justice Department charged him with failing to register under the [[foreign-agents-registration-act|Foreign Agents Registration Act]] (FARA) and with Iranian sanctions violations.[1] Prosecutors alleged that he conspired with others to act in China's interest, including by recruiting and paying a former high-ranking U.S. government official to support policies beneficial to China.[1] Luft publicly mocked the arms-dealing element of the case, saying, "The US, claiming I'm an arms dealer. It would be funny if it weren't tragic."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] characterized the prosecution as projection — "Blame others for what you yourself are doing" — and argued that the statutes used against Luft are so ambiguous that they can be applied to almost anything, which he suggested may be the point.[1] Deschamps contended that a legitimate law must be precise, clear, and definable, and that tolerating vague statutes on the books enables abuse by corrupt actors and by the government itself.[1] He also argued the case is so transparently an attempt to cover up alleged Biden family activity that any reader encountering sufficient detail will recognize it, raising the possibility that the spectacle is deliberate — "Kayfabe, anyone?"[1]

## Arrest and extradition (2024)

On Monday, September 9, 2024, prosecutors said that Luft had been arrested and would be extradited to the United States in the coming weeks or months.[2] He had originally been arrested in Cyprus in February 2023 but fled while released on bail, and was subsequently re-arrested at a time and place prosecutors declined to specify.[2] Luft, described as the co-director of the Washington-based [[institute-for-the-analysis-of-global-security|Institute for the Analysis of Global Security]], maintained on [[x|X]] that he had never been an arms dealer and that the charges were "politically motivated," and his think tank said it was convinced of his innocence.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] found the case incongruous, asking why federal authorities would arrest "an American-Israeli with dual citizenship for being an unregistered foreign [agent] for... China."[2] He drew particular attention to the arrest occurring in [[cyprus|Cyprus]], which he described as "an island-nation known for being a hotbed of transnational organized crime, particularly for the Ukrainian-Israeli Mafia," invoking [[igor-kolomoisky|Igor Kolomoisky]] by name.[2] From there the commentary branched into the 2014 Maidan events, the installation of [[oleksandr-turchynov|Oleksandr Turchynov]] as interim Ukrainian president, and the alleged retention of former [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[william-sessions|William Sessions]] as personal attorney to crime boss [[semion-mogilevich|Semion Mogilevich]].[2]

## Fugitive interview (May 2023)

At the end of May 2023, Luft — the "missing" witness in the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]]'s Biden family corruption investigation — told the [[new-york-post|New York Post]] that he was alive and living as a fugitive in an undisclosed location.[3] The former Israeli Defense Force colonel had vanished from Cyprus the previous month while on bail awaiting extradition to the United States on seven charges, five of them relating to the [[arms-export-control-act|Arms Export Control Act]] for allegedly conspiring to sell Chinese products to the United Arab Emirates, Kenya and Libya, along with a violation of the [[foreign-agents-registration-act|Foreign Agents Registration Act]] and a false-statement count.[3] He denied the allegations and claimed he had been forced to skip bail because he was the victim of political persecution intended to protect [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], his son [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] and his brother [[jim-biden|Jim Biden]], saying: "I've been arrested in Cyprus on a politically motivated extradition request by the US … claiming I'm an arms dealer … I've never been an arms dealer … DOJ is trying to bury me to protect Joe, Jim and Hunter Biden."[3]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that by the timeline in the report Luft had been blowing the whistle on the Biden family since March 2019, having had a business relationship with a nonprofit think tank associated with [[cefc-china-energy|CEFC]] and having told investigators four years earlier that the CCP was paying Biden family members in exchange for "their FBI connections and use of the Biden name to promote China's Belt and Road Initiative around the world."[3] She linked the account to the [[fbi|FBI]]'s years-long possession of the Biden laptop before John Paul Mac Isaac went public, and argued that in an environment of weaponized justice the public faced a choice between the whistleblower and the institutions he was accusing.[3] She closed by quoting Post columnist Miranda Devine: "The allegations against Luft are serious and should be fully investigated, but so too are his allegations of a cover-up."[3]
