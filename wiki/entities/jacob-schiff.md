# Jacob Schiff

Jacob Schiff was a Wall Street financier described in Badlands Media coverage as a central private-sector actor in the financial destabilization of Imperial Russia during the first two decades of the twentieth century.[1]

## Financing of Japan in the Russo-Japanese War

In 1905, at the moment the [[trans-siberian-railway|Trans-Siberian Railway]] was completed and American engineers were drawing up plans for the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait]] rail project, Schiff extended $200 million to the Japanese government to assist its victory over Russia in the [[russo-japanese-war|1904–05 Russo-Japanese War]].[1] The Russian navy was destroyed in the conflict, and the Russian state and military were left crippled.[1] For this assistance Schiff was awarded the Medal of the Rising Sun at the Meiji Palace in 1907.[1]

## Financing of revolution inside Russia

After the war, Schiff turned his attention to financing revolutionary activity inside Russia itself.[1] The scale of that spending remained obscure until 1949, when Schiff's grandson John Schiff told the *New York Journal* that his grandfather had given $20 million "for the triumph of communism in Russia."[1]

Schiff worked in tandem with the journalist and propagandist [[george-kennan|George Kennan]], who publicly credited "a New York banker you all know and love" with financing the distribution of revolutionary literature to Russian prisoners of war held in Japan.[1] On March 18, 1917, Schiff published a statement in the *New York Times* expressing "my joy that the Russian nation, a great and good people, have at last effected their deliverance from centuries of autocratic oppression and through an almost bloodless revolution have now come into their own."[1]

Schiff is grouped with [[alfred-milner|Lord Alfred Milner]] and [[paul-warburg|Paul Warburg]] as backers who intended [[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]] to lead the movement that would take power over the bodies of the Romanovs.[1]

## Kuhn, Loeb and the Push for a Central Bank

Jacob H. Schiff was the son-in-law of Solomon Loeb and, in Badlands Media's account, an alleged [[rothschild-family|Rothschild]] agent who led [[kuhn-loeb-and-co|Kuhn, Loeb & Co.]] to become one of the most influential investment banks of the late 19th and early 20th centuries.[2]

In the early 1900s, with the money changers of the world already anxious to establish another private central bank in America, Schiff told the New York Chamber of Commerce: "Unless we have a Central Bank with adequate control of credit resources, this country is going to undergo the most severe and far reaching money panic in its history."[2] The article notes the quotation has been interpreted as an admonition by some and as a threat by others, and that soon afterward came the year the "banksters" went on the offensive — the [[panic-of-1907|Panic of 1907]].[2]

Schiff was present at the [[jekyll-island-meeting|Jekyll Island meeting]], where the article emphasizes that the Rothschilds, Warburgs and Schiffs were interconnected by marriage and thus essentially one extended family.[2] The [[pujo-committee|Pujo Committee]] later found that Schiff, along with [[jp-morgan|Morgan]], Rockefeller and [[paul-warburg|Warburg]], had gained control of major manufacturing, transportation, mining, telecommunications and financial markets throughout the United States.[2] The Kuhn Loeb Bank of New York appears on the article's list of institutions identified as owners of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[2]

## Rothschild lineage and rise at Kuhn, Loeb

Ryan DeLarme's "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 6" places the Schiff family in the same Frankfurt quarters as the [[rothschild-family|Rothschilds]] in 1785, and describes the grandson Jacob Schiff as emigrating to America in 1865 after meeting [[abraham-kuhn|Abraham Kuhn]], who invited him to join his New York investment firm.[3] In 1875 Schiff married the daughter of [[solomon-loeb|Solomon Loeb]], then head of [[kuhn-loeb-and-co|Kuhn, Loeb & Company]], and became head of the firm on Loeb's death in 1885.[3] It was Schiff who financed the purchase of the Union Pacific for the railroad magnate [[edward-h-harriman|Edward H. Harriman]], father of [[averell-harriman|Averell Harriman]]; DeLarme writes that both Schiff and Harriman would play important roles in the rise of communism in Russia.[3]

DeLarme cites historian Howard M. Sachar on the intermarriage pattern that bound these houses together — Loeb and Kuhn married each other's sisters, and Schiff became "an instant partner by marrying Loeb's daughter Nina."[3] The article further asserts that when the Rothschilds established their third American central bank, the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], in 1913, they placed "one of their own bloodline," Jacob Schiff, in charge of the project.[3]
