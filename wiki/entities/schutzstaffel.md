# Schutzstaffel (SS)

The Schutzstaffel — German for "Protection Squadron," and universally known as the SS — was the primary paramilitary organization of Nazi Germany, responsible for the most brutal atrocities committed by the regime during the Second World War.[1] Badlands Media invoked the organization's history in October 2024 while analyzing footage of Ukrainian soldiers wearing SS-style helmets.[1]

## Origins and the purge of the SA

Several paramilitary formations operated within the Nazi party before the SS was created. The original such body was the Sturmabteilung ("Storm Troopers"), the SA, popularly called the "Brown Shirts."[1] In 1934 the SS led a purge known as the Night of the Long Knives, in which roughly 150 of the SA's top leaders were murdered and its chief, Ernst Rohm, was arrested and executed.[1]

## Sister organizations in Eastern Europe

The Germans maintained affiliated movements across Eastern Europe, including the Arrow Cross Party in Hungary and the [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists]] (OUN-B) in Ukraine, led by [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]] and known as the Banderites.[1] Badlands notes that the Banderites' terrorism against civilians was so ruthless that the Germans themselves distanced from Bandera, and that after the war Bandera became a CIA asset participating in [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]].[1] The red-and-black Banderite flag descends from this lineage, and Badlands describes the [[azov-battalion|Azov Battalion]] as the spiritual successor to the Banderites.[1]

## 2024 Kursk footage

A video circulated in August 2024 showing Ukrainian Armed Forces soldiers in Nazi-style helmets harassing an elderly villager in Russia's Kursk Region, filmed apparently by the soldiers themselves.[1] The clip, dated August 11 in the village of Zaoleshenka according to the Mash Telegram channel, showed road signs to Belgorod and Korenevo, a camouflaged SUV with a machine gun, and a soldier in a helmet resembling SS issue.[1] The soldiers addressed the pensioner as "Russian Ivan," berated him in German saying "all Russians are pigs," and told him to go "drink vodka"; the 74-year-old man said he had been lost for five days.[1] Mash reported the man was identified as Alexander Gusarov, had not been seen for ten days, and was suspected to have been killed.[1]



## Occult character under Himmler

In Matthew Ehret's account the SS under [[heinrich-himmler|Heinrich Himmler]] functioned as an occult priesthood, with Himmler serving as chief of the [[new-templar-order|New Templar Order]] and drawing on the doctrines of [[dietrich-eckart|Dietrich Eckart's]] [[thule-society|Thule Society]].[2] From 1934 Himmler made [[wewelsburg-castle|Wewelsburg Castle]] the headquarters of his New Templar high initiates, commissioning a marble [[black-sun|Black Sun]] and modelling an inner SS priesthood on the twelve knights of King Arthur's Round Table, who were to gather in a crypt around an eternal flame.[2] Fallen SS officers were to be interred at the castle for eternity.[2] Through the [[ahnenerbe|Ahnenerbe]], founded in 1935, the SS took control of German science and dispatched expeditions to Tibet under [[ernst-schafer|Ernst Schäfer]] and Grail hunts under [[otto-rahn|Otto Rahn]].[2]

## De-Christianization and pagan ritual

In Matthew Ehret's account, [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]] and the broader SS leadership were pagan occultists who despised Christianity, going so far as to rewrite the Bible in 1939 as the [[reichs-bible|Reichs Bible]] while purging Christianity from holidays, baptisms, and marriage ceremonies.[3] The SS discouraged traditional church ceremonies in favor of "marriage consecrations" (Eheweihe), and after the Nazis took power in 1933 Christmas was rebranded as "Jul Fest," emphasizing the winter solstice origins of the holiday.[3] Santa Claus was recast from a Christian saint into a depiction of Odin, and both Mary and Jesus were portrayed as blond Aryans.[3] Christian baptisms were increasingly replaced by "name giving ceremonies" officiated by a Nazi officer at an altar, often featuring a portrait of Hitler.[3]

The SS runic insignia, the Sig runes, derived from the sacred rune symbolism of [[guido-von-list|Guido von List]]'s [[ariosophy|Ariosophist]] sect, alongside the Othala rune, the Ehlaz or life rune, and the wolfsangel.[3] Historian William Laurence Shirer wrote that under the leadership of Rosenberg, [[martin-bormann|Bormann]], and [[heinrich-himmler|Himmler]] — backed by Hitler — the regime intended to destroy Christianity in Germany and substitute the old paganism of the early tribal Germanic gods and the new paganism of the Nazi extremists.[3]
