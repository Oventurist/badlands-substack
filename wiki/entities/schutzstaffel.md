# Schutzstaffel (SS)

The Schutzstaffel — German for "Protection Squadron," and universally known as the SS — was the primary paramilitary organization of Nazi Germany, responsible for the most brutal atrocities committed by the regime during the Second World War.[1] Badlands Media invoked the organization's history in October 2024 while analyzing footage of Ukrainian soldiers wearing SS-style helmets.[1]

## Origins and the purge of the SA

Several paramilitary formations operated within the Nazi party before the SS was created. The original such body was the Sturmabteilung ("Storm Troopers"), the SA, popularly called the "Brown Shirts."[1] In 1934 the SS led a purge known as the Night of the Long Knives, in which roughly 150 of the SA's top leaders were murdered and its chief, Ernst Rohm, was arrested and executed.[1]

## Sister organizations in Eastern Europe

The Germans maintained affiliated movements across Eastern Europe, including the Arrow Cross Party in Hungary and the [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists]] (OUN-B) in Ukraine, led by [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]] and known as the Banderites.[1] Badlands notes that the Banderites' terrorism against civilians was so ruthless that the Germans themselves distanced from Bandera, and that after the war Bandera became a CIA asset participating in [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]].[1] The red-and-black Banderite flag descends from this lineage, and Badlands describes the [[azov-battalion|Azov Battalion]] as the spiritual successor to the Banderites.[1]

## 2024 Kursk footage

A video circulated in August 2024 showing Ukrainian Armed Forces soldiers in Nazi-style helmets harassing an elderly villager in Russia's Kursk Region, filmed apparently by the soldiers themselves.[1] The clip, dated August 11 in the village of Zaoleshenka according to the Mash Telegram channel, showed road signs to Belgorod and Korenevo, a camouflaged SUV with a machine gun, and a soldier in a helmet resembling SS issue.[1] The soldiers addressed the pensioner as "Russian Ivan," berated him in German saying "all Russians are pigs," and told him to go "drink vodka"; the 74-year-old man said he had been lost for five days.[1] Mash reported the man was identified as Alexander Gusarov, had not been seen for ten days, and was suspected to have been killed.[1]
