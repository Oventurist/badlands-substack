# Aidan Maese-Czeropski

Aidan Maese-Czeropski is a former congressional staffer who became the subject of a December 2023 scandal after leaked amateur pornography appeared to show a Senate aide having sex with an unknown man inside a [[us-senate|United States Senate]] hearing room.[1] The footage, obtained and reported by the [[daily-caller|Daily Caller]], also included a photo of the alleged staffer naked on the table where senators sit to question witnesses during hearings; the outlet blurred the man's face because his identity had not been confirmed.[1]

## The Hart 216 incident

A source identified the location to the Daily Caller as Senate room Hart 216, the Judiciary Room, used by the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]].[1] The room had most recently hosted a Judiciary Committee hearing titled "Cleaning Up the C-Suite."[1] Reporting indicated the encounter shown in the video appeared to be unprotected sex.[1]

## Response

Maese-Czeropski, who was linked to the office of Senator [[ben-cardin|Ben Cardin]] of Maryland, responded publicly by framing the ensuing coverage as an attack on his sexuality: "This has been a difficult time for me, as I have been attacked for who I love to pursue a political agenda ... I love my job and would never disrespect my workplace."[1] A spokesperson for Cardin said only that "we have seen media reports emanating from the right-wing media. As this is a personnel matter and under review, we will not be commenting further at this time."[1]

Commentary published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] noted that the responses on Maese-Czeropski's LinkedIn post came heavily from self-identified gay professionals who rejected his homophobia framing, with commenters writing that filming a sex tape in the workplace "is inherently disrespectful and would be a sackable offence anywhere" and that invoking sexual orientation minimized the struggles of people genuinely attacked for who they love.[1]

## Commentary

Writing for Badlands, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] contrasted the episode with the treatment of January 6 defendants, arguing that the same political class that describes government buildings as hallowed and sacred when prosecuting protesters had little to say about a sex tape filmed in a Senate hearing room.[1] Her summary of the affair: "We are not a serious country."[1]
