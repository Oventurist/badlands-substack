# Michaela Murphy

Michaela Murphy is a Maine Superior Court justice who presided over the judicial review of Secretary of State [[shenna-bellows|Shenna Bellows]]'s decision to disqualify [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the state's 2024 presidential primary ballot.[1]

## Deferral of the Maine disqualification case

In January 2024, Murphy deferred judgment in the case, declining to rule on the merits pending the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]]'s scheduled February 8 argument over the Colorado disqualification decision.[1] Bellows appealed the deferral, but the Maine Supreme Court noted that Murphy had only deferred judgment and had not actually ruled, holding that "because the appeal is not from a final judgment, we dismiss the appeal as interlocutory and not justiciable."[1]

Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] characterized the outcome as a second failed attempt by Bellows to force appellate review of her own determination before the national question was resolved, leaving Colorado as the sole state whose supreme court had embraced the disqualification theory.[1]

## January 2024 stay pending the Colorado case

On Wednesday, January 17, 2024, Murphy put a decision on Trump's ballot status on hold to allow time for the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] to rule on the parallel Colorado disqualification case.[2] Trump's lawyers had appealed in state court after Secretary of State [[shenna-bellows|Shenna Bellows]] removed him from the presidential primary ballot, then asked the judge to pause proceedings because the Supreme Court's ruling could render the lawsuit moot.[2]

Murphy concluded that she lacked authority to stay the judicial proceedings themselves, but held that she did have authority to remand the case to the secretary of state with instructions to await the outcome of the Supreme Court case before withdrawing, modifying or upholding her original decision.[2] She wrote that the Maine issues mirrored those in the Colorado case and that her approach "minimizes any potentially destabilizing effect of inconsistent decisions and will promote greater predictability in the weeks ahead of the primary election," adding that "the United State Supreme Court's acceptance of the Colorado case changes everything."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Maine was up against its ballot-printing deadline — critical for uniformed and overseas voters ahead of the March 5 Super Tuesday primary — and that with SCOTUS arguments scheduled for February 8, Trump would remain on the Maine primary ballot.[2] She characterized the litigation as "frivolous impact law intended to interfere in an election, and expand and advance the insurrection narrative," and agreed with Colorado Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]] that the high court should squarely address whether January 6 was an insurrection.[2]
