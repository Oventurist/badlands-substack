# Chega

Chega is a Portuguese populist political party characterized in coverage of the March 2024 Portuguese parliamentary election as anti-immigration and tough on crime.[1] Despite having been founded less than five years before that election, the party surged to become the third-largest force in Portugal's parliament, a result described as the biggest storyline of the vote.[1]

## 2024 parliamentary election

The election ended the Socialist Party's eight-year grip on power in Portugal, with the center-right Democratic Alliance (AD) coalition finishing narrowly ahead at 79 seats to the Socialists' 77 with 99% of votes tallied.[1] Because the AD fell short of a majority in the 230-seat chamber, Chega's bloc of seats placed it in a potential kingmaker position.[1]

AD leader [[luis-montenegro|Luís Montenegro]], head of the Social Democratic Party, had repeatedly promised he would not form a governing coalition with Chega, describing its positions as "often xenophobic, racist, populist and excessively demagogic," and he forcefully reiterated that stance after the results were posted.[1] Chega leader [[andre-ventura|André Ventura]] countered that his party had a rightful place in a new ruling coalition, saying, "We are available to provide a stable government in Portugal," and arguing that voters had asked for a two-party AD–Chega government.[1]

Analysts noted that the Socialist Party could box Chega out of the process by abstaining from the parliamentary vote to form the next government, but that without Chega, Portugal risked a fragile minority administration; Bloomberg reporters observed that in the 50 years since Portugal became a democracy, only two minority governments had lasted a full term.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] used the Chega result to argue that the label "populist" has been weaponized by establishment media against nationalists and conservatives, writing that when the press says "populist," his audience hears "man of the people," and treating the Portuguese outcome as evidence that a worldwide political awakening was spreading.[1]
