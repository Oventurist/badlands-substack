# European Parliament

The European Parliament is the directly elected legislative body of the [[european-union|European Union]], with committees that hold approval authority over the bloc's international trade agreements.[1]

## Freeze of the US trade deal (January 2026)

In response to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s January 2026 Davos remarks on [[greenland|Greenland]] — which closed with the warning "you can say no, and we will remember" — the European Parliament's trade committee froze approval of a United States trade deal indefinitely, citing "coercion" and "territorial integrity."[1] The freeze formed part of a broader European reaction in which Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]] said the bloc was "prepared to act" and French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] floated the [[anti-coercion-instrument|Anti-Coercion Instrument]] against the United States for the first time.[1]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized these moves as posture rather than leverage, noting that markets which had panicked earlier in the week "shrugged in response to the response," and citing Slovak Prime Minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]]'s judgment that the EU is "not taken completely seriously" by world leaders.[1]

## April 2024 approval of the Migration Pact

In April 2024 the European Parliament approved the controversial [[eu-asylum-and-migration-pact|EU Asylum and Migration Pact]], under which member countries must accept a designated share of new arrivals into the bloc or pay a fine for every migrant they reject.[2] The package passed largely with votes from lawmakers affiliated with the [[european-peoples-party|European People's Party]], the Socialists and Democrats, and [[renew-europe|Renew Europe]], with MEPs urged to swallow their criticisms and back the compromise text; some on both the left and center-right admitted voting for it despite its flaws.[2] Parliament President [[roberta-metsola|Roberta Metsola]] hailed the vote as "History made" and a "robust legislative framework," while nationalist politicians including [[marine-le-pen|Marine Le Pen]] denounced it as ceding sovereignty to an ever-centralizing [[european-union|European Union]] and vowed to overturn it after the June 9 European elections.[2]

## The 2024 elections

The [[2024-european-parliament-elections|June 2024 elections]] to the 720-seat assembly returned a clear centrist majority of more than 400 seats while delivering significant gains to nationalist and anti-immigrant groups, projected at roughly 130 seats, a better showing than in 2019, with conservatives marking a decisive victory.[3] Badlands commentary characterized the body as globalist-centered and argued that because the European Union is far from a sovereign actor on the world stage, a parliamentary majority matters mainly for EU policy and trade among member states, which is why the twice-a-decade elections normally see low turnout and enthusiasm.[3] The 2024 round broke that pattern with record turnout, which the commentary read as a bellwether of continental sentiment swinging toward strong borders and anti-interventionism rather than as a direct change in national leadership.[3]
