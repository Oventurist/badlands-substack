# Stian Jenssen

Stian Jenssen is the chief of staff to [[nato|NATO]] Secretary General [[jens-stoltenberg|Jens Stoltenberg]]. He became the focus of an international controversy in August 2023 after appearing to float a land-for-peace settlement to the war in Ukraine, and then retracting the suggestion within a day.[1]

## The land-for-peace remark

Speaking to the Norwegian press on Tuesday, August 15, 2023, Jenssen was asked by journalists from the newspaper VG whether Ukraine would have to give up territory in order to achieve peace and NATO membership.[1] He indicated that the issue had already been discussed within the alliance, adding, "I'm not saying it has to be that way, but it could be a possible solution."[1]

The remark cut against Stoltenberg's own careful line. The Secretary General had consistently avoided questions about how Ukraine should end the war, replying that the decision on whether to open negotiations with Russia belongs to Ukraine alone and not to third countries.[1]

## Retraction

The comment angered Kyiv, and by Wednesday afternoon Jenssen had retracted it, clarifying to the same Norwegian newspaper that had published the original statement.[1] He called the remark "a mistake," explaining that "it was part of a larger discussion about possible future scenarios in Ukraine, and I shouldn't have said it that way."[1] He added that if Ukraine reached the point where it could negotiate, the military posture on the battlefield would play an absolutely central role and would decisively influence the terms on which peace was achieved.[1] The episode was reported by [[remix-news|Remix News]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the retraction through the lens of the [[overton-window|Overton Window]], offering a variant he calls Overton's Goalpost: the sequence of Deny, Diminish, Qualify, Distract, and Admit that a psychological or informational faction deploys when its narrative position diverges from conditions on the ground.[1] He closed by asking which of those stages NATO had reached with respect to the war in Ukraine.[1]
