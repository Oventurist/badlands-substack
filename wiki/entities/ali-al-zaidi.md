# Ali Al-Zaidi

Ali Al-Zaidi is described in the Badlands Brief as the newly elected Prime Minister of Iraq. In July 2026 he met President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the White House, where Trump professed deep affection for the young leader and subsequently announced U.S. support for an Iraq–Syria oil pipeline that would bypass the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea, delivering product directly to the Mediterranean.[1]

The Brief placed the meeting in a sequence of regional realignments: it followed Trump's meeting with Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed Al-Sharaa]] on the sidelines of the [[nato|NATO]] summit in Ankara, [[turkey|Turkey]], where Trump declared his intention to remove Syria from the state sponsor of terrorism list.[1] Iranian state media subsequently reported [[iran|Iran]] and Iraq — described by the Brief as bitter enemies who fought the bloodiest war of the 20th century in the 1980s — working together on regional de-escalation and stabilization.[1]

## Regional tour and Ankara agreements (July 2026)

In July 2026 al-Zaidi conducted a sequence of foreign visits — the United States, then [[iran|Iran]], then [[turkey|Turkey]] — with major energy deals announced at each stop.[2] In his meeting with [[donald-trump|President Trump]], the U.S. was reported to be backing a project to rebuild pipelines that once ran from the supergiant oilfields at Kirkuk to the [[syria|Syrian]] coastline and down to the al-Basra fields on the Persian Gulf, destroyed during the 2003 invasion of Iraq and intended to serve as an alternative to the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and the Suez/Red Sea route.[2] During his Tehran visit he signed deals with the Iranian government including a railroad ultimately connecting the two capitals, and Iraq and Iran agreed to cooperate in dismantling militias operating on their shared border.[2]

On 29 July 2026, talks in Ankara between al-Zaidi and President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] produced five agreements covering railway and road transport via the Ovakoy-Fishkhabur crossing, infrastructure development in exchange for natural resources, police academy education, youth and sports, and industrial property.[2] Erdoğan described the pacts as steps to deepen bilateral ties and stressed that Iraq's security is inseparable from Turkey's, with both sides cooperating on counterterrorism, energy, and a "terror-free region."[2] In parallel, the [[turkish-petroleum-corporation|Turkish Petroleum Corporation]] acquired a 15% stake in bp's Kirkuk oil project alongside ConocoPhillips.[2]

Al-Zaidi appointed [[qasim-al-aboudi|Qasim al-Aboudi]] as Iraq's National Security Adviser the previous month; al-Aboudi later announced that arrested militants had confessed to attacking Iraqi facilities on behalf of [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[2]

## White House meeting and the Hormuz toll reversal (July 2026)

Al-Zaidi's [[white-house|White House]] meeting with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in July 2026 was the setting in which the administration unwound its [[hormuz-transit-fee-proposal|proposed 20% Hormuz transit fee]], replacing it with trade and investment arrangements and an announcement of free trade in the Persian Gulf.[3] Trump described al-Zaidi as a young, handsome, smart and capable leader, while al-Zaidi presented himself as a businessman with a master's degree in finance who was new to politics — a self-description Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized as an Arab version of Trump.[3]
