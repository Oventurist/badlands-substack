# Gene Hackman

Gene Hackman was an American film actor whose death, alongside that of his wife, classical pianist [[betsy-arakawa|Betsy Arakawa]], became a major news story in late February 2025.[1]

## Death and investigation

Hackman and Arakawa were found partially mummified at the couple's $3.3 million mansion in Santa Fe, New Mexico, with pills strewn in their bathroom.[1] A search warrant obtained by DailyMail.com revealed that Arakawa was found decomposed, with bloating on her face and mummification of her hands and feet; sheriffs said Hackman displayed the same signs of decomposition.[1] Mummification occurs when skin and tissues are preserved after death, a process that can occur when a body is left in cold, dry air where bacteria struggle to grow — conditions typical of Santa Fe in winter.[1]

Two maintenance workers, Roland Lowe Begay and Jesse Kesler, discovered the bodies on a Wednesday at 1:46 p.m. Mountain Time.[1] The identities of the pair were not released for nearly twelve hours, a delay that reporting suggested was explained by the condition in which they were found.[1] Begay and Kesler told investigators they had not seen the couple for two weeks, and both appeared to have been dead for some time.[1]

Hackman's daughter, Elizabeth Jean Hackman, told TMZ that the family suspected her 95-year-old father, his 65-year-old wife, and one of their dogs died from carbon monoxide poisoning.[1] Authorities, however, said they found no signs of a carbon monoxide leak, and fire department officials found no evidence of a toxic leak in the home.[1] Investigators described the deaths as "suspicious enough in nature to require a thorough search and investigation" after finding an "unsecured and opened" door to the home along with a prescription pill bottle and pills scattered around the room where Arakawa and one of the dogs were discovered.[1]

## Commentary and speculation

Badlands contributors treated the case as a data point worth examining rather than a settled matter. One contributor noted that while no direct connection between Hackman and [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] could be identified, Hackman had been a very well-connected figure in Hollywood and had owned very private islands.[1] The same commentary observed that Hackman's Santa Fe home was 39 miles from Epstein's [[zorro-ranch|Zorro Ranch]], the New Mexico property where Epstein was said to have pursued interests in eugenics and transhumanism.[1] ABC News was described as reporting no foul play while still characterizing the deaths as "suspicious circumstances."[1]

A separate Badlands commentary highlighted Hackman's role in the 1998 film [[enemy-of-the-state|Enemy of the State]], in which he plays an intelligence-community "white hat" opposite Will Smith's labor lawyer, arguing the film's civil-liberties themes had renewed relevance.[1]
