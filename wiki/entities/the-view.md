# The View

The View is a daytime American talk show whose panel discussions of political events have repeatedly drawn criticism from independent media for advancing progressive framings of breaking news.[1]

## Coverage of the Texas mall shooting

In May 2023, co-host [[ana-navarro|Ana Navarro]] provoked debate on the program by arguing that being Hispanic or Black does not preclude a person from holding white-supremacist views, in a discussion of the mass shooting at a Dallas-area mall.[1] Law-enforcement sources identified 33-year-old Mauricio Garcia as the suspected shooter in the attack, which left eight dead and several injured, and anonymous sources said Garcia may have been motivated by white-supremacist ideology, citing extremist social-media posts and a chest patch reading "RWDS."[1]

Navarro's co-hosts expressed surprise, but she maintained that the suspect's Hispanic background was not disqualifying, invoking [[proud-boys|Proud Boys]] chairman [[enrique-tarrio|Enrique Tarrio]] as an example: "being Hispanic or being Black does not, or being anything does not make you immune from being racist, from being radicalized, from being a White supremacist, from being evil, from being homicidal."[1] Similar arguments had previously been made by progressive commentators including Jemele Hill and Bree Newsome Bass, who contended that Black police officers can be motivated by white-supremacist ideology.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] described the segment as "cackling from the hen-house," arguing that under the show's logic holding conventional morals and values can make a person of any ethnicity a "white supremacist."[1] He accused the program of amplifying "woke virtue signaling" and of exploiting a mass-casualty event to fit a narrative rather than mourning the dead, writing that the left "disenfranchised their own" by labeling Black and Latino people racist against themselves.[1] Johnny Q framed the episode as part of a broader push he said would continue until "our guns, freedom of speech, and our identity are stripped," and called on Americans to reject what he characterized as subversion of constitutional freedoms.[1]
