# Alexander King

**Sir Alexander King** was the figure who began the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]]'s *Limits to Growth* study in 1970, described by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as the launching point of the oligarchy's "grand green design."[1]

Ehret quotes King's 1990 book *The First Global Revolution*: "In searching for a new enemy to unite us, we came up with the idea that pollution, the threat of global warming, water shortages, famine and the like would fit the bill... All these dangers are caused by human intervention, and it is only through changed attitudes and behavior that they can be overcome. The real enemy then, is humanity itself."[1] For Ehret this passage confirms that the environmental agenda was conceived as a unifying enemy rather than a scientific finding, and that its promoters understood green energy sources would constrict global population.[1]

Ehret situates King as a successor to UNESCO founder Sir [[julian-huxley|Julian Huxley]] in the same [[malthusianism|Malthusian]] tradition.[1]

## Co-founder of the Club of Rome

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes King and [[aurelio-peccei|Aurelio Peccei]] as "two misanthropes" who led the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]] from its formation in 1968, and, citing [[f-william-engdahl|F. William Engdahl]], records that the think tank was founded by [[david-rockefeller|David Rockefeller]] together with the two men.[2] King was the [[oecd|OECD]] Director General for Scientific Affairs and head of its Science Program, and was also a consultant to [[nato|NATO]].[2] With Peccei he promulgated the gospel that the age of scientific progress and industrial growth must stop so the world could reset its values under a new paradigm of zero technological growth, and he advocated the pseudoscience dubbed [[world-problematique|"World Problematique"]].[2]

## "The real enemy is humanity itself"

King wrote: "In searching for a new enemy to unite us, we came up with the idea that pollution, the threat of global warming, water shortages, famine and the like would fit the bill…. All these dangers are caused by human intervention, and it is only through changed attitudes and behavior that they can be overcome. The real enemy then, is humanity itself."[2]

He also stood at the centre of the network of Rhodes Scholars and Privy Councillors — with [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]], [[maurice-lamontagne|Maurice Lamontagne]], Michael Pitfield and Governor General Roland Michener — that presided over the 1971 Montebello, Quebec meeting at which [[pierre-trudeau|Pierre Trudeau]] allocated Canadian taxpayer money to launch the study that became [[limits-to-growth|*Limits to Growth*]].[2]
