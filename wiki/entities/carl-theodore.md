# Carl Theodore

Duke Carl Theodore was the Elector of Bavaria who suppressed the [[bavarian-illuminati|Order of the Illuminati]] through a series of three edicts issued between 1784 and 1787.[1]

## First edict (1784)

On June 22, 1784, Carl Theodore issued the first edict against secret societies not authorized by law or the sovereign.[1] The measure appears to have been prompted by the ex-member Professor [[joseph-utzschneider|Joseph Utzschneider]], who had quit the Order in August 1783 and who, in October, along with his fellow Marianen (Marienburg) Academy professors and Order members Grünberger and Cosandey, presented the Duchess Maria Anna with an internal Illuminati document and a membership list. The Duchess was thoroughly alarmed and passed the material to the Duke.[1]

## Second edict (1785)

In February 1785 some members of the Illuminati petitioned Carl Theodore for an appearance before him to prove their innocence; the offer was rejected.[1] On March 2, 1785 he issued a second edict against secret societies, this time naming the Illuminati and [[freemasonry|Freemasonry]] specifically; shortly afterward a considerable quantity of important documents was concealed or burned.[1] The second ban "left no room for evasion," and government enforcers were given the weapons to "wage an effective command."[1]

Judicial inquiries followed at Ingolstadt. A considerable membership was found within the military, and officers and soldiers were ordered to come forward and confess involvement; state officials, professors, teachers and students identified as members were summarily dismissed, and some were banished from the country.[1] [[adam-weishaupt|Adam Weishaupt]] had already abandoned his university post two weeks earlier and fled the country.[1]

## Third edict (1787)

On August 16, 1787 the Duke put into effect the third and final edict against the Order, reinforcing the earlier bans: "criminal process, without distinction of person, dignity, state, or quality, was ordered against any Illuminatus who should be discovered continuing the work of recruiting. Any so charged and found guilty were to be deprived of their lives by the sword; while those thus recruited were to have their goods confiscated and themselves to be condemned to perpetual banishment from the territories of the duke."[1] Under the same penalties, members were forbidden to assemble as lodges under any name or circumstance.[1] After this point, all mention of the Illuminati appears to disappear from the records.[1]
