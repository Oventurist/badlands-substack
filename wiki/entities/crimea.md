# Crimea

Crimea is a peninsula on the northern Black Sea coast annexed by [[russia|Russia]] from [[ukraine|Ukraine]] in 2014, an act the United States and its European allies long regarded as illegal.[1]

## Ukrainian signal of willingness to negotiate (April 2023)

In April 2023 [[andriy-sybiha|Andriy Sybiha]], deputy head of President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]]'s office, told the *Financial Times* that Kyiv was willing to discuss the future of Crimea with Moscow if Ukrainian forces reached the border of the Russian-occupied peninsula — described by the paper as the most explicit statement of Ukrainian interest in negotiations since Kyiv cut off peace talks with the Kremlin in April 2022.[5] The remarks came amid the difficult fight for Bakhmut, high Ukrainian casualties in the east and anticipation of a spring counteroffensive, and marked a public reversal from Zelensky's October 2022 declaration that "We will definitely liberate Crimea."[5] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Zelensky was ceding narrative ground to Russia as US and NATO rhetoric about Ukrainian victory was simultaneously dialled back.[5]

## 2025 United States recognition proposal

In April 2025 Ukrainian and European officials preparing to meet in London were confronted with a fast-moving United States proposal to recognize Russia's annexation of Crimea and to freeze the war's front lines as part of a peace agreement.[1] The proposals, presented to Ukraine in Paris the previous week, included formal United States recognition of Crimea as Russian territory and the eventual lifting of sanctions on Russia under a future accord; in exchange Moscow would end hostilities at a moment when its military enjoyed battlefield momentum and sizable advantages in troop strength and weaponry.[1] Ukraine's allies hoped to obtain security guarantees and reconstruction programs in return for any territorial concessions.[1]

Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] told reporters that Ukraine would not recognize Crimea as Russian, noting that doing so would violate the country's constitution, and argued that discussing Crimea and other Ukrainian territory aided Moscow by prolonging the war because "it will not be possible to agree on everything quickly."[1]

## March 2023 NBC News reporting from the peninsula

In early March 2023 a correspondent for [[nbc-news|NBC News]] reporting from inside Crimea drew attention — and anger from Ukrainian officials and pro-Kyiv commentators — by describing the peninsula as a heavily militarized "military town" locked in "a very, very dangerous standoff" from which negotiation appeared difficult.[2] The correspondent questioned how the demilitarization of Crimea sought by [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]] and other United States officials could realistically be achieved.[2] The segment repeatedly noted that residents of Crimea see themselves as Russians; an accompanying NBC written report acknowledged that Kyiv, its Western allies and the [[united-nations|United Nations]] do not recognize the peninsula as Russian and that [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] had vowed to retake it, while profiling a 73-year-old resident who "speaks Russian, feels Russian and lives here."[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the broadcast as evidence that the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] was losing control of the Ukraine narrative, arguing that it conceded both the impossibility of dislodging Russia's armed presence in Crimea and the Russian self-identification of its population, and speculating that a similar admission about the Donbas would follow.[2]

## Badlands commentary

Contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the proposal against [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s three stated demands preceding the invasion: recognition of the Donbas as Russian, a guarantee that Ukraine would not join [[nato|NATO]], and that Crimea would not be returned to Ukraine.[1] He argued that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had already publicly conceded two of the three and that the third was coming "because the deals are already done."[1]

## Proposed U.S. recognition as Russian territory (March 2025)

In March 2025 the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] was reported to be considering recognizing Crimea as Russian territory as part of any future agreement to end the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[3] According to two people familiar with the matter, administration officials also discussed having the United States urge the United Nations to do the same — a request that would align Washington with the longstanding position of Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[3]

The previously unreported openness to these options emerged as Trump prepared for a Tuesday call with Putin, with a potential 30-day ceasefire on the table; Trump told reporters aboard Air Force One that negotiators had already discussed "dividing up certain assets."[3] No formal decisions had been made, and the Crimea options were among a multitude being floated.[3]

Badlands commentary called recognition a "no-brainer," citing the 2014 vote to leave Ukraine and join [[russia|Russia]], alleging that the Azov Battalion cut off water and electricity and terrorized the population after deploying to the peninsula, and invoking the baptism of Vladimir the Great in 988 on a beach in western Crimea near present-day Sevastopol — the Baptism of Rus — as evidence of a deeper historical claim.[3]

## Proposed US recognition of Russian control, April 2025

In April 2025, Bloomberg reported that the United States was prepared to recognize Russia's control over Crimea as part of a broader peace agreement between Moscow and Kyiv.[4] The potential concession was read as a signal of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s desire to secure a ceasefire deal; Trump and Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] indicated on April 18 that the administration might abandon its peace-brokering efforts altogether if negotiations failed to progress soon.[4]

Contemporary coverage recounted the peninsula's seizure: in late February 2014, Russian troops without military insignia invaded Crimea after Russia had quietly increased its troop presence through much of that month, blocking off airports in Sevastopol and Simferopol and seizing the Crimean Parliament building.[4] They also blockaded Ukrainian military bases on the peninsula, and Ukrainian troops received no order to shoot.[4] In March 2014 the Russian-controlled Crimean parliament voted to hold a "referendum" on joining Russia, a vote conducted without international observers and with armed Russian soldiers present at polling locations.[4]

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] linked the Crimea offer to the wider realignment, noting that Trump was simultaneously refusing to back Israeli strikes on Iran while the Saudis met with Iran on security deals, and was offering to lift Russian sanctions while recognizing Crimea as Russian territory — developments he argued make little sense without accounting for the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[4]
