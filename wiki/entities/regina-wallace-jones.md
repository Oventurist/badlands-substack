# Regina Wallace-Jones

**Regina Wallace-Jones** is the chief executive officer of [[actblue|ActBlue]], the online fundraising platform used by Democratic candidates and progressive organisations.[1]

## Congressional testimony, June 2026

Wallace-Jones appeared before a House hearing in June 2026 convened by Republicans to examine allegations of fraud, foreign contributions, and weaknesses in donor-verification systems at online political fundraising platforms.[1] During the hearing she repeatedly invoked her Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination when questioned by lawmakers.[1] The proceeding did not yield new evidence of criminal wrongdoing by ActBlue or by [[winred|WinRed]].[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the invocation as confirmation that ActBlue was under active prosecution, writing that this "is why Wallace Jones pled the fifth" and that the matter was not going away.[1]

## Contempt warning, June 2026

Later in June 2026, three House committee chairmen — [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] of Judiciary, [[bryan-steil|Bryan Steil]] of House Administration, and [[james-comer|James Comer]] of Oversight — sent Wallace-Jones a letter warning that ActBlue could face [[contempt-of-congress|contempt of Congress]] for failing to comply with subpoenas issued on July 22, 2025.[2] The chairmen said ActBlue had continued to withhold documents, including a June 5, 2026 privilege log covering at least 420 additional records, and alleged that the withheld materials pointed to potential foreign donations, misleading statements to Congress, and retaliation against a whistleblower.[2]

In testimony before the [[committee-on-house-administration|Committee on House Administration]] earlier that month, Wallace-Jones declined to answer questions regarding allegations that ActBlue facilitated foreign campaign donations to Democratic candidates in federal elections, again invoking the Fifth Amendment.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Wallace-Jones's rights had been carefully protected through the process, that she appeared "set up to take the fall," and that she should consider cooperating with investigators.[2]
