# J.P. Morgan Jr.

J.P. Morgan Jr. was the head of the Morgan banking house whose associates dominated the early leadership of the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]].[1]

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 1" states that representatives of the Morgan family were among the Council's original backers, alongside [[john-d-rockefeller|John D. Rockefeller]] and bankers [[paul-warburg|Paul Warburg]], [[jacob-schiff|Jacob Schiff]] and [[otto-kahn|Otto Kahn]].[1] The Council's founding president, [[john-w-davis|John W. Davis]], was Morgan's personal attorney; vice president [[paul-cravath|Paul Cravath]] represented Morgan properties; and first chairman [[russell-leffingwell|Russell Leffingwell]] was one of Morgan's partners.[1] Because most early members had Morgan connections, the article reports the view that the Council was heavily influenced by Morgan's interests.[1]

The same financial circle is described as having been intimately involved with both the [[panic-of-1907|Panic of 1907]] and the subsequent creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[1]

## Target of the Pecora hearings

[[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] encouraged [[ferdinand-pecora|Ferdinand Pecora]] to take on John Pierpont Morgan Jr. before the [[senate-committee-on-banking-and-currency|Senate Committee on Banking and Currency]].[2] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that, rather than controlling a purely American institution as many believed, Morgan Jr. was running an operation created in the mid-19th century as part of a British infiltration of America — the [[house-of-morgan|House of Morgan]], which by 1933 had grown into "a multi-headed hydra, controlling utilities, holding companies, banks and countless other subsidiaries."[2] Ehret groups him with the "financial gremlins" whose exposure allowed the American population to make sense of the injustices that befell them after 1929.[2]
