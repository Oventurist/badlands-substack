# Scott McAfee

Scott McAfee is the Georgia judge who presided over State of Georgia v. Trump, et al., the Fulton County racketeering prosecution arising from the 2020 presidential election.[1]

## Unsealing of the grand jury transcripts

In January 2026, McAfee lifted the protective order covering the special grand jury transcripts from the case, which had been dismissed on November 25, 2025.[1] The release made public the September 6, 2022 testimony of former Senator [[david-perdue|David Perdue]], in which Perdue alleged that Governor [[brian-kemp|Brian Kemp]] had directed the Georgia Bureau of Investigation not to investigate the election.[1] Badlands framed the unsealing as continuing to supply "more context of what actually happened (and what didn't happen) surrounding the 2020 election" even after the prosecution's collapse.[1]

## Disqualification ruling (March 2024)

In March 2024 McAfee ruled on the defendants' motion to disqualify District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] over her romantic relationship with special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]].[2] He found the allegations and evidence "legally insufficient to support a finding of an actual conflict of interest," but held that "the appearance of impropriety remains" and that "the established record now highlights a significant appearance of impropriety that infects the current structure of the prosecution team — an appearance that must be removed."[2] The remedy he imposed was that either Willis and her office or Wade had to leave the case; Wade resigned the same week, leaving Willis in place.[2]

The ruling followed an earlier McAfee order that struck six charges connected to the [[brad-raffensperger|Raffensperger]] phone call, which Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] said had already weakened a [[election-fraud-rico|RICO]] theory that depended on binding every act into a single criminal enterprise.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] praised McAfee for grounding the remedy in the protection of public trust, calling him the first court in a while to acknowledge public trust at all, and noted that his order pointed to the state ethics inquiry, the Georgia Bar complaint and the congressional inquiry as the appropriate forums for examining Willis's conduct.[2] She also observed that popular expectations — that McAfee would disbar Willis, jail her and Wade, dismiss the case and declare [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] the rightful winner of the 2020 and 2024 elections — were "detached from reality."[2]

## Bradley privilege ruling (February 2024)

Late on Monday, February 26, 2024, McAfee's chambers notified attorneys by email that certain communications between [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] and his former law partner and divorce lawyer [[terrence-bradley|Terrence Bradley]] were not protected by attorney-client privilege, meaning Bradley could be compelled to testify about Wade's relationship with [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] as soon as Tuesday afternoon.[3] The decision followed a closed-door meeting between the judge and Bradley earlier that day that lasted nearly an hour and twenty minutes, held to validate Bradley's understanding of privilege and determine next steps for his testimony.[3] The ruling was expected to produce further bombshell testimony as defense attorneys sought to disqualify the district attorney from prosecuting the Fulton County election interference case, with summations in [[michael-roman|Michael Roman]]'s disqualification motion scheduled for Friday, March 1.[3]

## March 2024 motions hearing

On March 28, 2024, McAfee presided over a hearing on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s motion to dismiss the indictment on [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] grounds and on two pretrial motions filed by co-defendant [[david-shafer|David Shafer]].[4] The session centered on technical legal arguments and marked something of a return to normalcy after the case was rocked by allegations that District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] improperly benefited from her romantic relationship with special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]].[4]

## February 2024 ruling on the disqualification hearing

At a hearing on Monday, February 12, 2024, McAfee said that misconduct allegations against District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] "could result in disqualification" if the evidence showed she had financially benefited from the [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|Georgia election interference case]], and confirmed that the evidentiary hearing scheduled for that Thursday would proceed.[5] He explained: "The state has admitted a relationship existed. And so what remains to be proven is the existence and extent of any financial benefit, again if there even was one. And so because I think it's possible that the facts alleged by the defendant could result in disqualification, I think an evidentiary hearing must occur to establish the record on those core allegations."[5]

The Monday hearing was convened to determine whether Willis, special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] and other employees of the district attorney's office would have to testify Thursday; all had been subpoenaed by co-defendant [[michael-roman|Michael Roman]].[5] McAfee declined to rule out testimony from Willis or Wade but cautioned, "We're not talking about calling Ms. Willis as the first witness, and we need to get over a few procedural hurdles before we can get there."[5] The hearing was set for 9:30 a.m. Eastern on Thursday.[5]

## May 2024 election victory

McAfee, the Fulton County Superior Court judge randomly assigned to preside over the Georgia election interference case, fended off a challenger in May 2024, winning a nonpartisan election to keep his seat.[6] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] stressed that McAfee had not been in a primary but in the definitive contest, and would serve a full four-year term beginning in January 2025 — meaning Georgia voters had decisively chosen for him to continue judging, his most high-profile assignment being [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]]'s case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[6] She recalled that McAfee had ruled Willis could only continue the case if special prosecutor [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] stepped aside; Wade promptly left, but a defense appeal of the ruling was pending before the Georgia Court of Appeals.[6] During the campaign, critics had claimed McAfee's notoriety hurt his electability and that electability pressures might compromise his impartiality — concerns Ashe deemed moot after the win.[6] She also noted that the Atlanta Journal-Constitution devoted barely two paragraphs to McAfee's victory while concentrating on Willis's primary.[6]

## Cameras in the courtroom (August 2023)

On Tuesday, August 22, 2023, McAfee granted a request from four local television stations to bring live cameras and other recording devices into his courtroom through September 8, marking the first time one of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s criminal proceedings would be televised.[7] District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] was seeking to hold the arraignments of all defendants the week following Labor Day, which fell inside that window; if the timeline were delayed, McAfee's order would expire.[7] The order did not indicate whether cameras would be allowed during a trial or any other future proceedings in the case.[7]

## March 2024 ruling on the Georgia indictment

On Wednesday, March 13, 2024, McAfee quashed six counts of the Fulton County racketeering indictment, three of them against former President Donald Trump, finding that prosecutors had not provided enough detail about the alleged crimes.[8] He left the rest of the indictment intact, including ten counts against Trump and the overarching RICO charge, and stated that prosecutors could seek a new indictment to reinstate the dismissed counts.[8] The counts struck down included those concerning the Brad Raffensperger phone call and the defendants' oaths of office.[8]

The ruling resolved a pending motion unrelated to the separate effort to disqualify District Attorney Fani Willis, on which McAfee's decision was expected within days.[8] Commentators divided over whether the dismissal signalled anything about his thinking on disqualification: Norm Eisen read it as a slight lean toward keeping Willis, while CNN's Elie Honig treated it purely as an embarrassing setback for the prosecution.[8]

## Campaign donation to Fani Willis

In February 2024 the Daily Caller reported that McAfee had made a small $150 donation to [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]]'s campaign for Fulton County District Attorney before his own appointment to the bench.[9] McAfee, sworn in on February 1, 2023 after being appointed by Republican Governor [[brian-kemp|Brian Kemp]], made the contribution in June 2020 while still working as an assistant U.S. Attorney for the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], according to financial disclosures.[9] The New York Times had earlier reported that McAfee formerly worked under Willis when she led the complex trial division in the Fulton County District Attorney's Office.[9] Atlanta criminal defense attorney [[philip-holloway|Philip Holloway]] called the donation "nominal" but said it should still have been disclosed to the defendants so they could judge whether it amounted to a conflict of interest.[9]

The disclosure came as McAfee was preparing to rule on whether Willis should be disqualified over allegations that she financially benefited from appointing her romantic partner [[nathan-wade|Nathan Wade]] to the Trump case, a decision expected within about a week.[9] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that while the donation was small and years old, the judge's failure to disclose it was concerning because even the appearance of impropriety can materially harm public trust.[9] She nonetheless credited McAfee with having been fair — allowing [[harrison-floyd|Harrison Floyd]] to explore evidence blocked in other courts, letting Willis speak at length on the stand, and reviewing Terrence Bradley's text messages in camera — and said his handling of the case had stirred in her some hope that justice still existed.[9] She also asked pointedly who benefited from the character assassination of the first judge who appeared fair.[9]

## Bond decisions of August 21, 2023

On Monday, August 21, 2023, McAfee set bond for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at $200,000 in the [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|Fulton County election interference case]], along with $100,000 each for attorneys [[john-eastman|John Eastman]] and [[kenneth-chesebro|Kenneth Chesebro]], $50,000 for [[ray-smith-iii|Ray Smith III]], and $10,000 for [[scott-hall|Scott Hall]].[10] The bond agreements he approved barred every defendant from performing any act to intimidate a codefendant or witness or otherwise obstruct the administration of justice, with Trump's agreement expressly covering "no direct or indirect threat."[10] All nineteen defendants were required to report to the [[fulton-county-jail|Fulton County Jail]] for processing by noon that Friday.[10]
