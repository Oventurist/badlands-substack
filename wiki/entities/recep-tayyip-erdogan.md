# Recep Tayyip Erdogan

Recep Tayyip Erdogan is the president of [[turkey|Turkiye]] and, in Badlands analysis, a figure associated with the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] of nationalist heads of state.[1]

## Role in the MBZ death rumors

In February 2026 Erdogan posted on X that he was postponing a visit to Abu Dhabi because UAE President [[mohamed-bin-zayed-al-nahyan|Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan]] had a "health problem," reporting that the two had spoken by phone and that he had wished the Emirati leader a prompt recovery. The post was later deleted.[1]

The deleted message coincided with — and by some accounts triggered — a wave of online claims that MBZ had died or suffered a serious stroke. Both the Turkish and Emirati governments issued statements about the phone call, but these did not settle the speculation.[1] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Erdogan's status as a Sovereign Alliance figure made it plausible the episode was a deliberate psyop designed to sow confusion while regional alignments — including tension between [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]] and new bilateral deals with India and Pakistan — were being reshaped.[1]

## March 2026 missile dispute with Iran

In March 2026 Turkish officials said the country's air defenses had shot down a second missile that Ankara claimed was fired from [[iran|Iran]], intercepted near Türkiye's eastern border.[2] Iran denied launching any attacks against Türkiye, Azerbaijan or Greek Cyprus, and Iranian officials said Tehran had carried out no strikes on those locations.[2] Erdoğan said Ankara had warned Iran that Türkiye could defend itself against any threat and that its military had the capacity to repel potential attacks on its territory.[2]

Separately, Erdoğan accused [[israel|Israel]] of taking advantage of shifting global attention to sabotage efforts toward a two-state solution in the [[israel-palestine-conflict|Israeli–Palestinian conflict]], adding that regional developments were increasing pressure across the Middle East.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] counted Erdoğan among the regional strongmen — alongside [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and Pakistan's [[shehbaz-sharif|Shehbaz Sharif]] — who refused to be baited into conflict with their neighbors and whose restraint was framed as the first real stress test of the region's new security architecture.[2]

## Ankara NATO summit (July 2026)

Erdoğan hosted the [[nato|NATO]] summit in Ankara, where he and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] exchanged warm compliments ahead of the meeting, to the visible discomfort of other alliance members.[3] Trump said the only reason he attended was that the summit was hosted by his friend Erdoğan, and Badlands commentary read the episode as evidence that Washington is prioritizing its relationship with [[turkey|Turkey]] over Europe, predicting similar treatment for other Muslim-majority states of the Middle East including [[iran|Iran]].[3] Potential U.S. arms sales to Turkey were cited by [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] as a point of occasional disagreement with Trump.[3]

## Contrast with Netanyahu, 2025–2026
Badlands commentary recounted that after Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] appeared to invite himself to the White House around the UN General Assembly, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that Erdogan would visit the White House the day before Netanyahu's General Assembly address, rolling out the red carpet for the Turkish president.[4] The gesture was reciprocated when Trump visited Turkey for the NATO Summit in July 2026, and Trump signed a nuclear trade deal with Turkey the day before Netanyahu spoke at the UN.[4] Netanyahu's public rebuke of a proposed U.S. sale of F-35 fighter jets to Turkey, delivered on Fox News the day before Trump's Turkey trip, reportedly angered the White House.[4]

## Use as a signal against Netanyahu

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued in July 2026 that [[donald-trump|Trump]] repeatedly deploys Erdoğan as a diplomatic signal against Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[5] He recalled that when Netanyahu sought to "crash the White House" in September 2025 during the UN General Assembly, Trump invited Erdoğan to the White House the day before Netanyahu was scheduled to address the assembly, and that the pattern recurred when Netanyahu again invited himself in July 2026.[5] Around the same time Trump publicly hailed [[turkey|Turkey]] as a "tremendous ally" while criticizing Israeli military operations, and the commentary asked what moves would be made against Turkey after Netanyahu's visit.[5]

## Lebanon genocide accusations (April 2026)

As Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] posted videos surrounded by masked [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] personnel declaring an intent to continue bombing villages in southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]], Erdoğan repeatedly stated that [[israel|Israel]] had committed a genocide in Gaza and was conducting another in Lebanon.[6] He said Israel was threatening the stability of the region and that [[turkey|Turkey]] may need to intervene.[6]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that he had forecast a year earlier that Turkey might become the army that intervenes to stop Netanyahu and the IDF.[6]

## February 2026 regional tour

In early February 2026 Erdoğan advanced a broad regional push across the Middle East, North Africa, and Eurasia, combining economic diplomacy, military expansion, and mediation.[7] Visiting [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], Turkey and the Kingdom agreed to cooperate on $2 billion in solar energy projects, which Turkish Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar called "one of the most important examples of direct foreign investment in our energy sector," to be financed entirely through external financing and international lenders.[7]

Turkey and [[egypt|Egypt]] issued a joint declaration following their second Strategic Cooperation Council meeting, committing to expanded cooperation on trade, energy, defense, and regional security, agreeing to support de-escalation in the Middle East and a resumption of negotiations between the United States and [[iran|Iran]], and setting the next High-Level Strategic Cooperation Council meeting for 2028 in Ankara.[7] Ankara simultaneously positioned itself as a diplomatic intermediary, reportedly preparing to host US–Iran talks in Istanbul, while separate de-escalation talks were reported for Muscat, [[oman|Oman]] — confirmed on X by Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]].[7]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the tour as the visible surface of "the rise of a new Middle East," tracing it back to the November 2023 Organization of Islamic Cooperation meeting at which Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] called on the Ummah to set aside internal disputes and build rather than wage war.[7] Erdoğan's Egypt leg was described as completing a "redemption arc," given his earlier support for the Arab Spring and the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] government of Mohamed Morsi, the 2013 counterinsurgency led by General al-Sisi, and the rupture in Turkish–Egyptian relations that lasted until MBS brokered contact in late 2023.[7] The Turkey–Egypt defense pact, coupled with the Turkey–[[saudi-pakistan-defense-pact|Saudi Arabia–Pakistan]] arrangement, was read as covering the region's three continental gateways — Turkey to Europe, [[pakistan|Pakistan]] to Asia, and Egypt to Africa — with F-16 deployments to [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]] and [[somalia|Somalia]] marking its northern and southern access points.[7] In this account [[israel|Israel]] has been entirely bypassed and must now decide whether to live as a citizen of the new order or continue at war.[7]

## Ankara agreements with Iraq (July 2026)

On 29 July 2026, Erdoğan hosted Iraqi Prime Minister [[ali-al-zaidi|Ali al-Zaidi]] in Ankara, where [[turkey|Turkey]] and [[iraq|Iraq]] signed five agreements covering railway and road transport via the Ovakoy-Fishkhabur crossing, infrastructure development in exchange for natural resources, police academy education, youth and sports, and industrial property.[8] Erdoğan described the pacts as steps to deepen bilateral ties and stressed that Iraq's security is inseparable from Turkey's, with both sides cooperating on counterterrorism, energy, and a "terror-free region."[8]

In parallel, the [[turkish-petroleum-corporation|Turkish Petroleum Corporation]] acquired a 15% stake in bp's Kirkuk oil project alongside ConocoPhillips, giving Turkey a share in fields holding more than 3 billion barrels of oil equivalent; Energy Minister [[alparslan-bayraktar|Alparslan Bayraktar]] called it a major milestone toward TPAO's target of one million barrels per day.[8] Turkish Foreign Minister [[hakan-fidan|Hakan Fidan]] had met U.S. Ambassador and Special Presidential Envoy for Syria and Iraq [[tom-barrack|Tom Barrack]] in Ankara on 27 July, in talks focused on the bilateral partnership and regional stability regarding [[syria|Syria]] and Iraq.[8]

Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmad al-Sharaa]] separately credited Erdoğan — along with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] — for mediating with [[donald-trump|President Trump]] the previous year to lift most U.S. sanctions on Syria following the fall of the Assad regime, while noting that Syria's remaining designation as a state sponsor of terrorism remains an economic obstacle.[8] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the Ankara meetings as the substantive event of the week, arguing that the emerging rail and pipeline architecture amounts to a realization of the [[five-seas-vision|Five Seas Vision]].[8]

## Confrontation with Netanyahu (June 2026)

In June 2026 Erdoğan sharply criticized Israeli military operations in [[syria|Syria]] and [[lebanon|Lebanon]], warning that Israel's regional actions could eventually threaten [[turkey|Turkey]] itself.[9] Addressing the Turkish Parliament, he accused Israel of pursuing expansionist policies, renewed calls for stronger support of the Palestinian cause including the eventual "liberation" of Jerusalem, and said Israel's stated objective of establishing [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] through conquest and annexation of neighboring states must never be allowed to succeed.[9] Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] responded on X by branding Erdoğan an "antisemitic dictator" who supports [[hamas|Hamas]], and said Israel would keep acting against security threats across the region.[9]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] situated the exchange in a pattern of escalating rebukes dating to the October 7 attacks, recalling that Israeli minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] had warned Erdoğan at the United Nations that he might become the next Saddam Hussein, and that figures in the Israeli [[knesset|Knesset]] opposition had spoken of wanting Turkey to become "the next Iran."[9] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] struck a markedly different tone, praising Erdoğan as a strong and effective negotiator when asked in the Oval Office about the Turkish leader's rhetoric and about the withheld F-35 jets Turkey has already paid for — aircraft held back over Erdoğan's relationship with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and fears of Russian access to the technology.[9]

The Badlands analysis argued that as the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] advances north in Lebanon toward [[beirut|Beirut]], it moves closer to Turkey's practical sphere in Syria, and that Ankara is most concerned about an Israeli push toward Damascus that would jeopardize Erdoğan's plans with the Saudis to rebuild Syria.[9] It speculated that Netanyahu might leverage Israel's new security agreement with [[greece|Greece]] — forged over shared antagonism toward Turkey — to provoke Turkey in the Aegean Sea or around [[cyprus|Cyprus]], noting that a Greek–Turkish clash between two [[nato|NATO]] members would not invoke [[article-5|Article 5]], whereas an Israeli attack on Turkey would place Israel at war with the whole alliance.[9] The writer concluded that Erdoğan must work with allies in [[pakistan|Pakistan]], Egypt and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to contain the IDF in Lebanon and fold [[iran|Iran]] into an emerging Middle East security architecture.[9]

## Warning to Netanyahu on behalf of the "Muslim world" (May 2026)

In late May 2026 Erdoğan warned Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] that the "Muslim world" would not forget or forgive Israel's actions in Gaza, accusing the Israeli government of pursuing policies that threaten regional stability and deepen humanitarian suffering.[10] He said: "I pray, God willing, that this tyrant called Netanyahu will receive the lesson he deserves from the Muslim world. May we witness this outcome as soon as possible."[10] The remarks came amid Turkey's continued positioning as a leading voice against Israel's Gaza campaign and a broader deterioration in Turkey–Israel relations, with Ankara increasing diplomatic pressure, trade restrictions, and rhetorical attacks while calling for greater international intervention on behalf of Palestinians.[10]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the statement as confirmation that Muslim states will not join the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] as currently written, and that the more likely outcome is that they set aside their differences and unite against [[israel|Israel]] — not necessarily in imminent kinetic conflict, but as an accumulating response to annexation and to atrocity footage replayed at public gatherings.[10] The commentary emphasized that Erdoğan is a favored partner of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who praises him at every opportunity, and that the sentiment expressed is found across the Muslim world.[10] It paired this with Israeli media reports that the US fleet stationed at Israeli air bases is prepared to withdraw from the Middle East entirely within 72 hours of a peace deal being finalized, concluding that a measured strategist would seek peace with adversaries who vastly outnumber him, but that hubris is blinding.[10]

## F-35 sale and the "bromance" controversy (2026)

In February 2026 Erdogan became a flashpoint in the American debate over Iran policy. U.S. Ambassador to Turkey and Special Envoy to Syria [[tom-barrack|Tom Barrack]], speaking at the Milken Institute Middle East and Africa Summit in Abu Dhabi, described [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s relationship with Erdogan as a "bromance."[11] [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] responded by calling Erdogan "evil" in a post addressing the nuclear deal Trump signed with the Turkish president, under which Trump promised Turkey F-35 fighter jets.[11] Separately, U.S. Ambassador to Israel [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] told Israeli media that Turkey would never receive the F-35s because the Deep State would block approval of the sale in [[congress|Congress]].[11] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated both statements as subversion of Trump's peacemaking with foreign allies.[11]

## November 2025 call with Putin on Ukraine

On Monday, November 24, 2025, the Kremlin said Erdogan and Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had agreed in a phone conversation to intensify contacts between their countries' diplomats on settling the Ukraine conflict.[12] The two leaders discussed a leaked U.S.-drafted proposal that Washington had presented to Moscow and Kiev earlier that month; details were not officially released, but media reports indicated it included de facto recognition of Crimea and Donbass as Russian, a 600,000-personnel cap on the Ukrainian military, and a requirement that Kiev stay out of NATO.[12] Putin said Moscow had received the plan but had not discussed it in detail, adding that the proposals were consistent with understandings reached at the Alaska summit in August and could serve as the basis for a peace agreement.[12]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] speculated that the two leaders likely also discussed Syria and how to drive the IDF out permanently, citing reports that Russian military leaders had visited southern Syria for the first time since the fall of Assad and suggesting they might be helping Jolani's Syria resist Israeli aggression.[12]

## September 2025 White House visit

Erdogan visited the [[white-house|White House]] on September 25, 2025, his first such visit in six years, meeting President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at a moment when the international community was grappling with how to end the war in Gaza and Russia's war on Ukraine.[13] The atmosphere was markedly less tense than his 2019 visit, which had followed a significant fallout between the two countries over Syria — a subject on which the leaders were now more aligned.[13] Trump dominated the press availability, telling reporters the pair would discuss tariffs and a Ukraine ceasefire and that Erdogan might be "successful" regarding the sale of F-35 and F-16 jets, and he urged Erdogan to stop buying Russian oil.[13] After roughly two hours of private talks Trump gave a thumbs-up and called the meeting "great."[13]

During the visit [[turkey|Türkiye]] signed the [[us-turkiye-memorandum-on-strategic-civil-nuclear-cooperation|Memorandum of Understanding on Strategic Civil Nuclear Cooperation]] with the United States, announced by Energy Minister [[alparslan-bayraktar|Alparslan Bayraktar]] and signed with Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] in the presence of the leaders.[13] Separately, state-run BOTAS signed contracts for a combined 75.8 billion cubic meters of LNG imports with Swiss-based Mercuria and Australia's Woodside Energy.[13]

Badlands commentary noted Trump's remark, "Rigged election. *points to Erdogan* He knows about rigged elections better than anybody," and Erdogan's line that "when I was in exile, we were still friends," using both to argue that foreign leaders had maintained relationships with Trump throughout what contributors call the illegitimate Biden period.[13] Other contributors framed Erdogan as delivering on a 2023 campaign promise of energy independence while chafing at Turkey's exclusion from nuclear weapons development, a posture expected to antagonize Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] ahead of his UN address.[13] Commentary also held that the F-16 deal covering 40 delayed aircraft had actually been signed the previous year and was intended to modernize Turkey's existing fleet — plausibly for contesting Syrian airspace against Israeli F-35s.[13]

## Blocking Sweden's NATO accession (2023)

As of March 2023 Erdoğan continued to block Sweden's accession to [[nato|NATO]], a stance that drew sustained criticism in the German press.[14] An opinion piece by Clemens Wergin in Die Welt argued that the alliance should no longer trust [[turkey|Turkey]] or [[hungary|Hungary]], claiming both had developed "unseemly" ties to [[russia|Russia]] and proposing that NATO stop sharing certain sensitive data with Ankara and Budapest because their "turn toward authoritarianism" had distanced them from the alliance's values.[14] Badlands commentary treated the attack as confirmation that globalist and NATO-aligned outlets were targeting states hedging between blocs as the [[multipolar-world|multipolar world order]] advanced.[14]

## Warning to Syria's government (December 2024)

As Islamist militants of [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] and allied groups launched a large-scale offensive from Idlib province toward Aleppo, Hama and Homs in late 2024, Erdoğan said Ankara remained committed to the territorial integrity of [[syria|Syria]] and wanted the conflict resolved in line with the "legitimate wishes" of the Syrian people.[15] Speaking at a joint press conference with Montenegrin President Jakov Milatović, he said: "Our greatest wish is for Syria's territorial integrity and national unity to be preserved and for [the conflict] to end with consensus in line with the legitimate demands of the Syrian people."[15] He said recent events had "confirmed and certified" that Türkiye had been correct, and that Türkiye's foreign minister and intelligence chief were in "continuous consultations" with counterparts while Ankara "constantly monitor[ed] the process on the ground."[15] Foreign Minister [[hakan-fidan|Hakan Fidan]] appeared to blame the government in Damascus for the jihadist onslaught.[15]

## Badlands assessment of Erdoğan's duplicity (December 2024)

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Erdoğan as "an enigma" who had played both sides of nearly every conflict since coming to power roughly 25 years earlier, most notably in overtly supporting the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]]'s attempted overthrow of Arab governments across the Middle East and North Africa during the Arab Spring of 2011.[15] The commentary noted that [[turkey|Turkey]] has been a key [[nato|NATO]] member since 1952 because of its geography relative to Russia and the Black Sea, and cited Colonel Towner-Watkins's account of Turkey as a major hub for [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]], presenting the Syrian "rebels" as another iteration of that operation.[15] It observed that Türkiye, Iran and Russia had signed a 2017 agreement to support Syria's territorial integrity, and that the rebels Erdoğan was now backing were fighting [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s military even as Ankara courted membership in [[brics|BRICS]] and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization.[15]

The writer allowed that the maneuver might form part of a ruse run by the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], but argued that [[nato|NATO]], the [[cia|CIA]] and British intelligence remain deeply entrenched in Turkey and may still exert coercive influence over Erdoğan — a brittle control mechanism likely to fail.[15] He recalled that Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] first shipped weapons out of Benghazi through Turkey into Syria in 2012 to create [[isis|ISIS]], almost certainly in coordination with Erdoğan, and cited a reported failed assassination attempt against Erdoğan in May 2024.[15] The analysis concluded that Syria is "a giant exposure operation" that would ultimately discredit both the government in Tel Aviv and the Republican establishment in Washington.[15]

## Statements on Syria (December 2024)

Speaking at a joint press conference with Montenegrin President Jakov Milatović on Monday, December 2, 2024, Erdogan said Ankara was committed to Syria's territorial integrity and wanted the conflict resolved in accordance with the "legitimate wishes" of the Syrian people.[15] The remarks followed the resumption of fighting as [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] and allied Islamist militants launched a large-scale offensive out of Idlib province toward Aleppo, Hama and Homs.[15] "Our greatest wish is for Syria's territorial integrity and national unity to be preserved and for [the conflict] to end with consensus in line with the legitimate demands of the Syrian people," he said, adding that recent events had "confirmed and certified" that Türkiye had been correct.[15] He said Türkiye's foreign minister and intelligence chief were in "continuous consultations" with counterparts and that Ankara was "constantly monitoring the process on the ground," while Foreign Minister [[hakan-fidan|Hakan Fidan]] appeared to blame the government in Damascus for the offensive.[15]

## Badlands assessment

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Erdogan as an enigma who has played both sides of nearly every conflict in his roughly 25 years in power, most conspicuously by backing the Muslim Brotherhood's attempts to overthrow Arab governments across the Middle East and North Africa during the 2011 Arab Spring.[15] The Brief noted that Türkiye, Iran and Russia signed an agreement in 2017 to support Syria's territorial integrity and end the war begun by anti-government rebels in 2011, making Erdogan's support for forces now fighting Russian-backed troops a further example of his duplicity — particularly while courting membership in [[brics|BRICS]] and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization.[15]

The commentary argued that Turkey has served as a major hub for [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]] and that the current "rebels" represent another iteration of that operation, that [[nato|NATO]], the [[cia|CIA]] and British intelligence remain deeply entrenched in the country, and that Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] shipped weapons from Benghazi through Turkey into Syria in 2012 to create [[isis|ISIS]] in coordination with Erdogan.[15] It also recalled a reported failed assassination attempt against Erdogan in May 2024, and suggested his current posture may be a hedge unlikely to be well received in Moscow or Tehran.[15]

## 2024 threats against Israel and calls for NATO expulsion

In late July 2024, Israeli Foreign Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] demanded that [[nato|NATO]] expel Turkey from the alliance after Erdoğan threatened to invade Israel.[16] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the threats — recurring since October 7 even as Turkey continued supplying Israel with fuel and resources — as political posturing, while noting that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Xi Jinping had succeeded in drawing Erdoğan to BRICS and Shanghai Cooperation Organization meetings.[16] The same commentary recalled that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] endorsed Erdoğan in 2019, describing himself as a "big fan" of the Turkish leader.[16]

## BRICS application and the September 2024 Izmir episode

In early September 2024, Erdoğan was reported to be petitioning to join the expanding [[brics|BRICS]] alliance while remaining a member of the [[nato|NATO]] bloc, a step Badlands described as a massive symbolic break with the Western alliance.[17] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the near-simultaneous wave of "Turkey Bad" coverage — the staged and ultimately harmless assault on US service members in Izmir and the resulting port protests — had everything to do with that application, since it would not suit the proxy and sanction war efforts of the Western hegemon for a major cultural and economic centre straddling the Middle East and Asia to appear reasonable and conciliatory.[17] The same passage noted, crediting [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was reportedly "a very big fan of" Erdoğan.[17] See [[2024-izmir-assault-on-us-soldiers]].

## Post-Assad positioning (December 2024)

Badlands described Erdoğan as continuing "to play enigma" in the weeks after the fall of Damascus: he issued public concerns over [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s ambitions in the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]] even though, in the Brief's telling, he had used his own proxy forces in northern Syria to help overthrow [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] in the first place.[18] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told reporters that Turkey was effectively in control of Syria, saying of the land, "They have wanted that land for thousands of years" — a remark Badlands read as coded misdirection, noting that the Ottoman Empire held Damascus and modern Syria for roughly 700 years before losing it in 1918 at the end of World War I, and implying another group has coveted Damascus far longer.[18] Turkish Energy Minister [[alparslan-bayraktar|Alparslan Bayraktar]] simultaneously floated reviving the [[qatar-turkey-pipeline|Qatar–Türkiye pipeline]] through a stabilized Syria.[18]

## ICC war crimes push against Israel (2023)

In late October 2023, Erdoğan moved to make good on threats issued before a large pro-Palestinian rally, with Turkish media reporting that officials were exploring ways to bring a war crimes case centered on [[israel|Israel]] before the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] at The Hague as the death toll in [[gaza|Gaza]] soared past 8,500.[19] Addressing a crowd of hundreds of thousands in Istanbul, he said: "Israel, we will also declare you as a war criminal to the world, we are preparing for it, and we will introduce Israel to the world as a war criminal," calling Israel an "occupier" committing war crimes and adding, "The West owes you, but Türkiye does not owe you."[19]

The principal obstacle was that [[turkey|Turkey]] is not a party to the [[rome-statute|Rome Statute]] and so cannot apply directly to the court.[19] As Turkey's *Daily Sabah* explained, Ankara could instead notify the ICC Prosecutor's office through government agencies and non-governmental organizations about alleged crimes against humanity, since Article 15 of the Rome Statute permits the Prosecutor to initiate investigations *proprio motu* on the basis of such information.[19] The publication cited the visit of ICC chief prosecutor [[karim-khan|Karim Khan]] to the Rafah crossing, where he warned that impeding relief supplies protected by the Geneva Conventions could constitute a crime within the court's jurisdiction.[19]

## March 2025 protest crackdown

The arrest in March 2025 of Istanbul's opposition mayor [[ekrem-imamoglu|Ekrem Imamoglu]] — Erdogan's main political rival — on corruption charges he denies triggered mass anti-government protests that the authorities banned but could not stop.[20] Seven journalists covering the demonstrations, among them AFP photojournalist [[yasin-akgul|Yasin Akgul]], were detained in dawn raids on 23 March, prompting international concern for press freedom and democracy in Turkey.[20] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] suggested a potential [[color-revolution|color revolution]] or "psy-coup" was underway against Erdogan, motivated by Syria policy and his pivot toward Russia, China and BRICS.[20]

## Counterintelligence sweeps against Mossad networks (2024)

The Badlands News Brief highlighted Türkiye's 2024 campaign against alleged [[mossad|Mossad]] networks under Erdogan, culminating in the 3 September arrest of [[liridon-rexhepi|Liridon Rexhepi]], named by [[turkish-national-intelligence-organization|MIT]] as the head of Mossad's financial network in the country.[21] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that a pattern was forming across the January round-up of Mossad agents, a May bust of an Israeli organ-trafficking ring, and the Rexhepi case, and suspected links to organized crime in Ukraine given the region's geography.[21] He noted that [[donald-trump|Trump]] is a fan of Erdogan, and that Colonel Towner Watkins had described Turkey on [[breaking-history|Breaking History]] as a major hub — effectively the headquarters — for both [[nato|NATO]] and [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]], making Erdogan's moves against intelligence agents and crime syndicates tied to Israel and possibly Ukraine especially notable.[21]

## Hosting the 2024 US–Russia prisoner swap

Speaking at the [[eastern-economic-forum|Eastern Economic Forum]] in Vladivostok in September 2024, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] thanked Erdogan for providing the venue for the [[2024-us-russia-prisoner-swap|largest US–Russia prisoner exchange since the Cold War]], in which 24 prisoners were released — 16 returned to the West and eight to Russia — including journalist Evan Gershkovich and former US Marine Paul Whelan.[22] Putin credited Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] with an active role in the initial stages of the negotiation and said several other Arab countries had helped facilitate the swap without naming them.[22]

## Call for an emergency Islamic summit, September 2024

During the [[gulf-cooperation-council|Gulf Cooperation Council]] regional meeting held in Riyadh on September 9, 2024, Erdoğan publicly called for an emergency meeting of all Islamic world leaders.[23] Saudi Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] left the Riyadh gathering immediately afterward, travelling directly to the airport and flying to [[egypt|Egypt]].[23] The call came days after [[turkey|Turkey]] and Egypt held their first diplomatic meeting in 12 years — a gap dating to the Arab Spring, when Turkey was accused of supporting the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]]'s temporary overthrow of the Egyptian government.[23] Badlands commentary read the sequence as part of a broader healing of the Middle East and the consolidation of an alliance across the Islamic world.[23]

## Turkey as diplomatic pivot (December 2025)

Turkey featured prominently in the December 1, 2025 news cycle as a point of convergence between Washington and Moscow. The Kremlin condemned Ukrainian sea-drone attacks on tankers off the Turkish coast as an infringement of Turkish sovereignty, while the Washington Post reported that Turkey had emerged as a possible destination for [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] in the event of a negotiated exit from Venezuela.[24] Badlands commentary observed that Trump was defending Turkey over Syria against Israeli aggression while [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] defended Turkey against Ukraine, and argued that unidentified actors were running false-flag operations in Iraqi Kurdistan to blame Erdoğan even as he pursued unprecedented peace talks with the Kurds.[24]

## Positioning in the 2025 Russia–Azerbaijan rift

Badlands analysis in July 2025 identified Erdogan as the decisive variable in the deepening rift between Moscow and Baku, given Turkey's historic ties to [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]] and the likelihood that he was being pressured by [[nato|NATO]] to help the Azeris "twist the knife" on [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] and Iran.[25] Cutting against that pressure were Erdogan's flirtation with BRICS and the [[shanghai-cooperation-organization|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]], his rapprochement with Iran and the Arab states, and a collapsing situation in Syria as [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] pushed to expand Israel's borders.[25] Putin and Erdogan had also announced a third round of Russia–Ukraine peace talks to be hosted in Istanbul.[25] The expectation offered was that Erdogan would play his cards close to the chest while continuing to antagonize the Zionists.[25] See [[2025-russia-azerbaijan-rift|2025 Russia–Azerbaijan rift]].[25]

## Encirclement of Israel and the TurkStream guarantee (December 2025)

Analysis published in December 2025 argued that Turkish foreign policy since 2019 had shifted from opportunistic adventurism to a structured, region-wide projection strategy, building through military bases, naval power, proxy networks and ideological patronage an arc of influence around [[israel|Israel]] stretching from northern Syria to the Mediterranean and into the Palestinian political arena.[26] The architecture rested on three pillars — forward military presence, Islamist political patronage and assertive maritime behavior — with Ankara controlling meaningful territory in northern Syria, leveraging its hold over northern Cyprus and sustaining entrenched involvement in Libya, while its growing role in Palestinian politics was described as the most significant pillar.[26] What the analysis found most puzzling was Washington's restraint.[26]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the same facts inversely, arguing that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was deliberately using NATO's second-largest military — the largest in the Middle East — to bring the Israeli war machine to heel by plugging Turkish soldiers into both Syria and Gaza.[26] He further contended that Erdoğan was being used to subvert [[european-union|EU]] efforts to widen war with [[russia|Russia]], pointing to Erdoğan's meeting with Hungarian Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] and their announcement that Turkey would guarantee continued and safe transit of Russian gas to Hungary through the [[turkstream-pipeline|TurkStream pipeline]], building on a 15-year agreement signed in 2021.[26] With Turkey becoming the central player in resolving both the Ukrainian and Israeli conflicts, he predicted Ankara would become the target of both kinetic and narrative attacks.[26]

## June 2025 comparison of Netanyahu to Hitler

Speaking to members of his ruling AK Party in Parliament in June 2025, Erdogan condemned Israel's aggression in Gaza and its ongoing attacks on [[iran|Iran]], declaring that "the most horrifying photos and videos from World War II pale in comparison to what we're seeing from Gaza" and claiming that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had "long surpassed the tyrant Hitler in the crime of genocide."[27] He also said Iran had the legal right to respond to Israel's attacks, which began that Friday on the claim that Tehran was nearing completion of a nuclear bomb — an accusation Iran dismissed.[27] It was not the first time Erdogan had accused Israel of "banditry and state terrorism."[27] Israeli Foreign Minister [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] shot back on X that "the sultan, in his own eyes, in yet another inflammatory speech, continues to incite against Israel and against the Israeli prime minister."[27]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] recalled branding Erdogan "The Enigma" of Trump-a-Mania, noting that Erdogan had been promoted as a white hat by both President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and General [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]], and had allegedly faced a failed coup and/or assassination attempt in May 2024 as he escalated his rhetoric against Netanyahu.[27] After Erdogan threatened to invade Israel, then-Foreign Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] responded with a not-so-subtle reminder of what happened to [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]] and called for [[turkey|Turkey]]'s expulsion from NATO.[27] The analysis held that such exchanges were never especially dangerous, since Israel would not attack Turkey and trigger Article 5 of the NATO treaty, and few expected Erdogan to alienate his Western partners by striking Israel.[27] That calculus shifted, however, with Israel reportedly seeking a corridor through Syria to Kurdistan as a staging ground for operations against Iran, and with Netanyahu's government vocal about annexing Damascus and southern Syria — moves that would provoke the Arabs and Persians alike, and which only Turkey, already on the ground in northern Syria with a negotiated peace with the al-Jolani regime, might be positioned to stop.[27]

## Turkey's role in US-Russia diplomacy (February 2025)

Badlands commentary on the February 2025 U.S.-Russia talks in Istanbul argued that, after the Riyadh summit established the Saudis and Arabs as peacemakers, the Turkish summit had the potential to weaken [[nato|NATO]] by moving Ankara out of Europe's sphere of influence and into Asia's.[28] Such a shift was said to align with Erdogan's public comments on BRICS, the Shanghai Cooperation Organization, and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s Russia.[28] The commentary credited Erdogan with maintaining a persona as an "incendiary enigma" who antagonizes Israel with hollow threats, and noted that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had publicly given Erdogan his seal of approval as far back as 2019.[28] With the IDF moving on Damascus — territory the Turks also want — and Trump having communicated that Syria was under Turkish control, the analysis held that Israel could not attack Turkish forces without triggering [[article-5|Article 5]], a fact said to be reason enough to keep NATO on life support while negotiations unfolded.[28]

## Advocacy for Syria Sanctions Relief (May 2025)

Erdoğan was one of two foreign leaders — the other being Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] — credited with persuading [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to lift United States sanctions on Syria and normalize relations with its new government, an announcement Trump made at the [[saudi-us-investment-forum|Saudi-US Investment Forum]] in Riyadh on May 13, 2025.[29] Trump said the move toward rapprochement followed discussions with the crown prince and the Turkish president, and the decision was made despite objections from within his own administration and from Israel.[29] See [[syria-sanctions-relief|Syria sanctions relief]].

## Regional diplomacy and African leverage (2024)

The September 2, 2024 Badlands News Brief cited Erdogan as a central figure in what it calls the Sovereign Alliance's reshaping of the Middle East.[30] It noted that when Turkey faced financial collapse the previous year, Saudi Arabia injected billions of dollars to keep the country propped up, and that Erdogan repaid that deposit in August 2024 as reported by the Financial Times.[30]

The brief also pointed to a Financial Times article titled "Turkey's expanding leverage in Africa," which described Erdogan's efforts to settle the escalating conflict between Somalia and Ethiopia, as well as Turkey's growing influence in the Sahel region encompassing Burkina Faso, Mali and Nigeria.[30] It further reported that Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] had called Erdogan to discuss the situation in Palestine, matching the dynamics [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] was describing.[30] Russian diplomacy at the time was aimed at normalizing relations between Damascus and Ankara, long-time adversaries since Turkey's 2012 involvement in Syria alongside [[nato|NATO]] and its continued hold on northern Syrian territory.[30]

## UN General Assembly meetings and prospective jet deals (September 2025)

Erdogan attended the September 2025 meeting at the [[united-nations|United Nations]] at which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] assured Arab and Muslim leaders he would not permit Israeli annexation of the West Bank, describing the session as "fruitful" on Fox News without providing details.[31] Trump and Erdogan were scheduled to meet again at the [[white-house|White House]] the following Thursday.[31] Badlands commentary reported that Erdogan had been among three leaders of Muslim-majority countries whose microphones cut out during General Assembly speeches as they called for worldwide support for Palestinian statehood.[31]

When Trump had posted about inviting Erdogan to the White House, he teased a potential F-35 deal and also mentioned an [[f-16-fighting-falcon|F-16]] deal described as sounding like a certainty.[31] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that [[turkey|Turkey]] was the ideal candidate to stop [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] from annexing southern Syria, which he expected to be used as a land bridge toward a war with Iran.[31]
