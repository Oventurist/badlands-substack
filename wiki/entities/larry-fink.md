# Larry Fink

Larry Fink is the chief executive officer of [[blackrock|BlackRock]], the world's largest asset manager, and a recurring presence at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland.[1]

## Davos 2026 opening remarks

In his opening remarks at the 2026 Davos meeting, Fink warned that rapid growth in artificial intelligence could harm the prospects of working and professional classes, framing AI as a major stress test for capitalism.[1] The warning came at a forum whose agenda was dominated by AI's economic impact and by the infrastructure constraints — electricity supply and grid capacity for large-scale compute — that accompany it.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated Fink's speech as evidence of a deliberate narrative pivot, listing "Larry Fink/BlackRock pivots from Climate Alarmism to AI Doom Mongering" among the confirmations of his long-running thesis about a [[nuclear-renaissance|nuclear renaissance]].[1] On that reading, the establishment that once demonized nuclear power is now demonizing the technology nuclear power will supply, because nuclear-powered AI would accelerate the transition to what Badlands calls the [[global-golden-age|Golden Age]].[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] situated the same shift within a longer pattern of "fear porn," arguing that the 2026 WEF's change of fear target from climate to AI was predictable.[1]

## Tokenization and energy realism at Davos

At the January 2026 Davos meeting Fink told the assembled elite that the current financial system was doomed and that tokenization represented the future of banking and of all asset classes — a message echoed by the chief executive of [[ubs|UBS]].[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted the irony that Fink and the crypto commentator GMONEY were now championing the same thesis.[2]

Fink further argued that the emerging energy demands of artificial intelligence and massive datacenters required the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] to reevaluate its position on renewable energy, saying solar and wind were not reliable sources and therefore insufficient.[2] Badlands writers contrasted this posture with his earlier insistence that he would force behavioral change on the public, treating the visit as an occasion on which he brought his old friends bad news.[2]

## Trajectory with Trump and the Panama ports deal

In March 2025 Fink announced, alongside Terminal Investment Limited, a $23 billion agreement under which a [[blackrock|BlackRock]]-led consortium would take control of 43 ports in 23 countries from [[ck-hutchison|CK Hutchison]], including the Panama Canal ports of Balboa and Cristobal.[3] Fink called the agreement "a powerful illustration of BlackRock and GIP's combined platform and our ability to deliver differentiated investments for clients," saying "these world-class ports facilitate global growth."[3] See [[blackrock-panama-canal-ports-deal|the BlackRock Panama Canal ports deal]].[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] traced Fink's long arc with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]: in 2017 he resigned from Trump's Strategic and Policy Forum after the Charlottesville riots, saying he could not continue "in good conscience."[3] In 2018 he went all in on environmental, social and governance investing, emphasizing ESG in his annual letter to CEOs and urging companies to address climate change; by the end of Trump's first term BlackRock had become a prominent advocate of sustainable investing.[3] He doubled down on ESG during the Biden administration in 2021, softened his stance by 2022, and in 2024 scaled back as Trump's return looked likely, withdrawing BlackRock from the Net Zero Asset Managers alliance and shifting away from aggressive climate commitments.[3] Since Trump assumed office BlackRock has further distanced itself from DEI, including removing DEI-related language from its annual review process — changes the commentary noted are "not the stuff of whims" but require alterations to people, process and technology.[3] The unresolved question posed was whether BlackRock had bent the knee or was infiltrating, with the observation that Marxism never gets defeated but evolves, and that the Prussian playbook is infiltration instead of invasion.[3]

## Panama Canal ports deal (2025)

Fink, as chief executive of [[blackrock|BlackRock]] and described as a longtime confidant of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], spearheaded the roughly $23 billion consortium purchase of 43 port facilities from [[ck-hutchison|CK Hutchison]], including ports at both ends of the [[panama-canal|Panama Canal]] and near the Suez Canal.[4] The agreement was to be signed by April 2, 2025, but China's State Administration for Market Regulation opened an anti-monopoly investigation in late March that effectively stalled the deal, with [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] reportedly angry that Beijing had not been consulted.[4]
