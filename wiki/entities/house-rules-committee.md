# House Rules Committee

The House Rules Committee is the standing committee of the U.S. House of Representatives that governs the terms under which legislation reaches the floor, giving its members substantial leverage over what may be bundled into or stripped from a bill.[1]

## Role in the 2023 Ukraine aid fight

In February 2023, Rep. [[ralph-norman|Ralph Norman]] told [[breitbart-news|Breitbart News]] in an exclusive interview that House Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] had set the "gears in place" for conservatives on the House Rules Committee to strip [[ukraine-aid-funding-controversy|Ukraine aid]] from must-pass spending bills.[1] Norman spoke as conservatives railed against the inclusion of Ukraine aid in must-pass vehicles such as the $1.7 trillion omnibus spending bills and stop-gap measures known as a [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]].[1]

Asked whether House conservatives on the committee would move to strip Ukraine aid from must-pass bills, Norman replied, "We're going to make sure it happens."[1] He elaborated that "If it's spending American dollars, then each representative is going to have to take a vote on it," adding that conservatives were "simply not going to let them bundle it up on something completely off the radar with having anything to do with where the money's going."[1]

The seats held by conservative members on the Rules Committee had been secured as part of the concessions extracted during McCarthy's contested election as Speaker, and their use in blocking bundled foreign-aid provisions was read by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] as a test of whether McCarthy possessed the political will to confront the Regime's spending priorities.[1]

## Approval of the One Big Beautiful Bill (May 2025)

On the night of Wednesday, May 21, 2025, the committee narrowly approved [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]]'s sprawling tax and immigration package — the [[one-big-beautiful-bill-act|One Big Beautiful Bill]] — clearing the path for the full House to take up the measure as soon as the following morning.[2] The bill would extend trillions of dollars in Tax Cuts and Jobs Act provisions, add campaign promises such as no taxes on tips or overtime, and fund mass deportation and defense investments, while hard-line budget hawks objected to its effect on a national debt exceeding $36 trillion.[2]
