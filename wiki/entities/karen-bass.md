# Karen Bass

Karen Bass is the incumbent Mayor of Los Angeles. In the city's 2026 nonpartisan primary, covered in the June 9, 2026 *Badlands Brief*, Bass advanced to the November runoff and will face Los Angeles City Council member [[nithya-raman|Nithya Raman]].[1]

Before the primary, public polling had shown Bass, Raman, and former reality television personality [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]] competing in a closely contested race.[1] The contest drew national attention largely because of Pratt's celebrity profile and his criticism of city leadership following the Palisades Fire.[1] Voters chose from among 14 candidates in the primary.[1]

Badlands contributors treated the outcome — Pratt finishing outside the top two — as suspicious rather than routine, given that Los Angeles is, per [[canncon|CannCon]]'s research, the only US jurisdiction still using [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]] equipment.[1]

## Pratt's guerrilla campaign against Bass

In the months before the primary, Pratt targeted Bass with a guerrilla advertising tactic, power-washing slogans such as "IMAGINE IF THE STREETS WERE THIS CLEAN" into filthy Los Angeles sidewalks — an argument that worked precisely because the city's streets were dirty under her administration.[2] His campaign ran on homelessness, street disorder, anger over fire response, and anti-bureaucracy messaging, and at that stage polling and fundraising momentum placed him in serious contention for a runoff against Bass, with a *New York Times*-cited poll showing him within three points of her.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] contrasted Bass's presumed reliance on traditional, expensive campaign media with Pratt's viral low-budget stunts.[2]

## Skid Row voter fraud allegations

In June 2026, videos surfaced in which Skid Row residents claimed they had been paid cash to sign registration forms, forge signatures, and complete voter information, and said they had been encouraged to support incumbent Mayor Bass and Councilmember [[nithya-raman|Nithya Raman]] ahead of the mayoral race and primary elections.[3] The allegations prompted a federal operation in which roughly twenty agents, including personnel from [[homeland-security-investigations|Homeland Security Investigations]], canvassed the district; the matter is documented at [[skid-row-voter-fraud-investigation|Skid Row voter fraud investigation]].[3] Bass was not reported to have been accused of wrongdoing, but her campaign was named as a beneficiary in the residents' accounts.[3]

## Cuba-linked commentary (July 2026)

Commenting on a previewed [[state-department|State Department]] report alleging decades of [[cuba|Cuban]] support for radical left-wing activism in the United States, Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Bass was once a Cuban-trained Marxist radical and is now Mayor of Los Angeles, on her way to be Governor, and at the center of the biggest alleged fraud scandal of the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterms]].[4]

## Potential 2026 runoff

As late mail ballots were counted in the [[2026-los-angeles-mayoral-election|2026 Los Angeles mayoral race]], City Councilmember [[nithya-raman|Nithya Raman]] overtook challenger [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]] and was positioned to potentially advance to a runoff against Bass, the incumbent mayor.[5] The multi-day reversal drew national attention as an example of the [[red-mirage|Red Mirage]] pattern in California's all-mail voting system.[5]


## LADWP Hiring and the Palisades Fire

On Mayor Karen Bass's orders, the city of Los Angeles maxed out its budget to "attract private-sector talent," hiring [[janisse-quinones|Janisse Quiñones]] as chief executive of the [[los-angeles-department-of-water-and-power|Los Angeles Department of Water and Power]] in May 2024 on a $750,000 salary, almost double that of her predecessor.[6] After the January 2025 [[palisades-fire|Palisades Fire]], fire department insiders blamed Quiñones for leaving a reservoir disconnected and hydrants broken for months, failures that left firefighters without water.[6]

## June 2025 downtown curfew

During the [[2025-los-angeles-immigration-enforcement-protests|June 2025 immigration enforcement protests]], Bass imposed a nighttime curfew over pockets of downtown Los Angeles where demonstrations were concentrated, running from 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. Pacific Time.[7] She said the order could remain in effect for several days; authorities said it covered about one square mile and would affect fewer than 100,000 of the city's 4 million residents.[7] The [[lapd|Los Angeles Police Department]] began "mass arrests" of demonstrators who failed to disperse, reporting at least 378 arrests over four days.[7] The [[white-house|White House]] argued that Bass and Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] should be thanking President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for removing criminal illegal immigrants from their streets rather than opposing the federal operation.[7]

## Cash cards for families affected by ICE raids

On Friday, July 11, 2025, Bass announced that Los Angeles would give cash to families affected by [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] raids, as part of an effort she described as protecting "immigrant" communities.[8] She unveiled the program during a press conference about a new executive order directing all city departments to refuse to cooperate with the federal government on immigration enforcement.[8] Bass said the assistance would go to families whose members could not go to work for fear of being arrested and deported, or who had otherwise lost income, and cited the model used by her predecessor [[eric-garcetti|Eric Garcetti]], who distributed "Angeleno cards" loaded with cash during the coronavirus pandemic while the city imposed shutdowns on workplaces.[8]

The announcement coincided with [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] operations at two licensed cannabis cultivation sites in Carpinteria and Camarillo, in which about 200 suspected illegal aliens were detained and 10 migrant children were said to have been rescued from potential exploitation, forced labor, and human trafficking.[8] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] asked why pro-immigrant officials were not considered to be aiding and abetting an invasion, and framed Bass's executive order — alongside Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]]'s posture for California — as a claw-back of municipal and state power from the federal government that, if accepted, would leave ordinary Americans believing the Constitution was being overthrown and the country fragmenting.[8]

## Absence during the January 2025 wildfires

When wind-driven fires broke out across the Los Angeles area on Tuesday, January 7, 2025, Bass was on the other side of the globe as part of a delegation sent by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to Ghana for the inauguration of its new president.[9] A former Democratic congresswoman who became mayor in late 2022, she did not return to Los Angeles until Wednesday afternoon, by which point more than 1,000 homes had burned and 100,000 people across the region had been forced to flee.[9] Her absence drew criticism from Angelenos who said officials had given insufficient warning despite forecasts of extreme fire danger; Pacific Palisades resident Michael Gonzales, a 47-year-old lawyer whose home burned down, said "there was zero preparation, there was zero thought here" and argued that Bass made a poor decision to remain overseas.[9] The episode is treated at length at [[january-2025-los-angeles-wildfires|the January 2025 Los Angeles wildfires]] page.[9]

## MacArthur Park confrontation (July 2025)

On Monday, July 7, 2025, dozens of federal immigration agents in tactical gear, accompanied by members of the California National Guard, deployed to a mostly empty MacArthur Park in Los Angeles, in an operation Bass called "outrageous" and "an attempt to spread fear."[10] Bass said she went to the park both to witness what was happening and "to do what I could to stop it," asked a Customs and Border Protection official who was in charge, was put on the phone with that individual, and told him the operation needed to end; she said he replied that the agents would be leaving within minutes and that they left a few minutes later.[10] It was unclear whether anyone was taken into custody, and an ICE spokesperson said the agency does not comment on ongoing operations.[10] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that Bass was asserting a right to violate federal law to preserve California's illegal alien policy, warning that losing that population would diminish the state's outsized political power, and posed the question of whether city officials should be allowed to keep federal agents out of their jurisdictions.[10] The episode is treated in detail at [[macarthur-park-immigration-operation|the MacArthur Park immigration operation]].[10]

## DOJ sanctuary city lawsuit (June 2025)

On Monday, June 30, 2025, the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] sued the City of Los Angeles and Bass over the city's [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary city]] policy, adopted in the weeks following [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2024 election victory, which bars city resources from being used for immigration enforcement.[11] The DOJ complaint argued that the city's laws "interfere with and discriminate against the Federal Government's enforcement of federal immigration law."[11]

The complaint further alleged that "the practical upshot of Los Angeles' refusal to cooperate with federal immigration authorities has, since June 6, 2025, been lawlessness, rioting, looting, and vandalism," a situation it described as so dire that the federal government deployed the California National Guard and United States Marines to quell the chaos.[11] When announcing the ordinance's passage in November 2024, the [[los-angeles-city-council|Los Angeles City Council]] said it "permanently enshrines sanctuary policies into municipal law" and prohibits the use of city property and personnel for immigration enforcement or cooperation with federal immigration agents.[11]

Badlands contributor Ashe in America connected the suit to [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]]'s December 2024 warning that he was coming for sanctuary cities, writing that the DOJ was now proving it had his back and that Los Angeles "continues to prove the insanity of sanctuary cities, and the harm they facilitate, nurture, and protect."[11]
