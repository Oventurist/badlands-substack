# Silvano Arieti

**Silvano Arieti** was a psychiatrist described by Badlands Media as one of the twentieth century's foremost authorities on schizophrenia.[1] His account of the psychogenic steps that lead an individual into madness supplies the analytical spine of [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]]'s January 20, 2023 essay on the triggering of [[mass-psychosis]].[1]

## Stage one: panic

Arieti's first step is the stage of panic, the point at which an individual begins to perceive things differently, becomes frightened on account of that change in perception, and lacks the knowledge necessary to understand or explain what is happening.[1] Deschamps treated the last element as critical: without understanding, the psyche recoils and triggers a flight or freeze response rather than the fight, or bravery, response.[1] That gap creates the opening for psychological manipulation, because the panicked mind is searching for an easy explanation offering a way out, and there is no shortage of manipulative forces willing to supply one — at which point the panicked person becomes willing to deny objective reality and even to commit atrocities while feeling justified.[1]

## Stage two: psychotic insight

The next step Arieti identifies is a phase of "psychotic insight," in which the individual succeeds in putting things together through a pathological way of interpreting reality that explains away their experience regardless of the explanation's validity.[1] Deschamps illustrated the stage with the Nazi dehumanization of the Jews, blaming them for all of Germany's woes while adding a chosen-people, superior-race doctrine, which opened the door to self-justified inhumanity.[1] The insight is psychotic because it rests on delusions rather than adaptive, life-promoting responses to whatever threat originally precipitated the panic; the delusion that harmful behavior solves the triggering problem relieves the flood of negative emotion at the cost of contact with reality.[1]

## Application to contemporary events

Deschamps mapped Arieti's stages onto recent history, arguing that the collective rage felt toward "the system" and police by believers in the [[black-lives-matter|BLM]] account of the [[george-floyd|George Floyd]] events was "relieved" by protests and rioting, a response promoted by the media and social media influencers.[1] He applied the same schema to the early pandemic, contending that the masses were shepherded into a state of fear disproportionate to the danger, that most lacked scientific knowledge about viral transmission beyond what was promoted in media, and that knowledgeable detractors were silenced, humiliated, and in many cases stripped of medical licenses or scientific grants.[1]
