# Kyrie Irving

Kyrie Irving is an American professional basketball player whose 2022–23 season became a recurring flashpoint in debates over speech, corporate discipline, and league control of athletes. Irving shared a documentary widely characterized as antisemitic on his social media platforms during the season and initially refused to apologize for doing so, holding to that position through several media sessions.[1]

## Suspension and apology

After Irving declined to back down publicly, he was suspended by the [[brooklyn-nets|Brooklyn Nets]], at which point he relented and posted an apology on Instagram. He served the suspension, returned to the Nets, and the controversy gradually receded from the news cycle.[1]

## Trade to Dallas and deletion of the apology

Irving returned to the headlines when he requested a trade from Brooklyn following a breakdown in contract extension talks, and was ultimately dealt to the [[dallas-mavericks|Dallas Mavericks]]. In the course of covering the trade, The Athletic's Sam Amick noticed that Irving's Instagram apology post was no longer visible on his account; the exact date of deletion was not clear.[1]

Asked about the deletion at his introductory press availability with the Mavericks on Tuesday, February 7, 2023, Irving replied that he deletes "a lot of things" on Instagram, said that the earlier episode had produced "confusion and uncertainty" about what he meant and what he stands for, and stated that he had been made to "sit up with these mics and explain to the world who I am." He added that the deletion was "no disrespect to anyone within the community," and that he was "just living my life."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[absolutetruth1776]] argued that the timing — the deletion coming just after Irving was moved from Brooklyn to Dallas — made it plausible that the apology had been coerced in the first place as a condition of returning to the court, and framed the episode as evidence of how the [[national-basketball-association|NBA]] seeks to control players and constrain their speech.[1]

## Vaccine refusal

Irving also stood against the COVID-19 vaccine and, as a result, was not allowed to play home games in Brooklyn.[2] He never relented and remained unvaccinated despite enduring public ridicule from the sports media establishment.[2] Badlands cited him alongside [[aaron-rodgers|Aaron Rodgers]], [[novak-djokovic|Novak Djokovic]] and [[ivan-provorov|Ivan Provorov]] as examples of athletes willing to take a stand against prevailing agendas in professional sport.[2]
