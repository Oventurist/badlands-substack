# Todd Blanche

Todd Blanche served as Acting Attorney General in the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration and was the official who announced the federal reclassification of state-licensed medical marijuana in April 2026.[1]

## Marijuana rescheduling

Blanche stated that moving medical marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III allows research into the safety and efficacy of the substance, ultimately providing patients with better care and doctors with more reliable information.[1] He credited President Trump's "decisive leadership" and referenced a December 2025 executive order that removed federal hurdles to medical marijuana and CBD research while directing an expedited rescheduling process.[1] Blanche also announced plans for a new expedited hearing with set deadlines to fully reschedule marijuana in the near future.[1]

The action, taken through the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], did not legalize marijuana federally but eased restrictions on a substance that had sat in the most restrictive category since the 1937 Marijuana Tax Act.[1] It legitimized medical marijuana programs operating in 40 states, allowed tax deductions for state-licensed companies, and set up a system for state-licensed producers to register with the [[drug-enforcement-administration|Drug Enforcement Administration]].[1]

## Beef packer antitrust probe

In May 2026, Blanche announced an intensified [[beef-packer-antitrust-investigation-2026|antitrust investigation]] into the Big Four beef packers — [[jbs|JBS]], [[cargill|Cargill]], [[tyson-foods|Tyson Foods]], and [[national-beef|National Beef]] — revealing reviews of more than three million documents and hundreds of rancher interviews.[2] He said the Department had executed on the President's executive order to stop anti-competitive behavior in the broader food supply market and would announce a historic settlement later that week directly affecting the prices of proteins such as chicken, pork and turkey.[2] The probe was conducted jointly with the [[department-of-agriculture|Department of Agriculture]] under Secretary [[brooke-rollins|Brooke Rollins]] and followed [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s November 2025 directive.[2]


## Authorization of the Singham grand jury probe
As Acting US Attorney General, Blanche authorized the federal grand jury investigation in Manhattan into [[neville-roy-singham|Neville Roy Singham]]'s funding network, an inquiry initiated by US Attorney [[jay-clayton|Jay Clayton]] for the Southern District of New York.[3] The probe examines possible wire fraud, bank fraud, money laundering, and related violations tied to the funding of socialist, communist, and Marxist organizations in the United States, with subpoenas issued for bank records and financial documents.[3]

## Epstein files release (2026)

As Deputy Attorney General, Blanche announced that the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] had fulfilled its obligations under the [[epstein-files-transparency-act|Epstein Files Transparency Act]] upon releasing over 3 million pages of previously undisclosed [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] investigative files, including submitting a report to Congress and publishing redaction justifications.[4] The release came nearly six weeks after the Act's December 19, 2025 deadline and was criticized by victims' advocates and by Democrats including Rep. [[jamie-raskin|Jamie Raskin]] as a potential cover-up.[4]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] alluded to Blanche's polarizing standing in the movement, noting that confirmation of a parallel ODNI election inquiry left "Todd Blanche haters most impacted."[4]

## ISIS terrorism prosecution (March 2026)

In the March 2026 announcement of terrorism charges against two [[isis|ISIS]] supporters, Blanche said the defendants "allegedly sought to inflict mass casualties in service to ISIS with the hope of exceeding the carnage of the Boston Marathon bombing."[5] He credited law enforcement officers who "ran into harm's way to apprehend these individuals and disarm the explosives before anyone was harmed," and said the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] would prosecute men who pledged allegiance to a foreign terrorist organization to the fullest extent.[5]

## Revived Comey leaks probe

As acting attorney general, Blanche was credited with restoring momentum to a stalled investigation into former FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]]'s alleged transmission of classified documents to Columbia Law professor [[daniel-richman|Daniel Richman]].[6] Reporting cited by Badlands described ongoing meetings between a small group of Eastern District of Virginia prosecutors and officials in the deputy attorney general's office since Blanche's elevation, with officials saying the probe had regained traction under his leadership.[6]

## Denial of Trump involvement in Comey prosecution

In April 2026, as acting attorney general, Blanche stated that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had no role in directing or initiating any prosecution involving [[james-comey|James Comey]].[7] He emphasized that any legal action tied to Comey was being handled independently through the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], rejecting claims that the case was politically driven by the White House.[7] The statement followed reporting that Comey had been indicted, which prompted scrutiny over whether the prosecution was connected to Trump's longstanding public criticism of the former FBI director.[7] Blanche reiterated that prosecutorial decisions are based on evidence and standard DOJ procedures rather than presidential direction.[7]


## WHCA dinner prosecution recusal request (2026)

Blanche, serving as Acting Attorney General, attended the [[white-house-correspondents-association-dinner|White House Correspondents' Association dinner]] at which a man allegedly attempted to attack President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[8] In May 2026 the defendant's lawyers moved to remove Blanche and US Attorney [[jeanine-pirro|Jeanine Pirro]] from the prosecution, arguing that as attendees they could be considered victims or witnesses and therefore had a possible conflict of interest; US District Judge [[trevor-mcfadden|Trevor McFadden]] deferred ruling and asked for clarification of the request's scope.[8] The case is described at [[whca-dinner-assassination-attempt-prosecution|the WHCA dinner assassination attempt prosecution]].[8]

## Ethics complaint over the Abrego Garcia case

In late May 2026 a public interest group filed an ethics complaint against Blanche in his capacity as Acting Attorney General, after a federal judge dismissed the Tennessee criminal case against [[kilmar-abrego-garcia|Kilmar Abrego Garcia]], according to a May 31 commentary by [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]].[9] U.S. District Judge [[waverly-crenshaw|Waverly Crenshaw]] had dismissed the human-smuggling prosecution on May 22, 2026 after granting a motion alleging vindictive prosecution, with [[abc-news|ABC News]] reporting that the court found the government had not rebutted a presumption of vindictiveness.[9] The complaint became the occasion for [[ed-martin|Ed Martin]]'s argument that the bar associations are destroying their own authority.[9]

## SPLC indictment statement (April 2026)

Announcing the [[southern-poverty-law-center|Southern Poverty Law Center]]'s 11-count federal indictment in Montgomery, Alabama, Blanche said the organization "was not dismantling these groups. It was instead manufacturing the extremism it purports to oppose by paying sources to stoke racial hatred."[10] The charges — wire fraud, bank fraud, and conspiracy to commit money laundering — allege the SPLC funneled more than $3 million between 2014 and 2023 to paid informants and leaders inside the Ku Klux Klan and other extremist groups.[10]

## 2020 election investigation remarks (May 2026)

In May 2026 Blanche, as Acting Attorney General, said publicly that "there's a ton of evidence that the election was rigged," referring to 2020, and confirmed that the Department had multiple investigations underway in Arizona and in Fulton County, Georgia.[11] He cautioned that the work was difficult because the subjects are "very good at hiding misconduct," and described the Department's focus as determining "whether the right people voted, whether people who were supposed to vote voted, whether there was one vote cast per voter" across multiple states.[11] Asked why the process was taking so long, Blanche attributed the pace to "good old-fashioned law enforcement, police work" and said the Department had "great prosecutors working on it as well."[11] He assured the American people that as soon as there was something to say — "whether it's charges, whether it's a report, whether it's the results of an investigation" — they would learn what had been uncovered.[11]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] welcomed confirmation of the Arizona and Georgia probes but noted that Blanche's framing appeared to emphasize voter fraud rather than systemic election fraud, while arguing that in those two states the individual and systemic dimensions are inextricably connected; she added that a report without charges "would be quite the rugpull."[11] The remarks were previewed for discussion on [[why-we-vote|Why We Vote]] with [[canncon|CannCon]].[11]

## National Fraud Enforcement Division launch (April 2026)

On April 7, 2026, Blanche, as Acting Attorney General, announced the launch of the [[national-fraud-enforcement-division|National Fraud Enforcement Division]], which would add 93 dedicated prosecutors across every federal judicial district working exclusively on fraud cases.[12] He said the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] was already handling more than 8,000 ongoing fraud matters, describing them as only a portion of a much broader scope of abuse, and framed the effort as a "supercharging" of enforcement in coordination with the [[task-force-to-eliminate-fraud|Task Force to Eliminate Fraud]] led by Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]].[12] Blanche warned that "fraudsters, scammers, tax cheats, or anyone who lies to get rich off the generosity of the American people should be on notice."[12]

## WSJ subpoenas over Iran war leaks (2026)

As Acting Attorney General, Blanche said the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] is prioritizing the prosecution of leakers who share classified information with reporters, warning that journalists should not be surprised to receive subpoenas.[13] The remarks accompanied grand jury subpoenas issued by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] on March 4 to the [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]], seeking reporters' records related to leaks about the U.S.-Israeli military campaign against [[iran|Iran]].[13] Blanche emphasized that such leaks endanger national security and soldiers' lives, while Dow Jones condemned the subpoenas as an attack on constitutionally protected newsgathering.[13]

## Appointment of a national trafficking coordinator (June 2026)

As Acting Attorney General, Blanche appointed longtime federal prosecutor [[alessandra-serano|Alessandra Serano]] as the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s national coordinator for human trafficking and child exploitation cases in June 2026.[14] The role consolidates oversight of cases handled across all 93 US Attorney's Offices and specialized units within the DOJ Criminal Division, elevates the position within the deputy attorney general's office, and carries a 120-day deadline for helping deliver an updated departmental strategy on combating trafficking and exploitation.[14] The appointment followed a DOJ announcement of an investigation into the status of roughly 300,000 unaccompanied immigrant children, including concerns that some may have been exploited through labor or sex trafficking via so-called "super sponsors."[14]

## April 2026: correspondents' dinner shooting and the SPLC indictment

Following the April 25, 2026 shooting at the White House Correspondents' Dinner, Acting Attorney General Blanche said the suspect appeared to be targeting Trump administration officials, potentially including President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] himself.[15] Days earlier, on April 21, Blanche announced an 11-count federal indictment against the [[southern-poverty-law-center|Southern Poverty Law Center]] alongside FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]], accusing the Alabama-based nonprofit of wire fraud, bank fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering.[15]

## May 2026: election fraud enforcement and the Comey case

In early May 2026, Blanche told Fox News that the Justice Department had "been very aggressively going after anybody in this country who is in any way voting illegally," describing the effort as a priority of the department amid a wave of federal and state election cases.[16] Badlands writers set his remarks against their argument that individual voter-fraud prosecutions do not substitute for the [[election-fraud-rico|election fraud RICO]].[16]

The same weekend, Blanche appeared with [[kristen-welker|Kristen Welker]] on NBC's Meet the Press and said the [[comey-8647-indictment|"8647" indictment]] of [[james-comey|James Comey]] rests on more than the Instagram post that prompted it, telling viewers that "it's not just the Instagram post that leads somebody to get indicted" and that intent is proven with witnesses, documents and materials.[16] He promised a public trial at which the government's evidence would be disclosed, while declining to provide further detail.[16] Blanche also chairs the Task Force to Eradicate Anti-Christian Bias, which published a report that week with the Weaponization Working Group on the Biden administration's treatment of Christians.[16]

## Morens and Comey indictments (April 2026)

On April 28, 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] under Blanche as Acting Attorney General announced two high-profile indictments of figures tied to prior [[donald-trump|Trump]]-era controversies.[17] In the first, former Fauci adviser [[david-morens|David Morens]] was charged with conspiracy against the United States, destruction and falsification of federal records, concealment of documents, and aiding and abetting; Blanche described the alleged conduct as a "profound abuse of trust" at the height of the global health crisis.[17] In the second, former FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] was indicted a second time, in the Eastern District of North Carolina, on charges of threatening the president over the [[comey-8647-indictment|"86 47"]] Instagram post.[17] Badlands framed both actions as reflecting intensified efforts under Blanche's leadership to pursue accountability tied to the COVID-19 origins investigation and alleged threats against the president.[17]

## Recipient of the Jack Smith referral (July 2026)

On July 22, 2026, in his capacity as Acting Attorney General, Blanche received a letter from the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] referring former Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] for criminal prosecution.[18] The committee said it had strong evidence that Smith knowingly made false statements during his December 17, 2025 sworn deposition, and that his testimony directly contradicted new DOJ documents obtained by lawmakers.[18] The referral, arising from the [[arctic-frost|Arctic Frost]] probe, highlighted potential violations of 18 U.S.C. § 1001 and of the Speech or Debate Clause.[18]

## Elevation to Acting Attorney General (April 2026)

On 3 April 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced on Truth Social that Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] would depart for an unspecified private-sector role and that Blanche, then Deputy Attorney General, would "step in to serve as Acting Attorney General."[19] Trump described him in the statement as "a very talented and respected Legal Mind."[19] Badlands commentary framed the move as another instance of Trump quietly removing cabinet officials one by one while supporters recast the departures as promotions, and treated Blanche's elevation as a caretaker arrangement rather than evidence that the officials involved were read in on the president's underlying plan.[19]

## Sought Testimony Before House Oversight (June 2026)

In June 2026 [[united-states-house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] said he was working to bring Blanche, in his capacity as Acting Attorney General, before the committee in July as part of its [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]] investigation, with lawmakers focused on whether any Epstein-related documents remain unreleased.[20] Democrats on the committee pressed for Blanche's appearance to take the form of a videotaped deposition under oath.[20]

## Maxwell prison interview (July 2025)

As Deputy Attorney General, Blanche personally conducted a July 2025 interview with [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]] at the low-security federal prison in Florida where she is serving a 20-year sentence for child sex trafficking and related offenses.[21] The meeting, teased publicly two days in advance, came as the Trump administration faced backlash for shutting down investigations into [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] despite lingering questions about a supposed client list.[21] An attorney for Maxwell said afterward that she had answered every question the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] asked without invoking the Fifth Amendment.[21]

## Defense rests in the New York hush money trial (2024)

On 21 May 2024, Blanche told Judge [[juan-merchan|Juan M. Merchan]], 'Your honor, the defense rests,' concluding the defense case in [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s New York hush money trial without Trump testifying.[22] The defense closed after testimony from a former federal prosecutor who attacked the credibility of the prosecution's principal witness.[22] Blanche also framed the core legal objection to the prosecution's theory, asking, 'How can withholding a false story from the voters be considered criminal?'— a formulation Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited in arguing that no underlying crime existed in the case.[22]

## Push to move Tina Peters into federal custody

In November 2025 the Office of the Deputy Attorney General, led by Blanche, asked the [[federal-bureau-of-prisons|Bureau of Prisons]] to explore transferring former Mesa County Clerk [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] out of Colorado state prison and into federal custody, following President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s calls for her release.[23] An email sent on the evening of Wednesday, November 5, 2025 to BOP Director William Marshall asked the bureau to "explore any and all avenues within BOP's authority" to seek Peters's transfer from the [[colorado-department-of-corrections|Colorado Department of Corrections]], to send a request to Colorado as soon as possible, and to begin preparations for a possible transfer.[23]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that Blanche was not advancing a specific legal argument but surveying available avenues, and notably was not describing Peters as a federal election whistleblower — an argument she considered legally weak — while suggesting the [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of government]] theory offered the strongest path.[23]

## Acting Librarian of Congress (2025)

On Monday, May 12, 2025, the Justice Department announced that Blanche — then Deputy Attorney General, and the lawyer who had represented [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during his 2024 criminal trial — had been appointed acting Librarian of Congress.[24] He replaced longtime librarian [[carla-hayden|Carla Hayden]], whom the White House had fired the previous week amid conservative criticism that she was advancing a "woke" agenda.[24]

Senate Majority Leader [[john-thune|John Thune]] told reporters that lawmakers were investigating whether Trump has the power to fire the Librarian of Congress, who is technically a legislative branch employee, saying, "We want to make sure congressional equities are respected and protected in this process."[24] Reporting noted the appointment could be far-reaching: a congressional aide speaking anonymously said the librarian could see requests made by lawmakers to the [[congressional-research-service|Congressional Research Service]], which are ordinarily visible only to the requesting office and the CRS itself.[24]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] remarked that she had never cared about the Librarian of Congress until Senate Republicans became concerned about Trump appointing a loyalist, and wondered whether Blanche might find "Cíbola – or maybe something even cooler, like prosecutable evidence of government corruption."[24]

## Cross-examination of Michael Cohen, May 2024

On May 16, 2024, in [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Manhattan "hush money" trial, Blanche conducted a cross-examination of prosecution star witness [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] that observers described as having Cohen's "knees chopped out."[25] Blanche grilled Cohen about a pivotal October 2016 phone call said to connect Trump to the approval of reimbursements for the payment to [[stormy-daniels|Stormy Daniels]], and confronted him with text messages showing that the call had in fact been with Trump's bodyguard [[keith-schiller|Keith Schiller]].[25] He also exposed contradictions in Cohen's congressional testimony about wanting no role in the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] and in his denial of having sought a pardon.[25] [[cnn|CNN]] host [[anderson-cooper|Anderson Cooper]] called it "an extraordinary cross," saying Blanche had built a box around the witness and slammed it shut.[25]
