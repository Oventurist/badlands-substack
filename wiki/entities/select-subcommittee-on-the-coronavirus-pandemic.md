# Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic

The Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic — often referred to as the "Covid Select" committee — is a House investigative panel established to examine the origins of COVID-19 and the federal pandemic response.[1] It is chaired by Representative [[brad-wenstrup|Brad Wenstrup]] (R-OH).[1]

## EcoHealth Alliance recommendation

In May 2024 the subcommittee's recommendation that the federal government debar [[ecohealth-alliance|EcoHealth Alliance Inc.]] was accepted by the U.S. [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]], which agreed to initiate official debarment proceedings and impose a government-wide suspension of taxpayer funds to the organization, including a halt on all active grants.[1] Wenstrup released a statement confirming HHS's acceptance of the recommendation.[1]

## February 2024 vaccine safety systems hearing

On February 15, 2024, the subcommittee held a hearing titled "Assessing America's Vaccine Safety Systems—Part 1," at which U.S. health officials side-stepped a question on whether the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] was actively conducting extended safety surveillance of those who received early [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]].[2] Rep. [[nicole-malliotakis|Nicole Malliotakis]] pressed [[peter-marks|Peter Marks]], director of the FDA's Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research, on whether specific health markers were being tracked for trends requiring further inquiry; Marks described surveillance running back to 2020 and deferred to [[daniel-jernigan|Daniel Jernigan]] of the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]], who responded only on myocarditis.[2] [[liz-willner|Liz Willner]] of [[openvaers|OpenVAERS]] subsequently told [[epoch-times|The Epoch Times]] that "there is no system in place for long-term vaccine safety surveillance in this country."[2] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the under-oath testimony was especially significant in light of a Canadian study, then in peer review, claiming large excess mortality correlated with vaccine timing, and called for legislators to be pressed for accountability.[2]
