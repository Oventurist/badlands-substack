# David Sacks

David Sacks is a technology investor who served as the White House AI and Crypto Czar under [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]]. He is a central figure in the Trump-aligned technology community's argument that leading frontier artificial intelligence laboratories are pursuing regulation for competitive rather than safety reasons.[1]

## Position on AI regulation

In late July 2026, Sacks — identified in Badlands coverage as the former White House AI and Crypto Czar (rendered there as "David Sachs") — publicly stated that he believed [[anthropic|Anthropic]] and [[openai|OpenAI]] were seeking government regulation primarily in order to stave off competitors.[1] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that Sacks' framing tracked "almost beat for beat" the argument he had advanced days earlier on the Devolution Power Hour: that [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]] and [[dario-amodei|Dario Amodei]] were acting as retardants rather than accelerants to the AI agenda by inviting state limits on a rapidly accelerating industry.[1]

## Significance

Sacks' remarks were treated in the Badlands account as one of three converging weekend indicators that the administration had aligned itself with the pro-acceleration, pro-[[open-source-artificial-intelligence|open-source]] side of the dispute, the others being Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]]'s reported statement that the administration aims to "protect open source AI," and China's continued strategically timed releases of open-weight models.[1]

## Democratic scrutiny and press investigation (December 2025)

Through 2025 the crypto industry underwent significant regulatory change under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s policies, backed by a coalition of senators favoring the adoption and growth of digital assets, but tensions escalated when a group of Democratic senators began challenging those policies as reflecting a significant conflict of interest — focusing in particular on Sacks in his role as the White House AI and Crypto Czar.[2] In a statement on X, Sacks said that five months earlier several reporters from The New York Times had been assigned to investigate supposed conflicts of interest tied to his position.[2] He described an investigation that persisted through numerous "fact checks" scrutinizing various accusations against him, and said that despite his thorough rebuttals the published article included only fragments of his responses while the accusations themselves remained largely speculative.[2] Badlands contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] framed the episode as part of a broader "war on crypto," asking why Trump kept surrounding himself with Bitcoin maximalists and suggesting "there is some sort of plan or something."[2]
