# Vic Reynolds

Vic Reynolds is a Georgia law-enforcement official and jurist who served as Director of the [[georgia-bureau-of-investigation|Georgia Bureau of Investigation]] (GBI) before being appointed a Superior Court judge in Cobb County. He became a central figure in disputes over whether Georgia authorities meaningfully investigated allegations of [[ballot-harvesting|ballot harvesting]] following the 2020 presidential election.[1]

## Role in the 2020 election investigation dispute

In May 2021, Reynolds was presented with material developed by [[true-the-vote|True the Vote]] alleging organized ballot harvesting in Georgia. According to former U.S. Senator [[david-perdue|David Perdue]], the package included "video evidence and cell phone evidence, along with testimony and bank records that are corroborated," and Reynolds "looked at that and said it was compelling [enough] to be investigated."[1]

Perdue testified that in November 2021 he received a phone call from Reynolds in which the GBI director said, "We're not going to investigate. The governor wants me to tell you why we're not going to investigate." Perdue said Reynolds explained that he was "a team player" and that "if the governor doesn't want to investigate, we're not going to investigate."[1]

In a follow-up letter, Reynolds wrote that the data provided identified "279 cellphones which had made multiple trips to within 100 feet of a voter drop box," but argued that nothing tied those phones to ballot harvesting: there were "no statements of witnesses and no names of any potential defendants to interview," and a purported source who could validate ballot harvesting had not been produced to the GBI or the FBI despite "repeated requests." He concluded that the data, "while curious, does not rise to the level of probable cause that a crime has been committed," and that the GBI could not obtain a search warrant for equivalent cell-site location information on that showing.[1]

Reynolds also told Perdue that without the whistleblower witness the matter could not move forward; Perdue asked whether Reynolds could secure whistleblower protections for the individual.[1]

## Disputed account

[[catherine-engelbrecht|Catherine Engelbrecht]], founder of True the Vote, publicly disputed Reynolds's characterization in a statement to Badlands Media, saying the organization met with Reynolds and his team only once, in the Governor's office in early 2021, and that subsequent attempts to re-engage the GBI drew no response. She said the first time Reynolds resurfaced was when a letter addressed to True the Vote appeared in the Atlanta Journal-Constitution, which the group had not received at the time of publication.[1]

## Judicial appointment

Reynolds was appointed a Superior Court judge of Cobb County by Governor [[brian-kemp|Brian Kemp]] in August 2022 — one month before Perdue's grand jury testimony.[1] Badlands Media framed the appointment as part of a pattern in which Georgia officials involved in 2020 election matters — including [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]] and [[alison-sosebee|Alison Sosebee]] — subsequently received judgeships from Kemp.[1]
