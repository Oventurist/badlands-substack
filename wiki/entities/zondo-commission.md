# Zondo Commission

The Zondo Commission was the South African judicial inquiry formally known as "The Judicial Commission of Inquiry into Allegations of State Capture, Corruption and Fraud in the Public Sector including Organs of State."[1] It conducted its public inquiry from August 2018 until June 2022.[1]

## Origins

The commission was created in 2018 as public dissent against the overt corruption of President [[jacob-zuma|Jacob Zuma]] reached a fever pitch; Zuma resigned upon its creation, ending a decade in which he had served simultaneously as executive president of [[south-africa|South Africa]] and as president of the [[african-national-congress|African National Congress]] from 2008 until 2018.[1]

## Findings

At the heart of the inquiry was Zuma's "state capture" of the South African government.[1] Roughly 300 witnesses testified that this capture had been facilitated in large part by the [[gupta-brothers|Gupta brothers]], through disinformation disseminated by their media companies and through state contracts illegally procured for their portfolio of businesses.[1] Zuma was ultimately able to evade the prison sentence arising from the proceedings by claiming terminal illness, securing release in 2023.[1]

## Legacy

Badlands commentary treats the commission's record — and the parallel United States designation of the Guptas under [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]] — as background to Zuma's 2024 electoral re-emergence at the head of the [[umkhonto-we-sizwe-party|MK party]], arguing that mainstream coverage of that comeback systematically omitted the state-capture history.[1]
