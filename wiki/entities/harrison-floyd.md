# Harrison Floyd

Harrison Floyd is one of the co-defendants in the Fulton County racketeering prosecution brought by District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] over the 2020 presidential election in Georgia.[1]

## Affirmative defense

Floyd's attorneys posed the question "What if we can prove that Trump won?" during a motions hearing, and their subpoenas made clear that they were pursuing that affirmative defense.[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited Floyd's approach as the reason she did not want the case dismissed before the evidence was heard: the trial would be televised, the evidence would be aired, and the "Trump won" defense was in play, making the proceeding what she described as the best opportunity to litigate [[2020-election-fraud-claims|2020 election fraud]].[1]

## Kachouroff recording allegation (2024)

In April 2024 Floyd's attorney, Christopher Kachouroff, alleged that Fulton
County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] had illegally recorded a
phone call with one of his Maryland colleagues, a felony in Maryland because
the state requires the consent of both parties to a recording.[2] Badlands
contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Floyd had been
mistreated and discriminated against by Willis and by the government broadly
since before he turned himself in, that he was treating his defense as a
patriotic mission, and that Willis might face further trouble if the judge
agreed to hear Floyd's latest motion.[2] Floyd was tentatively scheduled to
join Ashe and Brian on that Friday's episode of *Why We Vote*.[2]

## Evidentiary hearing and its implications (February 2024)

Ahead of the February 15, 2024 hearing on [[michael-roman|Michael Roman]]'s motion to disqualify District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]], Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] recalled that the previous hearing on Floyd's evidentiary questions had "raised real issues for the county on the veracity of their election procedures."[3] She argued those issues might be enough to debunk the "big lie" as the alleged intent behind the defendants' actions in the [[state-of-georgia-v-trump|Georgia RICO case]] — that is, to show that their actions had legitimate, lawful cause.[3]
