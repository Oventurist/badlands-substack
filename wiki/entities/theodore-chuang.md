# Theodore Chuang

Theodore Chuang is a United States District Judge in Maryland who, in March 2025, ruled that the dismantling of the [[usaid|U.S. Agency for International Development]] by [[doge|the Department of Government Efficiency]] likely violated the Constitution.[1]

## USAID ruling

On Tuesday, March 18, 2025, Chuang indefinitely blocked DOGE from making further cuts to USAID.[1] His order required the Trump administration to restore email and computer access to all USAID employees, including those placed on administrative leave, although it stopped short of reversing firings or fully resurrecting the agency.[1] The judge found it likely that USAID was no longer capable of performing some of its statutorily required functions.[1]

In what was described as one of the first DOGE lawsuits directed at [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] personally, Chuang rejected the administration's position that Musk was merely an adviser to [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]].[1] He held that Musk's public statements and social media posts demonstrated that he had "firm control over DOGE," pointing in particular to an online post in which Musk said he had "fed USAID into the wood chipper."[1]

## Context and commentary

Chuang's ruling came on the same day that Judge [[ana-reyes|Ana Reyes]] blocked enforcement of the executive order barring transgender people from military service, and hours after Trump called for the impeachment of Judge [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]] over deportation flights.[1] Badlands commentary characterized the cluster of rulings as evidence of a judiciary exceeding its authority; Chris Paul wrote that "judges have no say in any of these matters," describing the situation as a "collision course" and calling to "tear the whole artifice down."[1]
