# USA Rare Earth

USA Rare Earth is an American rare-earth mining and processing company that became the subject of a major federal industrial-policy intervention in January 2026, when the [[department-of-commerce|U.S. Department of Commerce]] issued a non-binding letter of intent to support the firm with a $1.6 billion funding package in exchange for a roughly 10% government equity stake.[1]

## Commerce Department letter of intent

The [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] announced that the U.S. government would back a $1.6 billion funding package for USA Rare Earth under a non-binding letter of intent with the Department of Commerce.[1] The package comprises $277 million in proposed federal funding and a $1.3 billion senior secured loan extended under the CHIPS and Science Act.[1]

As part of the arrangement, the federal government is expected to acquire approximately 10% of the company's equity by receiving 16.1 million shares and roughly 17.6 million warrants, according to reporting by [[reuters|Reuters]].[1] Following the announcement, USA Rare Earth's shares rose about 15% to $28.42 on January 26, 2026.[1]

## Operations

USA Rare Earth is developing a mine at Sierra Blanca, Texas, which is slated to open by 2028.[1] The company also operates a magnet manufacturing plant in Stillwater, Oklahoma, which is expected to begin production later in 2026.[1] The funding package is part of broader U.S. efforts to strengthen domestic rare-earth production and reduce reliance on [[china|China]] for materials used in critical infrastructure.[1]

## Commentary and the "rare processing" framing

Badlands contributor Ashe in America connected the deal to remarks [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] made at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], where he argued that rare-earth elements are not in fact scarce — the scarcity lies in processing capacity: "There's no such thing as rare earth. There's rare processing. But there's so much rare earth."[1] Trump made the comments in the context of [[greenland|Greenland]], framing the pursuit of the ice-covered landmass as a matter of strategic national and international security rather than mineral rights alone.[1]

The Badlands reading is that mineral rights *are* national security, since rare earths are embedded in communications, weapons, and healthcare systems, and that the Commerce package is designed to make American processing capability "less rare" — a precondition for ending dependence on foreign capabilities for critical infrastructure.[1]
