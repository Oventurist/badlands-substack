# Cities Church

Cities Church is a congregation in St. Paul, Minnesota that became a flashpoint in the January 2026 unrest surrounding federal immigration enforcement in the Minneapolis–St. Paul area.[1]

## Storming of the Sunday service

Dozens of left-wing activists stormed the church during a Sunday service, apparently targeting a pastor believed to be linked to the local field office operations of [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]].[1] Former CNN host [[don-lemon|Don Lemon]] is alleged to have joined the mob, prompting the [[department-of-justice|U.S. Department of Justice]] to place him "on notice" for potential federal charges.[1]

Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]] said the incident could violate the [[ku-klux-klan-act|Ku Klux Klan Act]], which prohibits conspiracies to intimidate or violate civil rights, and the [[face-act|FACE Act]], which protects houses of worship from interference.[1] She emphasized zero tolerance, noting that disrupting religious services is not protected speech, and confirmed that the Civil Rights Division and the [[fbi|FBI]] were investigating.[1] Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] spoke directly with the affected pastor, affirming that attacks on law enforcement and intimidation of Christians would face the "full force of federal law," and [[white-house|White House]] officials and religious leaders condemned the actions as a desecration of a sacred space.[1]

## Context

The incident occurred amid surging tensions over federal ICE deportation operations targeting criminal illegal aliens in the sanctuary-city region, exacerbated by the fatal shooting of Renee Good by an ICE agent after she allegedly attempted to run over a federal officer with her vehicle.[1] The broader federal response included the deployment of 3,000 ICE agents to the area with 1,500 troops on standby, amid warnings of escalating left-wing violence in Democrat-run cities.[1]

## Indictments following the protest

Federal prosecutors said that [[don-lemon|Don Lemon]] and three others were indicted by a Minnesota grand jury over the January 18, 2026 protest at the church, which disrupted a worship service and targeted a pastor affiliated with [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]].[2] The charges include conspiracy and interfering with the First Amendment rights of worshippers, invoking federal statutes including the Freedom of Access to Clinic Entrances Act.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that congregants present during the disruption may each hold a civil claim against Lemon and should bring them, describing the episode as one in which children in the church were terrified and real harm was done.[2]
