# International Medical Centers

International Medical Centers (IMC) was the Miami-based health maintenance organization built by Cuban émigré [[miguel-recarey|Miguel Recarey]] through political influence, campaign contributions to both parties and well-connected lobbyists. Between 1980 and 1987 it received almost $1 billion from Medicare under what the Tampa Bay Times called kid-glove treatment.[1]

Federal law capped Medicare patients at 50 percent of an HMO's enrollees in order to limit the size of possible fraud; [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]] lobbied the relevant federal agency by telephone and secured IMC an exemption.[1] The company subsequently collapsed amid allegations of shoddy medical care, forged enrollments, phony billings and bribery of union officials, leaving $230 million in unpaid claims for the state of Florida to sort through.[1] IMC paid Bush's real-estate company $75,000; Bush said the payment was for real-estate work, though IMC never selected a building he showed.[1]
