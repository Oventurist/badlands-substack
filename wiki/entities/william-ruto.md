# William Ruto

**William Ruto** is the president of Kenya.[1] On Wednesday, 29 January 2025, Ruto organized a virtual conference between eight African countries concerning the outbreak of violence on the eastern border of the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]], where the [[m23|M23]] militia had taken control of the city of Goma and its surrounding provinces and was pushing south.[1]

Rwandan president [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]], whose military was alleged to be aiding M23, took part in the virtual conference.[1] Notably absent from the call was DRC president [[felix-tshisekedi|Felix Tshisekedi]], who was said to have a scheduling conflict.[1]

Badlands commentary treated the conference as one element of a broader geopolitical struggle the author calls the War of Sovereignty between the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and the Globalist Regime.[1]

## Finance Bill 2024 protests

When anti-tax protesters breached and set fire to Kenya's parliament in June 2024 over new levies in the Finance Bill 2024, Ruto was outside the capital attending an African Union event, but struck a conciliatory tone and praised the enthusiasm of the young people who formed the backbone of the demonstrations.[2]

## EAC chairmanship and the 2025 DRC–Rwanda crisis

As Kenyan head of state and chairman of the [[east-african-community|East African Community]], Ruto announced on Sunday, January 26, 2025 that the bloc would convene an extraordinary summit on the DRC–Rwanda crisis within 48 hours, bringing together Rwandan President [[paul-kagame|Paul Kagame]] and DRC President [[felix-tshisekedi|Félix Tshisekedi]].[3] "After consulting the heads of state of the EAC member states, we will convene an extraordinary summit in the next 48 hours to chart the way forward," he said in an address on X; the summit was later expected to be organized that Wednesday.[3] The announcement followed reports that rebel forces including [[m23|M23]] had captured [[2025-goma-offensive|Goma]] in eastern DRC.[3]

## Maisha Namba digital ID program

Badlands Media reported in November 2023 that Ruto had held a series of "closed-door meetings" with [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]], after which Gates's role in assisting the Kenyan government with the development and rollout of the [[maisha-namba|Maisha Namba]] digital identity program was announced.[4] The *Kenyan Daily Post* reported that Gates had met Ruto in a series of meetings since he came to power, with most of the engagements shrouded in secrecy, and that the resulting deal would connect the government to key technical experts and partners offering professional advice to guarantee a smooth rollout.[4]

Kenya prepared for a US$6.8 million launch of the biometric digital ID system, but authorities postponed the launch at the beginning of October 2023 due to "unfavorable circumstances."[4] Rights groups warned the program risked discrimination and the erosion of privacy, arguing Ruto's government was repeating the mistakes of the previous Huduma Namba system, declared unconstitutional by Kenya's High Court in October 2021 for conflicting with the Data Protection Act.[4] Residents of the eastern city of Garissa said they were not ready for the rollout because of poor infrastructure and network availability.[4] Ruto's government promised the third-generation ID card would not be mandatory and would rely on the existing civil registration system rather than requiring new registration.[4]
