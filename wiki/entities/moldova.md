# Moldova

**Moldova** is an eastern European state from which the predominantly Russian-speaking territory of [[transnistria|Transnistria]] declared independence during the collapse of the Soviet Union.[1]

## Economic blockade of Transnistria (2024)

In February 2024 Transnistrian lawmakers asked [[russia|Russia]] for assistance in response to what they characterized as an economic blockade and mounting economic pressure imposed by Moldova.[1] Their appeal to the Federation Council and State Duma cited the more than 220,000 Russian citizens resident in the territory and warned that "the critical situation requires urgent and maximum active international intervention in order to prevent an escalation of tensions and not allow the situation to develop into a crisis."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands analyst [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] compared the Moldova–Transnistria standoff to the Donbas region of eastern Ukraine circa 2014, and treated US warnings to the region about Russian interventionism as escalatory.[1] He noted that European institutions had warned EU citizens of Russia's supposedly inevitable "invasion" of Moldova as early as 2022, and that the Council of Europe reclassified Transnistria in 2023 as "Russian-occupied territory," which he read as evidence of a pre-scripted "Problem > Reaction" dynamic.[1]

## Oligarchy and the Great Game

Moldova is the eastern-most country in Europe, a small landlocked state bordering Ukraine with only the thin strip of [[transnistria|Transnistria]] — occupied by some 1,500 Russian soldiers — separating the two nations.[2] For years the country was run as an instrument of what the author calls the trans-Atlantic financier cabal, with the oligarch [[vlad-plahotniuc|Vlad Plahotniuc]] controlling the government, the judiciary, law enforcement and 60% of the mass media.[2] Moldova was also used as a proxy in NATO's anti-Russian operations and as an influence operation undermining Muammar Gaddafi's plans for a Libyan industrial renaissance.[2]

The 2009 [[2009-moldovan-twitter-revolution|"Twitter Revolution"]] displaced the pro-Russian Communist Party and brought the pro-EU [[democratic-party-of-moldova|Democratic Party]] to prominence, after which three pro-Western parties formed the Alliance for European Integration and won 53 seats.[2] By 2014 Moldova had signed EU association agreements alongside Georgia and Ukraine, and although its 1994 Constitution barred alliance membership, the country was already part of the [[nato|NATO]] Partnership for Peace Program and had participated in NATO's Kosovo Force since 2014.[2]

Between 2011 and 2014 the [[moldovan-billion-dollar-bank-fraud|"theft of the century"]] stripped roughly $1 billion — one-eighth of GDP — from a country ranked lowest on the European Union poverty index, where nearly 50% of the male population lives and works abroad as cheap labour.[2] The backlash returned the Socialists to power under [[igor-dodon|Igor Dodon]], who obtained observer status in the [[eurasian-economic-union|Eurasian Economic Union]] in March 2018 and signed $400 million in [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road]] infrastructure deals with China.[2] That course was reversed after December 2020 by [[maia-sandu|Maia Sandu]] and the [[party-of-action-and-solidarity|Party of Action and Solidarity]], who applied for EU membership, restricted Russian media and language, and banned pro-Russian parties before the September 2025 elections.[2]

The article warns of the ease with which Moldova could be turned into a cauldron of war as part of NATO's broader agenda to open additional fronts against Russia, citing a November 2025 incident in which a Russian-modelled drone was reportedly programmed to crash onto Moldovan buildings and then placed on the steps of the Foreign Ministry as a "gift" during a visit by Russia's ambassador.[2]

## Money-laundering conduit thesis

Moldova, a former Soviet republic lying between [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and Romania, is described by Forbes as having been a hub for smuggling and contraband since the end of the Soviet Union and, from 2009, "a conduit of international money laundering."[3] In January 2010 the country signed a five-year, $262 million [[millennium-challenge-corporation|Millennium Challenge Corporation]] compact with the United States, presided over by Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and prime minister [[vlad-filat|Vladimir Filat]], including an "innovative financing facility" co-financed by [[usaid|USAID]]; a previous $24.7 million Threshold program had targeted corruption in the judiciary, tax, customs and police services.[3]

Between 2012 and 2014 roughly $1 billion — 12 to 15% of GDP — was taken from the country's three largest banks, depreciating the currency by 42%.[3] The Open Dialogue Foundation characterised 2013 to 2019 as a period of a "captured state" under oligarch [[vlad-plahotniuc|Vlad Plahotniuc]], who controlled the legislature, executive, courts, law enforcement and about 60% of mass media.[3] Badlands writer Joe Lange argues Moldova was deliberately cultivated as a laundering platform, and that gold and cash looted from [[libya|Libya]] after 2011 passed through it, with the [[podesta-group|Podesta Group]] serving as the Washington end of the arrangement.[3] These are the author's inferences.[3]
