# Libs of TikTok

Libs of TikTok is a popular social media account, run by [[chaya-raichik|Chaya Raichik]], that reposts content from progressive users. It features in Badlands Media's coverage chiefly through the controversy over its exposure by [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

Lorenz gained a substantially greater degree of notoriety with the American right after she doxed the account, publishing identifying information about the person behind it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Badlands notes that she "then had the audacity to go on a media tour complaining about online harassment in its wake," offering the episode as evidence of the inability of journalists of her type to reflect on whether they deserve the hostility they invite.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Laken Riley headline episode

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] used a Libs of TikTok post as a "micro-example" of what he described as reflexive, fact-free outrage within the conservative movement.[2] The account reposted an [[msnbc|MSNBC]] headline reading "Laken Riley's killer never stood a chance," adding the comment "The media is the enemy of the people."[2]

DeLarme argued that the account holder appeared to have assumed from the headline alone — after years of consuming what he called the contaminated rot of modern journalism — that MSNBC was drumming up sympathy for [[laken-riley|Laken Riley]]'s killer and his attorney.[2] In fact, he wrote, the article described the killer as essentially indefensible, quoting its observation that "sometimes defense counsel just gets handed a truly awful, unwinnable case" and that the defense's choice of a bench trial saved state resources and avoided prolonging the family's ordeal.[2] Had the article been read rather than reacted to, DeLarme concluded, the account would have found that it shared the author's opinion.[2]

He framed the episode as symptomatic of a broader problem: figures who treat every event as material for a right-versus-left attack line erode public trust in the movement and damage its ability to win hearts and minds.[2]
