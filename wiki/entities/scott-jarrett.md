# Scott Jarrett

Scott Jarrett is the Director of Elections for [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]], Arizona. He was named in his official capacity as a defendant in the election contest filed by [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] on December 9, 2022, alongside [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]], County Recorder [[stephen-richer|Stephen Richer]], the individual members of the [[maricopa-county-board-of-supervisors|Maricopa County Board of Supervisors]], and the Board itself.[1]

As elections director, Jarrett's office was responsible for the Election Day operation at the center of the complaint: the county's 223 vote centers, of which the suit alleged 59 percent suffered printer and tabulator failures, and the handling of the uncounted "[[door-3-ballots|Door 3]]" ballots that the complaint said were co-mingled with already-tabulated ballots.[1]

## Perjury allegation in the first Lake trial

In the first trial of [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]], Maricopa County Elections Director Scott Jarrett was accused of perjuring himself when, on day one, he testified that he had not heard of any reports of a 19-inch ballot image being placed on 20-inch ballot paper.[2] According to Jordan Conradson of [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]], Jarrett "claimed to have 'no knowledge' of this occurring and stated, 'I don't believe that that occurred,'" then changed his testimony the next day to say he had learned of a fit-to-paper issue "a few days after Election Day" that printed "a slightly smaller image of a 20-inch image on a 20-inch paper ballot."[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Jarrett was never held accountable for the contradictory testimony.[2]

[[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]]'s own [[maricopa-county-2022-ballot-on-demand-printer-investigation|Ballot on Demand printer investigation]], released in April 2023, later confirmed that the ballot image had been lengthened from 19 to 20 inches before the election.[2] At the May 2023 Count III trial, observers judged that county elections director [[ray-valenzuela|Rey Valenzuela]] performed even worse on the stand than Jarrett had.[2]
