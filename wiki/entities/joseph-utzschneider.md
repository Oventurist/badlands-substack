# Joseph Utzschneider

Joseph Utzschneider was a Bavarian professor and former member of the [[bavarian-illuminati|Order of the Illuminati]] whose defection triggered the state suppression of the Order.[1]

Utzschneider quit the Order in August 1783.[1] In October of that year he, together with Grünberger and Cosandey — fellow professors at the Marianen (Marienburg) Academy and fellow Order members — presented the Duchess Maria Anna with an internal Illuminati document and a membership list. The Duchess was thoroughly alarmed and passed the material to her husband, prompting [[carl-theodore|Duke Carl Theodore]]'s first edict against unauthorized secret societies on June 22, 1784.[1]

## Juridical deposition of 1785

On September 9, 1785 Utzschneider, Grünberger and Cosandey made a joint juridical deposition before the Elector. They testified that "the object of the first degrees of Illuminism is at once to train their young men, and to be informed of every thing that is going forward by a system of espionage," with superiors seeking diplomatic acts, documents and original writings from inferiors, and welcoming treasonous acts because the secrets betrayed could be turned to advantage while leaving the traitors "in a perpetual dread" of exposure — summarized in the maxim *Oderint dum metuant*, "let them hate, provided they fear."[1]

The deposition listed the principles in which lower-degree Illuminees were educated: that any candidate for the highest degree "must be free from all religion," since a "religionist" would never be admitted; the doctrine of *Patet Exitus* on suicide; that "the end sanctifies the means," so that the welfare of the Order justified "calumnies, poisonings, assassinations, perjuries, treasons, rebellions"; that submission to Illuminati superiors outranked obedience to sovereigns or magistrates, with "honor, life, and fortune" to be sacrificed to superiors; and that love of one's prince and country was incompatible with "the ultimate ends of the Order."[1] Superiors, the deponents said, were "to be looked upon as the most perfect and the most enlightened of men; no doubts are to be entertained even of their infallibility."[1] They further reported that the Illuminati used "every possible artifice to get the different post offices in all countries entrusted to the care of their adepts only," boasting of possessing the secret of opening and reclosing letters undetected.[1]
