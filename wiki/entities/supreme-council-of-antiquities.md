# Supreme Council of Antiquities

The **Supreme Council of Antiquities** is the Egyptian body responsible for overseeing archaeological work and heritage management in Egypt, operating in conjunction with the Egyptian Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities.[1] Its secretary-general, Mohamed Ismail Khaled, commented publicly in May 2025 on the discovery of three New Kingdom tombs at the Dra' Abu el-Naga area on the West Bank of [[luxor|Luxor]], noting that further research was needed to establish more about the tombs' owners but that the findings enriched understanding of the period.[1]

The excavation was carried out by an all-Egyptian research team, which used inscriptions inside the tombs to identify the names and titles of the owners — prominent statesmen of Egypt's New Kingdom era, dated roughly 1550 B.C.E. to 1070 B.C.E.[1] The first of the three tombs was dated to the [[ramesside-period|Ramesside Period]] and belonged to Amum-em-Ipet, who worked in either the temple or the estate of Amun.[1]
