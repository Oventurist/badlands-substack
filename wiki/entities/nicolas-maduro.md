# Nicolás Maduro

Nicolás Maduro is the Venezuelan president who was captured in a U.S. military operation on January 3, 2026, an event that reshaped U.S.–Venezuelan relations and, in the Badlands reading, opened a front against the global banking system.[1]

## Capture and aftermath

U.S. officials engaged in months of covert discussions with Venezuelan Interior Minister [[diosdado-cabello|Diosdado Cabello]] both before and after the January 3 operation that resulted in Maduro's capture.[1] Following his ouster, [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] served as acting Venezuelan president, and [[cia|CIA]] Director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] traveled to Caracas to meet her — the highest-ranking U.S. official visit since Maduro's removal.[1]

## Continuity across Trump administrations

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argues there is a clear continuity of the Maduro plotline between the first and second Trump administrations, pointing to [[richard-grenell|Ric Grenell]]'s late-2020 Mexico City meeting with [[jorge-rodriguez|Jorge Rodríguez]] — brother of Delcy Rodríguez — reported at the time as an attempt to secretly negotiate Maduro's exit, and to Grenell's January 2025 trip to Caracas to meet Maduro and retrieve six imprisoned Americans.[1] In this reading Maduro's exit was timed to coincide with a move against the global banking system, and the Maduro/Rodríguez government is operating under a [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] protocol.[1]

## Litigation against banks

A civil action lawsuit brought by Maduro against executives of banks, commodity trading houses and oil companies — dismissed without prejudice in 2019 over national security issues tied to Trump's sanctions — can be resumed now that sanctions have been lifted, potentially opening discovery into the global banking system.[1]

## Prosecution and international fallout (January 2026)

Mexican President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]] publicly defended Maduro, requesting that he be given a fair trial.[2] Badlands reported emerging claims that the US government's case against Maduro hinges on the testimony of a single Venezuelan general given as part of a plea deal for clemency, and speculated that if true the prosecution could collapse in court and Maduro be publicly exonerated of allegations that he was a cartel boss.[2] Badlands also noted earlier assertions by what it termed NeoCons and Zionists that Maduro headed Colombia's [[national-liberation-army|National Liberation Army (ELN)]], asserting that no evidence had been provided.[2]

The operation that captured Maduro became a reference point in international politics: Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] cited it in urging a similar US operation against Chechen leader [[ramzan-kadyrov|Ramzan Kadyrov]], German President [[frank-walter-steinmeier|Frank-Walter Steinmeier]] pointed to the episode as destabilizing to the global order, and Slovakian President [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]] said it represented the end of the post-WWII international order.[2] An EU Commission spokesperson said the bloc does not recognize the legitimacy of interim president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] while maintaining "targeted engagement" with Venezuelan authorities.[2]

## Re-normalization following his capture (January 2026)

Maduro's capture by the US military on January 3, 2026 was followed within weeks by a rapid normalization of US–[[venezuela|Venezuela]] relations.[3] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced the reopening of commercial airspace over Venezuela, [[american-airlines|American Airlines]] moved to resume direct flights after more than six years, and the US eased certain oil-sector sanctions via a general license covering purchase, sale, transport, storage, marketing, and refining of Venezuelan-origin crude — though not new production.[3] Acting President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] signed a hydrocarbons law overhaul opening the industry to private investment.[3] Badlands commentary argued that the "arrest" of Maduro functioned to place the Venezuelan government into a state of continuity rather than collapse.[3]

## The "Discombobulator" account and the 15-minute ultimatum

In a January 25, 2026 interview, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said American forces had employed a classified device he called the [[discombobulator|"Discombobulator"]] during the January 3 raid, disabling Venezuelan defense equipment and preventing the launch of Russian and Chinese missiles.[4] Maduro and his wife [[cilia-flores|Cilia Flores]] were captured and transferred to the United States to face charges.[4] A leaked recording of a post-operation meeting captured acting president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] saying that U.S. forces gave members of Maduro's cabinet "15 minutes to comply or they would kill us," and claiming that the remaining leadership was initially told Maduro and his wife had been killed rather than captured.[4] Badlands commentary treated the account as theater, noting Rodríguez's earlier affirmation that Maduro had signed a Continuity of Government order in December in anticipation of his "departure."[4]

Maduro's earlier litigation also resurfaced: commodity traders [[vitol|Vitol]] and [[trafigura|Trafigura]], both enlisted by Trump to broker sales of Venezuelan oil, had been named as defendants in a lawsuit Maduro filed in U.S. and Swiss courts in March 2018.[4]

## Saab deportation and the corruption-purge thesis (2026)

In May 2026 Venezuela's government deported longtime Maduro ally [[alex-saab|Alex Saab]] to the United States, less than three years after [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] pardoned Saab in a prisoner swap; Venezuelan authorities said the transfer was tied to multiple ongoing U.S. criminal investigations.[5] Saab, whom U.S. officials long described as Maduro's "bag man," had been appointed Minister of Industry by Maduro in October 2024 and was removed from his Cabinet role and his oversight of foreign investment deals after [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] took power on January 3.[5]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the episode inverts the standard media narrative about Maduro.[5] He pointed to note 32, "Investigation Case," on pages 78–79 of the 2015 Consolidated Financial Statement of [[pdvsa|PDVSA]], describing a U.S. criminal indictment of multiple PDVSA representatives who all pleaded guilty, with PDVSA presenting itself as a victim and pledging an internal investigation that could yield Venezuelan indictments.[5] On this reading, that investigation drove Maduro's corruption purges, his lawsuits against 42 corrupt oil executives in U.S. court, and Saab's cooperation with U.S. authorities during the first Trump administration "with the full knowledge and support of Maduro," according to Saab's lawyers.[5] The arrests of corrupt officials, the author contended, were what led legacy media to brand Maduro a ruthless dictator when he was cleaning up corruption tied to the Bush dynasty and its allies in Big Oil.[5]

The Brief concluded that Saab, Maduro, Rodríguez and the Venezuelan government are working with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] against a cabal that has long controlled both countries and the oil and energy sectors, and noted that the media anticipated a "trial of the century" for Maduro, who has been a persistent irritant to the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] establishment since first winning office in 2013.[5]

## Argentine extradition request (February 2026)

In February 2026 an Argentine federal judge requested Maduro's extradition from the United States to face prosecution for alleged crimes against humanity, according to court filings reported by the Associated Press, which did not publish the filings themselves.[6] The warrant, reportedly signed by federal judge Sebastián Ramos, ordered "the urgent translation of the international request and the documentation attached thereto," and alleged torture, unlawful detention, and forced disappearances.[6] Maduro was at that time detained in the United States facing federal narcotics and narco-terrorism charges.[6] Badlands treated the request skeptically, framing it as an item the [[cia|CIA]] was "desperately trying to make related" to the [[puerto-rico|Puerto Rico]] voting machine story and questioning [[argentina|Argentina]]'s independence given [[imf|IMF]] influence.[6]

## Cooperation against Tren de Aragua

In June 2026 Maduro's government confirmed its participation in a joint operation with the United States that killed Héctor Rusthenford Guerrero Flores, the leader of [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]], during clashes in Bolívar state.[7] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued the cooperation refuted the Uniparty claim that Tren de Aragua was created and led by Maduro, contending instead that the gang was most likely created by the [[cia|CIA]] to subvert and overthrow him.[7]

He cited a January 2025 speech in which Maduro described the "Aragua Train" and "Tren del Llano" as "a construct" instrument of policies designed to attack the Venezuelan people, destabilize the country and facilitate interventionist scenarios, delivered at the inauguration of the "Bolivarian Shield 2025" military and police exercises.[7] Maduro, who had been sworn in for a third presidential term on January 10 despite accusations of electoral fraud, said [[venezuela|Venezuela]] had largely defeated such criminal groups and offered to share its experience with other countries.[7] In the same address he blamed the former Colombian presidents Álvaro Uribe and Iván Duque, declaring them "public enemies number one" of the Bolivarian National Armed Forces.[7]

## Sanctions relief for his legal defense (April 2026)

In April 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] agreed in a court filing to modify [[venezuela|Venezuela]] [[sanctions]] so that the Venezuelan government could pay Maduro's defense lawyer in his US narcoterrorism case, after his attorney argued the sanctions blocked his [[sixth-amendment|Sixth Amendment]] right to counsel.[8] Prosecutors said the change made Maduro's motion to dismiss the case moot.[8] The dispute traced back to the [[office-of-foreign-assets-control|Office of Foreign Asset Control]] initially allowing payment for Maduro's counsel and then revoking that permission, prompting defense attorney [[barry-pollack|Barry Pollack]] to argue that the government was interfering with Maduro's chosen legal representation; OFAC's Venezuela framework allows certain activities, including some legal-fee arrangements, only by license.[8]

A February court filing stated that "Mr. Maduro, as Venezuela's head of state, has both a right and an expectation to have legal fees associated with these charges funded by the government of Venezuela."[8] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read this as the US government casually admitting that Maduro remains President of Venezuela — the position Interim President Dulce Rodriguez has maintained since Maduro was extracted and taken to the United States.[8] He argued that by lifting sanctions [[donald-trump|President Trump]] has made the Venezuelan government wealthy amid a spike in oil prices, leaving it able to buy the best defense available, and predicted that [[david-boies|David Boies]] or an associate of his firm would take the case.[8] He recalled that Maduro first hired Boies in 2018 to sue 42 of the world's most powerful oil executives, a lawsuit he calls the catalyst for the wider geopolitical operation against the banking and energy sectors.[8]


## Detention and continuity of government (January 2026)

Following a dramatic U.S. operation that detained Maduro abroad, Venezuela's Supreme Court ordered Vice President [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] to assume the role of acting president "in the absence" of Maduro, saying she would ensure continuity of government.[9] Rodríguez was formally sworn in on January 5, 2026 as interim president at the National Assembly while Maduro appeared in a New York court on federal drug charges.[9] Rodríguez immediately clarified that Maduro remained the one true president of Venezuela, a claim Maduro himself repeated during his court appearance.[9] His son, [[nicolas-maduro-guerra|Nicolás Maduro Guerra]], told a weekend rally that a continuity-of-government plan was in effect and that the government was following directions left by his father.[9]

Badlands commentators advanced a [[venezuelan-devolution|Venezuelan Devolution]] theory holding that Maduro, with constitutional authority as president, deliberately initiated a COG contingency plan negotiated through the Qataris with [[richard-grenell|Ric Grenell]] as intermediary and approved by [[donald-trump|President Trump]].[9] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued Maduro "fell on the story's sword" as the prime villain of the regime-change narrative, and noted that Russia had already offered him pre-emptive asylum on the back of Trump's earlier promises of amnesty.[9]

Maduro was given a laptop in March 2017 by the scorned wife of one of his corrupt officials; his lawyer [[david-boies|David Boies]] said of the evidence that "in terms of the quality of the evidence, the extent of the misconduct, the amount of money, this was right at the top of anything I've seen in my career," describing a long-running, massive and sophisticated conspiracy.[9]

## Operation Absolute Resolve and the SDNY arraignment

The January 3, 2026 action, codenamed [[operation-absolute-resolve|Operation Absolute Resolve]], involved more than 150 US aircraft, airstrikes on installations including La Carlota airbase and Fuerte Tiuna, and ground raids in Caracas and in Miranda, Aragua and La Guaira, with explosions reported around 2 a.m. local time.[10] Maduro and his wife [[cilia-flores|Cilia Flores]] were captured and transported into the custody of the US District Court for the Southern District of New York to face charges of narco-terrorism conspiracy, cocaine importation conspiracy and additional counts under a superseding indictment.[10] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took a victory lap, declaring "We are going to run the country" and saying US oil majors would spend billions to repair Venezuela's oil infrastructure, while dismissing opposition figure [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] as lacking support at home and honoring Maduro's designation of [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] as acting president.[10]

Imagery from the transfer became a focus of Badlands commentary: Trump posted a photograph of Maduro handcuffed with eyes and ears bound; early footage showed Maduro in what appeared to be a Mickey Mouse hat saying "goodnight" and "Happy New Year" as he arrived at DEA offices in New York; another image showed him seated and giving two thumbs up amid posing [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]] agents; and a convoy paraded him through New York City in a van driven with its doors open.[10] Observers also noted multiple wardrobe changes during the journey.[10] *The New York Times* reported that Maduro's dancing had ultimately provoked Trump into military action, and the [[cia|CIA]] reportedly leaked operational details to the *Times* and *The Washington Post* in advance.[10]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], who had predicted the extraction and a willing surrender as early as October 7, argued that the indictment of Maduro's wife as a narco-terrorist — unprecedented treatment for the spouse of a head of state — confirmed that Maduro was a cooperating witness entering protective custody rather than a toppled dictator.[10] He read Trump's pledge that oil companies would fund reconstruction as potential reparations connected to the 2018 [[pdvsa|PDVSA]] lawsuits against dozens of powerful oil executives accused of stealing Venezuela's wealth.[10] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the exit as a managed one facilitated by Trump and the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] in keeping with the "Assad Template."[10] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] observed that "no war" was precisely what Maduro had long been saying, and [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] joked that the ideal ending would be Trump collecting the $50 million bounty on Maduro himself.[10]

Badlands also circulated an earlier clip of Maduro on television asking how far the United States would let [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] go in his warmongering and calling on the Sovereign Alliance — naming China, Russia, Turkey, Qatar, the UAE and Saudi Arabia — to back [[iran|Iran]]'s calls for peace.[10]

## Capture and prosecution in New York (January 2026)

The pre-dawn raid on Caracas ordered by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] resulted in the capture of both Maduro and his wife [[cilia-flores|Cilia Flores]], who were transferred to New York and pleaded not guilty to US federal charges of narco-terrorism conspiracy, cocaine importation and weapons offenses.[11] Maduro declared himself innocent and a "prisoner of war," while his son accused the United States of kidnapping.[11] [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] was sworn in as interim president atop the sarcophagus of [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]], delivering a speech echoing Maduro's own rhetoric and insisting there was only one true president of Venezuela — Maduro himself.[11]

Badlands analysts noted the unusual respect afforded Maduro throughout the episode, observing that Trump had referred to him as "President Maduro" on Truth Social only once, in the post announcing the arrest.[11] The arrest reportedly turned Maduro into a folk hero across South America as a symbol of defiance against American imperialism, with his son addressing large rallies claiming the [[deep-state|Deep State]] had planned to trigger a civil war in Venezuela — raising the possibility, under the [[trumpamania|TrumpaMania]] frame, that the arrest subverted that plot.[11] Trump also publicly called Maduro a "violent guy" before accusing him of stealing his dance moves.[11] See [[capture-of-nicolas-maduro]] for the full episode.[11]

## Arraignment in New York (January 2026)

In early January 2026, Maduro pleaded not guilty in a New York federal court to four counts: narco-terrorism, cocaine importation conspiracy, and possession of and conspiracy to possess machine guns and destructive devices.[12] His wife, [[cilia-flores|Cilia Flores]], also pleaded not guilty to related charges, and the couple faces decades to life in prison if convicted.[12] Some legal analysts argued that the prosecution is unlawful under international law.[12]

Following his removal under [[operation-absolute-resolve|Operation Absolute Resolve]], Vice President and Oil Minister [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] was sworn in as interim president at [[miraflores-palace|Miraflores Palace]].[12] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the episode was less a hostile regime change than a managed, largely bloodless transition on the Syrian model, noting that Maduro's administration appeared largely intact and that globalist anger centered on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s refusal to endorse opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]].[12]

## New Year's 2026 interview

In a New Year's interview published January 2, 2026, Maduro was asked why he was out in public rather than hiding in a bunker after five months of nonstop threats against his life by U.S. government officials, and answered by invoking Jesus Christ: "I have an infallible bunker: God Almighty. I entrust myself to Him every day. I represent a historical project; 500 years of struggle. If God is with us, who can stand against us?"[13] He went on to say he intended to make [[venezuela|Venezuela]] great again just as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] wants to make America great again, and that both countries could do so through peaceful vocation rather than war.[13] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Maduro sounded far more reasonable and level-headed than [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], and argued that Trump and Maduro had been working together since 2017 while a coordinated transnational effort — centered, in his reading, on Netanyahu — sought to link Venezuela and [[iran|Iran]] as a pretext for war.[13]

## Possible talks with Trump (November 2025)

Speaking in Florida on Sunday, November 16, 2025, before boarding a flight to Washington, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he was open to negotiating with Maduro to defuse tensions and the ongoing war scare between the two countries, remarking, "We may be having some discussions with Maduro, and we'll see how that turns out," and adding, "They would like to talk."[14] Trump repeated his claim that the Venezuelan government has helped cartels smuggle drugs into the United States and released "hundreds of thousands" of violent criminals across the U.S. border — allegations of operating "narcoterrorist" cartels that Maduro has denied.[14] Trump argued that the [[state-department|State Department]]'s designation of the [[cartel-de-los-soles|Cartel de los Soles]] as a foreign terrorist organization would permit strikes on Venezuelan soil, saying, "It allows us to do that, but we haven't said we're going to do that."[14]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the soundbite as affirming his speculation, discussed on The Narrative with [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], that Maduro is cooperating with the Trump administration against cartels operating in the jungles of South America.[14] He also flagged a widely circulated video of Maduro singing a John Lennon song as strange but consistent with [[trumpamania|Trump-a-Mania]], and asked whether Maduro would ultimately be "arrested" or extracted.[14]

## Trump breaks off diplomacy (October 2025)

In October 2025 the New York Times reported that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had ordered his diplomats to stop making overtures to Caracas, a step described as paving the way for a potential military escalation or an attempt to remove Maduro from power, amid what the United States characterized as a campaign against drug cartels.[15] Trump reportedly conveyed the instruction to special presidential envoy [[richard-grenell|Richard Grenell]], who had been leading negotiations with Maduro and his government, during a meeting with senior military leaders.[15]

According to the report, Trump had grown frustrated that Maduro would not voluntarily relinquish power and that Venezuelan officials continued to deny involvement in drug trafficking, while officials described multiple military options for escalation that "could also include plans designed to force Maduro from power."[15] Grenell had been seeking a deal to avoid a larger conflict and grant American companies access to Venezuelan oil, an effort Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] reportedly found "unhelpful and creating confusion."[15] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] urged readers to watch whether Maduro would be "extracted via an arrest while the cartels are being wiped out."[15]

## 2024 election dispute and the Glencore lawsuit

Venezuela's National Electoral Council declared Maduro the winner of the July 28, 2024 presidential election, but the United States recognized opposition candidate [[edmundo-gonzalez|Edmundo González]] as the victor on August 1, 2024, with Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] citing "overwhelming evidence" that González had won the most votes.[16] Maduro responded: "The United States needs to keep its nose out of Venezuela!"[16] The main opposition coalition claimed to hold more than two-thirds of the machine-printed tally sheets as contrary evidence, while multiple governments — including Maduro allies — pressed the council to publish detailed counts.[16]

Badlands commentary connected the 2024 crisis to an earlier and largely unreported chapter: Maduro's own multi-year anti-corruption purge of Venezuela's oil sector.[16] According to that account, a laptop belonging to lead conspirator William Ruperti's rival was passed via a scorned ex-wife to investor Bill Duker in March 2017 and reached Maduro, containing instant-message chats and offshore account statements documenting large unexplained payments to family members of Venezuelan oil executives.[16] Roughly six months later Maduro began arresting former oil heads, and in March 2018 he filed suit in U.S. court against [[glencore|Glencore]], Trafigura and Vitol — the three largest commodity trading houses in the world — naming some 40 executives and involving over 70 attorneys, represented by [[david-boies|David Boies]].[16] The purge continued into 2024, when a former oil minister and onetime Hugo Chávez protégé was arrested for stealing millions and "conspiracy to destroy the Venezuelan economy," part of a campaign that had detained some 50 officials.[16]

The commentary drew a parallel between Maduro's purge and the contemporaneous anti-corruption purge launched by Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], and noted Maduro's public statement that the same actors who killed John F. Kennedy had also tried to kill [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and had been overthrowing governments for over 200 years.[16]

## Cartel de los Soles terrorist designation (November 2025)

On Monday, November 24, 2025, the United States formally designated Venezuela's [[cartel-de-los-soles|Cartel de los Soles]] as a foreign terrorist organization, layering additional terrorism-related sanctions on a network Washington says includes Maduro and other high-ranking officials; Venezuela's government rejected what it called a "ridiculous" plan to designate a "non-existent" group.[17] Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] had announced earlier that month that the designation would proceed over the network's alleged role in importing illegal drugs into the United States, and the move came as Maduro faced escalating pressure from a U.S. military buildup in the Caribbean, raising concerns the designation might be used to justify military action — though sanctions experts said the underlying statute does not authorize such a step.[17]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] highlighted Maduro's claim in an interview that 85% of the cartels' cash is laundered through major U.S. banks and that the cartels succeed only because they work with people inside the U.S. government, casting Maduro and other members of the Sovereign Alliance as speaking hard truth about the criminals running Washington.[17] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] echoed the 85% figure, called the entire fiat banking system a fraud, and remarked on the "amazing timing" relative to the markets, crypto, and "the fake war the Regime is begging for."[17]

## Reported assassination plot (October 2025)

In October 2025 Politico reported, citing informed sources, that the United States could attempt to assassinate Maduro after declaring him a drug lord and a terrorist.[18] Over the preceding weeks Washington had conducted strikes off the Venezuelan coast against what it called "drug boats," killing more than two dozen people, and had expanded its military presence in the region; U.S. officials accused Maduro of links to narcotics networks, a claim he rejected, while Caracas accused Washington of seeking to topple his government — a charge U.S. officials denied.[18] Politico reported that although President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had not spoken publicly about regime change in the oil-rich country, he wanted Maduro out of power and planned to use the war on drug cartels as a pretext, with one unnamed administration official saying, "Would everyone like Maduro to go? Yes."[18]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the leak as a sign that the [[state-department|State Department]] and [[cia|CIA]] were growing desperate, noting that Trump had always said he would not telegraph military plans, and floated the alternative that Trump might be seeding the idea to cultivate a controlled opposition.[18] The more theatrical the story became, the commentary argued, the more plausible the inverse reading — that Maduro was working with Trump against the globalist cabal.[18]

## Oslo escape and Russian support (December 2025)

In December 2025 Maduro's government was the focus of two parallel storylines in the Badlands News Brief. Opposition figurehead [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]], who had been in hiding in a Caracas suburb for nearly a year after a brief detention on 9 January, was reportedly whisked out of the country while Caracas authorities were preoccupied with the US seizure of a Venezuelan oil tanker, resurfacing on a hotel balcony in Oslo to accept the Nobel Peace Prize and urge regime change.[19] Badlands commentators dismissed the escape as a contrived psyop, while cautioning that Maduro himself "might not be what you thought he was."[19]

Meanwhile Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] telephoned Maduro on 11 December amid the US military buildup in the Caribbean, expressing solidarity with the Venezuelan people and reaffirming support for the Maduro government's resolve to defend national sovereignty against foreign pressure.[19] Both governments reaffirmed the strategic partnership treaty signed in May and described bilateral relations as strategic, strong and growing.[19]

The call followed the seizure of an oil tanker that had left a Venezuelan port earlier that month; Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] said the vessel had previously been sanctioned for allegedly transporting Iranian oil, while Venezuela called the operation piracy and accused Washington of seeking to "plunder" its natural resources.[19] Maduro responded publicly by addressing "American citizens who are against the war" in Spanish and singing a version of "Don't Worry, Be Happy" — "Not war, be happy" — a performance [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] compared to WrestleMania.[19]

## The "Trump–Maduro kayfabe" thesis (August 2025)

Following Bolivian President [[luis-arce|Luis Arce]]'s accusation that Washington was using the drug war as cover for controlling Latin America, Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that "the Trump–Maduro kayfabe is starting to make more and more sense."[20] In this reading, the strange tension between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s camp and Maduro gives Maduro the narrative shielding he needs to avoid being targeted by the cartels, providing plausible deniability that he is aiding Trump's efforts to bring down the drug trade — with the [[state-department|State Department]]'s $50 million bounty on his head, announced about a week earlier, serving as convincing cover.[20]

We know where the bad ones live, and we're going to start that very soon," Trump said, hinting at further escalation between Washington and Caracas.[45] Reporting the same day noted that it appeared all but certain Trump was prepared to use force to oust Maduro, unsettling anti-interventionists within his own coalition who feared an Iraq-style entanglement, regional destabilisation and damage to the MAGA brand.[45] Other Republicans defended the policy by arguing that intervention in the Western Hemisphere was more defensible than Middle East wars and that Trump sought a change in Venezuelan leadership rather than a wholesale remaking of the country's political system; Alex Gray, a former National Security Council chief of staff, said Trump was "not trying to play God with what regime is in which country."[45] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that the anti-Maduro mobilisation would ultimately draw the same actors into operations against Mexican cartels, which he described as private armies of the Regime intertwined with high-profile American industries.[45] Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] separately argued that the treatment of [[juan-orlando-hernandez|Juan Orlando Hernández]] was a template he now saw applied to Maduro and to [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]].[45]

## Response to Trump's blockade, December 2025

In a televised address on Wednesday, December 17, 2025, Maduro accused the United States of seeking to overthrow his government and turn [[venezuela|Venezuela]] into a colony, rejecting Washington's threats and oil blockade as a "diplomacy of barbarism."[46] He said the US was attempting to impose a "puppet government" in Caracas that "would not last even 47 hours," and described President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s pressure campaign as "warmongering" aimed at seizing the country's natural wealth: "They want regime change in Venezuela to impose a puppet government that would hand over the constitution, sovereignty, and all our riches and turn the country into a colony. That is not going to happen – never."[46]

The remarks followed Trump's announcement of a blockade on "sanctioned" oil tankers carrying Venezuelan crude, his designation of the government in Caracas as a "foreign terrorist organization," and his claim that Venezuela had stolen US oil and other assets.[46] Representative [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] countered that the assets in question were oil company projects nationalized two decades earlier, a point Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] cited against what he called the "Maduro haters."[46]

## Reported offer to step down (November 2025)

## Alleged 2024 assassination plot arrests

On Saturday, September 14, 2024, Venezuelan authorities arrested three Americans, two Spaniards and a Czech citizen whom officials accused of entering the country to assassinate Maduro.[51] Interior minister [[diosdado-cabello|Diosdado Cabello]] announced the arrests on state television, describing the detainees as part of a CIA-led plot to overthrow the Venezuelan government and kill several members of its leadership, and displaying images of rifles he said had been confiscated from the plotters.[51] Among the Americans was a Navy SEAL identified by Cabello as Wilbert Joseph Castañeda Gomez, said to have served in Afghanistan, Iraq and Colombia.[51]

The [[state-department|US State Department]] confirmed the detention of a US military member and said it was aware of "unconfirmed reports of two additional U.S. citizens detained in Venezuela," while stating that "any claims of U.S. involvement in a plot to overthrow Maduro are categorically false" and that the United States "continues to support a democratic solution to the political crisis in Venezuela."[51] Observers noted the episode's resemblance to the failed 2020 "Bay of Piglets" raid organized by former Green Beret [[jordan-goudreau|Jordan Goudreau]], and to the older pattern of [[operation-condor|Operation Condor]].[51] Reporting also noted that through the summer of 2024 Maduro had been cracking down on "far right" opposition movements he said were backed by the CIA and other external agitators.[51]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] questioned the timing, coming days after the [[2024-trump-golf-course-assassination-attempt|second attempt on Donald Trump's life]], and suggested the animus toward Maduro was connected to the civil actions he brought against roughly 40 oil executives in US court in 2018 — material from which, in Badlands' account, later fed US action against oligarchs worldwide and the indictment of former Glencore CEO [[alex-beard|Alex Beard]].[51]

## July 2024 election dispute

Following Venezuela's July 28, 2024 election, the government-controlled electoral authority proclaimed Maduro the victor on the Monday morning after the vote, and he called his claimed victory "irreversible" despite widespread international doubt.[52] He told the Brazilian administration of [[luiz-inacio-lula-da-silva|Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva]] — which had declined for the time being to congratulate him — that he would soon release the election data with full transparency to the world.[52] At that moment no fewer than nine Latin American countries had yet to recognise his third term, and the [[white-house|White House]] said it was "reviewing" available data, saw "signs" the official results did not reflect the will of the people, and called on the government not to repress protesters.[52]

Maduro's security services detained [[freddy-superlano|Freddy Superlano]], national coordinator of the opposition party [[voluntad-popular|Voluntad Popular]], on Tuesday, July 30, as opposition candidate [[edmundo-gonzalez-urrutia|Edmundo González Urrutia]] continued to insist the election had been stolen.[52] Venezuela's top military leadership and generals in Caracas declared in a televised address that they remained loyal to the Maduro government.[52] Opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] urged him to accept that his exit was inevitable, describing the end of an 11-year rule during which Venezuela fell into a devastating economic and humanitarian crisis that forced millions to flee abroad.[52]

Badlands commentary urged caution about the emerging narrative, arguing that the enthusiasm of "all of the worst people in the world" for Maduro's removal bore the hallmarks of a color revolution, and that it could simultaneously be true that publicising large-scale election fraud is useful and that globalist interests had been moving against Venezuela for years.[52]

## Moscow strategic partnership signing (May 2025)

Maduro travelled to Moscow in early May 2025 for the May 9 Victory Day celebrations marking the 80th anniversary of the Soviet defeat of Nazi Germany, and on Wednesday, May 7 signed a strategic partnership agreement with President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] after talks in the Russian capital.[53] Under the deal, announced by the Kremlin, Moscow and Caracas pledged to oppose unilateral sanctions, violations of the UN Charter and what they termed manifestations of neo-colonialism, and set a long-term course for cooperation across security, counterterrorism, the economy, energy, investment, mineral extraction, transport, telecommunications, healthcare and culture.[53] See [[russia-venezuela-strategic-partnership-law|Russia–Venezuela strategic partnership]].[53]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry cited the trip in addressing whether Maduro is part of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], recalling the "laptop from hell" given to Maduro in 2017 by the scorned ex-wife of one of his oil officials, which he said documented corruption within Maduro's government and among dozens of asset-management executives at firms such as Glencore.[53] He argued that the forty lawsuits Maduro filed in U.S. court in 2018 against those executives, though dismissed, furnished materials used in later prosecutions and civil actions.[53]

## Alleged facilitation of Tren de Aragua migration

A declassified intelligence report titled "Venezuela: Examining Regime Ties to Tren de Aragua," publicised in May 2025, assessed that the socialist regime led by Maduro had "facilitated" the illegal migration of [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] gang members into the United States "to advance what they see as the Maduro regime's goal of destabilizing governments and undermining public safety."[53] Legacy outlets including the New York Times framed the memo as contradicting President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s claims about Venezuelan gang ties, a characterisation the Daily Signal disputed.[53]

## Inheritance and the sanctions decade

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] describes Maduro as [[hugo-chavez|Hugo Chávez]]'s hand-picked successor, a former bus driver and union organizer who never attended university and who lacked his mentor's charisma and battlefield credentials.[54] What he inherited was Chávez's enemies, domestic and foreign, and a global oil market about to crater.[54] Maduro won a narrow, contested election in April 2013, defeating opposition candidate [[henrique-capriles|Henrique Capriles]] by 1.5 percentage points, whereupon the [[state-department|U.S. State Department]] immediately refused to recognize the results.[54] Within months the Obama administration imposed its first sanctions on Venezuelan officials accused of human rights abuses and drug trafficking — targeted measures that would balloon into economy-wide strangulation under Trump.[54]

By 2014 oil prices had collapsed from $110 to under $50 per barrel; [[venezuela|Venezuela]], which derived 96 percent of export revenue from crude, saw its foreign reserves evaporate, and hyperinflation followed, estimated by the [[imf|IMF]] at 1.7 million percent in 2018 alone.[54] The government responded with price controls, currency pegs, and mass importation of subsidized food, policies that accelerated shortages and black markets.[54] DeLarme argues the resulting conditions created fertile ground for the [[cia|CIA]], [[usaid|USAID]], the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|NED]] and the State Department, and that the "nonviolent" street protest waves of 2014, 2017, and 2019 followed a familiar choreography, with NED grantees such as [[foro-penal|Foro Penal]] documenting repression and training local journalists to livestream the chaos.[54]

In an author's note, DeLarme states explicitly that he is not painting Maduro as a good guy, listing human rights abuses, opposition bans, financial corruption, and the facilitation of drug trafficking, while adding that the United States often overlooks such conduct in leaders who are pro-U.S. and pro-NATO.[54]

## Survival through Beijing and Moscow

Despite [[juan-guaido|Juan Guaidó]]'s parallel presidency, the freezing of $7 billion in [[citgo|Citgo]] assets, and the collapse of [[pdvsa|PDVSA]] output to 300,000 barrels per day in 2020, Maduro survived.[54] [[china|Beijing]] restructured $60 billion in loans, accepting delayed oil shipments, while [[russia|Moscow]] refinanced $17 billion in military contracts and dispatched Wagner-linked technicians to guard gold mines in the [[orinoco-mining-arc|Orinoco Belt]].[54] [[rosneft|Rosneft]] prepaid for Venezuelan crude, injecting billions into PDVSA, and Russian mercenaries secured illegal gold mines run by Maduro's inner circle, including the [[cartel-of-the-suns|Cartel of the Suns]], generating off-the-books revenue to pay soldiers and fund repression.[54] DeLarme summarizes the arrangement: "Beijing bought time with oil; Moscow bought loyalty with gold and guns. The opposition had hashtags and headlines. Maduro had hydrocarbons and mercenaries."[54]

As of October 2025, with [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] awarded the Nobel Peace Prize and the second Trump administration tightening sanctions on Russian and Chinese firms operating in Venezuela, Maduro's camp branded her "La Sayona," a vengeful specter from folklore, while loyalist rallies torched her effigies.[54] Machado refuses dialogue unless Maduro agrees to a verified transition, and the regime shows no sign of conceding.[54]
