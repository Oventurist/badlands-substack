# American Eagle Outfitters

American Eagle Outfitters is an American clothing retailer best known for denim apparel. In August 2025 the company became the centre of a culture-war advertising controversy after debuting a divisive commercial starring actress [[sydney-sweeney|Sydney Sweeney]].[1]

## Sweeney advertisement and stock surge

American Eagle's stock price rose nearly 23% as of 3 p.m. EDT on Monday, 4 August 2025, a steep increase that began hours earlier after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted on Truth Social praising the commercial.[1] Trump wrote that the jeans were "flying off the shelves" and added, "Go get 'em Sydney!"[1] He also hailed reports over the preceding weekend that Sweeney was registered as a Republican voter in Florida, and compared her favourably against "Woke singer" [[taylor-swift|Taylor Swift]], whom he had previously said he "hates" after she endorsed Vice President Kamala Harris in October, calling Swift "NO LONGER HOT."[1]

Badlands commentary read the episode as an instance of "cultural whiplash" and as evidence for the proposition that "politics is not downstream from culture; it IS culture," placing the American Eagle campaign in a lineage with earlier brand-and-politics flashpoints such as the Jaguar rebrand and Bud Light.[1]

## Q2 2025 results and campaign performance

By early September 2025 the [[sydney-sweeney|Sydney Sweeney]] campaign had been credited with a nearly 30% increase in American Eagle's stock value along with a substantial rise in sales.[2] The campaign, titled "Sydney Sweeney Has Great Jeans," launched in July 2025 and featured the then-27-year-old "Euphoria" actress.[2]

On the company's second-quarter earnings call, chief marketing officer Craig Brommers said "Sweeney is a winner, and in just six weeks, the campaign has generated unprecedented new customer acquisition."[2] American Eagle reported that products from the Sweeney collaboration, including custom jeans, sold out within a week — some items within a single day — and that her "Syd's Picks" shop had been restocked at least four times, with the brand gaining new customers in every U.S. county.[2] The expansion was supported by a broader marketing strategy that included a collaboration with NFL player Travis Kelce's Tru Kolors brand.[2]
