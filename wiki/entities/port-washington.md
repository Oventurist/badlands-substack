# Port Washington

Port Washington is a lakeside city of roughly 12,000 residents located north of Milwaukee, Wisconsin.[1] In April 2026 it became the focal point of a national dispute over artificial intelligence infrastructure when it prepared to vote on what could be the first municipal ballot referendum in the United States aimed at restricting data center development.[1]

## 2026 data center referendum

The measure before Port Washington voters would require voter approval before city leaders could award lucrative tax incentives to major projects.[1] Its immediate target was a proposed $15 billion, 1.3-gigawatt AI data center campus described as part of the [[project-stargate|"Stargate"]] megaproject backed by [[openai|OpenAI]], [[oracle|Oracle]], and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

The referendum was placed on the ballot by [[great-lakes-neighbors-united|Great Lakes Neighbors United]], a nonprofit formed in October 2025 by about a dozen residents who gathered the roughly 1,000 required signatures in just ten days after connecting on Facebook and protesting at city council meetings.[1]

Companies involved in the project promoted its benefits: 4,000 construction jobs, roughly 1,000 long-term positions, $175 million in local infrastructure upgrades to water mains, sewer lines, a new water tower and power, and a closed-loop cooling system powered by zero-emissions energy sources intended to leave residents' utility bills untouched.[1] Port Washington's mayor warned that the referendum could derail economic revitalization in a former Rust Belt city that has lost industry.[1]

## Context and commentary

Reporting placed the vote within a broader national backlash, with at least three other communities preparing similar data center ballot measures during 2026.[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the opposition was primarily a social media effort whose stated concerns — higher electricity bills, rolling blackouts and increased taxpayer costs — appeared only halfway through Politico's coverage, and that residents appeared to be following the lead of influencers and NGOs such as [[clean-wisconsin|Clean Wisconsin]] and comedian [[charlie-berens|Charlie Berens]] rather than organizing organically.[1] She added that while she supported communities deciding what they allow, images of the towns in the coverage "look abandoned and desperately in need of industry."[1]
