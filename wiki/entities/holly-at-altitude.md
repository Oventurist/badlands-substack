# Holly at Altitude

Holly at Altitude is an independent writer and election-integrity commentator who publishes at hollyataltitude.com and moves in the same orbit as the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] collective.[1]

## The "public trust prenup"

In September 2024, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] credited Holly at Altitude with the idea of a "public trust prenup," a proposal she relayed in the Badlands News Brief and said Americans should seriously consider ahead of the November 2024 election.[1] The concept is simple: audit the election regardless of the outcome, on the reasoning that win or lose, public trust demands elections that are verifiably accurate and therefore demands that they actually be checked.[1] The proposal explicitly excludes risk-limiting audits conducted without oversight by the government — described as the government essentially checking its own work — in favour of independent audits conducted as a matter of course.[1] Ashe in America argued that such audits, applied regardless of who wins, would go a long way toward restoring public trust in the system, if such a restoration is warranted.[1]

The idea was raised alongside coverage of the [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany]]'s decision to investigate an alleged software error and call for an end to [[mail-in-voting|mail-in ballots]] after winning the [[2024-saxony-state-election|Saxony state election]], which Ashe in America presented as the same principle in practice: "audit elections even — especially — if you win."[1]

## Writing on blockchain voting (2023)

On 3 March 2023 Badlands Media published her article "Blockchain, Good. Blockchain Voting, Bad.", subtitled "The Non-technical Case Against Blockchain Voting," which was cross-promoted with her own Substack, Holly at Altitude, at hollyataltitude.substack.com.[2] In it she argued that the push to replace U.S. election infrastructure with [[blockchain-voting]] is quietly gaining momentum through county beta tests and vendor pitches, and that election officials making adoption decisions are woefully uninformed about the technology.[2] She stated that she is personally a big fan of [[blockchain]] technology and confident its potential will be realised, but that its time and place is not a citizen's voting process.[2]

Her case rested on three non-technical grounds — ownership and profitability, security and integration, and centralization and trust — and on the claim that the [[help-america-vote-act|Help Americans Vote Act]] of 2002 created the corrupted, privately owned "U.S. election machine" that any blockchain system would have to integrate with.[2] She published a diagram of that machine, credited to hollyataltitude.substack.com, as part of the article.[2] Her conclusion was that [[blockchain-voting-companies|blockchain voting companies]] are for-profit firms subject to the same corruption risks as incumbent electronic voting vendors, and that "techies tech" — reaching for familiar tools without correctly diagnosing the problem.[2]

## "Rooting Out Election Corruption" (2023)

On March 23, 2023 Badlands Media published her article "Rooting Out Election Corruption," subtitled "What Can We Do to Fix Our Elections?", which was cross-promoted with her Substack, Holly at Altitude.[3] The piece opened with the assertion that the U.S. election system "has never been free from fraud, cheating, or manipulation," and traced the modern problem to the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]] and the creation of the [[election-assistance-commission|U.S. Election Assistance Commission]].[3]

She argued that the winners of the system — companies, government agencies, politicians, the corporate media, NGOs, and an array of power brokers — have convinced the majority of the public that concerns about elections are unfounded "conspiracy theories," using propaganda that distracts, sows confusion and doubt, discredits and personally destroys truth-tellers, and keeps the public focused on an entrenched "fake war" between the establishment Democrat and Republican parties.[3] She connected everyday grievances such as high gas and food prices, inflation, damaged 401(k)s, U.S.-funded foreign wars, pharmaceutical and censorship scandals, open borders, and a public-education crisis to what she called the root cause: a corrupted election system.[3]

Her stated end-goal was to eliminate technology from the election machine: voting on a single day made a national holiday, at local precincts, on hand-marked paper ballots, counted by hand after polls close under live-streaming high-definition cameras, with precinct totals released publicly at the precinct before rolling up to state totals.[3] To advance toward it she proposed five lines of local action — challenging election equipment budgets, challenging equipment contracts for statutory violations, conducting [[canvassing]] using [[useip|USEIP]] materials, forcing constitutional challenges to centralizing election laws and rules, and holding election officials accountable for dereliction of duty or maladministration, including ending the use of tax dollars for membership in associations such as NASS and NASED.[3] She also referenced causeofamerica.org as a source of budgeting help and tools, and closed by urging readers to focus on local action, signing off "Happy hunting!"[3]
