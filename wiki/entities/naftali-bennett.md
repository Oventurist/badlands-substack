# Naftali Bennett

Naftali Bennett is an Israeli opposition leader described in July 2026 Badlands coverage as the favorite to defeat Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] in the upcoming October election.[1]

Bennett describes himself as "ten degrees more right-wing than Netanyahu," while also presenting as a secularist with no religious element to his political platform and no ties or allegiance to any faction in the Ultra-Orthodox community.[1] Although he had years earlier said he would be willing to sign a Two-State Solution to make peace with Palestine, in a recent interview he stated that as prime minister he would never allow Palestine to exist, and would erase it from the map and absorb it into [[israel|Israel]].[1]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited Bennett's position to argue against the belief that Netanyahu personally is the root of Israeli warmongering, concluding that there is no "pro-peace" faction in Israeli politics with any chance of taking power.[1]

## February 2026 remarks

In February 2026 Badlands again identified Bennett as one of the two favorites, alongside [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]], to win the October election for prime minister, both of them opponents of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]].[2] Bennett was quoted as saying that "the world will never love Israel, so it should learn to fear it," a remark [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized as evil and ominous and offered as further evidence that no Israeli electoral outcome would produce a peace-oriented government.[2]

## 2026 election campaign and settler violence

By May 2026, [[jewish-terrorism|Jewish terrorism]] had become a talking point in the upcoming Israeli elections, and Bennett — a prime ministerial candidate — publicly acknowledged the descent into lawlessness across Israel and the rise of terrorism among the settlers, vowing to restore law and order.[3] Badlands presented his remarks as evidence that Israel's own political class could no longer deny the phenomenon, even as knives came out for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[3]

## Alleged mediation role in the Ukraine war

In June 2026 Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Bennett, as Israeli prime minister in 2022, served as mediator between [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Volodymyr Zelensky and most likely orchestrated the deception that led Russian forces to pull back from Kiev — a role previously credited to Britain's Boris Johnson.[4] The claim was advanced alongside [[alexander-lukashenko|Alexander Lukashenko]]'s assertion that a "Jewish lobby" in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] had misled Moscow into expecting its forces would be welcomed as liberators.[4]

## Reaction to the 2026 Iran deal

Bennett was cited again in June 2026 as [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s chief archrival and the favorite to replace him after that year's elections, with his objections to the emerging US–[[iran|Iran]] agreement offered as evidence that most of the Israeli political spectrum is pro-war with Iran.[4]

## June 2026 political platform

On June 18, 2026, Bennett — a Knesset member and prime-ministerial candidate — outlined plans for a new political movement and called for major changes in Israeli leadership and governance, including the establishment of a constitution "in the spirit of the [US] Declaration of Independence."[5] Describing his program should he be elected in October, he said: "We're going to address some of the biggest problems we're facing: cost of living, education, crime, crazy housing costs, our international standing, and security above all."[5]

Bennett said his first order of business in office would be to establish a state commission of inquiry into the events surrounding October 7, 2023, and why the mistakes that led to the massacre were allowed to occur.[5] He also proposed cutting benefits to ultra-Orthodox draft-dodgers and focusing on AI development and innovation.[5] On the economy he argued that "prices are high for one main reason: we don't have real competition. We'll break up monopolies, cut regulation, and open the market to imports" — a statement Badlands read as a reference to the socialist elements still embedded in Israel's founding economic model.[5]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry characterized Bennett as one of the main opposition leaders against President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], noting that Bennett and his team have expressed an intent to restart the war against [[iran|Iran]], making an Israel–Iran war "all but inevitable" in his assessment.[5] Against that backdrop, Trump and his cabinet were described as still ostensibly supporting [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]], whom Badlands framed as Trump's controllable horse in the race.[5]

## "Existential moment" and the Octopus Doctrine (June 2026)

Following the announcement of the U.S.–Iran framework agreement in mid-June 2026, Bennett — identified as an ally of [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] — told The Times of Israel that Israel was facing "an existential moment," warning that another four years under the current government would leave the country without an economy, a society, or international standing.[6] He said that, if elected, he would advance his [[octopus-doctrine|Octopus Doctrine]], a multiyear plan aimed at accelerating the collapse of Iran's regime while ensuring it does not acquire nuclear weapons.[6] He added that his Haredi policy would end state funding for schools that refuse to teach the core curriculum and cancel certain benefits for those who do not serve in the military or work.[6]

## Starlink smuggling disclosure (2026)

In June 2026, a Reuters report quoted Bennett saying that [[israel|Israel]] had smuggled tens of thousands of [[starlink|Starlink]] satellite internet terminals into [[iran|Iran]] to help anti-government protesters maintain internet and social media access for coordination and ultimately to topple the Iranian government.[7] Bennett said he had initiated the effort to acquire and smuggle the systems, but claimed that Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s government halted the program, leaving the infrastructure unavailable during the protests.[7] Starlink, operated by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s SpaceX, is not licensed to operate in Iran, and Iranian authorities have repeatedly shut down public internet access during unrest, including deadly nationwide protests in January 2026 and throughout the war that began at the end of February 2026.[7] Iran had previously accused Israel and the United States of smuggling such devices to undermine its security.[7]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the statement as an admission that Bennett had flooded Iran with receivers so that [[mossad|Mossad]] assets could coordinate a violent overthrow of the Iranian government, and recalled that the same former prime minister had, in 2022, been accused of deceiving Vladimir Putin into pulling back from Kiev on the belief that a genuine ceasefire was at hand.[7] The commentary argued that Bennett's role showed Netanyahu was not the sole warmonger in Israeli politics and that Israeli policy toward Iran would remain confrontational regardless of who won the October election.[7]

## Beyahad and the 2026 election bid

In April 2026 Bennett and [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] announced they would run together to unseat [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], creating a new party named [[beyahad|Beyahad]], meaning "Together," under Bennett's leadership.[8] Lapid called Bennett "a right-wing politician, but an honest one" and said the alliance would unify what he termed the "Repair Bloc."[8] Bennett pledged that if elected he would establish a national commission of inquiry into the failures preceding the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack, an inquiry the sitting government has rejected.[8]

Opinion polls cited in reporting on the announcement suggested Bennett is the opposition figure best positioned to defeat Netanyahu in the election, which must be held no later than the end of October.[8] Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry, who said he had anticipated the merger, argued that the difference from their 2021 co-premiership is that the two have now actually merged their parties onto a single ticket, and predicted the resulting pressure would push Netanyahu deeper into war since his only route to a public mandate lies in expanding [[israel|Israel]]'s borders or defeating [[iran|Iran]].[8]

## April 2025 attack on Netanyahu over the scuttled Iran strike

After reports that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had blocked an Israeli-proposed strike on Iran's nuclear facilities, Bennett — expected to compete with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] for right-wing votes in the next election — implied on Thursday, April 17, 2025 that the premier had himself leaked the stalled plan to The New York Times.[9] "The Netanyahu doctrine is to threaten, threaten, threaten and then leak that he meant to [do something] but was prevented from doing it," Bennett said, calling it "another dangerous conception that mustn't blow up in our faces" and adding that "there won't be another such opportunity [to hit Iran]."[9]

Badlands commentary described Bennett's alignment with Opposition Leader [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] against Netanyahu as a political double envelopment, or pincer move, noting the two are not natural allies.[9] Bennett, a former member of the Sayeret Matkal — Israel's most elite special forces unit, modeled after the British SAS — leads the [[new-right-party|New Right]], founded at the end of 2018 and branded as the "most right-wing" party in Israel in an attempt to supplant [[likud|Likud]], which had expanded to include many moderates and centrists.[9] Running for Prime Minister in 2021, Bennett branded himself as "10% more right-wing than Netanyahu."[9] While drawing Likud's most right-wing factions into the New Right, he rejected the Kahanists of [[otzma-yehudit|Otzma Yehudit]] under [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]], whom Netanyahu was then forced to embrace for political survival.[9]

Bennett and Lapid had served as co-Prime Ministers for 18 months between June 2021 and December 2022 — Bennett for 12 months and Lapid for six, with the other holding the post of Alternate Prime Minister — in an effort to end Netanyahu's political career.[9]
