# Andrew Korybko

Andrew Korybko is a geopolitical analyst who publishes on Substack and writes frequently about Russian strategy and the alternative-media ecosystem.[1]

## Analysis of Russian red lines

In September 2024, Korybko analyzed an interview that Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] gave to Sky News Arabia explaining what Moscow hopes to achieve by talking about its "red lines."[1] Korybko observed that the mainstream media is convinced such lines are meaningless and can be crossed without fear of world war, while the Alt-Media Community reads every such statement as hinting at nuclear retaliation — concluding that both camps are "half-right and half-wrong."[1]

He quoted Lavrov's remark that Western leaders "really seem to have a child's mentality, even though they are adults holding positions of responsibility," and that Russia speaks of red lines "in the hope that our assessments and statements will be heard by clever decision-makers," adding that "no one wants a nuclear war" but that Russia possesses "weapons whose use will involve grave consequences for the masters of the Ukrainian regime."[1] Korybko recalled that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] famously described NATO's expansion into Ukraine as crossing a Russian red line in his February 24, 2022 speech announcing the special military operation.[1]

Badlands Media's Burning Bright read Lavrov's language as brushing against Devolution framing without crossing into it, noting the reference to "decision-makers" in the United States working to avert the war the Globalist Hegemon is trying to provoke, and concluding that "war will NOT happen, but the STORY of the war is inevitable."[1]

## Analysis of Russia–Taliban strategic partnership (May 2024)

In late May 2024 Korybko published "Russia Is Preparing To Strategically Partner With The Taliban," arguing that Moscow's push to remove the [[taliban|Taliban]]'s terrorist designation and invite it to the following month's investment forum was driven by the desire to reach a strategic energy deal with Pakistan that would complete Russia's "Ummah Pivot" and Greater Eurasian Partnership.[2] He documented a cluster of May 2024 signals — a Russian diplomat declaring the Taliban no longer an enemy, an expansion of Afghan exports to Russia, an FSB assessment that the Taliban could stabilize Afghanistan on its own, an invitation to the St. Petersburg International Economic Forum, and a ministerial proposal to [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to delist the group.[2]

Korybko noted that the Taliban remained international outcasts over their failure to build an inclusive government and their treatment of women, but that economic and security interests had pushed regional stakeholders into de facto relations, with Russia furthest ahead and now viewing the group as a regional security provider containing [[isis-k|ISIS-K]].[2] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the piece as evidence of [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] dynamics and of the continuing effects of the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]].[2]
