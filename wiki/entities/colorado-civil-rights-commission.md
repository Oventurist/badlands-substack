# Colorado Civil Rights Commission

The Colorado Civil Rights Commission is the Colorado state government agency responsible for enforcing the state's anti-discrimination laws, including the provisions barring businesses from denying services on the basis of a customer's sexual orientation. In the Badlands corpus the commission appears as the enforcement body at the center of a series of religious-liberty disputes that reached the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[1]

## Role in religious-liberty litigation

Baker [[jack-phillips|Jack Phillips]] of [[masterpiece-cakeshop|Masterpiece Cakeshop]] has been contesting Colorado's enforcement of its public-accommodations law since 2012, asserting a right to refuse to participate in ceremonies that violate his religious beliefs. Commentary in the July 3, 2023 Badlands News Brief noted that although Phillips prevailed at the Supreme Court, he continued to face legal action from the commission, described as a government agency.[1]

A second Colorado case, brought by graphic designer [[lorie-smith|Lorie Smith]] and decided as [[303-creative-llc-v-elenis|303 Creative LLC v. Elenis]], produced another Supreme Court ruling upholding [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] protections for religious objectors in June 2023.[1]

## Criticism

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that "Colorado courts are friendly to persecuting Christians for their wrong-think beliefs," and contended that the state's government and captured courts had demonstrated that unfavorable appellate outcomes had no practical impact on their ability to pursue dissenters, regardless of what the Supreme Court ruled.[1]
