# Russia Today (RT)

Russia Today, commonly known as RT, is a Russian state-backed media company that became the centerpiece of a United States [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] enforcement action announced on September 4, 2024.[1]

## 2024 indictment

A federal indictment unsealed that Wednesday alleged that employees of RT funneled nearly $10 million to [[tenet-media|Tenet Media]], a Tennessee-based media company, as part of "a scheme to create and distribute content to U.S. audiences with hidden Russian government messaging."[1] Two RT employees — Kostiantyn "Kostya" Kalashnikov and Elena "Lena" Afanasyeva — were named as having worked to move the money as part of a series of "covert projects" aimed at shaping narratives within Western audiences.[1] The indictment stated that the influencers who appeared on the network's US-facing front company were deceived about the source of the funds.[1]

## Context within the wider action

The RT indictments were one component of a broader package of measures announced by the Biden administration on the same day, which also included sanctions on ten individuals and entities and the seizure of 32 internet domains, framed as a response to Russian efforts to influence the 2024 US presidential election.[1] Deputy Attorney General [[lisa-monaco|Lisa Monaco]] said that, at the direction of Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], three Russian companies used fake profiles to promote false narratives on social media.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the "Russia rerun" was notable for the regime's insistence on the word "influence" rather than vote manipulation, pointing to [[cnn|CNN]]'s own caveat that "there is no evidence of successful efforts — foreign or domestic — to swing an American election by changing vote tallies."[1] In this reading, the accusation amounted to a claim that Russia and China were deploying narratives judged to have a measurable effect on public opinion, prompting the question of whether truth itself was the perceived threat.[1]
