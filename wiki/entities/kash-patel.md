# Kash Patel

**Kash Patel** is a close ally of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] frequently cited within Badlands Media commentary as a source of advance signalling about Trump's intentions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/all-assets-deployed)

## Role in the 2022 announcement

Ahead of Trump's November 2022 candidacy announcement, Patel and other close allies projected that the announcement would "light the media world on fire."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/all-assets-deployed) Badlands commentary judged that it had done exactly that — though, in the author's reading, not because the announcement itself was unexpected, but because [[con-inc|Con Inc.]] and the "DeSantis over Trump" faction would seize on a demoralized MAGA base and attempt to widen hairline fractures into open division.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/all-assets-deployed)

## Defamation lawsuit against The Atlantic (2026)

In April 2026 Patel sought to compel testimony from [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] and one of its journalists as part of an ongoing defamation lawsuit, according to [[cnn|CNN]].[2] Patel's legal team argued that communications and reporting tied to the outlet are central to his claims, and sought materials and testimony they believed could support his case.[2] The lawsuit raised questions about the scope of discovery and whether journalists can be required to disclose sources or internal reporting processes; The Atlantic and its reporter pushed back, citing press-freedom protections and reporter privilege, arguing that forced disclosure would undermine newsgathering and source confidentiality.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that while the First Amendment is "a hill to die on," defamation remains a reviewable legal domain, and that for a public official it requires actual malice — knowingly false statements shared to cause harm.[2] She noted that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] won $15 million from ABC in 2024 and $16 million from CBS in 2025 in defamation cases, and argued that truth is an absolute defense but must be provable, concluding that Patel's suit appeared poised to pierce a "Smith-Mundtian shield" for the fake news.[2]

## Arctic Frost disclosures (2026)

By February 2026 Patel was serving as Director of the [[fbi|FBI]].[3] On a Wednesday in late February he disclosed that the Bureau had subpoenaed his own phone records and those of White House Chief of Staff [[susie-wiles|Susie Wiles]] during the Biden administration, while both were private citizens, as part of the [[arctic-frost|Arctic Frost]] investigation; Reuters reported that the records were found in files labeled "Prohibited."[3]

Congressional oversight disclosures indicated that hundreds of Trump-aligned individuals and groups, including sitting members of [[congress|Congress]], were targeted with subpoenas and data requests under the same investigation.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the collection as political targeting and weaponization of government, writing that "the hunted have become the hunters."[3]

## FBI SCIF records cache (2026)

In March 2026, Freedom of Information Act litigation over as many as 1.9 million pages of [[fbi|FBI]] records held in a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility at bureau headquarters expanded to seek internal FBI communications involving Patel as Director and former Deputy Director [[dan-bongino|Dan Bongino]] regarding the discovery of the cache, together with directives on how the materials were handled.[4] The bureau told the court it would need 10 to 12 months to digitize and review the records, a timeline Badlands commentary read as institutional obstruction under Patel's tenure.[4] See [[fbi-scif-records-cache|FBI SCIF records cache]].[4]

## ISIS plot arrests (March 2026)

Commenting on the March 2026 charges against two [[isis|ISIS]] supporters accused of attempting to detonate explosive devices in a crowd, Patel said the defendants "allegedly support ISIS and tried to follow the path of that deadly group," and that "the [[fbi|FBI]] and our partners have no tolerance for terrorist organizations or those inspired by them to engage in attacks."[5] He said the Bureau was committed to stopping acts of violence and holding perpetrators accountable, and commended NYPD officers who acted to prevent injuries or loss of life on the streets of New York.[5]

## Swalwell file declassification push (2026)

Patel pressed to release a decade-old investigative file involving Democratic Rep. [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] of California, despite no public evidence of wrongdoing tied to the lawmaker.[8] He directed agents in the [[fbi|FBI]]'s San Francisco field office to accelerate redaction of the documents in preparation for possible public disclosure; the file concerns Swalwell's past contacts with [[christine-fang|Christine Fang]], investigated years earlier as a suspected Chinese intelligence operative.[8] Current and former officials said the push raised concerns within the bureau about departing from standard FBI practice and politicizing investigative records, given that the prior examination produced no charges.[8]

## Removal as acting ATF director (April 2025)

In April 2025 the Trump administration removed Patel as interim head of the [[bureau-of-alcohol-tobacco-firearms-and-explosives|Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives]], replacing him with Secretary of the Army [[dan-driscoll|Dan Driscoll]], according to two sources familiar with the decision who spoke on condition of anonymity.[15] Patel had held an unusual dual-hat arrangement, serving simultaneously as FBI director and acting ATF director, and Driscoll would likewise hold the top roles at both the ATF and the Army.[15] The people familiar with the change said they had not been informed why the administration made it, and a spokesman for the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]], which oversees the ATF, did not immediately respond to a request for comment.[15]

Patel expressed frustration at the strain on Bureau resources, asking, "Do you know how many agents I've had to take offline from chasing down child sex predators, fentanyl traffickers, terrorists?"[20] He suggested the post had inspired people across the country to make threats in the belief that such behavior was permissible because Comey had done it.[20] The Bureau did not provide specific details about the threats; the disclosure came less than a year after a gunman's bullet struck Trump's ear at a campaign event in Pennsylvania.[20]

## Nomination as FBI Director (November 2024)

On Saturday, November 30, 2024, President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that he would nominate Patel, then 44 years old and with little management experience in federal law enforcement, to serve as director of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]].[24] Writing on Truth Social, Trump called Patel "a brilliant lawyer, investigator, and 'America First' fighter who has spent his career exposing corruption, defending Justice, and protecting the American People," and credited him with a pivotal role in uncovering what Trump called "the Russia, Russia, Russia Hoax."[24] Patel, a former federal prosecutor in Washington and public defender in Florida, said in a statement that the nomination was "the honor of a lifetime" and pledged to "restore integrity, accountability, and equal justice to our justice system."[24]

NBC News described Patel as an extreme Trump loyalist who had spread "deep state" claims and called for a purge of perceived Trump enemies from the Bureau, and quoted a former senior law enforcement official who said he was not qualified for the position.[24] The nomination, which required Senate confirmation, was expected to renew pressure on Senate Republicans who had earlier signaled they would reject [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] as attorney general.[24]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the pick as the fulfillment of a 2018 intelligence drop that instructed followers to remember Patel's name, noting the reference arose in the context of [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] pressure on congressional review of Russiagate origins.[24] Citing Fox News reporting on emails memorializing a January 2018 closed-door meeting between senior FBI and DOJ officials and members of the House Intelligence Committee, Badlands recounted the claim that Deputy Attorney General Rod Rosenstein threatened to subpoena the committee's records and emails, with Patel reportedly recording that "we [DOJ] too [are] litigators."[24] Burning Bright summarized the appointment with the question "Who watches the Watchmen? Who investigates the investigators?" answering, "Kash and company."[24] Badlands also argued that the [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] pardon of December 1, 2024 granted Patel and the incoming DOJ "narrative shielding" to pursue investigations without effective accusations of weaponizing justice.[24]

In the same brief, contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Trump had sequenced his announcements deliberately, naming overt Zionist appointees early and holding the Patel "white pill" until later so that supporters would still scrutinize the surrounding picks.[24]

## ATF reorganization plan (2025)

As FBI Director and concurrent acting director of the [[bureau-of-alcohol-tobacco-firearms-and-explosives|Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives]], Patel outlined plans in March 2025 to move as many as 1,000 ATF agents to the [[fbi|FBI]], a shift that would cut the ATF's agent corps by more than a third, according to three people briefed on the plan.[27] The move represented a major cutback of an agency long targeted by gun-rights groups that regard its work as an infringement on Second Amendment rights; the ATF has roughly 2,600 agents and more than 5,000 employees, figures that had remained largely unchanged for years.[27] The plan was expected to begin with the reassignment of a couple hundred ATF agents to border-related criminal enforcement duty as FBI agents.[27] After publication of the reporting and resulting pushback, including from Republican allies, FBI officials began backing off aspects of the plan, and an ATF spokeswoman disputed the characterization, stating that approximately 150 agents would be temporarily assigned from existing field offices to other ATF field offices, where they would continue serving as ATF agents in support of a border surge initiative.[27]

## Reported consideration for FBI or DOJ post (November 2024)
