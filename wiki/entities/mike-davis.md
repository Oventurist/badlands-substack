# Mike Davis

Mike Davis is an American attorney and legal activist who previously served as Chief Counsel for the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]] and who became a prominent advocate for confirming President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second-term nominees.[1]

## Advocacy for Kash Patel

In December 2024, as incoming California Senator [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] publicly demanded that the Senate reject [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]]'s nomination to lead the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]], the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] cited Davis as having "damn near guaranteed" Patel's confirmation.[1] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] pointed to Davis's assurance as grounds for dismissing the threat posed by Schiff's opposition.[1]

## Response to the April 2025 Supreme Court order

Davis, who leads the [[article-iii-project|Article III Project]], reacted sharply to the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court's]] April 19, 2025 order blocking removals of Venezuelan detainees under the [[alien-enemies-act|Alien Enemies Act]].[2] Describing himself as a persistent reminder that the courts' legitimacy depends on public belief in their ability to deliver justice, he wrote: "This is shameful lawlessness and cowardice from the conservative justices. You expect this nonsense from the three leftists. Are they trying to destroy the federal judiciary's legitimacy?"[2] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called him her "favorite legal shitposter."[2]

## Summary of the Tina Peters case (March 2025)

In March 2025, following the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s announcement that it would review the case, the Article III Project's Mike Davis published a summary of [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]]'s saga on X.[3] He wrote that Peters, then the Mesa County, Colorado clerk and recorder, investigated voter fraud after the 2021 Grand Junction municipal election, and that Colorado Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]], Attorney General [[phil-weiser|Phil Weiser]], and a local District Attorney, Dan Rubenstein, brought what he called trumped-up felony charges falsely accusing her of unauthorized access to voting machines.[3] Davis alleged that during the same period Griswold allowed voting-machine passwords to remain publicly posted online for four months, and that Judge [[matthew-barrett|Matthew Barrett]] stated on the record that he sentenced the 69-year-old Peters to nine years in prison because of her First Amendment-protected views on election integrity, concluding that Peters was suffering greatly in prison because of "these sadistic partisans."[3]
