# F-35 Lightning II

The F-35 is an advanced American fifth-generation fighter jet that the United States sells primarily to formal military allies, such as NATO members and Japan.[1] Within the Middle East, [[israel|Israel]] has historically been the only country to operate the aircraft, a fact tied to the long-standing U.S. policy of preserving Israel's [[qualitative-military-edge|qualitative military edge]] (QME), which Congress codified into law in 2008.[1]

## Proposed sale to Saudi Arabia

On November 17, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would approve the sale of F-35s to [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], which would make the kingdom the first Middle Eastern state other than Israel to obtain the jets.[1] "They want to buy. They are a great ally. We will be doing that. We will be selling them F-35s," Trump told reporters in the Oval Office.[1] The announcement came a day before Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] was scheduled to meet Trump at the White House, where an F-35 deal, a U.S.–Saudi security agreement, and possible Saudi normalization with Israel were all on the agenda.[1]

Supplying F-35s to Riyadh would alter the regional military balance and affect Israel's QME, which administrations of both U.S. parties have worked to maintain.[1] Israel did not oppose the sale outright but asked the Trump administration to condition it on Saudi normalization of relations with Israel.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that national security narratives for weeks had framed the jets as an inducement to coax Mohammed bin Salman into abandoning the Palestinian statehood issue, but argued that the crown prince would not compromise on Palestine for material gain.[1] He characterized Trump's willingness to provide the F-35s despite Israeli QME concerns as a significant signal of the president's alignment with Arab states over Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.[1]

## 2023 South Carolina mishap

In September 2023 a Marine Corps F-35B Lightning II went missing over South Carolina after its pilot ejected during a "mishap," prompting a public appeal for help locating the roughly $80 million aircraft and, on September 18, the discovery of a debris field in Williamsburg County near [[joint-base-charleston|Joint Base Charleston]].[2] The pilot survived in stable condition and the cause remained unexplained; the episode is covered at [[2023-f-35b-disappearance-in-south-carolina|2023 F-35B disappearance in South Carolina]].[2]
