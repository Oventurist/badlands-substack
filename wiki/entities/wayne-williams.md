# Wayne Williams

Wayne Williams is a Colorado elections official and politician, a former Colorado Secretary of State who later served as Colorado Springs City Councilman at Large. He became a focus of election-integrity researchers because of his simultaneous role as a senior advisor to the election services vendor [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]].[1]

## Secretary of State tenure and Dominion

As Colorado Secretary of State, Williams mandated the use of [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion]] voting equipment in the state before losing that battle in court.[1]

## Runbeck advisory role and conflict-of-interest allegations

In August 2021, Colorado voters learned that Williams — then Colorado Springs City Councilman at Large, and the former Secretary of State who had mandated Dominion — was also a senior advisor at Runbeck Election Services.[1] The connection was discovered by a citizen journalist who found his business card at the 2021 annual conference of the National Association of Secretaries of State.[1]

Commentary in the Badlands News Brief noted that Williams's wife, as a county commissioner, held statutory authority over El Paso County's Runbeck contracts, and that she recused herself from the vote on Runbeck funding only after the public discovered the conflict.[1] The commentary, attributed to [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], argued that recusal after the fact was insufficient to prevent the appearance of impropriety — the standard by which public trust is judged and damaged — and that similar "incestuous" appearances of impropriety recur throughout the election vendor ecosystem nationwide.[1]
