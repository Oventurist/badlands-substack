# Martin Mayer

Martin Mayer was the author of *The Greatest-Ever Bank Robbery: The Collapse of the Savings and Loan Industry*, a book about the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan crisis]] praised by Entertainment Weekly and by Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]].[1]

Mayer's most quoted line — "Deposit insurance has proved to be the crack cocaine of American finance" — was described as shedding more light on the S&L disaster than any of the half-dozen books written about the scandal.[1] While he retold the standard horror stories of [[charles-keating|Charles Keating]] defrauding little old ladies, crooked Texas daisy chains and self-dealing loans, his book was the first to concentrate on the forest rather than the trees.[1] If the trees were the crooks who took over the industry in the 1980s, the forest was Washington, D.C., where a combination of naiveté, ineptitude, bad faith and cravenness made crookedness both likely and inevitable.[1] Government [[deregulation|deregulation]] of a once closely regulated industry was bad enough, but raising federal [[deposit-insurance|deposit insurance]] on individual accounts from $40,000 to $100,000 in 1980 guaranteed that the new "entrepreneurial" owners of S&Ls took no risk gambling with depositors' money; the government became the ultimate safety net and the industry a giant Ponzi scheme.[1]
