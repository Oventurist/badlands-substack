# Haredi Judaism

The Haredi — Orthodox and Hasidic Jews — constitute a religious community in Israel that has enjoyed an exemption from military conscription since the founding of the modern State of Israel.[1]

## Draft exemption dispute

The Israeli Supreme Court ruled to nullify the Haredi draft exemption, creating a politically explosive enforcement question for whichever minister holds the defense portfolio. Badlands Media commentary noted that [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] would likely have been more willing than [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] to enforce the ruling, and that Sa'ar would therefore have been the more favorable pick among [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] generals.[1]

By elevating Katz to the defense ministry in November 2024, [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] installed a minister judged far less likely to compel Haredi service — despite IDF generals stating they were desperate for soldiers. Badlands commentary raised the possibility that this could strain relations between the political leadership and the general staff.[1]

## Global day of prayer and fasting (August 2025)

In August 2025, ultra-Orthodox leaders called for an international day of prayer and fasting on Thursday in response to Israeli government efforts to conscript ultra-Orthodox yeshiva students and arrest those who ignored orders to enlist.[2] Rabbi [[dov-lando|Dov Lando]], spiritual leader of [[degel-hatorah|Degel HaTorah]] — one of the factions of the Haredi [[united-torah-judaism|United Torah Judaism]] party — issued a public appeal to Jews around the world to treat the day as Yom Kippur Katan, a minor fast day on which worshipers recite prayers usually read on the Day of Atonement.[2]

The party newspaper Yated Neeman declared on its front page that Haredi Jews in the United States and Europe were "preparing to tear open the gates of heaven to overturn the conscription decree that threatens the Torah world," quoting Lando as saying that "Jews are persecuting Jews because they are learning Torah."[2] Badlands commentary described the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] generals and the Haredim as engaged in a game of chicken, with the military seeking to conscript between 54,000 and 80,000 young Haredi men while some Haredi leaders threatened extreme economic warfare against Israel and potentially violence against the IDF.[2]
