# Phyllis Fong

Phyllis Fong is an American government oversight official who served as inspector general of the U.S. [[department-of-agriculture|Department of Agriculture]], a post she held for 22 years before her removal in the opening weeks of the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1]

## Removal from office

The [[white-house|White House]] terminated Fong on Friday, January 24, 2025, as part of a broader wave of inspector general firings. Fong told colleagues that she intended to remain in her position because she did not believe the administration had followed proper protocols in dismissing her.[1] In an email to colleagues sent the following Saturday and reviewed by Reuters, she wrote that the independent Council of the Inspectors General on Integrity and Efficiency "has taken the position that these termination notices do not comply with the requirements set out in law and therefore are not effective at this time."[1]

On Monday, January 27, 2025, security agents escorted Fong out of her office after she refused to comply with the firing, according to sources familiar with the matter cited by Reuters.[1] Fong declined to comment and the Office of the Inspector General did not respond to multiple requests for comment.[1] After Reuters published its account, a USDA spokesperson disputed the characterization, saying Fong left the office of her own accord and that "she was accompanied by two friends who she paused to take selfies with on her way out. Security officials did not play any role in her departure."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the episode as Trump's first major efficiency-focused move consisting of going to war with the officials originally tasked with rooting out fraud, waste, and abuse in government, invoking the question "who watches the Watchmen?" and describing the inspector general purge as "glorious signal."[1]
