# Kenneth J. Zucker

Kenneth J. Zucker is a Canadian psychologist and researcher in the field of childhood [[gender-dysphoria|gender dysphoria]]. He is identified in Badlands Media coverage as one of three researchers — with Devita Singh and [[susan-bradley|Susan J. Bradley]] — who conducted the long-running [[toronto-desistance-study|Toronto desistance study]] out of the [[centre-for-addiction-and-mental-health|Centre for Addiction and Mental Health]] in Toronto, Ontario.[1]

The study, begun in 1975 and following 139 birth-assigned males referred to the clinic between the ages of three and twelve, was described by the authors as reporting follow-up data on the largest sample to date of boys clinic-referred for gender dysphoria.[1] Its central finding, published decades later, was that 17 participants (12.2%) were classified as persisters while the remaining 122 (87.8%) were classified as desisters.[1]

Badlands contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] cited the work of Zucker and his colleagues as evidence that should have halted the spread of the [[gender-affirming-care|gender affirmative model]] in North America, arguing that its suppression in the Canadian and American medical and academic communities is best explained as information warfare.[1]
