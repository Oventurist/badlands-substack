# American Psychoanalytic Association

The American Psychoanalytic Association is the professional body founded in 1911 to organize the practice of [[psychoanalysis]] in the United States.[1]

Its founding was organized by [[james-jackson-putnam|James Jackson Putnam]], Professor of Diseases of the Nervous System at [[harvard-university|Harvard]], together with fellow neurologist [[ernest-jones|Ernest Jones]], using the work of [[sigmund-freud|Sigmund Freud]]. Putnam was elected president and Jones secretary.[1] Putnam's earlier public endorsement of Freud's work is described as the significant breakthrough that made the psychoanalytic cause viable in America.[1]

Badlands Media commentary counts the association's creation among the institutional developments of the 1910s that, in its telling, established mechanisms of control over the American public — in this case control exercised at the unconscious level, a line of influence that ran onward through Freud's nephew [[edward-bernays|Edward Bernays]] into public relations and advertising.[1]
