# Pope Francis

Pope Francis was the head of the Roman Catholic Church and sovereign of Vatican City whose appointments in the final weeks before the second inauguration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were read by Badlands Media as a deliberate exercise of institutional power.[1]

## McElroy appointment

In January 2025 Francis appointed Cardinal [[robert-mcelroy|Robert McElroy]] — a vocal critic of Trump's immigration policies and an advocate of the "radical inclusion" of LGBTQ church members — as the next archbishop of Washington, D.C., at a moment when the Catholic Church was described as splintering between its liberal and conservative factions.[1]

The appointment was made after Trump had tapped [[brian-burch|Brian Burch]], a Francis critic who leads the right-wing advocacy group [[catholicvote|CatholicVote]], as his nominee for ambassador to the Vatican. Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the sequence as a "Papal flex" and grouped it with Washington, D.C. and the City of London as one of three "city states" simultaneously in the headlines two weeks before Inauguration Day, all of the relevant stories turning on power dynamics and signaling a shift.[1]

## Badlands framing

In the Badlands reading, "city state chaos" was not about Trump personally but about the movement he built and the unfulfilled promise of "giving it back to you, the people," with the claim that the shadow controls were already defeated even though the stories of those battles had not yet been told.[1]

## Health crisis (February 2025)

Francis was admitted to Rome's Agostino Gemelli Polyclinic Hospital with a respiratory tract infection on 14 February 2025.[2] Early the following week the Vatican disclosed that he had double pneumonia, and on Saturday 22 February it acknowledged that the 88-year-old pontiff — who had one lung removed in his youth — was in "critical condition," having suffered a "prolonged asthmatic crisis" and a low platelet count.[2] He was reported to be alert and sitting in an armchair but in more pain than the previous day.[2]

Doctors treated the respiratory crisis with additional oxygen and his thrombocytopenia, a deficiency of the platelets that clot blood, with transfusions.[2] The Vatican said on Sunday that the pope had slept well following the crisis.[2] In the written text of his regular Angelus prayer that Sunday, Francis reflected on what was described as the most serious medical crisis of his papacy, saying he was "confidently continuing" his extended hospitalization while acknowledging that "rest is also part of the therapy."[2]

Badlands commentator Paul Fleuret summarised the situation bluntly — "Pope has pneumonia. Pope is entering kidney failure" — and remarked ironically on the coincidence of a film on papal succession having been released the previous year.[2]

## Hospitalization (February 2025)

Pope Francis was admitted to the Agostino Gemelli Hospital in Rome on Friday, February 14, 2025 following a bout of bronchitis, and was subsequently treated for double pneumonia along with signs of mild renal insufficiency described as under control.[3] The Holy See Press Office issued twice-daily statements to keep the faithful informed, reporting on Wednesday, February 26 that "the Pope had a quiet night and is resting," that he sat in his armchair upon waking to continue the same oxygen therapy as previous days, and that an evening update might include results of a pulmonary CT scan carried out the day before.[3] Badlands commentator Chris Paul reacted skeptically to the drip-feed of updates, asking whether the Pope was being "Ruth Bader Ginsburged" or "Jimmy Cartered."[3]


## Death (April 2025)

Pope Francis died on April 21, 2025 at the age of 88, one day after making an Easter Sunday appearance at St. Peter's Square where he offered well-wishes to thousands of Catholic supporters; the [[vatican|Vatican]] said he died following a stroke.[4] Born Jorge Mario Bergoglio, he had been elected in 2013 after Benedict XVI became the first pope in roughly 600 years to resign, and he was the first Latin American pontiff and TIME's 2013 Person of the Year.[4] Over his pontificate he was known for personal humility and repeated calls for peace during the COVID-19 pandemic and the Gaza humanitarian crisis.[4] His death opened the interregnum, or *sede vacante*, and set in motion the [[papal-conclave|conclave]] process.[4] Badlands commentary surrounding the death noted that [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] had visited Francis only hours before the announcement, recalled Archbishop Vigano's scathing criticism of Bergoglio, and revisited the 2017 conflict between the pope and the Knights of Malta; [[donald-trump|President Trump]] ordered flags lowered worldwide.[4]

### February 2025 double pneumonia hospitalization
Francis, 88, was rushed to hospital on Friday, February 14, 2025 and was subsequently diagnosed with pneumonia in both lungs.[5] Reports in February 2025 said he had warned aides he might not survive the illness and was preparing his legacy, with two people close to the pontiff claiming he told them he "might not make it this time."[5] Despite the Vatican previously describing his condition as stable, the reports suggested he feared he would soon die and was "tying up loose ends" ahead of his successor; aides said he was suffering intense pain and was completely at the mercy of "doctors' orders," and that he was distressed at being barred from delivering his regular morning Angelus sermon, which he had rarely missed even when hospitalized.[5]

The Vatican's messaging was mixed: it warned that a longer hospital stay was needed in the face of a "complex clinical picture" involving a "polymicrobial infection of the respiratory tract," then offered hope by saying his condition was "stable" and that he did not have a fever, even as all of his engagements were cancelled.[5] Badlands commentary questioned whether there had been proof of life, invoking the notion of a "CGI Pope" and comparing the situation to public doubts raised over Joe Biden, Jimmy Carter and Ruth Bader Ginsburg.[5]

## Gaza remarks and Israeli protest (December 2024)

Ahead of Christmas 2024 Pope Francis renewed his call for a ceasefire in Gaza and highlighted the civilian death toll from Israeli airstrikes, saying "This is cruelty. This is not war. I wanted to say this because it touches the heart."[6] The previous month Vatican News had cited the pontiff as writing in a forthcoming book that allegations of genocide committed by the Israeli army against the Palestinians "should be carefully investigated."[6] In response to the remarks, the [[vatican|Vatican]]'s ambassador Archbishop Adolfo Tito Yllana was summoned to the Israeli Foreign Ministry for a conversation with Director General Eyal Bar-Tal, who condemned the pope's comments without formally reprimanding the nuncio, according to Israeli media reports.[6] Badlands contributor [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the summons an act of hubris, noting that condemning civilian deaths in a kinetic conflict is standard practice for the papacy, and contrasted the episode with a Christmas message from [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] that he said promised more war.[6]

## Audience with Bill Clinton and Alex Soros (July 2023)

On Wednesday, July 5, 2023, Pope Francis met former U.S. President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] in a private audience at the Casa Santa Marta papal residence.[8] Clinton's delegation included several prominent Americans, among them [[alex-soros|Alex Soros]], the new chairman of the [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]] founded by his father [[george-soros|George Soros]].[8] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] mocked the gathering, quoting the remark that "it's crazy that they didn't all spontaneously burst into flames," and drew attention to the Vatican's separate welcome of an artist known for submerging a crucifix in a vat of urine as an example of the pontificate's cultural posture.[8]

## Bilateral pneumonia (February 2025)

On February 18, 2025, the Vatican announced that the 88-year-old pontiff had developed pneumonia in both lungs and that his condition remained "complex."[7] He had been suffering from a respiratory infection for more than a week and was admitted to Rome's Gemelli hospital on Friday, February 14.[7] The Vatican said a follow-up chest CT scan that afternoon "demonstrated the onset of bilateral pneumonia, which required additional drug therapy," and that laboratory tests, a chest X-ray and his clinical condition continued to present a complex picture.[7]

Despite this, the Vatican reported that the Pope remained in "good spirits" and spent the day "reading, resting and praying," expressing gratitude to well-wishers and asking them to "pray for him."[7] He had had bronchitis symptoms for several days before admission and had delegated officials to read prepared speeches at events.[7] He had been due to lead several weekend events for the 2025 Catholic Holy Year, which runs to the following January, but all public engagements on his calendar were cancelled through the Sunday.[7] Badlands commentary joked that he had "survived worse," recalling claims that he had once appeared to vanish from public view, and predicted that pneumonia would not take him out.[7]
