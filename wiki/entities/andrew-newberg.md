# Andrew Newberg

**Dr. Andrew Newberg** is described in the April 26, 2025 Badlands Media feature [[the-emergence|"The Emergence"]] as a researcher who "studies the neurobiology of spiritual experience."[1]

The essay, by [[observing-consciousness|Observing Consciousness]], reports Newberg's finding that during moments of deep contemplative practice the parts of the brain responsible for self-identification and spatial orientation go quiet, so that "the illusion of separation fades."[1] The author invokes the result to argue that the state under discussion "isn't metaphor, it's measurable."[1]

Newberg's research appears in the essay's "Crossing into Embodied Truth" section, where it supports the claim that [[coherence|coherence]] is physiological, chemical and electromagnetic as well as emotional, and is followed immediately by the physicist [[carlo-rovelli|Carlo Rovelli]]'s formulation that "we are not things, we are events."[1]

## "The Liminal" (April 2025)

Newberg is cited again in the earlier April 5, 2025 Badlands Media feature "The Liminal," where he is described as a researcher who studies the neurological basis of spiritual experience.[2] That essay reports his finding that such states can deactivate the parts of the brain responsible for ego and spatial orientation.[2] The author applies the result to the [[liminality|liminal]] passage, arguing that a person in that state is "literally losing your sense of self, time, and place - not as a loss, but as a gateway into a more expansive state of being."[2] The citation sits within the essay's neuropsychological framing of liminality, immediately after [[victor-turner|Victor Turner]]'s definition of the threshold as "a sacred disorientation" and before [[shamini-jain|Shamini Jain]] on energetic recalibration.[2]

## "Transcending Ego" (April 2025)

Newberg appears a third time in the April 12, 2025 Badlands Media feature "Transcending Ego: Meeting the Mirror at the Threshold," where he is described as "a neuroscientist who researches spiritual experience."[3] The essay reports his explanation that during states of deep presence the brain areas associated with the sense of self become less active, and stresses that this does not leave a void but "allows something larger to enter, a state often described as unity."[3] The finding is deployed at the hinge of the essay's argument for [[ego-transcendence|ego transcendence]], supporting its claim that boundaries dissolve, duality fades and paradox becomes natural in a condition the author calls coherence and embodied truth.[3]
