# Stepan Bandera

Stepan Bandera was a Ukrainian nationalist leader and, with [[andriy-melnyk|Andriy Melnyk]], a founder of the [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists]] (OUN) in 1929. As described in the Badlands Brief edition "[[badlands-brief-3d4|Canadian Separatists & Guerilla Campaigns]]," Bandera was born an Austro-Hungarian citizen of German/Cossack descent in the western regions of modern [[ukraine|Ukraine]], territories that had belonged to the Austro-Hungarian (Hapsburg) Empire and the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth rather than the Russian Empire.

## Contested memory

Bandera is venerated by many Ukrainians as an anti-Soviet independence fighter, while Israeli and Polish critics point to documented collaboration between OUN factions and Nazi Germany, including participation in atrocities against Jews and Poles. [[israel|Israel]] and Poland jointly condemned state-sponsored glorification of Bandera and Melnyk as early as 2020, a dispute revived in May 2026 by Ukraine's state reburial ceremony for Melnyk.

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the homogeneous ethno-state Bandera and Melnyk aspired to create was never achievable given the extraordinary ethnic and cultural diversity of the Ukrainian lands. He noted that the OUN's predecessor, the [[ukrainian-military-organization|Ukrainian Military Organization]] (UVO), was founded in 1920 and operated in secret out of Prague using terrorism to advance its aims, and that the OUN "immediately allied with the Nazis in the 1930's and began running pogroms." He further contended that the "Banderite culture of violent insurgency" was subsequently adopted by Jewish-Ukrainian militants, and drew a parallel between the OUN and the Bolsheviks (see [[bolshevik-revolution|Bolshevik Revolution]]) as hyper-violent movements sharing a historic hatred of Russians.
