# Anton Chaitkin

**Anton Chaitkin** is an American historian and author associated with the American System school of economic history, best known for his multi-volume work *Who We Are: America's Fight For Universal Progress*.[1]

In volume two of that book, Chaitkin describes the artificial crisis of 1837–1860, writing that under anti-national economic regimes since the 1830s there had been no national currency and no federal regulations on banks. State-chartered banks used deposits for dangerous speculation and issued paper notes promising payment in gold or silver specie — a promise that could be kept only if the bank did not overextend itself — and those notes were often not accepted for payment far from the locality of the issuing bank.[1] He adds that in the crisis of the Union, fear for the future shriveled credit and investment, Southerners stopped paying most debts owed to northern business, and the bigger northern banks tried to hoard gold, leaving depositors and note-holders across the country with nothing.[1]

Chaitkin also documents the postwar campaign against Lincoln's monetary system, writing that immediately after the President's assassination the Anglo-American financier faction ramped up its attack on [[abraham-lincoln|Lincoln]]'s national-sovereign economic policy, demanding "honest money" and clamoring for the "accursed greenbacks" to be taken quickly out of circulation so gold could be restored to its throne. Shrinking the money supply through this [[specie-resumption-act|specie resumption]] would sharply reduce consumer demand and bring hard times to workers, farmers and businessmen, but America might thereby be stopped from what the financiers viewed as its dangerous race to an unnaturally high place in the world order.[1]

His work is cited by Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] in defense of Lincoln and the [[greenbacks|greenbacks]].[1]
