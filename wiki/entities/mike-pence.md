# Mike Pence

Mike Pence is the former Vice President of the United States who served under [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from 2017 to 2021 and who presided over the congressional certification of the 2020 presidential election results on January 6, 2021.[1]

## Criticism in the White House January 6 page (2026)

In January 2026 the [[white-house|White House]] published the [[white-house-january-6-webpage|official January 6 webpage]], which criticises Pence's actions related to the certification of the 2020 election results and asserts that the 2020 presidential election was stolen.[1] The page, released ahead of the fifth anniversary of the Capitol breach, disputes the "insurrection" framing adopted by earlier congressional investigations and court proceedings and challenges the findings of the House [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]].[1]

## Break with Trump and the 2024 election

Pence and Trump fell out after the 2020 election, and in 2024 Pence said he would not endorse Trump in that year's presidential election.[2] His refusal fed speculation over Trump's choice of a new running mate after Trump became the presumptive Republican nominee following a string of primary victories on Super Tuesday, with figures such as former Secretary of State [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]] publicly signalling willingness to join the ticket.[2]

## George Mason University professorship (2025)

In September 2025 it was announced that Pence would teach politics at [[george-mason-university|George Mason University]]'s Schar School of Policy and Government in the spring term.[3] The university, located in Virginia outside the nation's capital, said he would teach students of political science, law, public administration, and related fields in seminars that "explore the intersection of politics, leadership, and national governance."[3] Pence served as vice president to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from 2017 to 2021.[3]

## Classified documents discovery (January 2023)

On January 24, 2023, Pence informed [[congress|Congress]] that he had discovered documents bearing classified markings from his time as vice president at his home in Carmel, Indiana, on January 16 of that year.[4] The [[pence-classified-documents-discovery|discovery]] followed revelations that classified documents from President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s tenure as vice president had been found at the [[penn-biden-center|Penn Biden Center]] think tank and at Biden's Wilmington, Delaware property, after which Pence's team searched both his Indiana home and the office of his political advocacy group, [[advancing-american-freedom|Advancing American Freedom]].[4] Badlands contributor [[sean-morgan|Sean Morgan]] wrote that it was hard to tell whether Pence was a secret ally of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] or whether his public distance was genuine, judging that the initial optics helped Biden and hurt Trump.[4] [[just-human|Just Human]] responded more succinctly that "Pence is a patriot."[4]

## Special counsel subpoena (2023)

In February 2023, Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] issued subpoenas for interviews and documents to Pence and to former National Security Advisor [[robert-obrien|Robert O'Brien]].[5] Pence's subpoena came after months of negotiations between his legal team and federal prosecutors working with Smith.[5] The subpoenas were part of Smith's inquiries into [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], which also encompassed the events of January 6, 2021 and attempts to interfere with the transfer of power following the 2020 presidential election.[5]

## FBI search of the Indiana home (February 2023)

In early February 2023 the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] was reported to be preparing to search Pence's Indiana home for additional classified material, part of a widening pattern of law-enforcement scrutiny of senior officials' handling of such documents.[6] The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] was in talks with Pence's legal team about scheduling the search.[6]

FBI agents had already collected documents from the Indiana home on January 19, 2023 at the Justice Department's request, according to a January 22 letter from Pence's lawyer Greg Jacob to the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]]; Pence agreed to that transfer, but agents had not at that point conducted a search of their own.[6]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] compared the episode to the searches of Biden's office and homes, noting Pence's lawyers' argument that because the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] took the documents directly from Pence rather than routing them first through the National Archives, it had "bypassed the standard procedures" — adding that there "might be something to that."[6]


## Classified documents narrative (2023)

In June 2023, [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary noted that Pence, like [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]], had been found to have retained classified documents, yet the prevailing media narrative continued to treat [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as uniquely unlawful in his handling of such material.[7] The observation accompanied the publication of Trump's January 19, 2021 [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] declassification letter.[7]
