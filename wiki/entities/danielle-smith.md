# Danielle Smith

Danielle Smith is the premier of the Canadian province of Alberta and the political figure most directly associated with the province's 2026 push toward a formal test of its constitutional relationship with Ottawa.[1]

## October independence referendum

Smith announced that Alberta will hold an October referendum asking voters whether the province should begin the legal process toward a future binding independence vote — a two-step structure in which the first ballot authorizes a process rather than secession itself.[1] The announcement prompted Canadian Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] to publicly warn Alberta separatists against repeating what he called the "dangerous bluff" of [[brexit|Brexit]].[1]

## Political framing

Alberta conservatives aligned with Smith's move argue that federal elites are attempting to frighten voters away from asserting provincial leverage and sovereignty, drawing an explicit parallel to accusations of "Project Fear" leveled at Carney during the 2016 UK referendum campaign.[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the Alberta initiative approvingly, describing support for "the Alberta separatists" and for a broader "Canexit" as consistent with opposition to Canada's allegiance to the British Crown.[1]

## 2024 transgender youth policy package

In February 2024, years before the independence push, Smith announced what the [[cbc|CBC]] described as Canada's most restrictive and wide-ranging set of policies governing the rights and aspirations of transgender, gender-fluid, and questioning minors.[2] The [[alberta-transgender-youth-policies|package]] blocked minors from obtaining gender-affirming surgery and imposed Canada's first ban on hormone treatment and puberty-blocking medication for anyone under sixteen, while requiring parental notification and consent before a school could alter the name or pronouns of a child aged fifteen or under, and parental notification alone for sixteen- and seventeen-year-olds.[2]

Smith had insisted for months that she did not want to politicize questions surrounding transgender youth, yet she appeared alone at the announcement lectern on the Thursday of the rollout, without health, education, or sport ministers, civil servants, or subject-matter experts present.[2] In the social-media video that launched the moves and again the following day, she told those children that "you are loved and supported as you work through your complex and often changing emotions, feelings and beliefs," even as the policy implied they might be making a terrible mistake about who they want to be.[2] [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], treated the episode as a study in [[narrative-warfare|narrative warfare]] and said he would watch closely whether Smith's position held up against her provincial opposition and [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]]'s federal government, or whether she had ceded too much ideological ground.[2]
