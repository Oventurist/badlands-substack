# Inslaw

Inslaw was the American software company that developed the [[promis-software|PROMIS software]], and which Badlands Media describes as an Israeli intelligence front company at whose senior levels Ghislaine Maxwell's twin sisters [[christine-maxwell|Christine]] and [[isabel-maxwell|Isabel Maxwell]] served.[1]

## The PROMIS lease

In 1982 Inslaw leased its revolutionary software to the U.S. Justice Department, then headed by Attorney General [[edwin-meese-iii|Edwin Meese III]], described in the article as an "arch neocon."[1] PROMIS — an acronym for Prosecutor's Information Management System — enabled information analysis and the integration of disparate databases on an unprecedented scale.[1]

## Trapdoor scheme and bankruptcy

The software caught the attention of [[rafi-eitan|Rafi Eitan]], then head of [[lekem|Lekem]], the Israeli Bureau of Scientific Relations, who was introduced to it by the former CIA covert operations officer [[earl-brian|Earl Brian]].[1] Brian was in 1982 building a business empire in which Meese's wife was a major investor.[1] Brian and Eitan conceived the plan to install a "trapdoor" or backdoor in the software and market it globally, giving Israel and allied elements of the U.S. security state a digital peephole into the affairs of enemies and allies alike while generating large profits.[1]

The plan did not initially succeed: the Eitan–Brian–Meese collaboration went badly and led to Inslaw's bankruptcy.[1] Displeased, Eitan turned instead to the publishing magnate [[robert-maxwell|Robert Maxwell]], whose distribution of PROMIS proved far more successful.[1]

## The Maxwell sisters

Christine and Isabel Maxwell held senior positions at Inslaw and, in the Badlands account, facilitated their father's most consequential act of espionage on behalf of Israel — the sale of the compromised PROMIS software to the U.S. national laboratories housing the core of the country's nuclear weapons system.[1] After the multimillion-dollar sale of the venture to a competitor, both sisters became associated with successor enterprises to the PROMIS scandal, as did [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]] and [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] following Epstein's first arrest.[1]
