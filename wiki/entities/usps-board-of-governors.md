# USPS Board of Governors

The USPS Board of Governors is the nine-member body that oversees the [[united-states-postal-service|United States Postal Service]] and selects the Postmaster General. Badlands Media presents its complete vacancy in 2017 and subsequent reconstitution under [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as a decisive, deliberately overlooked event.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Collapse of the quorum

By 2014 there were fewer than six members on the Board of Governors, meaning the body lacked a quorum and was unable to perform key functions.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Barack Obama nominated seven people to fill the vacancies, but according to a 2018 Congressional Research Service report, none received a vote in the Senate.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) By 2016 the board was down to a single member, James Bilbray, whose term ended in December of that year, so that when Trump took office in January 2017 the board was officially empty — offering him what one account called a unique opportunity that he quickly seized.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Reconstitution under Trump

The president picks the members of the Board of Governors, and Trump was therefore able to fill the entire board.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Of the six members serving at the time of the cited reporting, four had donated to Republican candidates including Trump and had served as officials in prior Republican administrations or political organizations; the board was described as controlled five to one by loyalists to the president, who told reporters simply, "We just got the board."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) All the nominations were confirmed by voice vote in the Senate, restoring a quorum and allowing the board to resume its major responsibilities by August 2019.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

After Postmaster General Megan Brennan announced her intention to retire, the reconstituted board was in a position to elect her successor and in May picked [[louis-dejoy|Louis DeJoy]], the first person selected for the position who had not come up through the organization.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)

## Significance

The Washington Post described how, after years without a voting quorum, Trump reshaped the once-obscure Postal Service Board of Governors in three years into a behind-the-scenes powerhouse setting his priorities in motion, possibly for years to come, and noted that the traditionally nonpartisan governing board sat at the center of one of the biggest controversies over election integrity in years.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Lobbyist Arthur Sackler is quoted saying the president had a unique opportunity to start from scratch and that he could not recall another time when there was literally no one on the board.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift) Badlands Media reads this as evidence that Trump was fixated on the Post Office from the beginning of his administration, intending to use it to protect elections and transform the banking system through a [[public-banking-option|public banking option]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-seismic-shift)
