# USPS Board of Governors

The Board of Governors of the [[united-states-postal-service|United States Postal Service]] is the nine-member body, appointed by the President, that oversees the agency and selects the Postmaster General. In [[a-seismic-shift|A Seismic Shift]], [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] treats the board's history between 2014 and 2020 as a key piece of evidence that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] deliberately took control of the Postal Service.

## Loss of quorum

Citing Time magazine, the article recounts that by 2014 the board had fewer than six members and therefore lacked a quorum, leaving it unable to perform key functions. President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] nominated seven people to fill the vacancies, but according to a 2018 Congressional Research Service report none received a vote in the Senate. By 2016 the board was down to one member, James Bilbray, whose term ended in December of that year. When Trump took office in January 2017 the board was officially empty — what Time called "a unique opportunity that he quickly seized." The lobbyist Arthur Sackler is quoted saying the president "had a unique opportunity to start from scratch" and that he could not recall another time when there was literally no one on the board.

## Reconstitution under Trump

Trump's nominees were confirmed by voice vote in the Senate, restoring a quorum by August 2019. Of the six members serving at the time of the Time report, four had donated to Republican candidates including Trump and had served as officials in prior Republican administrations or political organizations. The Washington Post described the board as controlled five to one by Trump loyalists. With a quorum restored, the board was able to select [[louis-dejoy|Louis DeJoy]] as Postmaster General following Megan Brennan's retirement announcement.

## Significance in the article

The Washington Post is quoted describing how Trump reshaped the "once-obscure" Postal Service Board of Governors in three years into a "behind-the-scenes powerhouse" setting his priorities in motion, possibly for years to come, and noting that the traditionally nonpartisan board now sat at the centre of a major controversy over election integrity. Lange draws from this the conclusion that Trump was fixated on the Post Office from the start of his administration because the institution could be used both to protect elections — via the agency's blockchain voting patent — and to transform the banking system through [[postal-savings-system|postal banking]] and [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]].
