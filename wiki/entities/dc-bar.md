# DC Bar

The DC Bar and its associated disciplinary authorities regulate the professional conduct of attorneys licensed in the District of Columbia — a jurisdiction where a large share of federal government lawyers hold their licenses.[1]

## Discipline of federal attorneys

DC disciplinary authorities pursued attorney discipline against former Assistant Attorney General [[jeff-clark|Jeff Clark]] over an unsent internal memo setting out his legal theory about election irregularities in Georgia following the 2020 presidential election.[1] After a hearing, the authorities recommended disbarment based on what they characterized as dishonest conduct tied to the draft letter.[1]

## United States lawsuit

In May 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] filed a complaint against the DC disciplinary authorities, alleging they were improperly using the attorney discipline process to regulate the official actions of federal government lawyers.[1] The complaint asks a court to declare the proceedings against Clark void as violations of the Supremacy Clause and Article II, and to enjoin further disciplinary actions premised on his conduct as a federal attorney.[1]

Officials argued the disciplinary process improperly reaches executive branch deliberations and could deter federal attorneys from giving candid legal advice.[1] The Justice Department presented the suit as an implementation of President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s executive order on ending the [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of the federal government]] and a related memorandum on preventing abuses of the legal system and federal courts.[1] Badlands commentary framed the case as a test of whether local bar regulators may exercise oversight of presidential legal decision-making through "ethics" reviews.[1]
