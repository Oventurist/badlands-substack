# Open Society Foundations

The Open Society Foundations is the philanthropic network founded by George Soros. In Badlands coverage it is treated as a recurring node of what the outlet describes as globalist and Deep State influence networks.[1]

## Appearance in the Clinton annex disclosures

Intercepted Russian intelligence discussed in connection with the declassified [[clinton-annex|Clinton annex]] described purported communications between Rep. [[debbie-wasserman-schultz|Debbie Wasserman Schultz]] and "two different individuals who worked for the Open Society Foundations," in which Wasserman Schultz allegedly said the [[fbi|FBI]] had no hard evidence against [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] because data had been removed from the mail servers in time.[1]

Badlands writer Burning Bright characterized the disclosures as revealing "shields erected by the DNC and Soros affiliates to protect the System," placing the foundations among the institutional protectors of the Clinton email operation.[1]

## Hungary Precedent and the Texas Comparison (2024)

In January 2024 Badlands commentary revisited the Open Society Foundations' 2018 withdrawal from Hungary, noting that OSF said it was forced to shutter operations there after the government passed the "Stop Soros" law, which outlawed much of the group's work with illegal immigrants in the country.[2] Fact-checking outlets had cited this history while disputing claims that [[george-soros|George Soros]] himself had been banned from six countries, resting the rebuttal on the distinction between the man and his charities.[2] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the distinction was thin and that the Hungarian episode illuminated OSF-aligned spending in Texas, where more than $3 million flowed to left-wing groups seeking to flip the state.[2]

## Capital Research Center report (September 2025)

On 17 September 2025 — the same day President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced he was moving to designate [[antifa|Antifa]] a domestic terror organization — the [[capital-research-center|Capital Research Center]] published an exclusive report alleging that the Open Society Foundations had, since 2016, poured over $80 million into groups tied to terrorism or extremist violence.[3] The report stated that OSF, by then run by [[george-soros|George Soros]] with his son [[alex-soros|Alexander Soros]], had sent millions into U.S.-based organizations engaged in "direct actions" that the FBI defines as domestic terrorism.[3]

Named recipients included the [[center-for-third-world-organizing|Center for Third World Organizing]] and its militant partner the [[ruckus-society|Ruckus Society]], said to have trained activists in property destruction and sabotage during the 2020 riots, and the [[sunrise-movement|Sunrise Movement]], which endorsed the Antifa-linked [[stop-cop-city|Stop Cop City]] campaign whose activists faced more than 40 domestic terrorism charges and 60 racketeering indictments.[3] The report further stated that OSF awarded $18 million to the [[movement-for-black-lives|Movement for Black Lives]], a group it said co-authored a guide glorifying Hamas's October 7 massacre and instructing activists in the use of false IDs, blockades, and economic disruption.[3] Internationally, more than $2.3 million was said to have been funneled into [[al-haq|Al-Haq]], a West Bank NGO long accused of ties to the [[popular-front-for-the-liberation-of-palestine|Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine]].[3]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright argued the report could serve as a predicate for the Antifa designation and as a fulcrum for RICO charges against Soros and similar funders that Trump had been signaling throughout the summer of 2025, and noted that one Capital Research Center board member, [[edwin-meese-iii|Edwin Meese III]], had received the Presidential Medal of Freedom from Trump in 2019.[3]

## 2023 withdrawal from the European Union

In August 2023 Bloomberg reported that OSF, the philanthropy controlling most of [[george-soros|George Soros]]'s $25 billion family office assets, would end a large part of its EU operations under new leadership.[4] [[alexander-soros|Alexander Soros]] had taken control in June and announced a headcount reduction of at least 40%.[4] A letter to Hungarian grantees cited a "radical shift of strategic direction," "significant further restructuring," and the "closing all regional and global programs," quoting a note to Berlin headquarters staff stating the new direction "provides for withdrawal and termination of large parts of our current work within the European Union."[4] OSF said funding within the bloc would be "extremely limited," justifying the shift on grounds that EU institutions and governments were already allocating significant resources to human rights, freedom and pluralism; its press office did not respond to Bloomberg's inquiries.[4]

## Indian Enforcement Directorate raids (March 2025)

On Tuesday, March 18, 2025, India's [[enforcement-directorate|Enforcement Directorate]] conducted extensive searches on the Open Society Foundations, described in Indian coverage as promoted by "US billionaire currency speculator and hedge fund operator" [[george-soros|George Soros]], who has been accused by the Bharatiya Janata Party of acting against India's interests.[5] The raids in Bengaluru targeted OSF and linked entities and were the fallout of an inquiry into foreign direct investment sourced by OSF in alleged violation of Foreign Exchange Management Act guidelines.[5]

Sources cited in the reporting said the Open Society Institute (US) had been placed under the home ministry's prior reference category since 2016 due to allegedly undesirable activities, requiring OSI to secure prior ministry sanction before sending funds to FCRA-registered organisations in India.[5] Investigators reportedly found that the [[soros-economic-development-fund|Soros Economic Development Fund]], OSI's social impact investment arm, had provided funds to three Indian companies: Rootbridge Services Pvt Ltd, Rootbridge Academy Pvt Ltd, and ASAR Social Impact Advisors Pvt Ltd.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the Indian action coincided with pressure on Soros in the United States following the election of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and that "Soros is getting pinched and pincered by the Sovereign Alliance."[5] He recalled that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had effectively defeated Soros within a few years of taking office in 2000, and that in 2015 — twelve years after Soros closed his Open Society office in Moscow — Russia formally banned the foundation, with the official statement holding that "the activity of the Open Society Foundations and the Open Society Institute Assistance Foundation represents a threat to the foundations of the constitutional system of the Russian Federation and the security of the state."[5] He also drew a parallel between Trump's campaign against [[columbia-university|Columbia University]] and Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orban]]'s 2018 effort to drive Soros out of Hungary, and characterised the affair as "yet another narrative that acts as a stepping stone in disclosure of how NGOs are used to launder money and topple governments."[5]

## Čaputová's tenure

[[zuzana-caputova|Zuzana Čaputová]], later President of [[slovakia|Slovakia]], worked for the Open Society Foundation beginning in 2001, where by published accounts she handled public administration and the issue of abused and exploited children, apparently remaining until 2017 when she helped found [[progressive-slovakia|Progressive Slovakia]].[6] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that her work history there is described only vaguely and in near-identical language across profiles.[6]

## Leadership succession and Vatican visit (July 2023)

By July 2023 [[alex-soros|Alex Soros]], son of founder [[george-soros|George Soros]], had become the new chairman of the Open Society Foundations.[8] In that capacity he accompanied former President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] on a trip to Albania and then to a private audience with Pope [[pope-francis|Francis]] at the Casa Santa Marta papal residence on July 5, 2023, a pairing that Badlands commentator [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] treated as emblematic of the network of institutional philanthropy the outlet criticises.[8]
