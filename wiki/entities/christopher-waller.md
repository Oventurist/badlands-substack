# Christopher Waller

Christopher Waller is a sitting Governor of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] who, in January 2026, was named among the candidates to succeed [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] as Fed Chair.[1] Waller was appointed to the Board during President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term, and was seen as the option providing institutional continuity.[1]

His name appeared on a shortlist vetted by Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] alongside [[kevin-warsh|Kevin Warsh]], [[kevin-hassett|Kevin Hassett]], and [[rick-rieder|Rick Rieder]]; all of the candidates were described as supportive of cutting interest rates.[1]

## July 2026 rate-hike signaling

Ahead of June 2026's inflation report, Waller was among [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] officials who signaled openness to raising interest rates as soon as July if incoming data stayed hot.[2] The June consumer price index instead fell 0.4 percent — the largest monthly drop since April 2020 — weakening the immediate case for a hike and giving Chair [[kevin-warsh|Kevin Warsh]] room to wait.[2]

## August 2025 prediction-market favorite for Fed chair

On August 7, 2025, the odds that Waller would be named the next chairman of the Federal Reserve crossed above 50 percent for the first time on the prediction market [[kalshi|Kalshi]].[3] His implied probability spiked from roughly 16 percent the previous day after Bloomberg News reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s advisors liked Waller's openness to adjusting monetary policy on the basis of forecasting rather than current data.[3] The same reporting indicated that Waller had met with Trump's team about the Fed chief role but had not met with the president himself.[3]

Waller's Kalshi odds surpassed those of previous front-runners, former Fed official [[kevin-warsh|Kevin Warsh]] at about 20 percent and [[national-economic-council|National Economic Council]] Director [[kevin-hassett|Kevin Hassett]] at about 35 percent.[3] The shift came the same day Trump announced [[stephen-miran|Stephen Miran]]'s nomination to the board seat vacated by [[adriana-kugler|Adriana Kugler]].[3]
