# Kyrsten Sinema

Kyrsten Sinema is a United States senator from Arizona who sits as an independent after leaving the Democratic Party, and who became a central negotiator in bipartisan immigration and border legislation during the 2024 election cycle.[1]

## Role in the 2024 Senate border negotiations

Sinema was one of three principal negotiators — alongside James Lankford of Oklahoma and Chris Murphy of Connecticut — of the $118 billion bipartisan Senate package that paired border provisions with foreign aid.[1] Critics of the agreement characterized it as permitting as many as 1.5 million migrants to enter the United States each year, allocating roughly $2.3 billion to non-governmental organizations involved in transporting and resettling migrants, providing $14.1 billion in security assistance to Israel, and directing approximately $60 billion in support to Ukraine.[1] The bill was also described as locking in green card allocations through 2030.[1]

The negotiating trio drew intense criticism from the political right. Lankford's own state legislature censured him for having struck what opponents called a poor bargain with Democrats, and commentary surrounding the deal argued that the president already possessed the executive authority to close the border without new legislation but was withholding action until Ukraine and Israel funding was secured.[1] Detractors also focused on a carve-out of about $2.33 billion for "Refugee and Entrant Assistance," which authorized grants or contracts with qualified organizations, including nonprofit entities, to provide "culturally and linguistically appropriate services."[1]

Sinema's participation reflected her broader positioning as a dealmaker willing to work across party lines, a posture that made her simultaneously indispensable to negotiations and a target of criticism from activists in both coalitions.[1]
