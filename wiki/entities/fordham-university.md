# Fordham University

**Fordham University** is a Jesuit university in New York City. It appears in the Badlands Media feature "Trump Did It" by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] as the setting for a formative — and explicitly fictionalized — episode in the life of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Role in the article

In a passage presented as a future interview, Trump recalls attending "that Jesuit Training school, before my real estate training," identifying it as Fordham University and calling it "a hell of a school back then."[1] Trump historically attended Fordham for two years before transferring to the University of Pennsylvania; the article uses the biographical detail as the hinge for its theological argument.[1]

## The teacher's lesson

The article's Trump credits an unnamed Fordham teacher with two statements that shaped his understanding of why "the founding fathers thought a God-centered nation was so critical to freedom and prosperity."[1] The teacher tells him that "true law and God's will are one and the same," and that until man incorporates God's law into his legal systems, "man will be enslaved by those who do know the truth, but keep it for themselves for their own greedy ambitions."[1]

Asked why man refuses the truth, the teacher answers that "you can't fill a cup that's already full," describing humanity as "drunk with delusions and lies" fed by "bread and circus from his godless masters," yet adds that "evil always contains the seeds of its own destruction."[1] The teacher finally predicts a coming exposure of the world's evil, a moment of confusion "at the precipice" when people let go of the old way — and tells the young Trump, "You have a chance to help in that moment."[1] Trump adds that the teacher told all his students the same thing, "but I guess I was the only one who believed it."[1]

## Significance

The Fordham anecdote functions in the article as the origin story for Deschamps's central thesis that Trump privately understands the true [[rule-of-law|rule of law]] and has been preparing the public to rediscover it, a process the author links to the [[great-awakening|Great Awakening]].[1] As with the rest of the piece, the exchange is a fictional construction rather than a documented event.[1]
