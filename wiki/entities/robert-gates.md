# Robert Gates

Robert Gates is a former [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] analyst who rose through the intelligence bureaucracy and later served as Secretary of Defense under both George W. Bush and [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[1]

## Role in CIA politicization claims

In the account of former CIA officer [[john-gentry|John Gentry]], the politicization of the ostensibly non-partisan CIA first became a live issue in the 1990s under Gates, who is alleged to have ordered intelligence reports to be skewed so as to support the political narratives favored by elected officials.[1] Gentry presents this episode as the beginning of a longer trajectory that accelerated sharply during the Obama years, when new structures, policies and incentives were said to have been institutionalized to align the agency's culture with the administration's political agenda.[1] Gates's service as defense secretary across administrations of both parties is cited as evidence of the continuity of the national-security establishment across nominally opposed presidencies.[1]

## Operation Cyclone testimony

Gates held the position of deputy CIA Director at the height of the [[soviet-afghan-war|Soviet-Afghan War]].2 In his personal memoirs *From the Shadows* he suggests that U.S. intelligence was directly involved from the outset of [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]], channeling aid to the Islamic brigades well before Soviet forces set foot in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] — corroborating [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]]'s later admission that covert aid began in July 1979.2

## Harvard Board of Overseers

"The Harvard Control Grid" cites Gates — former CIA Director and Secretary of Defense — as having served on Harvard's Board of Overseers, offering the appointment as evidence of the university's institutional continuity with the U.S. defense and intelligence establishment.[3] He appears in the article alongside [[ash-carter|Ash Carter]], who directed the [[belfer-center|Belfer Center]], and [[james-woolsey|James Woolsey]], who sat on Harvard-linked advisory panels.[3]
