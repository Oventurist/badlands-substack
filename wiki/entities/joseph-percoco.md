# Joseph Percoco

Joseph Percoco is a former aide to New York Governor [[andrew-cuomo|Andrew Cuomo]] whose federal corruption conviction was overturned by the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] in a unanimous 9-0 decision reported in May 2023.[1]

## Conviction and reversal

Percoco was convicted of accepting a $35,000 payment from a real estate developer during a period in which he was managing Cuomo's 2014 re-election campaign rather than serving in a government post.[1] The Supreme Court threw out the conviction, holding that his conduct was not covered by the federal statute requiring that "honest services" be provided to the public: because he was not working for the government at the time of the payment, the Court reasoned, he owed no duty to provide honest services.[1] The ruling narrowed the scope of the federal anticorruption law and was one of two corruption convictions the Court discarded on the same day, which NBC News characterized as a "double blow to federal prosecutors."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] used the decision to criticize what she described as [[nbc-news|NBC News]]' inability to conceal a pro-[[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] bias, noting the outlet's framing that the justices — "whose members face claims of ethics lapses" — had made corruption prosecutions harder.[1] She characterized the DOJ's underlying legal theory as treating the deprivation of "potentially valuable economic information" as fraud, comparing it sardonically to [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] withholding stock tips, and welcomed the Court's unanimous conclusion that the government "cannot use vague fraud statutes to advance novel and sweeping theories in prosecutions of political actors," with fraud instead requiring a loss of money or property.[1] She added that further corruption cases remained pending before the Court and that it was refreshing to see limits imposed on the executive branch and its justice department.[1] See [[honest-services-fraud]].
