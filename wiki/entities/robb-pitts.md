# Robb Pitts

Pitts is the [[fulton-county|Fulton County]], Georgia official who publicly led the county's response to the [[fbi|FBI]]'s seizure of 2020 election materials from the county's Election Hub.[1]

## February 2026 press conference

At a press conference in early February 2026, Pitts claimed he had been told to be prepared for arrest warrants against himself and other state officials.[1] He framed the dispute in constitutional terms, saying, "Our Constitution itself is at stake in this fight," and adding, "The Constitution is the law of the land. The Constitution is not a suggestion."[1]

## Related litigation

His remarks accompanied Fulton County's February 4, 2026 motion in the Northern District of Georgia challenging the FBI search warrant, seeking the return or narrowing of seized 2020 election records including ballots and voter rolls, and requesting the unsealing of the supporting affidavit.[1]

## Response to the 2026 grand jury subpoena

After the Justice Department served a grand jury subpoena in April 2026 seeking identifying information for thousands of 2020 Fulton County election workers, Pitts denounced it as "outrageous federal overreach designed to intimidate and chill participation in elections" and declared that "Fulton County will not be intimidated."[2] The county moved to quash the subpoena on May 4, 2026, an episode described in [[fulton-county-2020-election-grand-jury-subpoena|the Fulton County 2020 election grand jury subpoena]].[2]

## 2024 cyberattack response

As chairman of the Fulton County Board of Commissioners, Pitts confirmed at a news briefing on Monday, January 29, 2024 that a weekend outage affecting the county's phones, courts and tax systems "was a result of a cybersecurity incident" and that law enforcement was investigating.[3] The [[2024-fulton-county-cyberattack|incident]] took down desktop phones, the intranet and server-dependent devices across all departments, including the office of District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]], who was then prosecuting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on election interference charges.[3] "We do not yet have a specific time frame for when these systems will be restored," Pitts said, adding that the county was not aware of any transfer of sensitive information about citizens or employees but would continue to look carefully at the issue.[3]

## Response to the December 2025 ballot access ruling

After Fulton County Superior Court Judge [[robert-mcburney|Robert McBurney]] granted the Republican-led [[georgia-state-election-board|Georgia Election Board]] access to the county's 2020 presidential ballots and documents in December 2025, Pitts told FOX5 in Atlanta that the elections had been open and transparent and insisted that all votes were counted.[4] He further asserted that Fulton County taxpayers would not be on the hook for the roughly $400,000 that production of the documents was estimated to cost, which the judge had ordered the board to bear.[4]
