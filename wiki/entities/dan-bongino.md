# Dan Bongino

Dan Bongino is a former Deputy Director of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]], having served in the bureau's senior leadership alongside Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] before departing the post.[1]

## FBI records disclosure litigation

Bongino features in Freedom of Information Act litigation over a large cache of records located at FBI headquarters. A lawsuit filed in August 2025 seeks, among other materials, internal FBI communications involving Patel and Bongino concerning the discovery of as many as 1.9 million pages of records stored in a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility, along with any directives issued about how those materials were to be handled.[1]

The request situates Bongino as a principal in the internal decision-making around the disclosure — or non-disclosure — of the cache, a matter Badlands commentators treated as a test of whether new bureau leadership would break with the institution's record of delay.[1]

## Resignation as FBI Deputy Director (January 2026)

Bongino's final day as Deputy Director of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] was January 3, 2026, ending a tenure of less than one year that had begun in March 2025.[2] As the bureau's No. 2 official he served under Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]], and he was replaced by co-Deputy Director [[andrew-bailey|Andrew Bailey]], who had been appointed to that role in September 2025.[2]

Patel credited Bongino with helping implement internal reforms aimed at making the bureau more efficient, with leading the "Summer Heat" operation, and with securing breakthroughs in long-unsolved cases such as the pipe bomb investigation.[2] Bongino said he was returning to civilian life without offering many further details, and was widely expected — though it was not confirmed — to resume his popular podcast.[2] Sharing a post from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on the Saturday after his departure, he wrote: "It was a busy last day on the job. This will be my last post on this account. Tomorrow I return to civilian life… It was the honor of a lifetime to work with Director Patel, and to serve you, the American people. See you on the other side."[2]

## Swearing-in as FBI Deputy Director (March 2025)

Bongino, a former [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]] agent, was sworn in as deputy director of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] on Monday, March 17, 2025.[3] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced the assignment on social media, writing that "working with our great new United States Attorney General, [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]], and Director Patel (Kash Patel – Director of the Federal Bureau of Investigation), Fairness, Justice, Law and Order will be brought back to America, and quickly."[3]

Following the ceremony, Bongino posted a pledge: "My promise to you is that I will work tirelessly to help restore integrity, eliminate political bias, and ensure the FBI remains dedicated to its core mission of protecting the United States and upholding the Constitution."[3] The FBI's Facebook account showed Bongino visiting the bureau's Wall of Honor, commemorating members who died in the line of duty.[3] Badlands commentary greeted the swearing-in as a turning point — "cutesy time is over" — linking it to the voiding of the Biden pardons and to a letter sent by Senators Grassley and Johnson concerning former FBI official Timothy Thibault.[3]

## Announcement as FBI deputy director (February 2025)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced on the evening of Sunday, 23 February 2025 that Bongino, a conservative commentator and former Secret Service agent, had been named deputy director of the FBI.[4] Writing on Truth Social, Trump called it "great news for Law Enforcement and American Justice," saying Bongino was "a man of incredible love and passion for our Country" and had been named by "the man who will be the best ever Director, [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]]."[4]

Bongino, then 50, was a former NYPD officer who had worked as a special agent for the Secret Service before changing careers; he previously hosted the Fox News program "Unfiltered" on Saturday nights before parting ways with the network in 2023.[4] Badlands commentator Chris Paul responded sardonically that "soon, we will be totally ruled by the television," adding the qualification "unless, that is, these agencies don't exist anymore. And they must not."[4]

## Reopening of public-corruption cases (May 2025)

On Monday, May 26, 2025, Bongino announced via X that several cases of "potential public corruption" were either being reopened or given renewed "investigative attention" by the [[fbi|FBI]].[5] Among the matters listed were the 2023 [[white-house-cocaine-incident|discovery of cocaine at the White House]] during the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration, the 2022 [[dobbs-draft-opinion-leak-investigation|Dobbs Supreme Court leak]], and the pipe bombs found outside the DNC and RNC headquarters in Washington, D.C.[5] NewsNation legal contributor [[jesse-weber|Jesse Weber]] said the announcement responded to a public that "deserves answers," noting there was "evidence of a crime" in each matter.[5]

## Health care fraud takedown (July 2025)

On July 1, 2025, in his capacity as Deputy Director of the [[fbi|FBI]], Bongino announced the results of the [[2025-national-health-care-fraud-takedown|largest health care fraud investigation in DOJ history]] as measured by financial losses, conducted in conjunction with the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and federal partners.[6] He reported that the investigation spanned fifty federal districts, uncovered nearly $3 billion in false claims and over 15 million illegal distributions of pills, and produced $245 million in seizures and charges against 324 defendants including 96 medical professionals, with intended losses approaching $15 billion.[6]

Bongino framed the action within a wider anti-corruption posture, stating that "public corruption will not be tolerated as the Director and I vigorously pursue bad actors who violated their oaths to all of us" and that the theft of public funds was "a crime against all of us."[6] His closing line — "Results matter. Talk is cheap. And this is not even the beginning of the beginning" — was read by Badlands contributor CannCon as a deliberate signal of further enforcement to come.[6]
