# Bo Wagner

**Dr. Bo Wagner** is the author of an August 15, 2013 column published in the *Cleveland Corner* section of the Shelby Star recounting the survival of [[george-washington|George Washington]] at the [[battle-of-the-monongahela|Battle of the Monongahela]].[1] His retelling is the source quoted at length by [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] in the July 2024 Badlands Media essay comparing Washington's deliverance with the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Donald Trump]].[1]

Wagner's column relates that during the two-hour battle on July 9, 1755, Native American warriors specifically targeted officers, that 63 of 86 British and American officers became casualties, and that Washington — then a 23-year-old colonel riding across the field with the general's orders — was the only mounted officer not shot down.[1] He quotes Washington's subsequent letter to his brother attributing his survival to "the all-powerful dispensations of Providence," notes the four bullet holes found in Washington's coat, and states flatly of the outcome: "Do you know what you call that? A miracle!"[1]

The column further recounts that fifteen years afterward an old and respected Indian chief sought Washington out to reveal that he had led the warriors and had ordered concentrated fire on the "tall and daring warrior," only to find that "a power mightier far than we shielded you."[1] Wagner draws the theological conclusion that the bullets "just ceased to exist in the 1/8 of an inch between his coat and his body," that the chief was correct in calling Washington "the particular favorite of Heaven," and that God raised Washington up specifically to lead the nation against Britain in its hour of greatest need.[1]
