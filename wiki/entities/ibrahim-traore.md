# Ibrahim Traoré

Captain Ibrahim Traoré is the military leader of [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] and one of the figures the Badlands Brief treats as a credible voice against Western intelligence operations in Africa.

## Chemical poisoning allegation

Speaking in the Yaadga region in July 2026, Traoré said that operatives of an unnamed Western country's intelligence service had once offered his government four chemical products capable of being mixed into food and consumer goods destined for territory controlled by armed groups. He said his government asked about the products and then rejected the proposal, and that the visitors told him the substances had already been used elsewhere in Africa and Asia. According to the Brief's commentary, Traoré explained that the chemicals target neurological and physical functions. He did not name the country involved, supply evidence, or specify when the meeting occurred.

## Broader claims

In the same address Traoré urged Burkinabè citizens to support locally produced goods, arguing that dependence on imports exposes the country to unknown risks, and alleged that foreign actors exploit ethnic and religious divisions to destabilize African countries. He also said he had received a 2023 letter from a French national he associated with [[france|France]]'s external intelligence agency, again without providing supporting evidence.

## Reception in the Brief

Contributor [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the report "quite the sinister development," asserted that "Traore has a proven record of telling the truth in his public statements," and reasoned that if the account is accurate, Western agencies had constructed a [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian Dialectic]] — funding [[isis|ISIS]]-aligned groups in the Sahel and then proposing to poison the populations among whom they operate. He closed with a demand that Americans hold their own agencies accountable.
