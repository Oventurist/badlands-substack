# Ibrahim Traoré

Captain Ibrahim Traoré is the military leader of [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]].[1] On January 29, 2026, his government dissolved all political parties by executive decree, transferring party assets to the state and repealing laws on party status, financing, and the opposition leader's role.[1]

Traoré justified the dissolution on the grounds that Burkina Faso's political parties had become subversive.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that over 100 parties existed in the country while only 15 held parliamentary seats, and predicted that "muh democracy" critics would react with outrage.[1]

Shortly before the decree, former President [[paul-henri-sandaogo-damiba|Paul-Henri Sandaogo Damiba]] was arrested in Togo under suspicion of involvement in an elaborate scheme to kill Traoré.[1] Under Traoré, Burkina Faso has pursued nuclear cooperation with [[russia|Russia]] and thermal power development with the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]].[1]

## Sahel conflict context (May 2026)

Badlands commentary recounts that Traoré led a military coup in [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] in 2022 against his own commander, who had himself overthrown the government eight months earlier, and that the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]] has been in rebellion against the UN-affiliated [[ecowas|ECOWAS]] bloc ever since.[2] [[russia|Russia]] has assisted Traoré and his allies in reclaiming sovereignty from [[islamic-state|ISIS]]-linked groups, while Russia and the AES have accused [[ukraine|Ukraine]] of funding and arming those groups.[2]

The same analysis argued that by killing [[abu-bilal-al-manuki|Abu-Bilal Al-Manuki]] in a joint operation with [[nigeria|Nigeria]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] did a great service to Traoré and his Sahelian allies, even though mainstream media would present it as Trump siding with ECOWAS against the AES.[2]

## Nuclear power agreement with Russia

Traoré's government signed an agreement with [[russia|Russia]] to build a nuclear power plant in [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]], described as the junta's latest step in aligning with Moscow after falling out with most Western partners.[3] The deal culminated talks he held with Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in July at the Russia–Africa summit in Moscow, where he requested support for a plant that would serve both Burkina Faso and neighbouring states: "We have a critical need for energy, this is an important point for me."[3] He cited the country's strategic position in the heart of West Africa and the sub-region's energy deficit.[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed Traoré's seizure of power — from a junta installed only eight months earlier — and his approach to Putin as rational responses to a country where only 21% of people have electricity after long French imperial involvement.[3]

## Bellwether for the Western Sahara dispute

Commenting in April 2025 on US recognition of Moroccan sovereignty over [[western-sahara|Western Sahara]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said he looked to local leaders like Traore in neighboring [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] to determine which side of the dispute — [[morocco|Morocco]] or the [[polisario-front|Polisario Front]] — represents imperial interests.[4] He cautioned that Traore's recent rejection of Western offers of assistance, including declining President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s invitation to his inauguration, meant one should not assume Traore and Trump would immediately align on the conflict.[4] He nonetheless read Trump's decision to place zero tariffs on Burkina Faso as a major signal of belief in Traore and his campaign against ISIS and what he called the last vestiges of the globalist empire, allegedly funded by the same money the US was sending to Ukraine.[4] In his framing it was narratively important that the heroes of Africa rise and resolve the continent's conflicts with as little outside help as possible, though the "Master Negotiator" might eventually need to help finalize Africa's liberation from globalist proxy forces.[4]

## April 2025 alleged coup plot

In April 2025 Burkina Faso's military government announced that it had foiled a "major plot" to overthrow Traoré, in what became known as the [[2025-burkina-faso-coup-plot|2025 Burkina Faso coup plot]].[5] Security Minister Mahamadou Sana said the attempt was led by current and former soldiers working with "terrorist leaders" who had planned to attack the presidential palace, and alleged that the plotters were based in neighbouring [[ivory-coast|Ivory Coast]].[5] It was the latest of several claimed attempts against Traoré since he seized power in 2022.[5] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the 36-year-old as the world's youngest head of state and a target of the Deep State, arguing that the chief beneficiary of his death would be Langley rather than Abidjan.[5]

## Rise to power and 2024 economic measures

Traoré, an army captain, came to power in 2022 after overthrowing — with the help of the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]], according to Badlands commentary — the military junta that had itself seized control eight months earlier, becoming at 34 the youngest national leader in the world.[6] He subsequently aligned himself with Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], who was described as providing support and resources to resist [[nato|NATO]] and what the commentary called globalist oligarchs.[6]

In early August 2024 Traoré announced that Burkinabè gold would no longer be exported for processing but would remain in country and be used to back the national currency, following Russia's example.[6] In May of that year he had announced a 30% cut to the salaries of politicians and ministers alongside a 50% increase for ordinary workers, and he takes no salary as president.[6] He has framed his program in continental terms, asking, "My generation does not understand this: how can Africa, which has so much wealth, become the poorest continent in the world today? And why African leaders travel the world to beg."[6] His government's foreign ministry also publicly rebuked Ukraine's [[main-directorate-of-intelligence|GUR]] for claiming credit for an attack on Wagner personnel and Malian troops in [[mali|Mali]].[6]

## Declined inauguration invitation

Badlands commentary from November 2025 noted that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had invited Traoré, the president of Burkina Faso, to his January inauguration, and that the captain turned down the invitation.[7] The observation was made in the context of the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]]' confrontation with [[ecowas|ECOWAS]] and Trump's subsequent threat of military action against [[nigeria|Nigeria]].[7]

## Cited amid the French withdrawal from Chad (December 2024)

When French forces began withdrawing from [[chad|Chad]] in December 2024, Badlands commentary invoked Burkina Faso under Traore's leadership as a country that had "gained new life," while cautioning that reports indicated roughly 30% of its territory remained under some form of control or influence by groups the writer described as NATO-cartel proxies such as ISIS.[8] The passage concluded that sovereignty is not a privilege but a responsibility, sometimes requiring the cleaning up of entrenched corruption.[8]

## Dissolution of the government (December 2024)

In December 2024 Traoré sacked Prime Minister Apollinaire Joachim Kyelem de Tambela and dissolved the entire government by decree, which did not specify a reason but provided that members of the dissolved cabinet would continue serving until a new one was appointed.[9] Tambela, a civilian, had been named prime minister in 2022 shortly after Traoré seized power in a coup; his dismissal came weeks after another civilian prime minister, Choguel Maïga, was sacked by coup leader General [[assimi-goita|Assimi Goïta]] in neighbouring [[mali|Mali]], and it was unclear whether Traoré would similarly appoint a military officer.[9] [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]], like its Sahel neighbours, continued to face jihadist attacks despite the junta's promises to improve security.[9]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] tied the move to the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]]' announced departure from [[ecowas|ECOWAS]] and the bloc's six-month "grace period," warning that outside powers might seek to overthrow the three governments and install puppet leaders during that window — making Traoré and his Sahel partners vulnerable and giving them reason to dissolve their governments to avoid internal power struggles.[9] He contrasted Western hostility with the reception Traoré received from Burkinabè crowds at the opening of a new tomato processing factory, the second built that year, and noted that ISIS reportedly controls roughly 30% of the country.[9]

He also observed that the day before the dissolution, President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] told the VTB "Russia Calling!" investment forum that Africa "may seem geographically distant, but it is a continent close to us in spirit," praising African nations for cherishing their sovereignty — remarks Traoré echoed in his own public address.[9]

## 2025 territorial recapture pledge

Marking his second anniversary as [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]]'s military ruler after ousting interim President Paul-Henri Sandaogo Damiba in a September 2022 coup, Traoré told national broadcaster RTB in October 2024 that he intended to recapture all territory still under terrorist control by 2025.[10] He said his government had recruited more than 30,000 people into the army as part of its commitment to defeat rebel groups, and stated: "The intensity of the offensives has decreased due to the rainy season and we will resume them very soon. The year 2025 is crucial and we will conquer the remaining 30% of the territory."[10] The country had been fighting jihadist groups, including some affiliated with [[al-qaeda|al-Qaeda]], since 2015.[10]

Traoré also addressed the August 24, 2024 attack at Barsalogho, one of the deadliest since violence spilled over from neighbouring Mali, in which at least 200 people were reportedly killed and 169 injured when Islamist militants fired on residents digging defensive trenches.[10] He said he believed he had been personally targeted: "In July 2022, I defied many orders to intervene so that Barsalogho would not fall. I sacrificed a lot of things for Barsalogho. They targeted Barsalogho because it was going to affect me."[10]

On regional integration, Traoré said that "seeing Africans unite is not good for other people," and that opponents were therefore doing everything possible to destroy the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]], which is why liaison officers had been established early in each member country.[10] He and allies in Niamey and Bamako had previously accused Ukraine of supporting terrorism in the Sahel, alleging that insurgents used intelligence from Kyiv in a July attack that killed dozens of Malian soldiers and [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] personnel.[10] All three military-led states have severed defence ties with their former colonial power, France, and sought security cooperation with Russia.[10]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry, describing Traoré as at 36 the world's youngest leader and "our favorite African leader," invoked General Steve Kwast's remark that Africa's "hero system" had been broken by colonial interests that destroyed capable leaders while elevating controllable ones.[10] He speculated that the Barsalogho attackers' knowledge of Traoré's attachment to the town implied cooperation between ISIS and prior external powers, and framed Traoré alongside Bukele, Xi and Orbán as leaders so far absent from a long list of assassinated or targeted heads of state.[10]

## Talks with the Russian delegation (September 2023)

In late August 2023, Traoré received a Russian delegation led by Deputy Defence Minister [[yunus-bek-yevkurov|Yunus-Bek Yevkurov]] in Ouagadougou to discuss possible military cooperation, in a meeting the [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] presidency described as a follow-up to his talks with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] at the Russia-Africa summit in St. Petersburg in July.[11] The agenda covered military-sphere cooperation including the training of Burkinabè cadet officers and officers at all levels, pilots among them, in Russia.[11] Traoré had taken power in a September 2022 coup against Lieutenant Colonel [[paul-henri-sandaogo-damiba|Paul-Henri Sandaogo Damiba]], and relations with Moscow deepened thereafter.[11]


## December 2025 Benin and airspace disputes

Amid the December 2025 coup attempt in [[benin|Benin]], Badlands noted that Traoré had previously accused that country of establishing French military bases along its border — claims Beninese President [[patrice-talon|Patrice Talon]] had publicly denied over several years — feeding allegations that the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|AES]] had backed the mutineers.[12] Badlands published a video of Traoré making those claims together with an AI-generated transcript.[12]

Days later, Traoré's government in [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] grounded a [[nigeria|Nigerian]] Air Force C-130 for entering Burkinabè airspace without authorization, and Ibrahima Maiga, described as a close ally of Traoré, publicly questioned the aircraft's flight path and asserted its transponder had been switched off.[12]
