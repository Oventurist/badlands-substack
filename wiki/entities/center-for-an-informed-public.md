# Center for an Informed Public

The Center for an Informed Public (CIP) is a research center at the [[university-of-washington|University of Washington]] funded by the [[national-science-foundation|National Science Foundation]] and other agencies.[1] It studies "misinformation" and develops rapid-response frameworks intended to counter online narratives.[1]

## International collaboration and shadow diplomacy

CIP maintains international collaborations, including with European and Asian research networks.[1] Its research on global disinformation campaigns — such as those alleged during the 2020 U.S. election — is shared with foreign governments and international organizations including the UN and NATO.[1] These exchanges, often conducted through academic conferences, are described as backchannels used to align media policies across borders, a form of [[shadow-diplomacy|shadow diplomacy]].[1]

## Virality circuit breakers

Among CIP's outputs are frameworks such as "virality circuit breakers," designed to slow the spread of material labeled misinformation.[1] Critics argue this amounts to weeding out dissenting voices, particularly populist sentiment, which is treated as the most immediate threat to the international rules-based order; the evidence offered is that a surplus of genuine misinformation is permitted to proliferate online because it serves a purpose.[1]

## Funding

NSF award #2120496 granted $2.25 million to the University of Washington in 2021 for disinformation research.[1] NSF funding cuts in 2025 may have reduced CIP's scope.[1]
