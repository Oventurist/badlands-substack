# Walt Nauta

Walt Nauta is a personal aide to former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and a co-defendant in the [[justice-department|Justice Department]]'s classified records prosecution of Trump.[1]

## Superseding indictment

In a superseding indictment filed on Thursday, July 27, 2023, the Justice Department accused Trump of acting with Nauta and with Mar-a-Lago property manager [[carlos-de-oliveira|Carlos de Oliveira]] in an attempt to delete surveillance footage at Trump's Mar-a-Lago property.[1] The filing brought the total number of counts facing Trump to 40 and added a further [[espionage-act|Espionage Act]] charge tied to military documents Trump had reportedly boasted of possessing in a recorded meeting, in which he warned that he could not share them because he had not declassified them.[1]

## Commentary

The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of July 28, 2023 covered the superseding indictment under the heading "The Walls Are Closing in ... Or So We're Told," treating the new charges as part of a recurring media narrative cycle rather than a decisive legal turn.[1] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that the [[truth-community|Truth Community]] greeted each new indictment with anticipation rather than alarm, interpreting the prosecutions through the lens of [[devolution|devolution]] and an ongoing disclosure operation.[1]

## December 2023 unsealing (co-defendant)

Nauta, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s longtime aide, was named a co-defendant in the [[trump-classified-documents-case|classified documents case]] alongside Mar-a-Lago property manager [[carlos-de-oliveira|Carlos De Oliveira]]; both pleaded not guilty.[2] Judge [[aileen-cannon|Aileen Cannon]]'s December 4, 2023 order unsealing docket entries 223, 224 and 230 — over the objection of special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] — was characterized as a win of sorts for the co-defendants amid litigation over what classified materials they could view.[2]

## Motions against the special counsel report (January 2025)

Exactly one week before January 13, 2025, Waltine Nauta and co-defendant [[carlos-de-oliveira|Carlos De Oliveira]] filed two emergency motions to enjoin the release of [[jack-smith|Special Counsel Jack Smith]]'s final report, opening a flurry of court filings before [[aileen-cannon|Judge Aileen Cannon]].[3] Smith had indicted Nauta, De Oliveira, and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the Southern District of Florida for alleged crimes related to Trump's retention of classified documents.[3] A brief filed that weekend argued that release of the report by Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] would severely prejudice the due process rights of the individuals identified in it.[3] Cannon's January 13 order set a Friday hearing on whether the volume concerning the documents case could be shared with members of [[congress|Congress]].[3]
