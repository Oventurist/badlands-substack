# Vadym Skibitsky

Maj. Gen. Vadym Skibitsky is the deputy head of HUR, [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s military intelligence agency. In remarks to The Economist published in early May 2024 and relayed by [[the-telegraph|The Telegraph]], he said Ukraine was preparing for peace talks with Russia because there was "no way to win on the battlefield alone."[1]

## May 2024 assessment

Skibitsky said both sides were then vying for "the most favourable position" ahead of possible negotiations, which he predicted could take place in late 2025.[1] He argued that, as with virtually all wars, negotiations would likely be the final stage of the conflict, and that even if Ukrainian forces succeeded in pushing Russian troops back to the border, that alone was unlikely to end the war — such wars, he said, "can only end with treaties."[1]

His timeline rested in part on an expectation that Russian arms production would plateau in early 2026 because of shortages of engineers and supplies.[1] The remarks came as Ukrainian forces, short of manpower and ammunition, were suffering repeated setbacks on the battlefield, conditions that had revived discussion of a negotiated settlement.[1] Badlands Media carried the item as a bonus entry in its May 7, 2024 news brief, without added commentary.[1]

## Confusion over Trump's "17 Patriots" claim

In July 2025, Skibitsky — identified as the deputy head of Ukraine's military intelligence agency (HUR) — said [[ukraine|Ukraine]] was unsure what US President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] meant when he announced that Kiev would receive "17 Patriots" from [[nato|NATO]] countries.[2] Trump made the statement during his meeting with NATO Secretary-General [[mark-rutte|Mark Rutte]] on Monday, saying a European member of the bloc would supply the American-made air defense systems but not specifying whether he was referring to whole [[patriot-missile-system|Patriot]] batteries or separate launchers.[2] Badlands commentary noted that the ambiguity spanned a range from 17 launchers to roughly 136, with corresponding differences in manpower and logistics planning for Ukrainian commanders.[2]
