# Bevelyn Williams

Bevelyn Williams is an American pro-life activist and mother who runs a Christian nonprofit organization that aims to help families in need. She became one of the best-known defendants prosecuted under the Freedom of Access to Clinic Entrances Act during the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration.[1]

## Prosecution and sentence

Williams received a 41-month prison sentence handed down by a New York judge who, according to reporting cited in the Badlands News Brief, "made it very clear in the courtroom that she was going to [make an] example out" of the pro-life activist for protesting at a Manhattan abortion facility in June 2020.[1]

Her case was cited as emblematic of a broader shift in federal enforcement. As of Biden's inauguration in January 2021, the federal government had brought fewer than 100 [[face-act|FACE Act]] prosecutions since the law's enactment in 1994. By 2024, the administration had opened 60 cases, of which only five targeted pro-abortion extremists; the other 55 targeted peaceful pro-life demonstrators.[1]

## Expected pardon

In January 2025, reporting indicated that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was expected to follow through on his promise to get pro-lifers jailed for peaceful protest "out of the gulags" via presidential pardon, and that Williams was among those expected to benefit. The pardons were framed as significant both for the pro-life movement — then preparing for its annual March For Life in Washington, D.C. — and for Americans who hoped Trump would remedy what they viewed as partisan prosecutions.[1]

Badlands Media commentator Ashe in America noted that on the Monday night of inauguration week, Williams's husband posted a video of himself preparing to leave for the prison to pick up his wife, which proved slightly premature; by Thursday he had posted another video indicating that Williams was finally coming home. The commentary characterized the development as the end, at least as a matter of [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] policy, of the political persecution of Christian conservatives.[1]
