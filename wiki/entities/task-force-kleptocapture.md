# Task Force KleptoCapture

Task Force KleptoCapture is a United States [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] unit created to enforce sanctions and export restrictions imposed on Russian individuals and entities, and to seize assets connected to sanctioned oligarchs.[1]

The task force's director, Andrew C. Adams, appeared among the officials announcing the February 2023 unsealing of the indictment of [[vladimir-voronchenko|Vladimir Voronchenko]] for sanctions evasion and money laundering, alongside U.S. Attorney [[damian-williams|Damian Williams]] of the [[southern-district-of-new-york|Southern District of New York]] and senior agents of [[homeland-security-investigations|Homeland Security Investigations]] and the [[fbi|FBI]].[1]

The Voronchenko case concerned more than $4 million in payments made to maintain four U.S. properties owned by the sanctioned oligarch [[viktor-vekselberg|Viktor Vekselberg]], as well as attempts to sell two of them — the type of proxy-maintenance arrangement the task force was established to unwind.[1]

## Vekselberg property forfeiture

Task Force KleptoCapture director Andrew C. Adams joined U.S. Attorney [[damian-williams|Damian Williams]], HSI New York Special Agent in Charge Ivan J. Arvelo and Acting FBI Miami Special Agent in Charge Maged Behnam in announcing a civil forfeiture complaint, filed in late February 2023, against six luxury real properties in Manhattan, Southampton and Fisher Island, Florida worth roughly $75 million and beneficially owned by Vekselberg.[2] The complaint alleged the properties were proceeds of sanctions violations and vehicles for international money laundering carried out by associates including Voronchenko.[2] Williams framed the filing, made on the anniversary of Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, as a signal that the United States would forfeit criminal proceeds and channel the money toward assistance for Ukraine under newly enacted law.[2]
