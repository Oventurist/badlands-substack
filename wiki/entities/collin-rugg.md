# Collin Rugg

Collin Rugg is an independent social-media journalist and news aggregator whose posts on Twitter/X frequently surface documents, clips, and archived material that later circulate through alternative and conservative media outlets.[1]

## Role in the Fani Willis Facebook posts story

In August 2023, Rugg published a widely circulated thread reporting that newly discovered Facebook posts by Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] showed her commenting on the 2020 presidential election results, including one post raising theories of election irregularity similar to those for which she had indicted [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and eighteen associates.[1] Rugg framed the discovery with the rhetorical question of whether the self-described "unbiased" and "fair" district attorney should indict herself.[1]

The specific post Rugg highlighted was dated November 4, 2020, and referenced the so-called Fulton County "water leak" episode: "Georgia could determine who is our next president. A TEAM of lawyers needs to watch them count every single VOTE. They can start in Fulton where we are having water leaks. What ballots are they throwing out? Georgia lets give an honest accounting. No stunts!"[1]

Rugg's thread was picked up by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]], which used it as the basis for a report arguing that Willis had pushed [[2020-election-fraud-claims|election fraud theories]] materially similar to those underpinning her prosecution of Trump.[1] Coverage of the item was subsequently aggregated by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] in its [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], where contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] used the episode to argue that officials who certified the 2020 result while aware of irregularities were themselves exposed to the conspiracy theories being applied to Trump.[1]

## Significance in alternative media

Rugg's function in the episode is characteristic of the [[decentralized-media|decentralized media]] ecosystem the Badlands writers describe: an individual account performing original document surfacing that legacy outlets did not, with the material then amplified through a chain of independent publications rather than through wire services.[1]
