# Rafael Grossi

Rafael Grossi is Director General of the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|International Atomic Energy Agency]].[1]

## Statement on the Barakah strike

Following the 17 May 2026 drone strike near the [[barakah-nuclear-power-plant|Barakah nuclear power plant]] in the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]], Grossi said that military activity threatening nuclear safety was "unacceptable," speaking after discussions with UAE officials about the incident.[1] The UAE's nuclear regulator reported no impact on reactor operations or public safety.[1]

## Pessimistic outlook on Iran diplomacy (February 2026)

In February 2026, as Director General of the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|International Atomic Energy Agency]], Grossi stated that the diplomatic window regarding [[iran|Iran]]'s nuclear program is narrowing.[2] He publicly expressed concern about monitoring and verification challenges related to Iran's nuclear activities, remarks that coincided with a large US military build-up in the Middle East and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s warning that Washington "may have to take it a step further" within roughly ten days.[2]

## Warning over Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear sites (April 2024)

On Monday, April 15, 2024, in the aftermath of Iran's weekend drone and missile barrage against Israel, Grossi said he was concerned about the possibility that [[israel|Israel]] might target Iranian nuclear facilities.[3] [[iran|Iran]] had closed its nuclear facilities on the Sunday over "security considerations," and although they reopened on the Monday, Grossi kept [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] inspectors away "until we see that the situation is completely calm."[3] "We are going to resume tomorrow," he told reporters in New York, adding that the closure "has not had an impact on our inspection activity."[3] Asked about the prospect of an Israeli strike on Iranian nuclear sites, he replied, "We are always concerned about this possibility," and urged "extreme restraint."[3]

## Assessment after the June 2025 US strikes on Iran
After US bombers and submarines struck Iran's Fordow, Natanz and Isfahan nuclear sites in June 2025, Grossi said he believed Iran's stockpile of near-bomb-grade enriched uranium — stored in special casks small enough to fit in the trunks of about ten cars — had been moved before the attack.[4] His assessment aligned with evidence cited by two Israeli officials that Iran had relocated equipment and uranium from the site in the preceding days, and with the admission by senior US officials that they did not know the whereabouts of the stockpile.[4] Grossi also said the strikes had caused "a sharp degradation in nuclear safety and security."[4]
