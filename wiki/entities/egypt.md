# Egypt

Egypt is an Arab republic described in Badlands analysis as the traditional military power of the Middle East.[1]

In April 2026, Egypt attended the [[antalya-diplomacy-forum|Antalya Diplomacy Forum]] hosted by [[turkey|Turkey]] alongside [[pakistan|Pakistan]] and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to discuss the [[iran|Iran]] situation.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] counted Egypt among the four states forming a new regional "security architecture," with Iran expected to join later, and posed the question of what would follow when Turkey, Egypt, and Pakistan concluded that the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]]'s campaign to conquer the Middle East had to be stopped kinetically.[1]

## Turkey–Egypt reconciliation and defense pact

In February 2026 Egypt and [[turkey|Turkey]] issued a joint declaration following their second Strategic Cooperation Council meeting, committing to expanded cooperation on trade, energy, defense, and regional security.[2] The declaration pledged intensified coordination with other regional states to support de-escalation in the Middle East and to create conditions for resumed negotiations between the United States and [[iran|Iran]] "based on good faith and mutual respect," and set the next High-Level Strategic Cooperation Council meeting for 2028 in Ankara.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Erdoğan]]'s Egypt visit as completing a "redemption arc."[2] Erdoğan had publicly supported the Arab Spring, which led to the 2011 overthrow of the Egyptian government and the 2012 election of [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] figure Mohamed Morsi; in 2013 a counterinsurgency organized by the US and Egyptian military loyalists under General al-Sisi reversed that outcome, and Turkish–Egyptian relations were severed until Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] facilitated contact at the end of 2023.[2] The resulting Turkey–Egypt defense pact, paired with the Turkey–[[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]]–[[pakistan|Pakistan]] pact, was presented as covering the Middle East's three continental gateways, with Egypt serving as the gateway to Africa.[2]

## Role in Phase Two of the Gaza plan (January 2026)

When Special Envoy for Peace Missions [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] announced the launch of Phase Two of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[comprehensive-plan-to-end-the-gaza-conflict|20-Point Plan to End the Gaza Conflict]] in January 2026, he credited Egypt, [[qatar|Qatar]] and [[turkey|Turkey]] with finalizing the plan and its necessary particulars.[3]

## Regional diplomacy, September 2024

Badlands commentary in September 2024 traced Egypt's centrality in a shifting Middle East.[4] Egypt became the first Arab nation to sign a peace deal with [[israel|Israel]] in 1979, after three decades of direct warfare; that peace agreement was suspended in May 2024 when the IDF moved to occupy the [[philadelphi-corridor|Philadelphi Corridor]], the narrow strip of land between Egypt and Israel that had always been under Egyptian control.[4] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said in early September 2024 that Israel had no intention of relinquishing the corridor to Egypt, further exacerbating tensions.[4]

The same week, [[turkey|Turkey]] and Egypt held a diplomatic meeting for the first time in 12 years — since the Arab Spring, when Turkey was accused of supporting the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]]'s temporary overthrow of the Egyptian government.[4] After Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] called for an emergency meeting of all Islamic world leaders during the Riyadh [[gulf-cooperation-council|GCC]] gathering, Saudi Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] left the meeting, went straight to the airport, and flew to Egypt.[4]

## Rafah border shooting (May 2024)

On May 27, 2024 a member of Egypt's security forces was killed in a shooting near the [[rafah-border-crossing|Rafah border crossing]] with the Gaza Strip, with Egypt's military spokesperson announcing an investigation and Egypt warning it would take necessary measures to prevent a recurrence.[5] Egyptian security sources said the soldier had opened fire after seeing an Israeli armoured vehicle cross a boundary line while troops pursued and killed several Palestinians, and that Israeli return fire killed him and touched off a wider exchange in which several Israeli soldiers were wounded.[5] Israel had seized the Rafah crossing from the Gaza side earlier that month, drawing strong Egyptian criticism.[5] Badlands commentary invoked the memory of Egyptian President Nasser and the mid-century Arab-Israeli conflicts, warning the episode risked dragging Egypt into the war; the incident is treated in full at [[2024-rafah-border-shooting-incident|the 2024 Rafah border shooting incident]].[5]
