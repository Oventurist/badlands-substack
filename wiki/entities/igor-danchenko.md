# Igor Danchenko

Igor Danchenko is the analyst identified as a primary sub-source for the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]] and one of the two defendants tried by Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]].[1]

## Role in the Durham investigation

Danchenko's trial, together with that of [[michael-sussmann|Michael Sussmann]], constituted essentially the whole of the courtroom output of the Durham inquiry.[1] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] invoked both prosecutions rhetorically — "how well did that work with Sussmann and Danchenko?" — in questioning whether the pursuit of individual indictments had ever been a viable route to accountability.[1]

The Danchenko case bore directly on Durham's central finding that the [[fbi|FBI]] "was too willing to accept and use politically funded and uncorroborated opposition research, such as the Steele dossier," and that the bureau relied on the dossier in FISA applications while knowing that material within it likely originated with a political campaign or political opponent.[1] Critics of the investigation cited the lack of convictions in the Danchenko and Sussmann matters as proof the [[durham-report|Durham Report]] amounted to nothing, while Badlands commentators argued the modest trial record concealed far more serious unexposed conduct.[1]

## The 2022 trial in Alexandria

Danchenko's trial took place in an Alexandria, Virginia courtroom and was winding down in mid-October 2022, when Badlands Media contributor [[brian-cates|Brian Cates]] described him as a member of the [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] 2016 campaign's "dirty tricks team."[2] Cates argued that Durham used the proceeding, as he had the Sussmann case, to surface evidence concealed for more than five years.[2]

Among the disclosures Cates enumerated: the FBI verified not a single allegation drawn from the dossier before using it in a spy warrant on [[carter-page|Carter Page]]; in October 2016, unable to corroborate the material with the election approaching, the Bureau offered [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]] up to $1 million to substantiate it, which Steele could not do; and at the time the warrant issued Steele had still not identified his primary sub-source, whom the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] team supposedly did not learn was Danchenko until late December.[2]

Once Danchenko was identified, the FBI became aware that its Baltimore field office had run a counterintelligence investigation of him for suspected espionage, so that by January 2017 the Bureau knew Steele's primary sub-source was a suspected Russian agent.[2] The warrant on Page was nonetheless renewed three times by Crossfire Hurricane and, in the final renewal, the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller Special Counsel's Office]].[2] The Bureau also paid Danchenko as a Confidential Human Source for three and a half years, from March 2017 through October 2020.[2] Cates attributed the burial of the espionage inquiry to a series of "miscommunications" between Danchenko's FBI handler and the Baltimore field office.[2] The trial further established that one of the dossier's sources was longtime Democratic operative [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles H. "Chuck" Dolan]].[2]

## Reaction to the acquittal

Durham's failure to convict Danchenko drew a dismissive response from Badlands Media contributor [[pepe-lives-matter|Pepe Lives Matter]], who wrote in October 2022 that the prosecution "was never about a minor offense" and that the real reckoning concerned crimes against children and treason.[3] In that framing the Danchenko case was a pawn sacrificed in pursuit of higher-level figures such as [[hillary-clinton|Clinton]], [[barack-obama|Obama]] and [[george-soros|Soros]], with the writer arguing that "lying means nothing" beside the underlying conduct and that each perceived legal loss served mainly to raise public awareness.[3]

## Day one of the 2022 trial

Igor Yurievich Danchenko was the third individual indicted by Special Counsel [[john-durham|John H. Durham]] as part of his examination of the origins of [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]], the [[robert-mueller|Mueller]] Special Counsel, and possible wrongdoing connected to the 2016 presidential campaigns and the Trump administration.[4] Beginning in March 2016 he was hired by former MI6 officer [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]] to work for [[orbis-business-intelligence|Orbis Business Intelligence]] and to investigate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], [[paul-manafort|Paul Manafort]], [[carter-page|Carter Page]], [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] and others associated with the Trump campaign, with the goal of establishing a basis for allegations of collusion between Russia and the campaign; the information he supplied was folded into the Steele or "Company" reports, and some of it was later used in [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|FISA warrant applications]] targeting Page.[4] He became Steele's sub-source and was ultimately charged with five counts of willfully and knowingly making materially false statements to the [[fbi|FBI]].[4]

The trial opened on 11 October 2022 in the Eastern District of Virginia at Alexandria before United States District Judge [[anthony-trenga|Anthony J. Trenga]], with Danchenko represented by [[stuart-sears|Stuart A. Sears]] and [[danny-onorato|Danny Onorato]] of [[schertler-onorato-mead-and-sears|Schertler, Onorato, Mead & Sears]] — the same firm that had represented Hillary for America in the [[michael-sussmann|Michael Sussmann]] case.[4]

Pre-trial argument on day one disclosed that Danchenko had been the subject of an earlier FBI counterespionage investigation, opened around 2009–2010 and closed after the Bureau mistakenly concluded he had left the country, and that FBI Supervisory Intelligence Analyst [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]] had inquired about Danchenko by name to the Baltimore field office in 2012, roughly two years after the case was closed.[4] Prosecutors also revealed that Danchenko had signed an [[danchenko-immunity-agreement|immunity agreement]] in January 2017 requiring full and complete disclosure, which the government argued he violated by withholding information about his contacts with [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]] and with the person he claimed was [[sergei-millian|Sergei Millian]].[4]

The defence position, previewed in argument and in its motion to dismiss, was that Danchenko gave truthful answers to ambiguous questions and that the agents interviewing him had not been sufficiently clear, particularly as to whether he attributed all or only part of [[dossier-report-2016-95|Report 2016/95]] to "Source E."[4] The defence sought to introduce Amtrak records placing Danchenko in New York from about 26 to 28 July 2016 and a 28 July 2016 Facebook message to his then-wife, sent with a photograph of giraffes at the Bronx Zoo and the words "Another meeting tonight," which it said referred to the anticipated meeting with the supposed Millian.[4]

Prosecutors told the jury that Danchenko had described himself to Dolan as "working on a very important project against Donald Trump."[4] Auten testified that Danchenko was identified as Steele's primary sub-source only in December 2016, that Danchenko was interviewed over three days in January 2017 by Auten and Agent [[steve-somma|Steve Somma]] with [[david-laufman|David Laufman]] and Richard Scott of the National Security Division attending and Danchenko's counsel Mark Schamel present, and that Danchenko spoke English and never appeared confused by the questions.[4] Danchenko was subsequently made a Confidential Human Source of the Washington Field Office during what Auten termed "Crossfire Hurricane 2.0," handled first by Somma and then by Agent [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]]; even after becoming a source he never corroborated the dossier's allegations.[4] Durham also introduced LinkedIn messages Danchenko sent in 2020 to a person named Anastasia, the year his source status was terminated in October 2020.[4]

## Day four and the verdict

On the fourth and final day of testimony, Durham, Assistant Special Counsel Michael Keilty and DOJ Trial Attorney Brittain Shaw closed the government's case in chief with Agent [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]] recalled for redirect, former analyst [[brittany-hertzog|Brittany Hertzog]], Supervisory Special Agent [[amy-anderson|Amy Anderson]] and Supervisory Special Agent [[ryan-james|Ryan James]].[5] Helson testified that in his initial discussions with Danchenko the name of [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]] never came up, and that questions about Dolan put to Danchenko on 15 June 2017 yielded nothing: Danchenko disclosed neither his messages and meetings with Dolan nor that Dolan-derived material had entered the dossier.[5] Records introduced through James showed that [[sergei-millian|Sergei Millian]] was outside the United States from 14 to 27 July 2016 and that no call in Danchenko's phone records matched the purported ten-to-fifteen-minute anonymous call from the man he "believed to be" Millian.[5]

After the lunch break the defence declined to cross examine James and Durham rested; the defence then rested without presenting a case in chief at all.[5] That Friday afternoon Judge [[anthony-trenga|Trenga]] dismissed Count One of the indictment, and the jury, receiving Counts Two through Five the following Monday, returned a not guilty verdict by Tuesday evening.[5] The Badlands account argued that acquittal was near inevitable absent testimony from Millian, and framed the trial's real significance as the record it created: the dossier's allegations discredited, Dolan referred for investigation and now a subject of the [[durham-special-counsel-investigation|Durham Special Counsel]], and [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]] and Helson exposed as subjects of Inspector General inquiries.[5] It also noted that a year after the FBI offered [[christopher-steele|Steele]] $1,000,000 for corroboration, the Bureau turned Danchenko against Steele and sent him back in to gather intelligence on what Steele was doing.[5]

## Day two of the trial

On the second day of trial the government's evidence focused on Danchenko's contacts with [[sergei-millian|Sergei Millian]] and [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles Dolan]].[6] Danchenko's account to the FBI in January 2017 shifted between interview days — two emails and a phone call, then one email, a call, and a further email in September — and he turned over only a single Russian-language email concerning Millian while withholding others.[6] Those withheld messages, obtained by [[john-durham|Durham]], showed that Danchenko emailed Millian on July 21, 2016, that Millian forwarded the message to [[dmitri-zlodorev|Dmitri Zlodorev]] on July 26 rather than replying, and that Danchenko wrote to Zlodorev in late August that "Sergei doesn't respond."[6] The only means of contact Danchenko offered Millian were his email address and telephone number, a point Durham established to pre-empt the defense theory that Millian might have reached him through an application such as Skype or FaceTime.[6]

The same day's [[kglobal|KGlobal]] records showed Danchenko and Dolan corresponding on August 19, 2016, and the article characterized the pair — a Clinton-linked Democratic operative and a Russian lawyer and [[brookings-institution|Brookings Institution]] analyst — as working together to gather damaging material on Donald Trump for [[christopher-steele|Steele]]'s dossier.[6] Danchenko had told the FBI about the June 2016 Moscow conference but omitted Dolan's name, and the article observed that withholding such information would have breached his immunity agreement.[6] Defense counsel [[danny-onorato|Danny Onorato]] emphasized Danchenko's habitual hedging — "I believed it to be," "I was under the impression" — and that the Lewandowski and [[paul-manafort|Manafort]] allegations Dolan passed him had already appeared in the press.[6]

## Day three of the trial

The third day of trial was devoted to two witnesses: [[charles-h-dolan-jr|Charles "Chuck" Dolan Jr.]] and Danchenko's FBI handling agent [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]].[7] Testimony established that both men were under investigation, that Danchenko had been well paid and had supplied substantial information as a confidential human source, that the FBI had tried to turn Danchenko against [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]], and that circumstantial evidence existed suggesting Danchenko may genuinely have received a phone call and planned a meeting with [[sergei-millian|Millian]] in New York.[7]

Dolan's testimony documented an extensive relationship with Danchenko: a March 2016 meeting in Washington with [[olga-galkina|Olga Galkina]], two April 29, 2016 emails including one attaching a Danchenko-prepared [[orbis-business-intelligence|Orbis]] briefing note on "Kompromat Nadzor in the Russian banking sector," spring 2016 correspondence about the [[young-presidents-organization|Young Presidents' Organization]] Moscow conference, joint attendance at that October 2016 conference alongside Russian officials, and a January 1, 2017 meeting at Tuckahoe Park at which Danchenko photographed him.[7] Dolan admitted lying to Danchenko in an August 19–20, 2016 exchange, passing off television and POLITICO material as inside information from a Republican source — material that surfaced two days later in dossier report 95.[7] Dolan called Danchenko immediately after the January 10, 2017 BuzzFeed publication of the dossier, before Danchenko's role as a source was public.[7]

Helson testified that he received the assignment to handle Danchenko from the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] team but was never part of it, having been asked to join and declined.[7] Their first meeting occurred at the Washington Field Office in the February–March 2017 window with Agents Jason Ruehle and [[steve-somma|Steve Somma]] present; Danchenko had no lawyer, spoke fluent English, and received roughly $3,000 in cash at the following meeting.[7] Recorded sessions on March 16, May 18 and June 15, 2017 were entered into evidence, together with an exhibit recording Danchenko's claim to have supplied "80 percent of the raw intelligence and half the analysis in the dossier."[7] Although Danchenko boasted of keeping records of his source communications, the dossier-related material he produced was slim, and he never gave Helson the Dolan emails, the Galkina messages or his Facebook records — all of which Helson first saw in trial preparation and said would have changed the course of the investigation.[7] By October 2017 Danchenko was telling Helson he had never met Millian face-to-face in the United States.[7]

The defense used Helson to portray Danchenko as a valuable source: Field Office Annual Source Reports of April 2, 2019 and March 2020 described inconsistencies as "minor," a payment justification supported $10,000 across at least twenty-five useful reports in his first year, and his stated motivation was recorded as patriotism toward the United States.[7] Evidence also showed Helson advised Danchenko to scrub his phone of anything leading back to Steele and that the Bureau worked the two men against each other.[7] An exhibit established that Danchenko, whose priority as a source was to corroborate Trump–Russia collusion, could not "obtain any …information that would indicate collusion between the Trump campaign, the Trump administration, and the Russian government and/or any of its representatives."[7] On re-redirect, [[john-durham|Durham]] revealed that a former army counterintelligence officer in the FBI's human validation unit believed Danchenko was a GRU officer and wanted him polygraphed and closed as a source, and that Danchenko's 2009 solicitation of [[brookings-institution|Brookings Institution]] figures entering the Obama administration for saleable classified information was never revisited.[7]
