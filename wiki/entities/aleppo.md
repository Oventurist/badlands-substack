# Aleppo

Aleppo is the second-largest city in Syria and an ancient business hub in the north of the country.[1] In late November and early December 2024 it became the first major prize of a surprise offensive launched from Idlib by the jihadist group [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] (HTS) and allied factions, a capture described in wire coverage as a stunning gain for opponents of President [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar Assad]] that reignited a Syrian civil war which had been largely stalemated for several years.[1]

## The Assad Military Engineering Academy siege

Syria's Defense Ministry said in a Facebook statement published Wednesday, December 4, 2024, that army cadets and officers had been rescued near Aleppo in a joint Syrian-Russian counter-terrorism operation mounted to break a siege by Islamist militants.[1] Cadets from the Assad Military Engineering Academy near Aleppo fought off the attackers before being aided by what the ministry called "joint Syrian-Russian military-political coordination."[1] The ministry said jihadists had stormed the academy with medium- and heavy-caliber weapons and advanced drones, and that the students were subsequently encircled again by militants equipped with tanks, artillery, machine guns and drones; some cadets and officers were killed and others injured, though no figures were provided.[1]

## Strategic significance

Badlands commentary, citing *Breaking History* co-host [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], argued that Aleppo's value goes well beyond its size.[1] In 2004 Assad first unveiled what he called the [[five-seas-vision|Five Seas Vision]], a proposal for a regional trade and energy transportation corridor connecting five seas, at the heart of which sat a large railroad network whose natural geographic center is Aleppo.[1] Assad revived the proposal in 2015, and the call was answered in 2022 when Syria formally joined China's [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]].[1] In this framing, control of Aleppo is control of the hinge of a proposed Eurasian trade corridor rather than merely a battlefield gain.[1]

Aleppo lies to the north of [[hama|Hama]], which fell to the insurgents on Thursday, December 5, 2024, and further north of the capital [[damascus|Damascus]] — a geography that Badlands commentary described as "a tale of two cities," with CIA-linked mercenaries taking the city between Aleppo and Damascus while Russian special forces worked to secure the capital.[1]
