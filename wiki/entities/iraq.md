# Iraq

Iraq is a Middle Eastern state that in July 2026 became the territory on which US and Saudi military action against [[iran|Iran]]-backed militias was carried out.[1]

## July 2026 joint US–Saudi strikes

The United States and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] launched coordinated airstrikes against Iran-backed militia targets in Iraq after US officials said Iran-directed forces had attempted missile and drone attacks against American troops and Saudi energy infrastructure. Iraqi officials condemned the strikes as a violation of their sovereignty, while regional tensions continued to escalate.[1]

## Ukrainian backing claim

According to the Iraqi government, the militias operating in Iraq are being backed by [[ukraine|Ukraine]] — a conclusion Baghdad said it reached after interrogating captured operatives. Badlands commentary held that this account aligns with what Saudi Defense Minister [[khalid-bin-salman|Khalid bin Salman]] conveyed to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his team, namely that Saudi Arabia is not escalating against Iran and has no plans to do so, while [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and the Israelis are hellbent on escalation into open war.[1]

## Drone flight paths and secret Israeli bases (May 2026)

[[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] reported intercepting three drones approaching from Iraqi airspace in May 2026, in an incident separate from the strike near the [[barakah-nuclear-power-plant|Barakah nuclear power plant]] in the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]].[2] The Wall Street Journal reported that [[israel|Israel]] had built and defended a secret base in Iraq during the [[iran-war|Iran war]] to support operations against Tehran, and Badlands commentary described two secret Israeli bases in remote Iraq discovered by the Bedouin shepherd [[awad-al-shammari|Awad al-Shammari]], who was reportedly killed in a helicopter strike after reporting them.[2]

Commentary also noted that Iraq had recently joined a Saudi-led initiative to integrate economic infrastructure and investment across the Middle East, an endeavour potentially worth $100 billion, shortly before the drone incidents.[2]

## Iraq-Syria crude oil pipeline revival (2026)

US officials said in July 2026 that the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] is supporting efforts to revive a long-idled crude oil pipeline connecting [[iraq|Iraq]] to [[syria|Syria]]'s Mediterranean coast, giving Iraq an alternative export route and reducing its dependence on the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and the Persian Gulf.[3] Officials described the proposal as part of a broader strategy to strengthen regional energy security and limit [[iran|Iran]]'s leverage over global oil shipments, though significant infrastructure repairs, security challenges, and political agreements between Iraq and Syria would still be required.[3] Iran-backed Iraqi militias opposed the plan, with one prominent group threatening to disrupt any future pipeline project.[3] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted the announcement came less than a week after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] revealed his intention to remove Syria from the state sponsors of terrorism list, opening the country to international investors, and predicted Saudi involvement.[3] See [[iraq-syria-crude-oil-pipeline|the pipeline]] for fuller coverage.[3]

## 2026 anti-corruption purge and the Green Zone lockdown

In late June 2026 Iraqi authorities carried out a series of arrests as part of a corruption investigation linked to former Deputy Oil Minister [[adnan-al-jumaili|Adnan al-Jumaili]], according to Iraq's state-run Iraqi News Agency as cited by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]].[4] Security forces locked down Baghdad's [[green-zone|Green Zone]] — the fortified district housing key government institutions and foreign embassies — and conducted raids inside the area, with video footage showing tanks and other heavily armed vehicles deployed.[4] A security report obtained by the Associated Press said seven people were arrested, including five members of parliament whose immunity had been revoked, some of them linked to the political bloc of former Prime Minister [[mohammed-shia-al-sudani|Mohammed Shia al-Sudani]].[4]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] connected the sweep to the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|U.S. Treasury]] designation of [[salim-ahmed-said|Salim Ahmed Said]], an Iraqi-British national accused of running a network — including [[vs-tankers-fze|VS Tankers FZE]] — that blended Iranian oil with Iraqi oil and sold it to Western buyers through Iraq or the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]] using forged documentation, while bribing members of Iraqi government bodies including parliament.[4] He compared the affair to the 2018 sanctions [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] imposed on Iranian central bankers accused of laundering U.S. cash through Iraqi and UAE banks, and argued that despite predictions the new Iraqi government would align with [[iran|Iran]] against the West, the round-up of officials with Iranian ties showed [[donald-trump|Trump]] and the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] dismantling a [[deep-state|Deep State]] apparatus he speculated had been installed by Bush-era neoconservatives.[4]

## January 2026: ISIS prosecutions and the Al-Maliki candidacy

Iraq's highest judicial body announced in January 2026 that it planned to prosecute [[isis|Islamic State]] militants transferred from [[syria|Syria]] to Iraq, citing jurisdiction under Iraqi law; officials said the move followed coordination with international partners and related to detainees captured during anti-ISIS operations.[5]

In the same period, [[nouri-al-maliki|Nouri Al-Maliki]] was announced as the Shia party's candidate in Iraq's upcoming elections, generating unusual attention in the information space.[5] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Iraq is normally ignored because it has been presumed under [[deep-state|Deep State]] control since Saddam Hussein's fall, and noted that Al-Maliki — prime minister from 2006 for two consecutive terms — was among the figures the United States installed after the invasion.[5] [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] issued thinly veiled warnings against any Iraqi alliance with [[iran|Iran]], and the [[jerusalem-post|Jerusalem Post]] alleged that Al-Maliki had helped create ISIS.[5] The commentary recalled [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s remark that everyone the U.S. installed in Iraq ended up being ISIS.[5]

## Red Sea pipeline revival (April 2026)

In April 2026 Iraq was reported to be seeking to revive the long-idled [[iraq-saudi-pipeline|Iraq–Saudi Pipeline (IPSA)]] through [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to the Red Sea, creating an alternative crude export route outside the Persian Gulf that would bypass the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[6] Iraqi officials framed the project as part of a broader effort to diversify export infrastructure and reduce dependence on Gulf shipping lanes amid rising regional instability.[6] Badlands commentary noted that the Iraqi government elected by popular vote in February is aligned with Shia Islam and [[iran|Iran]], yet is executing deals that could produce a public reconciliation between Arabs and Persians.[6]

## In "Trump Cuts the Head off the Snake"

Joe Lange's account of the transformation of the Middle East treats Iraq as the arena in which the confrontation between [[donald-trump|Trump]] and [[iran|Iran]] came to a head, stressing a chain of connections running Iraq–Iran–[[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[7] After American airstrikes on 29 December 2019 against [[kataib-hizballah|Kataib Hezbollah]] facilities in Iraq and Syria, Baghdad warned that its relations with Washington were at risk, with officials accusing the United States of acting without sufficient evidence, violating Iraqi sovereignty and threatening the country's security.[7] Demonstrators drawn mostly from the [[popular-mobilization-forces|Popular Mobilization Units]] then carried out the [[2019-baghdad-embassy-attack|attack on the US embassy in Baghdad]], and questions were raised as to whether Iraqi forces had allowed them to reach the heavily restricted compound.[7]

Trump emphasised to Prime Minister [[adil-abdul-mahdi|Adil Abdul-Mahdi]] that Iraq bore responsibility for protecting the embassy and its personnel.[7] On 3 January 2020 the United States killed [[qasem-soleimani|Qasem Soleimani]] and [[abu-mahdi-al-muhandis|Abu Mahdi al-Muhandis]] near Baghdad International Airport, where Soleimani had arrived to meet the same prime minister.[7] The article notes that around 5,000 American troops were based in Iraq at the time and that the [[quds-force|Quds Force]] had trained Iraqi militants in the use of explosively formed penetrators that killed 196 US troops over the preceding decade.[7]
