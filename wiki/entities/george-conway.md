# George Conway

George Conway is an American attorney and prominent critic of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and the former husband of Trump adviser Kellyanne Conway.[1]

## Role in the E. Jean Carroll litigation

Conway was identified as the person who persuaded advice columnist [[e-jean-carroll|E. Jean Carroll]] to sue Trump over her allegation that he had sexually assaulted her in a Bergdorf Goodman changing room in the mid-1990s.[1] According to reporting confirmed by Carroll under questioning in January 2024, Conway urged her to file suit following a meeting at a party hosted by columnist [[molly-jong-fast|Molly Jong-Fast]].[1] The claim had first been reported in April 2023 and was subsequently acknowledged by Carroll herself in court.[1]

Commentary published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] treated Conway's involvement as evidence that the Carroll suit formed part of a coordinated political effort against Trump rather than a spontaneous legal grievance, and argued that the case illustrated how the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure had been used to pursue political opponents.[1]

## Prospective congressional run (November 2025)

George T. Conway III, the former conservative lawyer and one of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s fiercest critics, moved closer in November 2025 to announcing a run for Congress in one of Manhattan's wealthiest and bluest districts, pitching himself to potential donors as a top attorney who does not "have any more effs to give."[2] Conway, 62, then living in Bethesda, Maryland, hired Democratic pollster Anna Greenberg for a budding campaign to succeed retiring Representative [[jerrold-nadler|Jerrold Nadler]], and was scouting apartments in the district where he had lived and worked before relocating to Washington in 2017, when his wife at the time, Kellyanne Conway, joined the first Trump administration as a senior White House official.[2] The Conways divorced in 2023.[2] He outlined his pitch at a private virtual event for paid members of [[democracy-docket|Democracy Docket]], the media outlet founded by Democratic attorney [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]].[2]
