# Jake Sullivan

**Jake Sullivan** is identified by Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] as one of the principal architects of the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|Iran Nuclear Deal]] at the policy level, and as a recurring character in the geopolitical drama the [[deep-state|deep state]] series has been tracking.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Affiliations

Sullivan is associated with the so-called 7th Floor group at the [[state-department|State Department]], the [[carnegie-endowment-for-international-peace|Carnegie Endowment for International Peace]], the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]], and the Rhodes Scholarship.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) DeLarme groups him with [[william-burns|William Burns]] as one of "two certified swamp creatures in high places."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Career

Sullivan was the top cabinet official for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and had earlier been a substantial behind-the-scenes part of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s campaign.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) At the time of the Iran Deal negotiations, [[tom-donilon|Tom Donilon]] was technically Sullivan's boss.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Sullivan's role in the deal places him among the officials of the foreign policy establishment who, in DeLarme's account, fought to keep the United States from withdrawing from the agreement.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Badlands framing of the Seditious Six link

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted in November 2025 that Sullivan's wife, Rep. [[maggie-goodlander|Maggie Goodlander]], was one of the [[seditious-six|Seditious Six]] lawmakers who urged service members to refuse illegal orders, quipping that "sedition is a Sullivan family value."[2] She described Sullivan as a key figure in the autopen scandal and Russiagate — in her view two overt acts in a "treasonous grand conspiracy" — and wondered whether the Justice Department would use Goodlander as leverage over Sullivan in the way it reportedly used Michael Flynn Jr. against General Michael Flynn, calling the tactic distasteful but poetic.[2]

## April 2024 Iran sanctions announcement

As White House National Security Advisor, Sullivan released a statement on April 16, 2024 announcing new sanctions against [[iran|Iran]] in the wake of the regime's attacks on [[israel|Israel]] the preceding weekend.[3] He said President [[joe-biden|Biden]] was "coordinating with allies and partners, including the [[g7|G7]], and with bipartisan leaders in [[congress|Congress]], on a comprehensive response."[3] The statement specified that "in the coming days, the United States will impose new sanctions targeting Iran, including its missile and drone program as well as new sanctions against entities supporting the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]] (IRGC) and Iran's Defense Ministry."[3]

The announcement came amid Republican criticism that the Biden administration had not been tough enough on Tehran, particularly after the [[white-house|White House]] extended a waiver in November 2023 that gave Iran access to $10 billion of previously escrowed funds.[3] Badlands commentary noted that one of the sanctions targeted Iranian oil sold to [[china|China]] — by a wide margin the leading consumer of that oil — and claimed the Western hegemony intended to assign an agent to supervise every such purchase.[3]

## Statement on the 2023 Niger coup

As National Security Advisor, Sullivan issued the administration's condemnation of the [[2023-niger-coup|Niger coup]] in the week it occurred, saying: "We strongly condemn any effort to detain or subvert the functioning of Niger's democratically elected government, led by President Bazoum."[4] He added that "[[niger|Niger]] is a critical partner for the United States," a framing that accompanied US statements of support for [[ecowas|ECOWAS]] and calls for an end to the conflict.[4]

## Response to the Orbán–Trump meeting (July 2024)

Asked in July 2024 about Hungarian prime minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]]'s plan to fly from the [[2024-nato-washington-summit|NATO Washington Summit]] directly to Mar-a-Lago to meet [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Sullivan, then the US national security adviser, said: "I haven't heard directly from anybody about this meeting, I've heard indirectly about it as many of you have."[5] He added that the meeting "certainly isn't coordinated with the Ukrainians," who he said had "great misgivings about any effort to negotiate some kind of fake peace with Russia without the Ukrainians being a part of that effort."[5]

Orbán had met [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] en route to the summit without briefing the Biden administration, and did not request a bilateral meeting with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[5]

## February 2024 threat briefing controversy

At the White House press briefing on Wednesday, February 14, 2024, Sullivan appeared alongside Press Secretary [[karine-jean-pierre|Karine Jean-Pierre]] and addressed the cryptic "serious national security threat" warning issued that day by House Intelligence Committee chairman [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]], later reported to concern the alleged [[russian-space-nuclear-weapon-program|Russian space nuclear weapon program]].[6] Sullivan said he had reached out to the Gang of Eight the previous week and that a meeting was scheduled for Thursday, and appeared baffled that Turner would take threat rhetoric public before that meeting.[6] He declined to confirm whether the meeting concerned the new threat, telling reporters: "Well, I — again, I'll — I'll leave it to you to draw whatever connections you want. All I can say is I've reached out to see Turner. Turner has gone out publicly. I'm going to go see Turner tomorrow. That's where I want to leave things for today."[6] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the whole sequence as theatre designed to push the House into passing the Senate's national security supplemental.[6]

## 2016 Clinton campaign post-mortem

As policy adviser to the [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] campaign, Sullivan is named among the officials who participated in the coordinated 2016 effort to tie [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], an effort the [[fbi|FBI]] was warned about that year before launching its own sweeping Trump-Russia investigation.[7] The matter is now the subject of a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] strike force established by Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]], and evidence related to the [[russiagate-hoax|Russia collusion hoax]] has been ordered presented to a federal grand jury.[7]

## Cancelled meeting with Lord Cameron, April 2024

In April 2024, Sullivan — described by The Telegraph as [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s most senior foreign policy official — cancelled a planned meeting with British Foreign Secretary [[david-cameron|Lord Cameron]] during Cameron's trip to Washington, replacing it with a phone call after "diary clashes" related to a visit from the Japanese prime minister.[8] Cameron, who had flown to the US on the Monday evening and met Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] and Republican presidential candidate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], had arrived with a "plea" for continued American support for Kyiv and hoped to persuade wavering Republicans to back a bill funding the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[8] Two of his planned meetings were cancelled after Sullivan and House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] said they were too busy.[8]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] offered a caustic comedic take, mocking Sullivan as a coward whose lack of honor explained why he was not respected by his foreign counterparts, and joking that "the fact that he calls his calendar a 'diary' is just too perfect."[8] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] added that it was the second time Sullivan had failed to meet a world leader or leader-adjacent figure — the earlier instance being a physical ailment that prevented his meeting with [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], a meeting Trump appeared to front-run — and speculated that Sullivan and the rest of what he called the "Deep State department" refused the Cameron meeting because they were "sick of getting caught up in Trump honeypots," asking how the enemy could count on anyone's loyalty after a closed-door meeting with Trump.[8]

## December 2023 message to Israel

During a visit to Israel in mid-December 2023, national security adviser Jake Sullivan said the Israel Defense Forces needed to wrap up the current "high intensity" phase of its Gaza offensive "within weeks."[9] His remarks were cited as an indicator of the message Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] was expected to deliver during his own visit to Israel on December 18, as global pressure on both Tel Aviv and Washington to scale back the campaign intensified.[9]

## August 2024 Beijing trip

In late August 2024 Sullivan made a three-day visit to Beijing, the first such trip by a sitting US national security adviser in eight years, concluding with a meeting with [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] on Thursday, August 29.[10] The most consequential exchange came immediately beforehand, when Sullivan met [[zhang-youxia|Zhang Youxia]], a vice chairman of China's Central Military Commission, who confronted him over growing US support for [[taiwan|Taiwan]]'s military and stated that reunification of Taiwan with the mainland is "the mission and responsibility" of the [[peoples-liberation-army|People's Liberation Army]] and would not be deterred.[10] A Chinese defense ministry statement accompanying the meeting demanded that the United States "stop military collusion between the U.S. and Taiwan, stop arming Taiwan and stop spreading false narratives about Taiwan."[10]

Sullivan responded in conciliatory terms, remarking that "it is rare that we have the opportunity to have this kind of exchange" and stressing "the need for us to responsibly manage U.S.-China relations," while a follow-up White House statement highlighted "progress in sustained, regular military-military communications over the past 10 months."[10] Badlands Media read the encounter as a humiliation, describing Sullivan as visibly unsettled while Zhang appeared confident, and treating the brief Xi meeting as a photo opportunity rather than a substantive session.[10] The outlet argued the episode illustrated that foreign leaders — including Xi, Vladimir Putin and Mohammed bin Salman — do not regard Sullivan or the wider "Deep State Department" as legitimate representatives of the United States.[10]

## Demand for Israeli "clarity and transparency" (October 2024)

During a call in early October 2024, Sullivan told Israel's minister for strategic affairs [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] that the United States expected "clarity and transparency" from Israel about its plans to retaliate against Iran, because those plans carried implications for U.S. forces and interests in the region.[11] The exchange came as four U.S. officials described a deepening trust crisis, one saying, "Our trust of the Israelis is very low right now and for a good reason."[11] Sullivan was also scheduled to meet Israeli Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] in Washington on October 9, a trip cancelled at [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s direction.[11] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry characterized Sullivan as "present but forgotten because nobody respects him," part of an argument that the White House was sidelined in Israeli decision-making.[11]
