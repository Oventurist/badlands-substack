# Aaron Burr

Aaron Burr was the third vice president of the United States and, in Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], the organising figure behind a series of British-aligned conspiracies to break apart the young American republic.[1]

## Alleged conspiracies

Ehret writes that Burr led at least three attempts to destroy the republic, in 1800, 1804 and 1807, in which [[robert-livingston|Robert Livingston]] took part.[1] In 1799 Burr is said to have established a financial control grid within the United States, a branch of the City of London inside America dubbed "Wall Street."[1] The scheme attributed to him involved declaring himself a kind of tyrant-king of a new western front, seizing control of the White House, and forging an alliance integrating the free states with British Canada into a confederacy under his rule; insiders blew the whistle before the plan could proceed, and earlier incarnations had failed largely because of interventions by [[alexander-hamilton|Alexander Hamilton]] in 1797, 1800 and 1804.[1] The article cites Gerald Therrien's *Canadian History Unveiled* Volume 4 as documenting the story in detail.[1]

## Exile and connections

By the time the [[american-academy-of-fine-arts|American Academy of Fine Arts]] was created, Burr had recently departed for London, where he stayed five years in the house of Jeremy Bentham.[1] The Federalist speculators and political intelligence operatives around Trumbull's academy — James Pinchot, Thomas Clark and Ogden Haggerty — are described as implicated in the Burr-led conspiracy to dissolve the union.[1]
