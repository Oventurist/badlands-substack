# TJ Ducklo

TJ Ducklo is a Democratic communications operative who served as a senior adviser for communications on [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s 2024 presidential campaign.[1] He became a focal point of reporting on the campaign's adversarial posture toward the press in the spring of 2024.[1]

## Public dispute with the media

Ducklo posted on X complaining that after the president spoke to roughly 1,000 mostly Black voters in Philadelphia about the stakes of the election, MSNBC, CNN and other outlets declined to carry the event, choosing instead to cover "a trial that impacts one person: Trump."[1] He added that the same outlets would then ask why the campaign's message was not getting out.[1]

When the election statistician and Substack writer Nate Silver responded that Democrats frequently complain the media do not cover [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s misdeeds enough, Ducklo replied that the exchange "perfectly incapsulates the disconnect between the ivory tower/beltway know-it-alls and voters," arguing that Trump's trials affect only Trump while his policies affect voters, and urging journalists to "stop covering polls & process."[1]

## Coverage and Badlands commentary

[[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] used Ducklo's exchange as the entry point for a June 2024 feature comparing Biden campaign staff to a basketball star perpetually "working the refs," and judged his suggestion that a former and potentially future president's legal troubles were not worth discussing to be "frankly, absurd."[1] The magazine placed the episode within a broader pattern in which Biden allies accused journalists of retreating to horse-race coverage while ignoring the threat of fascism.[1]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the piece as an instance of what he calls [[narrative-convergence|Narrative Convergence]] in inverted form: establishment media reaching the same conclusion as the Truth Community — that the Biden operation was mismanaging its campaign — while framing the cause as media hostility rather than the president's decline.[1] He argued that the fracturing co-dependence between the Biden administration and its press allies had turned a former strength into a weakness.[1]
