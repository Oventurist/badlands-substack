# Iran International

Iran International is a Persian-language international news outlet covering [[iran|Iran]]. It published the July 2026 report on Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]]'s roughly 90-minute podcast interview with establishment podcaster Javad Mogoei, in which Araghchi discussed the opening hours of the war and the depth of foreign intelligence penetration of the Iranian state.[1]

The outlet's framing emphasized the backlash the interview triggered among Iranian hardliners and media affiliated with the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Revolutionary Guards]], who accused Araghchi of recklessly disclosing sensitive security details and leadership vulnerabilities.[1] Iran International also quoted Omid Memarian, an Iran analyst at DAWN, who suggested one manifestation of the influence Araghchi described would be officials advocating policies that prolong the war against the Islamic Republic's own interests — "exactly the things that Israelis might wish for."[1]

## Ownership

Iran International is owned by [[volant-media|Volant Media]], a privately owned United Kingdom–based company whose ownership is disclosed in the footer of the outlet's own website.[2] Volant Media in turn is owned by British-Saudi film executive [[adel-abdulkarim-alabdulkarim|Adel Abdulkarim Alabdulkarim]], whom a 2018 Financial Times of London report accused of receiving funding from [[israel|Israel]] — an allegation he denied.[2] The channel beams its signal directly into televisions inside [[iran|Iran]].[2]

## Pezeshkian resignation hoax

On May 31, 2026, Iran International published the false report "Iran's president offers resignation, citing total takeover by IRGC commanders," which was picked up and cited as source material by [[fox-news|Fox News]], the [[jerusalem-post|Jerusalem Post]] and other outlets before the Iranian government denied it.[2] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the episode confirmed Iran International is a propaganda outlet, likely tied to [[british-intelligence|British intelligence]], whose aim was to persuade audiences that [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] hardliners had seized control of Iran's civilian government and thereby build support for renewed US–Iran hostilities.[2] The incident is covered at [[pezeshkian-resignation-hoax-2026|the Pezeshkian resignation hoax]].[2]

## Reporting on the 2024 Iranian banking cyberattack

On August 14, 2024, Iran International reported that the [[central-bank-of-iran|Central Bank of Iran]] and several other banks had been hit by a significant cyberattack causing major disruptions to Iran's banking system, a claim that hours later remained uncorroborated by other sources and uncommented on by Iranian authorities.[3] The London-based anti-regime outlet described the incident as potentially one of the largest cyberattacks ever to target Iranian state infrastructure and reported that hackers may have accessed sensitive bank and credit card details of millions of Iranians.[3] Its Israel correspondent, Babak Itzhaki, claimed the Iranian government was attempting to conceal the breach and anticipated an official denial.[3] The outlet reported that the attack paralyzed all banking computer systems in Iran and resulted in stolen data, and framed the incident as a possible message from Western countries about the vulnerability of Iran's digital systems.[3]
