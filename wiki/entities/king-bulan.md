# King Bulan

King Bulan was the ruler of [[khazaria|Khazaria]] who converted his kingdom to Judaism in the mid-8th century, around 750 CE.[1] Matthew Ehret places him among four great ecumenical leaders of the era, pictured alongside [[charlemagne|Charlemagne]], [[harun-al-rashid|Harun al-Rashid]] and the Tang Emperor Xuanzong.[1]

Bulan's conversion is treated in the article as the decisive act that turned a Turkic steppe khaganate into a Jewish kingdom — but one whose court sat atop a majority population of Christians, Muslims and pagans, and which became renowned for tolerance rather than for the "evil Ashkenazi Jewry" attributed to it by later polemicists.[1] Before the conversion, the western Turkish Khaganate that founded Khazaria was shamanistic but carried a strong Confucian philosophy, with the Mandate of Heaven as a core belief.[1] Under Bulan's successors, Khazaria maintained a Supreme Court of Justice staffed by two Christians, two Muslims, two Jews and one pagan, and a military alliance with the [[abbasid-dynasty|Abbasid Dynasty]].[1]
