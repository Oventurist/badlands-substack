# Alex Jones

Alex Jones is an American broadcaster and host of the program Infowars, described in mainstream coverage as a conspiracy theorist. He is best known outside his own audience for having repeatedly characterized the 2012 Sandy Hook school shooting as a hoax, statements that later exposed him to ruinous civil liability and bankruptcy proceedings.[1]

## Ban and reinstatement on X

Jones and his Infowars show were permanently banned from Twitter in 2018 for what the platform described as abusive behavior.[1] In December 2023, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] restored Jones's account on [[x-corp|X]], the platform formerly known as Twitter, after posting a public poll asking whether Jones should be reinstated. Roughly 70 percent of respondents favored reinstatement, and early on the Sunday following the poll Musk wrote, "The people have spoken and so it shall be."[1] A few hours later Jones's posts were visible again and he retweeted a post promoting his video game.[1]

The Associated Press framed the restoration as posing new uncertainty for advertisers, who had already fled X over concerns about hate speech appearing alongside their ads, and characterized Jones as the latest divisive public personality to have a banned account returned.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] treated the reinstatement skeptically rather than celebrating it. Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported listening to Jones in an X Space—though on an alt account—while major influencers praised the supposed return of free speech to the public square.[1] Her assessment was blunt: "It's an Op." She noted that Badlands-affiliated writer Paul Fleuret remained banned from X and that every Badlands creator remained visibility filtered, a practice she summarized with the phrase "freedom of speech, but not freedom of reach."[1] The broader complaint was that [[con-inc|ConInc]] was gaslighting the public into believing censorship on X was no longer an issue, and that the useful signal lay in tracking who remained censored.[1]

## Infowars bankruptcy auction (November 2024)

In November 2024 the bankruptcy auction of Jones's [[infowars|Infowars]] assets was thrown into disarray after the presiding judge halted a sale that had apparently been won by the satirical publication [[the-onion|The Onion]].[2] The judge acted once it emerged that the anti-Jones bankruptcy trustee had not accepted the highest bid, instead allowing the Sandy Hook families to "assist" by pledging their large judgment against Jones toward the auction — a maneuver Jones argued was illegal.[2] Jones said a hearing was expected early the following week and predicted the judge would order a new [[infowars-bankruptcy-auction|auction]] in January, giving a Jones-allied group the chance to buy the company without the trustee effectively picking the winner.[2]

## Split with Owen Schroyer (2025)

On August 31, 2025, longtime Infowars host [[owen-schroyer|Owen Schroyer]] announced he was leaving the network immediately after nearly ten years, citing a long-growing rift with Jones over Schroyer's critical coverage of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s ostensible relationship with Israel.[3] Schroyer said he had offered to work another week and to appear on stream with Jones so the departure could be framed amicably; Jones refused and told him never to return to the studio.[3] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry cited the split, alongside episodes involving [[tim-pool|Tim Pool]] and [[turning-point-usa|Turning Point USA]], as evidence that platforms he described as dishonest about Israel were fracturing.[3]

## February 2025 bankruptcy settlement ruling

On February 5, 2025, U.S. Bankruptcy Judge Christopher Lopez blocked a proposed settlement between Jones and the families who sued him over his false claims about the 2012 Sandy Hook Elementary School shooting, complicating the proposed sale of the [[infowars|Infowars]] platform.[4] The ruling threatened to create divisions between the families who won nearly $1.3 billion in Connecticut court cases and those awarded $50 million in Texas courts.[4]

Lopez, ruling at a hearing in Houston, rejected the settlement on the ground that the families were asking him to divide up the assets of Infowars' parent company, Free Speech Systems, even though that company had been dismissed from bankruptcy the previous year.[4] "I can't do that," Lopez said. "That case is closed."[4]

## Planned lawsuit against the CIA

In April 2024 Jones announced that he planned to sue the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] following the release of an undercover sting recording in which an alleged CIA officer claimed the US intelligence community "took his money away" in order to "chop his legs off."[5] The employee, a CIA contracts officer in San Diego named [[gavin-oblennis|Gavin O'Blennis]], told the undercover journalist that "You can kind of put anyone in jail if you know what to do," describing a method of setting targets up until they act on impulse — "some would call that entrapment."[5]

Asked whom the agency had targeted, O'Blennis named Jones, implying the agency had been involved in the litigation against him and had encouraged Sandy Hook families to sue, telling them "there's no federal statute being broken but you have the option for a civil case and it's a pretty good case in our opinion."[5] He also remarked that there is "nothing like putting out a fake social media thing to like really get people mad."[5]

## Commentary on the February 2024 AT&T outage

During the nationwide [[at-and-t|AT&T]] cellular outage of February 22, 2024, Jones — described by Gizmodo as America's most well-known professional conspiracy theorist — insisted on his show that there was an 80 percent chance the disruption was the work of China, with a smaller chance that it was "the globalists themselves" carrying out a "false flag" attack.[6] AT&T said that evening that the outage was not a cyberattack but the result of "the application and execution of an incorrect process" during network expansion.[6]
