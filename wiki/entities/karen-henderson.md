# Karen Henderson

Karen Henderson is a judge of the federal appeals court in Washington, D.C., appointed by President George H.W. Bush, who sat on the three-judge panel that heard [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s claim of immunity from criminal prosecution.[1]

## Trump immunity appeal

At oral argument on Tuesday, January 9, 2024, Henderson joined her colleagues in expressing deep skepticism of Trump's contention that a president could not be prosecuted — even for assassinating a rival or selling military secrets — unless he had first been impeached and convicted by Congress.[1] She summarized the difficulty with the argument by saying, "I think it's paradoxical to say that his constitutional duty to take care that the laws be faithfully executed allows him to violate criminal law."[1]

Henderson sat alongside Biden appointees [[florence-pan|Florence Pan]] and [[michelle-childs|Michelle Childs]]; although the panel appeared inclined to reject the immunity claim, the judges seemed divided over how to frame their decision, and any ruling was expected to trigger a further appeal to the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[1]
