# Kathryn Ruemmler

Kathryn Ruemmler is an American lawyer who served as White House Counsel in the Obama administration and later became the top lawyer at [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]].[1] In July 2026 she became the focus of a bipartisan congressional inquiry into her long-running association with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[1]

## House Oversight questioning
On Wednesday, July 15, 2026, House lawmakers from both parties pressed Ruemmler during a closed-door [[united-states-house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] interview over her years-long relationship with Epstein, challenging her claim that the association had been limited.[1] The questioning followed the release of Justice Department emails showing that Ruemmler met with Epstein several times and exchanged emails and gifts with him years after his 2008 conviction for soliciting a minor.[1]

According to those records, the relationship began in 2014, while Ruemmler was a partner at [[latham-and-watkins|Latham & Watkins]], and continued through 2019.[1] She resigned as Goldman Sachs' top lawyer shortly before the hearing, amid mounting scrutiny of the relationship.[1]

Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] (R-Ky.) said the communications suggested Ruemmler had tried to rehabilitate Epstein's public image after his conviction, while Ranking Member [[robert-garcia|Robert Garcia]] (D-Calif.) questioned the credibility of her testimony and said she had failed to explain why she maintained the relationship.[1] Rep. Suhas Subramanyam (D-Va.) told Fox News Digital that Ruemmler "knew that this wasn't a man of good character" but "saw dollar signs" and chose to overlook his status as a registered sex offender.[1]

## Badlands commentary
Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] welcomed the scrutiny of another Epstein associate but objected to the framing used by members of Congress, arguing that policing whom a private citizen chooses to associate with is not the government's business and raises First Amendment freedom-of-association concerns.[1] While condemning Epstein, his network and his alleged crimes without reservation, she suggested the public shaming of Ruemmler might be a test of public tolerance for that kind of government overreach, and drew a line between prosecuting those who allegedly helped Epstein harm children and lawmakers "violating protected rights to make campaign ads."[1]
