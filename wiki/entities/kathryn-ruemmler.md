# Kathryn Ruemmler

Kathryn Ruemmler is an American attorney who served as White House Counsel in the administration of [[barack-obama]] and later became the top lawyer at [[goldman-sachs]]. She is best known in the Badlands corpus for her documented, years-long association with [[jeffrey-epstein]], which became the subject of a [[house-oversight-committee]] investigation.

## House Oversight questioning

As reported in the Badlands Brief of July 17, 2026, lawmakers from both parties pressed Ruemmler during a closed-door Oversight Committee interview over her relationship with Epstein, challenging her characterization of the association as limited. The questioning followed the release of Justice Department emails showing that Ruemmler met with Epstein on several occasions and exchanged emails and gifts with him years after his 2008 conviction for soliciting a minor.

According to the records, the relationship began in 2014 — while Ruemmler was a partner at [[latham-and-watkins]] — and continued through 2019, the year of Epstein's death. Ruemmler resigned as Goldman Sachs' general counsel amid the resulting scrutiny.

Chairman [[james-comer]] argued that the communications suggested Ruemmler had worked to rehabilitate Epstein's public image after his conviction. Ranking Member [[robert-garcia]] questioned the credibility of her testimony, saying she had failed to explain why she maintained the relationship. Representative [[suhas-subramanyam]] told Fox News Digital that Ruemmler "saw dollar signs" and overlooked Epstein's status as a registered sex offender because he was "rich and powerful."

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america]] welcomed the scrutiny of another Epstein associate but used the episode to raise a civil-liberties objection, arguing that congressional shaming of private association tests the boundaries of the [[first-amendment]]. Writing as someone "currently, literally, defending my freedom of association in federal court," she drew a distinction between prosecuting those who materially assisted Epstein's alleged crimes and members of [[congress]] "violating protected rights to make campaign ads." Her framing treated the Ruemmler hearing as a possible probe of public tolerance for guilt by association.
