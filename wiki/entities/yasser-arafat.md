# Yasser Arafat

Yasser Arafat was the leader of the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) and the central Palestinian figure in the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]], the 1993 agreement with Israeli Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin that established the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] and carried the promise of a two-state solution.[1] Badlands commentary notes that the peace talks were first initiated by the then-new Russian Federation.[1]

## The Oslo signing

Although Arafat is generally credited with the Oslo Accords, the Badlands account emphasizes that the document was signed on behalf of Palestine not by Arafat himself but by a young [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] at the ceremonies held at the Clinton White House.[1] This was read as a symbolic gesture by Arafat and the Arabs toward the West: a promise that the PLO — which had spent decades in bloody conflict with Israel — would be riding off into the sunset, allowing the new, peace-seeking Palestinian Authority to govern Palestine and make peace with its neighbors.[1]

## Death and aftermath

Arafat was, in the Badlands telling, assassinated with poison in 2004.[1] His death was followed by the entry of [[hamas|Hamas]] into real political activity in Gaza — then controlled by [[fatah|Fatah]] and occupied by the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] — with Hamas using Arafat's death to rally support for a more militant activism against their ostensible oppressors.[1] Hamas defeated Fatah in the 2006 election, immediately attacked Fatah security forces and drove them out of Gaza, then turned on the IDF.[1]
