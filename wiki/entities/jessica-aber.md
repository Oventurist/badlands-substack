# Jessica Aber

Jessica D. Aber was an American federal prosecutor who served as United States Attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia from 2021 to 2025. She was found dead at the age of 43 at a residence in Alexandria, Virginia, on a Saturday in March 2025, a death that drew heavy attention in independent media because of her recent tenure at the center of one of the country's most consequential federal prosecutorial districts.[1]

## Career

Aber was appointed United States Attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia by former President Joe Biden and held the office from 2021 until January 20, 2025 — the same day President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was inaugurated for his second term.[1] In her resignation letter she described the role as "an honor beyond measure," and thanked Senators Mark Warner and Tim Kaine, President Biden, and Attorney General Merrick Garland for what she called his "steadfast leadership."[1] She also wrote that she was proud of the work her office had done "with our federal, state and local law enforcement partners to enforce the law and build community trust."[1]

## Death and reporting

The Alexandria Police Department said it received a call about an "unresponsive woman" at approximately 9:18 a.m. and that, "as a matter of protocol, an investigation is underway surrounding the circumstances of her death," with the Office of the Chief Medical Examiner of Virginia to determine cause and manner of death.[1] Fox News subsequently reported, citing a family friend and unnamed sources, that police believed the death was the result of a "longstanding medical issue" and that Aber had suffered from a chronic medical condition.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] focused less on the medical claim itself than on the information environment surrounding it, noting that a Times of India article carried the most current information in search results while the local Fox affiliate that originated the "longstanding medical issue" detail was buried further down — a search-ranking pattern the author treated as symptomatic of the wider info war.[1] The commentary acknowledged an instinctive skepticism toward the official explanation, framing the suspicion as an involuntary product of critical thinking rather than a settled claim, and speculated in general terms about "loose ends" surviving from the Biden administration while expressing sympathy for Aber's family and friends.[1] No evidence of foul play was reported.[1]
