# Nathan of Gaza

Nathan of Gaza was the self-appointed public-relations man for [[sabbatai-zevi|Sabbatai Zevi]], who declared Zevi the Jewish Messiah sometime before 1666.[1] That declaration launched what became [[sabbateanism|Sabbateanism]], described by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] as among the most successful, popular and destructive Jewish reform movements in history.[1]

Nathan's promotion of Zevi's messianic claim gave the movement its public momentum in the years immediately preceding Zevi's entry into the holy land and his subsequent arrest and forced choice between conversion to Islam and a test of divine protection.[1] Zevi's conversion produced the crypto-Sabbatean phenomenon and, a century later, the antinomian escalation associated with [[jacob-frank|Jacob Frank]].[1]

Deschamps invokes this history as background to the religio-political struggle over the Holy Land visible in the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]].[1]
