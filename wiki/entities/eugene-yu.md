# Eugene Yu

Eugene Yu is the founder and chief executive officer of [[konnech|Konnech]], a software company that tracks the behavior of United States election workers.[1]

## Arrest and dismissal

Yu was arrested in early October 2022 on suspicion of stealing the personal information of United States election workers and illegally storing that data on Chinese computer servers.[1] According to the Los Angeles County District Attorney's office, Yu was required by contract to keep Konnech's data within the United States and to provide access only to American citizens and permanent residents, but instead stored the information on servers located in the People's Republic of China.[1]

The [[los-angeles-county-district-attorney|Los Angeles County District Attorney's office]] later asked the court to dismiss the case without prejudice. A spokesperson, Tiffany Blacknell, Director of the Bureau of Communications for the office, said the office was "concerned about both the pace of the investigation and the potential bias in the presentation and investigation of the evidence," and had decided to seek dismissal while alerting the public in the interest of transparency.[1]

## Aftermath

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] wrote that the dismissal was unlikely to be the end of the matter, noting that nothing prevents Yu from being indicted again by the Los Angeles District Attorney or by prosecutors elsewhere in the country where Konnech operated, and citing [[gregg-phillips|Greg Phillips]]'s view that more would be heard about the case because it touches on national security.[1]

## "The keystone" (December 2022)

In his December 28, 2022 essay "What's Taking So Long?", Lange described Yu as "the keystone" — in his framing, the key to a nationwide conspiracy using voting machines connected to the Chinese Communist Party.[2] Because elections are designated critical infrastructure, Lange argued that such a conspiracy, if true, would constitute an act of war, and that only the military possessed the evidence required to prove it, since the matter involves national security.[2] He suggested that the dropping of charges against Yu created the possibility that Yu had flipped and was cooperating with investigators, and guessed that a new indictment would follow.[2]

Lange placed Yu at the centre of his argument that prosecutions rather than election challenges are the mechanism by which fraud evidence would reach a courtroom, contending that prosecuting election officials would clear the deliberately high burden of proof needed to overturn an election — a burden he judged [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]]'s lawsuit could not meet.[2] He also raised the possibility of a large RICO case proceeding in the background.[2]
