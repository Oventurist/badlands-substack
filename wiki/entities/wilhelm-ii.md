# Wilhelm II

**Kaiser Wilhelm II** was the German emperor whose reign spanned the construction of the Anglo-French–Russian entente system and the outbreak of the First World War.[1]

In 1897 [[sergei-witte|Sergei Witte]] explained to Wilhelm the case for a "Continental League," urging that "we must first make all haste toward the establishment of solid united relations between Russia, Germany, and France," after which the rest of continental Europe would join and be freed "from the burden which she imposes on herself on account of reciprocal rivalry."[1] Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] treats this Russo-German-French alliance for progress as the alternative that the [[entente-cordiale|Entente Cordiale]] was built to destroy.[1]

Writing in August 1914, Wilhelm concluded that "England, Russia, and France have agreed among themselves… to take the Austro-Serbian conflict for an excuse for waging a war of extermination against us," a design "slowly and cleverly set going by [[edward-vii|Edward VII]] and… finally brought to a conclusion by George V."[1] He described Germany as squirming "isolated in the net" while England twisted "the noose of our political and economic destruction out of our fidelity to Austria," and admitted a bitter admiration: "Edward VII is stronger after his death than am I who am still alive!"[1]

The article states that Germany was the last nation prepared for the war set in motion by the architects of the entente, and that after defeat under the [[treaty-of-versailles|Versailles Treaty]] the country was stripped of agriculture, mines, rail, industry and colonies.[1]
