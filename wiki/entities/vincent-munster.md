# Vincent Munster

Vincent Munster is a Netherlands citizen and chief of the Virus Ecology Section at the Rocky Mountain Laboratory in Montana, a facility of the [[national-institutes-of-health|National Institutes of Health]].[1]

## Criminal complaint

In June 2026 Munster and Claude Kwe, a Cameroon national and research fellow in Munster's section, were charged in a federal criminal complaint with conspiracy to smuggle monkeypox into the United States and making false statements to federal authorities, according to the US Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of Michigan.[1] The two are accused of attempting to bring biological materials into the country after traveling from the Republic of Congo during a monkeypox outbreak.[1]

Authorities say they arrived at Detroit Metropolitan Airport on January 25, 2026, where [[customs-and-border-protection|Customs and Border Protection]] officers examined a black case the men described as diagnostic equipment; investigators allege it held 113 vials in Styrofoam coolers, with 17 of those tested containing deactivated monkeypox virus, one chickenpox virus and two human DNA.[1] The case is being investigated by the [[fbi|FBI]], Customs and Border Protection, and the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|HHS]] Office of Inspector General; both men face up to five years in prison if convicted.[1]

## Mpox smuggling indictment (May 2026)

In May 2026, Munster and a colleague were indicted for allegedly smuggling mpox virus samples into the United States without proper documentation and for making false statements to customs officials.[2] The case was one of five actions surfacing after April 2026 against scientists affiliated with the [[national-institute-of-allergy-and-infectious-diseases|National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases]], alongside the debarment referral of [[ralph-baric|Ralph Baric]], the indictment of [[david-morens|David Morens]], the removal of [[jeffrey-taubenberger|Jeffrey Taubenberger]], and the Senate subpoena of [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]].[2] Proponents of the [[lab-leak-theory|lab-leak theory]] presented the cluster as accountability for the COVID-19 origins cover-up.[2]
