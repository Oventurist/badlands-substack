# Club of Rome

The Club of Rome is described in Badlands Media reporting as the vehicle of a 1968 neo-Malthusian revival that used computer models to justify supposedly "fixed limits to humanity's growth".[1]

## Intellectual lineage

According to [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], the closed-system theories advanced by [[british-east-india-company|British Imperial]] economists such as [[john-stuart-mill|J. S. Mill]] and [[david-ricardo|David Ricardo]] were not only the basis on which Marx and Engels crafted their theory of class struggle — ignoring the anti-imperial American economic school of figures like [[henry-c-carey|Henry C. Carey]] — but also the basis of the Club of Rome's neo-Malthusian revival.[1] Its [[limits-to-growth|limits-to-growth]] modelling recast the [[malthusianism|Malthusian]] doctrine in the language of systems analysis.[1]

## Absorption into Davos

These models were incorporated into the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] during the 1973 event that saw the crafting of the [[davos-manifesto|Davos Manifesto]], outlining [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]]'s notions of [[stakeholder-capitalism|stakeholder capitalism]].[1] Ehret treats this transmission as a key link between nineteenth-century imperial economics and the contemporary [[transhumanism|transhumanist]] and degrowth ideologies promoted by figures such as [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]].[1]

## Founding, funding and the World Problematique

Citing historian [[f-william-engdahl|F. William Engdahl]], Badlands contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that the Club of Rome was founded in 1968 by [[david-rockefeller|David Rockefeller]] together with [[aurelio-peccei|Aurelio Peccei]], a senior manager of the Fiat car company owned by the Agnelli family, and [[alexander-king|Sir Alexander King]], head of the [[oecd|OECD]] Science Program and a consultant to [[nato|NATO]].[2] Fiat's Gianni Agnelli was an intimate friend of Rockefeller since 1957, sat on the International Advisory Committee of Chase Manhattan Bank, and became a founding member of the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] in 1973.[2] Peccei and King are described as self-professed Malthusians who promulgated the doctrine that the age of scientific progress and industrial growth must stop so the world could reset its values under a paradigm of zero technological growth.[2]

Both men advocated the pseudoscience dubbed [[world-problematique|"World Problematique"]], the "science of global problems," whose adherents held that the future could be known by analysing the infinite array of problems humanity creates in modifying its environment, and whose implicit conclusion was that eliminating "the problem that causes all problems" would produce a world of perfect stasis and predictability.[2] Reporting on the agenda's growth in 1972, OECD Vice Chair and Club member Hugo Thiemann told *Europhysics News* that a serious conflict was developing between planetary dimensions and population and that "science policy should be guided by preservation of the biosphere."[2] The organization quickly set up branches across the Anglo-Saxon world among members who agreed that society's best form of governance was a scientific dictatorship.[2]

## Canadian branch and the Limits to Growth study

Financing for the paradigm shift came through the [[1001-nature-trust|1001 Trust]], founded in 1970 by [[prince-bernhard-of-the-netherlands|Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands]], many of whose members were also Club of Rome members — including Canada's [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]], later Vice President of the [[world-wildlife-fund|WWF]].[2] The Club of Rome–MIT study that became [[limits-to-growth|*Limits to Growth*]] was not begun in the United States but at Montebello, Quebec in 1971, when Club backer [[pierre-trudeau|Pierre Trudeau]] allocated taxpayer money to start the project; a network of Rhodes Scholars and Privy Councillors around Alexander King, Maurice Strong, [[maurice-lamontagne|Maurice Lamontagne]], Michael Pitfield and Governor General Roland Michener presided over that meeting, after which funding passed to the Volkswagen Foundation.[2]

## Criticism

Canadian scientist [[ronald-hayes|Ronald Hayes]] attacked the Club-driven reconstruction of Canadian science policy in his 1973 book *The Chaining of Prometheus*, and [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]] argued in 1982 that the greatest single cause of ecological destruction over the previous fifteen years had been toleration of the policies demanded by the neo-Malthusians of the Club of Rome and allied institutions.[2] Ehret concludes that young people brainwashed by the Club of Rome may yet rediscover that life is enjoyable with a growing population, industrial progress and open system thinking.[2]
