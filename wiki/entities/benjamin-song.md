# Benjamin Song

**Benjamin Song** is one of nine defendants convicted by a federal jury in Fort Worth, Texas for roles in the July 4, 2025 attack outside the [[prairieland-detention-center|Prairieland Immigration and Customs Enforcement detention center]] in Alvarado, Texas.[1]

## Conviction

Prosecutors described the defendants as members of a "North Texas [[antifa|Antifa]] cell" involved in rioting, explosives offenses and providing material support to terrorists, according to the [[department-of-justice|U.S. Department of Justice]].[1] Authorities said the incident involved fireworks, vandalism and gunfire directed at responding law enforcement, and that Alvarado Police Lieutenant Thomas Gross was shot and injured during the confrontation.[1] Song was convicted of attempted murder and firearms offenses related to that shooting.[1]

Eight defendants were convicted of charges including rioting, providing material support to terrorists, conspiracy to use explosives and using explosives, with fireworks cited as the explosive devices.[1] Daniel Sanchez-Estrada was convicted of corruptly concealing documents, and he and Maricela Rueda were also convicted of conspiracy to conceal documents tied to the investigation.[1] Sentencing had not been announced at the time of reporting.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that while the term "[[domestic-terrorism|domestic terrorism]]" is often overused as a vehicle for overreach, attempting to bomb something for political purposes plainly qualifies, and that people planting bombs deserve prison.[1] She simultaneously insisted on due process rights for all accused persons and warned against expanding the domestic terror label to capture speech, assembly or association absent underlying criminal conduct, invoking the prosecution of [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] as the counterexample.[1] HSI Dallas Special Agent in Charge Travis Pickard called the case "a historic moment," describing it as the nation's first federal indictment targeting a coordinated group of Antifa cell members engaged in violent criminal activity.[1]
