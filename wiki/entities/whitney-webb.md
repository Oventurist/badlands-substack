# Whitney Webb

Whitney Webb is an independent investigative journalist and author who has become one of the most prominent voices in what Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] has termed the "anti-Trump, anti-establishment faction" (ATAEF) — commentators who oppose entrenched establishment power but decline to treat the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration as a genuine break from it.[1]

## Critique of the second Trump administration

Webb has expressed sustained skepticism about the Trump administration on the grounds of its perceived alignment with the [[deep-state|deep state]] and with entrenched bureaucratic and corporate interests.[1] Despite Trump's campaign rhetoric against the establishment, Webb has argued that many of his appointments and policies appeared to reinforce rather than dismantle those structures.[1] Her analysis frequently points to the influence of figures such as [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]], a member of the Bilderberg Steering Committee, and to the involvement of [[blackrock|BlackRock]] in shaping policy, which she reads as evidence of continuity rather than rupture with previous administrations.[1]

## Surveillance concerns

A recurring theme in Webb's commentary is the expansion of state surveillance capability through private contractors, particularly Thiel's [[palantir|Palantir Technologies]].[1] Palantir held a significant relationship with the U.S. government during Trump's first term, securing contracts with [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] for data analytics during immigration enforcement operations and with the Department of Defense for military intelligence work.[1] Reporting during Trump's second term indicated that the administration was considering, or had already considered, employing Palantir for a range of governmental functions in which data analytics could be presented as enhancing efficiency or security.[1]

Webb is frequently grouped with fellow independent journalist [[michael-tracey|Michael Tracey]] as an exemplar of this critical tendency.[1]

## One Nation Under Blackmail

Webb's book *One Nation Under Blackmail* is cited in the March 2023 Badlands essay "False Flags and the Occult" as revealing the shady and government-endorsed relationship between organized crime, government, banking and the intelligence community.[2] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] summarized her thesis as showing that criminal cartels do not merely take advantage of the banking system but work directly with it to manufacture chaos, which governments then enact legislation to solve, charging taxpayers top dollar in the process — a scheme he described as having become the foundation of all modern nations.[2] The citation anchors the article's broader argument that [[false-flag-operation|false flag]] events serve a banking-governmental industrial complex.[2]

## On Jeffrey Epstein's role

Ryan DeLarme's May 2024 feature quotes Webb's characterization of [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] as "not just a sex criminal, that's what they want to paint him as exclusively; he was a financial criminal who dabbled in sex crimes."[3] DeLarme extends the point, arguing that even "financial criminal" understates Epstein's role as a key executive in the vaguely labelled world of "transnational organized crime," an integral part of the amorphous [[deep-state|deep state]].[3] He cites Webb's *One Nation Under Blackmail* for the proposition that the marriage between organized crime and the intelligence community is one of the most critical yet best kept secrets in the West.[3]
