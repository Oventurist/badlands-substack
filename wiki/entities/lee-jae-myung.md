# Lee Jae-myung

Lee Jae-myung is the President of [[south-korea|South Korea]].[1]

In March 2026 Lee publicly addressed the transfer of American air defense assets out of his country, stating that Seoul had expressed opposition to [[us-forces-korea|US Forces Korea]] moving Patriot interceptors and related systems from Osan Air Base "according to its own military needs," while acknowledging that "it is also the reality that we cannot fully enforce our position."[1] The equipment was bound for bases in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] to replenish interceptor stocks depleted by the [[iran-war|war against Iran]].[1]

Lee added that the transfer would not cause a "serious setback" to South Korea's deterrence capability against nuclear-armed [[north-korea|North Korea]].[1]

## Role in the 2024 martial law crisis

Before his presidency, Lee led the opposition [[democratic-party-of-korea|Democratic Party]] during the [[2024-south-korea-martial-law-declaration|2024 South Korea martial law declaration]].[2] On the night of December 3, 2024, he posted a video filmed from the backseat of his car declaring that President [[yoon-suk-yeol|Yoon Suk Yeol]] was no longer president, and later livestreamed a selfie video of himself scaling the walls of the [[national-assembly-of-south-korea|National Assembly]] to reach the chamber.[2] Similar videos showed other opposition members of parliament following suit and climbing the walls of the legislature as they openly decried Yoon's action as a coup and called for his removal from office.[2] His party subsequently announced it would pursue treason charges and impeachment against Yoon.[2]

## Loudspeaker de-escalation with North Korea (June 2025)

Shortly after taking office in June 2025, Lee ordered the South Korean military to suspend its loudspeaker propaganda broadcasts across the inter-Korean border, in a move aimed at easing tensions and reopening dialogue with Pyongyang.[3] [[north-korea|North Korea]] appeared to reciprocate: South Korea's military reported that the North's own border broadcasts had stopped as of Thursday morning, June 12, 2025.[3] Joint Chiefs of Staff spokesperson Col. Lee Sung-Jun said that "there was no region where North Korea's noise broadcasts to the South were heard today," noting that the broadcasts had still been audible until around 11 p.m. the previous night; North Korea issued no official confirmation.[3] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the North had first responded to Lee's initiative by playing soothing, calming music before switching the speakers off entirely, joking that this marked the end of the "Poop Balloon Wars."[3]

## Trump's October 2025 visit

Lee was expected to host President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during the third stop of his weeklong Asia trip in late October 2025, at which Trump was also to deliver a speech to business leaders and attend a dinner with other heads of state.[4] Shipbuilding featured prominently in the accompanying trade discussions, with South Korean giant [[hanwha-ocean|Hanwha Ocean]] — owner of the Philly Shipyard since 2024 — positioned as the linchpin of an American effort to counter Chinese dominance in the sector.[4]

## Trump tariff letter (July 2025)

On Monday, July 7, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] imposed a 25 percent tax on goods imported from [[south-korea|South Korea]], effective August 1, notifying Lee as South Korean President through a letter posted on Truth Social.[5] Trump warned in the letters — sent also to Japanese Prime Minister [[shigeru-ishiba|Shigeru Ishiba]] — that any retaliatory tariff increase would be added on top of the 25 percent rate.[5] Badlands commentary framed the South Korea tariff as especially telling, citing Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]]'s characterization of the country as a CIA client state since the Korean War.[5]
