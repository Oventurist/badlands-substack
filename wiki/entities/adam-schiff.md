# Adam Schiff

**Adam Schiff** is a Democratic U.S. Senator from California and a member of the [[senate-intelligence-committee|Senate Intelligence Committee]], a long-standing figure of suspicion in Badlands commentary on intelligence-community leaks.[1]

## 2026 leak investigation commentary

When the [[fbi|FBI]] opened an investigation in May 2026 into possible leaks of classified [[national-security-agency|NSA]] intercept material by Democrats or staff on the Senate Intelligence Committee, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] questioned the framing that only staffers would be implicated and asked whose staffers — and which senators — were involved.[1] She closed her commentary by pointedly noting that Schiff is a senator on that committee.[1] She argued that the Speech and Debate Clause would not shield members from prosecution for espionage, unauthorized disclosure of classified information, conspiracy or obstruction, and that leaks aimed at undermining the President's ODNI pick, [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], could touch several of those exceptions.[1]


## Gabbard declassification (April 2026)

Documents released by [[tulsi-gabbard|DNI Tulsi Gabbard]] on 14 April 2026 identified Schiff — then chairman of the [[house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence|House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence]] — and Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] as the officials who used a false, second-hand narrative to create media intrigue and ultimately spark the basis to impeach President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in December 2019.[2] The Office of the Director of National Intelligence framed the episode as a coordinated effort by elements within the Intelligence Community, including former IC Inspector General [[michael-atkinson|Michael Atkinson]], to manufacture a conspiracy.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported that Schiff and Adam Kinzinger were rumored to be under investigation, in the same week that [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] and [[tony-gonzales|Tony Gonzales]] resigned from [[congress|Congress]] and further resignations were expected.[2]

## Badlands commentary on the whistleblower secrecy

Following the April 2026 criminal referrals against [[eric-ciaramella|Eric Ciaramella]] and former Inspector General [[michael-atkinson|Michael Atkinson]], Badlands contributor Ashe in America recalled that during the first Trump term Schiff was "super somber and serious" about the whistleblower, insisting that no one could say his name because the danger was real.[3] The commentary characterized that posture as a purposeful lie that helped maintain a Ukrainian money and secrets apparatus while undermining the agenda of the sitting president.[3]

## Named in Trump's "enemy from within" remarks

At an October 2024 [[fox-news|Fox News]] town hall hosted by [[harris-faulkner|Harris Faulkner]], [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] singled out Schiff, alongside former Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]], while defending his description of his political opponents as "the enemy from within."[4] Trump said of the two California Democrats, "These people — they're so sick, and they're so evil," and added that he was "not threatening anybody" because "they're the ones doing the threatening. They do phony investigations."[4]

## Durham hearing (June 2023)

At the [[durham-house-judiciary-testimony-2023|June 21, 2023 House Judiciary hearing]] on the [[durham-report|Durham Report]], Schiff continued to assert the legitimacy of the [[robert-mueller|Mueller]] report and the Russia collusion narrative, despite the report's conclusions about the [[steele-dossier|Steele dossier]].[5] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] called him "the king of misdirection and diversion" and described other Democratic members at the hearing as "Schiff-inspired buffoons" who used their questioning time on matters Durham said he had not been tasked to investigate.[5] The article's headline framed the day as Durham going off on the [[fbi|FBI]], the [[cia|CIA]] and Schiff.[5]

## Opposition to Kash Patel's FBI nomination (December 2024)

On Sunday, December 1, 2024, Schiff — then a congressman and incoming senator from California — demanded that the Senate reject the nomination of [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] as director of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]], continuing what Breitbart described as a long personal vendetta.[6] Schiff's antagonism toward Patel dated to 2017, when Patel helped expose the fact that members of the outgoing Obama administration had abused their power to "unmask" the names of Americans caught on foreign wiretaps — a practice thought to have contributed to the firing and prosecution of National Security Advisor [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] over allegations of collusion with Russia.[6]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Schiff had been comparatively quiet during the Biden administration after serving as one of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s loudest critics in his first term, and would return to Washington as a freshman senator having won Dianne Feinstein's California seat in November 2024.[6] She characterized his reaction to Patel's nomination as panic, arguing that Patel had been one of Schiff's primary targets and had promised extensive declassification if confirmed, making the prospect of disclosure "terrifying" to Schiff.[6] The Brief cited Mike Davis, former Chief Counsel for the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]], as having all but guaranteed Patel's confirmation despite Schiff's objections.[6]


## August 2024 exchange with Trump

In August 2024, while serving as a representative from California and as the Democratic nominee for a California U.S. Senate seat, Schiff mocked former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for suggesting that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] might reclaim the Democratic nomination.[7] Trump had posted on Truth Social that Biden — whose presidency he said was "Unconstitutionally STOLEN from him by Kamabla, Barrack HUSSEIN Obama, Crazy Nancy Pelosi, Shifty Adam Schiff, Cryin' [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]], and others on the Lunatic Left" — might crash the Democratic National Convention and challenge him to another debate.[7]

Schiff replied on X with the message, "It's ok to be scared," while California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] posted, "Oh, he's scared."[7] Two Republican anti-Trump groups, Republican Voters Against Trump and The Lincoln Project, also attacked Trump over the post.[7] Schiff had been among the Democrats who publicly pressed Biden to leave the 2024 race over concerns about his age, mental fitness, and electoral prospects, a campaign that culminated in [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] becoming the presumptive nominee.[7]

## Removal from the House Intelligence Committee (2023)

On January 24, 2023, House Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] formally blocked Schiff, along with fellow California Democrat [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]], from serving on the [[house-intelligence-committee|House Intelligence Committee]], acting on a pledge he had made before becoming Speaker.[8] Special rules for the panel permit the Speaker to halt the seating of lawmakers unilaterally without a full floor vote, and McCarthy conveyed the decision in a letter to House Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]], stating that he was rejecting the appointments "in order to maintain a standard worthy of this committee's responsibilities."[8] Jeffries had recommended Schiff and Swalwell for the committee despite McCarthy's earlier vow to block them and [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]].[8]

## 2024 Senate campaign launch

On Thursday, January 26, 2023, Schiff launched his 2024 campaign for the United States Senate, a move that set up a possible Democratic primary against incumbent Senator [[dianne-feinstein|Dianne Feinstein]].[9] His first campaign advertisement featured the voice-over line "When a dangerous demagogue tried to undermine our democracy, I wasn't about to let him" over footage of Donald Trump.[9] Schiff had served in Congress since 2001, was one of the lead investigators in Trump's first impeachment inquiry, and served as a lead impeachment manager in the second.[9] Justin Deschamps described him as a "deep state shill and mouth-piece" who had pushed the [[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate]] narrative from his seat on the House Intelligence Committee, contrasted his continued political career with the removal of [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] from committee positions, credited [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] with a good decision in removing Schiff from committee, and argued that the lies Schiff promoted were later used as a pretext to deny January 6 defendants due process.[9]

## Bondi Hearing (January 2025)

Badlands commentary listed Schiff, alongside [[dick-durbin|Dick Durbin]] and [[mazie-hirono|Mazie Hirono]], among the senators who repeatedly pressed attorney general nominee [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] on January 15, 2025 to affirm that Joe Biden won the 2020 election, and who reclaimed their time rather than let her describe what she said she had seen in Pennsylvania.[10]

## Earmarks and defense-contractor donations (2023)

While campaigning for a U.S. Senate seat on a platform of earmarks for local causes, the 12-term congressman was reported in September 2023 to have steered millions in taxpayer dollars to for-profit defense contractors, many of whom had donated to his campaigns.[11] An investigation by [[politico|Politico]] concluded that Schiff "has offered an incomplete and potentially misleading account of his record on earmarks," finding that in addition to money for homelessness and drug treatment he had directed millions to for-profit companies while raising tens of thousands of dollars for his House reelection campaigns from corporate executives and people connected to them.[11] The review was largely limited to publicly available data from the brief three-year window when corporate earmarks were disclosed.[11]

Two cases were highlighted as particularly egregious: $6 million steered to [[smiths-detection|Smiths Detection]] and $3 million to Phasebridge, Inc., both defense firms within his district, in moves that would have been barred under reforms adopted in 2010.[11] The funding coincided with Schiff receiving $8,500 in contributions from the [[pma-group|PMA Group]] PAC and from two family members of Paul Magliocchetti, founder and owner of the lobbying firm retained by both companies; Magliocchetti was sentenced in 2011 to 27 months in prison for making illegal campaign contributions.[11] Schiff claimed not to have a full accounting of his earmarks.[11]

His campaign moved quickly to minimize the connections, with spokesperson Marisol Samayoa saying "Californians know and trust Adam's record of getting things done" and arguing that the contributions "amount to less than one tenth of one percent of what Adam has raised over 20 years in Congress," having been "driven by what was best for our troops and his constituents."[11] Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] characterized the conduct as years of pay-to-play, noting the absence of clear reporting guidelines when a legislator earmarks funds for a contractor that donated to their campaign — or, alternatively, that Schiff had simply ignored them in a calculated move.[11]

## Considered for a preemptive pardon (December 2024)

Politico reported in December 2024 that Schiff, then a senator-elect from California, was among the figures named in White House deliberations over [[preemptive-pardons|preemptive pardons]] for officials who could be targeted after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s return to the presidency.[12] The reporting grouped him with other members of Congress' [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]], including former Representative [[liz-cheney|Liz Cheney]], and noted that alarm inside the [[joe-biden|Biden]] White House had accelerated after Trump named [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] to lead the [[fbi|FBI]].[12]

## Whistleblower allegations on classified leaks (2025)

In August 2025 Just the News reported that a career intelligence officer who had worked for Democrats on the [[house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence|House Intelligence Committee]] for more than a decade had repeatedly warned the [[fbi|FBI]], beginning in 2017, that Schiff had approved leaking classified information to smear then-President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over the Russiagate scandal.[13] The account came from FBI 302 interview reports that Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] turned over to Congress.[13] The staffer, a Democrat by affiliation who described himself as a friend of both Schiff and former Republican Intelligence Committee chairman [[devin-nunes|Devin Nunes]], considered the leaking "unethical," "illegal," and "treasonous," but said he was told not to worry because Schiff believed he would be shielded from prosecution by the Constitution's speech and debate clause.[13]

Badlands commentary tied the episode to Schiff's reported expectation that he would have become director of the CIA had Hillary Clinton won in 2016, drawing a parallel with [[rodney-joffe|Rodney Joffe]], who allegedly manipulated internet data to support the collusion narrative while expecting a top cybersecurity post under a Clinton administration.[13] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Schiff's access to classified information should be revoked immediately and that Congress, being implicated itself, could not be trusted to deliver justice.[13]

## Proposed shadow attorney general

Following the 2024 election, Representative [[wiley-nickel|Wiley Nickel]]'s [[shadow-cabinet-proposal-2024|shadow cabinet proposal]] named Senator-elect Schiff as an illustrative choice for shadow attorney general, a role in which he would publicly contest [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s efforts to replace career Justice Department attorneys with lawyers loyal to the president.[14]

## Cited as a cautionary example (July 2024)

In July 2024 commentary on the articles of impeachment against [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] urged House Republicans to pursue fact- and evidence-based charges and to avoid theatrics, instructing them: "Don't doctor tweets, like Schiff."[15]

## Federal mortgage fraud investigation (August 2025)

In August 2025 Fox News reported, via a Trump administration source, that Schiff was under federal criminal investigation for mortgage fraud, with the inquiry being conducted by the U.S. Attorney's Office in Maryland.[16] Host Laura Ingraham broke the news on "The Ingraham Angle" on Tuesday, August 5.[16] The investigation followed by roughly a month the disclosure that the [[federal-housing-finance-agency|Federal Housing Finance Agency]] had sent a criminal referral to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] alleging that Schiff had, in multiple instances, falsified bank documents and property records to obtain more favourable loan terms.[16]

## Clash with Kash Patel (September 2025)

In September 2025, Schiff's long-running feud with FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] escalated into a shouting match that briefly derailed a [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]] hearing.[17] Schiff, a Democratic senator from California, was questioning Patel about the [[justice-department|Justice Department]]'s decision to transfer [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]] to a lower-security prison facility when the confrontation erupted.[17]

Schiff asked whether Patel believed the American people were "stupid" enough to accept the claim that the [[bureau-of-prisons|Bureau of Prisons]] had made the decision independently rather than at the political direction of the White House.[17] Patel cut him off, accusing him of lying about Russiagate and January 6 and calling him "the biggest fraud to ever sit in the United States Senate" and "a political buffoon at best."[17] Chairman [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] appeared to cut Schiff's microphone during the exchange.[17] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] asked afterward whether anyone intended to prosecute Schiff or merely to yell at him.[17]
