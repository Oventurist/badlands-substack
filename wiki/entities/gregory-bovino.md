# Gregory Bovino

**Gregory Bovino** is a senior United States Border Patrol official whose conduct became the subject of a state-level criminal investigation in Minnesota in early 2026.[1]

## Hennepin County investigation

Hennepin County Attorney Mary Moriarty announced that her office had launched an investigation into the conduct of federal immigration agents tied to enforcement actions earlier in 2026, naming Bovino among the officials under scrutiny.[1] Moriarty said her office created a public portal for the submission of evidence, would probe the "potentially unlawful behavior" of Border Patrol agents, and would pursue charges where appropriate.[1]

The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] responded that immigration enforcement is a federal responsibility and that state prosecutors lack the authority to charge federal officers.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the Minnesota probe as part of an escalating pattern of states pursuing legal offensives against the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]], calling the use of state resources to investigate federal agents "a gratuitous and wasteful political stunt."[1]

## Defense of agents in the Pretti shooting

After federal immigration agents fatally shot 37-year-old U.S. citizen [[alex-jeffrey-pretti|Alex Jeffrey Pretti]] in Minneapolis on January 24, 2026, Bovino, as Border Patrol chief, publicly defended the officers involved, characterizing them as victims of the encounter and asserting that Pretti had interfered with their actions.[2] His defense contrasted with bystander video and witness accounts indicating Pretti had been filming officers and was pepper-sprayed and pinned down before the shots were fired.[2] Minnesota Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] and Minneapolis Mayor [[jacob-frey|Jacob Frey]] called the operation excessive and demanded the withdrawal of federal agents.[2]

## Disputed removal report (January 2026)

On January 26, 2026, The Atlantic reported that Bovino had been relieved of his duties in [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]], a claim conservative influencers amplified into virality online.[3] [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] spokesperson [[tricia-mclaughlin|Tricia McLaughlin]] disputed the report on social media, stating that "Chief Gregory Bovino has NOT been relieved of his duties" and describing him as "a key part of the President's team and a great American."[3] Both DHS and the [[white-house|White House]] denied the claim, and Badlands treated the episode as another outrage cycle that dissolved on contact with official rebuttal.[3]

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] observed that the "Commander at Large" position at the center of the dispute did not exist before the previous year.[3] The announcement coincided with President [[donald-trump|Trump]] dispatching Border Czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]] to take charge of federal immigration operations in Minnesota.[3]
