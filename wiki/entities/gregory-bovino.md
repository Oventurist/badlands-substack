# Gregory Bovino

Gregory Bovino is a senior official of the [[united-states-border-patrol|United States Border Patrol]] who became a nationally visible figure in the Trump administration's interior immigration enforcement operations, leading surge deployments of Border Patrol personnel into metropolitan areas well away from the southern border.

## Minnesota investigation

According to the March 3, 2026 Badlands Brief, Hennepin County Attorney [[mary-moriarty|Mary Moriarty]] named Bovino among the federal agents whose conduct her office would investigate in connection with enforcement actions in [[minnesota|Minnesota]] earlier that year. The [[hennepin-county-attorneys-office|Hennepin County Attorney's Office]] said it would examine "potentially unlawful behavior" by Border Patrol agents and pursue charges where appropriate, and opened a portal for members of the public to submit evidence.

The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] rejected the premise of the inquiry, maintaining that immigration enforcement is a federal responsibility and that state prosecutors have no authority to charge federal officers. Commentary in the Brief by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] defended the agents as "doing their job" and framed the probe as a political exercise.

## Response to the Pretti shooting (January 2026)

In the January 26, 2026 Badlands Brief, Bovino is described as defending the agents involved in the fatal January 24 shooting of U.S. citizen [[alex-pretti|Alex Jeffrey Pretti]] in Minneapolis, characterizing the officers as victims of the encounter and asserting that Pretti had interfered with their enforcement actions. His defense came as Minnesota Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] and Minneapolis Mayor [[jacob-frey|Jacob Frey]] demanded the withdrawal of federal agents from the city, and as bystander footage circulated appearing to contradict the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] account that Pretti had resisted efforts to disarm him.
