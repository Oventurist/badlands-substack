# Alessandra Serano

Alessandra Serano is a longtime American federal prosecutor who was appointed in June 2026 as the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s national coordinator for human trafficking and child exploitation cases.[1] The appointment was made by Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]].[1]

## Career

Serano has been a Justice Department attorney since 2003 and has served in multiple United States Attorney's Offices over the course of her career, including the Southern District of California, the District of the Virgin Islands, and most recently the Eastern District of Virginia.[1] Prior to her appointment as national coordinator she was working as senior counsel to the deputy attorney general, and had recently completed a temporary detail with the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]].[1]

## Role as national coordinator

In her new position, Serano is charged with overseeing federal efforts to investigate and prosecute trafficking and child exploitation cases across the Justice Department and partner agencies, and with coordinating with the Office of Justice Programs on victim services funding.[1] The role consolidates oversight of cases handled across all 93 US Attorney's Offices as well as specialized units within the DOJ Criminal Division, and elevates the position within the deputy attorney general's office.[1]

Within 120 days of the appointment, Serano is expected to help deliver an updated Justice Department strategy on combating trafficking and exploitation.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that the appointment was largely ignored by commentators otherwise consumed by the Epstein files controversy, and argued that the administration appeared to be quietly taking steps to roll up trafficking networks.[1] She highlighted the 120-day deliverable timeline, which would fall in mid-October 2026 — less than a month before the [[2026-midterm-elections|midterm elections]] — as politically notable.[1] The commentary also connected Serano's appointment to a recent DOJ announcement of an investigation into the status of roughly 300,000 unaccompanied immigrant children, including concerns that some may have been exploited through labor or sex trafficking via so-called "super sponsors."[1] The portfolio overlaps directly with the broader issue of [[child-trafficking]].[1]
