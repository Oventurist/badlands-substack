# United States Secret Service

The United States Secret Service is the federal law enforcement agency responsible for the physical protection of the President of the United States, the First Family, and other designated officials, in addition to its financial-crimes investigative mandate. In Badlands coverage the agency appears principally in connection with threats against President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Mar-a-Lago perimeter breach, February 2026

On Sunday, February 22, 2026, Secret Service agents, working alongside a Palm Beach County sheriff's deputy, shot and killed [[austin-tucker-martin|Austin Tucker Martin]], a 21-year-old North Carolina man who had breached the secure perimeter of Trump's [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]] resort in Palm Beach, Florida.[1] The shooting occurred at roughly 1:38 AM ET near the property's north gate.[1]

Martin was reportedly carrying a shotgun and a fuel canister. Officers ordered him to drop the items; he released the fuel can but raised the shotgun into a firing position, prompting agents and the deputy to fire.[1] Neither the President nor First Lady [[melania-trump|Melania Trump]] was at the property, both being at the White House at the time.[1] The [[fbi|FBI]] and local authorities took up the investigation into motive.[1]

## Context

The incident renewed scrutiny of the protective posture surrounding Trump, who publicly acknowledged persistent threats in the days afterward.[1] Badlands commentary tied the episode to a broader pattern of security concerns within the First Family, including reported anxieties about the 2025 Inauguration Day parade, which was ultimately held indoors.[1]

## 2024 supplemental funding

The short-term spending bill agreed by congressional leaders in September 2024 included an additional $231 million to bolster the Secret Service following the two assassination attempts against Republican presidential nominee [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[2] The measure otherwise funded federal agencies at current levels for about three months and also added money to aid the presidential transition.[2]

## White House cocaine investigation (2023)

In July 2023 the Secret Service led the inquiry into the [[white-house-cocaine-incident|cocaine found in a secure area of the White House]]. Spokesman [[anthony-guglielmi|Anthony Guglielmi]] said on July 10 that the probe was "still open and active at this time" and that no suspect had been identified, since identifying one "would come after an investigation is completed."[3] The [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] requested a briefing from the agency by that Friday.[3]


## Denial of security requests before the 2024 assassination attempt

The Washington Post reported on July 20, 2024 that top officials at the U.S. Secret Service had repeatedly denied requests for additional resources and personnel sought by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s security detail in the two years leading up to the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempt on his life]] at a rally in Pennsylvania.[4] According to four people familiar with the requests, agents protecting the former president had asked for magnetometers and more agents to screen attendees at sporting events and other large public gatherings, and for additional snipers and specialty teams at outdoor events.[4] Senior agency officials sometimes denied those requests — several of which were made in writing — citing reasons including a lack of resources at an agency long troubled by staffing shortages.[4]

The rejections produced lasting tensions pitting Trump, his top aides and his detail against Secret Service leadership, with Trump advisers privately worried that the agency was not doing enough to protect him.[4] After initially denying that it had turned down requests, the Secret Service acknowledged that some may have been rejected, while agency veterans said the organization had been forced into difficult trade-offs amid competing demands, a growing list of protectees and limited funding.[4] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the disclosures as Trump marking a target for online researchers, urging readers to "follow the money, follow the lies, find the contradictions" rather than settle on a single storyline.[4]

## Biden Delaware residence records (January 2023)

In January 2023 Fox News reported that the U.S. Secret Service was prepared to provide the names of individuals who had visited President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s Wilmington, Delaware, home if Congress requested them, in connection with the investigation into two troves of classified documents found at the property.[5] The [[white-house|White House]] maintained that no formal visitor log existed for the personal residence, but the Secret Service does collect information on guests with regular access to the home.[5] [[badlands-media|Badlands]] analyst [[just-human|Just Human]] observed that the agency sets up checkpoints around the residence and screens visitors, keeping logs as part of that work, and argued House Republicans should request the records regardless.[5]

## Protection for RFK Jr. and the Butler review (July 2024)

Following the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Donald Trump]] on July 13, 2024, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] directed the Secret Service to provide protection to independent presidential candidate [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], an instruction announced by Homeland Security Secretary [[alejandro-mayorkas|Alejandro Mayorkas]] at a White House press briefing on Monday, July 15.[6] The Kennedy campaign had sought protection long before the attempt.[6]

Biden also ordered an independent review of security at Trump's Butler County, Pennsylvania rally and of law enforcement's handling of the situation.[6] The gunman, [[thomas-matthew-crooks|Thomas Matthew Crooks]], had operated from outside the security perimeter yet fired from mere hundreds of feet from the stage, killing one spectator and critically injuring two others before a Secret Service sniper shot and killed him.[6]
