# John Bolton

John Bolton is a hawkish U.S. foreign-policy official named by Badlands Media among the members of the [[project-for-the-new-american-century|Project for the New American Century]].[1]

PNAC was created in 1997 by [[william-kristol|William Kristol]] and [[robert-kagan|Robert Kagan]] out of the neoconservative network around the [[american-enterprise-institute|American Enterprise Institute]], and was populated by Bolton along with [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney]], [[paul-wolfowitz|Paul Wolfowitz]], and [[donald-rumsfeld|Donald Rumsfeld]].[1] The article presents that group's [[pax-americana|Pax Americana]] doctrine as the origin of a decades-long push for regime change in Syria, culminating in Assad's fall in December 2024.[1]

## Tenure in the first Trump administration (Badlands account)

Bolton served in the first [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration from April 2018 until September 2019.[2] In a May 2026 Badlands Brief, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that it was his understanding that Bolton was responsible for the sanctions imposed on [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] and others in the [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] circle, a timeline he said "tracks" with Bolton's dates of service.[2]

Citing Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]], the author claimed Trump wanted nothing to do with Bolton until Israeli tycoon [[sheldon-adelson|Sheldon Adelson]] asked that Bolton be brought onboard, after which Trump reversed position.[2] The Brief speculated that Bolton was "in all likelihood" a spy sent by the Israeli oligarchy to infiltrate the Trump cabinet and report back, and that the administration's conduct between April 2018 and September 2019 suggests Trump was under the influence of the NeoCons and carrying out their Venezuela objectives, possibly unwittingly.[2] After Bolton's firing, [[erik-prince|Erik Prince]] was dispatched to meet secretly with Rodríguez in Caracas in November 2019.[2] These claims are Badlands commentary rather than documented findings.[2]

## Reaction to the March 2026 Iran campaign

Following the launch of the US–Israeli air campaign against [[iran|Iran]] in early March 2026, John Bolton warned that the strategy could lead to "turmoil and bloodshed" and urged clearer post-strike planning.[3] He also criticized the lack of consultation with Iranian opposition figures on the ground and what he described as insufficient strategic thinking about decisively removing the Iranian leadership structure.[3]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read Bolton's reaction as evidence that the operation was not the neoconservative forever war it appeared to be, arguing that Bolton — "the walking, talking mascot of the entire NeoCon Collective" — was "sounding the alarm instead of sounding the victory horn" precisely because [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was not pursuing full-scale regime change but rather the [[venezuela-model|Venezuela Model]].[3]

## Classified records prosecution and 2026 plea deal

Federal prosecutors charged Bolton earlier in 2026 after alleging that he kept diary entries and other records from his time in the first Trump administration at his residence; the original indictment included eight counts related to transmitting national defense information and ten counts tied to retaining such material.[4] Investigators also alleged that Bolton shared more than 1,000 pages detailing his daily activities through a personal email account with two unauthorized individuals, though those allegations were not folded into the count he was expected to plead to.[4]

CNN reported on June 4, 2026 that Bolton was expected to plead guilty later that month to a single count of illegally retaining sensitive national security documents.[4] Under the anticipated agreement he would pay a fine exceeding $2 million, with the charge carrying a potential sentence ranging from no incarceration to as much as five years; a plea hearing was scheduled for June 26.[4]

### Badlands commentary on Bolton's career

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded by cataloguing Bolton's career and asking in which of those jobs he had helped plan coups d'état: General Counsel and then Assistant Administrator at [[usaid|USAID]] (1981–1983); partner at Covington & Burling (1983–1985); Assistant Attorney General for Legislative Affairs at the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] (1985–1989); Assistant Secretary for International Organization Affairs at the [[state-department|State Department]] (1989–1993); partner at Lerner, Reed, Bolton & McManus (1993–1999); Vice President of the [[american-enterprise-institute|American Enterprise Institute]] (1997–2001); Under Secretary of State for Arms Control and International Security Affairs (2001–2005); U.S. Ambassador to the [[united-nations|United Nations]] (2005–2006); Of Counsel at Kirkland & Ellis and foreign policy adviser to Mitt Romney (2007–2018); and National Security Advisor to the President (2018–2019).[4] The author argued that the real question was not what Bolton put in his book but whether there would be accountability for a portfolio of work that "sold out the American people so the rest of the world could rise" — conduct she called literal treason.[4]

## August 2025 FBI search of Bolton's Maryland home

On Friday, August 22, 2025, the [[fbi|FBI]] searched Bolton's Maryland home, a pre-dawn raid that Democrats characterized as a politically targeted attack.[6] Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] defended the federal investigation in an interview with NBC's Kristen Welker that aired on "Meet the Press" that Sunday, saying he could not specify what Bolton might have done and deferring to the bureau: "Classified documents are certainly part of it. But I think that there's a broad concern about, about Ambassador Bolton. They're going to look into it. And like I said, if there's no crime here, we're not going to prosecute it."[6] Politico noted that Bolton served as [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s top national security official for 17 months during the president's first term before the two parted ways in September 2019, and that Bolton became a leading critic of the president, clashing with the administration over a 2020 tell-all memoir that the Justice Department sued to block over its purported use of classified material.[6] During the 2024 campaign Bolton told MSNBC's Jen Psaki that "there will be celebrations in the Kremlin" if Trump were elected.[6]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the raid on "Bolton's Maryland lair" as a "seismic declaration" and a deliberate thrust into the heart of the American [[deep-state|Deep State]] marking the dawn of what he called the Accountability Phase, with [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] declaring that "NO ONE is above the law."[6] In his reading, Bolton — "that mustachioed avatar of endless war" — is the first major domino in the cascade, allowing Trump to maintain [[narrative-shielding|Narrative Shielding]] against charges of partisanship by targeting a figure from his own first administration.[6] He described Bolton as the prime exemplar of modern False Conservatism, a rebranded Bush-era [[neoconservatives|NeoConservatism]] birthed in the aftermath of 9/11 that peddled interventionism as patriotism, and read his reemergence as retroactively codifying Trump's shadow war on war itself and his alignment with the theorized [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] of Trump, [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] and Xi.[6] The timing, he added, was auspicious — near the 9/11 anniversary and just before the premiere of [[brad-zerbo|Brad Zerbo]]'s [[codex-911|CODEX 9/11]].[6]

[[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] asked whether the episode would produce Bolton's most viral tweet and why "one side or the other" wanted that message seen.[6] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] again catalogued Bolton's career — beginning at [[usaid|USAID]] — and argued that while legacy media framed the raid as being solely about book-related leaks, it was also possible investigators would evaluate "his entire portfolio of criminal conduct," urging that he be placed under oath.[6]

## October 2025 indictment

On October 16, 2025, a federal grand jury handed down a lengthy criminal indictment against Bolton, charging him with mishandling classified information.[5] Prosecutors allege that while working in the White House between 2018 and 2019, Bolton shared contemporaneous notes containing classified material with his wife and daughter over his personal email and messaging apps.[5] After he left government, an Iranian allegedly hacked his email, accessed the trove, and sent him a series of messages that read like blackmail; in August of the following year the FBI raided his home, where prosecutors say they found classified documents in violation of his prior commitment to return all such material.[5] The Economist observed that Bolton had once likened the Trump administration to "Hotel California," where "you can check out any time you like, but you can never leave."[5]

The publication noted it was no surprise that Bolton took minute-by-minute notes with an eye to writing his memoir — at roughly 500 pages, the manuscript was ready within two months of his exit — nor that [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s justice department pursued the case.[5] It added that Bolton's self-styling as someone who understands Washington inside out would not help his defense, since the indictment cites past comments in which he called others criminal and reckless for similar conduct, and that Bolton took a big risk in 2020 assuming Trump, then facing his first impeachment, would not long be in a position to act on his vendetta.[5]

### Badlands commentary on the indictment

The 26-page indictment was made publicly available via CourtListener.[5] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] summarized the sequence as Iran allegedly hacking Bolton's email — which held national security secrets he was retaining to write his book — after which Bolton told the government about the hack but not about the secrets, and only went to the FBI once the alleged hackers began blackmailing him.[5] She noted that "Individual 1" and "Individual 2" in the indictment are alleged to be Bolton's wife and daughter, neither of whom holds a security clearance.[5] Because the sole source for Iranian attribution is Bolton himself, she argued the hacker was likely fictional and asked who Bolton would be sharing secrets with that would want any trail to point to Iran, invoking the phrase "pull a Khashoggi" and suggesting his wife and daughter functioned as editors — or handlers.[5] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] added that [[abbe-lowell|Abbe Lowell]] "is in charge of all the psyop cases."[5]

### Arraignment and the "not like the others" debate

Bolton pleaded not guilty in court on the morning after the 18-count indictment was filed, and an October 2025 essay in [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] argued that his case should not be lumped in with what it called the specious prosecutions of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s enemies.[7] The magazine described the indictment as compiled by experienced prosecutors rather than political lackeys, detailed and precise, and reliant on Bolton's own words to implicate him in systematically sharing classified information with people who were not authorized to read it, all in the service of writing a tell-all book.[7] It conceded that officials in Joe Biden's administration had passed on the chance to bring charges and that Bolton has ample basis to argue he is being singled out as one of Trump's most persistent critics, but maintained that political animus does not make the charges baseless.[7] The piece distinguished the Bolton case from those against [[james-comey|James Comey]] and [[letitia-james|Letitia James]], which it called so weak that a U.S. attorney — [[erik-siebert|Erik Siebert]] — resigned rather than present them to a grand jury.[7]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that the indictment would have actual effects on the battlespace but that its biggest benefit to Trump's justice campaign was psychological: because Bolton is a "former Trump ally," the move served simultaneously as [[narrative-shielding|narrative shielding]] and as a warning shot that nobody is safe — friends close, enemies closer.[7] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that four days before the indictment, Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]] told Judge [[andrew-napolitano|Andrew Napolitano]] that it was [[sheldon-adelson|Sheldon Adelson]] who convinced Trump to bring Bolton into the administration, reading this as both signal and proof of "drafting" — Trump using the inertia of the NeoCon/Zionist agenda to advance the white hat plan.[7] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] inverted the Atlantic's headline, asking "what if the others are just like the Bolton case?", predicting superseding indictments that add new charges and new defendants for Comey and James as well, and questioning why the magazine appeared to be rewriting the Erik Siebert story.[7]

## Removal of Secret Service detail (January 2025)

Bolton confirmed to Newsweek on Tuesday, January 21, 2025 that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had ended the [[united-states-secret-service|Secret Service]] protection assigned to him.[8] In a statement shared by email, the former official said the Secret Service had called late the previous night — just hours after Trump took office — to inform him that his protection would end at noon that day.[8] Bolton, a national-security hawk who played a key role in shaping US foreign policy during the first Trump administration, had continued to require Secret Service protection because of threats from Iran even after leaving the White House in 2019.[8] After Bolton was fired by Trump, the president terminated his security detail; former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] reinstated the protection upon taking office in 2021.[8] Bolton said he was "disappointed" by the renewed removal.[8]

## Opposition to JD Vance (July 2024)

After Donald Trump named [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] as his 2024 running mate, Bolton appeared on [[msnbc|MSNBC]] with [[katy-tur|Katy Tur]] to assess the implications for American foreign policy.[9] Badlands Media described Bolton as one of the most unabashed warmongers in modern American politics, a Doctor Strangelove-like figure of the Bush/Cheney era who had been an unexplained Trump hire, resigned, and then become a favored critic of Trump on liberal networks that had once treated him as an adversary.[9]

Asked about Vance's view that Ukraine should negotiate a settlement with Russia and cede territory if necessary, Bolton said the outcome would be "very bad for Ukraine" and that Vance was "wrong in many material respects," failing to appreciate the American national security interest in protecting Ukraine against what he called unprovoked Russian aggression.[9] Bolton argued that American involvement served interests in European peace and security rather than charity, rejected Vance's argument that Europe should shoulder more of the burden so the United States could focus on China, and warned that Russia and China were forming an axis linking the conflicts.[9] He also criticized Trump's Bloomberg News remark that Taiwan, 9,000 miles from the United States and 100 miles from China, was not worth the effort, concluding that neither candidate understood that a strong American presence abroad benefits the country at home.[9]

Badlands treated Bolton's dismay as an endorsement in reverse, arguing that his worldview equates any avoidance of war with weakened national security while ignoring the monetary cost and the radicalization produced by decades of intervention.[9]
