# Vogue

Vogue is an American fashion magazine whose coverage of first ladies has historically functioned as a marker of cultural approval, including cover features and admiring profiles. Badlands Media commentators treat the magazine as a partisan cultural institution rather than a neutral arbiter of taste.[1]

## Criticism of Melania Trump's official portrait

In late January 2025, Vogue criticized the official White House portrait of First Lady [[melania-trump|Melania Trump]], with its writers saying the photograph made her look like "a freelance magician" and comparing the image to an appearance on "The Apprentice," the NBC reality show hosted by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the 2000s.[1] In the portrait, Melania Trump appears in a dark suit and white blouse with her hands resting on a reflective table and the Washington Monument visible behind her.[1] Vogue wrote that "Trump's clothing certainly didn't help the boardroom pastiche," identifying a black Dolce & Gabbana tuxedo jacket with satin-trimmed lapels over a white button-up, paired with a Ralph Lauren cummerbund and trousers, and argued that the choice of a tuxedo rather than a blazer or blouse "made Trump look more like a freelance magician than a public servant."[1] The magazine added that it was "perhaps unsurprising that a woman who lived in a gold-encrusted penthouse, whose fame is so intertwined with a reality television empire, would refuse to abandon theatrics—even when faced with 248 years of tradition."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], who described himself as formally trained in fine arts, argued that the portrait deliberately conveys a masculine and powerful posture, comparing Melania Trump's pose to the marketing imagery for the [[netflix|Netflix]] series House of Cards featuring the character Frank Underwood, and to poses Donald Trump himself has used, on the grounds that filmmakers understand what the pose communicates.[1] He read the tuxedo worn without a tie and with an unbuttoned shirt as implying a casual moment at a formal event, and found it ironic that Vogue would mock a former supermodel and fashion icon for wearing a man's suit given the magazine's prior alignment with feminist framing about women adopting traditionally male roles.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] contrasted the treatment with the magazine's coverage of Dr. [[jill-biden|Jill Biden]], citing a [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]] summary stating that Vogue had celebrated Biden's fashion sense, her promotion of American designers, and her role in connecting with the American public, while noting the same model's observation that during Melania Trump's tenure as first lady from 2017 to 2021 Vogue did not feature her on its cover, a departure from its tradition of highlighting first ladies.[1] Ashe concluded that Vogue is "trash — political hacks with zero credibility."[1]
