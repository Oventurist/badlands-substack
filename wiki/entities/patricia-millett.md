# Patricia Millett

Patricia Millett is a judge of the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit and an appointee of former President Barack Obama.[1] She became a focal point of conservative criticism in March 2025 after remarks she made from the bench during oral argument over the Trump administration's use of the [[alien-enemies-act|Alien Enemies Act]].[1]

## Role in the Alien Enemies Act appeal

On Monday, March 24, 2025, Millett sat on the three-judge D.C. Circuit panel considering the Justice Department's request to lift [[james-boasberg|U.S. District Judge James Boasberg]]'s order temporarily blocking the administration from invoking the centuries-old wartime statute.[1] During argument she sharply criticized the administration's conduct, saying: "There were planeloads of people. There were no procedures in place to notify people. Nazis got better treatment under the Alien Enemies Act than has happened here."[1]

Millett repeatedly pressed government counsel on how the deported migrants could have exercised due process before being flown to a notorious Salvadoran prison in [[el-salvador|El Salvador]].[1] Framing the problem in personal terms, she said: "Y'all could have put me up on Saturday and thrown me on a plane, thinking I'm a member of [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] and giving me no chance to protest it and say somehow it's a violation of presidential war powers. For me to say, 'Excuse me, no, I'm not, I'd like a hearing.'"[1]

It remained unclear following the argument how the panel would ultimately rule.[1]

## Reception in Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized Millett as a "nazi-obsessed appellate judge" and used the episode to argue that the federal district courts are "rapidly spiraling into illegitimacy," questioning how many presumptively unconstitutional orders the Executive Branch should entertain before disregarding the forum entirely.[1] The commentary noted that courts have no power to carry out their own orders and rely on the Executive Branch to do so.[1]
