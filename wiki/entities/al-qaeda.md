# Al-Qaeda

Al-Qaeda is a militant Islamist organization founded by [[osama-bin-laden|Osama bin Laden]] in the late 1980s, originally composed of Arab mujahideen who had fought against the Soviet Union in Afghanistan. In the Badlands Media article "A Conflict of Interest," Al-Qaeda is portrayed as a terrorist organization that was deliberately created, armed, and manipulated by the [[cia|CIA]] and what the source calls the "shadow government" for geopolitical and financial purposes.

According to the article, the CIA created and funded the Mujahideen in Afghanistan as a "private army" to protect its heroin trade. It claims that Al-Qaeda training camps were located in Afghanistan before the 9/11 attacks, and that the group's affiliate, the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]], had two training camps in Afghanistan at the time of 9/11.

The source asserts that Al-Qaeda and its affiliates were not merely enemies of the United States but were often covert partners in a scheme to generate perpetual war. It claims that the "never-ending war on terrorism was by design," and that the longer the war lasted, the more money could be stolen from U.S. taxpayers. The article specifically claims that the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|LIFG]] officially joined Al-Qaeda in 2007, was sending fighters to kill American soldiers in Iraq, and was then supported by [[john-mccain|John McCain]] and [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] during the 2011 Libya intervention.

According to the source, Al-Qaeda's connection to U.S. intelligence runs deeper than most people realize. It claims that [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] was not just a member but a close associate of Osama Bin Laden and one of Al-Qaeda's most important leaders, and that he was picked up by the CIA after 9/11 and sent back to Libya, where Gaddafi imprisoned him. The article frames this as evidence that the CIA was managing Al-Qaeda-affiliated individuals as assets, imprisoning them when convenient and releasing them when the geopolitical winds shifted.
