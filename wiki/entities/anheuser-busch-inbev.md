# Anheuser-Busch InBev

Anheuser-Busch InBev (AB InBev) is the multinational brewing conglomerate that owns [[bud-light|Bud Light]] and that became the target of a nationwide consumer boycott in 2023 following a marketing partnership with transgender TikTok influencer [[dylan-mulvaney|Dylan Mulvaney]].[1]

## The Bud Light boycott

The company, described in coverage as self-styled "inclusive," promoted Bud Light with Mulvaney, prompting widespread boycott calls among the brand's traditional customer base.[1] Chief executive [[michel-doukeris|Michel Doukeris]] subsequently blamed "misinformation" on social media for stoking the boycott, telling the Financial Times that on social media "you have one fact and every person puts an opinion behind the fact," so that after ten or twenty comments "the reality is no longer what the fact is, but is more about what the comments were."[1]

Rather than accept responsibility for the campaign, Doukeris attributed the damage to online "misinformation and confusion," including reports about the Mulvaney can, which the company said was never "a production can" and was "never intended to make it for general production and sale for the public."[1] Commentators noted that this distancing risked alienating progressive consumers as well as the boycotting customers.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] characterized the CEO's explanation as gaslighting, arguing that invoking the word "misinformation" was being used to make demonstrable realities "evaporate."[1] He wrote that the boycott stemmed from the company's decision to place Mulvaney on Bud Light packaging while ignoring its customers' views of the transgender movement, and that an accusation of lying carries no weight unless it is paired with demonstrated evidence.[1] Deschamps compared the appeal to "misinformation" to similar labeling used against counter-mainstream claims about vaccine efficacy and election integrity, calling unevidenced accusations from mainstream sources "a serious red flag."[1]

## July 2023 U.S. layoffs

In late July 2023, following months of slumping [[bud-light|Bud Light]] sales, Anheuser-Busch InBev laid off hundreds of workers at its U.S. offices.[2] The world's largest brewer, which also sells Stella Artois and [[budweiser|Budweiser]], said on Wednesday that the cuts would affect less than 2% of its roughly 18,000 U.S. employees and would not impact front-line workers such as brewery and warehouse staff.[2] The restructuring eliminated corporate and marketing roles at major U.S. offices including St. Louis, New York and Los Angeles.[2] Anheuser-Busch Chief Executive [[brendan-whitworth|Brendan Whitworth]] said the company never took such decisions lightly but wanted to ensure the organization remained set for long-term success.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the burden of the failed [[bud-light-boycott|Mulvaney campaign]] fell on 645 employees rather than executives, and called the arrangement immoral though entirely expected.[2]

## Emblem of the anti-ESG boycott movement

By June 2023, Anheuser-Busch stood with [[target|Target]] as the paradigm case cited by conservative groups arguing that consumer boycotts of "woke" corporations were the key to reversing race, gender and environmental activism in corporate America.[3] [[scott-shepard|Scott Shepard]] of the [[free-enterprise-project|Free Enterprise Project]] argued that customer defections from such companies had given his organization the traction needed to challenge [[esg|ESG]] commitments legally as breaches of fiduciary duty to shareholders.[3]

Reporting that month described a "Target and Bud Light stock-market disaster" that led executives across corporate America — including at [[starbucks|Starbucks]] — to quiet their advocacy on green and social initiatives.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited Anheuser-Busch alongside [[disney|Disney]] as a global brand that had lost significant market share to poorly executed virtue signals yet continued "riding the rainbow."[3]

## Marketing leadership change after the Bud Light backlash (April 2023)

Three weeks after [[bud-light|Bud Light]]'s partnership with transgender TikTok influencer [[dylan-mulvaney|Dylan Mulvaney]] triggered a consumer backlash, Anheuser-Busch InBev abruptly changed the brand's marketing leadership.[4] Ad Age reported on Friday, April 21, 2023 that Bud Light vice president of marketing [[alissa-heinerscheid|Alissa Heinerscheid]] had taken a leave of absence and would be replaced by [[todd-allen|Todd Allen]], previously global marketing vice president for [[budweiser|Budweiser]].[4] A company spokesperson said top-level executives would closely monitor Bud Light's marketing activities so as to "maintain focus on the things we do best: brewing great beer for all consumers, while always making a positive impact in our communities and on our country."[4]

[[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], which carried the report, framed the episode as the brand's failed attempt to position itself as the "queen" of woke beers.[4] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that the controversy had persisted well over a week and a half in the national news cycle, with far more outlets than culture-war-adjacent ones covering it, and that even the legacy financial press was grudgingly admitting the company might have made a catastrophic marketing blunder that would show red in its ledgers for several quarters and more.[4]

## $6 billion market-cap loss (April 2023)

After reaching a three-year high of $66.73 per share, Anheuser-Busch InBev lost more than $6 billion in market capitalisation in the six days following the announcement of the [[dylan-mulvaney|Dylan Mulvaney]] partnership on April 2, 2023, as bar owners and distributors reported a sharp decline in [[bud-light|Bud Light]] sales.[5] The company's market capitalisation fell as low as $125.7 billion, down from $132.8 billion six days earlier — a drop of more than five percent.[5]

The partnership included custom cans featuring Mulvaney's face and pro-LGBTQ language commemorating a year of Mulvaney publicly identifying as a woman, and the campaign launched with a video of Mulvaney drinking Bud Light in a bathtub.[5] While Anheuser-Busch issued a statement of support saying it "works with hundreds of influencers across our brands as one of many ways to authentically connect with audiences across various demographics," the Daily Wire reported — as relayed by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] — that company executives had no knowledge of the ad campaign.[5]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the most significant effect of the backlash was less about the transgender issue than about ordinary consumers realising they could inflict massive damage on a Fortune 500 company not by taking action but by declining to participate, summarising that to destroy a [[system-of-systems|System of Systems]] "all you have to do is not take part in it."[5]
