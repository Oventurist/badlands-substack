# Sergey Lavrov

Sergey Lavrov is the Foreign Minister of the Russian Federation and one of the most recognizable figures in contemporary Russian diplomacy.[1] In Badlands Media coverage he is treated as a principal voice articulating Moscow's view of great-power competition in the Global South, particularly in Africa.[1]

## Accusations against France in Africa (February 2026)

Speaking to the Russian parliament on Wednesday, February 11, 2026, Lavrov accused [[france|France]] of pursuing "revanchist" policies on the African continent.[1] According to RT, Lavrov said that "France is attempting to overthrow undesirable nationalist governments in the Sahara-Sahel region and other parts of Africa," and charged that although African nations "have long since abandoned their ties to their former metropolis," Paris had chosen to undermine them through opposition figures, "outright terrorist groups and Ukrainian militants."[1]

Lavrov made the remarks against a backdrop of sustained diplomatic friction between Russia and Western states over influence in Africa.[1] France has for its part stated that it respects the sovereignty of African nations while expressing concern about unconstitutional changes of government in the region.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized Lavrov as a "legendary diplomat" whose statement confirmed what the contributor calls the War of Sovereignty — described as a coordinated subversive campaign orchestrated by what he terms the Unipolar Hegemony.[1] In that framing, the destabilization effort is directed at the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]], led by Burkinabè President Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]], and involves not only ISIS and coup plotters allegedly funded by the [[deep-state|Deep State]] but also Ukrainian mercenaries.[1] The commentator noted that the Russian military and the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] had trained Sahelian forces in Syria, and speculated about a possible Russian re-mobilization and redeployment to the Sahel.[1]

## April 2026: "rampant Satanism" accusation

In April 2026 Lavrov accused parts of the European Union of promoting "rampant Satanism," linking the charge to Western support for [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[2] The remarks centered on tensions over the [[kyiv-pechersk-lavra|Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra]], where Ukrainian authorities have taken action against clergy tied to Moscow.[2] "The Ukrainian Orthodox Church has been persecuted for over a decade now, with churches [forcibly] taken over, vandalized and clergy and parishioners harassed," he said, adding that the Ukrainian Ministry of Culture "used this bureaucratic formula to conceal its legalized blasphemous practices, while several European countries have turned a blind eye to these developments or even directly supported them."[2] He concluded: "There is rampant Satanism in these countries, too."[2]

[[russia|Russia]] has framed the Ukrainian measures as religious persecution, while Ukraine and its allies argue they are national security actions taken amid the ongoing war; the exchange reflects escalating ideological and religious framing in the broader Russia–West conflict.[2] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that Lavrov, "one of the most respected and measured diplomats in the world," openly accusing the European elite and the leadership in Kiev of practicing Satanism "speaks to the gravity of the moment," and suggested it may illuminate what President Putin meant in saying Russia knows how the conflict will end.[2] He tied the claims to reported desecration of Christian monuments by the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] in southern Lebanon and to unsubstantiated allegations of child trafficking and organ harvesting networks in Ukraine.[2]

## Cuba as a "brotherly nation" (February 2026)

In February 2026, as [[cuba|Cuba]] endured worsening blackouts, waste accumulation and fuel shortages, Lavrov publicly referred to the island as a "brotherly nation" while criticizing United States sanctions.[3] His remarks came in the same news cycle in which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] described Cuba as "a failed nation" lacking even jet fuel for aircraft.[3]

## "Reshaping of the world order" remarks

Amid the 2026 US-Israeli war on [[iran|Iran]], Lavrov said: "We are in the middle of reshaping of the world order that will hopefully lead to the formation of a stable and fair multipolar world. Right now, this restructuring looks more like disruption. The fight for power across the world is fierce."[4] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the statement as the signal in the noise, reading it as a description of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and its struggle against unipolar hegemony, and argued that Lavrov's "we" encompassed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as well as Iran and its cultivated relationships with [[russia|Russia]] and [[china|China]].[4]

## Manila meeting with Rubio (July 2026)

In July 2026 Lavrov met Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] in Manila as part of renewed American outreach on the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[5] Rubio said afterwards that the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] remained prepared to play a "constructive role" in helping end the war but warned there was no quick path to a negotiated settlement, describing the conversation as "good" and "frank" while declining to disclose details on the grounds that diplomacy is best conducted privately.[5]

[[russia|Russia]] continued to state that it is open to a political settlement while maintaining its previously stated negotiating positions, and Ukraine reiterated that any peace agreement must preserve its sovereignty and territorial integrity.[5] The meeting followed Washington's decision to withhold portions of previously planned military assistance to Kyiv pending a review of US weapons stockpiles.[5]

## Warning on US alignment with Israel (April 2026)

In April 2026 Lavrov sharply criticized US alignment with [[israel|Israel]] amid the escalating Iran conflict, warning that Israeli military actions risked triggering a broader regional war and pulling Washington further into the crisis.[6] Speaking during a visit to [[china|China]] on a Wednesday, he described the situation as "a crisis knot that will be extremely difficult to untangle," adding that "some parties are trying to cut it now – I don't believe that would produce a [favorable] result."[6] He attributed the crisis directly to the US-Israeli attack on [[iran|Iran]] in late February.[6]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that Lavrov has served as Foreign Minister longer than [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] has been president of Russia, a tenure beginning in 2000, and argued that Lavrov's comments were paving the way for the United States to exit the Iran conflict and abandon Israel militarily.[6]

## Abraham Accords and the Saudi Peace Initiative (April 2026)

In April 2026 Lavrov explained how the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] had been used to subvert the [[saudi-peace-initiative|Saudi Peace Initiative]] of 2002, which required a two-state solution.[7] Badlands cited the remarks as part of a broader pattern in which Russia's top leaders had spent months warning [[israel|Israel]] or exposing what they characterized as its malevolence, alongside [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]]'s demand that Israel stop attacking [[iran|Iran]] and [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]]'s warning over strikes on the [[bushehr-nuclear-power-plant|Bushehr nuclear power plant]].[7]

Lavrov also addressed [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]] in January 2026, describing the initiative as reflecting U.S. recognition of the need for cooperation among key countries.[8] He said Russia had received specific proposals and viewed the body as potentially constructive for Middle East stability if it implements existing [[united-nations|U.N.]] decisions, particularly on creating an independent Palestinian state, while stressing that many questions remained and it was too early to confirm participation; the Kremlin echoed that caution over unresolved details.[8] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited Lavrov's remarks as evidence that Board of Peace members were prepared to take on more than the scope of the Gaza war, consistent with his reading of the body as the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] in tangible form.[8]

## Russian bases in post-Assad Syria

In a December 2024 interview with RIA Novosti, Lavrov said the continued presence of Russian military bases in Syria and the conditions of their operation could become the subject of future negotiations with Syria's new leadership.[9] "The deployment of Russian military bases in Syria is stipulated by valid interstate agreements, concluded under the norms of international law," he said, adding that each agreement defines operating conditions and includes specific obligations for both sides.[9] He acknowledged that the overthrow of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] had brought adjustments to Russia's military arrangements in the country, and noted that while no formal requests to revise the agreements had been made, the transition period declared until March 1, 2025 under the interim Syrian government imposed limitations on its policy-making.[9] "It is not only about preserving our bases or strongholds but also about the conditions of their operation, maintenance, and provision, as well as interaction with the local side," the minister said.[9] The bases at issue were [[khmeimim-air-base|Khmeimim]] and [[tartus-naval-base|Tartus]], where Moscow and Damascus agreed in 2017 to station Russian troops for 49 years.[9]

## Sahel talks at the 80th UN General Assembly (September 2025)

On Wednesday, September 24, 2025, Lavrov held talks with his counterparts from [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]], Mali and [[niger|Niger]] on the sidelines of the 80th UN General Assembly in New York, focusing on stronger cooperation and preparations for the third Russia-Africa Summit.[10] Moscow described the meeting as the second joint discussion between the foreign ministers of the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]] and Russia.[10] In his opening remarks Lavrov said Moscow and the Sahel states share interests extending beyond trade, defense and cultural affairs to cooperation at the UN and other international bodies, and added: "We are now entering the preparatory phase for the third Russia-Africa Summit, scheduled for 2026. We are confident that the countries represented here will exert every effort to ensure its success."[10]

The three military-led governments have sought closer cooperation with Russia under the AES, formed in September 2023, after cutting defense ties with France and accusing their former colonial power of failing to contain the jihadist insurgency devastating the Sahel.[10] Badlands commentary framed the alignment as the Sahel states, led by Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]], viewing Christian Russia as the one earnest actor on the world stage, while the United States alternates between Christian messaging and coercive demands that other nations surrender their sovereignty.[10]

## July 2024 UN Security Council remarks

Chairing the [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]] in July 2024, Lavrov told a meeting on the Middle East and the Palestinian issue that Washington's diplomatic cover for Israel's actions had made it directly involved in the conflict.[11] "By offering a diplomatic cover-up for Israel's actions and sending weapons and munitions, Washington — everybody understands this — has become directly complicit in the conflict, just as it does regarding the situation in Ukraine," he said, adding that once such support ended the bloodshed would stop, but that the United States was "either unwilling or unable" to do so, apparently prioritizing "the maneuvers that will enable them to score more points in the election campaign."[11]

Lavrov emphasized that "the current unprecedented outburst of violence in the Middle East is largely a consequence of the well-known US policy in the region," and criticized repeated American use of the veto to block calls for an immediate, lasting, and comprehensive ceasefire, naming his counterpart [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] among those demanding that Russia curtail work on the Security Council platform.[11]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] expressed deep admiration for Lavrov as a key figure of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], calling him a remarkable leader with a great mind for diplomacy who "knows exactly how to deliver truth bombs, without being overtly incendiary."[11]

## Pyongyang visit (July 2025)

In July 2025 Lavrov made a three-day visit to Pyongyang, during which North Korean leader [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] pledged "unconditional support" for Russia's war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and both sides reaffirmed plans to expand military ties under the defense pact Kim signed with President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] the previous year.[12] Lavrov praised the growing military alliance and thanked Kim for deploying more than 12,000 [[north-korea|North Korean]] troops to support Russian forces, who reportedly helped Moscow reclaim the Kursk region after a Ukrainian counteroffensive.[12] Russia has maintained that the war's "root cause" lies in anti-Russian sentiment in Ukraine, demanding that Kyiv demilitarize, remove President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], and abandon its [[nato|NATO]] ambitions — demands Ukraine has rejected.[12]

## Criticism of US Middle East force build-up (2023)

Speaking during a meeting in Tehran on Monday, October 23, 2023, Russian Foreign Minister Lavrov argued that additional US warships dispatched by the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] to the Middle East raised the danger of a wider war, saying that "the more a state takes this kind of proactive measures, the greater the risk and the danger of an escalation of the conflict."[13] He singled out Washington as "already among the countries intervening the most" since the October 7 [[hamas|Hamas]] attack on Israel.[13]

Badlands commentary placed Lavrov's remarks within a broader pattern in which world leaders including [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Vladimir Putin, Xi Jinping and [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] were calling for peace, US neutrality and a two-state solution, positions the outlet described as aligning with the only point of agreement among most Americans and as an optics problem for the Biden administration.[13]

## Remarks on African colonial borders (2025)

In an interview with *Kommersant* published Monday, April 14, 2025, Lavrov said the legacy of colonialism remains a key driver of instability across Africa, attributing much of the continent's turmoil to [[colonial-borders-in-africa|borders drawn by departing colonial powers]] without regard for ethnic, cultural, and historical realities.[14] He said colonial authorities drew maps "with a ruler," fragmenting ethnic groups across new frontiers and producing "chaos," citing the Tuareg split between Algeria and Mali and the Tutsi and Hutu of Rwanda.[14] Lavrov noted that the [[african-union|African Union]] had decided not to revise those borders, preferring to "find ways to reach agreements, and to build good-neighborly arrangements so that people – relatives – can cross borders freely."[14] He also pointed to the unfinished process of decolonization, saying 17 territories remain under the control of former colonial powers, echoing a 2019 statement by UN Secretary-General [[antonio-guterres|Antonio Guterres]].[14]

## Warning on European forces in Ukraine (December 2025)

Speaking in late December 2025, Lavrov warned that any European troops deployed to Ukraine would be considered legitimate targets by Russia's armed forces.[15] He accused European politicians of being driven by "ambitions" in their relations with Kiev while disregarding both the people of Ukraine and the populations of their own nations.[15]

Lavrov had earlier said Moscow would respond to any move to deploy European troops in Ukraine or to use frozen Russian assets to support Kiev, while underlining that Russia has no plans to go to war with Europe.[15] "However, we will respond to any hostile actions, including the deployment of European military contingents in Ukraine and the seizure of Russian assets. We are already prepared for such a response," he said.[15]

## 'Dangerous consequences' warning to the United States (October 2024)

In exclusive written responses to *Newsweek* published in early October 2024, Lavrov — described as [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s top diplomat for twenty years — warned that the United States would face "dangerous consequences" if it pressed on with growing military aid to Ukraine rather than backing a proposed Russian settlement that would see Moscow take over swathes of territory.[16] More than two and a half years after Putin ordered a "special military operation" in what had become the deadliest conflict in Europe since World War II, Lavrov said the Kremlin was offering a viable blueprint to end the bloodshed and revamp the security architecture of the continent, and accused the US-led [[nato|NATO]] alliance of first sowing the seeds of war a decade earlier and continuing to fan the flames.[16]

"Russia is open to a politico-diplomatic settlement that should remove the root causes of the crisis," he said. "It should aim to end the conflict rather than achieve a ceasefire."[16] The Russian plan would require Ukraine to cede the substantially Russian-held provinces of Donetsk, Kherson, Luhansk and Zaporizhzhia — formally annexed by Moscow following an internationally disputed referendum in September 2022 — as well as Crimea, seized and annexed through a similar vote in 2014, and to abandon its quest for NATO membership, terms rejected by [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] and his international backers.[16]

Lavrov also dismissed the significance of the American presidential contest for Moscow: "Generally speaking, the outcome of this election makes no difference to us, as the two parties have reached a consensus as to countering our country," he said, adding that "it would be natural for the White House resident, no matter who they are, to mind their domestic business, rather than looking for adventures tens of thousands of miles away from American coasts."[16] Badlands writers praised the remark as "based" and read Lavrov's warning as evidence that the American political class was cornered and increasingly dangerous, while speculating about what form the threatened consequences might take given that a strike on US soil appeared inconsistent with his stated view of the American electorate.[16]

## Announcement of new Russia–US embassy talks (February 2025)

Speaking at a press conference in Doha, Qatar on Wednesday, February 26, 2025, Lavrov announced that high-level diplomats from [[russia|Russia]] and the United States would meet in Istanbul, [[turkey|Türkiye]], on February 27 to discuss normalizing the operations of their respective embassies.[17] He said the outcome of the talks would determine how "quickly and effectively we can move forward."[17] According to Lavrov, the two sides would address "systemic issues" stemming from the "previous US administration's unlawful actions to create artificial obstacles" for the Russian embassy's operations, against a backdrop of long-running diplomatic tensions including mutual expulsions of embassy staff predating the 2022 escalation in Ukraine.[17]

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read the meeting as a power move by Trump and [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] to flex on [[nato|NATO]], noting Turkey's 1952 accession and its later role as a staging ground for [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]], and predicted the talks would yield a narrative NATO would find irritating.[17]

## Denial of Assad poisoning claims (October 2025)

In mid-October 2025, Lavrov rejected rumors that former Syrian President [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar Assad]] had been poisoned, saying Assad and his family were safe in Moscow and had been living there without any problems since being granted asylum.[18] The rumor originated with the [[syrian-observatory-for-human-rights|Syrian Observatory for Human Rights]], which cited an anonymous source claiming Assad had been discharged from a hospital in the Moscow Region after a supposed September poisoning attempt, and was then widely circulated by both Western and Russian media.[18]

Lavrov stressed that Assad "has no problem living in our capital" and that "no poisonings have occurred," adding: "If such rumors appear, I leave them to the conscience of those who spread them."[18] He said [[russia|Russia]] had provided asylum to Assad and his family "for purely humanitarian reasons," noting they had faced threats of physical harm after the previous year's change of power in Damascus.[18] Lavrov had also said earlier that week that Russia sheltered Assad because his life had been under threat, as Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]] prepared to press Moscow for Assad's extradition.[18]

## November 2025 press remarks

On Tuesday, November 11, 2025, Lavrov used remarks to Russian journalists to attack Western media and U.S. policy on several fronts.[19] He called attempts to deflect from the [[bbc|BBC]]'s responsibility for the [[bbc-january-6-footage-editing-scandal|January 6 footage editing scandal]] "a disgrace," saying that "the fact that some 'figures' are trying to justify what happened and are talking about some kind of staged campaign is a disgrace," and framing the episode as part of a pattern of unprofessional and harmful coverage by international, primarily British, outlets.[19]

At a press conference the same day he denounced U.S. maritime airstrikes on vessels off [[venezuela|Venezuela]] as "acts taken without due process" demonstrating that the United States "places itself above the law," and argued Washington should instead address drug trafficking in Belgium, which one of that country's own judges had described as an emerging narco-state.[19] "The US already has troops there and will not have to chase after small boats with crews of three people each," he said, in what became known as the [[belgium-narco-state-controversy|Belgium narco-state controversy]].[19]

Lavrov also addressed nuclear testing, stating that if the United States conducts a nuclear weapons test, "Russia will respond in kind," citing a position [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] articulated in 2023 distinguishing an actual nuclear detonation from carrier tests or subcritical experiments.[19] These comments came less than two weeks after Trump directed the [[2025-resumption-of-us-nuclear-weapons-testing|resumption of US nuclear weapons testing]].[19] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry dubbed him "Big Bear" and cast his interventions as part of a Trump-Russia pincer movement against Western legacy media and the EU/NATO establishment.[19]

## Accusations of British involvement in Ukrainian sabotage (June 2025)

Speaking at the "Forum of the Future 2050" in Moscow in June 2025, Lavrov said there was no doubt that Britain was helping [[ukraine|Ukraine]] carry out terrorist attacks inside Russia, acknowledging that the "risk of the rise in the level of terrorist threat exists" in the country.[20] "It is obvious that all this is being done by the Ukrainian side, but it would have been helpless without the support... from the British," he said.[20] His remarks followed several sabotage incidents on Russian railways that month, the worst on June 1 when a bridge collapsed in front of a moving passenger train in Bryansk Region, killing seven people and injuring more than 100.[20] The same day, Ukraine targeted Russian airbases from Murmansk in the Arctic to Irkutsk in Siberia in a coordinated assault using explosive-laden drones launched from commercial trucks; according to Moscow, most of the incoming UAVs were shot down and none of the affected aircraft were damaged beyond repair.[20]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the episode as evidence of an "unholy alliance" between elite networks in Ukraine and London, tracing purported collaborations across the 19th and 20th centuries, the 1980s activities of [[robert-maxwell|Robert Maxwell]] and [[semion-mogilevich|Semion Mogilevich]], and a 1999 episode in which Kroll Associates and others allegedly helped parties in Moscow move money out ahead of the incoming Putin administration — all of which he tied back to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[20]


## Remarks on the Iran Treaty (April 2025)

In the week before ratification, Foreign Minister Lavrov emphasized that the [[russia-iran-comprehensive-strategic-partnership-treaty|Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Agreement]] with [[iran|Iran]] was being finalized "despite the challenging regional and global landscape and attempts to exert pressure on our nations" by third parties.[21]

## Sky News Arabia interview on red lines (September 2024)

In an interview with Sky News Arabia reported in September 2024, Lavrov explained what Russia hopes to achieve by talking about its "red lines," an exchange analyzed at length by [[andrew-korybko|Andrew Korybko]].[22] Lavrov said Western leaders "really seem to have a child's mentality, even though they are adults holding positions of responsibility: ministers, prime ministers, chancellors, presidents," and complained that for months there had been discourse about Russia merely threatening while the West crosses its lines and "nothing happens."[22]

He framed the rhetoric as signaling rather than ultimatum: "We talk about the 'red lines' in the hope that our assessments and statements will be heard by clever decision-makers. It is silly to say that we will push the red button, if tomorrow you fail to do as I demand. I am confident that the decision-makers are aware of what we mean in these situations. No one wants a nuclear war."[22] He added that Russia has "weapons whose use will involve grave consequences for the masters of the Ukrainian regime."[22]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright read the remarks as approaching Devolution framing without crossing into it, noting Lavrov's reference to American "decision-makers" working to avert the war the Globalist Hegemon seeks to provoke, and paralleling earlier comments by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] that in the United States "there are enough specialists in the field of Russian-American relations and in the field of strategic restraint."[22] The conclusion drawn was that "war will NOT happen, but the STORY of the war is inevitable."[22]

## "Western hegemony not sustainable," June 2024

Speaking at a press conference following a meeting of [[brics|BRICS]] foreign ministers in June 2024, Lavrov argued that the West would eventually recognize that its efforts to preserve hegemony "through blackmail, illegal sanctions, ultimatums and even military force are doomed to failure," while cautioning that he did not expect that realization soon.[23] He added that if the West did come to accept the need for an equal dialogue with the rest of the world, the BRICS member states and other countries of the world majority would be ready for it, subject to agreement on the terms of that dialogue.[23] Lavrov stressed that BRICS does not seek to isolate itself from the West, noting that communication remains possible within the framework of the United Nations even as, in his account, the Americans and their allies attempt to substitute collective work with ideas formulated among themselves and imposed through dependent international secretariats.[23]

Badlands commentary paired the remarks with a survey of what it called the hegemony's fight to the bitter end, including the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]'s quiet lifting of a 2018 Trump-era ban on sending American weapons to the [[azov-battalion|Azov Battalion]] — described by ex-Pentagon official Michael Maloof as "a sign of desperation" — and Lavrov's own statement ten days earlier that Eastern Europe had seen an explosion in Neo-Nazism since [[nato|NATO]] expanded into the territory.[23] The same analysis linked [[armenia|Armenia]]'s withdrawal from the [[collective-security-treaty-organization|Collective Security Treaty Organization]], pressure on Georgia over its foreign-agents legislation, and Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Mikhail Galuzin's accusation that the EU used an Armenian "monitoring mission" to spy on Azerbaijan, to a broader campaign for control of the Caucasus aimed at severing the International North–South Transportation Corridor.[23]

## Condemnation of Israeli strikes on Qatar (2025)

Speaking on September 11, 2025 at a Russia–[[gulf-cooperation-council|Gulf Cooperation Council]] Strategic Dialogue session in Sochi, Lavrov said Israel's attack on the Qatari capital demonstrated its unwillingness to end the humanitarian catastrophe in Gaza and warned that the strike would further destabilize the Middle East.[24] He said the meeting was taking place against the backdrop of a sharp increase in military-political tensions caused by the [[2025-israeli-strike-on-doha|Israeli missile and bomb attacks on Doha]] of September 9, which Russia had noted with deep concern.[24] Lavrov called the attack "a gross violation of international law, primarily the UN Charter, an encroachment on the sovereignty and territorial integrity of an independent state."[24] He emphasized [[qatar|Qatar]]'s role as a key mediator in indirect Israel–[[hamas|Hamas]] ceasefire negotiations, describing the particular cynicism of striking a principal mediator and saying the Israeli steps unambiguously showed intent to undermine international peace efforts.[24] Badlands host [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the episode as Russia beginning to confront [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] directly and asked what Lavrov and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] would do to help Gulf leaders navigate the crisis.[24]


## February 2025 op-ed on the post-WWII order

In an op-ed published in the magazine Russia in Global Affairs in early February 2025, Lavrov argued that the administration of U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] regards the UN-based international system and the "rules-based order" as undesirable and would likely test the limits of both.[25] He wrote that the United States and its allies had never genuinely followed the UN Charter's principle of the equality of states, believing the Yalta-Potsdam agreements — signed in 1945 by the victorious World War II allies, the Soviet Union, the United States, and the United Kingdom — to run against their interests.[25] "The West evidently subscribed to these principles with ulterior motives, and then grossly violated them in Yugoslavia, Iraq, Libya, and Ukraine," Lavrov said, while cautioning that the UN Charter should not be abandoned lest the world lose its common guiding values.[25]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the op-ed as evidence that Lavrov and Vladimir Putin share an adversary list with Trump and were "saying the quiet part out loud," characterizing Lavrov's posture of geopolitical opposition as kayfabe that would eventually flip in Western perception.[25]

## Warning to Israel over Syria (December 2024)

At an online press conference in late December 2024, Lavrov cautioned [[israel|Israel]] against solving its geopolitical problems at the expense of war-torn [[syria|Syria]], warning that reckless actions could erode the Middle East security framework.[26] He stressed that [[russia|Russia]] insists on Syria remaining independent following the demise of Bashar al-Assad, said Moscow maintains contact with Damascus and regional partners, and declared that "the disintegration of Syria must not be allowed."[26] Addressing the [[israeli-buffer-zone-in-syria|buffer zone Israel established]] in internationally recognised Syrian territory, he urged Israel "to understand its responsibility in these collective [stabilization] efforts and refrain from ensuring its security at the expense of others," adding that "one cannot expect to destroy all military facilities in a neighboring country and then live in peace and harmony forever. This is like sowing a storm that will inevitably come back to haunt those who engage in such actions."[26]

## Blaming EU elites for Ukrainian drone strikes (May 2025)

During a Q&A session in Moscow in late May 2025, Lavrov said that some of Kiev's Western backers — particularly the UK, France, Germany, and the EU's leadership — bore responsibility for the [[2025-ukrainian-drone-campaign-against-russia|latest Ukrainian drone strikes]] on Russian territory, claiming they sought to derail renewed peace talks to serve their own political agendas.[27] He accused those governments of backing "Ukrainian Nazis," stating, "We are convinced that they bear their share of responsibility for these crimes," and adding that Moscow would seek to "put an end to this policy."[27] Lavrov spoke after Russian forces reported intercepting 776 drones and 12 missiles between Tuesday and Friday morning, with 12 drones hitting their targets and another 104 UAVs downed overnight into Saturday, and after a helicopter carrying [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] was caught in a massive drone raid over the Kursk Region.[27] He tied the escalation to an attempt to disrupt negotiations renewed in Istanbul after Putin offered to resume talks without preconditions.[27]


## Tartus denial during the fall of Assad (December 2024)
