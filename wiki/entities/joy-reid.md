# Joy Reid

Joy Reid is an American television host who presented "The ReidOut" on [[msnbc|MSNBC]].[1]

## Hot-mic incident on Biden and the border (January 2024)

During the Monday, January 29, 2024 edition of *The ReidOut*, Reid was caught by a live microphone swearing during a segment about President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], reportedly saying "Starting another f****** war," apparently believing her microphone was off.[2] She had introduced a discussion of the border crisis and cut to a clip of Biden's South Carolina speech in which he said that if the pending bill were law he would "shut down the border now and I'd fix it quickly," adding that [[congress|Congress]] "needs to get it done."[2] Biden was referring to a bipartisan Senate border deal that would grant him authority to close the southern border when it became overwhelmed; its progress had stalled among Republicans and Democrats alike, with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] urging House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] to accept it only if it were "perfect on the border."[2] Reid reportedly apologized publicly afterward.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], argued the moment "hilariously revealed" Reid to be a propagandist, contending that she peddles a great deal of Biden messaging and that the slip showed her audience she was misleading them "as a matter of business."[2] The same commentary allowed that on the substance of Biden's war record "she's not wrong," noting that a [[cnn|CNN]] opinion piece published a day later described the United States as already in the midst of a Middle East regional war and read in parts "like a Trump Campaign Advertorial."[2]

## Interview with Don Lemon on black male Trump support

On September 17, 2024, Reid hosted former [[cnn|CNN]] host [[don-lemon|Don Lemon]] to discuss Lemon's conversations with black men who said they supported [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] Reid introduced a clip of those interviews and then asked Lemon, "What do you make of black men, as disdainful as Trump is to black people?"[1]

Lemon replied that some of the men cited stimulus checks issued during Trump's presidency, which Lemon attributed instead to a Democratic Congress and Nancy Pelosi, and argued that part of the support was "bravado" and part "aspirational"—suggesting that men who "don't have an agency that white men have in society" wanted to emulate Trump's lifestyle of airplanes, gold toilets and beautiful women.[1]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized Lemon's framing as itself racist, arguing that telling an entire racially-defined voting bloc that it lacks agency was a stunning statement for any man to make, and predicted the remarks would expand rather than reduce Trump's support among black voters.[1] The commentary described Reid and Lemon as speaking to an "ever-dwindling audience."[1]

## "DEI Mayor" segment

In March 2024, Reid hosted Baltimore mayor [[brandon-scott|Brandon Scott]] on her MSNBC program to discuss conservative critics who had labeled him the "DEI Mayor" following the [[francis-scott-key-bridge-collapse|Francis Scott Key Bridge collapse]].[3] Reid asserted that when right-wingers mock DEI they actually mean "black people," saying, "It's the reason they complained about [[critical-race-theory|Critical Race Theory]], it's not fashionable to be openly racist anymore in America."[3] Modernity News, in coverage carried by Badlands, described her as a "professional race-bator" who angrily framed opposition to DEI as inherently racist.[3]

## Electoral College commentary (October 2024)

In the final week of the 2024 campaign, Reid was among the first prominent voices to renew calls for abolishing the [[electoral-college|Electoral College]], preceding a [[politico|Politico]] column making the same argument by a couple of days.[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the sequence as evidence of coordinated talking points, asking "Which radical communist is delivering the talking points today?" and arguing that the campaign was pre-emptive positioning for a [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] popular-vote victory.[4] Her rebuttal was that without the Electoral College the United States would no longer be a representative republic.[4]
