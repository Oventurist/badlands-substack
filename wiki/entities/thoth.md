# Thoth

Thoth is the Egyptian god who, fused with the Greek god Hermes, produced the syncretic figure of [[hermes-trismegistus|Hermes Trismegistus]], the legendary source of the teachings of [[hermeticism|Hermeticism]].[1] The identification was described by [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] in his February 2023 [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] survey of the subject.[1]

## Identification with Hermes

Greeks in the Ptolemaic Kingdom of Egypt recognised the equivalence of Hermes and Thoth through [[interpretatio-graeca|interpretatio graeca]], the "Greek translation" discourse used to interpret the mythology and religion of other cultures, and the two gods were consequently worshiped as one.[1] DeLarme situates this within the broader Greek willingness to acknowledge that the Olympians had exact analogs among the gods of other nations, particularly Egypt, whose pantheon the Greeks revered as prototypes of their own.[1] As an illustration, the Temple of Thoth at Khemenu became known in the Hellenistic period as Hermopolis.[1]

## Attributes

Hermes/Thoth — who may or may not have been a real individual, or multiple individuals — is considered the founder of science, religion, mathematics, geometry, [[alchemy|alchemy]], philosophy, medicine and magic.[1] One proposed origin of the epithet "Trismegistus" is an epithet of Thoth found at the Temple of Esna: "Thoth the great, the great, the great."[1] Clement of Alexandria understood the Egyptians to possess forty-two sacred writings by Hermes encapsulating the whole training of Egyptian priests.[1]
