# Robert Corson

Robert Corson was a Houston developer who in March 1986 bought the Kleberg County (Texas) Savings and Loan for $6 million and renamed it [[vision-banc-savings|Vision Banc Savings]].[1] To get the thrift he reportedly used a letter of recommendation from a judge who in 1988 would head [[george-hw-bush|George Bush]]'s campaign in Harris County, Houston — one of many Bush associates, Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] notes, who were starting up or buying banks and thrifts at that time.[1]

Journalist Pete Brewton reported that Corson is identified in federal law enforcement records as a known money launderer, and that one former [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] operative said Corson frequently transported large sums of cash for the agency.[1] At the time of purchase the thrift had assets of $70 million; four months later it was insolvent, a speed Lange calls possibly the fastest bankruptcy in history.[1]

Brewton attributed the failure partly to a $20 million loan to help finance a Florida land deal, on which Corson's thrift lost $17 million.[1] Another thrift, [[hill-financial-savings|Hill Financial Savings]] of Red Hill, Pennsylvania, put up $80 million to seal the deal and lost $40 million.[1] Brewton said the Pennsylvania thrift, since bankrupt, had ties to the CIA and organised crime, as did one of the men who assembled the land deal, Miami lawyer [[lawrence-freeman|Lawrence Freeman]], a convicted money launderer for drug runners.[1] Lange concludes that the [[shadow-government|shadow government]] and its drug trafficking partners made a great deal of money on the arrangement.[1]
