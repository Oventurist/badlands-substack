# Mark Zwonitzer

Mark Zwonitzer is the ghostwriter who worked with [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on his memoir and recorded private conversations with him at Biden's home during 2016 and 2017.[1] Those recordings were later obtained by the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] as part of Special Counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s investigation into Biden's handling of classified documents.[1]

The Zwonitzer tapes became the central object of a records fight. After the Justice Department agreed to release redacted audio and transcripts to the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]], [[mike-howell|Mike Howell]], and the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], Biden intervened in the litigation to block their release.[1] The [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-dc-circuit|D.C. Circuit]] rejected his emergency bid in July 2026, finding a substantial public interest in the recordings while observing that redactions would protect sensitive personal details.[1]

## Release of the recordings

In July 2026 the [[oversight-project|Oversight Project]] published more than 70 hours of Zwonitzer's 2016 and 2017 conversations with [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], obtained during special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s classified documents probe, after Biden's attorneys abandoned a months-long effort to block release.[2] In one exchange Biden cautions Zwonitzer, "Some of this may be classified … So, be careful. I'm not sure … It's not marked classified but —," and at another point says, "The next thing I have here is, um, this is classified …"[2]

## House Judiciary subpoena (March 2024)

On Friday, March 22, 2024, Republicans on the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] subpoenaed Zwonitzer, ghostwriter for President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s memoir, seeking recordings and a transcript of conversations referenced by special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]].[3] Hur's report indicated that Biden on three separate occasions read passages from his notebook referencing classified information while working with Zwonitzer.[3] Zwonitzer deleted the recordings at one point, but they were recovered by Hur's team, which recommended no charges against either the ghostwriter or Biden.[3] The escalation followed Zwonitzer's apparent initial agreement to hand over the materials and subsequent reversal; committee chair [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] wrote that on March 11, 2024, over two weeks after the return date on the original letter, Zwonitzer's attorney told the committee he would not produce documents absent a compelling subpoena.[3] Badlands commentary characterized the reversal as possible obstruction of justice and noted that the underlying allegation — passing classified information to a ghostwriter — would be a crime.[3]
