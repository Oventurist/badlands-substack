# Mark Zwonitzer

Mark Zwonitzer is the ghostwriter who collaborated with [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on his memoir and whose recorded conversations with Biden became central evidence in Special Counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s classified-documents investigation.

The recordings at issue were made during 2016–2017 at Biden's home while the two worked on the book. Because Biden discussed material that investigators later examined in the context of classified-records handling, the audio and transcripts were obtained by the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] and folded into the Hur record.

## Litigation over the recordings

As reported in the July 22, 2026 [[badlands-brief-1ae|Badlands Brief]], the Zwonitzer tapes became the object of a FOIA fight. The Justice Department first withheld most of the material, then agreed to release redacted versions to the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]], [[mike-howell|Mike Howell]], and the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]]. Biden intervened seeking an injunction; the [[dc-circuit-court-of-appeals|D.C. Circuit]] denied his emergency request, finding no likelihood of success on the merits and citing the public interest in assessing the declination decision, while noting that redactions would shield sensitive personal details and that much of the substance was already public through Hur's report and Biden's memoir. The court extended an administrative stay until August 3, 2026.

In the Badlands framing, the significance of the Zwonitzer material lies less in its documentary content than in what the audio is expected to reveal about Biden's cognitive state — a question tied directly to the corpus's [[autopen-scandal|autopen scandal]] thesis.
