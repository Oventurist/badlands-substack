# Barry Seal

**Barry Seal** was an American pilot and drug smuggler who became a central figure in the alleged convergence of narcotics trafficking, arms running, and United States covert operations at [[mena-arkansas|Mena, Arkansas]] during the 1980s.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] describes him as "a morally gray pilot with a rap sheet longer than a Mena runway."[1]

## Smuggling career

Seal arrived in Mena in 1981, having previously smuggled for the [[medellin-cartel|Medellín Cartel]] — by his own court testimony during his 1983 [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]] case, some $50 million in cocaine at a rate of roughly 1,000 pounds a month.[1] The account is documented in Mara Leveritt's *The Boys on the Tracks* (1999) and covered extensively in [[whitney-webb|Whitney Webb]]'s *One Nation Under Blackmail* Vol. 1.[1] He established himself at Rich Mountain Aviation, effectively turning the airstrip into his personal fiefdom.[1]

## Informant and CIA work

The DEA arrested Seal in 1983, but he flipped and became an informant.[1] By 1984 he was on the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] payroll, his C-123 aircraft fitted with a concealed camera for a Nicaragua sting operation that photographed a Sandinista official allegedly handling cocaine — an episode documented in declassified CIA records cited in the 1996 CIA Inspector General report on Mena, released via [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] in 2020.[1]

The [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]] administration leaked the photographs in order to portray the Sandinistas as narco-traffickers, but the gambit backfired and is believed to have blown Seal's cover.[1]

## Death and aftermath

In February 1986 Colombian hitmen shot Seal dead in Baton Rouge, though many observers believe the killing was orchestrated from within the United States government.[1] Eight months later, in October 1986, his aircraft — by then tied to [[oliver-north|Oliver North]]'s Contra resupply network — crashed in Nicaragua carrying a load of weapons, pulling Mena into the glare of the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra scandal]], as reported by PBS Frontline in 1987.[1]
