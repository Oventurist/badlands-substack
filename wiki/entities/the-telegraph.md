# The Telegraph

The Telegraph is a British newspaper whose editorial decisions drew Badlands scrutiny in March 2026 after it removed an article documenting alleged cooperation between Christian villages in southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]] and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[1]

## Removal of the Lebanon story (March 2026)

Third-party traces indicated the piece was published on March 23, 2026 under the headline "Christians and Hezbollah unite against 'Epstein empire'" before being taken down roughly a day later.[1] The reason for the removal was not publicly explained, and the original article is no longer accessible for independent review.[1]

Badlands noted that the underlying claim sits within a complex and contested reality: separate reporting from Christianity Today describes Christian communities in southern Lebanon as geographically embedded in Hezbollah-influenced territory and exposed to Israeli military operations, with some residents denying affiliation with Hezbollah even while remaining inside its operational environment.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued the takedown reflected a failure of nerve, writing that as Christians and Muslims in southern Lebanon joined forces against the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]], The Telegraph "doesn't even have the decency or courage to keep their published story on these events."[1] He framed the retraction as part of a wider media pattern of suppressing evidence that complicates the standard portrayal of Hezbollah as an organization dedicated to killing Christians.[1]

## Ownership and the RedBird sale

Founded in 1855, The Telegraph is regarded as Britain's most prominent center-right newspaper, and its ownership became the subject of a prolonged foreign-influence controversy.[2] The paper was purchased by RedBird IMI — a subsidiary of [[redbird-capital-partners|RedBird Capital Partners]] partnered with the United Arab Emirates' [[international-media-investments|International Media Investments]] and helmed by former CNN president [[jeff-zucker|Jeff Zucker]] — before that deal was derailed in 2023 when the Conservative government passed legislation curbing foreign state ownership of British media.[2] IMI is owned primarily by Emirati royal and UAE vice president [[mansour-bin-zayed-al-nahyan|Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed Al Nahyan]].[2]

In May 2025 a transatlantic consortium led by RedBird Capital Partners agreed to acquire the title for £500 million (about $675 million), a transaction that would leave IMI as a minority stakeholder provided the [[labour-party|Labour]] government enacted pending rules allowing foreign state-owned investors to hold up to 15 percent of British newspaper publishers.[2] Zucker's influence was expected to persist because he is also an operating partner at RedBird Capital Partners.[2]
