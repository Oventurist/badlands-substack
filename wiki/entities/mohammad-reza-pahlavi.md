# Mohammad Reza Pahlavi

Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, commonly called the Shah, was installed as ruler of Iran following the 1953 coup and was overthrown in the Iranian Revolution of 1979.[1]

## Installation and the Consortium Agreement

In 1953 President [[dwight-eisenhower|Dwight Eisenhower]] authorized the [[cia|CIA]] to orchestrate a [[1953-iranian-coup|coup]] overthrowing [[mohammad-mosaddegh|Mohammad Mosaddegh]] and installing Mohammad Reza Shah as the new ruler in Iran. The Shah ratified the Consortium Agreement of 1954, which restored Britain's ownership over the oil fields in exchange for lifting the sanctions that were crippling the Iranian people. The [[anglo-iranian-oil-company|Anglo-Iranian Oil Company]] was subsequently renamed British Petroleum.[1]

## Disillusionment and downfall

The Consortium Agreement expired in 1979. In the intervening decade, the 1973 Yom Kippur War produced the oil crisis in the West, culminating in the 1974 settlement negotiated by Secretary of State [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] with Saudi King Faisal that created the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] — a deal that placed Aramco at the top of the industry while Iran remained subservient to the British.[1]

According to [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], the Shah had grown disillusioned with the Western imperialists, and when it became clear he intended to refuse renewal of the Consortium Agreement, the CIA declassified its own documents in 1978, revealing to the Iranian people that their government was a puppet state created and managed by the Anglosphere. In this account, operatives dressed as the Shah's secret police chained shut the doors of the 500-person Rex Theater during a show and burned the occupants alive using jet fuel, an event that became the catalyst igniting the [[iranian-revolution|Iranian Revolution]] of 1979.[1]
