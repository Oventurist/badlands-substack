# Fox News

Fox News is an American cable news network, part of Rupert Murdoch's NewsCorp, which Badlands Media treats as the conservative mirror image of the liberal mainstream rather than a genuine alternative to it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

The article argues that what passes for journalism at Fox is the equivalent of what appears on [[msnbc|MSNBC]] — substance-free affirmations of a partisan audience's talking points, catering to "garden variety conservatives."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Viewers receive either establishment Democrat talking points or establishment Republican talking points, with little else on offer.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

Badlands further notes that the Fox News audience is never served the difficult but important subjects discussed on [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], just as [[cnn|CNN]] avoids the reporting found at [[the-grayzone|The Grayzone]] or [[consortium-news|Consortium News]]. Though the two networks appear to present opposing perspectives, they are described as "two heads on the same wretched snake."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)


## Singham network investigation
A Fox News Digital investigation published in mid-March 2026 reported that China-based tech tycoon [[neville-roy-singham|Neville Roy Singham]] routed hundreds of millions of dollars from his Shanghai base into US nonprofits, media outlets, and activist groups promoting identity politics, sectarian division, and socialist causes.[2] That reporting preceded the federal grand jury action opened in Manhattan under US Attorney [[jay-clayton|Jay Clayton]].[2]

## Pezeshkian resignation hoax, May 2026
Fox News ran the false claim that Iranian President [[masoud-pezeshkian|Masoud Pezeshkian]] had resigned as the lead story on its website on May 31, 2026, under the headline "Iran's president reportedly submits resignation letter amid claims of deepening internal divisions," sourced to [[iran-international|Iran International]].[3] After the Iranian government denied the report, the outlet amended the headline to "Iran president's request adds new twist to US-Iran talks as Trump weighs peace proposal" while leaving the article body unchanged and the accompanying image still captioned "REGIME RIFT."[3] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] mocked the outlet as "Faux News," arguing that quietly swapping the headline rather than issuing a correction indicated the newsroom knew the story was propaganda and was managing embarrassment.[3] The episode is documented at [[pezeshkian-resignation-hoax-2026|the Pezeshkian resignation hoax]].[3]

## Badlands critique of "secret comms" claims (2026)

In February 2026, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] rejected the idea that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] uses FOX News as a primary force multiplier because the network is secretly packed with "white hats" delivering coded communications, arguing instead that he is forced to engage with it because significant portions of the awakened audience still consume it — often explicitly because they believe it carries secret comms.[4] He called this a slap in the face to a community that once defined itself in opposition to the Media Industrial Complex, warning that lending attention and energy to the three-letter networks perpetuates their relevance and compels those dismantling the broader System to spend strategic resources on them.[4]

## Dominion defamation settlement (April 2023)

On April 18, 2023, Fox News settled the defamation lawsuit brought by [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] for $787.5 million, averting a trial that would have put one of the world's top media companies in the crosshairs over its coverage of false vote-rigging claims in the 2020 U.S. election.[5] Legal experts called it the largest settlement ever struck by an American media company, announced at the eleventh hour in Wilmington, Delaware after a jury had already been selected.[5] The deal spared Fox the peril of having chairman [[rupert-murdoch|Rupert Murdoch]] and on-air hosts [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]] and [[jeanine-pirro|Jeanine Pirro]] called to the witness stand.[5] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Fox had been the first Media Industrial Complex outlet to call Arizona for [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on November 3, 2020, and that settling both painted [[2020-election-fraud-claims|election fraud]] as a conspiracy theory and provided "narrative shielding" against future exposure; details are covered at [[fox-dominion-defamation-settlement|the Fox–Dominion defamation settlement]].[5]

## Kirby email disclosure (September 2024)

In September 2024 Fox News Digital obtained an unintended admission from the [[white-house|White House]] when [[national-security-council|National Security Council]] communications adviser [[john-kirby|John Kirby]] replied to all on an email chain that included a Fox reporter, dismissing veterans critical of the 2021 [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] withdrawal as "a 'handful' of vets indeed and all of one stripe."[6] Fox had solicited comment on criticism from four veterans, including Rep. [[cory-mills|Cory Mills]], R-Fla.; Kirby subsequently told the reporter, "Clearly, I didn't realize you were on the chain."[6]

## The January 6 footage broadcasts (2023)

In March 2023 Fox News host [[tucker-carlson]] was given access to roughly 42,000 hours of Capitol surveillance video from January 6, 2021 and used it to challenge the findings of the [[january-6-committee|January 6 committee]].[7] Senate leaders [[chuck-schumer]] and [[mitch-mcconnell]] publicly condemned both Carlson and the network for airing the footage.[7]

When Carlson's following broadcast contained no further revelations, [[the-gateway-pundit]] reported suggestions that [[rupert-murdoch]] and the Fox News executive leadership had intervened to alter the program's plan under external pressure.[7] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief]] argued that the footage should never have been routed through a single network and should instead have been released in full to the public.[7]

## Jankowicz defamation suit (2023)

In May 2023 [[nina-jankowicz|Nina Jankowicz]], the head of the short-lived [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] [[disinformation-governance-board|Disinformation Governance Board]], launched a defamation suit against Fox News, alleging the network published false reports about her that endangered her life and threatened her safety.[8] Although the board never held a meeting, it was heavily criticized by the GOP and regularly covered by Fox News, which mentioned Jankowicz and her role more than 300 times, with hosts and commentators asserting she planned to censor speech and using nicknames such as "disinfo overlord," "disinformation czaress" and "minister of truth."[8]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], argued that because Jankowicz headed a government entity she was a representative of the government, and that criticism of government is among the most protected activities under the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]]; a win for Jankowicz would, in this view, show that constitutional protections for media entities and individuals were no longer in place.[8] The commentary described the case as a potential historic precedent for free speech and warned that in the prevailing judicial climate she could prevail.[8]

## Hosts' private doubts about 2020 fraud claims

Court filings released in February 2023 in the [[dominion-v-fox-news-defamation-lawsuit|$1.6 billion Dominion Voting Systems defamation suit]] showed that Fox News hosts had serious private concerns about the voter fraud allegations made on air by allies of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[9] [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] told a producer on November 16, 2020 that "[[sidney-powell|Sidney Powell]] is lying" about having evidence, and called her an "unguided missile" and "dangerous as hell"; [[laura-ingraham|Laura Ingraham]] called Powell "a complete nut" and extended the judgment to [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]]; [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]] testified that he "did not believe it for one second."[9]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] argued the disclosures illustrate the network's mindset and explain both its early Arizona call and its reluctance to air fraud claims, since its hosts never valued them, adding that Fox misses the pre-Trump era when the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] was easier to conceal.[9]

## Hosting the September 2024 debate

On August 2, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that he had agreed with Fox News to debate [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] on September 4 in Pennsylvania, with [[bret-baier|Bret Baier]] and [[martha-maccallum|Martha MacCallum]] as moderators and — unlike his earlier debate with [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] — a full arena audience.[10] Trump said the debate previously scheduled on ABC had been terminated because Biden would no longer participate and because his litigation against that network created a conflict of interest.[10]

## Carlson's breach-of-contract claim and ratings decline (May 2023)

In May 2023 attorneys for [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] reportedly sent Fox News a letter accusing the network of breach of contract and fraud, alleging that it had fired the host as part of its $787.5 million settlement with [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]].[11] Carlson remained under contract with Fox until January 2025 and announced that week that he would bring "a new version" of his show to [[twitter|Twitter]].[11]

Fox News Tonight, the replacement program in Carlson's former slot, reclaimed prime time's top spot the following week with 1.4 million viewers, but was down nearly 1.2 million from both its own debut broadcast and Carlson's roughly 2.6 million average in the 8pm hour.[11] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the numbers as evidence that only "establishment RINOs" were still watching and that their number was shrinking, and described Dominion's role in Fox's decision to exit its most profitable prime-time talent as the most significant disclosure of the affair.[11]

## Smartmatic defamation defense (May 2025)

In May 2025, Fox News won a key victory in the defamation lawsuit brought against it by the electronic voting systems company [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]] when a New York appeals court overturned a lower court ruling and granted the network access to materials from the 2024 federal bribery indictment of several Smartmatic executives.[12] Fox then filed a 156-page brief setting out its arguments for summary judgment, following an initial request made on May 1.[12] The indictment, returned in August 2024, charged three current and former Smartmatic executives — among them co-founder and president Roger Piñate — over an alleged bribery scheme in the Philippines.[12]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the development as "a pleasant surprise," noting that Fox had been mocked for settling with a related voting-systems vendor even though it had reason to believe that vendor's security assurances were false, and welcomed the sign that the network was fighting back this time.[12]

## Coverage of the New York airport caravan protest

Fox News Digital obtained video of the [[pro-palestinian-airport-caravan-protest|pro-Palestinian caravan]] that disrupted traffic around John F. Kennedy International and LaGuardia airports at the turn of 2024, showing protesters with a bullhorn chanting "NYPD, KKK, IDF they're all the same!"[13] The outlet's report, excerpted in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], described the Port Authority's closure of all JFK entrances to people without a boarding pass and the forcible removal of organizers from Terminal 4.[13]

## Carlson's removal and the Dominion settlement (May 2023)

On May 9, 2023, the legal team of former host [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] said a member of the Fox board had told Carlson that his abrupt removal from the air the previous month was part of the network's settlement with [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] over 2020 election claims, according to two people briefed on the conversation who spoke to [[axios|Axios]].[14] Carlson announced the same day that he was moving his program to [[twitter|Twitter]], taking a parting shot at the network: "If you bump up against the limits [in the news business] you will be fired for it."[14] Commentators noted that Carlson's Fox contract required him to stay off the air until 2025 as far as rival networks were concerned, and that [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] said no deal of any kind had been signed with him.[14] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the move as Musk and Carlson "cucking FOX News in the most humiliating and based public manner possible."[14]

## Ratings collapse after the Carlson exit (April 2023)

Fox News ratings fell sharply on the Monday night following the network's announcement that it and [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] had "agreed to part ways."[15] The replacement program *Fox News Tonight*, launched with [[brian-kilmeade|Brian Kilmeade]] as the first of a series of rotating hosts, drew just under 2.6 million viewers — roughly 21 percent below the 3.3 million average *Tucker Carlson Tonight* had posted over the prior eight Mondays.[15] In the 25-54 news demographic the new show's 0.24 rating trailed Carlson's 0.38 average by 37 percent.[15] Even so, the hour still led cable news by a sizable margin, ahead of a *Joe Scarborough Presents* special on [[msnbc|MSNBC]] (1.51 million) and *Anderson Cooper 360* on [[cnn|CNN]] (728,000).[15]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Fox had used [[narrative-shielding|narrative shielding]] in the [[fox-dominion-defamation-settlement|FOX–Dominion settlement]], protecting the election-fraud apparatus by setting legal and psychological precedent against election reform, and that "the Tucker deployment" served as a counter that exposed Fox to its own MAGA-leaning audience as a "den of wolves."[15] He characterised the network's continued ratings lead over rival outlets as being "the prettiest horse at the glue factory."[15]

## Market cost of the Carlson split

Fox News Media's announcement that it and [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] had "agreed to part ways" cost parent company [[fox-corporation|Fox Corporation]] more than $800 million in market value at Monday's low, when class B shares fell 5.2 percent to $29.27 before recovering part of the loss later that week.[16] The slide came days after the network's $787 million defamation settlement with [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]], and the reason for the split with its highest-rated host remained unexplained.[16] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] concluded that financial success was no longer the network's primary goal — "it's almost as if it was just another propaganda outlet all along" — and noted that Fox had also shed millions of Carlson viewers who made up a significant portion of its audience.[16] Former Army psychological warfare officer [[scott-bennett|Scott Bennett]] argued the firing served an agenda of keeping the public uninformed and unquestioning.[16]

## Anti-SLAPP countersuit against Smartmatic (January 2024)

On January 24, 2024, a New York judge ruled that Fox News could press claims that [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]]'s $2.7 billion lawsuit over the network's 2020 election coverage was designed to chill its speech.[17] Fox had settled with [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] for $787.5 million in April 2023, avoiding a trial on whether it knowingly spread false claims about [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s defeat to appease viewers, but faced potentially billions more in liability from the Smartmatic suit filed in Manhattan in 2021.[17]

Taking advantage of New York's broad journalistic protections, Fox countersued under the state's anti-SLAPP statute — aimed at "Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation" — arguing that Smartmatic's enormous damages demand had no basis in fact.[17] In a seven-page ruling, Manhattan Supreme Court Justice David Cohen wrote that no court had yet adjudicated whether Smartmatic's claims had a substantial factual basis or whether its damages were "so extenuated from their actual lost profits that they were pleaded and/or sought in order to chill defendants' free speech rights."[17]

## Coverage of the 2023 Trump indictment

[[media-matters-for-america|Media Matters]] reported in June 2023 that Fox News had responded to the [[2023-trump-classified-documents-indictment|indictment of Donald Trump]] over withheld classified documents with what it described as a swell of demagoguery suggesting the event signalled the end of American democracy.[18] The watchdog additionally faulted the network for pushing the claim that the indictment had been timed to distract from House Republican corruption accusations against [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], pointing out that Fox itself had reported two weeks earlier that special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] was about to conclude his months-long investigation.[18]

## Tucker Carlson's departure (April 2023)

Fox News announced on Monday, April 24, 2023 that it and prime-time host [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] had severed ties, offering no explanation beyond a statement thanking him "for his service to the network as a host and prior to that as a contributor" and confirming that his final show had aired Friday, April 21.[19] Reporting at the time attributed the decision to Fox Corporation chief executive [[lachlan-murdoch|Lachlan Murdoch]] and Fox News chief executive [[suzanne-scott|Suzanne Scott]], who are said to have made the call on the evening of April 21; Carlson, the network's highest-rated single host, was informed on Monday morning.[19] CNBC framed the exit as coming in the wake of the network's [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] defamation settlement.[19]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued the timing exposed Fox to its own audience. [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that firing Carlson on the same day [[cnn|CNN]] fired [[don-lemon|Don Lemon]] "outs FOX to 'Normie' MAGA viewers still bestowing misplaced loyalty to a network that provided Narrative Shielding to the Election Fraud machine by calling Arizona for Biden on 11/3/20 and settling with Dominion for $800 Million," and that the network was thereby kicking a hornet's nest of Carlson supporters.[19] [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], increasingly critical of the network, said he had been surprised Fox settled the Dominion case at all.[19]

## Reporting on Trump arraignment logistics (March 2023)

Fox News reported on March 20, 2023 that law enforcement officials had met behind closed doors to discuss the logistics of arraigning [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] following a possible indictment in the [[trump-hush-money-case|hush-money case]], citing a law enforcement source on street closures, barriers and extra police, on the expectation that no arraignment would occur before the following week, and on the rejection of a virtual option.[20] The outlet also reported that law enforcement was concerned about safety and that Trump had called on supporters to protest.[20]

## Lawsuit against Los Angeles County (January 2025)

In late January 2025, Fox News filed a public records lawsuit against Los Angeles County seeking documents about the county's dealings with the voting technology company [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]], days after a New York appeals court declined to dismiss Smartmatic's $2.7 billion defamation action against the network over coverage of alleged 2020 election rigging.[24] The network alleged corruption in the contracting process and said it had already uncovered evidence of undisclosed personal benefits flowing from Smartmatic to a county official.[24] See [[fox-news-lawsuit-against-los-angeles-county|the lawsuit]].[24]

## 2023 leadership change at the parent companies

The September 2023 announcement that Rupert Murdoch would relinquish the chairmanships of Fox Corp. and News Corp. drew attention to Fox News specifically, since Murdoch's continued influence over the network had been documented during the Dominion Voting Systems litigation, in which he conceded that some anchors had repeated false election claims.[25] Badlands contributor Paul Fleuret noted that Fox News had called the 2020 election early for Joe Biden, subsequently refuted fraud allegations, and released Tucker Carlson — then the highest-rated anchor in news — amid the Dominion settlement, arguing that any shift at the top of the corporate structure merited close attention.[25]
