# House Select Committee on Assassinations

The House Select Committee on Assassinations was the congressional body that reinvestigated the killings of prominent American figures, including [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr.]] Its firearms panel examined the .30-06 slug recovered from King's body (exhibit Q64) against the Remington 760 Gamemaster rifle recovered near the crime scene (exhibit Q2).[1]

The panel found that the bullet's class characteristics matched the rifle — six grooves and six lands with a right twist — but concluded it was "unable positively to identify or eliminate the Q64 bullet as having been fired from the Q2 rifle."[1] The committee also found no evidence that any U.S. government agency participated in the assassination, a conclusion [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated with open skepticism when drawing parallels to the inconclusive ballistics in the [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] case.[1]
