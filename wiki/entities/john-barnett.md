# John Barnett

John Barnett was a 62-year-old former [[boeing|Boeing]] quality manager and whistleblower who died of a self-inflicted gunshot wound in Charleston, South Carolina, in March 2024, according to the Charleston County coroner's office.[1] His death drew immediate scrutiny in the [[truth-community|truth community]] because he had reportedly told a close family friend not to believe any announcement that he had committed suicide.[1]

## Career and disclosures

Barnett worked at Boeing for 32 years before leaving the company in 2017, and had previously raised concerns about the company's production practices.[1] According to his attorneys, he had "exposed very serious safety problems with the Boeing 787 Dreamliner and was retaliated against and subjected to a hostile work environment."[1] At the time of his death he was in the middle of a legal deposition in his action against Boeing.[1]

## Circumstances of death

His attorneys said Barnett "was in very good spirits and really looking forward to putting this phase of his life behind him and moving on," adding that they saw "no indication he would take his own life" and that "no one can believe it."[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote plainly that "Barnett didn't kill himself," acknowledging that the coroner had ruled otherwise but arguing that those in the truth community are "rightly skeptical of highly-convenient suicides."[1] She emphasised that Barnett was testifying against a powerful Department of Defense contractor, wanted to testify, was in good spirits, and had pre-emptively warned friends not to believe a suicide report — calling the official account "literally unbelievable."[1]

## Context within Boeing's safety crisis

Barnett's death occurred as Boeing faced sustained scrutiny over its safety culture. An expert panel report cited in coverage of the case found a "disconnect" between senior management and regular staff and signs that employees were hesitant to report problems for fear of retaliation — precisely the retaliation Barnett's attorneys said he had already suffered.[1] Adam Dickson, a former senior manager who worked on the 737 Max programme, said the culture at Boeing "has been toxic to trust for over a decade now."[1] The [[federal-aviation-administration|Federal Aviation Administration]] separately warned that improperly installed wiring bundles on 737 Max aircraft could be damaged, causing wing controls to deploy unexpectedly.[1] Ashe closed her commentary with "RIP, Mr. Barnett," arguing that if the company were endangering passengers and killing off witnesses, commercial failure "should be the least of their consequences."[1]

## Contemporaneous reporting

The Hill reported that Barnett, 62, a prominent [[boeing|Boeing]] whistleblower who had raised safety and quality-control concerns about the company's production line, was found dead on Saturday, March 9, 2024, of an apparent self-inflicted wound sustained the previous day, according to the Charleston County Coroner's office; he was discovered in his truck in his hotel's parking lot.[2] A 32-year veteran of Boeing, Barnett's 2019 whistleblower allegations claimed that overworked employees at the company's South Carolina plant frequently fitted substandard parts on planes, and that faulty oxygen systems could result in as many as one in four oxygen masks failing to operate properly.[2] Boeing denied the claims, but a follow-up [[federal-aviation-administration|Federal Aviation Administration]] investigation lent credence to some aspects of them, finding that more than 50 "non-conforming" parts could not be traced and were lost in the company's system.[2] Barnett was in Charleston to be questioned for a long-running retaliation suit against the company.[2]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that the reaction of both the Truth Community and much of the Collective Mind to the headlines was "a sardonic, disbelieving snort," coming amid a rapid acceleration of anti-Boeing stories ranging from emergency landings to falling tires.[2] He argued for a "Bicameral approach" to the Boeing stories: either the enemy is trying to get out ahead of something, or their attempts to do so are being thwarted.[2]
