# Indian Springs State Bank

Indian Springs State Bank was a Kansas institution whose 1984 failure Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presents as another bank destroyed by its [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] connections.[1]

Its fourth largest stockholder was Iranian expatriate [[farhad-azima|Farhad Azima]], owner of the air charter company [[global-international-air|Global International Air]].[1] Indian Springs made several unsecured loans to Global International totalling $600,000, in violation of the bank's $349,000 borrower limit.[1] Global International filed for bankruptcy in 1983 and Indian Springs followed in 1984.[1]

Global International Air was part of [[oliver-north|Oliver North]]'s logistical network, which shipped arms for the U.S. government on several occasions including a shipment of 23 tons of TOW missiles to Iran by Race Aviation, another company owned by Azima.[1] The assistant U.S. Attorney handling the Indian Springs investigation was told to back off from a key figure in the collapse because he had ties to the CIA.[1] Azima's own relationship with the agency dated to the late 1970s, when he supplied air and logistical support to [[eatsco|EATSCO]], the Egyptian American Transport and Services Corporation owned by former CIA agents [[thomas-clines|Thomas Clines]], [[ted-shackley|Theodore Shackley]] and [[richard-secord|Richard Secord]]; EATSCO figured prominently in the activities of former CIA agent [[edwin-wilson|Edwin Wilson]], who shipped arms illegally to Libya.[1] Global International's Miami-based planes were maintained by [[southern-air-transport|Southern Air Transport]], another CIA proprietary, and according to Global employee Franck Van Geyso its pilots ferried arms into South and Central America and returned to Florida with drugs.[1]
