# Jon Ossoff

Jon Ossoff is a Democratic United States senator from Georgia. He has been among the more persistent Senate advocates for restricting the personal financial trading of federal officeholders.[1]

## Congressional stock trading ban

In July 2024, Ossoff was one of four senators — alongside Republican [[josh-hawley|Josh Hawley]] of Missouri and Democrats [[jeff-merkley|Jeff Merkley]] of Oregon and [[gary-peters|Gary Peters]] of Michigan — who negotiated and announced a bipartisan agreement to ban members of [[congress|Congress]] from trading stocks.[1]

The negotiated amendment to an existing stock trading ban bill would immediately forbid members of Congress, the president and the vice president from purchasing stocks and other covered investments, and would give lawmakers 90 days to sell holdings they already owned. It was the first such proposal to receive formal consideration by a Senate committee, scheduled before the Homeland Security & Governmental Affairs Committee on July 24, 2024.[1]

Commentary published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that the absence of such a law was itself an indictment of Washington, contrasting the annual "Independence" certification required of every employee at a large SEC audit firm — covering insider trading, antitrust, money laundering and bribery — with the far more lenient oversight enjoyed by legislative and executive branch officials who possess superior access to market-moving information. The same commentary predicted the measure would not pass in a form that meaningfully constrained lawmakers.[1]

## Smurfing allegations (February 2025)

On Monday, February 24, 2025, election investigator [[peter-bernegger|Peter Bernegger]] posted on X that "US Senator @SenOssoff caught Smurfing," saying "a quick data run on his campaign…doesn't show all of the laundering."[2] According to Bernegger's records, 20 people made 7,380 contributions to Ossoff totalling $197,223 — figures covering only the top 20 donors and only contributions routed through [[actblue|ActBlue]].[2] Badlands writer Ashe in America described [[smurfing]] as the laundering of money through stolen identities and argued each such contribution would constitute a campaign finance violation, falsifying business records, forgery, identity theft, and wire fraud.[2]

## Shutdown pay votes (October 2025)

During the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|October 2025 government shutdown]], Ossoff was one of three Democrats who crossed party lines on Thursday, October 23, 2025, to support the Republican-sponsored [[shutdown-fairness-act-of-2025|Shutdown Fairness Act of 2025]], which would have paid active-duty military and other essential federal workers; the measure nonetheless failed 54-45, short of the 60 votes needed.[3] Ossoff and fellow Georgia Democrat [[raphael-warnock|Raphael Warnock]] continued to vote against the House-passed stopgap funding bill.[3]
