# Jena Griswold

Jena Griswold is the Colorado Secretary of State and a leading antagonist in the prosecution and continued incarceration of former Mesa County Clerk [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]].[1]

## Opposition to Peters clemency

After Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]] signaled in March 2026 that he was open to commuting Peters' nine-year sentence, Griswold joined Attorney General [[phil-weiser|Phil Weiser]] and election official Matt Crane in releasing statements demanding that Polis show no mercy.[1] Peters' attorney [[john-case|John Case]] identified the three as "Tina's political enemies," describing Crane as a [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] "shill."[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the coordinated response of Griswold and her allies as evidence that Peters is the Colorado establishment's "most valuable political scalp" and the last support for a narrative about [[election-denial|election denialism]] being dangerous to democracy.[1] She argued that officials such as Griswold needed that narrative because it had been used to justify weaponizing state government.[1]

## Response to the commutation (May 2026)

After Polis commuted Peters' sentence in May 2026, Griswold and other critics said the decision risked weakening deterrence against election-system tampering and could damage public trust in election administration.[2] Peters had been convicted in 2024 for allowing unauthorized access to [[mesa-county|Mesa County]] voting systems, and the resulting data circulated among election-denial activists.[2]

## BIOS password scandal

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited Griswold's BIOS password scandal as a concrete illustration of official denial outrunning actual investigation: over 600 passwords for devices in 62 of 64 [[colorado|Colorado]] counties were exposed on the state's website, and the Colorado Department of State attempted to cover it up before being caught.[3] On a recorded line, the Deputy Secretary of State said the clerks would not be told because it would cause a media storm; instead, both clerks and the public were told there had been no breach.[3] The exposure spanned that year's primary and the 2024 early-voting period.[3]

Ashe argued the state's own story is self-defeating: if a breach requires physical access to machines held in the counties, the department could not know no breach occurred without informing the clerks and jointly investigating in 62 of 64 counties — which the Deputy Secretary admitted did not happen.[3] The only alternative, remote determination, would debunk the air-gap narrative and imply remote access to Colorado elections without the knowledge of elected clerks.[3] She concluded that the likeliest explanation is a combination of remote capability and outright lying, with resources devoted to a cover story rather than security, and that the declassified [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] memo shows officials would have known their 2020-era talking points were false.[3]

## 2026 attorney general nomination

Griswold secured the Colorado Democratic nomination for attorney general in the 2026 primary, while Attorney General [[phil-weiser|Phil Weiser]] secured the nomination for governor.[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the result as voters choosing change down ballot while retaining existing party leadership, and joked that when "Phil becomes Emperor and Jena becomes 'The Law'," she hoped to be broken out of the camps.[4]

## Pardon response and DOJ voter-data lawsuit (December 2025)

Griswold joined Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]], Attorney General [[phil-weiser|Phil Weiser]] and the Colorado Democratic Party in rejecting President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 11 December 2025 pardon of [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]], maintaining that no president has jurisdiction over state law or the power to pardon state convictions and that the question was one for the courts.[5]

On the same day, the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] sued Griswold to force her to produce detailed voter information without redactions.[5] The suit came alongside a separate DOJ investigation, announced earlier that week, into complaints about conditions in the [[colorado-department-of-corrections|Colorado Department of Corrections]] — developments Badlands described collectively as a "Rocky Mountain Showdown" between federal authorities and Colorado's state government.[5] Pollster [[rasmussen-reports|Rasmussen]] added to the pressure by asking publicly what federal officials had told Colorado officials about Colorado elections that "suddenly now has to be kept secret."[5]

## Kline appointment and the Peters case (2025)

At the beginning of 2025 Griswold's deputy secretary resigned and was replaced by "former" [[perkins-coie|Perkins Coie]] attorney [[andrew-kline|Andrew Kline]], who according to Badlands reporting was secretly appointed in March 2025 to run Griswold's office with no announcement — his name simply began appearing on Colorado Department of State mastheads.[6] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] raised the appointment while covering President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s August 2025 demand that Colorado release [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]], describing Griswold's office as the one that tried to remove Trump from the ballot "for democracy," that persecuted Trump supporters as a matter of policy, and that was the allegedly injured party in the prosecution of Peters.[6]

## PILF ERIC lawsuit (September 2024)

In September 2024 Griswold suffered another election law loss in federal court when the U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado ordered her office to release [[electronic-registration-information-center|Electronic Registration Information Center]] (ERIC) reports suspected of containing dead registrants on the state's voter rolls, covering individuals who may have died within the previous three years.[7] The Federalist described her as the secretary of state who would forever be known for her drive to knock [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] off the ballot, and framed the ruling as a victory for the [[public-interest-legal-foundation|Public Interest Legal Foundation]] and a loss for Griswold and ERIC.[7] Griswold signed the stipulation after the court denied her request to dismiss the case, and Judge [[philip-brimmer|Philip Brimmer]] ordered disclosure of the 2021 ERIC reports by November 1 with only minimal redactions to the ERIC Report Key; the judge then dismissed PILF's claim that Griswold had violated the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act]] of 1993.[7]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the article missed the most explosive detail of Colorado's relationship with ERIC: that in 2022 Griswold's office "mistakenly" mailed postcards to some 30,000 foreign nationals encouraging them to vote, blamed on a glitch in the database tied to Colorado's driver's license list — officially a "data coding mismatch" between the Colorado DMV database and ERIC.[7] She noted the same problem had occurred in 2020, and that Deputy Secretary of State [[chris-beall|Chris Beall]], whom she cross-examined at her own trial in July, runs every aspect of Griswold's office and admitted these facts under oath.[7]

## Anderson v. Griswold

As Colorado Secretary of State, Griswold was the named respondent in [[anderson-v-griswold|Anderson v. Griswold]], the Fourteenth Amendment challenge to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s place on the state's 2024 primary ballot.[8] Trump ultimately remained on the Colorado primary ballot, but the trial court's insurrection finding, upheld by the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]], was carried forward on appeal by the Colorado GOP and Trump to the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]].[8]

## December 2023 ballot certification

On December 28, 2023, Griswold announced that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would remain on Colorado's presidential primary ballot for the time being, after the Colorado Republican Party's appeal to the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] placed a stay on the ruling that had removed him under the insurrection clause of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]].[9] The ballot was scheduled to go to print on January 5, and Trump would remain listed unless the Supreme Court affirmed the lower court's ruling or declined to take the appeal.[9]

The [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]]'s 4-3 decision in [[anderson-v-griswold|Anderson v. Griswold]] had held that it would be a wrongful act under the Election Code for the Secretary to list Trump as a candidate, and that she could not count write-in votes for him, but stayed the ruling until January 4, 2024 — the day before her deadline to certify the ballot's content — with the stay to remain in place if review was sought in the Supreme Court.[9] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that, given the appeal, Griswold would have been violating a court order had she certified a ballot omitting Trump, and contrasted her action with Maine Secretary of State [[shenna-bellows|Shenna Bellows]]'s unilateral removal of Trump the same day.[9] Although Colorado's 10 electoral votes were unlikely to go to the Republican nominee, observers warned the ruling could set precedent for other states seeking to remove Trump from their ballots.[9]

## Outside investigation into the 2024 password breach

An outside investigation released in December 2024 found that Griswold and her office violated state security protocols in a way that allowed the disclosure of election equipment passwords.[10] The independent review, conducted by the Baird Quinn law firm on behalf of the Secretary of State's Office, determined that the passwords were "mistakenly, unknowingly and unintentionally" posted online; the secretary and the office were cited for violating state information security rules, but no repercussions were expected.[10] BIOS passwords for election equipment in 34 counties had been posted online on 21 June but were not discovered by the state until 24 October, and Griswold, the state's elections chief, did not disclose the breach until five days later, when the Republican Party made it public.[10] She said the disclosure posed no immediate threat to the state's election system and that all voting equipment passwords were reset before Election Day; the former employee who created the password spreadsheet was not admonished because she had left the office before the material was posted.[10] The breach undermined trust in the state's voting system days before the 2024 election and sparked an ongoing criminal investigation.[10]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] recalled that, in response to the 2021 Arizona Senate audit, Griswold had infamously said that "fraudits" have no place in Colorado, and observed wryly that fraudits do have a place there after all.[10] The commentary described the level of incompetence at the [[colorado-department-of-state|Colorado Department of State]] - of which Griswold is the head - as a threat to national security, noting that no one involved, including the investigator who cleared the office, appeared aware of hidden-file capability or the "inspect workbook" function in Microsoft Excel.[10] It emphasized that the investigator did not review or audit the department's actions after 24 October 2024, the period in which the department was allegedly caught in a cover-up when the Colorado GOP broke the scandal on 29 October, and that the department's legal privilege with the investigating law firm raised further questions about scope and direction.[10]

## Role in the Peters case as described in March 2025

In March 2025, following the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s announcement that it would review [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]]'s state election tampering conviction, Article III Project founder [[mike-davis|Mike Davis]] summarized the case on X, describing Griswold as a hyper-partisan Democrat Colorado Secretary of State who, together with Attorney General [[phil-weiser|Phil Weiser]] and local District Attorney Dan Rubenstein, brought what he called trumped-up felony charges falsely accusing Peters of unauthorized access to voting machines.[11] Davis further alleged that during the same period Griswold, whom he called "a partisan slob," allowed voting-machine passwords to remain publicly posted online for four months.[11] Badlands contributor Ashe in America referred to Colorado as "the State of Jena Griswold's Corruption" and noted that Griswold was running for Governor.[11]

## Statements on counting Trump's votes (January 2024)

As the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] agreed to review the [[colorado-ballot-disqualification-case|Colorado ballot disqualification case]] in January 2024, weekend clickbait circulated claiming Griswold had said she would not count [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s votes.[12] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Griswold had said no such thing: her actual position was that if the justices found the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] had not erred, Trump would be disqualified and his votes would not count, because Colorado law provides that only ballots for "qualified" candidates may be counted.[12] Ashe characterized this as Griswold simply announcing she would follow the law, adding that "they are way ahead of us in the Centennial state."[12]

## Obstruction claims and the DOJ records dispute

Badlands host Ashe in America wrote in December 2025 that Griswold, whose arbitrary COVID-era rules about who could enter county offices prompted the conduct for which Tina Peters was convicted, remained emboldened and was continuing to obstruct the Trump Justice Department on elections.[13] Griswold said in a statement that 'we will not comply with the Trump Department of Justice's request for Coloradans' sensitive voting information,' that the DOJ 'can take a hike' because it has no legal right to the information, and that Colorado 'will not help Donald Trump undermine our elections and hurt the American people.'[13] Ashe noted that Griswold had been obstructing inquiry since at least 2021, when she banned independent audits.[13]

## Voting system password leak (October 2024)

In late October 2024, the Colorado Secretary of State's office under Griswold "improperly" posted a spreadsheet to its public website containing passwords to some of the state's voting systems.[14] The exposure affected both [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion]] and [[clear-ballot|Clear Ballot]] equipment, and the explanations offered by Griswold were contradicted by her own staff's sworn testimony.[14]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who reported on the episode extensively for Colorado Free Press, argued she was increasingly convinced that "the regime is throwing Jena under the bus," and that the resulting saga would be used to fast-track [[ranked-choice-voting|ranked choice voting]] and promote some of the worst figures in Colorado government.[14] The leak surfaced the same week that Michigan Secretary of State [[jocelyn-benson|Jocelyn Benson]] admitted to a nationwide programming defect in Dominion voter assist terminals, reinforcing a wider argument that the election system was incapable of producing a result the public would accept as legitimate.[14]

Badlands commentary returned to the episode on 1 November 2024 to contrast it with warnings from the [[cisa|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]] that Russian, Chinese and Iranian operators were attempting to disrupt the vote with disinformation.[15] The writer noted that Griswold's team had left machine BIOS passwords for more than 600 pieces of equipment publicly available on the internet for months while her office concealed the breach for days, yet officials insisted there was no threat to Colorado elections — a juxtaposition summarized as "these are not serious professionals."[15]

## Cited as a Soros-affiliated secretary of state

Chapter 19 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series takes up Q's question of who decides which voting machines are used in each state, answering that the choice belongs to each state's secretary of state.[16] The author urges readers to research how many secretaries of state are Democrats and, more pointedly, how many were funded by or are affiliated with [[george-soros|George Soros]], naming Griswold as a good example.[16] The same passage identifies Soros as the answer to Q's question about who exerts outsized influence over American elections, and notes that some counties still hand-count ballots because they are controlled by patriots and therefore run clean elections.[16]

## 2022 primary recounts

As Colorado Secretary of State, Griswold oversaw the 2022 primary recounts requested by [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] — a statewide recount covering all 64 counties — and by State Senate candidate Lynda Zamora Wilson.[17] Recounts for the other two requesting candidates, El Paso County Clerk and Recorder candidate Peter Lupia and Coroner candidate Dr. Rae Ann Weber, fell to El Paso County Clerk and Recorder Chuck Broerman.[17] The [[2022-el-paso-county-recount|El Paso County recount]] was marked by a roughly 57% ballot adjudication rate during Logic and Accuracy Testing, reported alteration of original primary batch totals to match recount figures, and ballot boxes found with broken seals — none of which prevented officials from declaring the process a success.[17]

## Write-in hysteria and Colorado qualification law (January 2024)

In early January 2024, viral claims promoted by prominent conservative accounts asserted that Griswold had said she would not count votes cast for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[18] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], a frequent critic of Griswold, wrote that she had said no such thing and that she hated having to defend her: what Griswold actually said was that if the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] upheld the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]]'s decision removing Trump from the ballot, he would be disqualified and his votes would not count.[18]

Ashe explained that this outcome follows from Colorado law rather than from the Secretary's discretion. Colorado Revised Statute 1-4-501(1) provides that no person is eligible to be a designee or candidate for office "unless that person fully meets the qualifications of that office as stated in the constitution and statutes of this state on or before the date the term of that office begins," while 1-4-501(3) sets out the procedure by which an eligible elector may challenge a candidate's qualification by verified petition in district court, with a hearing held five to ten days after the certifying statement and a determination within forty-eight hours of its close.[18] Because only ballots for "qualified" candidates may be counted, Ashe concluded that if the Supreme Court upheld the Colorado ruling it would be against the law for Griswold to count votes for Trump, and that a Colorado write-in campaign for him would be "dead on arrival."[18] She framed the situation as evidence that "the communists are way ahead of us in the Centennial state," while acknowledging that Griswold was in this instance simply following the law.[18]

## Opposition to independent election audits

In "US Elections Must Be Independent," Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] identified a June 17, 2021 tweet by Griswold as one of the biggest red flags about elections independence, in which the Colorado Secretary of State announced that she had prohibited independent election audits in the state and labeled them "Fraudits."[19] The statement was made in response to the Arizona-ordered [[maricopa-county-forensic-audit|Senate audit]] in that state, and while that audit was still underway.[19] The author characterized it as the chief elections officer of a Democrat-controlled state inferring that the legitimate action of a Republican-controlled state senate was fraudulent — "not a good look for public trust."[19] Within the essay's framework, an elected Secretary of State's personal and party stake in election outcomes is itself a self-interest conflict of the sort that [[auditor-independence|auditor independence]] rules exist to prevent.[19]
