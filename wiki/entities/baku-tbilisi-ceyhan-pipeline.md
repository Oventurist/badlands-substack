# Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan Pipeline

The Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan pipeline, commonly abbreviated BTC, is a major crude oil export pipeline running from the Caspian basin to the Mediterranean coast of [[turkey|Turkey]]. It provides a route to European markets that bypasses maritime chokepoints such as the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[1]

## Role in the April 2026 Iraqi oil realignment

Badlands Media's April 3, 2026 Brief treats the BTC pipeline as a decisive clue to the geometry of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]]. Reporting on Iraq's revival of a land corridor through [[syria|Syria]] for oil exports to Europe carried a footnote stating that [[iraq|Iraq]] is also sending oil to Turkey via the BTC pipeline.[1]

For the Brief's contributors, that detail indicates that Turkey under President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] and the Iraqi government associated with [[al-malika|Al-Malika]] are working together, which in turn supports the reading that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s public hostility toward Iraq is [[kayfabe]].[1] Combined with the Syrian corridor, the BTC route is presented as part of a deliberate diversification of export paths that undercuts [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s March 19 proposal to run all Gulf pipelines to [[israel|Israel]].[1]

## Significance in the 2024 Georgian political crisis

The pipeline featured in Badlands analysis of Georgia's December 2024 presidential transition, in which [[mikheil-kavelashvili|Mikheil Kavelashvili]] of [[georgian-dream|Georgian Dream]] was sworn in while outgoing president [[salome-zourabichvili|Salome Zourabichvili]] disputed his legitimacy.[2] Commentators described Georgia as "an important chess piece" precisely because it houses the Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline, which brings oil from Azerbaijan to the Ceyhan terminal in Turkey for onward transport to Israel, providing a reported 40% of all of Israel's oil.[2] While greater pressure to cut the supply was seen falling on Turkey than on the incoming Georgian president, Kavelashvili had already been branded an "Iranian mullah" by media detractors, raising questions about his posture toward the pipeline's operation.[2] The route is also a critical line for bypassing Russian energy dominance, making Georgia an asset the analysis argued the globalist cartel would not forfeit lightly.[2]

## The November 2024 Turkey-Israel rupture

The pipeline became a focal point of Badlands analysis after President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] announced in November 2024 that Turkey had severed relations with [[israel|Israel]] entirely.[3] Because Azerbaijani crude reaching Israel travels from Baku to the Turkish port of Ceyhan through the BTC line, the arrangement was described as critical to sustaining fuel supplies for Israel's war effort.[3] The line is operated by [[botas-international|BOTAS International]], a Turkish state-owned company founded in 1974 to run the adjacent Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline connecting the Kirkuk oil fields in Iraq, and Turkish media reported that BTC throughput is governed by a trade agreement between [[azerbaijan|Azerbaijan]] and Georgia rather than by Turkey.[3] Turkish energy analyst Haluk Direskeneli argued in an op-ed published November 13, 2024 that Turkey could not halt oil shipments from Azerbaijan to Israel without United Nations sanctions; Turkey had already severed all trade with Israel in May 2024, but the BTC oil was not considered part of that exchange.[3]

Despite the legal constraints, the companies owning oil moving through the pipeline were reported to have chosen to respect Erdogan's decision and to have refused shipments, while Turkey's Ministry of Energy and Natural Resources denied interference and stressed that BOTAS has "no authority or involvement in the purchase or sale of oil."[3] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] predicted this would soon produce fuel shortages for the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]], and connected the episode to [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]]'s call a month earlier for an arms embargo on Israel and to [[ilham-aliyev|Ilham Aliyev]]'s COP29 attack on French colonialism.[3] He further speculated that prospective [[united-nations|UN]] sanctions on Israel — a possibility raised by the standoff over Israel's expulsion of [[unrwa|UNRWA]] and a bipartisan congressional letter threatening to defund the UN if Israel's status were downgraded — could give Erdogan justification to shut the BTC pipeline altogether.[3]
