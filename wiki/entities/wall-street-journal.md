# Wall Street Journal

The Wall Street Journal is an American newspaper owned by Dow Jones that became the target of federal grand jury subpoenas in 2026 over reporting on the U.S.-Israeli military campaign against [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## DOJ subpoenas over Iran war leaks

The [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] issued grand jury subpoenas to the Journal on March 4, seeking reporters' records related to leaks about the campaign.[1] The subpoenas targeted February 23 reporting that detailed warnings from Gen. [[dan-caine|Dan Caine]], chairman of the [[joint-chiefs-of-staff|Joint Chiefs of Staff]], and other [[pentagon|Pentagon]] officials to President [[donald-trump|Trump]] about the risks of such an operation; Trump launched the joint offensive with [[israel|Israel]] five days later.[1]

Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] said the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] is prioritizing prosecution of leakers who share classified information with reporters, warning that journalists should not be surprised to receive subpoenas and emphasizing that such leaks endanger national security and soldiers' lives.[1] Dow Jones condemned the subpoenas as an attack on constitutionally protected newsgathering and vowed to vigorously oppose them.[1]

## Gabbard whistleblower reporting (2026)

In early February 2026, the Journal reported that a classified whistleblower complaint concerning Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] had been filed within a US intelligence agency and had not advanced beyond internal channels, citing unnamed current and former officials who said the complaint had stalled and was not referred for further review or action.[2] The [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] publicly rejected the framing and implications of the reporting, saying it was aware of no legitimate or substantiated complaint being improperly handled or suppressed.[2] Badlands treated the story as part of a pattern in which the Journal "is leading the charge on Tulsi" while [[politico|Politico]] pressures CISA and conservative influencers target the Justice Department; see [[tulsi-gabbard-whistleblower-complaint-allegation|the Tulsi Gabbard whistleblower complaint allegation]].[2]

## Reporting on Iranian planning of the October 7 attacks

In October 2023 the Journal reported that Iranian security officials helped plan [[hamas|Hamas]]'s [[october-7-attacks|surprise attack on Israel]] and gave the green light for the assault at a meeting in [[beirut|Beirut]], according to senior members of Hamas and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[3] The report said officers of the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]] had worked with Hamas since August 2023 to devise the air, land and sea incursions, and that details were refined during several Beirut meetings attended by IRGC officers and representatives of four Iran-backed militant groups.[3] U.S. officials, including Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]], said they had not seen evidence corroborating the account.[3]

## Carlson's suppression claim over the Yermak story (November 2025)

On November 24, 2025, journalist [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] claimed on X that the Journal had for months withheld a story documenting the "personal corruption" of [[andrey-yermak|Andrey Yermak]], chief of staff to Ukrainian leader [[volodymyr-zelensky|Vladimir Zelensky]], alleging that Yermak "has skimmed hundreds of millions in American tax dollars meant for Ukraine aid" and that "the Journal's editors can prove that. But they're not. Instead they're protecting Yermak."[4] Carlson attributed the suppression to Yermak's role as Kiev's top negotiator working to scuttle the US-drafted peace plan, and to the Murdoch family's ownership interest in continuing the war with Russia.[4]

## Red Sea deterrence commentary (December 2023)

In late December 2023 the *Journal* published a commentary headlined "U.S. Naval Deterrence Is Going, Going, Maybe Even Gone," addressing Iranian and [[houthis|Houthi]] attacks in the [[red-sea-shipping-crisis|Red Sea]].[5] The piece pointed to the immediate consequences of the US Navy coming under attack while failing to respond or decisively hit back, and was cited by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] as evidence that it might already be too late to reestablish American deterrence in the waterway.[5]

## Coverage of the Israeli court ruling

The Wall Street Journal reported the January 1, 2024 decision by [[israeli-high-court-of-justice|Israel's highest court]] striking down the central law of the [[israeli-judicial-overhaul|judicial overhaul]] enacted under [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], noting the eight-to-seven split and the likelihood that the ruling would revive the previous year's political strife.[6] The report was excerpted in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[6]

## Instagram pedophile-network investigation (2023)

In June 2023, the Journal published a comprehensive investigation conducted jointly with the [[stanford-internet-observatory|Stanford Internet Observatory]] reporting that [[meta-platforms|Meta]]-owned [[instagram|Instagram]] had been home to an organized and massive network of pedophiles whose activity was amplified by the platform's own recommendation systems.[7] The reporting described a coded vocabulary — a map emoji for "minor-attracted person" and "cheese pizza" sharing initials with "child pornography," attributed to researcher Levine of the University of Massachusetts — and documented searchable hashtags such as #pedowhore and #preteensex that routed users to accounts advertising child-sex material.[7] The findings were widely aggregated, including by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] and [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]].[7]

## Hur audio scoop (May 2025)

The Journal obtained and reported on the previously withheld audio recordings of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s October 2023 interviews with special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]], material the Biden administration had declined to release after publishing transcripts in March 2024.[8] Its account described Biden speaking at times with a frail voice and struggling for words and dates across roughly five hours of interviews over two days.[8]
