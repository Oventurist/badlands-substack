# Chauncey McLean

Chauncey McLean is a Democratic political operative and the president of [[future-forward|Future Forward]], the principal outside spending group backing [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s 2024 presidential campaign.[1] A figure who "rarely talks publicly," McLean made news in August 2024 by publicly contradicting the optimistic tone of media polling on the Harris campaign.[1]

## August 2024 polling remarks

Speaking on Monday, August 19, 2024 at an event hosted by the University of Chicago Institute of Politics, McLean said that Future Forward's internal opinion research was less favorable to Harris than public surveys implied: "Our numbers are much less rosy than what you're seeing in the public."[1] He warned that Democrats faced much closer races in key battleground states than headline numbers suggested. At the time, Harris entered the [[2024-democratic-national-convention|Democratic National Convention]] in Chicago leading [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the FiveThirtyEight national polling average 46.6% to 43.8% and had pulled ahead in several battleground polls after replacing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] atop the ticket.[1]

McLean described a large polling and message-testing operation: Future Forward had created and tested roughly 500 digital and television advertisements for Biden and some 200 for Harris, and had spoken with about 375,000 Americans in the weeks after Harris became the presumptive nominee on July 22, 2024.[1] He said the group retained at least $250 million to spend on a wave of advertising between Labor Day on September 2 and Election Day on November 5.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

The Badlands News Brief, in commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], read McLean's candor as a hedge by big-money interests rather than as routine expectation-setting. The argument was that Harris was viable only with voters who had not heard her speak, that her convention appearances were awkward and scripted, and that donors and super PAC leaders would prioritize long-term donor relationships over an unviable candidate — shifting resources toward down-ballot races ahead of November 5.[1]
