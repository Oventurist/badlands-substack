# General Education Board

The General Education Board was a philanthropic body founded in 1902 with Rockefeller funding, described in the Badlands Media feature "Hijacked Consciousness - The Hijacking" as the organization that "laid the groundwork" for the modern American education system.[1] The essay, the second installment of the [[hijacked-consciousness-series|Hijacked Consciousness]] series by [[observing-consciousness|Observing Consciousness]], treats the Board as the institutional hinge between industrial philanthropy and mass schooling.[1]

## Stated versus alleged purpose

The article quotes the Board's stated object as "the promotion of education within the United States of America, without distinction of race, sex, or creed."[1] Against that public charter, the author sets what he calls its real goal: to shape minds to suit industry, train obedient workers, remove the sacred from learning, and "replace purpose with productivity."[1] In the essay's framing this was not generosity but "social engineering through curriculum design," accompanied by credential laundering and narrative control.[1]

## The Gates quotation

Central to the article's case is a quotation attributed to [[frederick-t-gates|Frederick T. Gates]], adviser to Rockefeller and described as the architect of the General Education Board: "In our dreams, people yield themselves with perfect docility to our molding hands... We shall organize children and teach them to do in a perfect way the things their fathers and mothers are doing in an imperfect way."[1] The author reads this passage not as education but as "software installation."[1]

## Legacy claimed in the article

Within decades of the Board's founding, the piece argues, public schools became pipelines into institutions mirroring the [[harvard-university|Harvard]] and broader Ivy League model — top-down instruction, memorization, competition and standardization — with the deepest standardization being of perception itself, training children to see the world through a manufactured lens.[1] The Board is grouped in the essay with the Rockefeller, Carnegie and Ford philanthropies that "flooded the education sector with cash" from the late 19th century into the early 20th.[1]
