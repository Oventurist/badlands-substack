# Radhanite Traders

The Radhanites were Jewish merchants originating from the city of Radhan in Iraq, described by Matthew Ehret as the primary group operating the renewed [[silk-road|Silk Road]] routes in their early phase.[1]

According to the Persian scholar al-Masudi (896–956), these Jewish traders spoke Arabic, Greek, Persian, Slavic, Spanish and Frankish.[1] The 9th-century geographer Ibn Khurdabhe recorded four Radhanite trade routes linking Europe to China, of which the most active corridor through the Middle East to Europe was the Steppe Silk Road, much of it under the jurisdiction of [[khazaria|Khazaria]].[1] Because Venetian-steered wars with Islam had made Mediterranean trade impossible and travel through rival confessional territories unsafe, the Khazarian routes and the mediating role of Jewish merchants were indispensable to east-west commerce.[1]

The Radhanite network did not survive the shift in European power. The article states that all trade routes established by Radhanite Jews were the first things destroyed in Europe by the Crusaders in 1095, organized by the [[knights-templar|Templar]] cultists, who then took over that infrastructure for their own war.[1] By the late 10th century Jews had been cut off from Khazaria as Genoa and the [[venetian-empire|Venetian Empire]] seized the east-west routes outright.[1]
