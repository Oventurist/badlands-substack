# George Marshall

George Marshall was the United States Secretary of State for whom the [[marshall-plan|Marshall Plan]] is named.[1]

## Proposal for European recovery

In 1947 Marshall proposed that the United States provide economic assistance to restore the economic infrastructure of postwar Europe.[1] He made the proposal in a speech at Harvard University on June 5, 1947, asking that European nations themselves create a plan for their economic reconstruction which the United States would fund.[1] The context was a continent in ruins after the Second World War — shattered cities, devastated economies, famine — compounded by the Soviet Union's control of Eastern Europe and the vulnerability of Western European states to Soviet expansionism.[1] President [[harry-truman|Harry Truman]] forwarded Marshall's ideas to [[congress|Congress]] in December 1947, and the resulting act was signed on April 3, 1948.[1]

## Critical reading

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argues that the humanitarian framing attached to Marshall's name obscures the plan's actual distribution, in which three countries took 55% of the funds while the countries that suffered the heaviest wartime losses received little or nothing.[1] In his account the program named for Marshall was the beginning of a new globalist order in which multinational corporations displaced elected governments as the real rulers.[1]
