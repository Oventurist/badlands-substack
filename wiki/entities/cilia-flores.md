# Cilia Flores

Cilia Flores is the wife of former Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]]. On January 3, 2026, U.S. forces carried out a military operation in Venezuela that led to the capture of both Maduro and Flores and their subsequent transfer to the United States to face charges.[1]

## The January 2026 operation

Following the couple's capture, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said American forces had used a classified device he called the [[discombobulator|"Discombobulator"]] to disable Venezuelan defense equipment during the raid.[1] In a leaked recording of a post-operation meeting, acting president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] said that remaining members of Maduro's cabinet were given "15 minutes to comply or they would kill us," and further claimed that the surviving leadership was initially told Maduro and his wife had been killed rather than captured.[1] Badlands commentary treated that framing as likely embellished, noting Rodríguez's own acknowledgement that Maduro had signed a Continuity of Government order in December 2025 in anticipation of his departure.[1]

## Indictment and the cooperating-witness thesis

Flores was captured alongside her husband during [[operation-absolute-resolve|Operation Absolute Resolve]] and transported into the custody of the US District Court for the Southern District of New York, where she was indicted as a narco-terrorist under the superseding indictment covering the couple.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated her indictment as the decisive tell that the operation was not a genuine decapitation strike: he observed that the wives of heads of state — citing Putin, Xi, Assad, the Ayatollah, Netanyahu, Stalin, Hitler and Mao — are never held accountable for the acts of the sovereign executive, and concluded that the charging of Flores indicated a cooperating witness and informant being brought into protective custody.[2]

## Arraignment in New York (January 2026)

Flores pleaded not guilty in a New York federal court to charges related to those filed against her husband, [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], who himself pleaded not guilty to four counts of narco-terrorism, cocaine importation conspiracy, and possession and conspiracy to possess machine guns and destructive devices.[3] The couple faces decades to life in prison if convicted.[3]

When 283 Venezuelan lawmakers elected the previous May were sworn in alongside interim president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]], Flores was the only lawmaker not in attendance, as she remained in US custody.[3]
