# Khalid al-Mansour

Khalid Abdullah Tariq al-Mansour, also known as Donald Warden, is an African-American Muslim attorney, author, and activist described in the Badlands Media article "[[bad-actors|Bad Actors]]" as a key adviser to Saudi billionaire [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] and as the intermediary who helped secure [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s admission to Harvard Law School.

## Rise and Saudi connections

According to Newsmax reporting quoted in the article, al-Mansour's rise to fame and fortune began with an introduction to the Saudi king in Riyadh in 1977, arranged by a "Saudi friend" who was then a student in Newport, California. Al-Mansour met and befriended Prince Alwaleed while the prince was studying at Menlo College in California in the late 1970s; al-Mansour's law partner was representing Alwaleed in a California court case at the time. Alwaleed took a degree in business administration from Menlo in 1979 before returning to Saudi Arabia. Newsmax separately characterised al-Mansour as an activist African-American Muslim who had mentored the founding members of the Black Panther organisation.

## The Obama recommendation controversy

In September 2008, former Manhattan Borough President [[percy-sutton|Percy Sutton]] told NY1's Dominic Carter that al-Mansour had asked him to write a letter of recommendation to Harvard Law School on behalf of a young Obama, describing al-Mansour as "the principle adviser to one of the world's richest men" and as raising money for Obama. Sutton, founder of Inner City Broadcasting, said he wrote to his friends at Harvard "saying to them I thought there was a genius that was going to be available."

Obama's campaign flatly denied the account. Spokesman Ben LaBolt told Politico that "Obama did not know and does not know Khalid al-Mansour," that Obama had no relationship with Sutton, and that to the campaign's knowledge no such letter was written. Al-Mansour himself told reporters "I've never met him," saying he first heard of Obama when a relative sent him a copy of the 2004 convention speech, and that while he was aware of Sutton's claims, "I've never confirmed it."

## Treatment in the corpus

The Badlands Media article treats the denials as false, arguing that puppet masters and their agents are supposed to remain hidden rather than be exposed funding the education of a future president. It notes that al-Mansour is a prolific author of books the article characterises as anti-American and promoting racial division, and speculates that he may have ghostwritten Obama's book and assisted him in running the Harvard Law Review. The article presents the al-Mansour link as the connection whose exposure could have derailed the [[sixteen-year-plan|sixteen year plan]] and Obama's presidential run, and it argues that the person ultimately directing al-Mansour's patronage was Prince Alwaleed.
