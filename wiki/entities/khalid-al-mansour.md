# Khalid Abdullah Tariq al-Mansour

Khalid Abdullah Tariq al-Mansour, also known by the name Donald Warden, is an American attorney, author and activist who, in the account presented by Joe Lange in the Badlands Media article "Bad Actors," served as the intermediary between Saudi financier [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]] and the young [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[1] Lange treats al-Mansour as the visible operational link in a chain of patronage that, in his telling, delivered Obama into Harvard Law School and ultimately into the presidency on behalf of a hidden sponsor.[1]

## Association with Prince Alwaleed

According to a Newsmax report quoted at length in the article, al-Mansour's "rise to fame and fortune" began with an introduction to the Saudi king in Riyadh in 1977, arranged by a "Saudi friend" who had been a student in Newport, California.[1] Al-Mansour met and befriended Prince Alwaleed while the prince was studying at Menlo College in California in the late 1970s; al-Mansour's law partner was at the time representing the prince in a California court case.[1] Alwaleed took a degree in business administration from Menlo in 1979 and returned to Saudi Arabia determined, in al-Mansour's recollection, to become extremely successful.[1] Lange treats the overlap between the unnamed "Saudi friend" of 1977 and Alwaleed's presence in the Newport area as more than coincidence.[1]

A second Newsmax article cited in the piece described al-Mansour as a "key adviser to a Saudi billionaire" who had mentored the founding members of the Black Panthers, and reported that Obama was "closely associated" with him as early as age 25.[1]

## The Harvard recommendation and the Sutton account

Former Manhattan Borough President [[percy-sutton|Percy Sutton]] told NY1's Dominic Carter that al-Mansour had contacted him to request a letter of recommendation supporting Obama's application to Harvard Law School, and had described himself as raising money for Obama.[1] Sutton said he wrote to friends at Harvard describing Obama as "a genius that was going to be available."[1] Sutton did not explain why al-Mansour was helping Obama, how he had discovered him, or on whose behalf the money was being raised — questions Lange identifies as the central unanswered ones.[1]

Obama's 2008 campaign flatly denied the account. Spokesman Ben LaBolt told Politico that "Obama did not know and does not know Khalid al-Mansour," that Obama had no relationship with Sutton, and that "to our knowledge, no such letter was written," noting Obama was in Chicago rather than New York when he applied.[1] Al-Mansour himself said he admired Obama but first heard of him from a relative's copy of Obama's 2004 convention speech, adding "I've never met him" and, of the Sutton claim, "But I've never confirmed it."[1]

Lange characterises these denials as "well-scripted LIES," argues that no records exist of the student loans Politico invoked as an alternative explanation for Obama's Harvard financing, and treats the Politico piece itself as an effort to bury the connection because exposure "may have ended Obama's presidential run."[1] He also questions whether Sutton's reported retraction was genuine, describing a purported family "spokesperson" who told the media the story had been withdrawn as a fabrication, and notes that Sutton was portrayed as old and senile to discredit him.[1]

## Ideological profile and Lange's inferences

Lange notes that al-Mansour is a prolific author whose books he characterises as anti-American works promoting racism and division, and speculates that al-Mansour may have ghostwritten Obama's book and assisted him in running the Harvard Law Review — institutions and credentials that, in Lange's account, an unknown community organiser could not have obtained on merit.[1] He describes al-Mansour as a known Marxist who mentored the Black Panther political organisation and whose family background was, in Lange's telling, deeply tied to the [[cia|CIA]] that Alwaleed controlled.[1]

The Q drops cited in the article — including drop 1948 of August 28, 2018 — pose the questions "Who paid _Hussein's Harvard Law Degree? Who financed _Hussein's political life? Who is Prince Al-Waleed bin Talal? Why are Hussein's school records under seal?", which Lange reads as identifying Alwaleed as the ultimate financier and al-Mansour as his agent.[1] "Puppet masters are supposed to stay hidden," he writes, "not be exposed funding the education of the future president of the United States."[1]
