# George Santos

George Santos is a Republican politician who represented New York in the United States House of Representatives during the 118th Congress.[1]

## Role in the Cannon fire alarm episode

Santos featured in the public reaction to the [[2023-cannon-building-fire-alarm-incident|2023 Cannon Building fire alarm incident]], in which Representative [[jamaal-bowman|Jamaal Bowman]] (D-NY) pulled a fire alarm while Republicans worked to pass a stopgap spending bill.[1] As the episode went viral, Santos filmed a mock public service announcement about how fire alarms work, a piece of political trolling that circulated widely alongside memes and Bowman's resurfaced January 2021 posts about obstructing official proceedings.[1]

## 2023 federal indictment and arraignment

Santos was charged in May 2023 with 13 counts including wire fraud, money laundering, theft of public funds, and lying on federal disclosure forms, and pleaded not guilty after surrendering at federal court on Long Island on Wednesday, May 10, 2023.[4] The New York Times described him as a scandal-plagued congressman who had run on "a life story littered with lies."[4] A second criminal matter loomed immediately: Brazilian law enforcement scheduled a hearing for the following day on an allegation of check fraud stemming from a 2008 incident involving a stolen checkbook, a case that had been suspended for the better part of a decade because police could not locate him and was revived in early 2023.[4]

U.S. Attorney [[breon-peace|Breon Peace]] said the indictment "seeks to hold Santos accountable for various alleged fraudulent schemes and brazen misrepresentations."[4]

### Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Santos had been under scrutiny for lying and misrepresenting himself since his election, and that if he was guilty of defrauding taxpayers by collecting unemployment while employed he should be held to account — adding that Badlands would "never advocate for harshness or leniency based on political party affiliation."[4] She contrasted the prosecution with the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] press conference held the same day on [[biden-family-foreign-payments|Biden family foreign payments]], asking whether the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] could apply comparable rigor to presidential-level corruption.[4]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted the timing — a notable Republican charged with money laundering on the same day Oversight leveled sourced money-laundering accusations at the Biden family — and argued the movement should welcome rather than resist media and centrist calls for justice, so that those voices would be "trapped in that position."[4]

## Conviction and prison sentence

Santos was scheduled to begin serving a federal prison sentence of over seven years on Friday, July 25, 2025, following his guilty plea to a number of federal charges including wire fraud, identity theft and money laundering.[2] The sentence runs 87 months, equivalent to seven years and three months.[2] Reporting noted that the New York Republican "did not exit the spotlight quietly."[2]

## Commutation of sentence (October 2025)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] granted clemency to Santos in October 2025, commuting the remainder of a seven-year sentence for wire fraud and identity theft that Santos had served less than three months of when Trump announced the decision in a social media post.[3] Santos told CNN's Dana Bash that other inmates saw the announcement and called him over, that he was not in the room at the time, and that when he called his family they confirmed it had happened — describing the commutation as a surprise.[3]

House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] defended the decision on ABC's "This Week," telling Jonathan Karl that "the president has the right in the Constitution for pardon and commutation, of course," adding that "we believe in redemption," calling it a personal belief and expressing hope that Santos would make the most of his second chance.[3] Johnson also argued that Trump did not act because of Santos's political affiliation, and that the president had been "transparent."[3]

## Sentencing and pardon request (2025)

Santos was sentenced to 87 months — more than seven years — in prison in April 2025 for what Politico described as his con artist ways, and on Friday, May 9, 2025, he asked the president for a pardon, with a July report date to prison.[5] The Justice Department press release accompanying the sentencing stated that Santos "Filed Fraudulent FEC Reports, Embezzled Funds from Campaign Donors, Stole Identities, Charged Credit Cards Without Authorization, Obtained Unemployment Benefits Through Fraud, and Lied in Reports to the U.S. House of Representatives," and that he was ordered to pay $373,749.97 in restitution to his victims plus $205,002.97 in forfeiture, having pleaded guilty in August 2024.[5]

Barring a last-minute presidential pardon, Santos was about to join the relatively exclusive group of Americans who have spent time in both Congress and federal prison.[5] For a sense of what awaited him, Politico Magazine spoke with former Representative Bob Ney, an Ohio Republican and former chairman of the House Administration Committee who served 17 months in federal prison for his role in the Jack Abramoff lobbying scandal.[5] Ney observed that both Congress and prison involve taxpayer subsidies and individuals of questionable character, though the lifestyles differ sharply — members of Congress have private dining rooms and private elevators, while prisoners have no privacy at all.[5] He noted that Santos, expelled by his colleagues, already had experience navigating a cold-blooded and adversarial environment, telling the magazine: "If you can survive the Hill, you can survive in prison."[5]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] compared the charges to "the same portfolio of crime we talk about with smurfing," asked whether Santos's 87 months was setting a precedent, and concluded that "no one is above the law."[5]
