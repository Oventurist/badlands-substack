# Michael Bloomberg

Michael Bloomberg is an American billionaire businessman, the founder of the financial data and media company Bloomberg, and a former mayor of New York City.[1] In April 2025 he re-entered the American fiscal policy debate with a warning, published in the media outlet he founded, that the United States was heading toward a "fiscal breakdown."[1]

## April 2025 warning of fiscal collapse

Writing in the outlet he established, Bloomberg argued that Congress should take urgent measures to restore fiscal control rather than contemplate additional borrowing.[1] He based the warning on the latest projections from the [[congressional-budget-office|Congressional Budget Office]], which indicated that the US budget deficit and federal debt were both on track to grow, albeit at a slower pace than had been projected a year earlier.[1] Bloomberg described the forecasts as sending an "unambiguous message" that "there'll be a reckoning, and it will be grim" unless Congress urgently changed course, noting that public borrowing was expected "to remain at this elevated level or higher for decades."[1]

He quantified the imbalance directly: "The federal government is currently spending roughly $7 trillion and collecting only $5 trillion in taxes annually. The resulting deficit is a little over 6% of gross domestic product, a disturbingly high number for an economy around full employment."[1] US deficits reached record levels during the 2020s decade.[1]

## Position on tariffs and Badlands criticism

Bloomberg also argued that the revenue effect of the new tariffs would "likely be negative, because tariffs depress commercial activity and job creation."[1] Badlands commentators treated the intervention as self-interested rather than analytical, characterizing Bloomberg as a billionaire responsible for the decline of New York City during his mayoralty who objected to [[liberation-day-tariffs|the tariff program]] because it would prevent him and allied oligarchs from flooding American markets with cheap imported goods.[1]

The Badlands rebuttal held that tariffs depress commercial activity only within a business model premised on low-wage overseas production of cheap goods sold into first-world markets at rock-bottom prices, a dynamic that erodes the manufacturing base of the importing country.[1] In that framing, Bloomberg was correct that the prospects of that particular model looked grim, but the model would be replaced by a more traditional economy of self-reliant communities responding organically to market demand.[1]
