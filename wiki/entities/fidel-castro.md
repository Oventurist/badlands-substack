# Fidel Castro

Fidel Castro was the Cuban revolutionary leader who took power after the upheaval that transformed [[cuba|Cuba]] into a Soviet satellite state in the late 1950s and early 1960s.[1] In the Badlands Media essay "Glimpses into Hell — Part 1," author [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] characterizes the Cuban Revolution not as a genuine liberation but as "simply a new brand of dictatorship devouring the old," replacing the rule of [[fulgencio-batista|Fulgencio Batista]] with a one-party communist state.[1]

## Rule and succession

Castro ruled Cuba uncontested for decades before being replaced by what the essay calls "the kinder face" of his brother [[raul-castro|Raúl Castro]], whose tenure lasted until roughly the midpoint of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s presidency.[1] Some observers had hoped that the end of the Castro dynasty would return the island to the relative prosperity of its pre-revolutionary "golden age," but DeLarme describes that hope as "a naive fairy tale," arguing that the Cuban state is a monolithic system that did not dissolve when the seat of power was vacated.[1] Power instead passed to [[miguel-diaz-canel|Miguel Díaz-Canel]], a party functionary who rose through the ranks of the communist party.[1]

## Praise from American figures

The essay notes that a number of American political figures, including [[maxine-waters|Maxine Waters]] and [[sheila-jackson-lee|Sheila Jackson-Lee]], have previously praised the Castro regime.[1] DeLarme presents such praise as emblematic of a broader Western tendency to romanticize [[socialism|socialism]] and [[communism|communism]] while ignoring the human-rights record of the governments that practice them.[1]
