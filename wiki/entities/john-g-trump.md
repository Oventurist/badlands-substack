# John G. Trump

John G. Trump was an American electrical engineer and physicist, and an uncle of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] He is best known in alternative-media circles for having been tasked with reviewing the papers of inventor [[nikola-tesla|Nikola Tesla]] following Tesla's death in New York in 1943.[1]

## Invocation at the 2026 quantum executive order signing

At the June 2026 quantum executive order signing, President Trump referenced his uncle John G. Trump, a detail highlighted by Badlands contributor [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]].[1] Sather noted that some of the trunks of Tesla material reportedly examined after Tesla's death remain unaccounted for, and speculated that the administration might roll out previously hidden technologies "under the guise of 'Quantum.'"[1]

## Claims in "Has Trump's Whole Life Been a Sting Operation?" (2024)

Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] made John G. Trump the starting point of a June 15, 2024 feature arguing that Donald Trump's public life may have been a decades-long covert operation.[2] Carlson summarised the conventional record: Trump (August 21, 1907 – February 21, 1985) was an American electrical engineer, inventor and physicist, a professor at the [[massachusetts-institute-of-technology|Massachusetts Institute of Technology]] from 1936 to 1973, a recipient of the National Medal of Science and a member of the National Academy of Engineering, noted for developing rotational radiation therapy and, with Robert J. Van de Graaff, one of the first million-volt X-ray generators.[2]

During the Second World War he moved from hospital X-ray machines to radar research, joining the newly formed National Defense Research Committee in 1940 as technical aide to MIT president Karl Compton, and becoming secretary of the microwave committee in 1942.[2] The work was housed in the MIT Radiation Laboratory, the "Rad Lab," which absorbed British radar researchers as wartime shortages deepened in Britain.[2] From February 1944 to the end of the war in Europe he directed the British Branch of the Radiation Laboratory, a department of Britain's Telecommunications Research Establishment at Malvern, Worcestershire.[2] He also served in the Advisory Specialist Group on Radar, advising USAAF General Carl Spaatz on navigational and precision-bombing radar and on countermeasures to German night-fighter and flak radars, and conducted debriefing interviews with Germany's principal radar technicians at war's end.[2]

In 1946 Trump, Van de Graaff and Denis M. Robinson founded the High Voltage Engineering Corporation to produce Van de Graaff generators; he directed the MIT High Voltage Research Laboratory from 1946 to 1980, including work using electron beams from a high-voltage accelerator to disinfect municipal wastewater sludge, tested at a Boston treatment plant.[2] His lab director James Melcher was quoted saying that Trump could "make megavolt beams of ions and electrons – death rays," but applied them to cancer research and sludge sterilisation and "didn't touch the weapons stuff."[2]

## Tesla papers and the FBI (Carlson's reading)

Carlson emphasised that in early 1943, two days after [[nikola-tesla|Nikola Tesla]]'s death, the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] directed the Office of Alien Property Custodian to seize Tesla's belongings, and that John G. Trump was called in to analyse the artifacts, concluding after a three-day review that nothing in them would constitute a hazard in unfriendly hands.[2] Carlson argued that the conclusion downplayed the material's significance, noting that Van de Graaff generators resemble the Tesla coil and that the timeline — Tesla papers in 1943, direction of the British radar laboratory in 1944, war's end in 1945 — is suggestive.[2] He also drew a parallel between Trump's disinfection and cancer-therapy work and remarks Donald Trump made at a 2020 Covid press conference for which he was widely mocked.[2]

Because the seizure review was FBI-directed, Carlson stressed that John G. Trump was working with the Bureau while [[j-edgar-hoover|J. Edgar Hoover]] was its director, though he allowed that Trump may have been working with others inside the Bureau rather than with Hoover himself.[2] He speculated that Donald Trump learned from his uncle about advanced medical procedures, cheap or free energy and powerful weapons, and that he avoids discussing his uncle's achievements to keep the [[deep-state|Deep State]] from knowing what those materials contained.[2]
