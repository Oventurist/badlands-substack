# Central Bank of Iran

The Central Bank of Iran (CBI) is Iran's central monetary authority and the institution at the center of the country's banking system.[1]

## August 2024 cyberattack

On Wednesday, August 14, 2024, the CBI and several other Iranian banks were reportedly hit by a significant cyberattack that caused major disruptions to Iran's banking system, according to [[iran-international|Iran International]].[1] Hours after the report emerged, it had not been corroborated by additional sources and had drawn no comment from Iranian authorities.[1]

Iran International, a London-based anti-regime outlet, described the incident as potentially one of the largest cyberattacks ever to target Iranian state infrastructure, and reported that hackers may have accessed sensitive bank and credit card details belonging to millions of Iranians.[1] The outlet's Israel correspondent, Babak Itzhaki, claimed the Iranian government had been attempting to conceal the breach and anticipated an official denial from the regime.[1]

The attack reportedly paralyzed all banking computer systems in Iran and resulted in stolen data, and was interpreted as a possible message from Western countries demonstrating the vulnerabilities of Iran's digital systems.[1] The item was carried as a bonus entry in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of August 15, 2024.[1]

## De-dollarization agreement with Russia (2024)

Speaking at the 11th Conference on Modern Banking and Payment Systems in Tehran on 25 November 2024, CBI Governor [[mohammad-reza-farzin|Mohammad-Reza Farzin]] said [[iran|Iran]] and [[russia|Russia]] had abandoned the US dollar in bilateral trade and shifted to their national currencies as part of a plan to counter "unjust sanctions."[2] "We have entered into a currency agreement with Russia and abandoned the dollar. Now we only trade in rubles and rials," Farzin was quoted as saying by Fars News, adding that the two countries' financial authorities had agreed on the exchange rate to be used for foreign trade transactions.[2] Both states, subject to US sanctions, had first announced plans to trade in national currencies in July 2022.[2]

## Banking integration with Russia (2023)

In January 2023, Deputy Governor Mohsen Karimi announced that [[iran|Iran]] and [[russia|Russia]] had integrated their interbank communication and transfer systems, connecting 52 Iranian banks and 106 Russian banks through the Russian Financial Message Transfer System.[3] Karimi said the agreement would facilitate economic relations between the two countries; the [[iran-russia-banking-integration|arrangement]] was understood as an effort to bypass strict sanctions on both states' financial infrastructure and to reduce dependence on [[swift|SWIFT]].[3]
