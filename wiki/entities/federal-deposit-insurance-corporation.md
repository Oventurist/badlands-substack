# Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation

The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) is the U.S. federal agency that insures deposits held at private banks. In [[a-seismic-shift|A Seismic Shift]], [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] casts federal deposit insurance not as a consumer protection but as what he calls "a cabal trick" — the device by which private bankers eliminated their public competition.

## Role in ending postal savings

Quoting the [[roosevelt-institute|Roosevelt Institute]], the article notes that during the post-war economic boom, as interest rates at private banks rose and after the FDIC offered depositors at private banks a guarantee, use of the [[postal-savings-system|Postal Savings System]] declined and the program was discontinued in 1967. Lange's reading is that private banks had previously been visibly risky, so the government's assumption of that risk removed the safety advantage that postal savings had enjoyed. Private banks could then attract deposits with higher interest generated by riskier investments while taxpayers underwrote the downside, placing the public option at a permanent competitive disadvantage.

## The author's critique

Lange disputes the industry claim that the FDIC is funded by banks themselves, arguing that it ignores "decades of back door bailouts for the banks using money from the Federal Reserve" — money he characterises as taxpayer funds handed to Wall Street banks so they can profit by pumping up the stock market. He similarly disputes the [[american-bankers-association|American Bankers Association]]'s assertion that FDIC-insured depositors have never lost a penny of an insured deposit, responding that taxpayers have paid for all bank bailouts for generations. He argues that if the banking system were genuinely healthy, private banks would not need deposit insurance at all.

## Statistics cited

The article also draws on FDIC household survey data reported by Cheddar News, which found that 6.5 percent of U.S. households — 8.4 million — were **unbanked**, having no bank account, and that 18.7 percent — 24.2 million — were **underbanked**, holding a savings or checking account but still going outside the banking system for some financial services. These figures are used to argue for postal banking through the [[united-states-postal-service|United States Postal Service]].

## Relation to FedAccounts

Lange argues that [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]] would render deposit insurance unnecessary, because accounts held directly at the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] — or, in his preferred formulation, at the [[treasury-department|Treasury]] — cannot be defaulted on and would hold "pure sovereign money." He contends that the banking industry's real fear is the loss of FDIC protection, since without it most Americans would choose the public option, and notes that a public option would remove the $250,000 ceiling on protected savings.
