# Syria

Syria is the Middle Eastern state whose government under [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] collapsed in December 2024 following a rapid rebel offensive, an event Badlands Media analyzes as a likely regime-change operation rather than a spontaneous popular uprising.[1]

## The 2024 collapse

Rebel forces led by [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] launched their offensive on November 27, 2024, taking Aleppo, Syria's second-largest city, before advancing south.[1] By December 8, 2024, they had captured Hama, Homs, and Damascus, prompting Assad's reported resignation and flight to Moscow.[1] The advance was facilitated by the withdrawal of Syrian government forces, possibly reflecting a lack of support from allies such as [[russia|Russia]] and [[iran|Iran]], both preoccupied with other conflicts.[1]

## Internal divisions

The article disputes Western reporting suggesting Syrians were unanimously ecstatic, observing that while many welcomed the liberation of prisoners, other constituencies held Assad in high regard.[1] These include Alawites, from whom Assad hails, and minorities such as Christians historically wary of Sunni Islamist opposition groups who supported Assad out of fear of persecution under different rule.[1] Many such groups were subsequently withdrawing support or adapting to the new political landscape.[1]

## Regional and international dimensions

The transition raised questions about Kurds, Turkish-backed groups, and other factions; [[turkey|Turkey]] denied supporting HTS while aiding some rebel factions, and Russia called for UN Security Council consultations, particularly regarding the buffer zone near the Golan Heights.[1] The conflict displaced millions and remained a humanitarian crisis, with the UN reporting significant need for assistance.[1] The shift also carried implications for Iran's influence through Hezbollah and Syrian territory, and for [[israel|Israel]], which had been targeting Iranian assets in Syria.[1]

## Long-running U.S. involvement

Syria appears on the list of countries targeted in the [[project-for-the-new-american-century|Project for the New American Century]]'s vision and in the post-9/11 [[pentagon|Pentagon]] memo described by [[wesley-clark|Wesley Clark]].[1] Both the [[cia|CIA]], through [[timber-sycamore|Timber Sycamore]], and the Pentagon, through the [[syrian-democratic-forces|Syrian Democratic Forces]], funded and bolstered Syrian armed groups.[1] The article rejects the claim that U.S. involvement was motivated by Assad's dictatorship, noting Washington's comfort with dictators in Egypt and Saudi Arabia.[1]

## Iraq-Syria crude oil pipeline revival (2026)

US officials said in July 2026 that the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] is supporting efforts to revive a long-idled crude oil pipeline connecting [[iraq|Iraq]] to [[syria|Syria]]'s Mediterranean coast, giving Iraq an alternative export route and reducing its dependence on the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and the Persian Gulf.[2] Officials described the proposal as part of a broader strategy to strengthen regional energy security and limit [[iran|Iran]]'s leverage over global oil shipments, though significant infrastructure repairs, security challenges, and political agreements between Iraq and Syria would still be required.[2] Iran-backed Iraqi militias opposed the plan, with one prominent group threatening to disrupt any future pipeline project.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted the announcement came less than a week after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] revealed his intention to remove Syria from the state sponsors of terrorism list, opening the country to international investors, and predicted Saudi involvement.[2] See [[iraq-syria-crude-oil-pipeline|the pipeline]] for fuller coverage.[2]

## Removal from the State Sponsors of Terrorism list (2026)

In July 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would remove Syria from the [[state-department|State Department]]'s [[state-sponsors-of-terrorism-list|State Sponsors of Terrorism list]], a designation Syria had held since the list was created in 1979.[3] Trump made the comments while seated next to Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]] at the [[2026-ankara-nato-summit|NATO summit in Turkey]], saying al-Sharaa had "done a great job."[3] The administration notified [[congress|Congress]], starting a mandatory 45-day review period before the removal could become final.[3]

Delisting would ease major restrictions on US aid, defense exports and financial transactions, and followed Trump's earlier move to unwind parts of the US sanctions program against Syria after the fall of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]]'s government.[3] Reporting cited by Badlands suggested lifting the designation could facilitate investment in Syrian oil, banking, technology and real estate, potentially producing an economic sea change and greater stability.[3] Commentators noted that Syria nonetheless achieved one of the strongest economies in the Middle East by 2010 under Assad despite the designation.[3]

## Saudi investment and reintegration (February 2026)

On February 7, 2026, Syria and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] signed a series of multibillion-dollar investment agreements aimed at revitalizing the Syrian economy after years of civil war.[4] The package included nearly $1 billion in telecommunications infrastructure under the [[silklink-project|SilkLink project]], a new Saudi-Syrian low-cost airline, and a $2 billion commitment to redevelop two airports in Aleppo through a Saudi-backed investment fund, with additional accords covering energy, real estate and water desalination — deepening ties with Syria's interim leadership after Western sanctions were lifted.[4]

In northeastern Syria, Syrian government security forces entered the Kurdish-majority city of [[qamishli|Qamishli]] under a U.S.-brokered deal intended to reintegrate Kurdish-controlled areas into central government authority, while Kurdish police continued day-to-day security.[4] At the same time the U.S. military launched renewed airstrikes against [[islamic-state|Islamic State]] targets in Syria, deploying aircraft, drones and helicopters against ISIS remnants.[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the Saudis, working with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the Turks, were using their wealth to pull Syria out of the pit into which the [[deep-state|Deep State]] had cast it, recalling their earlier offer to pay off Syria's debts.[4] He credited [[tom-barrack|Tom Barrack]] with getting the [[kurds|Kurds]] to bury the hatchet with Damascus and argued that stabilizing Syria is a prerequisite to stabilizing the region and attracting foreign investment, calling Syria "a Rubix cube that President Trump, MBS, and Erdogan are solving together."[4]

## Israeli Strikes and Turkish Reconstruction Plans (June 2026)

In June 2026 Israeli strikes on military assets in Syria became a principal grievance in Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]]'s public confrontation with Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], with Ankara portraying the operations as destabilizing and Israel defending them as necessary against Iranian-backed groups.[5] Badlands commentary observed that with the effective Turkish sphere of influence now extending into Syria, Ankara's chief concern was a possible [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] advance toward Damascus, which would threaten the plans Erdoğan and the Saudis have to rebuild the country.[5]

## Palmyra ambush (December 2025)

In December 2025 a gunman suspected of belonging to an ISIS affiliate opened fire during a meeting between U.S. and Syrian security officials near the historic city of Palmyra, killing two Iowa National Guard members and a civilian U.S. interpreter and injuring three other Guard members in what became known as the [[palmyra-attack-on-iowa-national-guard-members|Palmyra attack]].[6] Syrian Interior Ministry spokesman Nour al-Din al-Baba called the incident "a major security breach" while emphasizing that Syrian security forces had achieved "many more successes than failures" since the fall of Bashar Assad's government; the assailant, who had recently been reassigned amid suspicions of ISIS affiliation, was killed after clashing with Syrian security officers.[6]

## Israeli strike on the Iranian embassy in Damascus (April 2024)

On Monday, April 1, 2024, suspected Israeli warplanes bombed [[iran|Iran]]'s embassy compound in the Mezzeh district of Damascus, destroying a building inside the diplomatic compound adjacent to the main embassy structure.[7] Syrian Foreign Minister [[faisal-mekdad|Faisal Mekdad]], present at the site with Syria's interior minister, said: "We strongly condemn this atrocious terrorist attack that targeted the Iranian consulate building in Damascus and killed a number of innocents."[7] Iran said seven of its military advisers were killed, including senior [[quds-force|Quds Force]] commander [[mohammad-reza-zahedi|Mohammad Reza Zahedi]].[7] See [[2024-israeli-strike-on-iranian-embassy-in-damascus]].[7]

## Return of Freemasonry (January 2025)

In January 2025, a statement attributed to the Presidency of the Supreme Council of the Grand Syrian Orient Lodges announced that [[freemasonry|Freemasonry]] was returning to Syria after being banned for more than 50 years, declaring that the collapse of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]]'s government had created the opportunity for re-establishment.[8] Assad was ousted in December 2024 after his forces lost control to [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] jihadists and other opposition groups, and the new authorities — mainly Islamist factions — promised governance reforms amid continuing political uncertainty.[8] Syria's Ba'ath Party had branded freemasonry an "illegal secret society" and banned it in 1965 during the presidency of Amin al-Hafiz, associating the fraternal organization with Western influence and Zionism.[8] The lodges framed their return as part of Syria's reconstruction and pledged not to interfere in political affairs.[8]


## Collapse of the Syrian state (December 2024)

In December 2024 the Syrian government collapsed as opposition forces entered [[damascus|Damascus]], ending more than fifty years of Assad family rule and what Badlands described as the last secular and Baathist state in the Middle East.[9] Syrian military and security personnel abandoned the capital without a battle, [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] and US-armed [[free-syrian-army|Free Syrian Army]] fighters took the city, and [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] and his family were reported to have been granted asylum in Moscow.[9] The [[russian-foreign-ministry|Russian Foreign Ministry]] said Assad had agreed to step down after back-channel talks and had ordered a peaceful transfer of power.[9]

## Strategic significance in Badlands analysis

Badlands framed Syria's geography as the underlying reason for its destruction.[9] Two Persian Gulf natural gas pipeline proposals dating to around 2009 — one from Iran terminating in Lebanon, one from Qatar through Turkey into Europe — each required Syrian transit and each was reportedly rejected by Assad, protecting Russian gas exports.[9] Assad's 2005 [[five-seas-vision|Five Seas Vision]] would have made Aleppo the hub of a rail network linking five bodies of water, and had reportedly drawn renewed interest from China and Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] under the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]] and [[saudi-vision-2030|Saudi Vision 2030]].[9] Israeli claims on Syrian territory, including the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]] annexation moves and [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]]'s assertion that Damascus belongs to Israel, formed the third strand.[9] Russia's presence in the country, anchored at [[tartus-naval-base|Tartus]], was cast by Badlands as an effort to halt Western-sponsored extremist advances, and its drawdown as a scripted parallel to the 2021 Kabul withdrawal.[9]

## Missile fire on the Golan Heights (October 2023)

On the evening of Tuesday, 10 October 2023, the Syrian Army fired missiles toward the Israeli-occupied [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]], an attack confirmed by the Israeli Defense Force.[10] Commentators said the strike raised the risk of a broader conflict spiralling outward, given the earlier exchange of fire in northern Lebanon between [[israel|Israel]] and likely [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] positions.[10] [[al-jazeera|Al Jazeera]] senior correspondent Zeina Khodr described the mortar and rocket fire from Syria as a message to Israel from a new front and raised the prospect of a "multi front assault."[10] The exchange occurred within an hour of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] announcing that the United States would begin "surging" defense aid to Israel.[10]
