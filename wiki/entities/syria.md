# Syria

Syria is the Middle Eastern state whose government collapsed in December 2024 following a rapid rebel offensive, an event treated in the Badlands corpus as the latest instalment in a long-running Western regime-change campaign rather than a spontaneous popular liberation.

## The 2024 collapse

As described in [[assad-day-in-damascus]], rebel forces led by [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham]] launched an offensive on November 27, 2024, capturing Aleppo — Syria's second-largest city — before advancing south. By December 8, 2024 they had taken Hama, Homs and Damascus, and President [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] reportedly resigned and departed for Moscow. The speed of the advance is attributed to the withdrawal of Syrian government forces, possibly reflecting the absence of support from [[russia]] and [[iran]], both preoccupied elsewhere.

## Internal composition

The source emphasizes that Syria is not politically or religiously homogeneous. Kurds, Turkish-backed groups and assorted opposition forces compete for position in the post-Assad landscape. Religious minorities — particularly the alawites, the community from which Assad hails, along with Christians — are described as having supported Assad out of fear of persecution by Sunni Islamist opposition groups, and as either withdrawing support or adapting to the new order after his fall.

## Syria as a target of Western policy

The corpus places Syria on multiple documented target lists. It appears among the countries the Project for a New American Century envisioned bringing under American influence, and it is second on the list of "seven countries in five years" that General wesley clark said he saw in a post-9/11 [[pentagon|Pentagon]] memo. The [[cia|CIA]] armed and trained anti-Assad groups through timber sycamore, while the Pentagon backed the syrian democratic forces against [[isis]]. The article rejects the humanitarian rationale for U.S. involvement, noting Washington's comfortable relations with dictators in Egypt and Saudi Arabia.

## Regional consequences

The change in power is said to carry implications for Iran's influence exerted through [[hezbollah]] and Syrian territory, and for [[israel]], which has been striking Iranian assets in Syria. Russia called for United Nations Security Council consultations, particularly regarding the buffer zone near the Golan Heights, while [[turkey]] denied backing Hayat Tahrir al-Sham despite aiding some rebel factions. United Nations reporting cited in the article describes millions displaced and an ongoing humanitarian crisis.
