# North Carolina State Board of Elections

The North Carolina State Board of Elections is the state agency responsible for administering elections in North Carolina, including the printing and distribution of absentee ballots. In 2024 the Democratic-led board became the focus of litigation over whether [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] could have his name removed from the state's presidential ballot.[1]

## 2024 ballot removal dispute

After suspending his presidential campaign and endorsing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Kennedy — the nominee of the We The People party — asked to have his name struck from North Carolina ballots.[1] The board denied the request, citing the timing of ballot printing and the coding of tabulation machines.[1] Kennedy responded by filing a lawsuit; Wake County Superior Court Judge [[rebecca-holt|Rebecca Holt]] denied his effort, prompting an appeal.[1]

On Friday, September 6, 2024, the North Carolina Court of Appeals granted Kennedy's request to halt the mailing of ballots bearing his name and ordered the board to issue new ballots without it, without providing a legal explanation for the decision.[1]

## Operational consequences

Under state law, absentee ballots were required to be sent out that Friday, sixty days before the November 5 election.[1] State attorneys said reprinting and assembling new ballots could take more than two weeks, and election officials were instructed to hold off on mailing absentee ballots in light of the ruling.[1] By that point more than 132,500 absentee ballot requests had been made in North Carolina, and more than 2.9 million ballots — both absentee and in-person — had already been printed.[1] The ruling was reported as still potentially subject to further appeal.[1]
