# Hubert Bancroft

**Hubert Bancroft** was a nineteenth-century American publisher and historian who, between 1883 and 1890, issued a thirty-nine volume encyclopedia of world history.[1] The Badlands Media essay "[[the-werewolf-game|The Werewolf Game]] — On Languages and Cover-ups," written by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], treats Bancroft's project as a case study in how "official history" may have been manufactured and distributed to the public.[1]

## The thirty-nine volumes

The essay observes that Bancroft was already in his fifties when he began publishing the series, and speculates that he could not plausibly have closely proofread every page of so vast a body of work.[1] It notes that even mainstream historiography concedes Bancroft did not personally write the bulk of the volumes attributed to him.[1] From this the author raises what he presents as the central question: who wanted such a large body of "history" introduced to the world through the name of a single man, and by what methods was that material constructed?[1] Whether or not the arrangement was intentional, the author argues, releasing the material under one authorial name conveniently obscures its true origin — both its authorship and its methods of composition.[1]

## Relationship to the World's Fairs

Bancroft's publishing run coincided with the heyday of the [[worlds-fairs|World's Fairs]], which the series describes as events intended to define the world for the masses and to supply them with an entirely new self-concept complete with accompanying world histories.[1] The author states that he does not believe it a coincidence that the release of entire new world histories coincided with the fairs, while conceding that he has no means of proving the two are connected — adding that most supposedly "proven" facts of history were never proven either.[1]

## Assessment in the series

The author explicitly declines to assert that Bancroft's encyclopedias amount to fake history, noting that he has never read any of them; his stated point is narrower, that the nature of the creation and dissemination of the volumes is suspicious.[1] He extends the same reservation to most mainstream history, arguing that the public does not really know how it was curated, recorded or canonized, and simply accepts assertions about what happened in the past.[1]
