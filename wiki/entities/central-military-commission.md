# Central Military Commission

The Central Military Commission (CMC) is the body that exercises command authority over China's armed forces. Its composition became a key indicator of the scale of the purge inside the People's Liberation Army reported in January 2026.[1]

## Attrition of the 2022 term

The Central Military Commission had six professional military members when its current term began in 2022.[1] By January 2026 it had just one active uniformed officer, General Zhang Shengmin, who had been promoted to vice chairman only in October after the purge of another general who held that role.[1]

Reporting cited in the Badlands Brief noted that since the summer of 2023 the party had unseated top officers in China's army, air force, navy, strategic-missile force and paramilitary police, as well as in major theater commands — including the command focused on Taiwan.[1] More than fifty senior military officers and defense-industry executives were placed under investigation or removed from office over the preceding two and a half years, according to official disclosures.[1]

## The January 2026 arrests

Social-media reports alleged the simultaneous arrest of CMC vice chairman [[zhang-youxia|Zhang Youxia]] along with [[liu-zhenli|Liu Zhenli]] and [[zhong-shaojun|Zhong Shaojun]], reportedly carried out by [[wang-xiaohong|Wang Xiaohong]]'s special service bureau.[1] The Wall Street Journal reported that Zhang Youxia had been accused of leaking classified intelligence on China's nuclear weapons technology to the United States.[1] The Financial Times reported that the moves consolidated [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]]'s power, a reading Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] endorsed as a necessary step to stave off calamity, comparing it to expectations that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would wield emergency executive authority against a color revolution in the United States.[1]

## Confirmed investigations, January 2026

Chinese authorities formally announced investigations into CMC Vice Chairman Gen. [[zhang-youxia|Zhang Youxia]], 75, and Gen. [[liu-zhenli|Liu Zhenli]] for "serious violations of discipline and law," with the probes revealed over the weekend of January 25–26, 2026.[2] The People's Liberation Army Daily accused the two of "seriously betraying" the Communist Party's trust, "trampling on and undermining" the commission itself, and threatening the authority of its chairman, [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]].[2] The purges reduced the commission — China's top military body — to just Xi and one other member, leaving senior [[peoples-liberation-army|PLA]] ranks in disarray after years of anti-corruption campaigns that had ousted dozens of high-ranking officers since 2023.[2] Analysts described the outcome as strengthening Xi's absolute control while raising concerns about operational readiness, morale, and a climate of suspicion that could deter promotions and encourage cautious decision-making.[2]
