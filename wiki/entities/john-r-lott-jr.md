# John R. Lott Jr.

John R. Lott Jr. is an American researcher and commentator on crime and election policy whose opinion column arguing that mail-in ballots should be eliminated was carried in the Badlands News Brief's "Point & Counterpoint" segment on mail-in voting in August 2025.[1]

## Argument against mail-in ballots

Lott's column opened with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Truth Social declaration, "I am going to lead a movement to get rid of mail-in ballots," and his same-day promise of an executive order "to end mail-in ballots because they are corrupt," in which Trump asserted the United States was nearly alone among nations in using them because of "massive fraud all over the place."[1] The piece noted Trump had been consistent on the point, warning even before the 2020 election that "there is a lot of dishonesty going on with mail-in voting."[1]

Lott argued Trump did not need to hedge about foreign practice: Poland was the one other country that considered running its 2020 presidential election by mail during the pandemic, and even it abandoned the attempt.[1] He drew a distinction between absentee ballots — which require a request and traditionally an excuse such as being out of town on Election Day — and the mass [[mail-in-voting|mail-in voting]] used in eight states, where ballots are automatically sent to all registered voters.[1]

## Counterpoint and Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] contrasted Lott's case with material from the Center for Election Innovation & Research, whose executive director David Becker told CBS News that eliminating mail-in voting "would make our elections much less secure," citing the value of spreading voting across several days to absorb power outages or "disinformation."[1] She characterized that as an unexpected admission, arguing that the single security control for vote-by-mail is signature verification — the very control now attacked in court as a poll tax and a suppressive practice — which, if eliminated, would leave mail ballots with zero security controls.[1] She said the pattern of pivoting from security assertions to access talking points recurs "every single time," a theme she and [[canncon|CannCon]] discussed on [[why-we-vote|Why We Vote]].[1]
