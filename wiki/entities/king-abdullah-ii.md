# King Abdullah II

Abdullah II is the King of [[jordan|Jordan]], the Hashemite monarch whose government in April 2025 outlawed the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] and who conducted high-level diplomacy with [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] in the same period.[1]

## Jeddah meeting with Mohammed bin Salman

On Wednesday, April 23, 2025, King Abdullah met Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] during an official visit to Saudi Arabia.[1] The King arrived in Jeddah accompanied by Crown Prince Hussein and was received by the deputy governor of Makkah, Prince Saud bin Mishaal bin Abdulaziz, according to the Saudi Press Agency.[1] During the meeting he expressed pride in the historical relations between Riyadh and Amman and acknowledged the significant role of Saudi Arabia in supporting Arab and Islamic causes, as reported by the Petra news agency.[1] The two leaders discussed recent regional developments, particularly the situation in the Gaza Strip and the occupied West Bank.[1]

## Context with the Brotherhood ban

The Jeddah meeting occurred the same day that Jordan's Interior Ministry, under Interior Minister [[mazen-faraya|Mazen Faraya]], declared the Muslim Brotherhood dissolved and its membership illegal.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the timing as significant, reading it as part of a broader expulsion of Western intelligence-linked networks from the Middle East ahead of a planned visit by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to Riyadh.[1]

## Canceled Biden summit (October 2023)

On October 17, 2023 the [[white-house|White House]] canceled the Jordan leg of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s Middle East trip after consulting Abdullah II, and in light of the days of mourning declared by [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] following the [[al-ahli-baptist-hospital-explosion|al-Ahli Baptist Hospital explosion]] in Gaza.[2] The planned Amman summit was to have brought together Biden, Abdullah II, Abbas and Egyptian President [[abdel-fattah-el-sisi|Abdel Fattah el-Sisi]]; its collapse came in the same hour that Arab leaders announced they were unwilling to meet Biden given United States support for Israel.[2]

## February 2025 White House meeting on Gaza

On 11 February 2025 Abdullah II met President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the White House amid widespread Arab opposition to Trump's plan for the United States to take over Gaza and permanently resettle its residents.[3] Trump restated the plan during the visit, saying "we're going to take it, we're going to hold it, we're going to cherish it," and predicting it would create jobs across the Middle East and "bring peace" to the region; he had also said he would consider withholding aid from [[jordan|Jordan]] if it refused to resettle Palestinians.[3] Abdullah II, who has previously rejected any annexation of land or displacement of Palestinians, said he had to do what was best for his country and that Arab nations would bring a counterproposal to Washington, adding: "The point is how to make this work in a way that is good for everybody."[3] A Reuters/Ipsos poll conducted 7–9 February 2025 found 74% of Americans opposed the idea of the United States taking control of Gaza and displacing its residents, with Republicans split 55% opposed to 43% supportive.[3]

Badlands commentary read the king's caution against the history of his dynasty. Abdullah II and his son, Crown Prince Hussein, are [[hashemites|Hashemites]], described as the last two direct descendants of the Prophet Muhammad, a lineage that confers quasi-royal standing in the Islamic world even as some regard the king as a Western client.[3] The commentary recalled the 1958 destruction of the Hashemite Kingdom of Iraq, in which King Faisal — grandson of the Prince Faisal depicted in Lawrence of Arabia — was mutilated and displayed, another uncle named Abdullah met the same fate, and women and children of the family were shot in a courtyard; the killings were attributed to Faisal's attempt with his cousin King Hussein of Jordan, the current king's father, to merge Jordan and Iraq into a single Hashemite kingdom, an outcome the writer said the ruling Western oligarchs would not tolerate (see [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]]).[3] On this reading, Abdullah II and his family live under permanent threat, and forcing Palestinians into Jordan could expose them to mob violence, ending both a family the writer regarded as decent and the direct bloodline of Muhammad — a revelation that might in turn trigger a far wider reaction.[3]
