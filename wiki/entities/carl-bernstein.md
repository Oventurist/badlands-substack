# Carl Bernstein

**Carl Bernstein** is an American journalist best known, with [[bob-woodward|Bob Woodward]], for reporting on the Watergate scandal for [[washington-post|The Washington Post]].[1]

## 2024 non-endorsement controversy

In October 2024, following the decisions by The Washington Post and the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] not to endorse a presidential candidate, Bernstein and Woodward were among the prominent journalists who publicly demanded that the press act out of duty to endorse — and specifically to choose the Democratic nominee.[1] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited their intervention in the [[2024-newspaper-non-endorsements|non-endorsement controversy]] as evidence that the legacy press now openly demands political participation from ostensibly independent newspapers, marking the collapse of the fourth estate's claimed independence.[1]

## "The CIA and the Media" (1977)

In 1977 Bernstein published an article in [[rolling-stone|Rolling Stone]] titled "The CIA and the Media," alleging that hundreds of American journalists had worked cheek by jowl with the [[central-intelligence-agency|Central Intelligence Agency]] — specifically that for 25 years some 400 American journalists had "secretly carried out assignments" for the agency, in an operation spanning decades and involving prominent news organizations including The New York Times, CBS and Time Inc.[2] His reporting focused mostly on journalists working abroad, but rumors soon emerged that the CIA was also using reporters to spread propaganda inside America; by 1979 the alleged program had a name, [[operation-mockingbird|Operation Mockingbird]].[2]

Bernstein's article followed earlier disclosures: according to a December 1977 New York Times account, the closer-than-expected relationship between the CIA and the press had first been made public in 1973, when CIA Director [[william-colby|William E. Colby]] shared details with reporters and the Washington Star publicized the issue, prompting congressional investigations.[2] Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] notes the irony that Bernstein worked for the [[washington-post|Washington Post]], considered by some to be one of the outlets the CIA controlled at the time — arguing that Bernstein exposed the relationship only after the fact, once the cat was already out of the bag, in a way that lent the media borrowed credibility.[2]

## As an alleged limited hangout asset

In a January 2025 essay, Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] presented Bernstein as the archetype of a controlled disclosure asset.[3] Carlson wrote that when the [[church-committee|Church Committee]] was investigating the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s abuses of power in the 1970s, the agency knew it could not entirely avoid exposure and therefore had "their puppet, Carl Bernstein," investigate and write about how the CIA manipulates the media.[3] The effect, in his account, was to shift public attention away from the CIA's staging of [[false-flag-operation|false flag]] mass casualty events, assassination of world leaders, and control and overthrow of governments, and toward the comparatively minor narrative that a favorite talking head might be receiving kickbacks for avoiding criticism of the agency.[3]

Carlson generalized the episode into a rule of thumb: if the Deep State knows damaging information will surface regardless, it is better for the network that one of its own assets breaks the story first, since the trust gained by that exposure can later be used to lead the public astray.[3] He closed the section by asking, "Who are today's Carl Bernsteins?" and advising readers to be wary of figures who gained enormous followings overnight, who only recently began supporting [[donald-trump|Trump]], or whose reach far exceeds the quality of their content.[3]
