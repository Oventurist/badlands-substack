# Charles Clancy

Charles Clancy is a senior vice president and chief technology officer at the [[mitre-corporation|MITRE Corporation]], the non-profit, non-partisan research institution that operates Federally Funded Research and Development Centers (FFRDCs) on behalf of the U.S. government.[1] He testified on February 6, 2024 before the [[house-committee-on-homeland-security|House Committee on Homeland Security]]'s Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Protection Subcommittee at the hearing "Securing Operational Technology: A Deep Dive into the Water Sector."[1]

## Background

Before joining MITRE, Clancy spent nine years on the faculty of Virginia Tech and served as executive director of what is now the Virginia Tech National Security Institute.[1] He began his career at the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]], where he led advanced research and development programs.[1] MITRE personnel under his organization provide technical support across the executive branch, including to the [[cisa|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]], the National Institute of Standards and Technology, and [[us-cyber-command|U.S. Cyber Command]].[1]

## Testimony

Clancy was critical of the federal government's response to the November 2023 intrusion at the [[aliquippa-municipal-authority|Aliquippa Municipal Authority]], which consisted primarily of sanctions on Iran. He said the sanction-focused approach "does not scale to the strategic threat that we face," and argued that "we must think of these attacks in the same vein as a major natural disaster where the solution is not technology band aids but it's more about procedures and people."[1]

Asked for his nightmare scenario, Clancy pointed to interdependency among [[critical-infrastructure|critical infrastructure]] sectors: "You hit energy, water goes down shortly thereafter. Same thing with natural gas, right? So they're all interlinked. And if you have a significant attack on one, you can cause cascading of failures and others."[1]
