# The Jerusalem Post

The Jerusalem Post is an English-language Israeli newspaper cited in Badlands Media coverage of Israeli domestic politics.[1]

## Coverage of Bennett's platform (June 2026)

In June 2026 the Jerusalem Post reported [[knesset|Knesset]] member and prime-ministerial candidate [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]]'s call for the establishment of an Israeli constitution "in the spirit of the [US] Declaration of Independence," and carried his description of the government he intended to form should he win the October election.[1] The paper quoted Bennett saying his administration would address "cost of living, education, crime, crazy housing costs, our international standing, and security above all," and that his first order of business would be a state commission of inquiry into the events of October 7, 2023.[1]

The reporting also carried Bennett's intention to cut benefits to ultra-Orthodox draft evaders, to focus on artificial-intelligence development and innovation, and his economic argument that "prices are high for one main reason: we don't have real competition," to be remedied by breaking up monopolies, cutting regulation, and opening the market to imports.[1] Badlands read that last point as a reference to the socialist elements surviving in [[israel|Israel]]'s founding political economy, and noted that Bennett — one of the leading opposition figures arrayed against [[donald-trump|President Trump]] — had signaled an intent to restart the war with [[iran|Iran]].[1]


## Houthi announcement scoop (2025)

In May 2025 *The Jerusalem Post* reported, citing an informed source, that Israel was not informed in advance about President Trump's [[us-houthi-ceasefire-announcement-2025|announcement]] that the United States would stop bombing the [[houthis|Houthis]] in Yemen.[2]


## November 2024 election coverage

Ahead of the November 5, 2024 US presidential election, a Jerusalem Post investigation reported a potentially illegal [[ballot-harvesting]] scheme operating in central Israel, run by [[ivoteisrael|iVoteIsrael]], a group promoting absentee voting among American immigrants in Israel.[3] The paper had previously reported that US voters in Israel said iVoteIsrael mishandled their absentee ballot requests.[3]

On the same date the Post published an opinion piece arguing that "a Trump victory will have horrendous consequences" for the United States, the free world and Israel, and asserting that a period of unrest and even violence following a Trump defeat would be preferable to a Trump win.[3] The piece noted that Israeli polling showed well over 60 percent support for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] against around 20 percent for [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] paired the two pieces, calling their same-day publication "startling and sobering" and arguing that identical reporting about any other country would provoke broad alarm about sovereignty and foreign influence.[3]
