# Skull and Bones

Skull and Bones is the Yale University senior society named in "Hijacked Consciousness - Sacred Origins" as an institution of elite formation rather than a student curiosity.[1] The essay, written by [[observing-consciousness|Observing Consciousness]] for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] as the first installment of the [[hijacked-consciousness-series|Hijacked Consciousness]] series, states flatly that "Skull and Bones wasn't a prank. It was the ritual dressing room of power."[1]

## Role in the article's argument

The reference appears in the section on the [[industrial-model-of-schooling|industrial model of schooling]], where the author argues that behind the visible reforms of curriculum "the true architects consolidated power, the credential class. The Ivy network. The groomed handlers of empire."[1] In the same passage [[harvard-university|Harvard]] is characterised as "obedience, well-dressed," and Skull and Bones is presented as the ritual counterpart to that credentialing function.[1]

The article's broader claim is that these actors "didn't just rewrite curriculum. They rewrote reality," reclassifying medicine as chemistry, spirit as myth and biology as code.[1] Within that frame the society functions as evidence for the author's contention that the systems governing education are "not just political" but "energetic, psychological, and ritualistic," gatekeeping consciousness rather than merely disseminating knowledge.[1]

## Cited in the Harvard control grid essay

"The Harvard Control Grid" notes that [[mcgeorge-bundy|McGeorge Bundy]] — National Security Advisor to Kennedy and Johnson and later president of the [[ford-foundation|Ford Foundation]] — had been a Skull and Bones member before entering government, offering his career as an example of intelligence continuity maintained through the nonprofit sector.[2] The article concedes that direct affiliations between Harvard's current endowment board and Skull and Bones or Bohemian Grove "aren't public," but argues that the behavioral pattern of elite continuity is nonetheless evident in institutions such as the [[porcellian-club|Porcellian Club]] and Harvard's finals clubs.[2]

## Cited in the Ivy Dominion essay

"The Ivy Dominion" calls Skull & Bones "the ur-club" of the alleged [[ivy-dominion|Ivy Dominion]] — "a direct pipeline to the CIA, U.S. State Department, and global economic hitman networks" — and names [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]], [[john-kerry|John Kerry]] and [[austan-goolsbee|Austan Goolsbee]] among its alumni.[3] It describes the society as a relay point in a three-step sequence running from Bones to the CIA, from the CIA to the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]], and onward to the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]], [[international-monetary-fund|IMF]] and [[world-bank|World Bank]], forming what the author calls a recursive loop of policy, enforcement and consent engineering.[3]

The essay cites Bush's tenure as CIA Director before the presidency and [[william-bundy|William Bundy]]'s role running psychological operations during the Vietnam War as evidence that "Bones is not a club; it is a covert network," and pairs it with [[scroll-and-key|Scroll & Key]] as the two Yale societies grooming candidates for strategic intelligence work.[3] It further notes that resurfacing Skull and Bones affiliations in podcasts, documentaries and lawsuits tied to Cold War-era trauma programs placed [[yale-university|Yale]] under mounting pressure.[3]

## Role in the medical–Nazi nexus

In "The Inversion of Health," [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] writes that the modern Healthcare Industrial Complex is deeply intertwined with Nazi Germany, and that the Rockefellers played a large role in creating that union "along with a cadre of Bonesmen" — members of Yale's Skull and Bones.[4]

## Origins and incorporation

"The Men Behind the Curtain - Part 8" treats Skull and Bones as the subject of an entire installment, describing it as also known as The Order, Order 322, or the Brotherhood of Death, founded in 1832 and the oldest and most prestigious of [[yale-university|Yale]]'s secret societies.[5] It stands with [[scroll-and-key|Scroll and Key]] and [[wolfs-head|Wolf's Head]] as one of the three senior societies that have stood out for ages, though only Scroll and Key can claim near-equal influence on American affairs over the past 160 years.[5] The article notes that Harvard and Princeton host similar exclusive societies, but that Yale's — led by Bones — are unchallenged in their influence on American political affairs.[5]

Members are said to practice "constructive confusion," deliberately allowing conflicting mythologies about the Order's origins to circulate: one version makes it an outgrowth of a British or Scottish Freemasonic grouping founded at All Soul's College, Oxford, in the late seventeenth century; another traces it to German "nationalistic" secret societies of the early nineteenth century; a third holds it to be a uniquely American institution that adapted European masonic rituals into a new form.[5] An enduring rumour holds that the number "322" beneath the skull and crossbones denotes both the founding year and the society's status as the second lodge in an international system, with lodge "1" in Germany and lodge "3" at another American college.[5]

The Order was officially incorporated in 1856 as the [[russell-trust-association|Russell Trust Association]], and virtually all available information on its early members indicates that the money sustaining it derived from the Far East opium trade set up by the [[british-east-india-company|British East India Company]].[5] That trade was controlled first by [[baring-brothers-bank|Baring Brothers Bank]] and later by the [[rothschild-family|House of Rothschild]], whose sponsorship brought New England merchant families — Cabot, Coolidge, Forbes, Higginson, Sturgis, Lodge, Lowell, Perkins and Russell — into the business as junior partners; the Russell and Perkins lines furnished the society's founding families.[5] Since its founding the Order has inducted only about 2,500 members, with roughly 600 alive at any given time, a small number the article says underscores the concentration of power in its members' hands.[5]

## Chains of influence

In *America's Secret Establishment*, [[antony-sutton|Antony Sutton]] outlined the Order's ability to establish vertical and horizontal "chains of influence" ensuring the continuity of its schemes, with the [[william-collins-whitney|Whitney]]–Stimson–Bundy links exemplifying the "vertical chain."[5] [[hollister-bundy|Hollister Bundy]], Stimson's Pentagon point man for the [[manhattan-project|Manhattan Project]], fathered the Bonesmen [[william-bundy|William Bundy]] and [[mcgeorge-bundy|McGeorge Bundy]], who shaped the flow of intelligence during the Vietnam War from posts in the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] and the [[state-department|State Department]]; William later edited *Foreign Affairs* and McGeorge became president of the [[ford-foundation|Ford Foundation]].[5] Four members of the class of 1917 cell, including Prescott Bush, were directors of [[brown-brothers-harriman|Brown Brothers Harriman]], which together with [[guaranty-trust-company|Guaranty Trust]] was dominated by Bonesmen and, the article alleges, heavily involved in financing Communism and [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]]'s regime.[5] In 1932 the [[union-banking-corporation|Union Banking Corporation]] enlisted four directors from that cell alongside two Nazi bankers associated with [[fritz-thyssen|Fritz Thyssen]], and in 1942 Alien Property Custodian [[leo-t-crowley|Leo T. Crowley]] signed Vesting Order 248 seizing Prescott Bush's property under the Trading with the Enemy Act.[5]

Bonesmen are said to share an affinity for the [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian]] historical dialectic, using controlled conflict — thesis against antithesis — to produce a predetermined synthesis in which the state is absolute and individual freedoms are granted in exchange for obedience: a [[new-world-order|New World Order]].[5]

## Ritual and conditioning

Initiation proceeds through the "tapping" ritual and the [[three-ordeals|Three Ordeals]] of boarding school, trial by nature and war.[5] Historically the society excluded blacks, Jews and other non-WASPs, though token members from these groups have occasionally been selected in recent decades.[5] Within the society's crypt, known as "the Tomb," a "sacred room" numbered 322 bears a German inscription above the vault entrance: "Who was the fool, who was the wise man, beggar or king? Whether poor or rich, all's the same in death."[5] The quotation, drawn from a German Freemasonic ritual, is cited by critics as evidence of a "Nazi like" and "Germanic" character, though the article maintains the Order's rites closely resemble those of Scottish and English masonic lodges.[5] Ambiguity and secrecy are taught not as mystical ends but as practical tools for members who will go on to serve in government, the intelligence community, the military or the private sector.[5]
