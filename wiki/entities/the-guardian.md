# The Guardian

The Guardian is a British newspaper and international news organization whose reporting is regularly excerpted in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] as representative of legacy-media framing.[1]

## Coverage cited in the Brief

The Guardian supplied three items in the 27 November 2024 Brief. Its trade coverage reported that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s threat to impose steep tariffs on imports had set the stage for a bitter global trade war, with trade experts and economists warning consumers and companies to brace for steep costs.[1]

A second Guardian report described plans by Trump's transition team for all cabinet picks to receive sweeping security clearances directly from the president-elect, with [[fbi|FBI]] background checks deferred until the incoming administration had taken over the bureau and installed its own officials.[1] The paper attributed the team's contempt for the FBI vetting process to deep distrust dating from officials turning over transition records to the Russia investigation during the first Trump presidency.[1]

A third Guardian item reported that [[walmart|Walmart]] would phase out several diversity, equity and inclusion initiatives amid escalating attacks from conservatives, and carried the company's responsive statement.[1]

## Investigation into Israeli influence operations (June 2024)

On June 24, 2024, The Guardian published an investigation reporting that Israeli documents showed an expansive government effort to shape US discourse around the Gaza war.[2] The piece described how Diaspora Affairs Minister [[amichai-chikli|Amichai Chikli]] had briefed the [[knesset|Knesset]] in November 2023 on countering anti-war protests at American universities, and uncovered evidence that Israel had relaunched a controversial entity — the program originally called Kela Shlomo, then "Concert," now [[voices-of-israel|Voices of Israel]] — as part of a broader campaign to target US college campuses and redefine antisemitism in US law.[2] The paper characterized the latest incarnation as a hardline and sometimes covert operation against student protests, human rights organizations, and other voices of dissent.[2]

Badlands commentary urged readers to read the article in full, describing it as the moment a story the outlet's contributor had been reporting since September 2023 was "blown wide open," while advising independent discernment about individual details.[2]

## Coverage of Trump's "won't have to vote" remarks (July 2024)

The Guardian reported on July 27, 2024 that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had ignited alarm among critics by telling supporters at a [[turning-point-action|Turning Point Action]] rally in West Palm Beach, Florida — an event the paper described as hosted by a "far-right Christian advocacy group" — that they would not "have to vote again" if they returned him to the presidency.[3] The paper quoted him saying, "Christians, get out and vote! Just this time – you won't have to do it any more," and "It'll be fixed! It'll be fine. You won't have to vote any more, my beautiful Christians," noting the remarks were delivered near his Mar-a-Lago resort.[3] It cited constitutional and civil rights attorney Andrew Seidel, who replied to video of the comments on X that "this is not subtle Christian nationalism. He's talking about ending our democracy and installing a Christian nation."[3]

## Review of Ghosts of Afghanistan

"Who Is The Real Enemy?" cites a Guardian review of [[jonathan-steele|Jonathan Steele]]'s *Ghosts of Afghanistan: Hard Truths and Foreign Myths* as evidence against the standard account of the 1979 Soviet intervention.[4] The review states that the Russians invaded in December 1979 not as part of a plan for the world victory of communism, as Western propaganda had it, but to protect the security of their southern frontier against Islamic unrest and American intrigue.[4]

It further records that senior Soviet generals and officials warned against getting mixed up in an Afghan civil war, citing the precedent of Vietnam, but that the leadership proceeded for lack of a better alternative and was soon sucked into a quagmire and looking for a way out.[4]
