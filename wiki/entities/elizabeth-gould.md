# Elizabeth Gould

Elizabeth "Liz" Gould is an American journalist and author who, with her husband [[paul-fitzgerald|Paul Fitzgerald]], reported from Afghanistan in 1981 and 1983 and co-wrote [[valediction-three-nights-of-desmond|Valediction: Three Nights of Desmond]] for Trine Day Press.[1] The couple were, by the book's account, the only two American journalists permitted to enter the war-torn nation during that period.[1]

## Work

Gould and Fitzgerald's documentaries from Afghanistan undercut the carefully constructed "Russian Vietnam" narrative promoted for years by what they identify as a western deep state.[1] Their book situates Afghanistan within world history and advances the thesis that there were two opposing factions of U.S. intelligence at war with each other rather than a single American policy.[1]

In evaluating the neoconservative cabal that captured U.S. intelligence estimates in the 1970s, Fitzgerald and Gould write that "developed by an inbred class of former Trotskyist intellectuals, the Team B approach represented a radical transformation of America's national security bureaucracy into a new kind of elitist cult."[1] They trace the roots of these figures to Trotskyism and to an emerging "end times" Christian-Zionist movement, a genealogy also elaborated by Cynthia Chung.[1]

Gould co-authored the reconstruction of the killing of Ambassador [[adolph-dubs|Adolph Dubs]], including the discovery that Dubs had held at least fourteen secret meetings with Afghan President [[hafizullah-amin|Hafizullah Amin]], and that Amin and [[gulbuddin-hekmatyar|Gulbuddin Hekmatyar]] were united as members of the same Ghilzai tribe.[1]
