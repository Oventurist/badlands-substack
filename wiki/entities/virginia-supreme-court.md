# Virginia Supreme Court

The **Virginia Supreme Court** is the court of last resort in the Commonwealth of Virginia. In May 2026 it became the decisive actor in the state's congressional redistricting fight when it invalidated a voter-approved redistricting referendum ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]].[1]

## May 2026 redistricting ruling

On May 8, 2026, the court invalidated Virginia's voter-approved redistricting referendum in a 4–3 ruling that halted implementation of new congressional maps before the midterms.[1] The court found that the constitutional amendment process used by the [[virginia-general-assembly|General Assembly]] violated Virginia's constitution because lawmakers approved the amendment after early voting for the prior general election had already begun.[1]

Central to the ruling was the court's interpretation of the constitutional reference to the "next general election," which it determined includes Virginia's early voting period and not solely Election Day.[1] Republicans had argued that approving the amendment after early voting started disenfranchised voters who had already cast ballots before the measure was finalized for consideration.[1] Virginia Democrats said after the ruling that they planned to appeal to the U.S. Supreme Court.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the outcome "obviously the right decision" but said she was surprised it happened, noting that the ruling came during the election period — when, historically, nothing changes — and that the remedy overturned a vote of the people, something that almost never occurs.[1] She placed the fault for the resulting crisis entirely with the court rather than with Democrats, arguing that the court had "broad discretion" and instead "chose to create this crisis," and that the original error was allowing the referendum election to take place at all — a point she credited [[canncon|CannCon]] with making from the outset.[1] In her reading the episode forced the public to reckon with elections as "fake, politicized theatre," true even before a single voter is registered or a ballot cast.[1]

## February 2026 referendum ruling

On February 13, 2026, the court cleared the way for an April 21, 2026 referendum on a Democratic-backed congressional redistricting amendment, ruling that voters could decide the plan even as legal challenges continued.[2] The measure, passed by the Democratic majority in the General Assembly, would amend the state constitution to permit [[mid-decade-redistricting|mid-decade redistricting]] of U.S. House districts, a departure from the traditional post-census process.[2] If approved, the redrawn map could allow Democrats to expand their representation in Virginia's 11-member U.S. House delegation from six seats to potentially ten, making the referendum a national focal point in the battle over control of Congress.[2] A lower court had blocked the referendum on procedural grounds, but the Supreme Court reversed that decision and allowed the special election to proceed while litigation continued.[2]
