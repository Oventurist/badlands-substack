# Ron Brown

Ronald H. Brown (1941–1996) was United States Secretary of Commerce under [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and a former chairman of the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]]. He died in an Air Force plane crash near Dubrovnik, Croatia on April 3, 1996, along with thirty-four others accompanying him on a trade mission.

## In the Badlands corpus

The article America for Sale   Part 1 treats Brown as the central figure of the Clinton administration's alleged sale of American assets and secrets. It states he received the Commerce appointment because he had been a major fundraiser for Clinton's first presidential campaign, and describes him as the "liaison between China and the Clinton administration."

### Investigation and independent counsel

Attorney General [[janet-reno|Janet Reno]] said in the spring of 1995 that Brown had apparently failed to properly report large payments from his business partner [[nolanda-hill|Nolanda Sue Hill]]. A federal appeals court appointed Miami attorney [[daniel-pearson|Daniel S. Pearson]] as independent counsel to examine his private financial dealings and public reporting of them — the fourth special counsel then investigating Clinton administration officials. Legal sources expected the inquiry to run into the 1996 presidential campaign. Hill and her financial aide Kenneth C. White were later indicted on nine counts of conspiring to defraud the IRS and FDIC between 1989 and 1995; the *New York Times* noted that although the indictment never named him, Brown "was the original target of the investigation." After his death the case was transferred to the Justice Department, which the source argues removed the legal threat from the administration.

### Trade missions and technology transfer

Citing [[judicial-watch|Judicial Watch]] research reproduced on Buchal.com, the article states that seats on Brown's Commerce Department trade missions were sold to raise money for the DNC and the 1996 Clinton/Gore campaign, with "overwhelming evidence that Bill and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] knew of and approved" the scheme. More seriously, the same source alleges that technology transfers "approved and overseen by Ron Brown's Commerce Department" breached national security: on a 1994 trade mission to China, Brown arranged a meeting between [[bernard-schwartz|Bernard Schwartz]] of [[loral-corporation|Loral Corporation]], a major DNC donor, and a Chinese government official, leading to a transfer of American missile technology that [[congress|Congress]] later investigated.

At the time of his death Brown was under subpoena in Judicial Watch's suit against the Commerce Department to produce trade-mission documents. Hill testified that he had shown her such documents in an ostrich-skin portfolio; they were withheld in violation of the subpoena and a FOIA request. The article quotes Brown as saying shortly before his death: "I am too old to go to jail. If I go down, I'll take everyone else down with me."

### Circumstances of death

The source characterizes the crash as suspicious, noting the plane's "suspicious navigational failures" and questioning why a Commerce Secretary was flying into what was then a war zone. It reports that pathologists at the [[armed-forces-institute-of-pathology|Armed Forces Institute of Pathology]] observed "a perfectly round .45 inch inwardly beveling hole" in the top of Brown's head. Lt. Col. [[steve-cogswell|Steve Cogswell]] publicly questioned the official finding of blunt-force trauma, and Lt. Col. [[david-hause|David Hause]] corroborated that the wound "looked like a punched-out .45-caliber entrance hole." Original head x-rays reportedly showing metal fragments went missing, and AFIP imposed gag orders. The article concludes that Brown "knew too much" and "became expendable," comparing his fate to that of Ambassador [[christopher-stevens|Christopher Stevens]] in [[benghazi|Benghazi]] — "same playbook."
