# Alec Baldwin

Alec Baldwin is an American actor and producer who became the central figure in the criminal proceedings arising from the fatal shooting on the set of the Western film *Rust*, in which cinematographer [[halyna-hutchins|Halyna Hutchins]] was killed by a live round discharged from a prop firearm.[1]

## Involuntary manslaughter case

Baldwin was charged with involuntary manslaughter in New Mexico over the shooting, the same charge on which the film's armorer, [[hannah-gutierrez-reed|Hannah Gutierrez-Reed]], was convicted.[1] On Friday, July 12, 2024, a New Mexico judge dismissed Baldwin's case in the middle of trial, ruling that prosecutors had withheld evidence from the defense.[1] The abrupt dismissal was described in contemporaneous coverage as a "shocking twist."[1]

The armorer's father, veteran Hollywood armorer [[thell-reed|Thell Reed]], responded to the dismissal by arguing that his daughter's conviction should likewise be overturned, saying that "a lot of evidence" had never been heard and that prosecutors had "suppressed all the important evidence" until Baldwin's attorneys forced it into the open.[1]

## Start of trial

Baldwin's televised trial opened nearly three years after the October 21, 2021 shooting, marking his first appearance in a New Mexico courtroom over the death; the felony involuntary manslaughter charge carried up to 18 months in prison on a unanimous jury conviction.[2] He had been the star and a co-producer of the Western, and was pointing a revolver at Hutchins during a rehearsal in a small church at Bonanza Creek Ranch when the gun discharged, killing her and wounding director Joel Souza; Baldwin said he pulled back the hammer but not the trigger.[2] Two themes were expected to dominate the proceedings — the chaotic atmosphere of the set and the mechanics of the Italian-made classic revolver he was holding — while the question of who brought the live rounds onto the set had never been officially resolved.[2] See [[rust-film-set-shooting|Rust film set shooting]].[2]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the dismissal as a test of whether the rules of evidence apply equally to defendants of unequal means, noting that Baldwin retained a legal team compared to O. J. Simpson's and that "you get what you pay for," so that "the luck" of dismissal came at a premium.[1] While affirming that the rules of evidence exist to protect the rights of the accused and to govern a free and fair justice system, she asked whether those same protections were extended to Gutierrez-Reed, who was serving an 18-month sentence at the Western New Mexico Correctional Facility, and said the outstanding question remained who brought live rounds onto the set.[1]

Before the trial began, Ashe in America had grouped the case with the Karen Read and Young Thug trials as culturally polarizing court dramas, dismissed Baldwin's hammer-not-trigger account as inconsistent with how firearms operate, and noted that a witness claimed to have seen him pull the trigger.[2] She observed that Baldwin had assembled a legal team of mostly Harvard lawyers against a local New Mexico prosecution and predicted the case could become "the biggest red pill of all recent cultural court dramas" if the young armorer remained imprisoned while the actor went free.[2]
