# Tom Barrack

Tom Barrack is a longtime associate of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] who served as the United States ambassador to Turkey and Syria, described in Badlands coverage as "President Trump's best friend" in that diplomatic role.[1]

## Statements on Hezbollah

In a Fox News interview reported in April 2026, Barrack said that [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] cannot be annihilated through military force, citing the organization's deep integration into Lebanese society over the preceding several decades.[1] The remark placed him at odds with Israeli officials who were simultaneously preparing to dismantle Hezbollah by force, and aligned him with the Trump administration's emphasis on negotiated settlements in [[lebanon|Lebanon]].[1]

## Context

Barrack's comments followed an Oval Office meeting between Israeli and Lebanese representatives that produced a three-week extension of the Israel–Hezbollah ceasefire.[1] Israeli media had characterized that meeting as producing a united front to disarm Hezbollah, a framing complicated when the Lebanese Prime Minister subsequently accused [[israel|Israel]] of war crimes over the targeting of Lebanese journalists.[1]

## Ankara talks (July 2026)

Barrack, serving as U.S. Ambassador to [[turkey|Turkey]] and Special Presidential Envoy for [[syria|Syria]] and [[iraq|Iraq]], met Turkish Foreign Minister [[hakan-fidan|Hakan Fidan]] in Ankara on 27 July 2026 for discussions focused on strengthening the bilateral partnership and on regional stability, security, and prosperity, particularly regarding Syria and Iraq.[2]

The meeting took place one day before Iraqi Prime Minister [[ali-al-zaidi|Ali al-Zaidi]] arrived in Ankara for talks that produced five Turkey–Iraq agreements and the [[turkish-petroleum-corporation|Turkish Petroleum Corporation]]'s acquisition of a stake in the Kirkuk oil project.[2] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the sequencing as evidence that [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s "fingerprints are all over this situation," describing Barrack as Trump's oldest and closest friend who has become his personal diplomat to essentially every country in the Middle East.[2]

## Kurdish–Damascus reconciliation (February 2026)

In February 2026, as [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] signed multibillion-dollar reconstruction agreements with [[syria|Syria]], Badlands credited Barrack with working to get the [[kurds|Kurds]] to "bury the hatchet" with Damascus and restore peace to the country.[3] That track produced a U.S.-brokered deal under which Syrian government security forces entered the Kurdish-majority city of [[qamishli|Qamishli]] while Kurdish police retained day-to-day security duties, even as the U.S. military carried out renewed airstrikes against [[islamic-state|Islamic State]] remnants elsewhere in Syria.[3]

## Milken Institute remarks and MBS introduction

Speaking at the Milken Institute Middle East and Africa Summit in Abu Dhabi in early 2026, Barrack — U.S. Ambassador to [[turkey|Turkey]] and Special Envoy to Syria, and described as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s oldest friend — characterized Trump's relationship with Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] as a "bromance," a clip that [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] approvingly called "based" and which appeared to prompt a hostile response from [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]].[4]

Badlands commentary also notes Barrack's earlier role as a connector in Gulf diplomacy: in 2015 [[mohamed-bin-zayed-al-nahyan|Mohammed bin Zayed]] and Emirati ambassador [[youssef-al-otaiba|Youssef Al Otaiba]] introduced Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] to Barrack, the Lebanese-American businessman, who then connected MBS to then-candidate Trump.[4]

## Remarks on Hezbollah

In an interview with Mario Nawfal cited by Badlands in November 2025, Barrack — whose parents were Lebanese immigrants to America — said the American public had been grossly misinformed about the true nature of [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], explaining that it began as a political movement to expel [[israel|Israel]] from southern Lebanon and had since mutated into a militia.[5] He also stated that Hezbollah had not once violated the ceasefire it signed with Israel the previous year, not even after the pager attack.[5] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited these remarks while questioning Israel's airstrike on Beirut that killed Hezbollah commander [[haytham-ali-tabatabai|Haytham Ali Tabatabai]].[5]

## Damascus flag-raising and the Syria portfolio (May 2025)

On Thursday, May 29, 2025, the American flag was raised outside the long-dormant U.S. ambassador's residence in Damascus, the first time it had flown there since 2012, signaling a significant shift in relations between Washington and Syria's new leadership.[6] Tom Barrack — U.S. ambassador to [[turkey|Turkey]] and newly appointed special envoy to [[syria|Syria]] — arrived in Damascus for the symbolic ceremony.[6] Speaking to Al Arabiya, Barrack called the decades-old Syrian-Israeli conflict a "solvable problem" and floated the idea of a "non-aggression agreement" as a step toward regional stability.[6]

The thaw coincided with Reuters reports that Israel and Syria had been engaged in direct talks, including recent in-person meetings, to reduce tensions along their border.[6] Although the United States had not formally reopened its embassy, the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] — backed by regional allies [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and Turkey — had grown increasingly open to engaging Damascus, with President [[donald-trump|Trump]] having met Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmad al-Sharaa]] earlier that month in Riyadh and the U.S. beginning to scale back longstanding sanctions.[6]

During the visit Barrack met al-Sharaa and attended the signing of an agreement for a consortium of Qatari, Turkish, and U.S. companies to develop a 5,000-megawatt energy project intended to revitalize much of Syria's war-battered electricity grid.[6]

## Badlands assessment of Barrack's network

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized Barrack as a longtime friend and supporter of President Trump, best known for introducing Jared Kushner to [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] — then Saudi defense minister — in December 2016, after Trump's first presidential victory.[6] On that reading Barrack is also most likely responsible for piecing together the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]], given his extensive network of contacts in Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and [[qatar|Qatar]].[6] The same commentary noted that Barrack had been prosecuted by the Biden Justice Department on nine felony charges related to alleged foreign corruption — described by Badlands as bogus — and was ultimately found not guilty on all counts.[6]

Badlands read Turkey's participation in the Syrian energy consortium as a major signal that Trump's early nod to Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Erdogan]] after the collapse of the Assad regime in December had been an attempt to give Ankara an edge over [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] and Israel in the contest for influence over Damascus and southern Syria.[6]

## Riyadh meeting on Abraham Accords expansion (June 2025)

On June 26, 2025, as Israeli media reported a purported Trump–Netanyahu plan to end the Gaza war and expand the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]], Barrack — serving as US Special Envoy to [[syria|Syria]] — was meeting Saudi Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] in [[riyadh|Riyadh]].[7] Badlands analyst GhostofBasedPatrickHenry cited the timing of the meeting as evidence that the details, or at least the optics, of any such deal were still being developed, and expressed skepticism that the five-point plan leaked to Israel Hayom was the agreement [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] had alluded to, since it neither required Israel to recognize Palestinian statehood nor restored 1967 borders — the Saudis' stated non-negotiable conditions for joining the Accords.[7]

## Remarks on Trump's Ukraine patience (June 2025)

In June 2025, Barrack — US ambassador to Türkiye and special envoy for Syria — told the Turkish broadcaster NTV that [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] appeared to be "at the end of his rope" over the conflict in Ukraine.[8] The interview followed a second round of talks between Russian and Ukrainian delegations lasting roughly ninety minutes, and came amid Kiev's calls for a high-level summit including Trump, [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[volodymyr-zelensky|Vladimir Zelensky]].[8] Barrack said any such meeting would need to be "meaningful" and yield substantive outcomes, acknowledged he could not speak for the president directly, and noted that Trump — openly frustrated with both Zelensky and Putin — is not known for his patience.[8] He added that Türkiye could play a key role in facilitating a breakthrough given its status as a mediator.[8]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] highlighted that the remarks came from "President Trump's best friend," who five days earlier had said "Israel could be left behind" in reference to the evolving dynamics of Syria diplomacy with Turkey.[8] He also noted Barrack's post on X referencing the 1916 [[sykes-picot-agreement|Sykes-Picot Agreement]], which carved up the Middle East into today's borders in the interests of Britain and France, and praised Barrack's record — including his role in piecing together the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] — while hoping to see him in an expanded role.[8]

## Syria defense support and the Turkish green light (July 2025)

In July 2025, [[syria|Syria]]'s interim government asked [[turkey|Turkey]] for support in strengthening its defense capabilities after two weeks of sectarian violence that drew in Israeli strikes, and also sought assistance in combating "terrorist organizations," including the Islamic State group.[9] Turkish defense ministry officials, speaking anonymously, said Ankara was working toward providing training, advisory services, and technical support to bolster Syrian defense capacity.[9] Badlands commentary singled out remarks by Barrack — Ambassador to Turkey and U.S. Special Envoy to Syria — as a "buried gem," reading them as effectively giving [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] a green light to intervene and halt Israeli Defense Forces operations, while questioning whether he would act on it.[9]
