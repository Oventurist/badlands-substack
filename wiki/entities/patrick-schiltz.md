# Patrick Schiltz

Patrick Schiltz is the Chief Judge of the United States District Court for the District of Minnesota. In January 2026 he became a central figure in the confrontation between the federal judiciary and federal immigration enforcement, ordering the acting director of [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] to appear personally before him to answer for the agency's non-compliance with court orders.[1]

## Contempt order against ICE leadership

On or about January 28, 2026, Schiltz ordered acting ICE Director [[todd-lyons|Todd Lyons]] to appear in person in Minneapolis federal court on January 30, 2026, at 1:00 p.m. to show cause why he should not be held in contempt of court.[1] The order arose from ICE's failure to comply with a January 14, 2026 directive requiring that detainee [[juan-tobay-robles|Juan Tobay Robles]] — taken into custody by federal immigration agents earlier that month — receive either a bond hearing or release within seven days.[1]

Schiltz characterized the violation as part of a broader pattern, describing the directive as one of "dozens of court orders" that ICE had ignored in recent weeks.[1] He wrote that "The Court's patience is at an end... the extent of ICE's violation of court orders is likewise extraordinary, and lesser measures have been tried and failed."[1] The scheduled hearing was subject to cancellation if Robles were released beforehand.[1]

## Context

The order was issued amid escalating tensions in Minneapolis over federal immigration enforcement operations, including the fatal shooting of [[alex-jeffrey-pretti|Alex Jeffrey Pretti]] by [[united-states-border-patrol|U.S. Border Patrol]] agents on January 24, 2026, and the earlier shooting of Renee Good.[1] The same enforcement controversy fed a parallel appropriations fight in the [[united-states-senate|United States Senate]] over funding for the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]].[1]
