# House Foreign Affairs Committee

The House Foreign Affairs Committee is the standing committee of the U.S. House of Representatives with jurisdiction over foreign policy legislation and oversight. In January 2026 the committee became a focal point of the American debate over Venezuela's political future following the capture and removal of President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[1]

## Endorsement of María Corina Machado

Members of the committee reportedly expressed unanimous support for Venezuelan opposition leader [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]] as a potential future leader of the country after a closed-door meeting on Capitol Hill.[1] Lawmakers praised her long-standing opposition to Maduro's government and called for free and fair elections in the post-Maduro period.[1]

The endorsement was notable because President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had previously dismissed Machado's prospects as Venezuela's leader, describing her as unpopular and lacking the respect needed to govern.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the committee's backing as evidence of the "habitually-treasonous" Republican Party working subversively against Trump's stated Venezuela policy.[1] The episode illustrated a divergence between congressional preferences and White House posture toward the transitional government led by acting president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]].[1]

## Afghanistan withdrawal hearings (2023)

In March 2023 the committee held its first public hearing on the bungled 2021 evacuation of Afghanistan.[2] Marine sergeant [[tyler-vargas-andrews]] testified that his sniper team had identified the [[abbey-gate-bombing|Abbey Gate]] suicide bomber before the attack and had an opportunity to kill him, but was denied authority by military leadership and told that "the asset could not be compromised."[2] He also testified that the [[state-department]] turned Afghans away from the airport, condemning them to death at the hands of the [[taliban]].[2]

## Removal of Ilhan Omar (2023)

On February 2, 2023, the Republican-led House passed a resolution removing Democratic Rep. [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]] of Minnesota from the committee by an unofficial vote of 218-211, along party lines.[3] Republicans argued that Omar's past statements about Israel — criticized by Republicans and some Democrats as antisemitic — disqualified her from a seat on the panel, which oversees US foreign policy.[3] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] treated the vote as an overdue instance of Congress policing bad faith among its own members.[3]
