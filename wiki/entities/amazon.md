# Amazon

**Amazon** is an American e-commerce and cloud computing company founded by [[jeff-bezos|Jeff Bezos]], and one of the five largest American companies by market capitalization in 2025.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Market position and Covid

Amazon ranked among the top five U.S. companies by market cap in both 2020 and 2025.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] notes that during the Covid lockdowns smaller companies went out of business while mega-corporations grew larger, and that some referred to the pandemic as "the Amazon pandemic" because of the volume of business Amazon did and the market share it captured as smaller competitors failed. Carlson argues this was by design rather than organic.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## CIA ties

The article states that one of [[amazon-web-services|Amazon Web Services]]' first major contracts was with the [[cia|CIA]]: in early 2013 Amazon was awarded a $600 million contract, and in August 2013 Bezos purchased *The Washington Post* — described in the article as a CIA-run newspaper — for $250 million.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) Amazon is grouped with the other mega-cap technology firms Carlson alleges answer to the CIA and serve as instruments of influence and propaganda.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Trump-era realignment

Amazon was among the big tech firms donating a million dollars to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s inaugural fund.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) Bezos has also forced Amazon employees back into the office, which Carlson credits with easing the commercial real estate "ticking time bomb" created by remote work and restoring in-person human interaction.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) Carlson concludes it is hard to imagine an America without Amazon, and that Trump regards such firms as necessary to the economy.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Labor unrest, 2023

Amazon appeared in Badlands Media's accounting of the 2023 wave of American labor actions, in which 84 Amazon workers walked off the job — among the smallest of the year's job actions, cited alongside 1,400 electrical workers, 3,000 Starbucks workers and 75,000 Kaiser Permanente employees as evidence of a broad surge in what Axios termed "worker activism."[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) Writing for Badlands, Ashe in America placed these scattered walkouts inside a larger argument that automation, robotics and artificial intelligence had already made much of the disputed labour redundant, so that even successful strikes would win only short-lived concessions.[[2]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Postal subsidy dispute

*A Week to Remember* recalls that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told Amazon to deliver its own packages, arguing that taxpayer money should not be used to subsidise the company through the [[united-states-postal-service|United States Postal Service]].[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember) [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] points to the number of Amazon delivery trucks now on the road and to the company's continued health as evidence the subsidy was never necessary.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)


## AI capital expenditure, 2026

Amazon was named alongside [[alphabet|Google]], Meta and [[microsoft|Microsoft]] among the American hyperscalers guiding toward $600–700 billion in combined capital expenditure for 2026, much of it directed at data-center and AI infrastructure, even as power constraints became apparent.[4] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited these figures as the U.S. counterpart to Beijing's [[china-ai-data-center-buildout|AI data center buildout]].[4]

## October 2024 nuclear investment

On Wednesday, October 16, 2024, Amazon announced that it was investing in [[small-modular-reactor|small nuclear reactors]], two days after a similar announcement by [[google|Google]], as both companies sought new sources of carbon-free electricity to meet surging demand from data centers and [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]].[5] The announcements came weeks after the owner of the shuttered [[three-mile-island-nuclear-power-plant|Three Mile Island nuclear power plant]] said it planned to restart the reactor so [[microsoft|Microsoft]] could buy the power for its data centers.[5] Amazon, like the other two firms, had previously invested in solar and wind generation but said it now needed to go further to meet both demand growth and its own emissions commitments.[5] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the "dueling" investments as evidence that the American oligarchy was being forced into a nuclear future rather than choosing one.[5]


## Zoox robotaxi recall (2025)

Amazon-owned robotaxi maker [[zoox|Zoox]] initiated a recall in May 2025 to update software in 270 driverless vehicles after an April 8 crash in Las Vegas involving an unoccupied robotaxi and a passenger car.[6] Nobody was hurt, and Zoox said the update addressed the "root cause" of the collision and had already been deployed.[6]

## FTC monopoly lawsuit (2023)

On 26 September 2023, the United States government and 17 states sued Amazon in a landmark monopolization case, reflecting years of allegations that the company had abused its economic dominance and harmed fair competition.[7] The suit, brought by the [[federal-trade-commission|Federal Trade Commission]] and 17 state attorneys general, was the government's sharpest attack yet on a company that began by selling books online and became "the everything store," building a globe-spanning logistics network and a major cloud computing business.[7] The 172-page complaint alleges Amazon unfairly promotes its own platform and services at the expense of third-party sellers, requires sellers to buy its in-house logistics services to obtain "Prime" eligibility, and forces sellers to list products on Amazon at the lowest prices available anywhere online — a practice already challenged in a separate suit by California's attorney general.[7] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] linked the case to the [[justice-department|Department of Justice]]'s parallel antitrust action against [[google|Google]] and argued that monopolistic market-makers had to be curtailed for a genuine free market to return.[7] See [[ftc-v-amazon-antitrust-lawsuit|FTC v. Amazon Antitrust Lawsuit]].[7]
