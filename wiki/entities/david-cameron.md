# David Cameron

David Cameron is a former British Prime Minister who later served as Foreign Secretary in the government of Rishi Sunak.[1] In that capacity he became a central figure in reporting about British pressure on the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] over its investigation of Israeli conduct in the Gaza conflict.[1]

## Reported pressure on the ICC

According to reporting by Middle East Eye, cited in the Badlands News Brief, Cameron threatened to defund the ICC and to withdraw the United Kingdom from the court's founding treaty — the [[rome-statute|Rome Statute]] — if Chief Prosecutor [[karim-khan|Karim Khan]] sought arrest warrants for Israeli leaders.[1] Middle East Eye reported, citing anonymous staffers familiar with the conversation, that Khan first reached out to inform London of his intentions in April of the preceding year, and that Cameron telephoned Khan to warn him that seeking the warrants would be "like dropping a hydrogen bomb."[1] Cameron reportedly promised that London would "defund the court and withdraw from the Rome Statute" if the warrants were issued.[1]

The ICC nonetheless issued warrants in November for the arrest of Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and then Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]], as well as three [[hamas|Hamas]] leaders, over alleged war crimes committed in Gaza.[1] Washington subsequently imposed sanctions on Khan, accusing him of abuse of power for pursuing cases against US allies.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the Cameron episode as one node in a "London – Ukraine – [[israel|Israel]]" pattern running through the day's stories, arguing that the same networks recur across each theatre.[1] The commentary framed the prospect of a GOP-led defunding of the UN over the warrants as an inversion of the plan that [[david-ben-gurion|David Ben-Gurion]] had described decades earlier, and suggested that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had "turned the system of systems against itself."[1]

## April 2024 Washington trip

In April 2024, as British Foreign Secretary, Lord Cameron travelled to Washington on a Monday evening and met Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] as well as Republican presidential candidate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[2] The UK government hoped Cameron could convince wavering Republicans to vote for a bill funding the war in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] after months of holdup in Congress; Cameron said that while he was not in the US to "lecture" politicians, he had arrived with a "plea" to continue American support for Kyiv.[2]

The trip was widely reported as a snub. Two of Cameron's planned meetings were cancelled after national security adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]] and House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] said they were too busy, with Sullivan replacing the planned meeting with a phone call citing "diary clashes" tied to a visit from the Japanese prime minister.[2] Badlands commentators noted that Cameron's meeting with Trump — described by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] as a meeting with "the real leader of America" — had rustled the establishment, and [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] suggested Sullivan avoided Cameron out of concern over what he called Trump honeypots.[2]
