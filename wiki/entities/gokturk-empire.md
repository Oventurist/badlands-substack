# GokTurk Empire

The GokTurk Empire was a vast Turkic khaganate that by 578 AD extended from Mongolia through Eurasia all the way to Crimea in today's Ukraine, despite having arisen only some thirty years earlier.[1] It played a major diplomatic role mediating relations between the Byzantine Empire — the surviving eastern Roman Empire — and the pre-Muslim Sassanid Persian Empire.[1]

Through a series of marriages with leading princesses of the short-lived Western Wei and Northern Zhou dynasties of China in 562 and 568, the leading Khagans of the GokTurks became instrumental in reviving the old [[silk-road|Silk Road]] corridors that had fallen into disarray with the collapse of the Han Dynasty around 200 AD.[1]

After the death of the fourth ruler, Taspar Khagan, in 586, warring factions vied for control and a civil war split the empire into eastern and western divisions.[1] Rival Chinese dynasties before the rise of the [[tang-dynasty|Tang]] in 618 contributed further to the disarray across Central and Eastern Asia.[1] The eastern branch was defeated by the Tang general Li Jing in 630 and the western Khaganate by the Taizong Emperor in 643, after which only the most western branch of the GokTurks remained — as [[khazaria|Khazaria]].[1] Scholars have noted the strong Confucian philosophy embedded in the western Turkish Khaganate, whose shamanistic rulers nonetheless held the Mandate of Heaven as a core belief before [[king-bulan|King Bulan]]'s conversion to Judaism around 750 CE.[1]
