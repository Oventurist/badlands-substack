# President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST)

The President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST) is a federal advisory body that provides the president with independent advice on science, technology, and innovation policy.[1] It operates under the Federal Advisory Committee Act (FACA) framework, meaning its work is structured to produce formal recommendations to the president.[1]

## March 2026 appointments

The [[white-house|White House]] announced a new slate of PCAST appointments on Wednesday, March 25, 2026, drawing members from academia, industry, and research institutions in a mix of public- and private-sector expertise.[1] Appointees were tasked with advising on artificial intelligence, national security technology, economic competitiveness, and scientific research priorities, and the announcement stated the council would help guide policy on emerging technologies and on maintaining US leadership in science and innovation.[1]

The announced appointees were [[marc-andreessen|Marc Andreessen]], Sergey Brin, Safra Catz, Michael Dell, Jacob DeWitte, Fred Ehrsam, [[larry-ellison|Larry Ellison]], David Friedberg, Jensen Huang, John Martinis, Bob Mumgaard, Lisa Su, and [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]].[1]

## Reaction

Badlands contributor Ashe in America observed that the list would give "Thiel Doomers" — those who believe [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]] is shadow-running the government through a network of insider placements — ample material, characterizing roughly half the appointees (Zuckerberg, Andreessen, Ehrsam, Ellison, Friedberg, Huang) as Thiel-aligned and the remainder as not.[1] She singled out Zuckerberg's inclusion as difficult to reconcile with the unresolved controversy over the Center for Tech and Civic Life, arguing that "public trust is conditioned on accountability."[1]

Her broader response held two positions in tension: that if power is genuinely returned to the People then it falls to the People to refuse technocracy, and that saving the nation "at the enterprise level" may require committed patriots operating a sophisticated plan.[1] She likened the announcement to "the birth of Westworld, and its infamous board of governors," and closed by urging readers to keep "eyes and minds open, and brains on."[1]
