# Montpelier

Montpelier is the Virginia estate of President [[james-madison|James Madison]], operated as a historic site in partnership with the [[national-trust-for-historic-preservation|National Trust for Historic Preservation]].[1]

## 2023 "woke exhibits" dispute

In May 2023, two Republican lawmakers — Reps. [[bob-good|Bob Good]] of Virginia and [[eli-crane|Eli Crane]] of Arizona — demanded answers about "woke" influences at Montpelier and other historical sites, flagging concerns over how exhibits discuss slavery and the country's founding documents and asking whether federal funds had been used.[1] Their letter was addressed to Jay Clemens, interim president of the National Trust, and was obtained by the [[washington-examiner|Washington Examiner]].[1]

The lawmakers said the "sole exhibit focused on the Constitution" perpetuated the notion that it is a "pro-slavery document," and that "the only exhibit for children aims to teach them about race and slavery and includes a children's book with graphic images and 'imagination exercises' for whites and blacks that encourages children to imagine themselves as the aggressor and to examine their own capacity for evil."[1] They also questioned the lack of exhibits on Madison's achievements and on the importance of the Constitution, and the use of "restricted funds" for operating expenses.[1]

The letter cited Washington Examiner reporting that Montpelier's director of archaeology, [[matthew-reeves|Matthew Reeves]], "said something to the effect that staff members had no interest in honoring a 'dead white president and a dead white president's Constitution,'" and that he had described acting "less like a bulldozer and more like a termite that undermined a building's foundation, destroying it from within before tearing it down."[1]

## Commentary

Writing for Badlands Media, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] opened her commentary with George Orwell's line from *1984*, "History has stopped. Nothing exists except an endless present in which the Party is always right," and argued that the rewrite of American history was already underway at Montpelier.[1] She contended that lessons taught in schools were being reinforced at American historical sites, described the exhibits as "[[critical-race-theory|CRT]] on steroids," and urged readers to contact legislators, asking why only two US legislators were pushing back.[1]
