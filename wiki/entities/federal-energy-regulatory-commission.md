# Federal Energy Regulatory Commission

The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) is the United States federal regulator responsible for approving arrangements governing the interstate electric grid and the terms on which large customers connect to it.[1] In early 2025 the commission became the decisive venue in a dispute between technology companies seeking to plug data centers directly into power plants and utilities objecting that such deals shift grid costs onto everyone else.[1]

## Amazon–Susquehanna case

Front and center before the commission was the data center that [[amazon|Amazon]]'s cloud computing subsidiary [[amazon-web-services|Amazon Web Services]] is building next to the Susquehanna nuclear plant in eastern Pennsylvania.[1] The arrangement between the plant's owners and AWS — known as a "behind the meter" connection — was the first of its kind to come before FERC.[1] The commission rejected a deal that could eventually have sent 960 megawatts, about 40 percent of the plant's capacity, to the data center, an amount sufficient to power more than half a million homes.[1] See [[behind-the-meter-data-center-power-deals|behind-the-meter data center power deals]].[1]

Federal regulators were described as trying to determine quickly how to handle the broader question of whether diverting power to higher-paying customers leaves enough for others, and whether it is fair to excuse large power users from paying for the grid.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands commentator Ashe in America characterized the regulatory question as one with the potential to "unleash the golden age," observing that utilities occupy a nuanced regulatory position because of the essential services they provide and are in effect the gatekeepers of energy.[1] She argued that in an increasingly digital world those statutory definitions and protected relationships may no longer make sense, that technology companies say as much while regulators remain unconvinced, and predicted that the technology companies would prevail "one way or another."[1]
