# New York Custom House

The United States Custom House, sometimes referred to as the New York Custom House, was the place where the United States Customs Service collected federal customs duties on imported goods within New York City.[1]

In the 19th century the Port of New York was the primary port of entry for goods reaching the United States, making its custom house the most important in the country; in 1853 it collected almost 75 percent of all customs revenue in the nation.[1] Until the passage of the Sixteenth Amendment in 1913, which instituted a national income tax, the New York Custom House supplied two-thirds of the federal government's revenue.[1] The amount of money passing through it made working there a prime position, and corruption was widespread, with estimates that upwards of $25 million a year was lost in revenue.[1]

Joe Lange argues that [[citibank|Citibank]], then the biggest bank in New York City, controlled the politicians who appointed the customs house's leadership, and that whoever controls customs houses controls international trade and enormous sums of money.[1] He connects this to the customs houses seized in [[haiti|Haiti]] during the American occupation, and to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s later focus on crooked trade deals and the graft hidden within them.[1]

The customs house was the stronghold of Senator [[roscoe-conkling|Roscoe Conkling]], whose protégé [[chester-arthur|Chester Arthur]] ran it as the highest-paid federal employee.[1] President [[james-garfield|James Garfield]]'s attempt to strengthen federal authority over it preceded his assassination.[1]
