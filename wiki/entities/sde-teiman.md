# Sde Teiman

Sde Teiman is an Israeli military detention facility used to hold detainees captured during the war in Gaza.[1] It became internationally known as the site of an alleged sexual assault on a male Palestinian prisoner by [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] soldiers, an incident that generated a prolonged legal and political controversy inside [[israel|Israel]].[1]

## The abuse case

Israeli authorities opened an investigation after video footage of the alleged abuse surfaced, leading to the arrest of several soldiers and triggering public debate inside Israel about the treatment of Palestinian detainees.[1] Badlands Media's account, drawing on leaked footage and official reporting, described a group of IDF soldiers dragging male Palestinian prisoners into a corner of the detention facility and assaulting them while shielding themselves from security cameras with ballistic shields.[1] Official reports cited in the coverage stated that the Palestinian man shown in the video was sodomized with objects, including a knife, resulting in serious internal injuries requiring surgery — conduct documented on security camera footage, in IDF military police arrest reports, and in hospital medical records.[1]

## Charges dropped

In March 2026, Israeli military prosecutors dropped charges against the soldiers, citing evidentiary issues after months of investigation.[1] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was reported to have welcomed the decision.[1] Badlands commentary emphasized that the case had been heavily documented and that the charges were nevertheless withdrawn.[1]

## Political fallout

The arrests provoked demonstrations by Israeli settlers, led by members of the [[knesset|Knesset]] and National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]], who stormed the base holding the accused soldiers.[1] The multi-day demonstrations became known as the "Right to Rape" protests, with the underlying argument that Palestinians are sub-human.[1] Israeli media turned the four accused soldiers into public figures; only one, [[meir-shitrit|Meir Shitrit]], appeared on camera unmasked, on Israel Channel 14, while the other three appeared on primetime television with their identities concealed.[1] A pundit on Israel Channel 12 stated, "I told my friends that I don't give a rat's ass what they did to a guy like that."[1] The Knesset subsequently debated publicly whether inserting sharp objects into detainees' bodies should be lawful.[1]
