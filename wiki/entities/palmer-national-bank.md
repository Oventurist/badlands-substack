# Palmer National Bank

Palmer National Bank was a Washington, D.C. institution set up in 1983, as the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan scandal]] was heating up, by [[stefan-halper|Stefan Halper]] — policy director for [[george-hw-bush|George Bush]]'s 1980 presidential bid — and [[harvey-mclean-jr|Harvey McLean Jr.]], southern finance chairman on the same campaign and a former Dallas real estate developer.[1] The seed money, $2.8 million, came from [[herman-k-beebe|Herman K. Beebe]], the Louisiana figure with alleged Mafia connections responsible for roughly $10 billion in S&L fraud.[1]

One of the established accounts at Palmer belonged to the [[national-endowment-for-the-preservation-of-liberty|National Endowment for the Preservation of Liberty]] (NEPL), eventually found to be the front organisation for [[oliver-north|Oliver North]], who helped run the [[iran-contra-affair|Iran-Contra]] arms funding deal under the nose of President [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]] and [[congress|Congress]].[1] It was through Palmer that money was channelled to North's Swiss bank account, and through Palmer that [[carl-channell|Carl Channell]] — one of the few private citizens convicted in the scandal — obtained a $650,000 "loan" to purchase weapons for the Nicaraguan Contras.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] concludes that the bank, started by friends of George H.W. Bush, helped fund the [[shadow-government|shadow government]] and its covert operations beyond congressional control or oversight — consistent with the broader claim that the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] used thrift [[deregulation|deregulation]] to pay for activities Congress would not support.[1] Lange also notes that Halper is the same figure who later tried to frame Trump with Russian collusion accusations, and asks whether that is a coincidence.[1]
