# Vladimir Putin

Vladimir Putin is the Russian president whose decision to invade Ukraine the article argues has never been adequately explained to Western audiences.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) Badlands notes that Putin was described by [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and every subsequent U.S. president as rational, and questions whether the public should accept that he "suddenly turned into a psychopath over night," attacking Ukraine for no reason and planning to march across Western Europe attacking NATO countries next.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

The article's counter-thesis is that propagandized real-life supervillains do not spring up out of nowhere; there are always motivating factors, and those factors are almost always suppressed before they reach Western audiences.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) In Russia's case, the suppressed context is a nation that watched U.S.-sponsored regime change in neighboring countries despite promises to the contrary, followed by arms shipments to those neighbors and insistence that [[nato|NATO]] would expand to include Ukraine — a state occupying Russia's most sensitive border, the same border that played a critical role in how Russia was attacked in both world wars.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

Badlands dates the breaking point to 24 February 2022, when Putin initiated what Russia calls the special military operation in Ukraine, after Washington had for years steadily applied pressure it knew would produce that outcome.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The 2008 cable of [[william-burns|William Burns]] emphasized that opposition to Ukrainian NATO membership was the redline of the Russian elite generally, "not just Putin."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

In "Are CBDCs The Path To Fear, or Freedom?", Putin is quoted from a Sberbank artificial-intelligence conference pushing back on international sanctions and proposing a digital currency payment system for cross-border transactions independent of banks, analogous to the hawala remittance network.[4] "We are all well aware that in today's illegitimate restrictions, one of the lines of attack is settlements," he said, adding that the international payment system is expensive and that its correspondent accounts and regulation are "controlled by a narrow club of states and financial groups… In fact, they are the masters of life. ## Far Eastern Vision

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes Putin's Far Eastern Vision as a 21st-century "Russian Manifest Destiny" aimed at extending civilization into the Siberian Far East and Arctic and beyond Central Asia, Mongolia, Japan and China, converging since 2018 with the [[polar-silk-road|Polar Silk Road]].[5] Its obstacles are monetarist: Russia's privatized central bank, established in its current form in 1990, retains deep-seated structural ties to the [[imf|IMF]], the WTO and liberal ideologues who ensure that "balanced budgets" and free markets take precedence over the emission of productive credit, unlike China's state-owned banking system.[5]

Despite these blocks, Ehret writes, the vision has begun to spring to life through Sergei Shoigu's "grand masterplan for Siberia," starting with five new cities housing 500,000 to a million citizens each, alongside modernization and double-tracking of the 9,300 km Trans-Siberian Railway and the 4,300 km Baikal-Amur Mainline and the expansion of the International North South Transportation Corridor from Moscow to India.[5] Russia's Development Plan for the Northern Sea Route, adopted in 2019, targets 80 million tons of annual shipments by 2024 and 40 new vessels including nuclear icebreakers.[5] Ehret notes that Putin's repeated offers on the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait Tunnel]] — advanced in 2007 and again in 2011 — were ignored by both Bush Jr. and [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[5]

Reuters reported that "Russian hardliners" — including figures identified as the nationalist Konstantin Malofeyev and war blogger Boris Rozhin — reacted strongly to the strike, with some who had previously viewed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] favorably now describing him as a threat, and some hawks urging the Kremlin to abandon US-mediated Ukraine peace talks and escalate the war effort.[8]

## Navy Day remarks on maritime piracy (July 2026)

During Russia's Navy Day events in late July 2026, Putin convened his naval commanders and said Russian authorities should respond decisively to attempts to seize Russian vessels while acting within international maritime law, comparing such situations to efforts against piracy.[9] He described the incidents as "attempts by ill-intentioned parties to pressure our fleet, including the commercial fleet," adding that "in such cases, of course, we must act cautiously, within the framework of international maritime law, but decisively – just as piracy and pirates must be fought."[9] The remarks followed Ukrainian strikes on cargo shipping attributed to [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] across the Black, Mediterranean and Caspian Seas, and came as Moscow continued to criticize Western enforcement actions targeting ships associated with Russian trade under international sanctions.[9]

Russian officials said Putin and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] held a roughly 90-minute telephone conversation on July 4, 2026.[10] Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Trump expressed a willingness to work toward ending the fighting in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and finding solutions to the conflict, described the call as "business-like and quite constructive," and said [[russia|Russia]] continues to seek a political-diplomatic resolution while maintaining its stated positions.[10] Putin also renewed an invitation for Trump to visit Russia for further bilateral discussions, a detail attributed to reporting referenced from Axios.[10] Separately, [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] said he spoke with Trump and called the conversation "very good" in a Telegram message, citing a "real prospect" of ending the war and thanking the United States for military and political support including Javelin and Patriot systems.[10]

## Beijing visit (May 2026)

The Kremlin announced that Putin would visit Beijing on 19–20 May 2026 for talks with [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]], coinciding with the 25th anniversary of the 2001 Sino-Russian Treaty of Friendship and focused on bilateral relations, economic cooperation, and international and regional issues.[13] The trip came less than a week after U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] concluded a state visit to [[china|China]], a sequence Chinese state media described as unusual and indicative of Beijing's growing diplomatic influence.[13]

## April 2026 call with Trump

On 29 April 2026 Putin held a phone call with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] lasting roughly 90 minutes, described by both sides as "productive" and "businesslike."[14] The leaders discussed both the [[russian-invasion-of-ukraine|Ukraine war]] and the ongoing [[iran-war|Iran conflict]], with Putin proposing a temporary ceasefire in Ukraine tied to Russia's May 9 Victory Day celebrations.[14] Trump said he encouraged Putin to end the Ukraine war first, despite Putin offering assistance on issues related to Iran's nuclear program.[14] Putin also raised concerns about escalation in Iran and warned of broader global consequences if the conflict expanded further.[14] Major disagreements remained, particularly over territorial demands in Ukraine and the conditions for any lasting ceasefire.[14]

## Beijing summit and multipolar declaration (May 2026)

On May 20, 2026, Putin and Chinese President [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] signed a joint declaration in Beijing calling for a "[[multipolar-world|multipolar world]]" and criticizing what they described as destabilizing US foreign and nuclear policies.[16] The summit produced more than 40 agreements covering trade, technology, energy, media cooperation, and strategic coordination between Russia and China.[16]

The declaration framed the two states as defenders of a new international order less dominated by the United States and Western alliances; both leaders condemned "hegemonism," warned against a return to "the law of the jungle," criticized US missile-defense initiatives including President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s proposed [[golden-dome|Golden Dome]] system, and objected to the expiration of key nuclear arms limitations between Washington and Moscow.[16] The meeting followed days after Trump's own visit to Beijing.[16]

The summit also exposed limits in the partnership: Putin reportedly failed to secure final Chinese approval for the [[power-of-siberia-2|Power of Siberia 2]] gas pipeline, a project Moscow views as critical for redirecting energy exports away from Europe, with pricing and long-term dependency concerns unresolved.[16] Badlands contributor Burning Bright treated the declaration as public confirmation of the theorized [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] linking Trump, Xi, and Putin.[16]

On June 12, 2026 — Russia Day, the anniversary of the country's 1990 declaration of sovereignty — Putin stated that Russia is standing alone against the entire "[[collective-west|Collective West]]" in the Ukraine conflict.[18] He asserted that no external force can impose a strategic defeat on Moscow, described the war as highly technological, and said all NATO nations were uniformly intensifying efforts to act against Russia.[18]

In September 2024 Putin said that Western approval for Kyiv to use longer-range weapons against Russian targets would mean [[nato|NATO]] was "at war" with Russia.[22] "This would in a significant way change the very nature of the conflict," he told a state television reporter, adding that "it would mean that NATO countries, the US, European countries, are at war with Russia," and that "if that's the case, then taking into account the change of nature of the conflict, we will take the appropriate decisions based on the threats that we will face."[22] Clearing Kyiv to strike deep into Russia, he said, "is a decision on whether NATO countries are directly involved in the military conflict or not."[22]

The remarks came as U.S. and U.K. diplomats — Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] and Foreign Secretary [[david-lammy|David Lammy]] — discussed easing restrictions on Western weapons, more than two and a half years into the conflict and as Russian forces advanced in eastern Ukraine.[22] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] interpreted the statement as a warning that Russia would respond by striking the West, and speculated, given what he described as Putin's regard for civilian life and his relationship with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], that any such response would exclusively target Deep State leadership.[22] The same commentary noted assassination attempts against Putin, [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]], and Trump, and the successful assassination of [[shinzo-abe|Shinzo Abe]], as context.[22]

## September 2023 comments on NATO expansion

In September 2023, NATO Secretary General [[jens-stoltenberg|Jens Stoltenberg]] told the European Parliament's foreign affairs committee that Putin had declared in the autumn of 2021 and sent a draft treaty asking NATO to promise no further enlargement as a precondition for not invading Ukraine.[23] According to Stoltenberg, Putin also wanted NATO to remove military infrastructure from every ally that had joined since 1997 — effectively half the alliance across Central and Eastern Europe — a demand the alliance rejected as creating second-class membership.[23] Stoltenberg summarised the outcome by saying Putin "went to war to prevent NATO, more NATO, close to his borders."[23]

The same month, NATO announced the [[steadfast-defender-exercise|Steadfast Defender]] exercise, a drill of more than 40,000 troops modelled on repelling a Russian-led coalition; commentary at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterised it as a provocation aimed at Putin that he was unlikely to answer kinetically.[23]

## Prigozhin plane crash aftermath (2023)

Following the August 2023 plane crash in Russia's Tver region that was reported to have killed Wagner chief [[yevgeny-prigozhin|Yevgeny Prigozhin]], Russian authorities staged a funeral that Putin declined to attend.[24] Russian political analyst [[valery-solovey|Dr Valery Solovey]] claimed that Prigozhin had in fact survived an assassination attempt sanctioned by Putin and drawn up by his security council, that a body double had been killed in his place, and that Russian officials were lying about DNA identification at the crash site.[24] Solovey asserted Prigozhin was "alive, well, and free" abroad and plotting revenge against Putin.[24]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] cautioned that Solovey had a long record of unsupported claims about Putin — including that he was terminally ill, in "very poor physiological health," unable "to adequately assess the situation and make proper decisions," and intent on demonstrating tactical nuclear weapons — and that these should temper any confidence in the Prigozhin assertion.[24]

## 2024 Russian presidential election

Vladimir Putin secured a fifth term as Russia's president in the March 2024 election, winning 87% of the vote according to an exit poll broadcast on state television at the close of three days of voting, and exceeding the 77% he received in 2018.[25] Three other candidates, all from parties loyal to the Kremlin, took no more than 5% support between them, and nearly 4.5 million people voted online in a system used in 29 of Russia's regions for the first time in a presidential election.[25] Preliminary turnout reported by the Central Election Commission was 74.22% — the highest since Boris Yeltsin became president in 1991 after the Soviet Union's collapse, and well above the 67.5% recorded in 2018 — with at least six regions claiming turnout above 90%.[25] The commission also reported that Putin received 94.12% of votes after processing 100% of protocols in the Lugansk People's Republic, in the contested Donetsk region.[25]

Completing the six-year term would make Putin Russia's longest-serving ruler, surpassing Joseph Stalin, at the age of 77.[25] Coverage cited by Badlands framed the win as extending his nearly quarter-century rule at a moment when Russian troops were on the offensive in the third year of the invasion of [[ukraine|Ukraine]], with Kyiv struggling to supply its forces amid delays in military aid from its US and European allies.[25]

## March 2025 call with Trump on a Ukraine ceasefire

Putin held a highly anticipated telephone call with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on Tuesday, March 18, 2025, after [[ukraine|Ukraine]] had agreed the previous week to a U.S.-proposed 30-day ceasefire in the war with Moscow.[56] According to the Kremlin's readout, Putin rejected that full 30-day proposal but responded positively to Trump's suggestion of a more limited 30-day ceasefire covering energy facilities on both sides of the conflict.[56]

Putin laid down steep conditions for broader peace talks, including the "complete cessation of foreign military assistance and the provision of intelligence information to Kyiv."[56] Neither side mentioned any demand that Ukraine cede territory to Russia, something the Trump administration had suggested was inevitable in an eventual peace deal.[56] The two leaders expressed mutual interest in future economic cooperation, and the United States said negotiations toward a broader ceasefire and peace deal would begin immediately in the Middle East.[56] Both sides announced that further Russia–U.S. talks were coming soon, including an agreement to restore safety of navigation in the Black Sea.[56]

Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] had confirmed the conversation on the Monday beforehand; it was the second call between the two leaders since Trump took office two months earlier.[56] German tabloid Bild reported that EU leaders were "trembling" ahead of the call, which it said would address "the future of Ukraine and possibly also the future of Europe, with potentially devastating consequences for the security of our continent," producing "a mood of alarm in Europe's capitals."[56]

Badlands commentary framed the exchange as a demonstration of a "Sovereign Alliance" emerging from the shadows while the "Old Guard" of former colonial powers found itself neutered; [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] dubbed it "the Quid-Bro-Quo phone call," and Burning Bright described the branding of the call around "Normalization and Peace" as a masterstroke of narrative seeding.[56]

## Strategic partnership law with Venezuela (2025)

## September 2024: Kursk, Harris and the Eastern Economic Forum

Speaking during a school visit in early September 2024, Putin said of the Ukrainian [[kursk-incursion|incursion into Kursk]] that "the enemy did not achieve the main task that they set themselves: to stop our offensive in the Donbas," adding that Russian forces had not sustained such a pace of advance in the Donbas for a long time.[78] Badlands commentary held that missile strikes on Russian population centres in the Kursk area, killing more than thirty and injuring more than 140, had infuriated Putin — who was said to have prioritised the preservation of Ukrainian civilian life throughout the special military operation — and led him to conclude that peace could no longer be negotiated with Kiev.[78] Putin also alleged that NATO was attempting to strike the [[kursk-nuclear-power-plant|Kurskaya nuclear power plant]] deep inside Russian territory.[78]

At the [[eastern-economic-forum|Eastern Economic Forum]] in Vladivostok on September 5, 2024, Putin joked that Russia would support [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] in the U.S. presidential election, citing her "infectious" laugh: "she laughs so expressively and infectiously that it means that everything is fine with her."[78] He framed the remark as following [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s recommendation that his supporters back Harris, and concluded that "ultimately, the choice is up to the American people, and we will respect that choice."[78] The comment came a day after the U.S. [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] charged two Russian media executives over an alleged illegal scheme to influence the November election, and echoed his earlier ironic preference for Biden over [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as a more predictable "old school" politician; U.S. intelligence agencies maintained that Moscow actually favoured Trump.[78] Badlands writers read the remark as trolling that mirrored Trump's own comments on the Biden-to-Harris candidate switch, and [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Putin as "the main geopolitical protagonist" until Trump's return.[78]

At the same forum Putin thanked Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] for his role in arranging the [[2024-us-russia-prisoner-swap|largest US–Russia prisoner exchange since the Cold War]], and thanked Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] for providing the venue.[78]

## Defense of Steve Witkoff (November 2025)

On November 27, 2025, Putin publicly defended [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s special envoy, against accusations of pro-Kremlin bias, calling him "an intelligent man" who was properly representing the position of his country and its leader.[79] The defense followed Bloomberg News's publication of a transcript of a leaked telephone call between Witkoff and Kremlin foreign policy aide [[yuri-ushakov|Yuri Ushakov]], which critics used to portray the envoy as one-sided toward Moscow.[79]

Speaking at a news conference in Kyrgyzstan during a state visit, Putin observed that "Mr. Witkoff is clearly traveling to Moscow on President Trump's orders to negotiate with us," and that given the assignment, "it would probably be surprising if he cursed us with obscenities in his conversations with Ushakov."[79] Witkoff had met Putin several times in Moscow and was scheduled to return the following week to continue negotiations over Trump's peace proposal for [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[79] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that Putin, like Trump, had signaled that peace would be reached only at the "final stages."[79]

## Italian reassurance over drone claims, September 2025

Italian Foreign Minister [[antonio-tajani|Antonio Tajani]] said on Sunday 28 September 2025 that Putin was not interested in igniting a global conflict, responding to claims that Moscow could send drones against [[italy|Italy]].[81] "I don't think Putin wants to start World War III, so I want to reassure all Italians," Tajani said, adding that he did not believe Putin wanted to send drones to swarm the country.[81] The statement came a day after Ukraine's [[volodymyr-zelensky|Vladimir Zelensky]] claimed Russia could target Italy or other European nations with drone attacks, and in a month when [[poland|Poland]] and Estonia had accused Moscow of breaching their airspace — accusations Russia dismissed as unfounded.[81]

Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] argued that the drones allegedly found on Polish territory could not have been launched from Russia: "If they are the ones we are thinking of, then their flight range is shorter than the distance from the border of the Russian Federation to the border with Poland," he said on the sidelines of the 80th session of the UN General Assembly.[81] Polish Foreign Minister [[radoslaw-sikorski|Radoslaw Sikorski]] had warned that any intrusions would be met with force.[81] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] took Lavrov's point to imply that the drones penetrating Polish airspace may not have been Russian at all, or that the story may not have been real.[81]

## Trump's March 2025 comments and the Russia-US track

In early March 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told Americans to worry less about Putin and more about domestic issues such as migrant crime, writing on Truth Social, "We should spend less time worrying about Putin, and more time worrying about migrant rape gangs, drug lords, murderers, and people from mental institutions entering our Country – So that we don't end up like Europe!"[82] Recent Russia-US talks initiated by Putin and Trump, followed by Ukrainian leader [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]]'s disastrous White House appearance, triggered concern among Kyiv's backers that Trump's approach could benefit Russia more than Ukraine.[82] Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] commended Trump's approach, noting that it "largely aligns with our vision."[82]

## Call with Trump on the Ukraine war (May 2025)

Putin spoke with US President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] by telephone for over two hours on Monday, May 19, 2025, in a conversation focused primarily on the [[russia-ukraine-war|Ukraine conflict]].[83] Trump said the call "went very well" and announced that negotiations between Moscow and Kiev would begin "immediately," while Putin described the exchange as "very useful."[83] Writing on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] shortly afterward, Trump characterized the tone and spirit of the conversation as "excellent" and said, "If it wasn't, I would say so now, rather than later."[83]

Trump stated that "Russia and Ukraine will immediately start negotiations toward a Ceasefire and, more importantly, an END to the War," adding that the conditions "will be negotiated between the two parties, as it can only be, because they know details of a negotiation that nobody else would be aware of."[83] Badlands commentary observed that the [[vatican|Vatican]] had expressed strong interest in hosting the talks, with contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry writing that Trump had selected the Vatican as the stage for a meeting with Putin, and [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] offering a satirical "behind the scenes look" at the call.[83]

## February 2025 call with Trump

On Wednesday, February 12, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] held a call with Putin that CNN characterized as one of two "geopolitical thunderclaps" transforming transatlantic relations, bringing the Russian leader "in from the cold" as the two hatched plans to end the war in Ukraine and agreed to swap presidential visits.[84] CNN cautioned that Trump's detailed strategy remained opaque, that the dashing of many of [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]]'s aspirations meant Kyiv's agreement to any Putin-Trump deal could not be taken for granted, and that after steady battlefield gains there was no certainty Putin was as desperate for a swift settlement as Trump, who had long craved a Nobel Peace Prize.[84]

The outlet suggested the emerging framework resembled the partition of Germany after World War II, with Russian-occupied territory frozen under Moscow's control and the rest of Ukraine remaining a democracy behind a hard border, possibly with its western edge admitted to the European Union — but without US troops as guarantors.[84] Badlands commentary from [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] separately noted that [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] was preparing to host Trump and Putin at a highly anticipated peace summit.[84]

## Response to Operation Spider's Web (June 2025)

## "Illegitimate Kiev regime" remarks

Putin also said in June 2025 that, facing heavy battlefield losses, the "illegitimate Kiev regime" had resorted to organising terrorist attacks to intimidate Russia.[85] He described recent acts of railway sabotage in Russia's Bryansk and Kursk Regions as "undoubtedly a terrorist act," adding that "the decisions to carry out such crimes were, of course, made in Ukraine" by the political leadership.[85] "The strike on the civilian population was intentional," he said. [[badlands-media|Badlands]] contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the remarks "very based" but contended that the Kiev regime had always been a terrorist organisation because it was created by the [[cia|CIA]] in 2014, and argued that Putin would destroy rather than make peace with it.[85]


## 2023 BRICS Johannesburg address

Putin did not attend the [[2023-brics-johannesburg-summit|2023 BRICS summit]] in person; Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] took his place in the leaders' family photo because the International Criminal Court arrest warrant and the Rome Statute would have required South Africa to seek his arrest.[86] Speaking remotely in Russian, he said [[de-dollarization|de-dollarization]] was "gaining momentum" and that the dollar's receding global centrality was an "objective and irreversible" process.[86] He claimed the five [[brics|BRICS]] members were becoming the new world economic leaders with 26% of global GDP — 31% of the global economy by purchasing power parity, ahead of the [[g7|G7]]'s 30% — and cited a sixfold rise in mutual investment over a decade, doubled outward investment, and 20% of global exports.[86] He attacked "illegitimate sanctions" and the "unlawful freezing of sovereign states' assets," blamed international food shortages on Western sanctions, and said Russia was consistently increasing fuel, food and fertilizer supplies to the Global South.[86]

## Woodward's *War* and post-presidency contacts with Trump

Journalist [[bob-woodward|Bob Woodward]]'s book *War*, details of which emerged on October 8, 2024, reported that Putin had remained in contact with former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] after Trump left office, with an unnamed Trump aide cited as saying the two had "maybe as many as seven" conversations since 2021.[87] The book also said Trump secretly sent Putin scarce COVID-19 testing equipment for his personal use early in the pandemic — described by CNN as Abbott Point of Care machines and by the New York Times as "rare" tests — and quoted Putin as telling Trump, "Please don't tell anybody you sent these to me," out of apparent concern that the gesture could damage Trump politically.[87] Trump campaign spokesman [[steven-cheung|Steven Cheung]] dismissed the book's claims as "made up stories."[87]

Separately, Putin's envoy for Afghanistan [[zamir-kabulov|Zamir Kabulov]] announced in the same period that Russia had made a decision in principle to remove the [[taliban|Taliban]] from its list of terrorist organizations.[87]

Overnight into Thursday, April 24, 2025, Russia launched one of the most devastating bombardments of Ukraine's major cities since the war began, killing at least 12 people — hours after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he believed he had struck deals with both sides to end the war.[88] Trump responded on [[truth-social|Truth Social]]: "Vladimir, STOP!" adding, "I am not happy with the Russian strikes on KYIV. Not necessary, and very bad timing... 5000 soldiers a week are dying. Lets get the Peace Deal DONE!"[88] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Putin as long the most direct leader in what he calls the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], arguing the Russian people are further along an awakening pathway than Americans, and said Trump was rapidly pushing the Overton Window as narratives converged.[88]

The same week, Italian media reported that Putin would not face arrest in Italy under the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] warrant issued against him and [[maria-lvova-belova|Maria Lvova-Belova]] in 2023 over the alleged unlawful deportation of children from former Ukrainian territories, because Italian officials never completed the validating procedures.[88] Moscow does not recognize the court's jurisdiction and has declared the warrants null and void.[88]

Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] told the French magazine Le Point that Putin respects Trump as a strong personality open to productive dialogue, noting that both leaders "understand the absurdity of refusing dialogue" — something Peskov said was missing with the previous Biden administration.[88]

## Suspension of New START (February 2023)

On February 21, 2023, Putin announced a freeze on Russian participation in the [[new-start-treaty|New START]] nuclear arms control treaty during a speech to both houses of the Russian parliament, in which he also repeated accusations that the West was seeking to destroy Russia.[89] Moscow said that Russia would nevertheless continue to observe the treaty's limits on the number of nuclear warheads it may deploy.[89] Later the same day Putin submitted a draft law on the suspension to the [[state-duma|State Duma]]; speaker [[vyacheslav-volodin|Vyacheslav Volodin]] said the chamber would consider it the next day and take an immediate decision.[89]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] read the announcement as posturing and "more theater than anything else," judging an actual nuclear attack unlikely while arguing that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had undone fifty years of diplomacy.[89] He speculated that if [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] were to broker a deal pulling the parties back from nuclear brinkmanship, it would effectively secure his 2024 campaign.[89]

## Meeting with King Hamad of Bahrain, May 2024

Putin met Bahraini King [[hamad-bin-isa-al-khalifa|Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa]] in Moscow on May 23, 2024, where the two agreed to step up trade relations and to work together on resolving the crisis in the Middle East.[90] After a two-hour conversation they signed documents on pharmaceutical and medical cooperation, transport and transit, cultural exchange and environmental memoranda.[90] Putin said trade between Russia and [[bahrain|Bahrain]] was "at an early stage, but the trends are positive," while investment was better, with about 30 large projects worth roughly $500 million underway.[90] Hamad praised Putin's "wise policy" and acknowledged "Russia's role in the fair resolution of Arab issues," and Putin invited Bahrain to the upcoming [[brics|BRICS]] summit.[90] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the meeting as Putin "playing Peacemaker" within the emerging [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], with Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] looming off-screen, and argued that while the hegemony calls for war, leaders such as Putin, Xi and MBS sue for peace as a multipolar world emerges.[90]

## December 2025 Moscow talks with US envoys
