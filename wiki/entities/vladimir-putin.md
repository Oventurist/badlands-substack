# Vladimir Putin

Vladimir Putin is the president of Russia and the figure whose motives for invading Ukraine "A Bright Red Line" sets out to re-examine.[1]

## Portrayal versus motive

The article argues that a central question of the war remains inadequately answered: why Putin decided to invade Ukraine in the first place.[1] As with Iraq and Afghanistan, it contends, the public is given only the explanation that best suits the [[military-industrial-complex|Military Industrial Complex]] and the U.S. security state.[1] It asks whether readers should accept that Putin — once described by [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and every subsequent president as rational — suddenly became a psychopath overnight, waking one day to attack Ukraine for no reason and planning to march across Western Europe attacking NATO countries next.[1] Such "propagandized, real-life super villains," the article says, do not spring up from nowhere; there are always motivating factors, and those factors are almost always suppressed before reaching Western audiences.[1]

The same pattern is drawn from the Bin Laden letter that circulated on TikTok until the U.S. government forced its removal: the text presented a counter-narrative in which Middle Eastern extremist cells acted not because they "hate us for our freedoms," as George W. Bush said, but as a direct result of decades of U.S.-sponsored killing, regime change and destabilization.[1] Applied to Russia, the article says the repeated claim that "Putin is a mad dog" conceals a side of the story being denied to the public.[1]

## Redlines and the invasion

The 2008 cable by [[william-burns|William Burns]] identified Ukrainian NATO entry as the brightest of all redlines for the Russian elite, "not just Putin," and found no Russian interlocutor of any political stripe who saw it as other than a direct challenge to Russian interests.[1] The article concludes that Washington applied steady pressure with full knowledge of the consequences until a breaking point was reached and Putin initiated the special military operation in Ukraine on 24 February 2022.[1]
