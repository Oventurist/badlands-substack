# Sergei Millian

Sergei Millian is a Belarusian-American businessman who figures in the prosecution of [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]] as the person Danchenko told the [[fbi|FBI]] had been the source of key allegations later folded into the [[steele-dossier|Steele reporting]].[1] Four of the five counts brought by Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] concerned Danchenko's statements about Millian.[1]

## Identification as "Source E"

Dossier report 2016/95, which the prosecution designated Government Exhibit 109, attributes information to "Source E," identified as Millian, and a substantial part of the pre-trial argument on the opening day of the trial concerned how much of that [[dossier-report-2016-95|report]] should be admitted and whether Danchenko had attributed all or only part of its content to Source E.[1] The defence contended that the interviewing agents had not been clear enough in their questioning, framing the case as one in which Danchenko gave truthful answers to ambiguous questions.[1]

## Background established at trial

Under questioning by Durham, FBI Supervisory Intelligence Analyst [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]] testified that Millian had at one time been a Confidential Human Source for the Bureau's Atlanta field office — a disclosure the Badlands account treated as a bombshell.[1] Durham further established that Millian was president of the Russian American Chamber of Commerce, that he was a supporter of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and that a file had been opened on him and an investigation conducted that closed without charges.[1] Auten confirmed that Millian's name had been raised only twice in the relevant period: by [[christopher-steele|Christopher Steele]] in the October 2016 interview and by Danchenko in the January 2017 interviews.[1] Because Millian's name surfaced through Steele before Danchenko was identified as the primary sub-source in December 2016, the article's author speculated that this sequence might bear on why Danchenko later pointed back to Millian.[1]

## The alleged phone call and the New York trip

Durham questioned Auten about an alleged ten- to fifteen-minute telephone call between Danchenko and a person he "believed to be Sergei Millian"; Auten described the conversations with Danchenko about that call and the associated emails as "peculiar" and "inconsistent."[1] The defence sought to introduce Amtrak records showing Danchenko was in New York from roughly 26 to 28 July 2016, when he believed he was to meet the anonymous caller, together with a 28 July 2016 Facebook message to his then-wife accompanied by a photograph of giraffes at the Bronx Zoo and the words "Another meeting tonight."[1]

## The Zlodorev emails

A 26 July 2016 email between Millian and a Mr. Zlodorev formed a further evidentiary dispute.[1] Defence counsel [[danny-onorato|Danny Onorato]] complained that the government had excised the final message from the chain and invoked the rule of completeness, wanting the jury to see what Zlodorev told Millian about his relationship with Danchenko at the time.[1] Translations of Russian-language emails between Zlodorev and Millian reached Durham only at 12:01 a.m. on the morning of the first trial day; the defence wished to offer them and Durham argued they were hearsay, with [[anthony-trenga|Judge Trenga]] reserving the question.[1]

## Day two evidence

On the second day of trial it emerged that Millian, while willing to speak with the Special Counsel, would not come to the United States to testify out of concern for his family's safety, and that [[john-durham|Durham]] did not know where he was.[2] Durham had [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]] read into the record a 21 July 2016 email from [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]] to Millian, which Millian never answered; instead, on 26 July 2016, Millian forwarded it to [[dmitri-zlodorev|Dmitri Zlodorev]], who replied.[2] Later Russian-language messages of 24–25 August 2016 between Danchenko and Zlodorev included the line "Sergei doesn't respond."[2] The [[fbi|FBI]] never established whether Millian was even in the United States during the period of the supposed New York meeting, and Auten did not know whether investigators ever pulled Millian's phone records for the number he had given [[george-papadopoulos|George Papadopoulos]] on 15 July 2016, or his travel records.[2]

Durham emphasized repeatedly that the only means of contact Danchenko ever supplied to Millian were an email address and a telephone number, with no mention of FaceTime, WhatsApp or any similar application.[2] The defense countered with Exhibit 403, showing Facebook communications between Papadopoulos and Millian; LinkedIn messages in which Millian gave a 212 New York area-code number and whose text read "sent from my iPad"; and Exhibit 486, in which Papadopoulos passed on Millian's Skype ID — all offered to show that Millian used applications whose calls would not appear on phone records.[2]

## Day three of the trial

Millian remained central to the third day of the [[igor-danchenko|Danchenko]] trial even in his absence. Agent [[kevin-helson|Kevin Helson]] testified that he raised the alleged Millian phone call with Danchenko repeatedly — at the recorded March 16, 2017 meeting, at an unrecorded October 2017 meeting at [[brian-auten|Brian Auten]]'s tasking, and again on November 2, 2017 — and that in October 2017 Danchenko told him he had never met Millian face-to-face in the United States.[3] Helson also testified that he had not been informed of a prior relationship between the FBI and Millian.[3]

Because Millian declined to testify — saying he feared arrest if he returned to the United States and cited concerns for his safety — no witness could rebut the defense theory that a call or contact might have occurred, and defense counsel [[stuart-sears|Stuart Sears]] exploited that gap to considerable effect.[3] Commentary on the day observed that Millian could have shut the argument down by testifying that he never called Danchenko on any phone or through any application.[3] Sears also drew out that the Amtrak and flight records raised on day two had been subpoenaed by Durham's Special Counsel's Office rather than by the FBI.[3]
