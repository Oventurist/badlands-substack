# National Association of Secretaries of State

The National Association of Secretaries of State (NASS) is a membership association of state secretaries of state, the officials who in most U.S. states serve as chief election administrators. Badlands Media's election-integrity commentary treats it as one of a set of associations that gather election officials and, in the view of those critics, help manage and control them.[1]

## Badlands criticism

In her March 23, 2023 article "Rooting Out Election Corruption," [[holly-at-altitude|Holly at Altitude]] wrote that "there are a range of associations that gather election officials in order to manage and control elected and non-elected election officials," naming county clerk associations, NASS, and [[national-association-of-state-election-directors|NASED]] as examples.[1] She observed that tax dollars are often used to pay membership fees to what she characterized as questionable, quasi-governmental NGOs, and urged citizens to stop local and state election officials from using public money to maintain those memberships.[1]

That recommendation formed part of the article's fifth line of local action, holding election officials accountable, alongside legal challenges to dereliction of duty and maladministration.[1] The broader argument placed such associations among the "winners" of the post-[[help-america-vote-act|HAVA]] election system — a group the article said also includes companies, government agencies, politicians, the corporate media, NGOs, and other power brokers with an interest in preserving the status quo.[1]
