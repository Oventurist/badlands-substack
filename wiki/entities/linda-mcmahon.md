# Linda McMahon

Linda McMahon is President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s choice to lead the [[department-of-education|Education Department]] in his second administration.[1] She previously led the [[small-business-administration|Small Business Administration]] during Trump's first term.[1]

## Confirmation and mandate

McMahon was the most recent of Trump's Cabinet-level nominees to testify as of mid-February 2025, appearing on Thursday, February 13, before the Senate Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee.[1] Reporting at the time framed her assignment unusually: she was tasked with leading a department started in 1979 toward its closure.[1] Her hearing came amid a backlog in which six of 22 Cabinet-level picks still awaited [[united-states-senate|Senate]] action, with further hearings scheduled that week.[1]

## Plan to unwind the Department of Education (2025)

Appearing on CNN's "State of the Union" in March 2025, Education Secretary McMahon laid out her game plan to unwind the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] as far as possible, following President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s executive order directing its dismantling.[2] She insisted there would be no cuts to programs involving special education or student loans even if those programs ended up administered by other departments, saying "the outward-facing programs that are going to be affecting students are — there's not going to be any defunding for those programs."[2] She indicated the administration had at least loose ideas about where individual offices would go: student loans would move to the [[small-business-administration|Small Business Administration]], while programs for students with disabilities would "more than likely rest" in the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]], which she noted was where they began.[2] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary by [[brad-getz|Brad Cologero Getz]] summarized the effort as "Reagan knew it, and Trump will do it."[2]

## 2024 Republican National Convention speech

McMahon — wife of [[vince-mcmahon|Vince McMahon]], founder of the WWF/[[world-wrestling-entertainment|WWE]], and herself a former president and chief executive officer of that organization — spoke on the final day of the [[2024-republican-national-convention|2024 Republican National Convention]].[3] She recounted how [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], "for fun," joined what she called one of "the most compelling and highest-rated storylines" in WWE history, and revealed that she had served in Trump's cabinet running the Small Business Administration.[3] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the speech as a deliberate communications signal, noting that McMahon and [[hulk-hogan|Hulk Hogan]] were scheduled within hours of one another and that most of that evening's speakers had ties to combat sports.[3]

## California transgender athlete dispute (2025)

In June 2025, McMahon, as US Education Secretary, gave California 10 days from June 27 to rescind any sports prizes awarded to transgender athletes and to ban them, after the [[department-of-education|Education Department]]'s Office of Civil Rights found the state had violated federal anti-discrimination law by allowing transgender girls to compete against cisgender girls.[4] On Monday, July 7, 2025, the top attorney for the California Department of Education said the state would not comply with the federal agreement, which would have required rescinding trans-inclusionary guidelines and sending personalized apologies to cisgender female athletes who lost to a transgender opponent — making California the second state after Maine to defy the Trump administration on the issue.[4] The office of Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] called McMahon's proposed agreement a "political document" that carried no weight and would force the state to illegally violate its own anti-discrimination laws.[4] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] remarked that California was pressing states' rights and cities' rights on the same day.[4]

## Confirmation as Secretary of Education (March 2025)

On Monday, March 3, 2025, the Republican-led Senate voted to confirm McMahon as the new secretary of the U.S. [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] by a 51-45 vote along party lines.[5] A former professional wrestling magnate who led the U.S. Small Business Administration during President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first term, McMahon has a limited background in education, though she served on Connecticut's State Board of Education for about a year.[5] In the statement announcing her nomination, Trump said she would "empower the next Generation of American Students and Workers, and make America Number One in Education in the World."[5]

Her confirmation hearing was heated at times and was interrupted repeatedly by protesters shouting about protections for students; in it McMahon decried a public education "system in decline" and vowed to "reorient" the department and "invest in teachers not Washington bureaucrats."[5] The White House has been clear that it intends to dismantle the department and that overseeing that effort would be McMahon's job.[5] Badlands contributors welcomed the confirmation, with Ashe in America noting that the [[office-of-personnel-management|Office of Personnel Management]] had already prepared the department for a major workforce reduction in anticipation of McMahon's first day, and GhostofBasedPatrickHenry arguing that she could now focus on dismantling and ultimately abolishing the department so that its responsibilities could be returned to local school boards and parents.[5]

## Confirmation hearing (February 2025)

At her confirmation hearing on Thursday, February 13, 2025, McMahon sketched out how key functions of the [[department-of-education|Education Department]] could be carved up to achieve President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s goal of dismantling the agency, vowing to "reorient" the department while continuing some of its largest programs.[6] She said she would preserve core initiatives including Title I money for low-income schools, Pell grants for low-income college students, and [[public-service-loan-forgiveness|Public Service Loan Forgiveness]].[6]

McMahon said the administration wanted to "do this right" and that abolishing the department would require an act of Congress: "We'd like to make sure that we are presenting a plan that I think our senators could get on board with, and our Congress could get on board with, that would have a better functioning Department of Education."[6] She said the president's goal was not to defund key programs but to have them "operate more efficiently," while questioning whether some functions should move to other agencies — suggesting enforcement of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act "may very well rest better" at the Department of Health and Human Services, and that the department's Office for Civil Rights could fit better at the [[justice-department|Justice Department]].[6]

Covering the hearing for [[badlands-media|Badlands]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that McMahon "did a brilliant job" and presented herself as an impressive candidate, arguing that Democrats were caught off guard because they had expected the former World Wrestling Entertainment president to conform to their stereotypes.[6] He and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] contended that Democratic senators prioritized preserving the status quo and defending teachers and unions over students' interests.[6] He also noted that WWE superstar Triple H — McMahon's son-in-law and the company's CEO — was seated directly behind her during the hearing, closing with "TrumpaMania lives!"[6]

## Nomination as Secretary of Education (2024)

On November 19, 2024, President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] selected McMahon, his transition co-chair, to serve as secretary of the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]] — an agency he and his allies had pledged to abolish.[7] Then 76, McMahon had previously led the Small Business Administration during Trump's first term and was the former chief executive of [[world-wrestling-entertainment|World Wrestling Entertainment]], the company she built with her husband [[vince-mcmahon|Vince McMahon]].[7]

Trump said McMahon would use "decades of Leadership experience, and deep understanding of both Education and Business, to empower the next Generation of American Students and Workers," and indicated she would give states a larger role in education policy and pursue nationwide expansion of school choice.[7] He described her as a "fierce advocate for Parents' Rights" through her role as chairwoman of the America First Policy Institute.[7]

Badlands commentary noted that McMahon had described her WWE career in a speech at the 2024 Republican National Convention, that the promotion's highest-rated storylines of all time had involved Trump himself, and that she occasionally appeared as an on-screen character in its fictional universe.[7] The commentary also recalled that the McMahons' wrestling organization had helped fund Evel Knievel's failed Snake River Canyon jump, and that Linda left WWE in 2009 to run unsuccessfully for the U.S. Senate in Connecticut against [[richard-blumenthal|Richard Blumenthal]].[7]

## March 2025 executive order and Democratic pushback

On Thursday, March 20, 2025, [[donald-trump|President Trump]] signed an executive order directing the start of the dismantling of the [[department-of-education|Department of Education]], and instructed McMahon as Education Secretary to do everything within her power to bring about the department's abolition; the order acknowledged that full closure would require congressional action.[8] "Today, we take a very historic action that was 45 years in the making," Trump said at a [[white-house|White House]] signing ceremony. "I will sign an executive order to begin eliminating the federal Department of Education."[8]

Top House Democrats wrote to McMahon the same day urging her and the administration to reverse the decision, saying that "students, teachers, and schools across the country rely on the Department's important work" and that they had "serious doubts the remaining staff at the Department have the capacity to fulfill this mission."[8] The lawmakers asked her to "reverse these short-sighted decisions before they cause any more damage to America's students, families, and educators."[8] Badlands commentary defended the move as a promise kept, arguing that federal spending and intervention in education had risen while measured performance stagnated.[8]

## HELP Committee vote (February 20, 2025)

McMahon's nomination to lead the [[department-of-education|Education Department]] cleared a key procedural hurdle on Thursday, February 20, 2025, when the [[senate-help-committee|Senate HELP Committee]] advanced it to the Senate floor on a 12-11 party-line vote, one week after she appeared before the panel to explain President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s plans for the department's future.[9] Committee chair [[bill-cassidy|Bill Cassidy]] said that if confirmed she would have "the tall task of reforming a Department of Education that has lost its purpose," argued that for four years the department had "focused on everything but student learning with bureaucracy and red tape standing in the way of student success," and said she had "demonstrated a strong vision" for the agency.[9] At the earlier hearing senators had grilled her on what would become of programs housed within the agency — such as special education and grants for low-income schools — if the department were dismantled or shuttered.[9]

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] praised McMahon as a highly competent businesswoman and proven government administrator who had led the [[small-business-administration|Small Business Administration]] in Trump's first term, and credited her as longtime president of [[world-wrestling-entertainment|World Wrestling Entertainment]] with creating "Trump-a-Mania," including the storyline in which Trump appeared as a character and his 2013 induction into the WWE Hall of Fame.[9] He concluded that her task at the department was to dismantle it.[9] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] listed her among three nominees through committee and awaiting a floor vote, alongside Secretary of the Army nominee [[dan-driscoll|Dan Driscoll]] and U.S. Trade Representative nominee [[jamieson-greer|Jamison Greer]].[9]
