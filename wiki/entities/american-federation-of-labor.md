# American Federation of Labor

The American Federation of Labor (AFL) was a national federation of labor unions in the United States, founded in 1886, that merged with the [[congress-of-industrial-organizations|Congress of Industrial Organizations]] in December 1955 to form the [[afl-cio|AFL-CIO]]. In the Badlands Media article A More Perfect (Labor) Union, the AFL is presented as the earlier institutional vehicle through which American organized labor was integrated into covert U.S. foreign policy.

## Role in covert operations

The article highlights several episodes of AFL involvement in covert statecraft:

- **World War II-era origins.** As Allied forces liberated Western Europe, AFL leader [[matthew-woll|Matthew Woll]] viewed organized labor as a frontline in the ideological battle. The article notes Woll held key positions in two British intelligence fronts — the American Labor Committee to Aid British Labor and the League for Human Rights — and cites historian Thomas E. Mahl's *Desperate Deception* suggesting Woll "may have been a British intelligence contact."

- **Free Trade Union Committee (FTUC).** Formed in 1944, the AFL's FTUC targeted communist-led unions in France and Italy, funneling resources to splinter groups and providing intelligence to U.S. diplomats. By 1949 the [[cia|CIA]] funneled millions through the FTUC to split European labor federations along Cold War lines, funding non-communist rivals like [[force-ouvriere|France's Force Ouvrière]] and Italy's non-CGIL unions.

- **The World Federation of Trade Unions.** By 1945, the AFL had "successfully sabotaged" the [[world-federation-of-trade-unions|World Federation of Trade Unions]], withdrawing from it and denouncing it as a communist front.
