# Pramila Jayapal

Pramila Jayapal is a Democratic member of the United States House of Representatives from Washington state, associated with the chamber's progressive bloc.[1]

## Wealth tax legislation

In March 2024, Jayapal joined Representative [[brendan-boyle|Brendan Boyle]] (D-PA) in introducing the House companion bill to Senator [[elizabeth-warren|Elizabeth Warren]]'s [[ultra-millionaire-tax-act|Ultra-Millionaire Tax Act]], which would tax households worth between $50 million and $1 billion at 2 percent annually and households above $1 billion at 3 percent.[1] Badlands Media coverage grouped Jayapal among the "socialist pals" backing what it described as an effort to normalize the confiscation of private assets.[1]

## Yuma Border Patrol detention death (2025)

In late March 2025 Jayapal alleged that a 52-year-old Chinese national detained for overstaying a B1/B2 visitor visa died by suicide at the [[united-states-border-patrol|U.S. Border Patrol]] station in Yuma, Arizona, after officers failed to perform required welfare checks.[2] The woman had been picked up in California and held at Yuma until her death on 29 March; officials allegedly did not follow internal policies on publicly acknowledging in-custody deaths, issuing a notice only after the Tucson Sentinel reported the death.[2] Surveillance footage reportedly showed the woman making a noose, with no medical response for two hours.[2]

Jayapal said in a statement that as the CBP Office of Professional Responsibility investigated, it must explain why welfare checks were not conducted and were falsely recorded, and why the woman was able to die "without any guard intervention."[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] accused Jayapal of politicizing the death and of inconsistency, arguing that a lawmaker who champions bodily autonomy and "hands off healthcare" was simultaneously demanding guard intervention against a personal choice.[2]

## Exchange with Robert Hur (March 2024)

During a five-hour [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] hearing on March 12, 2024, Jayapal — described as the influential chairwoman of the Congressional Progressive Caucus — was corrected by former special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]] when he stated, "I did not exonerate him and that word does not appear in the report," referring to his findings on President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s handling of classified documents.[3] Jayapal spoke over Hur as he made the correction and then cut him off, saying, "Mr. Hur, it's my time. Thank you."[3]
