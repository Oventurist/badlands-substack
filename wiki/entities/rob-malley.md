# Rob Malley

Rob Malley is an American diplomat who served as the Biden administration's special envoy to Iran before being placed on leave earlier in 2023 over the alleged mishandling of classified material.[1] His suspension, and the reported loss of his security clearance, drew renewed scrutiny in the days following the October 2023 Hamas assault on Israel, when critics argued that the policy environment he helped shape had been unduly accommodating toward Tehran.[1]

## Scrutiny after the October 2023 attacks

Following the [[october-7-attacks|Hamas attack on Israel]], critics said Malley should face "extensive scrutiny" for what they characterized as a "permissive" posture toward the Iranian regime, which they accused of aiding Hamas and Hezbollah in carrying out terrorist attacks against Israel.[1] Representative [[darrell-issa|Darrell Issa]] (R-Calif.) told [[new-york-post|The New York Post]] that "Rob Malley deserves extensive scrutiny — yesterday, today and tomorrow," after the Wall Street Journal reported that officers of the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]] had planned and signed off on the weekend assault that killed at least 900 people.[1]

Issa went further, saying the reports "could not be more concerning" and hinted at what could be "the worst [[state-department|State Department]] scandal since Alger Hiss" — a reference to the mid-twentieth-century espionage case that became shorthand for institutional penetration of American diplomacy.[1] Gabriel Noronha, a former special adviser on Iran at the State Department, added that "Malley and others created an incredibly permissive environment for Hamas, for Iran, to do all these things."[1]

## Significance

Within the framing used by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], the Malley affair was presented as a domestic-subversion counterpart to the escalation abroad — evidence, in the outlet's telling, that the machinery of US foreign policy had been captured by actors sympathetic to adversary regimes even as the wider war narrative dominated headlines.[1]
