# Brian Kilmeade

Brian Kilmeade is a [[fox-news|Fox News]] personality who hosted the debut edition of *Fox News Tonight*, the rotating-host program installed in the network's 8 p.m. hour after [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s abrupt departure in April 2023.[1]

## Fox News Tonight debut

Kilmeade's first outing drew just under 2.6 million viewers — roughly 21 percent below the 3.3 million average that *Tucker Carlson Tonight* had posted over the prior eight Mondays.[1] In the key news demographic of adults 25-54, the program's 0.24 rating was 37 percent lower than the 0.38 Carlson had averaged across the same span.[1] Despite the decline, the hour still led cable news by a wide margin, ahead of a *Joe Scarborough Presents* special on [[msnbc|MSNBC]] at 1.51 million viewers and *Anderson Cooper 360* on [[cnn|CNN]] at 728,000.[1]

Badlands commentary treated the ratings drop as evidence that Fox remained "the prettiest horse at the glue factory" — still dominant among cable competitors, but newly exposed to its own audience as part of the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]].[1]

## Homelessness remarks and apology (September 2025)

On a "Fox & Friends" episode in September 2025, Kilmeade advocated the execution of mentally ill homeless people, saying "or involuntary lethal injection, or something — just kill 'em," during a discussion with co-hosts Lawrence Jones and Ainsley Earhardt about the August 22 stabbing murder of [[iryna-zarutska|Iryna Zarutska]] on a light rail train in Charlotte, North Carolina.[2] Jones had been discussing public money spent trying to help homeless people and suggested that those refusing services should be jailed; when Earhardt interjected, "Why did it have to get to this point?", Kilmeade replied, "I will say this, we're not voting for the right people."[2] A homeless and mentally ill man, [[decarlos-brown-jr|Decarlos Brown Jr.]], was arrested for the murder, and the case received extensive attention on [[fox-news|Fox News]] after the release of security video of the stabbing.[2]

The remark circulated widely online over the following weekend, and on the Sunday "Fox & Friends" weekend show Kilmeade said: "I wrongly said they should get lethal injection. I apologize for that extremely callous remark. I am obviously aware that not all mentally ill, homeless people act as the perpetrator did in North Carolina and that so many homeless people deserve our empathy and compassion."[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the Fox News audience "needs a wakeup call" if Kilmeade's comment required a public apology, adding that one should "never apologize to the mob, especially on a bullshit grievance," because doing so legitimizes and emboldens it.[2] She also reasserted her position that mentally ill homeless people committing atrocities is a real problem and that state mental hospitals should be reopened.[2]
