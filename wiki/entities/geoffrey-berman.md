# Geoffrey Berman

Geoffrey Berman is an American attorney who served as United States Attorney for the [[southern-district-of-new-york|Southern District of New York]] during the first term of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], holding the post in a temporary capacity because no nominee had been submitted to and confirmed by the Senate.[1]

## Appointment

Berman was placed into his temporary role at the SDNY by Attorney General [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]], an appointment made possible after Trump fired Preet Bharara as head of the office.[1] In the Badlands Media account by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]], this sequence was deliberate: Q drop 1370 (May 15, 2018) asked "Why was Preet Bharara fired?… What must be cleaned first? Who investigates? Who prosecutes? WHAT CAN FINALLY BE DONE?" — implying the SDNY had to be cleaned before the real investigations could proceed.[1] Lange argues Trump had "installed his man in the SDNY to help gather the evidence."[1]

## Role in the Cohen and Giuliani matters

[[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] handed the investigation of Trump's personal attorney [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] to Berman's office rather than retaining it.[1] The SDNY under Berman was also investigating [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]], another of Trump's personal attorneys, publicly framed as a lobbying-violations inquiry tied to Ukraine.[1] Lange contends the real legal jeopardy lay with [[tony-podesta|Tony Podesta]] and the [[podesta-group|Podesta Group]], and that Giuliani — who was never charged despite reported grand jury activity — used the SDNY as the legal channel through which his "insurance file," including Ukraine material and evidence from the Hunter Biden laptop, could be introduced into ongoing criminal investigations.[1] Beyond Cohen and Giuliani, the office is described as overseeing the Weiner/[[huma-abedin|Huma]] laptop matter and the Epstein and Maxwell cases.[1]

## The 2020 resignation standoff

In June 2020, Attorney General William Barr announced Berman's resignation; Berman publicly refused, saying he had learned of his supposed departure from a Department of Justice press release and would step down only when a presidentially appointed nominee was confirmed by the Senate.[1] He pledged that the office's investigations would "move forward without delay or interruption."[1] The Guardian described the SDNY as the home of investigations and prosecutions of Trump allies including Giuliani and Cohen.[1]

Lange reads the two-day standoff as staged: "The fake fight over refusing to resign was just a distraction, and cover to create 'distance' between Trump and the investigations," adding that "optics is very important."[1] In this framing Berman was a key player in a broader operation rather than an adversary of the administration. These interpretations reflect Badlands Media commentary rather than documented fact.[1]
