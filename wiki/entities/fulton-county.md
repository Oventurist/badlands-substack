# Fulton County

Fulton County is the Georgia jurisdiction containing Atlanta whose administration of the 2020 presidential election became the subject of an active federal criminal investigation disclosed in February 2026.[1]

## FBI investigation into 2020 ballot handling

According to court documents filed on February 10, 2026, in federal court by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], the [[fbi|FBI]] is actively investigating potential improprieties in Fulton County's handling of ballots from the 2020 presidential election.[1] The probe is detailed in search warrant affidavits authored by FBI Special Agent [[hugh-evans|Hugh Evans]] of the Atlanta Field Office and focuses on alleged deficiencies in vote counting and record-keeping.[1] The identified issues include missing ballot images, inconsistent totals across recounts, chain-of-custody documentation problems, duplicate ballot images, and absentee ballots that lack the creases typically expected from being folded and mailed in envelopes.[1]

The affidavits explain that access to the physical ballots is required to assess whether these issues stemmed from administrative errors or intentional acts.[1] In January 2026, FBI agents executed court-authorized search warrants at the Fulton County Election Hub and Operation Center to seize relevant materials.[1]

## Prior review of the allegations

Many of the specific allegations cited in the affidavits — such as discrepancies between ballot images and reported vote totals raised by a redacted witness described as a chemical engineer — had previously been addressed through a manual recount ordered by Georgia Secretary of State [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]] in 2020, reviewed by the Georgia State Election Board, and litigated in post-election challenges brought by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his legal team, all of which were ultimately dismissed by courts for lack of standing or merit.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the unsealed Fulton County search warrant was emotionally significant because the idea that such records would be useful for investigation had itself been treated as a conspiracy theory only a short time earlier.[1] She enumerated the categories of records at issue: missing ballot images from the original count and recount, duplicate ballots in both counts, inconsistent reported totals over time, missing SHA hash files used to verify the integrity of ballot images, potential tabulator tape manipulation such as inconsistencies between machines' reported votes and protective counter data, and ballots with broken security seals and unexplained handling.[1]

She argued that the DOJ's position is that the records themselves may prove violations of federal election law, and that Badlands contributors had been making that argument for years.[1] Citing [[brian-lupo|Brian Lupo]], she noted the warrant reads like a review of the Why We Vote catalog on Georgia.[1] She contended that Fulton County's Fourth Amendment lawsuit against the seizure is effectively dead, describing an exercise in which she fed a legal model Fulton's complaint, testimony from Perdue, and the unsealed warrant without commentary, and reported that the model rated Fulton's chances of prevailing as extremely poor.[1] She also questioned whether the county still possesses records that multiple courts have ordered it to retain, noting that in some cases it is already known that it does not.[1]

## NAACP motion to restrict use of seized records

In February 2026 the [[naacp|NAACP]] and other NGOs filed an emergency motion under Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 41(g) in the US District Court for the Northern District of Georgia, seeking limits on how the [[fbi|FBI]] and [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] may use the election materials seized from Fulton County in January.[2] The plaintiffs argued that Fulton County voters retain privacy and constitutional interests in personal data contained in the seized records — including Social Security numbers, dates of birth, driver's license numbers and ballot-related information — making them "persons aggrieved" under the rule.[2]

The requested order would bar use of the county's data for national voter roll programs, immigration enforcement, unrelated civil enforcement, interagency data sharing, compilation of voter databases, and public disclosure, and would impose access controls, an inventory requirement, access logging, and a protective order over storage and handling.[2] The seizure continued to draw national criticism, with [[the-atlantic|*The Atlantic*]] publishing an editorial warning that President [[donald-trump|Trump]] was nationalizing elections.[2]

## May 2026 DOJ grand jury subpoena

In April 2026 the U.S. Department of Justice issued a grand jury subpoena seeking the names, positions, residential and email addresses, and phone numbers of thousands of people who worked on the 2020 presidential election in Fulton County, including county employees, bus drivers operating mobile voting sites, volunteers, and temporary poll workers.[3] The subpoena was dated April 17 and served April 20 on the county's director of elections, prompting the county to file a motion to quash on May 4, 2026, arguing it was "grossly overbroad and untethered to any reasonable need" and could not yield evidence for prosecution because the statute of limitations on related federal crimes had expired.[3] Board of Commissioners Chairman Robb Pitts called the demand "outrageous federal overreach designed to intimidate and chill participation in elections," saying "Fulton County will not be intimidated."[3] County officials tied the subpoena to broader administration efforts to obtain 2020 records from swing states, including a January 2026 FBI seizure of ballots from a county warehouse.[3] The episode is detailed in [[fulton-county-2020-election-grand-jury-subpoena|the Fulton County 2020 election grand jury subpoena]].[3]


## January 2026 FBI search of the Election Hub

On Wednesday, January 28, 2026, the FBI executed a search warrant at the Fulton County Election Hub and Operations Center in Union City, Georgia, seeking records related to the 2020 General Election.[4] The warrant authorized the federal government to seize a wide range of 2020 election materials: all physical ballots (absentee, advance, provisional, in-person Election Day, emergency, damaged, duplicated, or otherwise used to vote), absentee ballot envelopes, provisional ballots, ballot images, tabulator tapes from scanners, and 2020 voter rolls.[4]

County Commissioner [[mo-ivory|Mo Ivory]] (D-04) confirmed that agents removed roughly 700 boxes of ballots and related records, and Fulton County Court Clerk [[che-alexander|Ché Alexander]], the custodian of the records, was present and involved in inventorying the items taken.[4] The warrant was signed by US Magistrate Judge [[catherine-salinas|Catherine M. Salinas]] in the Northern District of Georgia.[4] It came amid ongoing disputes over 2020 election records and access to ballots and voter data, tied to subpoenas from the [[georgia-state-election-board|Georgia State Election Board]].[4] The FBI released no official details on the investigative purpose, targets, or findings, and the investigation was described as ongoing.[4]

Badlands commentators framed the raid as a turning point in the accountability timeline: one week earlier President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had said people would be prosecuted for election fraud, and the same day the Georgia State Election Board heard testimony alleging outcome-altering criminal election fraud in the county.[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the evidence showed officials "knew it was fraudulent and covered it up," and that the county had refused to produce the seized materials since 2020.[4]

## December 2025 court order granting board access to 2020 ballots

In December 2025 a Georgia Superior Court judge granted a state election board access to Fulton County's 2020 presidential ballots and documents.[5] The Republican-led [[georgia-state-election-board|Georgia Election Board]] had reopened an investigation the previous year into how the county handled the 2020 election.[5] Fulton County Superior Court Judge [[robert-mcburney|Robert McBurney]] ruled that the board would need to cover the costs of producing the ballots and documents.[5]

County Chairman [[robb-pitts|Robb Pitts]] told FOX5 that the elections had been open and transparent and insisted that all votes were counted, asserting that Fulton County taxpayers would not be on the hook for the roughly $400,000 that production of the documents was estimated to cost.[5] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] questioned why compliance with a court order should cost $400,000 and why the county should be paid anything at all after what she described as years of obstruction and defiance of court orders, asking whether "everyone [is] above the law when it comes to elections."[5] She called the outcome anticlimactic but a step closer, crediting Georgia activists who had fought for the decision for years.[5]

## DOJ Civil Rights Division records demand (November 2025)

In November 2025 the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]]'s Civil Rights Division issued a formal demand to the Fulton County Board of Registration and Elections, ordering officials to produce all election records responsive to a subpoena from the [[georgia-state-election-board|Georgia State Election Board]].[6] The letter, signed by Assistant Attorney General [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]], directed Chairperson Sheri Allen and Elections Director Nadine Williams to make the records available "in its entirety and most original form," and was issued under the [[civil-rights-act-of-1960|Civil Rights Act of 1960]].[6] Its stated purpose was to ascertain Georgia's compliance with the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act]] and the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]], and it cited a July 30, 2025 State Election Board resolution inviting DOJ assistance as well as a November 7, 2024 board letter referencing "unexplained anomalies in vote tabulation and storage related to the 2020 election."[6] The county was given 15 days to comply.[6]

Badlands host Ashe in America argued that while the letter might prove to be "just another strongly worded letter," the DOJ's citation of Fulton County's record of repeatedly ignoring lawful transparency requests was significant, because legal proceedings normally extend a presumption of good faith to sworn government officers.[6] In her framing, "a pattern isn't an oversight": repeated refusal to answer lawful requests about the same subject implies bad faith and obstruction, and abuse of that presumption should carry severe consequences.[6] She characterized the federal government publicly carrying the ball on 2020 election questions — rather than leaving the work to citizen activist groups — as a genuine change.[6]

## Election Day 2024 bomb threats

On Election Day, November 5, 2024, five bomb threats determined to be "noncredible" prompted two polling locations in Union City, Georgia, within Fulton County, to close briefly.[7] County elections director Nadine Williams said at a news conference that the county was asking a court to keep the two locations open for an additional half hour that evening.[7]

A county spokeswoman said police had responded to multiple calls about threats against polling places: "We have investigated each of these and found no active threats. We will continue to remain vigilant."[7] County officials had promised to station a law enforcement officer at each of Fulton County's 177 polling places.[7] Georgia Secretary of State [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]] said officials had traced the threats to Russia, while his spokesman cautioned that the source was unconfirmed and could be someone spoofing Russia.[7]

## Certification lawsuit and ruling (October 2024)

On October 14, 2024, Fulton County Superior Court Judge [[robert-mcburney|Robert McBurney]] ruled that Georgia county election officials must certify election results by the statutory deadline regardless of irregularities or suspected fraud, writing that no election superintendent or member of a board of elections and registration "may refuse to certify or abstain from certifying election results under any circumstance."[8] The suit had been brought by [[julie-adams|Julie Adams]], a Republican member of the Fulton County Board of Elections and Registrations, after the county's appointed election director allegedly denied her repeated requests for access to election results and processes.[8] Georgia law requires county election superintendents to certify results by 5 p.m. on the Monday following the election, or the Tuesday when that date falls on a federal holiday, as it did in 2024.[8]
