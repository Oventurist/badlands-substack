# Foundation for Freedom Online

The Foundation for Freedom Online (FFO) is a research organization that documents government and public-private involvement in online speech moderation, a subject it frames as the [[censorship-industrial-complex]].[1]

## USAID Disinformation Primer reporting

In March 2024 the FFO published an analysis of internal documents from the U.S. Agency for International Development ([[usaid]]), specifically a "Disinformation Primer" produced by USAID's Center on Democracy, Human Rights, and Governance (DRG).[1] The FFO reported that the primer praised private-sector censorship strategies and proposed additional techniques aimed at technology companies, media organizations, education ministries, national governments and funding bodies.[1]

Among the practices the FFO said USAID endorsed were "Advertiser Outreach" — enlisting corporate advertisers to financially throttle disfavored outlets and social media accounts — as well as Google's Redirect Method and "prebunking," described as psychological inoculation against so-called fake news.[1] The FFO further reported that the primer proposed targeting gamers and gaming communities on the grounds that they form "interpretations of the world that differ from 'mainstream' sources," and sought to interrupt the process by which individuals contribute their own research to build collective "populist expertise."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the findings as "the sinister side of public-private partnerships" and argued the arrangement was unconstitutional, invoking the Supreme Court's holding in Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co. (1991) that governmental authority may so dominate an activity that private participants must be deemed state actors subject to constitutional constraints.[1] She contended that NGOs and technology companies acting on the primer's recommendations should be treated as state actors bound by the First Amendment, and expressed hope that [[america-first-legal|America First Legal]] would pursue the matter.[1]

## Reporting on Democratic election contingency plans

The Foundation for Freedom Online, led by executive director [[mike-benz|Mike Benz]], began publishing material in December 2022 on Democratic "election contingency" plans for 2020 and returned to the subject in July 2023, arguing that the Biden campaign had detailed plans to prevent a second Trump term regardless of the election's outcome, including its own alternate slates of electors overseen by [[john-podesta|John Podesta]].[2]
