# Five Eyes

The Five Eyes is the intelligence-sharing alliance linking the United States with the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and New Zealand.[1]

## Exclusion from Russia–Ukraine negotiation intelligence

In August 2025 the Kyiv Post reported that U.S. Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] had barred America's closest intelligence allies from accessing information about the ongoing Russia–Ukraine peace negotiations.[1] According to CBS News, a classified memo signed by Gabbard on July 20 directed U.S. intelligence agencies to designate all analysis and reporting on the talks as "NOFORN," meaning no foreign dissemination, effectively cutting the Five Eyes partners off from intelligence they had long relied on to coordinate with Washington.[1] Multiple U.S. intelligence officials told CBS News that the memo restricted distribution even within the U.S. intelligence community, limiting access to the agencies that produced the material, with the only exceptions being reports already made public.[1] The directive did not cover diplomatic reporting or military intelligence unrelated to the negotiations, but experts warned the order risked undermining decades of trust among allies.[1]

Steven Cash, a former intelligence officer at the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] and the Department of Homeland Security, told CBS News that the value of the partnership is that participants can augment each other's intelligence when making policy decisions, and that the arrangement rests on "the expectation that we and the other four are all sitting on the same side of the table with some other adversary on the other side," adding that allies need "a common intelligence picture" so negotiators "can coordinate our positions and get the best deal we can, or fight the best war we can."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the development "delightful," linking it to the previous day's coverage of officials whose security clearances had been stripped, and argued that for those who believe most of the horrors of recent decades were directed by a globalist intelligence apparatus the move was cause for excitement.[1] She framed the alliance as a vector of "infiltration instead of invasion," asking what happens when "your true adversary is sitting on the same side of the table as you, and you're sharing intelligence with them."[1] She invoked George Washington's farewell address warning against permanent antipathies and passionate attachments between nations — "the nation which indulges toward another an habitual hatred or an habitual fondness is in some degree a slave" — and concluded that Washington was right and that Gabbard appeared to be "finally taking him seriously."[1]

In separate commentary on Russian allegations of Ukrainian subversion in Africa, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] glossed Ukrainian intelligence activity on the continent as effectively a Five Eyes operation, writing that ISIS affiliates in the Sahel were being "animated by 'Ukraine'" and equating that with the Five Eyes network.[1]

## Alleged surveillance of the Trump campaign

Reporting published by Public and Racket in February 2024 alleged that the U.S. Intelligence Community, including the [[cia|CIA]], asked the Five Eyes partners to surveil associates of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and share the resulting intelligence with American agencies, well before the [[fbi|FBI]] formally opened its Russia collusion inquiry in July 2016.[2] The claim, attributed to sources close to a [[house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence|House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence]] investigation, displaced the long-standing official account in which the investigation was triggered by an Australian diplomat's report about a Trump aide's remarks concerning [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]].[2] The episode is covered further at [[russiagate-hoax|the Russiagate hoax]].[2]

## Assange's targeting of Five Eyes corruption

Badlands contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] wrote in February 2024 that [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] had been undermining deep state operations across the board by focusing on corruption within the Five Eyes intelligence networks, work he cited as context for concerns that Assange's extradition case was being heard by a judge with a long history of representing intelligence agencies.[3]

## The 2024 Israel-Iran assessment leak

The classified documents at the center of the November 2024 prosecution of [[asif-william-rahman|Asif William Rahman]] were sharable within the Five Eyes partnership — the United States, Britain, Canada, New Zealand and Australia — before they appeared on a Telegram channel.[4] Attributed to the [[national-geospatial-intelligence-agency|National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency]] and the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]], the material assessed Israel's movement of military assets ahead of a strike on Iran in response to Iran's October 1, 2024 ballistic missile attack.[4]

## Alleged use against the 2016 Trump campaign

"Moves and Countermoves" presents the Five Eyes arrangement — a Cold War–era intelligence-sharing agreement among the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, New Zealand and Australia — as the mechanism by which the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration circumvented the prohibition on [[cia|CIA]] surveillance of American citizens.[5] Citing The Federalist's February 2024 report, [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] writes that the U.S. Intelligence Community asked its FVEY partners to surveil [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s associates and share the product with American agencies, and that multiple credible sources described the IC, including the CIA, illegally mobilizing foreign intelligence services against Trump advisors long before the summer of 2016.[5] British intelligence sources, including GCHQ, are said to have begun targeting Trump possibly as early as 2015, and Australian intelligence supplied the tip that ostensibly launched [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] on July 31, 2016 — a tip Special Counsel John Durham later concluded did not justify the investigation.[5]

Lange argues that [[barack-obama|Obama]] and [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] used the alliance to evade congressional oversight while spying not only on the Trump campaign but on candidate [[ted-cruz|Ted Cruz]], members of [[congress|Congress]] and the press, and that the absence of any regular-channel record for the released transcripts of [[michael-flynn|Flynn]]'s calls with the Russian ambassador proved they originated with FVEY sources.[5] He contends the resulting need to launder this material through legitimate process drove the [[steele-dossier|fake dossier]] and the FISA warrants on Carter Page and George Papadopoulos, and that [[john-mccain|John McCain]] was himself tied to the FVEY operation.[5]

## FVEY in "The Biggest Boomerang In History"

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s article defines FVEY as the intelligence-sharing network between the United States, United Kingdom, Australia, Canada and New Zealand, and argues that the [[barack-obama|Obama]] administration used allied services in the United Kingdom and Australia through it to spy on the Trump campaign and many other people long before the [[fbi|FBI]] ever applied for a [[foreign-intelligence-surveillance-act|FISA]] warrant on [[carter-page|Carter Page]].[6]

Lange asserts Obama ordered [[john-brennan|John Brennan]]'s [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] to run this foreign-partner strand of the operation while [[james-comey|Comey]]'s FBI handled the domestic side and the cover-up.[6] Q drop 3784, quoted in the article, likewise references foreign classified intelligence provided from "[FVEY – Non FVEY]" sources as needed within the [[declas|DECLAS]] chain of command.[6] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
