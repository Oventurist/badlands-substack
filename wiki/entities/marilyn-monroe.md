# Marilyn Monroe

Marilyn Monroe was an American film actress whose body was found naked in her bed on the morning of August 4, 1962, with the official cause of death recorded as a "self-induced barbiturate overdose."[1] In the decades since, her death has become the anchor of one of the most widely circulated conspiracy narratives in American popular culture — the claim that [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] and his brother [[robert-f-kennedy|Robert F. Kennedy]] conspired to have her murdered.[1]

## The Kennedy murder narrative

Writing for Badlands Media, [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] observed that a very large number of people have embraced the narrative that Bobby Kennedy and his brother arranged Monroe's killing, and that this narrative has been amplified by mainstream productions to the point of curing millions of Americans of their romantic memories of the two brothers.[1] The 2022 [[netflix|Netflix]] film *The Mystery of Marilyn Monroe: The Unheard Tapes* presented the Kennedys as the purported organizers of Monroe's murder.[1] [[covert-action-magazine|Covert Action Magazine]] followed with an article titled "New Evidence Suggests Marilyn Monroe Was Murdered and LAPD Covered Up Murder," and the commentator Hrvoje Morik also embraced the narrative.[1]

Ehret argued that the evidentiary base for the "Bobby killed Marilyn" thesis rests on two unstable pillars: anecdotal testimonies from a closed array of sources presumed credible because no profit motive is imagined for them, and supposed tape recordings from Monroe's bugged house that no one has heard except the man who claims to have made them, [[fred-otash|Fred Otash]].[1]

## The alleged secret diary

Central to the narrative are Monroe's purported secret diary entries, which Ehret notes no one has seen except anonymous sources acquired by [[mike-rothmiller|Mike Rothmiller]].[1] Neither the identity of the secret source nor the diary pages themselves have been made available for examination, yet the storyline depends on their existence because the diary is where the alleged *motive* for the Kennedys' involvement is supposedly found.[1]

The claims attributed to the invisible diary include: Kennedy's plans to topple Fidel Castro; JFK's drug addiction; Robert Kennedy's controlling hand behind the invasion of Cuba; JFK's intention to increase troop levels in Vietnam; JFK's alleged directing role in the assassinations of President Trujillo of the Dominican Republic and President [[ngo-dinh-diem|Ngo Dinh Diem]]; Bobby Kennedy's supposed oversight of a global assassination complex; and RFK's alleged plans for an inside job killing American civilians to justify an invasion of Cuba.[1]

Ehret rejected these claims as inverted history, noting that the Kennedy brothers were the only senior figures opposed to toppling Castro and worked to subvert the [[bay-of-pigs|Bay of Pigs]] operation set in motion by the Dulles brothers during Eisenhower's final weeks; that Kennedy introduced NSAM 263 in October 1963 to begin a full withdrawal from Southeast Asia; and that the false-flag scheme attributed to RFK was in fact [[operation-northwoods|Operation Northwoods]], proposed by General [[lyman-lemnitzer|Lyman Lemnitzer]] and rejected by JFK.[1]

## Hollywood and Monroe Productions

Ehret situated Monroe inside a broader fight over the soul of Hollywood in which the Kennedys were heavily invested.[1] It is a documented fact, he wrote, that Bobby Kennedy communicated with Monroe while [[20th-century-fox|Fox Pictures]] was suing her for breach of contract as she created her own production studio to gain control over her creative work — communication he argues need not be read as evidence of a sexual relationship.[1] Describing Marilyn Monroe Productions, Maureen Lee Lenker wrote that it gave Monroe a nearly unmatched power to control her own career rather than remain at the mercy of a capricious studio, weakening the studio system's hold on its most valuable assets and kickstarting a trend followed by Frank Sinatra, Paul Newman, Sidney Poitier and Barbra Streisand.[1]

## MK Ultra thesis

Ehret stated that he positively agrees Monroe was murdered, but attributes a likely motive to her psychiatrist and her involvement in [[mk-ultra|MK Ultra]]-connected Monarch operations, adding that in the voluminous "Bobby Kennedy killed Marilyn" literature produced by Otash, Anthony Summers, Rothmiller and Netflix he has yet to encounter a single reference to MK Ultra, its Tavistockian management, or the broader occult takeover of Hollywood underway during the 1960s.[1]
