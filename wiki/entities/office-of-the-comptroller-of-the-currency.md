# Office of the Comptroller of the Currency

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) is one of the principal federal bank regulators in Washington, supervising nationally chartered banks.[1]

In December 2024 the [[trump-transition|Trump transition team]] began exploring pathways to dramatically shrink, consolidate or even eliminate the top bank watchdogs, and in interviews with potential nominees to lead the OCC and the [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation]], Trump advisers and officials from the newly created [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] asked whether the FDIC could be abolished and its deposit insurance function absorbed into the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]].[1] Any proposal to eliminate the FDIC or a comparable agency would require congressional action, and Washington has never shut down a major cabinet-level agency and has rarely closed non-cabinet agencies of the FDIC's stature.[1]

Bank executives were reported to be optimistic that the incoming administration would ease regulations on capital cushions, consumer protections and scrutiny of industry consolidation, though deposit insurance itself was described as near sacred.[1]
