# Lori Lightfoot

Lori Lightfoot is an American Democratic politician who served as mayor of Chicago and who lost her bid for a second term in the city's February 28, 2023 municipal election.[1]

## 2023 defeat

Lightfoot failed to advance to a runoff in the [[2023-chicago-mayoral-election|2023 Chicago mayoral race]], making her the first Chicago mayor to lose re-election since 1983.[1] She finished third with 16.4 percent of the vote, behind former Chicago Public Schools chief executive [[paul-vallas|Paul Vallas]] at 35.02 percent and Cook County Commissioner [[brandon-johnson|Brandon Johnson]] at 20.25 percent; Vallas and Johnson advanced to the April 4 runoff as the two highest vote-getters.[1] Addressing supporters shortly before 9 p.m. on election night, Lightfoot said, "Obviously, we didn't win the election. But, I stand here with my head held high and my heart full of thanks."[1] The result was reported by the Associated Press and the Chicago Sun-Times and aggregated by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], which also recalled her having claimed in the prior year to have the largest anatomy in the city during an incident in which she was accused of berating staff.[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] said he did not particularly care about Lightfoot's re-election or her replacement, considering the contest relatively micro, but found it significant that a New Englander with no habit of following the politics of states half a nation away nonetheless knew exactly who Lightfoot was and what system she represented.[1] He described this widespread recognition of "domestic enemies from the [[deep-state|Deep State]]'s System of Systems" as one of the great unwitting gifts of the COVID era: while the pandemic response inflicted lockdowns, potential vaccine injuries and immeasurable psychological damage on the young and old alike, it also pushed the officials nominally in charge of each state front and center, forcing them into the light — and, in his words, "we didn't forget those faces."[1]

## Harvard fellowship (2023)

Less than a month after leaving office, Lightfoot was named to a teaching post at Harvard.[2] Beginning in the fall of 2023 she was to teach a course at the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health tentatively titled "Health Policy and Leadership," and from the end of August would serve as the Richard L. and Ronay A. Menschel Senior Leadership Fellow.[2] Lightfoot had left office on May 15, 2023 after losing re-election.[2] The Menschel program is described by the school as an opportunity for people who have recently served in top-level positions in government, multilateral institutions, nonprofit organizations and journalism to mentor and teach students aspiring to similar roles, with Lightfoot holding regular office hours for students, faculty and staff.[2]

Badlands commentator [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] used the appointment to argue that [[harvard-university|Harvard]] had become an institution of ideological conformity rather than education, questioning what qualified a mayor who had presided over a major crime wave and troubled schools to teach, and framing the hire as a mechanism for maintaining and pushing an agenda rather than a merit-based selection.[2]
