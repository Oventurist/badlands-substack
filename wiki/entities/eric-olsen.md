# Eric Olsen

Eric Olsen is the attorney who argued against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s ballot eligibility before the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] in [[anderson-v-griswold|Anderson v. Griswold]] in December 2023.[1]

## Argument in Anderson v. Griswold

At the December 6, 2023 hearing, Olsen argued passionately that Trump's inclusion on the Republican primary ballot would dilute the votes cast for "qualified" candidates.[1] On the second question before the court — whether the events of January 6 constituted an insurrection at all — Olsen maintained that Judge Sarah Wallace's civil-court ruling that Trump intentionally incited insurrection should stand, a position Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized as asking the court to preserve a criminal finding delivered in a civil domain.[1] His opposing counsel, [[scott-gessler|Scott Gessler]], argued that January 6 was better understood as a riot; Ashe judged the arguments on both sides weak.[1] The case turned in part on whether Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]] applies to the president as an "Officer," and was expected to be appealed to the U.S. Supreme Court regardless of outcome.[1]

## The December 6 hearing

Olsen appeared alongside [[jason-murray|Jason Murray]] for the petitioners at the roughly two-hour argument before the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]].[2] Asked whether excluding [[donald-trump|Trump]] from Colorado's ballot while he appeared on ballots nationwide would mean unequal treatment of Colorado Republican voters, Olsen answered that Trump is disqualified because Judge [[sarah-wallace|Sarah Wallace]] had so found, and that his presence on the ballot would dilute Republicans' votes.[2] He said his clients were "Republicans and Independents who filed the suit because they want a fair shot in the Republican primary to vote for a qualified candidate, and have their support for a qualified candidate not be diluted through votes for a candidate who would definitely be disqualified," framing the "pro-democracy, pro-Colorado voter perspective" as declaring Trump ineligible before the election "so that the ballot reflects those who are qualified, and the will of the voter can be honored."[2]

One justice told Olsen he was placing too much weight on a single statutory reference to "qualified candidate," and several justices picked apart his arguments while others appeared sympathetic.[2] On the insurrection question he maintained that Wallace's civil-court criminal-style finding of intentional incitement must stand.[2] Closing, Olsen declared, "The Constitution, as we've discussed, is just a document," a remark [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read as revealing disdain for the will of the people.[2]

## Doxxing allegation

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], a Colorado voter who followed the state's ballot-access litigation closely, wrote in January 2024 that she had been doxxed by petitioners' counsel Eric Olsen, a former senior solicitor in the Colorado Attorney General's office, during the trial court proceedings in [[anderson-v-griswold|Anderson v. Griswold]].[3]
