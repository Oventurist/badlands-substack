# Ro Khanna

Ro Khanna is a Democratic member of the U.S. House of Representatives.[1]

## Epstein files disclosures

Following the February 2026 [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] release of millions of pages of [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] investigative documents, Khanna publicly named individuals appearing in the unredacted files, one of several congressional actions accompanying the disclosure.[1]

## Platner endorsement withdrawal (2026)

Khanna was among several prominent Democrats who quickly withdrew endorsements of Maine Senate candidate [[graham-platner|Graham Platner]] after sexual assault allegations against him became public.[2] Khanna called the allegations serious and credible and urged Platner to drop out of the race.[2]

## West Bank detention by Israeli settlers (July 2026)

During a three-day visit to the occupied West Bank, Khanna said armed Israeli settlers blocked his delegation near a Palestinian village, an account disputed in part by the Israeli military.[3] Photographs supplied to the Associated Press show armed men blocking vehicles carrying the California Democrat and his delegation near Khirbet Zanuta on July 8, and Khanna told Reuters that the men carried M4 rifles and prevented the group from leaving.[3]

Delegation member [[cameron-kasky|Cameron Kasky]] told Reuters that the group was held for more than an hour and appealed to the U.S. Embassy in Jerusalem for assistance, with officers who appeared to be police eventually intervening.[3] Khanna later said the settlers blocked the delegation for about 20 minutes before Israeli soldiers arrived and continued restricting its departure.[3] The Israeli military said troops had responded to a report that Israeli civilians were blocking foreign nationals and media, that they dispersed the civilians and reopened the road, and denied that soldiers participated in blocking the delegation; Khanna disputed that account, saying soldiers interacted with the settlers and continued preventing the group from leaving until the U.S. Embassy and Israeli police were contacted.[3]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] shared the short video Khanna posted of the encounter and wrote that Khanna was right, arguing that the [[israeli-settler-movement|settlers]] are zealots who cannot be reasoned with, negotiated with or intimidated, and who will fight anybody who obstructs them — including the United States.[3] He characterized the settler population as a monster created and enabled by American support: a minority within a highly insular society that protects its own, never told "no," never denied an indulgence, given every luxury, complete security and American weaponry, and now convinced it is on a mission from God.[3]
