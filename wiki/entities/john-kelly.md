# John Kelly

John Kelly is a retired United States Marine Corps general who served as White House Chief of Staff during the first administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] After leaving the administration he became a recurring critic of the former president, and his comments have frequently been cited by mainstream outlets as evidence of internal Republican misgivings about a second Trump term.[1]

## Prediction of a "non-stop gunfight"

In July 2023, The Hill reported that Kelly had predicted a second Trump term would amount to a "non-stop gunfight" with [[congress|Congress]].[1] The prediction was picked up by RedState columnist Ward Clark, who dismissed it as "belaboring the obvious" and argued that hostility toward Trump from Democrats — and from some Republicans — had been unprecedented in nearly fifty years of American political life, citing [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]]'s tearing up of a State of the Union address, two impeachments, and what the column described as politically motivated prosecutions.[1]

## Commentary in the Badlands News Brief

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], invoked Kelly's remark to argue the opposite conclusion: that a second, term-limited Trump presidency freed from re-election pressure would be an opportunity for aggressive political retribution and the reimplementation of [[america-first-movement|America First]] policy.[1] The commentary framed Kelly's warning as evidence that opponents feared an "O.K. Corral scenario" with Trump, and predicted a return to office exceeding a [[grover-cleveland|Grover Cleveland]]-style comeback in historical significance.[1]

## Cited as a first-term mole (Carlson, 2024)

In "The CIA is Being Thrown Under the Bus," Erik Carlson refers to "traitor John Kelly" as having lied about [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] calling veterans suckers and losers, complaining that people still believe such claims.[2]

Kelly is listed alongside [[alexander-vindman|Alexander Vindman]] and [[olivia-troye|Olivia Troye]] as figures whose accounts of the first Trump White House Carlson treats as fabrications, and as examples of the internal moles a second Trump administration would exclude.[2]
