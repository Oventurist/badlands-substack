# Viktor Vekselberg

Viktor Vekselberg is a Russian businessman and oligarch who has been the subject of United States sanctions and of long-running interest in Badlands Media coverage because of his documented proximity to American political figures.[1]

## Sanctions and property

Vekselberg owned four real properties in the United States that, following his designation as a sanctioned oligarch, could not lawfully be maintained with U.S. dollar payments. In February 2023 the [[southern-district-of-new-york|U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York]] unsealed an indictment charging his associate [[vladimir-voronchenko|Vladimir Voronchenko]] with running a scheme to make more than $4 million in such payments and with attempting to sell two of the properties.[1]

## Political connections

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] noted that Vekselberg was a substantial donor to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]], that [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] met with him during the trip to Russia on which Clinton was paid $500,000 for a speech, and that Vekselberg is a key figure in the [[uranium-one]] affair. Vekselberg was questioned by the Mueller special counsel team in 2018, later sanctioned by the Trump administration, sanctioned again under the Biden administration, and had his properties searched in September 2022.[1]

Just Human read the indictment of Vekselberg's associate — however perfunctory it might look given that the defendant had already fled the country — as an indication that the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] was "following the money" from Vekselberg outward.[1]

## February 2023 civil forfeiture complaint

On February 24, 2023 — the anniversary of Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine — federal prosecutors filed a civil forfeiture complaint against six real properties beneficially owned by Vekselberg, located in New York, New York; Southampton, New York; and Fisher Island, Florida, together worth approximately $75 million.[2] The complaint alleged the properties were the proceeds of sanctions violations and had been involved in international money laundering in promotion of sanctions violations committed by, among others, [[vladimir-voronchenko|Vladimir Voronchenko]], who had been indicted on February 7, 2023.[2]

The filing was announced by U.S. Attorney [[damian-williams|Damian Williams]], [[task-force-kleptocapture|Task Force KleptoCapture]] director Andrew C. Adams, HSI New York Special Agent in Charge Ivan J. Arvelo, and Acting Special Agent in Charge Maged Behnam of the [[fbi|FBI]] Miami Field Office.[2] Williams said the action sought forfeiture of properties "that his associate Vladimir Voronchenko maintained by funneling millions of dollars into the United States," and warned that the United States would "use every available tool to forfeit criminal proceeds" and direct that money to help Ukraine under newly enacted law.[2]

[[just-human|Just Human]] described Vekselberg as a long-time donor to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] and friend of the Clintons, a key figure in the Skolkovo and [[uranium-one]] scandals, and an indicted and sanctioned oligarch whose $90 million yacht had already been seized; his properties had been raided several months earlier and one of his British associates arrested the previous month, leading Just Human to conclude that "'Drain the Swamp' is real."[2]

## 2023 US enforcement campaign

Vekselberg was named by Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] as one of the most significant targets of the United States campaign against smuggling and money laundering networks supporting the Russian regime, alongside [[roman-abramovich|Roman Abramovich]], [[andrei-derkach|Andrei Derkach]] and [[oleg-deripaska|Oleg Deripaska]], all of whom the contributor described as members of "the international Swamp."[3] Just Human again identified Vekselberg as "a Clinton buddy and Foundation donor" in that commentary.[3] The wider effort, part of the [[us-sanctions-campaign-against-russian-oligarch-networks|U.S. sanctions campaign against Russian oligarch networks]], had by March 2023 produced more than $500 million in asset seizures and indictments of over 30 individuals and two corporate entities.[3]
