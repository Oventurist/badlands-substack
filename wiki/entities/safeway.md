# Safeway

Safeway is an American supermarket chain operated in common with [[albertsons|Albertsons]], and in June 2025 it became the focus of a multi-city labor dispute in Colorado that the Badlands News Brief treated as a case study in the decline of the "Big Grocery" model.[1]

## June 2025 Colorado strikes

Safeway workers in several Colorado cities entered the third day of labor strikes on Tuesday, June 17, 2025, striking against Safeway and Albertsons over alleged unfair labor practices, with picketing in Grand Junction prompting a temporary store closure.[1] The store at 2901 F Rd. appeared to have roughly 30 workers participating in the strike, according to a video posted on the [[united-food-and-commercial-workers|United Food and Commercial Workers]] Local 7 Facebook page.[1] On Monday night and Tuesday morning, Safeway and Albertsons workers in Fort Collins, Greeley, Longmont, Loveland and Colorado Springs all voted over 95% to authorize a strike, with next steps and walkout timing yet to be announced.[1]

## Commentary and context

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported visiting a Safeway to pick up prescriptions and finding about five workers striking, with the fish and meat departments unstaffed.[1] She argued the dispute had been simmering for years: preparation for the ill-fated Safeway–Albertsons merger led local stores to take extreme cost-cutting actions on headcount, and many store workers watched their jobs get automated away, particularly in skilled roles such as meat cutters (butchers), where local stores dramatically reduced hours and cut staffing for unionized meat department workers.[1] She noted the union had failed to organize effectively at the time because expectations of a post-merger windfall prevailed, and that conditions worsened after the merger fell through and the company struggled to define its future.[1] In every striking location, she observed, the meat cutters were the most visible participants.[1]

The commentary framed the strikes as too late to change the trajectory of grocery retail, predicting a future of sensors, self-checkouts and robots, and urging readers to reclaim their supply chains through local butchers, farmers markets and small businesses rather than relying on convenience-driven purchasing.[1]
