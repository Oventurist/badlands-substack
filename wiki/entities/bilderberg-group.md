# Bilderberg Group

The Bilderberg Group is a transatlantic private forum founded in 1955. [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes it as having been created by leading SS operatives such as [[prince-bernhard|Prince Bernhard]] of the Netherlands, and as having managed the takeover of nation states since its founding.[1]

Ehret raises the group in support of his broader argument that Nazism was not defeated in 1945 but absorbed: the majority of Nazis, including leading SS members, were sent to South America after the war and, instead of being punished, received new jobs working for [[allen-dulles|Allen Dulles]], MI6 and NATO as part of [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]].[1] He points to the same continuity in the Trans-Atlantic banking system, the revival of eugenics, proxy wars in Ukraine, and the technocracy of the European Union.[1]

## Harvard endowment overlap

"The Harvard Control Grid" lists Bilderberg advisory arms among the bodies on which the same people who run Harvard's endowment also serve, alongside [[brookings-institution|Brookings Institution]] boards, [[world-economic-forum|WEF]] strategic councils and the international investment committees of the Ford, Gates and [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller]] foundations.[2] The article names Bilderberg in its opening enumeration of the networks it says converge on the [[harvard-management-company|Harvard Management Company]], and argues that trustees, donors and advisors in these circles are often also recipients of the same institutions' funding, creating "a closed loop of thought, power, and self-preservation."[2]

## DeLarme's account: origins and structure

Part 4 of [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s series "The Men Behind the Curtain" treats the Bilderberg Group as the fourth clandestine faction of the Cabal examined after the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]], the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] and the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]].[3] He characterises the Bilderbergers as extremely powerful men and women, many of them belonging to royal European bloodlines, who convene annually in semi-secret to discuss the "issues of the day," and allows that conspiracy-minded observers who claim the group manufactures and manages world events "may not be wrong."[3]

DeLarme links the group's emergence to the creation of the [[european-union|European Union]], which he says some believe had been the goal of ancient orders dating as far back as the era of Merovingian King Dagobert II — an alleged brotherhood concealed within [[freemasonry|Freemasonry]] but hidden from lower-degree Masons, and the supposed progenitor of modern globalism.[3] He also identifies a constitutional motive: because several democratic monarchies in Western Europe bar members of their royal families from active political roles, Bilderberg meetings provide a workaround forum through which royal bloodlines can maintain influence over policy.[3]

The group originally had no name, and became identified with the Bilderberg Hotel in Oosterbeek, Holland, where it was first discovered by the public in 1954.[3] Its first U.S. gathering took place in February 1957 on Saint Simons Island near Jekyll Island, Georgia.[3] The official creation of the organisation followed unofficial meetings between members of Europe's elite in the 1940s, involving European foreign ministers, [[prince-bernhard|Prince Bernhard]] of the Netherlands and the Polish socialist [[jozef-retinger|Dr. Józef Hieronim Retinger]], who became known as the "father of the Bilderbergers."[3]

## Intelligence and CFR connections

Retinger was first ushered to America by then U.S. ambassador to England [[averell-harriman|Averell Harriman]], himself a CFR member, and on that trip is said to have visited [[david-rockefeller|David]] and [[nelson-rockefeller|Nelson Rockefeller]], [[john-foster-dulles|John Foster Dulles]] and then [[cia|CIA]] Director [[walter-bedell-smith|Walter Bedell Smith]].[3] Earlier he had formed the [[american-committee-on-united-europe|American Committee on a United Europe]] with future CIA Director and CFR member [[allen-dulles|Allen Dulles]], then CFR Director George Franklin, CIA official Thomas Braden and [[william-donovan|William Donovan]], former chief of the [[office-of-strategic-services|Office of Strategic Services]]; DeLarme notes Donovan began his intelligence career as an operative of J. P. Morgan Jr. and was known as an "Anglophile."[3] Another facilitator was Life magazine publisher [[c-d-jackson|C. D. Jackson]], who served under President Eisenhower as a special consultant for psychological warfare.[3]

DeLarme observes that many American legacy media figures have attended Bilderberg meetings yet little is ever reported, which has led some writers to allege that the group requests censorship and news management.[3] As with the Trilateral Commission and the CFR, Bilderbergers frequently carry cross-membership in two or more of the three groups.[3] Author Neil Wilgus is quoted calling the Bilderbergers "a sort of unofficial CFR, expanded to an international scale."[3] Former intelligence officer [[john-coleman|Dr. John Coleman]] claimed that "The Bilderberg Conference is the creation of MI6 under the direction of the Royal Institute of International Affairs," the British extension of the CFR known as [[chatham-house|Chatham House]]; DeLarme adds that given the U.S. intelligence links, it can legitimately be argued the conferences were at least partly organised and sponsored by the [[cia|CIA]].[3]

## Chairmen and notable participants

The primary impetus for the meetings came from Prince Bernhard, a former member of the Nazi Schutzstaffel and an employee of Germany's [[ig-farben|I.G. Farben]] in Paris, who chaired the group personally until 1976, when he resigned after revelations that he had accepted large payoffs from Lockheed to promote its aircraft in Holland.[3] Later chairmen include Britain's [[peter-carrington|Lord Peter Carrington]] — former cabinet minister, NATO secretary-general and president of the Royal Institute of International Affairs, and linked to the Rothschild banking empire by business and marriage — while DeLarme lists French businessman [[henri-de-castries|Henri de Castries]] as the then-current chairman.[3]

American participants named by DeLarme include CFR members George Ball, Dean Acheson, [[dean-rusk|Dean Rusk]], [[mcgeorge-bundy|McGeorge Bundy]], Christian Herter, [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]], Douglas Dillon, [[j-robert-oppenheimer|J. Robert Oppenheimer]], Walter Reuther, Jacob Javits, Robert McNamara, Walter Bedell Smith and General Lyman Lemnitzer.[3] Other noteworthy attendees have included J. William Fulbright, Henry Ford II, Georges Pompidou, Valéry Giscard d'Estaing, Helmut Schmidt and France's Baron Edmond de Rothschild.[3]

Heads of state have also passed through the group's orbit. DeLarme recounts the testimony of [[kitty-little|Dr. Kitty Little]], who said that at a 1940 Labour Party study group at Oxford a young speaker described a deliberately nameless group plotting a Marxist takeover of Britain, Europe and parts of Africa whose members would pose as moderates — the speaker being [[harold-wilson|Harold Wilson]], later prime minister.[3] In 1991 then-Arkansas Governor [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] was honoured as a Bilderberg guest and won the presidency the following year, and according to the Washington tabloid The Spotlight, [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] attended in 1997 as the first American first lady to do so.[3]

## Influence on policy

Investigative reporter [[james-p-tucker|James P. Tucker]] wrote that "The Bilderberg agenda is much the same as that of its brother group, the Trilateral Commission. . . . The two groups have an interlocking leadership and a common vision of the world. David Rockefeller founded the Trilateral but shares power in the older Bilderberg group with Rothschilds of Britain and Europe."[3] [[jack-sheinkman|Jack Sheinkman]], chairman of Amalgamated Bank and a Bilderberg member, said in 1996 that "In some cases discussions do have an impact and become policy," citing the common European currency and U.S. formal relations with China before Nixon opened them.[3] [[george-mcghee|George McGhee]], a Bilderberger and former U.S. ambassador to West Germany, acknowledged that the [[treaty-of-rome|Treaty of Rome]], which brought the European Common Market into being, "was nurtured at Bilderberg meetings."[3]

DeLarme speculates that Sheinkman may be among members who do not understand the true goals of the elite leadership, suggesting that much like the Templars within Freemasonry the Bilderbergs may have an inner circle privy to the real agenda.[3] That agenda, he proposes, may have been revealed by Prince Bernhard's remark that "It is difficult to reeducate the people who have been brought up on nationalism to the idea of relinquishing part of their sovereignty to a supernational body."[3]

## Bilderberg today

Nearly seventy years on, the group still meets and draws scrutiny from alternative media worldwide, though attention has shifted toward more public-facing bodies such as Klaus Schwab's [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]].[3] DeLarme notes that the same families recur across all of these gatherings, above all the Rockefellers and particularly David, and concedes the limits of research into a genuinely clandestine society: outside the inner circle, observers can only collect quotes from attendees and guess at the rest.[3]

## Blessing the Trilateral Commission

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 2" records that [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]]'s proposal for a council of the United States, Western Europe and Japan had first to be presented before "the creme de la creme of the ruling elite" at the Bilderberg group's April 1972 meeting in the Belgian town of Knokke-Heist, where it was reportedly met with enthusiasm.[4] Many of the financiers present were concerned about Nixon's devaluation of the dollar, surcharges on imports and the budding détente with China, all of which were causing relations with Japan to deteriorate, as well as growing energy problems arising from OPEC price increases.[4]

With the blessings of both the [[council-on-foreign-relations|CFR]] and the international Bilderbergers secured, the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] began organizing at [[kykuit|Kykuit]] in July 1972.[4] The article further notes overlapping membership among the three bodies — [[paul-volcker|Paul Volcker]] and [[alan-greenspan|Alan Greenspan]] belonged to all of them — and reports Jim Marrs's count of ten Bilderberg members on [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]]'s fifty-nine-member transition team.[4]
