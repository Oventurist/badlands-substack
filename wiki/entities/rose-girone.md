# Rose Girone

Rose Girone was believed to be the oldest living survivor of the Holocaust at the time of her death at the age of 113 in New York on Monday, March 3, 2025, where she had spent the last decade of her life.[1] Following her death, another woman, Mirjam Bolle, was believed to have become the oldest living Holocaust survivor.[1]

## Remembrances

Her daughter, Reha Benicassa, described her as "a terrific lady," saying that "nothing was too hard" and that "she wasn't fearful. She was an adventurous person."[1] Benicassa recalled her mother's maxims: "Don't sweat the small stuff," and "anything you can fix with money is not a problem."[1]

Greg Schneider, executive vice president of the Claims Conference, said that "Rose was an example of fortitude, but now we are obligated to carry on in her memory," adding that "the lessons of the Holocaust must not die with those who endured the suffering."[1]
