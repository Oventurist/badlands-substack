# Bear Stearns

Bear Stearns was one of the largest investment banks in the United States and a central casualty of the [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 financial crisis]].[1] In 2008 it was acquired by [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] for approximately $1.2 billion in a transaction facilitated by the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] in an attempt to prevent the firm's collapse.[1]

A May 2023 Badlands essay by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the Bear Stearns purchase, alongside JPMorgan's acquisition of [[washington-mutual|Washington Mutual]] for roughly $1.9 billion the same year, as evidence of a pattern in which the largest U.S. bank expands opportunistically through distressed acquisitions during financial crises — a pattern the essay argued repeated in the [[2023-banking-crisis|2023 banking crisis]] with the purchase of [[first-republic-bank|First Republic Bank]].[1] The essay grouped these deals with JPMorgan's crisis-era acquisitions of Bank of New York Mellon's shareholder services business, RBS Sempra Commodities' trading operations, and MF Global's stake in the London Metal Exchange.[1]

## BCCI clearing and Jeffrey Epstein (1976–1981)

[[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] was hired by Bear Stearns in 1976 without a degree or conventional credentials, on the strength of connections made while teaching at the [[dalton-school|Dalton School]].[2] By the late 1970s he handled complex trades in the firm's special products division.[2] During that period, Bear Stearns processed substantial transactions for the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|Bank of Credit and Commerce International]], an institution later exposed as a conduit for laundering funds in support of covert operations.[2] Court records and investigations, including the UK's "Sandstorm Report" unredacted in 2011, confirm the firm's role as a broker for BCCI, clearing transactions that disguised illicit cash movements.[2]

Epstein left the firm in 1981 under circumstances involving a regulatory violation related to insider-trading probes, which Bear Stearns downplayed; he immediately founded [[intercontinental-assets-group|Intercontinental Assets Group Inc.]][2] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] noted that the [[new-york-times|New York Times]]' December 2025 investigation of Epstein's wealth left the BCCI trades at Bear Stearns unmentioned entirely.[2]
