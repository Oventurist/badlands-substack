# Hosni Mubarak

Hosni Mubarak was the president of Egypt and a United States ally since 1981 who was deposed in 2011 during the [[arab-spring|Arab Spring]].[1] Badlands Media presents his abandonment by the Obama administration as one of the central puzzles of the period: Egypt was one of America's biggest allies in the Middle East, yet [[barack-obama|Obama]] withdrew support and allowed his removal.[1]

## The oppression question

The article concedes that Mubarak was indeed an oppressor, but argues that the group he was oppressing was the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]].[1] This, in the Badlands reading, is the common thread linking Mubarak to [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]], [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]], Zine El Abidine Ben Ali of Tunisia, and the other leaders targeted during the Arab Spring: each was preventing the Brotherhood from enforcing [[sharia-law|Sharia law]] in his country and therefore stood in the way of what the article calls the [[sixteen-year-plan|sixteen year plan]].[1]

## Aftermath

Mubarak's overthrow led to an election, promoted by U.S. government-financed NGOs including the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]] and the [[national-democratic-institute|National Democratic Institute]], that installed Muslim Brotherhood leader [[mohamed-morsi|Mohamed Morsi]] as president.[1] Morsi's subsequent attempt to impose Sharia law triggered mass protests and his removal by the Egyptian military, after which Egyptian investigations concluded that the American NGOs had been behind the overthrow of the government during the Arab Spring and began arresting those involved.[1]
