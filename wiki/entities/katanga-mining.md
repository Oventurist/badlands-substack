# Katanga Mining

Katanga Mining is a copper and cobalt mining company operating in the Katanga region of the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of Congo]], historically associated with the Israeli oligarch [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] and later wholly owned by [[glencore|Glencore]].[1]

## Ownership history

Gertler's company held the exclusive mining rights for the region for which it is named.[1] After experiencing financial trouble in 2007, Gertler sought a $45 million investment from Glencore to keep Katanga Mining solvent.[1] After Gertler's assets were frozen in 2017 under [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]] — issued by [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] and cited in connection with allegations that Gertler used child slave labor in his African mines — Glencore was forced to buy out Gertler's shares and became the sole owner of Katanga Mining.[1]

## Regional context

The Katanga region lies within the African copper belt, a roughly 450-kilometre stretch running from northwest Luanshya in Zambia to Katanga in Congo, and has hosted large-scale copper mining for more than a century; in the 1950s it was the world's largest copper-producing area.[1] A November 2024 mountain collapse in Katanga, captured on video and circulated on X, revealed large copper reserves and renewed attention to forced evictions and human rights abuses linked to mining expansion in the area.[1] Copper and cobalt from the region are extracted largely for lithium-ion batteries used in electric vehicles and other "clean energy" technologies.[1]
