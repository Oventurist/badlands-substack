# Percy Sutton

Percy Sutton was a former Manhattan Borough President, longtime Democratic Party figure and founder of Inner City Broadcasting whose 2008 television interview is treated in the Badlands Media article "Bad Actors" as the single public crack in an otherwise concealed patronage network surrounding [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[1]

## The NY1 interview

Speaking with NY1's Dominic Carter, Sutton — then in his late eighties and largely retired from public life — said he had been asked to write a letter of recommendation to Harvard Law School on Obama's behalf by [[khalid-al-mansour|Khalid al-Mansour]] of Texas, whom he described as "the principle adviser to one of the world's richest men" and as a man who was "raising money for [Obama]."[1] Sutton recalled the request: "He wrote to me about him... there is a young man that has applied to Harvard. I know that you have a few friends up there because you used to go up there to speak. Would you please write a letter in support of him?"[1] Sutton said he acted on al-Mansour's advice and wrote to his Harvard contacts "saying to them I thought there was a genius that was going to be available, and I certainly hoped they would treat him kindly."[1]

Sutton did not say why al-Mansour was assisting Obama, how al-Mansour had discovered him, or from whom the money was being raised.[1]

## Reaction and denials

The Obama campaign flatly denied the story, with spokesman Ben LaBolt stating that Obama did not know al-Mansour, had no relationship with Sutton, and that no such letter was known to have been written.[1] Politico noted an apparent inconsistency in Sutton's account — Obama applied to Harvard from Chicago, not New York — and pointed to $42,753 in reported student debt as the explanation for how Obama financed his education.[1]

Joe Lange argues that Sutton "wasn't supposed to ever mention any of this publicly," that the coordinated response portrayed the veteran operative as old and senile, and that a purported family spokesperson falsely told reporters Sutton had retracted the story.[1] In Lange's reading, Sutton's disclosure was a genuine breach of operational secrecy that could have derailed the [[sixteen-year-plan|sixteen year plan]] had it been pursued by the press.[1]
