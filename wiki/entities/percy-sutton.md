# Percy Sutton

Percy Sutton (1920–2009) was an American attorney, civil rights figure, broadcasting executive, and Democratic politician who served as Manhattan Borough President. He founded Inner City Broadcasting. In the Badlands Media article "[[bad-actors|Bad Actors]]" he appears as the source of a 2008 claim linking [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] to [[khalid-al-mansour|Khalid al-Mansour]].

## The 2008 NY1 interview

In an interview with NY1's Dominic Carter, Sutton — then in his late eighties and largely out of public life — said that al-Mansour, whom he described as the principal adviser to one of the world's richest men and as raising money for Obama, had contacted him to ask a favour: that he write a letter supporting Obama's application to Harvard Law School. Sutton recalled the request as "there is a young man that has applied to Harvard… I know that you have a few friends up there because you used to go up there to speak. Would you please write a letter in support of him?" Sutton said he complied, writing to his Harvard contacts that "I thought there was a genius that was going to be available, and I certainly hoped they would treat him kindly." He did not say why al-Mansour was helping Obama, how al-Mansour had discovered him, or from whom money was being raised.

## Aftermath

The Obama campaign denied the story, with spokesman Ben LaBolt telling Politico that Obama had no relationship with Sutton and that Obama was in Chicago, not New York, when he applied to Harvard. The Badlands Media article argues that Obama's allies worked to discredit Sutton by portraying the long-time Democratic operative as old and senile, and that a purported family spokesperson falsely told the media that Sutton had retracted the account. The article's position is that Sutton "wasn't supposed to ever mention any of this publicly," and it treats his disclosure as an accidental exposure of the patronage network the author attributes to [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]].
