# Dali (container ship)

The **Dali** is a 984-foot Singapore-flagged container ship that struck and destroyed the Francis Scott Key Bridge at the Port of Baltimore on March 26, 2024.[1] Following the [[francis-scott-key-bridge-collapse|collapse]], the vessel remained in the shipping channel amid the wreckage, blocking port traffic.[1]

Captain [[john-konrad|John Konrad]], CEO of the shipping-industry site [[gcaptain|gCaptain]], reported that the Dali was apparently "sitting atop a high-pressure underwater gas line," and that sources at ICS indicated the salvage effort would likely be delayed while the line was surveyed and additional risk assessed.[1]

The ship is carrying 1.8 million gallons of fuel, a fact [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] highlighted in raising questions about whether hull damage had compromised fuel storage, whether the pipeline beneath the bridge had been damaged, and whether moving the vessel could create spill conditions.[1] The ship's owner, Grace Ocean, had contended immediately after the disaster that no pollution had resulted, despite sheening observed on the water near the crash site.[1]

## Hazardous cargo

The [[national-transportation-safety-board|National Transportation Safety Board]] announced on March 27, 2024 that the Dali was carrying hazardous materials, with 56 containers aboard holding corrosives, flammables and lithium ion batteries; some containers were breached and a sheen was identified in the water.[2] The vessel's voyage data recorder was recovered, and NTSB Chair [[jennifer-homendy|Jennifer Homendy]] said the investigation could take 12 to 24 months with a preliminary report in two to four weeks.[2] The ship is owned by [[grace-ocean|Grace Ocean]] and was operated by [[maersk|Maersk]] between the US East Coast and East Asia.[2] Federal officials at the [[cybersecurity-and-infrastructure-security-agency|Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]] were monitoring the roughly 1.8 million gallons of fuel aboard for "spill potential," and the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] declared the surrounding water unsafe for divers.[2]

## FBI criminal investigation (April 2024)

In April 2024 the [[fbi|FBI]] opened a criminal investigation into the Dali, focused in part on whether crew members knew there were problems with the ship when it left port.[3] The 984-foot container vessel, managed by [[synergy-marine-group|Synergy Marine Group]], had suffered a "complete blackout" minutes before striking the bridge.[3] Agents boarded the ship just after dawn on Monday, April 15, claiming jurisdiction from the Coast Guard; the matter was handled by the U.S. attorney's office in Maryland and was separate from the [[national-transportation-safety-board|NTSB]] investigation into the cause of the crash.[3] Earlier that month Synergy Marine had filed a preemptive petition in the U.S. district court in Baltimore seeking to limit its liability for any loss or damage arising from the casualty.[3]

## Crew confinement and controlled demolition, May 2024

Seven weeks after the Dali struck the Francis Scott Key Bridge, a controlled explosion on Monday, May 13, 2024 sent the remaining spans of the bridge into the Patapsco River while nearly two dozen sailors sheltered below deck in the ship's hull.[4] The 948-foot (289 m) container ship had been at the start of a 27-day voyage from Baltimore to Sri Lanka when it struck the bridge, sending thousands of tonnes of steel and cement into the river and leaving the vessel stranded beneath a mass of shredded metal.[4] The collapse killed six people on the bridge.[4]

A preliminary [[national-transportation-safety-board|NTSB]] report found that two electrical blackouts disabled equipment ahead of the incident, and that the ship had lost power twice in the ten hours before the crash.[4] The crew of twenty Indians and one Sri Lankan national was unable to disembark because of visa restrictions, a lack of required shore passes, and parallel investigations by the NTSB and the [[fbi|FBI]].[4] The sailors had their phones confiscated and remained cut off from the outside world, with ministers, catering companies and local Baltimore community groups bringing them charity.[4] Operator [[synergy-marine-group|Synergy Marine]] said the sailors remained aboard because "nobody knows the ship better than they do."[4]
