# White House Correspondents' Association

The White House Correspondents' Association (WHCA) is the organisation of journalists who cover the presidency, historically responsible for administering press access and for the annual White House Correspondents' Dinner.[1] As described in March 2025 coverage, the group was founded because "the presidents at the time were not doing enough press conferences," giving the association a mandate to press for access on behalf of the working press corps.[1]

## Leavitt's boycott and the access fight

In March 2025, White House Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] announced on Sean Spicer's podcast that she would not attend the association's April 26 dinner, saying: "I will not be in attendance at the White House Correspondents' Dinner, and that's breaking news for 'The Sean Spicer Show.'"[1] Leavitt argued the WHCA "has truly become a monetized monopoly over the White House and the coverage of the president of the United States in America."[1]

She contrasted the association's founding rationale with present conditions, saying that under [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] the shortage of presidential press availability no longer exists and that "the priorities of the media have shifted, especially with this new digital age."[1] Leavitt further characterised the WHCA as an "exclusive group of journalists who cover this White House," who "have not really welcomed other people, new media, independent journalists, with open arms," justifying the administration's decision to determine itself "who gets to be part of that 13-person press pool, who gets to ask the president of the United States questions in the Oval Office, aboard Air Force One."[1]

## Badlands analysis

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the boycott as a test of whether the administration's press-access overhaul — including the creation of a "new media seat" and the removal of journalists deemed to have abused access — was substantive or cosmetic.[1] Her assessment was that "the WCHA's power lies in its legitimacy": if principal administration figures decline to attend its events, "their parties will be sad and super mockable, and the people that throw the parties will lose their credibility."[1] She welcomed the development as overdue, arguing it was "long past time for the professional corporate liars to lose their power and legitimacy."[1]
