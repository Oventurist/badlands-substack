# Optimus

Optimus is the humanoid robot developed by [[tesla-inc|Tesla Inc.]], which chief executive [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] has predicted could revolutionize the global economy even if it sharply reduces the need for human labor.[1]

## Development and demonstrations

As of November 2025 the robot was not yet ready for mass production, and technical challenges surrounding manufacturing remained unresolved.[1] Tesla nonetheless demonstrated its capabilities at public events, including handing out candy on Halloween and performing Kung Fu alongside the actor Jared Leto.[1]

## Musk's claims

Speaking at a Tesla shareholder meeting, Musk expressed confidence in the machine's potential to eradicate poverty, stating that "Optimus will actually eliminate poverty."[1] He further suggested that Optimus could transform the criminal justice system by monitoring and preventing crime rather than relying on physical incarceration, and tied the technology to the prospect of a universal income as human labor is displaced.[1]

## May 2025 dancing demonstration

In May 2025 Tesla released a roughly 42-second video, following a teaser shared earlier that week by Musk, showing Optimus performing a variety of dances including ballet, the running man, and a variation of the "Dougie."[2] Although the robot was likely trained using a motion-capture bodysuit, observers noted that the most impressive aspect was its ability to balance itself throughout the routines without a harness or other support.[2] Musk said in a reply that he would "have an Optimus dance troupe on stage with me at the Tesla shareholders meeting."[2]

The rollout drew unfavourable commentary when a user asked Musk's own AI chatbot, [[grok|Grok]], to evaluate the machine. Grok responded that Optimus "shows advanced mobility with its dancing, highlighting strong balance and coordination," but that "its real-world use is limited, mainly in Tesla's factories for simple tasks, with broader deployment planned for 2026."[2] Grok ranked Optimus last on its list of the top five humanoid robots, describing it as having "impressive mobility but early in practical application," a candid assessment that contrasted with the hype around the demonstration and drew criticism from fans and skeptics alike.[2] Badlands host GMONEY joked that readers unwilling to give up their combustion engines would nonetheless end up buying one of the robots.[2]

## Commentary

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded sardonically, writing that he hoped lawmakers would "fix the laws, the courts, and the definition of cruel and unusual punishment" before crime is stopped by citizens being constantly chaperoned by a personal Robocop.[1]
