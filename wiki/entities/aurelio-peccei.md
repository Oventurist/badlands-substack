# Aurelio Peccei

Aurelio Peccei was an Italian industrialist and a co-founder of the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]], the neo-Malthusian think tank formed in 1968.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes Peccei and his co-founder [[alexander-king|Sir Alexander King]] as "two misanthropes" and self-professed Malthusians who promulgated the doctrine that the age of scientific progress and industrial growth must stop so that the world could reset its values under a new paradigm of zero technological growth.[1]

## Corporate and financial connections

Quoting historian [[f-william-engdahl|F. William Engdahl]], Ehret records that Peccei was a senior manager of the Fiat car company, owned by the Italian Agnelli family, and that Fiat's Gianni Agnelli was an intimate friend of [[david-rockefeller|David Rockefeller]] and a member of the International Advisory Committee of Rockefeller's Chase Manhattan Bank.[1] Agnelli and Rockefeller are said to have been close friends since 1957, and Agnelli became a founding member of Rockefeller's [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] in 1973.[1] In Engdahl's account it was David Rockefeller who founded the Club of Rome in 1968 together with Peccei and King.[1]

## World Problematique

Peccei was an advocate of the pseudoscience dubbed [[world-problematique|"World Problematique"]], developed in the early 1960s and described as "the science of global problems."[1] Its adherents held that the future could be known by analysing the infinite array of problems humanity creates in modifying its environment, and that every human intervention produces dis-equilibrium and therefore unpredictability.[1] Ehret argues that the logical terminus of this outlook is the conclusion that eliminating "the problem that causes all problems" — humanity itself — would produce a clean, pre-determined world of perfect stasis.[1]

## Legacy

Under Peccei's leadership the Club of Rome commissioned the study that became [[limits-to-growth|*Limits to Growth*]] (1972), which Ehret calls the bible and blueprint of the anti-humanist movement that birthed today's [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]] agenda.[1]
