# Aimee Harris

Aimee Harris is the woman who stole the diary of first daughter Ashley Biden and
who, in April 2024, faced a Justice Department demand for prison time.[1]

## Prosecution and sentencing

Harris pleaded guilty in August 2022 to conspiring to commit interstate
transportation of stolen property in connection with the diary theft.[1]
Prosecutors for the US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York
had initially sought a sentence of six months of home confinement followed by
three years of supervised release.[1] On Tuesday, 2 April 2024, the Justice
Department instead asked a New York district judge to sentence her to four to
ten months in prison.[1]

Prosecutors argued for the stiffer sentence on the grounds that Harris had
requested that her sentencing hearing date be moved twelve times, characterizing
her excuses as an effort to "improperly delay" her punishment.[1]

## Sentencing, April 2024

Harris was sentenced on Tuesday, April 9, 2024 to one month in prison and three years of supervised release, with reporting also describing three months of home confinement; the statutory maximum had been five years.[2] Nicholas Biase, chief of public affairs for the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York, confirmed the sentence.[2] The Department of Justice had asked for four to ten months of incarceration plus three years of supervision, and the hearing had been postponed twelve times amid difficulties with paperwork deadlines and traffic violations.[2]

## Background to the theft

[[ashley-biden|Ashley Biden]] stayed at a Delray Beach, Florida property in June 2020 and was given permission to leave a number of personal items there when she departed.[2] Two days later Harris was invited to stay at the same property temporarily and discovered the belongings, which included highly personal diary entries, a digital storage card, tax and financial records, a cellphone, family photographs, books, clothing and luggage.[2] Two months afterward she contacted [[robert-kurlander|Robert Kurlander]], who agreed to help sell the material; the pair were paid $20,000 each by [[project-veritas|Project Veritas]].[2] Kurlander's own sentencing was set for October 25.[2]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] wrote that Harris and Kurlander first offered the diary to the campaign of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], which declined and told them to take it to the FBI, and argued the affair may have been a trap designed to induce the campaign to purchase and publicise possibly forged material.[2]
