# University of Toronto

The University of Toronto (commonly "U of T") is a major Canadian research university that features in commentary on academic freedom as a site where, critics argue, ideological orthodoxy has displaced open inquiry.[1]

## Sanctioning of Leigh Rivers

The university drew attention when [[leigh-rivers|Leigh Rivers]], an instructor in its chemical and physical sciences program, was chastised by his department for running a [[spectrum-street-epistemology|Spectrum Street epistemology]] exercise designed to encourage diversity of opinion on campus.[1] The chair of the Physical and Chemical Sciences Department reportedly objected to prompts including a statement about Prof. [[jordan-peterson|Jordan Peterson]], claims about political bias in Western science, and the proposition "Men can become women," which was described as traumatic and as requiring trigger warnings and safe spaces.[1]

Commentators found the episode particularly striking because Peterson had himself been a U of T professor, making the suggestion that students could not handle a debate touching on him seem, in the words of one account, "poignantly absurd."[1] The chair's suggested replacement prompt — "Pineapple belongs on pizza" — was cited as evidence of the trivialisation of classroom debate.[1]

## Wider context

The Rivers case is presented as one instance of a broader pattern in which North American universities are said to have been captured by neo-Marxist or "woke" ideology, with faculty reporting surges in student accommodation letters and an atmosphere in which certain topics are treated as too taboo for discussion.[1] The Toronto chapter of the [[heterodox-academy|Heterodox Academy]] held a screening of [[the-coddling-of-the-american-mind|The Coddling of the American Mind]] in the city, framed as a sign that the local academic class was beginning to organise a response.[1]
