# Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps

The Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps (XPCC) is a Chinese paramilitary corporate entity that was sanctioned by the United States government in 2020 for its role in the genocide of the [[uyghurs|Uyghur]] population in [[xinjiang|Xinjiang]].[1]

## Character and sanctions

The XPCC functions simultaneously as a commercial conglomerate and as an armed force. Journalist [[melissa-chen|Melissa Chen]] described it as "by all accounts a repressive, colonial institution" on account of its land seizures and its function as a military force that suppresses the Uyghurs.[1] Its designation rests on U.S. Department of State findings and is reinforced by the [[uyghur-forced-labor-prevention-act|Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act]] of 2022, which explicitly bans dealings with the XPCC and other entities tied to human rights abuses in Xinjiang.[1]

## Harvard connection

In April 2025 Chen reported that [[harvard-university|Harvard University]], through the [[harvard-th-chan-school-of-public-health|T.H. Chan School of Public Health]] and the [[harvard-china-health-partnership|Harvard-China Health Partnership]], had trained XPCC officials as late as October 2023 — three years after the sanctions took effect.[1] Harvard's public defense was that the goal of the training was to help China develop insurance programs, a rationale Badlands Media derided as equivalent to claiming a partnership with the Gestapo to improve retirement plans.[1]

Badlands Media argued that this conduct exposes Harvard to enforcement under [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]], which blocks the property of persons involved in serious human rights abuse or corruption.[1]
