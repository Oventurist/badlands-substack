# Maricopa County

Maricopa County is the most populous county in Arizona, encompassing the Phoenix metropolitan area, and became the central battleground in disputes over the [[2022-arizona-general-election|2022 Arizona general election]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Badlands Media contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]], a resident of the county since 1984, described it as offering "the best of all worlds" — palm trees, cactus, swimming pools and coveted winter weather — before turning to what she called the appalling state of its election administration.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## Election administration

County elections are overseen by the [[maricopa-county-board-of-supervisors|Maricopa County Board of Supervisors]], chaired in 2022 by [[bill-gates-arizona|Bill Gates]], with tabulation centralized at the Maricopa County Tabulation and Election Center (MCTEC).[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Ballots and materials move between county facilities and outside vendors — including [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]] — by transport truck, a practice the county publicly defended in a series of explainer videos posted to its Twitter account in the days after the election.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

On November 8, 2022, the county acknowledged that at least sixty voting locations suffered problems tied to non-uniform [[ballot-on-demand-printers|ballot-on-demand printer]] configuration settings, producing ballots that on-site tabulators rejected.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Voters affected by the failures were directed to deposit ballots in the containers known as [[door-3-ballots|"Door 3"]]; Gillespie recounted that her own polling place used a receptacle labeled "Box 3" and that she and her husband were assured the ballots would be escorted to MCTEC by one Republican and one Democrat.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## 2026 FBI investigation

In March 2026 the [[fbi|FBI]] obtained election-related records tied to Maricopa County as part of a federal criminal investigation into election administration running from 2020 through 2024, using a grand jury subpoena that sought "terabytes of data" and other materials connected to voting systems and election procedures.[2] The investigation followed observations by House Administration Committee monitors at the third-party ballot processing facility operated for the county by [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]], where completed mail ballots and blank ballots were reportedly stored in the same room without state officials present.[2] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] amplified the reporting on Truth Social, and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the development against her 2021 timeline of the county's obstruction during the Maricopa audit, writing that "this conduct is now being investigated" and that "we're closer than we've ever been."[2]

## Attorney General scrutiny

In a November 19, 2022 letter, Assistant Attorney General [[jennifer-wright|Jennifer Wright]] informed the [[maricopa-county-attorneys-office|Maricopa County Attorney's Office]] that hundreds of citizen complaints and first-hand witness accounts had raised concerns "regarding Maricopa's lawful compliance with Arizona election law."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) The letter asserted that the county "appears to have failed to adhere to the statutory guidelines in segregating, counting, tabulating, tallying, and transporting the 'Door 3' ballots," noted the county's admission that non-tabulated ballots had been commingled with tabulated ballots at some locations, and cited a sworn observer complaint that more than 1,700 non-tabulated "Door 3" ballots from a single location had been placed in black duffle bags reserved for tabulated ballots.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Wright demanded a response before the county submitted its official [[canvassing|canvass]] to the Secretary of State, due on or before November 28, 2022 under A.R.S. § 16-642(A).[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## Recorder–Board conflict (2026)

In June 2026, The Atlantic and local outlets described an ongoing power struggle between County Recorder [[justin-heap|Justin Heap]] and the [[maricopa-county-board-of-supervisors|Board of Supervisors]] over control of key election functions, including ballot drop-box locations and chain-of-custody procedures.[3] Officials warned that if the disputes remained unresolved they could complicate preparations for upcoming elections.[3] The tensions coincided with reporting that the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration was preparing guidance conditioning [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] grants on state adoption of election-related measures.[3]

## Supervisor Bill Gates PTSD controversy (2023)

In May 2023, county supervisor [[bill-gates-arizona|Bill Gates]] said he was suffering from PTSD and "crying" over criticism of the county's administration of the 2020 and 2022 elections, comparing the board's duty to certify results to his grandfather's World War II service.[4] Conservative outlets and Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] condemned the comparison, with DeLarme arguing that Maricopa's role made Arizona the most critical midterm state for establishment interests given the state's semiconductor industry, border corridors, and the candidacy of [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]].[4]

## Signature verification trial and county investigation (2023)

Maricopa County's conduct of the November 8, 2022 general election was the subject of the remanded Count III trial in [[lake-v-hobbs|Lake v. Hobbs et al.]], which asked solely whether the county complied with [[arizona-revised-statutes-16-550|A.R.S. § 16-550]] on [[signature-verification|signature verification]].[7] Voters across the county waited hours in line on election day only to be unable to cast ballots because of technology failures, and were told that ballots deposited in "Drawer Three" would still be counted.[7]

The county's own [[maricopa-county-2022-ballot-on-demand-printer-investigation|Ballot on Demand printer investigation]] revealed that Runbeck and county technical staff traveled to roughly 70 vote centers — about half — to troubleshoot printer problems, and confirmed that the ballot image had been increased from 19 to 20 inches before the election, while maintaining that the county did not know how the change was made.[7]

At the May 17–19, 2023 trial, two whistleblowers from the [[maricopa-county-tabulation-and-election-center|Maricopa County Tabulation and Election Center]] testified about breakdowns in the county's Level 1 and Level 2 review process, and plaintiffs' expert [[erich-speckin|Erich Speckin]] presented the county's own data showing 274,319 signature comparisons completed in two seconds or less and 70,839 in one second or less, plus more than 45,000 approvals via batch upload.[7] The county's director of elections for mail-in voting, [[ray-valenzuela|Rey Valenzuela]], was its only witness.[7] Plaintiffs also alleged that the county had conducted a secret logic and accuracy test in violation of Arizona law, producing the very failures seen on election day, and had hidden that evidence from the court and the public.[7]

## Voter roll citizenship litigation (2024)

In August 2024 [[america-first-legal|America First Legal]] sued Maricopa County Recorder [[stephen-richer|Stephen Richer]], alleging that the county had failed to remove non-citizens from its voter rolls and that Richer had not performed the monthly list maintenance required under Arizona law to verify the citizenship of registrants who had not provided proof.[5] The suit, brought for the [[strong-communities-foundation-of-arizona|Strong Communities Foundation of Arizona]] and a naturalized-citizen voter, cited an increase in county registrations lacking proof of citizenship from 21,595 in April 2024 to 26,108 in July 2024; such voters may participate in federal but not state elections in Arizona.[5]

## Focus of the "timing" thesis (November 2022)

Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argued that Maricopa County's centrality was not accidental: the only [[qanon|Q]] post giving actual vote counts from the [[2018-midterm-elections|2018 midterms]] named Maricopa alone among all counties in the country, Arizona was the only state legislature to act on alleged 2020 fraud, and the [[maricopa-county-forensic-audit|forensic audit]] conducted there was, in his account, the only such audit ever performed on an American election.[8]

That audit, commissioned by the [[arizona-state-senate|Arizona Senate]] and carried out by four out-of-state firms including [[cyber-ninjas|Cyber Ninjas]], produced a hand recount that added 360 votes to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s total in a state he had carried by 10,457 votes, alongside a ballot-level analysis identifying 49,718 questionable votes.[8] Following the [[2022-arizona-general-election|2022 general election]], the county faced simultaneous demands for evidence from the state legislature and from the [[arizona-attorney-generals-office|Attorney General's office]] Election Integrity Unit before certification, while a second Republican-controlled county delayed its own certification in protest at Maricopa's Election Day problems.[8]

## 2022 count delays

Maricopa County, home to Phoenix and its surrounding communities and roughly 4.5 million residents, became the focus of national and international attention after the November 8, 2022 election.[9] Writing on the afternoon of November 10, [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] reported that only about 62,000 ballots had been released in the two full days since polls closed, prompting a wave of memes and, in her words, making Arizona "the laughingstock" of the country and the world.[9] [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] observed that morning that "the most committed, loyal Republican voters in Arizona have not even had their envelopes opened yet."[9]

Gillespie described her own polling-place experience in the county: she completed a ballot at home for reference, was checked in and ID-verified by a poll worker who referred to the reference ballot as her "cheater ballot," had that ballot "spoiled" with a black marker, received a freshly printed ballot at a site that still had working printer paper and ink, and — with no functioning tabulator available — was made to deposit it in the "now-infamous 'Box 3'."[9]
