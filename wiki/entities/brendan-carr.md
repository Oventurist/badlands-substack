# Brendan Carr

Brendan Carr is a commissioner of the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]] who, in November 2023, publicly warned that a pending federal rulemaking would allow the government to "micromanage nearly every aspect of how the internet functions."[1]

## 2023 warning on internet regulation

Carr's statement centered on the Biden administration's push for the FCC to implement a one-page section of the 2021 [[infrastructure-investment-and-jobs-act|Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act]], which he characterized as containing "new rules of breathtaking scope, all in the name of 'digital equity'."[1] He argued that the administration's economic policies were to blame for the "skyrocketing costs of building Internet infrastructure in this country" and for "broadband policies that are failing," and that regulatory red tape had led the FCC to needlessly block and delay new broadband infrastructure builds even as it sought to expand new 5G services.[1]

## Commentary

Coverage of the warning appeared in [[vigilant-news|Vigilant News]] and was reprinted in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of November 10, 2023.[1] Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] noted that the FCC, ostensibly created to safeguard citizens from the telecommunications sector, has long been regarded within the [[truth-community|truth community]] as captured by the administrative state, and described Carr's stance as unexpected — "the equivalent of FDA Commissioner [[robert-califf|Robert Califf]] coming out and admitting that Big Pharma pressured them to approve new drugs."[1] DeLarme declined to call Carr a hero but credited him with bringing an otherwise obscure plan to public attention, expressing hope that more bureaucrats would similarly activate against the establishment.[1]

## Appointment as FCC chairman (2024)

On Sunday, November 17, 2024, President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] named Carr, then an FCC commissioner, as chair of the agency.[2] As one of the agency's five Senate-confirmed commissioners serving staggered five-year terms, Carr was at the time the senior Republican at the [[federal-communications-commission|FCC]]; the position of chair is designated by the president from among the pool of confirmed commissioners and does not require another Senate confirmation.[2] Carr had been nominated as a commissioner by both Trump and President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], serving across both administrations.[2]

In a statement posted to Truth Social, Trump said, "I first nominated Commissioner Carr to the FCC in 2017, and he has been confirmed unanimously by the United States Senate three times," adding that Carr's current term ran through 2029 and that he would be designated "permanent Chairman."[2] Trump described Carr as "a warrior for Free Speech" who "has fought against the regulatory Lawfare that has stifled Americans' Freedoms, and held back our Economy," and predicted he would "end the regulatory onslaught that has been crippling America's Job Creators and Innovators, and ensure that the FCC delivers for rural America."[2]

## Skydance–Paramount merger approval (July 2025)

On July 24, 2025, as chairman of the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]], Carr announced that the agency would allow [[paramount-global|Paramount]] to merge with the Hollywood studio [[skydance-media|Skydance]], clearing one of the most highly scrutinized media deals in a decade.[3] He said the approval followed assurances from Skydance that the new company was committed to unbiased journalism and would not establish programs related to diversity, equity and inclusion.[3]

"Americans no longer trust the legacy national news media to report fully, accurately and fairly," Carr said in his statement. "It is time for a change. That is why I welcome Skydance's commitment to make significant changes at the once storied CBS broadcast network."[3]

## Dissent from the Starlink subsidy denial

When the Federal Communications Commission reaffirmed in December 2023 its refusal to grant SpaceX's Starlink unit $885.5 million in rural broadband subsidies, Commissioner Brendan Carr dissented alongside the commission's other Republican member.[4] Carr said the FCC was joining "the growing list of administrative agencies that are taking action against Elon Musk's businesses" and argued that the decision "certainly fits the Biden Administration's pattern of regulatory harassment."[4] The dissenters also contended that the agency was improperly holding SpaceX to 2025 performance targets three years early.[4]
