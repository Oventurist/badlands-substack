# British Empire

The **British Empire** is treated in Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as the driving agency behind the alliance system that produced the First World War.[1]

At the opening of the twentieth century the empire faced what the article calls its major threat: the worldwide spread of [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]]'s [[american-system|American System]] of nationalist political economy, which was producing sovereign, cooperating industrial states in Germany, Japan, Russia, France, China and the [[ottoman-empire|Ottoman Empire]].[1] Leading members of the British elite, the article states, could see the stranglehold of empire evaporating as this model spread like wildfire, and the empire responded as any empire confronted with annihilation does — by burning the world.[1]

The instruments described include an unparalleled age of assassinations between 1880 and 1914, typically carried out by London-directed anarchists, which claimed Presidents Garfield and [[william-mckinley|McKinley]] in the United States, Czars Alexander I and II in Russia, and President Sadi Carnot in France in 1895.[1] Britain is further credited with annihilating the Russian army through the Imperial Japanese army in 1904, weakening Russia through the 1905 revolutionary attempt led by [[leon-trotsky|Trotsky]] and [[vladimir-lenin|Lenin]], and setting the Balkan Wars in motion via the [[young-turks|Young Turks]].[1] The [[entente-cordiale|Entente Cordiale]] and then the [[triple-entente|Triple Entente]] completed the encirclement of Germany designed by [[edward-vii|Edward VII]].[1]

After the war, the article argues, the [[league-of-nations|League of Nations]] was widely seen during the 1920s as the cover for a new British Empire, and the 1933 London Economic Conference organized under the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]] and the [[bank-for-international-settlements|Bank for International Settlements]] represented the same drive to strip nation states of the power to generate and direct credit.[1]

## Sun Yat-sen's analysis of British method

In his 1917 [[vital-problems-of-china|Vital Problems of China]], [[sun-yat-sen|Sun Yat-sen]] set out what [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes as a profound insight into the empire's manipulation of China and the world, writing that "the British are as cunning as the fox and as changeable as the weather" and that Britain "seeks friendship only with those which can render her services."[2] Sun compared Britain's friends to silkworms, destroyed by fire or fed to fish once the silk has been drawn from the cocoons.[2]

Sun argued the British oligarchy directly manipulated all the major wars of the 19th and early 20th centuries in pursuit of a "balance of power," summarizing the technique as attacking "the strongest enemy with the help of the weaker countries" and then joining the weakened enemy to check the growth of a third country — a policy he said had remained basically unchanged for two centuries.[2] Ehret ties this to [[lord-palmerston|Lord Palmerston]]'s assertion that the empire has no permanent friends but only permanent interests.[2]

The empire also figures as the antagonist in Sun's political economy: he rallied against the lies of British Free Trade used to justify the [[opium-wars|two opium wars]] of China's century of humiliation, and advocated protective tariffs as the means to develop native industry.[2] Ehret adds that [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]] fought tirelessly against the British imperial forces of the [[city-of-london|City of London]] before and during the Second World War.[2]

## The empire in the shadows (July 2023)

In "Who are the True Puppet Masters?", [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argued that the British Empire never really went away but simply transitioned into the shadows, becoming what he calls the [[british-cabal|British Cabal]] — the true puppet master behind every conflict and conspiracy since at least the [[french-revolution|French Revolution]].[3] England was once openly imperialist, he wrote, entering a country, taking control, enslaving it and extracting its natural resources; at some point its elite concluded it would be more effective to work unseen, "slowly backed into the bushes," and decided "it is better to be the ventriloquist than the puppet."[3]

On this account the empire garnered control of the United States and created both the [[soviet-union|Soviet Union]] and the [[chinese-communist-party|CCP]], controls Iran and [[israel|Israel]] alike, and controls the capitalists and the communists — having realised that controlling both sides of every conflict assures the outcome.[3] Carlson wrote that the empire decides when a great nation should fall and which will take its place, maintaining a facade in which the United States appears to be the world's economic and military superpower while England steers it like the rudder of a great ship.[3] War is characterised as the business model: constant wars and constant tension sustain the [[military-industrial-complex|military industrial complex]] and produce control and power.[3]

Carlson dated a resumption of open coercion to the [[war-of-1812|War of 1812]], an attempt to forcibly regain the States after the [[american-revolutionary-war|Revolutionary War]], and to a later attempt to instigate the [[american-civil-war|American Civil War]] as the United States grew economically powerful.[3] He also traced British drug trafficking into China from the first of the [[opium-wars|Opium Wars]] in 1839, when Chinese refusal of British opium cargoes was answered with attack.[3] His conclusion was that the empire is now in unprecedented difficulty — currently controlling neither side of the main conflict — and that "the British Empire has never stood on shakier ground."[3]
