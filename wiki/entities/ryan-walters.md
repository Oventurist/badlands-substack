# Ryan Walters

Ryan Walters is the Oklahoma State Superintendent of Public Instruction, who led the push to revise the state's social studies curriculum standards to incorporate questions about the 2020 presidential election.[1]

## 2025 social studies standards

Under newly adopted social studies standards taking effect the following school year, Oklahoma was set to incorporate 2020 election conspiracies into its schools' curricula.[1] Walters told the program "Morning in America" that the goal of the revision is to promote critical thinking: "We're going to ensure that kids are looking at graphs, charts and data points. There was a record amount of mail-in ballots. There was a record amount of discrepancies in Bellwether counties. Kids are going to study those, and they're going to come to their own conclusion."[1]

Reporting on the change noted that several investigations and court rulings found no evidence of fraud in the 2020 election and that former President Joe Biden was the legitimate winner.[1] Asked why such content should be included, Walters said, "Millions more folks voted in 2020 and then disappeared in 2024. Okay, so why did that happen? Let kids analyze it," adding, "They can come to their own conclusion. But what we're not going to do is act like 2020 nothing happened."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] used the story to revisit an internal debate among election-integrity commentators, asking whether those who continued to say the 2020 election was stolen — and that the system remained fraudulent in 2024 — were "doomers" or simply telling the truth as they understood it.[1] The commentary argued that believing in a broader patriot plan operating on inaccessible layers and continuing to speak the truth publicly are not mutually exclusive positions, concluding that "The Plan" does not rely on cheerleading but on truth telling.[1]
