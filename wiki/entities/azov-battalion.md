# Azov Battalion

The **Azov Battalion** is a Ukrainian paramilitary formation whose iconography — notably the Black Sun — has made it the central symbol in Russian and Badlands framings of the war in Ukraine as a campaign against resurgent Nazism.[1]

## Role in Badlands narrative

In commentary published around Russia's 2026 [[victory-day|Victory Day]] observance, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] quoted his earlier *Righteous Russia* series arguing that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] had offered the world an enemy "more ubiquitous than any singular or family name," one transcending political parties, ideologies and generations: the Azov battalion and the Black Sun, described as "the ultimate evil in the normie consciousness."[1] The argument holds that the unit's true origins and exploits need not burden unawakened minds, because its position in the collective consciousness suffices on its own.[1]

He characterized this as an evil that George Soros, [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] and the Bidens had mingled with "not just in the shadows and in ledgers and back rooms, but in the open," and that Russia was exposing through accounts of crimes against women and children in Ukraine — "four spokes on a wheel."[1] The rhetorical purpose, he wrote, is to cut past a long casting list of villains and return to archetype: "you've got to expose some Nazis."[1]

## Banderite iconography and the "Zhidobandera" formation

Badlands reporting on the January 2025 torchlit march in Lviv notes that the red-and-black Banderite flag associated with [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]] is flown by many Ukrainian militias today, including the Azov Battalion.[2] The unit is identified in photographs by its Wolfsangel insignia — described as a "Z" with a line through it — including an image of an Azov member embracing a Jewish worshipper in the city of Uman during Rosh Hashanah.[2]

The same commentary highlights the red-and-black Bandera flag bearing a Star of David, an insignia known as "Zhidobandera" (Judeo-Banderites), a term popularized by Ukrainian oligarch [[igor-kolomoisky|Igor Kolomoisky]] in 2014 during what Badlands calls the CIA's Maidan Coup against the Ukrainian government.[2] Kolomoisky is described as the man responsible for creating both the Azov Battalion and President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], and as tied to [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], Rosemont Seneca, and the 40-plus bioweapon labs that the assassinated Russian General Igor Kirillov publicized.[2] Kolomoisky also recruited Natan Khazin, a rabbi and former IDF officer, to form the "Jewish 100" militia to fight against Russia.[2]

## US lifting of the weapons ban (June 2024)

On Tuesday, June 11, 2024, the [[state-department|State Department]] said the United States had lifted its ban on providing American weapons and training to the Azov Brigade, a unit key to the defense of the port city of Mariupol.[3] The U.S. had previously barred the regiment from using American weapons, citing the neo-Nazi ideology of some of its founders, and the unit had long been dogged by its origins as a volunteer battalion drawing fighters from far-right circles as well as by criticism of some of its tactics.[3] By then the brigade had been absorbed into Ukraine's National Guard as the 12th Special Forces Brigade, and its current members rejected accusations of extremism and any ties to far-right movements.[3]

The Kremlin, which has seized on the regiment's origins to cast Russia's invasion as a battle against Nazi influence in Ukraine, took an "extremely negative" view of the decision; spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] described Azov as an "ultranationalist armed formation" and accused U.S. authorities of being "ready to flirt with neo-Nazis."[3]

## Rebranding as the 3rd Separate Assault Brigade

In a YouTube video released in January 2023 the unit announced its most recent rebrand, stating: "Today we officially announce that the SSO AZOV is expanding to a brigade. From now on, we are the 3rd separate assault brigade of the Ground Forces of the Armed Forces of Ukraine."[4] Like its predecessor formation, the brigade continued to be led by [[andriy-biletsky|Andriy Biletsky]], the founder of the Azov Battalion and a long-standing figurehead of the closely aligned National Corps political movement.[4]

On August 14, 2023, roughly an hour after Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] announced another $200 million in military aid to Kyiv, President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] published a Telegram video of an "open conversation" with the 3rd Separate Assault Brigade on the outskirts of Bakhmut, writing that he was "grateful to everyone who defends our country and people."[4] Reporting by [[the-grayzone|The Grayzone]] noted that casual Western observers would not necessarily have recognised the brigade as the newest iteration of the Azov Battalion.[4] Days later Zelensky praised the unit as "excellent fighters" who had halted the enemy advance towards Kostiantynivka, in a post that also alluded to a meeting with the [[aidar-battalion|Aidar Battalion]].[4]

## Zaluzhny's "role models" proposal (2025)

In August 2025 retired General [[valerii-zaluzhnyi|Valery Zaluzhny]], Ukraine's former top military commander and then ambassador to the United Kingdom, called for education programs highlighting members of the Azov unit as role models for Ukrainian children.[5] Praising the Soviet Union's approach to memorializing historic figures, he suggested Ukraine adopt a similar model using fighters from the regiment — which is accused of war crimes and recognized as a bastion of militarized neo-Nazism — as examples of proper behavior, arguing that promoting Azov's "heroism" would be beneficial.[5] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Zaluzhny's praise for the unit made his casting as a potential successor to [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] "a little too perfect," fitting the archetype of the "villainous Ukrainian military leader."[5]

## Banderite lineage and alleged Israeli ties

Badlands describes Azov as the spiritual successor to the Banderites, the [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists]] (OUN-B) led by [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]], itself a wartime sister organization of the German [[schutzstaffel|SS]] whose violence against civilians was extreme enough that the Germans distanced themselves from Bandera.[6] The red-and-black flag associated with that movement became known as the Banderite flag.[6]

In October 2024 Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry posted a collage connecting Azov — characterized as "the most notorious neo-Nazi militia of the modern age" — to the Israel Defense Forces, including an image of a Jewish mercenary group placing the Star of David on the Banderite flag.[6] The commentary posed the question of why such a group would adopt the emblem of an old Nazi affiliate and why Azov maintains deep ties with the State of Israel, concluding that whatever the answer, the public has undeniably been lied to about something.[6] The same piece cited footage of Ukrainian troops in SS-style helmets taunting an elderly Russian villager in the Kursk Region as evidence of the persistence of this current within Ukraine's armed forces.[6]


## Association with Ryan Routh (2024)

Following the September 2024 attempt on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s life, reporting cited by Badlands placed the accused gunman [[ryan-routh|Ryan Routh]] in the company of the Azov Brigade during his time in Ukraine, and indicated he had appeared in one of the unit's propaganda videos.[7] Badlands used the association to question why Western outlets including [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] had given favorable coverage to a man documented socializing with a formation widely described as neo-Nazi, and to press the broader question of what elements of the U.S. government were aware of Routh's foreign-fighter recruitment activity.[7]

## Lifting of the US weapons ban (June 2024)

In June 2024, Badlands reported that the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] had quietly lifted a 2018 [[donald-trump|Trump]]-led ban on sending American weapons to the Azov Battalion, described in the brief as a notorious Neo-Nazi group.[8] Former Pentagon official Michael Maloof characterized the reversal as "a sign of desperation."[8] The item was placed alongside remarks by Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] that Eastern Europe had seen an explosion in Neo-Nazism ever since [[nato|NATO]] expanded into the territory, and Lavrov's broader argument that Western hegemony maintained through blackmail, sanctions, and military force was unsustainable.[8]
