# Aharon Haliva

Maj. Gen. Aharon Haliva is the Israeli officer who served as chief of the [[israel-defense-forces]] Military Intelligence Directorate and who announced his resignation over his role in the failures that led to the [[hamas]] terror group's October 7 onslaught.[1] The IDF said he would step down from the military once a replacement was appointed.[1]

## Resignation

Haliva's departure was coordinated with IDF Chief of Staff Lt. Gen. [[herzi-halevi]] and approved by Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant]].[1] Once he quit, Haliva would become the first senior officer in the IDF to resign over the October 7 attack; another top intelligence general who had planned to step down over the onslaught instead quit after being diagnosed with cancer.[1] Alongside Haliva, other senior defense officials — including the head of the [[shin-bet]] security agency and the IDF chief of staff — have said they bear responsibility for the deadly invasion, though none had announced plans to resign, with many expected to do so once the security situation stabilizes.[1] Haliva framed his departure in personal terms, saying he would "always carry the pain."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry]] argued that while the resignation carries an element of scapegoating, the record shows Haliva first announced his decision to resign on November 29, leaving him a lame duck ever since.[1] A Jerusalem Post article from that date noted that all that remained for Haliva was to decide whether to retire after Israel officially announced the end of the war or before any permanent ceasefire, once the army shifted to raid-only operations.[1]

The commentary observed that the IDF does appear to have shifted somewhat toward a raid warfare strategy — smash-and-grab missions in which land is not captured and held, with raiders retreating to previously held positions before the enemy can mobilize.[1] With Gaza already effectively bombed into a crater, those raids were being conducted in the West Bank, where Hamas has no political presence and its relationship with Fatah has soured, while airstrikes were launched against Lebanon, Syria, and Iran.[1] The writer concluded that the war is not ending soon, as [[benjamin-netanyahu|Bibi Netanyahu]] has publicly stated, that it appears to be expanding by scope-creep amid growing dissension in the Israeli cabinet, and that with the United States poised to sanction multiple IDF battalions over alleged human rights abuses and Israelis protesting the Netanyahu government, the timing suggested "a discerning operative abandoning a sinking ship" rather than simple scapegoating.[1]
