# Texas National Guard

The **Texas National Guard** is the state militia force of Texas, elements of which were deployed to Illinois in October 2025 as part of a federal protection mission.[1]

## Illinois deployment, October 2025

Texas National Guard troops arrived in Illinois on Tuesday, October 7, 2025, to protect federal personnel and property amid anti-immigration protests, a deployment confirmed by the [[pentagon|Pentagon]].[1] A Pentagon official said 200 guardsmen had been mobilized for an initial 60-day period, and a Pentagon statement said the troops arrived "in support of the Federal Protection Mission to protect federal functions, personnel, and property."[1]

The deployment followed days of crowd gatherings near an [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] facility in Broadview, Illinois, where about a dozen people had been arrested.[1] [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] had announced days earlier that additional special operations personnel would be deployed to Illinois after federal agents were rammed and boxed in by ten cars.[1]

## Reaction

Chicago mayor [[brandon-johnson|Brandon Johnson]] invoked the phrase "Civil War" in response to the federal posture.[1] Badlands contributor [[canncon|CannCon]] defended the deployment as ordinary enforcement of the constitutional obligation under Article IV, Section 4 to protect states against invasion, and observed that the [[illinois-national-guard|Illinois National Guard]] had been ordered to conduct "training" on the same day the Texas Guard was en route.[1]

## June 2025 deployment ahead of protests

On Tuesday, June 10, 2025, Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]] announced that members of the Texas National Guard would be deployed to various locations across the state ahead of protests planned that week, including a [[no-kings-protests|"No Kings"]] demonstration in San Antonio.[2] The deployment came as the [[2025-los-angeles-immigration-enforcement-protests|Los Angeles anti-ICE protests]] spread to major cities nationwide and as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] federalized California Guard units over the objection of state officials.[2]

## El Paso border surge, March 2024

On Thursday, March 21, 2024, a swarm of migrants surged the border wall in El Paso and overwhelmed Texas National Guard soldiers who were arranging them into groups to be taken into [[customs-and-border-protection|Customs and Border Protection]] custody.[3] Authorities confiscated knives and shanks from the migrants, and a National Guard source told the New York Post that at least one person tried to grab a soldier's firearm during the incursion.[3] "These people were willing to assault military," the source said. "They were willing to assault law enforcement. They have complete disregard for our laws."[3] Some troops were treated at a nearby hospital for minor injuries.[3]

The skirmish occurred at Gate 36, a spot on US soil where potential asylum seekers congregate hoping to obtain permission to remain in the United States; it is not an official point of entry and is not equipped to process asylum claims.[3] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the episode amounted to an attempt to overthrow the government at the border and could fairly be called an insurrection, and predicted the regime would use such incidents to push for a border supplemental despite recent budget increases.[3]

## Razor-wire defiance and federalization demands (January 2024)

In January 2024 the Guard continued making and installing razor-wire barriers along the Rio Grande in defiance of a [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] order that permitted federal agents to remove them, acting under the direction of Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]], who vowed that authorities under his jurisdiction would not back down.[4] Abbott grounded the state's position in Article I, Section 10, Clause 3 of the Constitution, arguing that the federal government's failure to fulfill its Article IV, Section 4 duties had triggered Texas's reserved right of self-defense.[4]

Democratic Representatives [[joaquin-castro|Joaquin Castro]] and [[greg-casar|Greg Casar]] responded by calling on President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to seize the Texas Guard and place it under federal control unless it ceased installing the barriers.[4] Badlands commentary treated the standoff as the opening of a federal-versus-state lawfare contest whose greatest significance lay less in the disputed stretch of border than in the escalation it modeled for the wider public.[4]
