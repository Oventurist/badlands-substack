# Jack Smith

Jack Smith is the federal special counsel who brought criminal charges against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] arising out of Trump's conduct following the 2020 presidential election.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) In Badlands Media commentary, Smith is treated less as a conventional prosecutor than as an actor whose indictment may — intentionally or not — hand Trump a legal venue he never previously had.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## The federal indictment

Smith's indictment against Trump contains four counts, each of which, in the article's reading, requires proof that Trump acted in bad faith in his attempts to contest or "rectify" the election.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) The document asserts in its second paragraph that "Despite having lost, the Defendant was determined to remain in power," that Trump "spread lies that there had been outcome-determinative fraud in the election," and — critically — that "these claims were false, and the Defendant knew they were false."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

The article treats that final clause as the indictment's fatal weakness. Because the charges hinge on Trump's interior mental state, the author argues, Smith has effectively indicted the former president "based on the belief that they can somehow read his mind."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) If Trump held a good-faith belief that the election was stolen, then the necessary elements of every count collapse and there is no case.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## The "in on the plan" thesis

The article's central speculation is that indictments this legally thin may serve a purpose other than conviction.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) By making Trump a criminal defendant, Smith has given him the powers of discovery — the ability to place evidence of [[2020-election-fraud-claims|election fraud]] onto a court record for the first time, after some 50 of 55 prior election cases were dismissed on standing rather than on the merits of their evidence.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) The author notes that the [[deep-state|Deep State]] would ordinarily prefer to discredit Trump through media rather than create a courtroom where such evidence must be heard.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

The author explicitly declines to reach a conclusion, describing himself as "on the fence" about whether Smith and [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] are knowing participants in a larger plan or simply prosecutors "desperate to get Trump on anything they can."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) Either way, the indictments are filed and, in the article's phrase, "there's no going back now."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in) The framing situates the prosecutions within a broader [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation war]] narrative familiar to [[qanon|Q]] followers.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/are-jack-smith-and-fani-willis-in)

## Security clearance scrutiny (2026)

In July 2026 the [[center-to-advance-security-in-america|Center to Advance Security in America]], a government watchdog organization, asked Acting Director of National Intelligence [[william-pulte|William Pulte]] to review the security clearances of former members of Smith's investigative team, citing recently released Justice Department records it says raise questions about the handling of classified information.[2] CASA asked that clearances be revoked if an official review determines the allegations warrant it.[2]

The request relies on internal Justice Department communications released by Senate Judiciary Committee Chairman [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]]; as described in reporting, the records allege that members of Smith's office granted access to classified information without the required "need-to-know" authorization, and that concerns were raised internally about security procedures involving a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility.[2] House Judiciary Committee Chairman [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] separately referred Smith to the Justice Department for possible criminal investigation over testimony related to his office's access to members of [[congress|Congress]]' text messages.[2] No response from the Office of the Director of National Intelligence had been announced as of publication.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the episode as ironic — prosecutors accused of mishandling classified information while prosecuting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for the same — and argued that clearances should never be an entitlement and that revocation is "not retaliation, it's consequence."[2]

## Congressional testimony (January 2026)

On Thursday, January 22, 2026, Smith testified publicly before the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], defending his Trump investigations and stating that prosecutors had developed "proof beyond a reasonable doubt" of criminal activity and that "No one should be above the law."[3] During the five-hour hearing, Republican lawmakers questioned Smith about his oath of office, his office's use of subpoena powers, his handling of Republican lawmakers' phone toll records, and the January 6 committee, among other topics.[3]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Smith repeatedly relied on the authority of Georgia Secretary of State [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]] to justify what she called his unprecedented treatment of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and that Raffensperger was the only witness Smith mentioned — a point she considered notable given that Raffensperger had been implicated the day before at the [[georgia-state-election-board|Georgia State Election Board]] hearing.[3] She further alleged that Smith likely swore himself in and signed his own oath outside the presence of witnesses, framing the matter as part of "a Grand Treasonous Conspiracy… ongoing against the President since 2016."[3]

## Lineberger indictment (2026)

In May 2026 former federal prosecutor [[carmen-lineberger|Carmen Lineberger]] was indicted for allegedly sending herself a sealed, unreleased portion of Smith's report concerning the classified-documents investigation into [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[4] According to the indictment, Lineberger emailed the report from her [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] account to a personal account and disguised the file under names such as "Bundt_Cake_Recipe.pdf."[4]

## Grassley disclosures on congressional text messages (July 2026)

In July 2026 [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]] Chair [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] revealed that Smith's team had obtained and reviewed text messages from 44 members of [[us-congress|Congress]] — including Grassley himself, 19 other senators, and top House Republicans such as then-GOP Leader [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]], as well as some Democrats — as part of the investigations into [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s role in the January 6 Capitol attack and the Mar-a-Lago classified documents case.[5] The messages, sent to senior [[white-house|White House]] officials in the final weeks of Trump's first term, came from White House records subpoenaed by Smith's team and turned over by the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]] in August 2023.[5]

Grassley, joined by [[ron-johnson|Sen. Ron Johnson]], released a joint report based on whistleblower information and internal DOJ records alleging that Smith's prosecutors had bypassed standard document-filtering protocols and directly accessed communications falling outside the scope of the probes.[5] Grassley announced plans to compel Smith to testify before the Judiciary Committee and framed the episode as evidence of overreach, adding to Republican criticism of the Biden-era Justice Department's handling of the Trump investigations, which were dropped after Trump's 2024 election victory.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that if [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] and [[peter-navarro|Peter Navarro]] were held in contempt of Congress and imprisoned, a federal prosecutor trained at The Hague who lied under oath to Congress about spying on Congress warranted a far more precedent-setting outcome.[5] Noting that [[josh-hawley|Sen. Josh Hawley]] described the testimony as apparent perjury, she called it treason instead, and observed that with [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] announcing the dismantling of the international courts, "Smith has nowhere to run."[5]

## House Judiciary criminal referral (July 2026)

On July 22, 2026, the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] referred Smith to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] for criminal prosecution, citing what it described as strong evidence that he knowingly made false statements during his sworn deposition of December 17, 2025.[6] The referral letter, addressed to Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]], stated that Smith's testimony directly contradicted newly obtained DOJ documents.[6]

The letter concerned the [[arctic-frost|Arctic Frost]] probe conducted under the Biden-Harris DOJ, during which investigators subpoenaed the phone records of more than a dozen Republican Members of [[congress|Congress]] and used gag orders to conceal that the administration had obtained them.[6] According to the committee, Smith's team proposed — and he approved — the vast majority of additional toll record subpoenas, and internal DOJ experts warned the actions might be unconstitutional yet prosecutors proceeded anyway.[6] In his deposition Smith testified that he did not receive the content of Members' text messages, a claim the committee said appears false based on records showing he reviewed some 27,000 White House texts involving 44 lawmakers.[6] The referral highlighted potential violations of 18 U.S.C. § 1001 and of the Speech or Debate Clause protecting congressional communications.[6]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that legislators become authentically outraged about surveillance only when they are the targets, describing Smith as a "globalist creation" who should be prosecuted for conduct extending well beyond Arctic Frost, and noting that every January 6 defendant experienced greater civil-liberty violations than the largely elite victims of the probe.[6]

## House Judiciary deposition transcript (December 2025)

On New Year's Eve 2025, Republicans released the transcript of Smith's December 17 appearance before a closed-door House Judiciary session, in which he vigorously defended his actions and rejected accusations of political motivation and weaponization of government.[7] "The president was preying on the party allegiance of people who supported him," Smith said, adding that the most powerful evidence came from people in [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s own party who "put country before party and were willing to tell the truth to him, even though it could mean trouble for them."[7]

According to the transcript, discussion of Volume Two of the special counsel's reports and any information or conclusions contained in it or its drafts was specifically prohibited by Judge [[aileen-cannon|Cannon]]'s January 21, 2025, order, and the Department of Justice emailed the committee that morning affirming that the order applied to Smith and precluded him from disclosing nonpublic information including interview transcripts, search warrant materials, business records, toll records, video footage, grand jury subpoena records, attorney-client communications, and potential Rule 404(b) evidence — significantly limiting his ability to discuss the classified documents case.[7] The classified documents case was nonetheless raised 46 times, and Smith told the session, "I have no doubt that the president wants to seek retribution against me."[7]

## Pace of the Trump inquiries (February 2023)

By February 2023, roughly three months after being named special counsel, Smith had taken control of the federal investigations into [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s efforts to reverse his 2020 election loss and his handling of classified documents found in his possession after leaving office.[8] The New York Times reported that Justice Department investigators were asking witnesses whether Trump consumed detailed information about foreign countries while in office, how extensively he sought information about whether voting machines had been tampered with, and whether he indicated he knew he was leaving when his term ended.[8] Through witness interviews, subpoenas, and other steps, Smith was said to be moving aggressively toward his stated goal of resolving as quickly as possible whether Trump — then still a leading contender for the 2024 Republican presidential nomination — should face charges.[8]

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] mocked the expectation that "the walls are finally closing in on Trump," writing instead that "the walls are closing in on the Swamp."[8]

## Protective order request and the 2020 vote, August 2023

On August 4, 2023, Smith requested a "protective order" against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] after the former president posted "If you go after me, I'm coming after you" on Truth Social.[9] An Epoch Times opinion piece carried in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] argued that, given the nature of his indictment relating to the January 6, 2021 Capitol breach, the special counsel had opened the door to putting the 2020 presidential election itself on trial, because the indictment depends on proving that Trump was lying when he said the vote was fraudulent.[9] The piece noted that the mainstream media and former Attorney General [[bill-barr|William Barr]] asserted the charges would finally finish Trump, while other legal analysts — Jonathan Turley, Alan Dershowitz and Andrew C. McCarthy — disagreed.[9]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the episode as momentum for the "Disclosure Defense" theory advanced by [[just-human|Just Human]] and others, contending that the 2023 indictments accomplish legal template-setting for presidential declassification authority, indictments of former heads of state and evidence introduction; narrative shielding for Trump as anti-establishment; narrative shielding for a future Trump DOJ pursuing "the real criminals"; and destruction of the two-tier justice system.[9]

## Phone record subpoenas and the "grand conspiracy" probe

Reporting in November 2025 placed Smith at the center of a pattern of seizures of phone records tied to a Justice Department "grand conspiracy" weaponization case.[10] Revelations indicated that Smith, as DOJ special counsel, had cast a net of subpoenas for the phone records of several GOP members of [[congress|Congress]] and had even obtained government phones that belonged to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his former vice president.[10]

The account was linked to an earlier 2018 episode in which a respected former prosecutor approached the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] with a witness claiming to have evidence of Hunter Biden's alleged misdeeds; the department discounted the offer and later subpoenaed the prosecutor's private phone records.[10] The Trump Justice Department regards these incidents as interconnected, and officials quietly opened a probe in the summer of 2025 treating the last decade of political "weaponization" of law enforcement and intelligence agencies as an ongoing criminal conspiracy, reportedly spanning efforts from the Trump-Russia collusion narrative to Smith's own pursuit of Trump.[10] Badlands contributor [[canncon|CannCon]] tied the story to [[ed-martin|Ed Martin]]'s appointment to lead the Weaponization Group.[10]

## August 2023 January 6 indictment

On the evening of Tuesday, August 1, 2023, prosecutors under Smith unveiled a four-count indictment against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] related to the transfer of power after the 2020 election, charging him with conspiracy to defraud the United States among other counts.[11] Trump's first court appearance was scheduled for 4 p.m. on Thursday, August 3 before U.S. Magistrate Judge Moxila Upadhyaya at the E. Barrett Prettyman Courthouse in Washington, D.C.; a spokesperson for Smith's office said it understood the appearance would be in person, though the format was the court's decision.[11]

Days before the indictment, former New York Police Commissioner [[bernard-kerik|Bernard Kerik]] turned over to Smith a trove of documents from the Trump legal team's own post-election investigation, run under [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]], which a lawyer involved in the production argued could undercut Smith's theory of an intentional fraud conspiracy between Trump and six unnamed co-conspirators.[11] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Smith's case requires proving Trump knew his fraud claims were false, which she argued would force the underlying [[2020-election-fraud-claims|election fraud claims]] to be argued on the merits rather than dismissed on procedural grounds, and that a genuine belief in those claims would be protected by the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[11] See [[trump-january-6-indictment|Trump January 6 federal indictment]].[11]

## Concealment allegations in the classified documents case

Court filings unsealed in April 2024 in Trump's classified documents case were reported to show that the Biden [[white-house|White House]] colluded with the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]] (NARA) and the [[fbi|FBI]] to construct a case against the former president, and that Smith sought to conceal that coordination.[12] In February 2024 Smith told Judge [[aileen-cannon|Eileen Cannon]] that Trump's counsel was not entitled to discovery on documents between the White House and NARA, that the court should toss requests for evidence of the alleged coordination, that it should deny Trump's request for evidence related to secure facilities at his residences, and that his request for unredacted discovery of materials should be denied.[12] The unsealed trove also revealed that the FBI's code name for its investigation into Trump's presidential records was [[plasmic-echo|Plasmic Echo]].[12]

## Classified map inquiry (2023)

As special counsel overseeing the Justice Department's Trump-focused inquiries, Smith examined the former president's handling of classified material after leaving office and weighed charges that could include obstruction of justice.[13] In April 2023, reporting indicated that federal investigators working under his supervision were questioning witnesses about whether [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had shown aides, advisers, political donors and possibly a journalist a map containing sensitive intelligence information that he took with him from the [[white-house|White House]].[13]

## Resignation from the Justice Department (January 2025)

Smith completed his work on the two criminal investigations of President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and resigned from the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] on Friday, January 10, 2025.[14] Word of his departure came in a footnote to a court filing that Justice Department officials submitted to U.S. District Judge [[aileen-cannon|Aileen Cannon]] on Saturday afternoon, urging her not to extend a court order she had issued the previous week temporarily blocking release of the final report Smith had submitted to department leaders that Tuesday.[14] Department officials argued that Cannon's order overstepped her authority and that she had no power to block Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] from releasing Smith's findings; her ban on disclosure ran through the following Monday.[14] Garland said he planned to release publicly only the portion of Smith's report covering the investigation into Trump's effort to subvert the 2020 election.[14] Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] wrote that the next phase of the saga would be publication of volume one of Smith's two-volume report, whose release date remained under challenge in court.[14]

## Alleged coordination with the Biden White House

In August 2023 the *New York Post*, in reporting relayed by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], described meetings between Smith's team and the Biden White House that critics said undercut President Biden's repeated claims that the Justice Department enjoyed full autonomy and was not weaponized against political opponents.[15] [[jay-bratt|Jay Bratt]], a top aide who had worked under Smith since November 2022, met on March 31, 2023 with Caroline Saba, deputy chief of staff for the White House counsel's office, in a 10 a.m. meeting also attended by [[fbi|FBI]] agent Danielle Ray.[15] Nine weeks later Smith's office indicted Trump over the alleged mishandling of classified documents.[15] Bratt had also met Saba at the White House in November 2021, while Trump's lawyers were negotiating with the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]] over presidential records held at Mar-a-Lago and before any formal investigation existed.[15]

## Fallout from the Mar-a-Lago prosecution (August 2024)

Smith was appointed special counsel after the FBI's August 8, 2022 search of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Mar-a-Lago property, and ultimately brought 37 felony counts against Trump including willful retention of national defense information, conspiracy to obstruct justice, and false statements; Trump pleaded not guilty to all counts.[16] Judge [[aileen-cannon|Aileen Cannon]] subsequently ruled that Smith had been unlawfully appointed and that Trump enjoyed immunity regardless.[16] In August 2024, Trump moved to sue the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] for $100 million over the raid, with Badlands commentary anticipating that discovery could expose the government's conduct in the case.[16] See [[mar-a-lago-raid|Mar-a-Lago raid]].[16]

## August 2023 objection to Trump's SCIF request

On August 14, 2023, prosecutors working for Smith objected to a request by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s lawyers to establish a [[sensitive-compartmented-information-facility|sensitive compartmented information facility (SCIF)]] in which the former president and his legal team could discuss the classified documents at issue in the case.[17] In a filing to U.S. District Judge [[aileen-cannon|Aileen Cannon]], Smith's team argued the request sought "special treatment that no other criminal defendant would receive and that is unsupported by law or precedent."[17] Prosecutors added that the request rested on a "misrepresentation" of the proposed protective order issued by Judge [[tanya-chutkan|Tanya Chutkan]], which barred disclosure of sensitive materials to anyone outside the defense, and clarified that while classified discussions must occur in a SCIF, they need not occur in the same SCIF housing classified discovery.[17] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the exchange as reciprocal baiting within the wider [[lawfare|lawfare]] campaign, noting the irony that Smith's case rests on the handling of documents outside a certified facility at [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]].[17]

## Failed bid to expedite the immunity question (December 2023)

In December 2023 Smith petitioned the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] to short-circuit the appellate process and rule immediately on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s claim of [[presidential-immunity|presidential immunity]] in the 2020 election interference case, arguing that the public interest required intervention so his case could proceed on schedule in March 2024.[18] "This case involves — for the first time in our Nation's history — criminal charges against a former President based on his actions while in office," Smith wrote. "And not just any actions: alleged acts to perpetuate himself in power by frustrating the constitutionally prescribed process for certifying the lawful winner of an election."[18] Eight days after agreeing to consider the request, the Court released a docket that omitted the case, effectively denying expedition in a one-sentence order with no noted dissents and leaving a federal appeals court in Washington to review the immunity ruling first, with argument set for January 9, 2024.[18]

## Pence and O'Brien subpoenas (February 2023)

In February 2023, Smith issued subpoenas for interviews and documents to former Vice President [[mike-pence|Michael Pence]] and former National Security Advisor [[robert-obrien|Robert O'Brien]].[19] Pence's subpoena followed months of negotiations between his legal team and federal prosecutors working with Smith, while O'Brien received two subpoenas covering testimony and documents relating to the two separate inquiries into the former president.[19] Around the same time it emerged that former Acting DHS Secretary [[chad-wolf|Chad Wolf]] had been interviewed under oath for four hours as part of Smith's investigation into January 6, 2021 and attempts to interfere with the transfer of power after the 2020 election.[19] Badlands Media contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] argued that Smith's inquiries were likely to surface evidence of election interference and insurrection, "but not by Trump and Team."[19]

## Trump attacks after gag order lifted (October 2023)

After US District Judge [[tanya-chutkan|Tanya Chutkan]] temporarily lifted the gag order in the 2020 election conspiracy case, former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] criticized Smith on Truth Social as "deranged" and accused his team of leaking a story to the New York Times about Australian businessman Anthony Pratt, who claimed Trump had told him classified secrets.[20] Trump denied those reports.[20] Chutkan's earlier order had specifically barred Trump from making statements about Smith, his family, potential witnesses and court staff, while allowing general commentary about the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]].[20]

## Stefanik ethics complaint (April 2024)

On April 30, 2024, Representative [[elise-stefanik|Elise Stefanik]] filed an ethics complaint against Smith with the Justice Department's [[office-of-professional-responsibility|Office of Professional Responsibility]], accusing him of trying to "unlawfully interfere with the 2024 presidential election" by rushing Trump's federal election subversion case to trial before November.[21] Stefanik argued that Smith should be censured for violating the Justice Department's manual, citing a provision that attorneys may "never select the timing of any action … for the purpose of affecting any election," and said his conduct had "brought disrepute to the Department of Justice and the entire federal government."[21] She cited his request that the Supreme Court weigh Trump's immunity claims before an appeals court had ruled as evidence of political animus.[21] Badlands commentary framed the episode as both wings of the [[uniparty|uniparty]] leveling official accusations of election interference against the other's candidate ahead of the November vote.[21]

## Immunity appeal and the obstruction statute

Special counsel Jack Smith's prosecution of Donald Trump in the federal 2020 election case was paused on December 13, 2023 when Judge Tanya Chutkan stayed proceedings pending Trump's appeal of her rejection of his presidential immunity claim; the D.C. Circuit fast-tracked review and set a briefing schedule for Smith and Trump before Judges Karen Henderson, Michelle Childs and Florence Pan.[22] Smith's cases were also exposed to the Supreme Court's decision to hear Joseph Fischer's challenge to the 2002 obstruction statute, which Badlands commentary noted underpinned two of the four counts across his indictments and could see them thrown out if the Court found the law misapplied.[22]

## Supreme Court immunity petition (December 2023)

On Monday, December 11, 2023, Smith filed a petition asking the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] to decide whether former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] is immune from prosecution for crimes related to his alleged attempts to overturn the 2020 election, and the court agreed hours later to expedite consideration, ordering Trump to respond by 4 p.m. on December 20.[23] Smith wrote that the case "presents a fundamental question at the heart of our democracy: whether a former President is absolutely immune from federal prosecution for crimes committed while in office or is constitutionally protected from federal prosecution when he has been impeached but not convicted before the criminal proceedings begin."[23] He argued the district court had correctly recognized that "former Presidents are not above the law," while noting that Trump's appeal of that ruling suspended the trial scheduled for March 4, 2024.[23] Smith invoked the 1974 decision [[united-states-v-nixon|United States v. Nixon]] as the basis for skipping the federal appellate court, in a bid to keep the trial date on track.[23]

[[just-human|Just Human]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] read the maneuver as a self-inflicted wound, writing that Smith "just gave Trump a huge assist" by elevating an issue Trump was already appealing to the D.C. Court of Appeals, apparently out of desperation to preserve a trial start the day before Super Tuesday.[23] Under that analysis every possible outcome favored Trump to some degree, and Just Human predicted the March 4 date was "all but cancelled," with the [[presidential-immunity|immunity]] fight carrying deep into the 2024 campaign.[23]

## July 2023 target letter

On Sunday, July 16, 2023, Smith notified Trump by letter that he was a target of the January 6 grand jury investigation and gave him four days to report to the grand jury, a step Trump said almost always precedes arrest and indictment.[24] Trump announced the letter on Truth Social the following Tuesday, calling Smith "deranged" and describing him as "the prosecutor with Joe Biden's DOJ," and framing the probe as election interference and "a complete and total political weaponization of law enforcement."[24]

Senior Republicans responded by attacking Smith's conduct directly. ## Dismissal of both federal cases (November 2024)

On Monday, November 25, 2024, Smith moved to drop both the federal election subversion case and the classified documents case against President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], seeking their dismissal in court filings.[27] Trump had said he would fire Smith once he retook office, shattering previous norms around special counsel investigations.[27]

"The (Justice) Department's position is that the Constitution requires that this case be dismissed before the defendant is inaugurated," Smith wrote of the election subversion case in a six-page filing with US District Court Judge [[tanya-chutkan|Tanya Chutkan]] in Washington, DC, adding that "this outcome is not based on the merits or strength of the case against the defendant."[27] Chutkan formally dismissed the case without prejudice that afternoon.[27]

Smith's two-year criminal pursuit of Trump — over efforts to subvert the 2020 presidential election and over the mishandling of classified documents — represented a unique chapter in American history, as no former occupant of the White House had previously faced federal criminal charges.[27] Although the election case produced a landmark Supreme Court ruling in the summer of 2024 holding that Trump enjoyed some presidential immunity from criminal prosecution, Trump's strategy of delay ensured that no trial began before the November election.[27]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright framed the dismissal as the end of "one of the prime narrative deployments of the current age," arguing that the prosecutions had only bolstered Trump's campaign — that "the trial WAS his campaign" — and predicting that the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] would be "cleaned out and reformed."[27]

## Evidence tampering admission in the classified documents case

## December 2023 motion to limit Trump's trial arguments

On December 27, 2023, special counsel Jack Smith filed a motion in [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s federal election interference case seeking to block him from making political arguments or referring to conspiracy theories during the trial.[56] The filing stated that "the court should not permit the defendant to turn the courtroom into a forum in which he propagates irrelevant disinformation, and should reject his attempt to inject politics into this proceeding," and asked the court to prohibit Trump from advancing a theory of selective or vindictive prosecution.[56]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] criticised the motion as an attempt to silence a candidate while prosecuting him mid-campaign, noting that Smith sought to begin the trial the day before Super Tuesday, and invoked an October 2022 New York Times/Siena College finding that 68% of likely U.S. voters considered government corruption the greatest threat to American democracy.[56]

## Office of Special Counsel investigation (2025)

The Office of Special Counsel opened an investigation in 2025 into Jack Smith, the former Justice Department special counsel who led two criminal probes into Donald Trump under the Biden administration, both ultimately dismissed.[58] An internal email reviewed by the New York Post and sent by Senior Counsel Charles Baldis confirmed that OSC's Hatch Act Unit, which enforces restrictions on political activity by government employees, had opened a review of Smith's conduct.[58] The probe followed a formal complaint by Senator Tom Cotton accusing Smith of accelerating proceedings against Trump to interfere with campaign milestones including the Iowa caucuses, and calling the actions "very likely illegal campaign activity from a public office."[58] Badlands host Jon Herold linked the investigation to Justice Clarence Thomas's concurrence in the presidential immunity ruling questioning Smith's appointment, quoting that "a private citizen cannot criminally prosecute anyone, let alone a former President."[58]

## Dismissal of Smith's prosecutors (January 2025)

On Monday, January 27, 2025, Acting Attorney General [[james-mchenry|James McHenry]] terminated the employment of more than a dozen prosecutors who had worked on Smith's team, gutting the office that led two historic criminal prosecutions of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[59] A Justice Department spokesman confirmed the firings but did not disclose names, saying McHenry deemed the lawyers untrustworthy; McHenry wrote to them, "Given your significant role in prosecuting the President, I do not believe that the leadership of the Department can trust you to assist in implementing the President's agenda faithfully."[59] Smith had resigned as special counsel before Trump took office, having charged Trump in Florida over his handling of classified documents and in Washington, D.C. over his alleged attempts to overturn the 2020 election.[59]

## Anticipated grand jury vote, July 2023

By late July 2023, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s legal and political teams were preparing for the possibility that the federal grand jury convened by Smith would vote on charges against the former president as early as Thursday, July 27, according to three sources familiar with the thinking of his inner circle.[57] Trump had announced on his social media platform on July 18 that he had been notified he was a target of the investigation into the January 6 riot and efforts to overturn the 2020 election.[57] Smith's inquiry centered on the January 6, 2021 attack on the U.S. Capitol by a mob of pro-Trump supporters, alongside other matters, in Washington, D.C.[57]

## Wind-down of the federal cases after the 2024 election

Following [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s victory in the November 2024 election, Justice Department officials moved to "wind down" the two federal criminal cases Smith had brought — one over alleged efforts to overturn the 2020 election, the other over the alleged mishandling of classified documents — in order to comply with department policy against prosecuting a sitting president.[62] NBC News reported that the department saw no avenue to hold trials before Inauguration Day in January 2025, particularly given the many appellate opportunities available to Trump's legal team that could reach the Supreme Court.[62] The policy against prosecuting a sitting president had been developed in the wake of Watergate and functioned as a hard deadline.[62] Smith's prosecutors had carried out a flurry of pre-election activity, filing a retooled case arguing the merits of the charges in light of the Supreme Court's presidential-immunity ruling and a rebuttal to Trump's claim that Smith's appointment was unconstitutional.[62]

## Record before the special counsel appointment

[[the-federalist|The Federalist]] reported in June 2023 on what it called Smith's "sordid history of weaponizing the [[espionage-act|Espionage Act]]," tracing his role as the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s public integrity section chief overseeing the Espionage Act prosecution of former [[national-security-agency|NSA]] official [[thomas-drake|Thomas A. Drake]], a case that arose from a 2007 raid over classified documents and collapsed in 2011.[63] Smith signed off on the government's retreat in that case.[63]

He was subsequently responsible for the 2014 indictment and conviction of Virginia Governor [[robert-mcdonnell|Robert McDonnell]], which the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] reversed unanimously in 2016; Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]], writing for the Court, criticized the prosecution's "boundless" interpretation of the statute and called the case "distasteful."[63] The Federalist argued that with the intelligence apparatus, FBI, and DOJ arrayed against him, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was in genuine legal peril, and that even a defendant who beats the rap does not beat the ride — as Drake and McDonnell had not — suggesting that outcome "maybe that's the entire point of all of this."[63]

Badlands contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] used the same record to argue that the Espionage Act charge against Trump was ill-fitting and evidence-free, describing it as the ramping up of statutes into federal cases, and pointed readers toward [[andrew-weissmann|Andrew Weissmann]], [[just-security|Just Security]], and the [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundation]] as the surrounding infrastructure of the prosecution.[63]

## Planned resignation before the second Trump inauguration (November 2024)

On November 13, 2024, a source familiar with the matter said Smith and his team planned to resign before President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took office.[64] Smith's office had been evaluating the best path for winding down its work on the two outstanding federal criminal cases against Trump, given the Justice Department's longstanding position that a sitting president cannot be charged with a crime.[64] The New York Times first reported that Smith would step down.[64] The open question in the following weeks was whether Smith's final report detailing his charging decisions would be made public before Inauguration Day; under Justice Department regulations the special counsel's office is required to submit a confidential report to Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]], who may choose to release it.[64]
