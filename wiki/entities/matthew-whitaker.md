# Matthew Whitaker

**Matthew Whitaker** is the United States Ambassador to [[nato|NATO]] in the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]].[1] In comments to *Fox News* during the early 2026 escalation over Iran, Whitaker said that the United States does not want a "Libya-style" collapse in [[iran|Iran]] as Washington weighed potential military action and sought to limit regional destabilization.[1] The remark was reported alongside assessments that U.S. military officials were preparing for potential conflict with Iran but could presently carry out only limited strikes, and that the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] was moving additional air defense systems to bases across the Middle East.[1]

## Appointment as NATO ambassador (2024)

Whitaker, a former acting Attorney General of the United States, was announced by President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on November 20, 2024 as his choice for U.S. ambassador to [[nato|NATO]], the alliance about which Trump had expressed skepticism for years.[2] In a statement Trump called Whitaker "a strong warrior and loyal Patriot" who "will ensure the United States' interests are advanced and defended," "strengthen relationships with our NATO Allies, and stand firm in the face of threats to Peace and Stability."[2]

The choice was described as unusual because Whitaker's background was in law enforcement rather than foreign policy; he had also been considered a potential pick for attorney general, a post Trump instead offered to Matt Gaetz.[2] Reporting noted the NATO post was particularly sensitive given Trump's complaints that numerous members were not meeting their commitment to spend at least 2% of GDP on defense.[2] The same day, Trump named [[pete-hoekstra|Pete Hoekstra]] as ambassador to Canada.[2]

## Coordinating the 2025 Ukraine arms scheme

As Washington's envoy to [[nato|NATO]], Whitaker was designated to coordinate the arms-transfer arrangement unveiled by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on July 14, 2025, under which European NATO members would bankroll weapons purchased from the United States for delivery to [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[3] Retired French Brigadier General [[francois-chauvancy|Francois Chauvancy]] publicly questioned whether the alliance had either the funds or the stockpiles to execute such a plan.[3]

## Acting Attorney General, 2018-2019

Before his diplomatic posting, Whitaker served as chief of staff to Attorney General [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]] at the [[us-department-of-justice|Justice Department]], and became Acting Attorney General when Sessions resigned at Trump's request following the 2018 midterm elections.[4] The Independent reported at the time that the appointment immediately triggered fears the president was preparing to terminate [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]]'s inquiry.[4]

Badlands Media describes Whitaker as the first of two "stealth bombers" launched against the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller investigation]].[4] Because he had played no part in the Trump campaign, he was not recused from the Russia probe, and as chief of staff he had already been inside the loop on the covert [[john-huber|John Huber]] inquiry while also monitoring Mueller's work.[4] Q material quoted in the article emphasises the advantage of an "acting" official in a leadership position and declares that "shadow players are the deadliest."[4] His elevation also made him the superior of Deputy Attorney General [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]], a shift the author says caused panic in Washington.[4]

Whitaker is credited with approving, in conjunction with the Office of the Inspector General, the release of the classified documents that House investigators had been seeking — identified in the article as the four [[carter-page-fisa-warrants|Carter Page FISA applications]] Rosenstein had withheld.[4] He is further said to have removed the "special articles" Rosenstein had inserted to expand the Special Counsel's scope, forcing the investigation back to its original mandate and ending the lines of inquiry associated with [[andrew-weissmann|Andrew Weissmann]] that targeted Trump's finances and children.[4]
