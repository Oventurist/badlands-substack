# Georgia Court of Appeals

The Georgia Court of Appeals is the intermediate appellate court of the US state of Georgia, sitting between the state's trial courts and the Supreme Court of Georgia.[1]

## Trump election interference case

On 8 May 2024 the court agreed to hear a bid by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to disqualify [[fulton-county|Fulton County]] District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] from the former president's criminal election interference prosecution.[1] The appeal followed a March ruling by Fulton County Superior Court Judge [[scott-mcafee|Scott McAfee]], who declined to remove Willis from the case after revelations that she had been in a romantic relationship with a member of her prosecution team.[1]

Trump was charged in the case with illegally attempting to overturn his loss to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in Georgia's 2020 election, and no trial date had been set at the time the appeals court accepted the matter.[1] Trump attorney Steve Sadow said the former president looked forward to telling the appeals court "why the case should be dismissed and Fulton County DA Willis should be disqualified for her misconduct in this unjustified, unwarranted political persecution."[1] The decision to take up the disqualification bid was widely characterised as the latest setback for Willis's high-profile prosecution of the presumptive Republican presidential nominee.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media treated the ruling as part of a broader pattern of "collapsing Trump trials," invoking the argument — associated with commentators such as Jonathan Turley — that when lawfare cases rest on weak foundations the system seeks off-ramps to limit further damage to its own credibility.[1]

## Disqualification of Fani Willis (December 2024)

On December 19, 2024, the court disqualified Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]] from prosecuting the case against President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his alleged co-conspirators over efforts to overturn the 2020 election.[2] The panel found that Willis's office could not prosecute the case, meaning a new special prosecutor would have to be appointed for it to continue, but declined to dismiss the sprawling racketeering conspiracy case entirely.[2]

The court held that a "significant appearance of impropriety" was enough to potentially taint the case in the public eye: "While we recognize that an appearance of impropriety generally is not enough to support disqualification, this is the rare case in which disqualification is mandated and no other remedy will suffice to restore public confidence in the integrity of these proceedings."[2] It added that it could not conclude the record supported "the extreme sanction of dismissal of the indictment."[2] Willis's team asked the Supreme Court of Georgia to review the decision.[2]
