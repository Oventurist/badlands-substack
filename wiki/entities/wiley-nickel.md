# Wiley Nickel

Wiley Nickel is a Democratic member of the United States House of Representatives from North Carolina who, in November 2024, proposed that congressional Democrats organize a British-style "shadow cabinet" to oppose the incoming second administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] The proposal was advanced in an op-ed for the Washington Post and reported by Fox News, which rendered his name as "Wiley Nickels."[1]

## Shadow cabinet proposal

With Trump returning to the [[white-house|White House]] and Republicans holding majorities in [[congress|Congress]], Nickel argued that Democrats had few institutional levers left and should look "across the pond" to the United Kingdom for a model that would let the minority "go toe to toe" with the administration.[1] He described the British arrangement as "a team from the opposition that mirrors the government's cabinet members," which watches the cabinet closely while "publicly challenging, scrutinizing and offering new ideas," calling it "another form of checks and balances — a quiet guardrail that keeps power accountable."[1]

Concretely, Nickel proposed appointing 26 Democratic leaders in Congress to mirror Trump's Cabinet-level officials and to contest each initiative of the incoming administration.[1] Among the illustrative pairings reported were Senator-elect [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] of California as a shadow attorney general — tasked with calling out efforts to replace career Justice Department attorneys with presidential loyalists — and Representative [[gregory-meeks|Gregory W. Meeks]] of New York as a shadow secretary of state, who would publicly oppose any move to reduce U.S. support for Ukraine in its war with Russia.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media treated the proposal as an admission of intent rather than a good-government reform, characterizing it as a plan "to subvert a duly elected President, prevent his government from functioning, and effectively overthrow it."[1] The commentary mocked Nickel personally, joking that he had displaced Representative Hank Johnson as, in the writer's estimation, the least capable member of Congress.[1]

The outlet placed particular weight on Nickel's explicit borrowing of a British parliamentary device, drawing an analogy to the Federalist Party, which the piece said had to be abolished at the end of the War of 1812 after being accused of selling out to British interests.[1] From there the commentary extended its critique to "the banks of London" and the network of non-governmental organizations in its orbit, naming the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] and the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]].[1] It closed by calling for Nickel to be investigated for "seditious treason" and for indictments to be used as leverage.[1]
