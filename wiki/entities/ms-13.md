# MS-13

MS-13 is an international criminal gang cited by U.S. federal authorities as a transnational organized crime threat operating within immigrant communities in the United States.[1]

## 2025 Colorado Springs raid

In April 2025, U.S. Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] publicly linked MS-13 to the [[colorado-springs-nightclub-raid|Colorado Springs nightclub raid]], stating on X that the "underground" club targeted in the operation was frequented by members of MS-13 as well as the Venezuelan prison gang [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]].[1] Federal officials detained more than 100 people unlawfully residing in the country during the raid and seized cocaine and guns.[1]

The invocation of MS-13 alongside Tren de Aragua reflected the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s framing of immigration enforcement as inseparable from organized crime suppression, a framing also applied to [[operation-tidal-wave|Operation Tidal Wave]] in Florida during the same period.[1]

## Origins

According to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], MS-13 — formally La Mara Salvatrucha — was formed by Salvadoran immigrants who came to the United States to escape the crisis in their home country, and some of its members had been trained in guerrilla warfare and the use of military weapons.[2] The gang's structure includes a senior leadership body described in court documents as a "board of directors."[2]

## 2024 arrest of Freddy Ivan Jandres-Parada

In March 2024 it emerged that [[freddy-ivan-jandres-parada|Freddy Ivan Jandres-Parada]], described as one of the most senior leaders of MS-13 worldwide, had been arrested in San Diego County, California after four years as a fugitive.[2] He was wanted for allegedly directing MS-13 activity in the United States, Mexico and El Salvador, and had been charged with terrorism offenses tied to violence against civilians, law enforcement and rival gang members, along with drug distribution and extortion schemes.[2]

## February 2023 indictment of thirteen MS-13 leaders

On February 27, 2023, Badlands Media reported that a four-count indictment had been unsealed in federal court in Central Islip charging thirteen of the highest-ranking MS-13 leaders in the world with directing the organization's criminal activities in the United States, El Salvador, Mexico and elsewhere over the previous two decades.[3] The defendants were charged with racketeering conspiracy, conspiracy to provide or conceal material support to terrorists, and narco-terrorism conspiracy, with four of them additionally charged with alien smuggling conspiracy.[3]

On February 22, three of the defendants — Vladimir Antonio Arevalo-Chavez ("Vampiro de Monserrat Criminales"), Walter Yovani Hernandez-Rivera ("Baxter de Park View" and "Bastard de Park View") and Marlon Antonio Menjivar-Portillo ("Rojo de Park View") — were located by Mexican authorities and expelled from Mexico via the United States.[3] On arrival at George Bush Intercontinental Airport in Houston they were arrested by the [[fbi|FBI]] and [[homeland-security-investigations|Homeland Security Investigations]], and made initial appearances in the Southern District of Texas pending removal to the Eastern District of New York.[3]

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] attributed the case to [[joint-task-force-vulcan|Joint Task Force Vulcan]], a Trump- and Barr-created effort launched in August 2019 whose mission was to "go to war against MS-13," and whose director, [[john-j-durham|John J. Durham]], is a son of Special Counsel [[john-durham|John H. Durham]].[3]

## In the Q drops

Chapter 12 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series treats MS-13 as a recurring subject of drops #91 and #92, in which Q repeatedly asked why the gang was a priority.[4] The author's answer is that MS-13 had been converted into what amounts to a political hit squad for the [[deep-state|Deep State]], useful precisely because its operatives can be discarded once a job is finished and because many members are in the country illegally, making them hard to trace.[4] He notes that gangs were a major focus of the enforcement plan pursued by Attorney General [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]] under the Trump administration.[4]

The chapter states that Q made repeated inferences that MS-13 was used to murder [[seth-rich|Seth Rich]], with the speculated motive being that Rich was about to expose election fraud inside the [[democratic-national-committee|DNC]].[4] It further argues that MS-13 supplied additional shooters during the [[las-vegas-shooting|Las Vegas shooting]], which the author offers as the reason Q raised the gang's relevance again in the middle of the Vegas questions.[4]

The same reading appears at the very start of the series: in Chapter 1, [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] writes that MS-13 surfaces in Drop #5 of October 29, 2017, and that Q is there telling readers the gang "is indeed what amounts to a hit squad for the deep state."[5]

## Treatment in Q drop #85

Drop #85 of November 5, 2017 opened by asking who funds MS-13, a question Chapter 11 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series treats as one of the most consequential in the series.[6] The chapter pushes back on the perception that MS-13 are "just a bunch of punk kids," describing the gang as large in number, ruthless, and arguably the most dangerous street gang in the United States today, and noting that in many instances its members serve as foot soldiers for the cartels.[6] The author cites Fox Business reporting on the gang's danger and funding sources, in which Attorney General [[jeff-sessions|Jeff Sessions]] is quoted as believing MS-13 should be labeled a terrorist organization.[6]

The chapter connects this to a pre-Q tweet by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and argues that it explains why Trump and Sessions both prioritized the border wall and the deportation of MS-13 members: in the author's framing, MS-13 had become another army available to the deep state inside the United States.[6] He reads the drop's closing question, "who do you hire for a hit?", as pointing to the same conclusion — a gangster is hired because he can be disposed of afterward without public objection.[6] The illustration offered is the killing of [[seth-rich|Seth Rich]], whose two murderers turned up dead days later and were MS-13 members.[6] Drop #90 later had Q confirm an anon's finding that those two men were connected to both MS-13 and the [[democratic-national-committee|DNC]].[6]

## The fugitive redefinition and the war on MS-13

"Setting the Stage" links the Trump administration's campaign against MS-13 to a late [[barack-obama|Obama]]-era Justice Department decision on the definition of a "fugitive."[7] Joe Lange writes that the FBI had always treated anyone with an outstanding warrant as a fugitive, while the [[bureau-of-alcohol-tobacco-firearms-and-explosives|ATF]] required that the person also have crossed state lines; the Obama DOJ adopted the ATF definition and forced the FBI to purge at least 500,000 known criminals from the background-check database.[7] Townhall observed that warrants and violent histories do not disappear when a fugitive crosses a state line, that NICS applies in all fifty states, and that hundreds of thousands of ineligible criminals became eligible to buy firearms overnight.[7]

Lange presents this as purposeful and as the reason [[donald-trump|Trump]] went to war against MS-13 in his first term, alleging that Obama had instructed Homeland Security and Border Patrol to release MS-13 members captured at the border and that the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] — with ties to two major drug cartels — funds the gang.[7] He describes MS-13 members as CIA foot soldiers who sold drugs, committed violent crimes and served as paid assassins against patriots such as [[seth-rich|Seth Rich]].[7] The article further argues that the enemy later opened the borders under Joe Biden to import [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] members from Venezuela to replace the depleted MS-13 ranks, and that [[david-bowdich|David Bowdich]]'s background in gang and violent-crime enforcement made him a fitting choice for promotion at the FBI.[7]
