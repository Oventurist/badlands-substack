# Russia

Russia is the state that received [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] after his December 2024 departure from [[syria|Syria]], and a recurring subject of what Badlands Media describes as U.S. security-state narrative management.[1]

## Role in the fall of Assad

The rapid rebel advance was facilitated by the withdrawal of Syrian government forces, possibly due to a lack of support from allies such as Russia and [[iran|Iran]], who were preoccupied with other conflicts at the time.[1] Assad reportedly resigned and departed Syria for Moscow.[1] Russia subsequently called for UN Security Council consultations to manage the fallout, particularly concerning the buffer zone near the Golan Heights.[1]

## Narrative framing

The article argues that the U.S. security state has pursued a multi-decade endeavor to make Russia into a permanent enemy, and that anyone deviating from that line — such as [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] — is slandered as a "Putin puppet" or "Russian agent."[1] On [[ukraine|Ukraine]], it contends that a peace deal was near resolution in April 2022 before being thwarted by the Western hegemon via Boris Johnson, and that public support depended on repeating that the invasion was unprovoked and that Vladimir Putin was the "new Hitler."[1] It further asserts that large portions of Ukraine would prefer to be ruled by Moscow rather than Kyiv.[1]

## Emboldened posture after the Iran crisis (March 2026)

In March 2026 Ukraine said Russia launched one of the largest aerial attacks of the war, involving hundreds of drones and multiple missiles over roughly 24 hours.[2] At the same time, [[india|India's]] refiners booked approximately 60 million barrels of Russian crude for the following month — more than double February levels — as supply-disruption fears tied to the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] reshaped energy flows.[2] Indian buyers increasingly settled Russian oil trades in UAE dirhams and Chinese yuan, converting rupees to do so, in what analysts described as a possible acceleration away from dollar-denominated oil trade.[2]

Badlands contributor Burning Bright argued that the Russia–Ukraine story had faded from the narrative cycle precisely as the Iranian theater absorbed attention, and that the apparent pause "reeked of coordination – not paralysis."[2] He tied renewed Russian pressure in the Donbas to second-order fallout from the Iran crisis, noting that legacy outlets were anchoring it to [[donald-trump|Trump's]] temporary waivers easing sanctions on seaborne Russian oil amid Hormuz-driven price spikes and casting it as a blunder that fueled Putin's war machine.[2] In his reading, this was instead "the methodical unwind of the rules-based international order," a controlled release of pressure valves that stabilized markets at home while exposing the old sanctions regime as engineered scarcity rather than security policy — with Russia gaining breathing room in its borderlands as globalist chokeholds loosened elsewhere.[2]


## June 2026 claims on Ukraine
Russia claimed in June 2026 that it was expanding military operations in western [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and accused [[nato|NATO]] of supporting Ukrainian strikes on Russian airfields.[3] Russian officials additionally alleged links between Ukrainian forces and Mexican drug cartels, a claim Badlands commentary treated as part of an increasingly surreal set of Eastern European headlines.[3]

## Beijing diplomacy and the Sahel (May 2026)

The Kremlin announced that President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] would travel to Beijing on 19–20 May 2026 for talks with [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] on bilateral relations, economic cooperation and "key international and regional issues," coinciding with the 25th anniversary of the 2001 Sino-Russian Treaty of Friendship and following closely on a state visit to [[china|China]] by U.S. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[4] Moscow's dependence on Chinese trade and energy purchases has grown since the 2022 invasion of [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[4]

Badlands commentary also credits Russia with helping Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] and the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]] reclaim sovereignty from [[islamic-state|ISIS]]-linked groups in West Africa, noting that Russia and the AES have repeatedly accused Ukraine of funding, arming and training those groups.[4]

## Retaliatory strikes on Ukraine (May–June 2026)

After the [[starobelsk-dormitory-attack|Starobelsk dormitory attack]] of May 22, 2026 — a Ukrainian drone strike on a college dormitory in the Lugansk People's Republic that reportedly killed 21 people, mostly teenage girls, and injured 70 — Russia conducted a large-scale overnight missile and drone campaign using precision weapons including hypersonic missiles.[5] Targets spanned Ukrainian military airfields, defense industry facilities, fuel and transport infrastructure, and a drone production plant linked to President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], across Kyiv, Zaporozhye, Kherson, Dnipropetrovsk, Poltava, Khmelnytskyi, and Sumy.[5]

Russian Defense Ministry officials said all designated targets were struck and framed the operation as a response to "terrorist attacks" by Kiev, while President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] described it as a new chapter in Ukraine's "crime spree" that would bring "well-deserved and inevitable punishment."[5] Separately, [[rosatom|Rosatom]] reported that a fiber-optic-guided drone had punctured the machine hall of the sixth power unit at the [[zaporozhye-nuclear-power-plant|Zaporozhye Nuclear Power Plant]], which Russia has controlled since 2022.[5] Putin also publicly denied Russian responsibility for a drone that struck Romania, suggesting instead a Western or Ukrainian false flag.[5]

## "Standing single-handedly" against the Collective West

On June 12, 2026 — Russia Day, the anniversary of the country's 1990 declaration of sovereignty — President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said that Russia was standing alone against the entire "[[collective-west|Collective West]]" in the Ukraine conflict, and asserted that no external force could impose a strategic defeat on Moscow.[6] He described the war as highly technological and said all NATO nations were uniformly intensifying efforts against Russia.[6]

Putin maintained that Russia did not start the conflict, claiming Western powers orchestrated a coup in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] which forced Russia to protect Crimea's population, and that Ukrainian forces then initiated large-scale hostilities by bombing Donetsk with warplanes.[6] He drew historical parallels to earlier united European coalitions against Russia under Napoleon and Hitler, framing the present situation as a continuation of external pressure.[6] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the statement as evidence for his thesis that Russia is a core animating engine of a theorized Sovereign Alliance, noting that the 1990 sovereignty declaration opened the decade of the Russian oligarchy that Putin later purged.[6]

## Saudi partnership at SPIEF 2026

Russia hosted [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] as the official guest country at the [[2026-st-petersburg-international-economic-forum|2026 St. Petersburg International Economic Forum]], where the two sides signed approximately 30 agreements spanning energy, investment, education, tourism and humanitarian cooperation.[7] Russian companies signaled interest in petrochemicals, hydrogen, digital technologies and nuclear energy, while Saudi officials sought Russian expertise in IT, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity and agriculture, with both governments emphasizing sovereignty and a multipolar order.[7] Badlands commentary highlighted that Russia and Saudi Arabia had jointly stabilized world energy markets in 2020 and that Russia was the first state to recognize the Kingdom in 1926, a centenary that made President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s choice of guest appear deliberate.[7]

## Marinera tanker seizure (January 2026)

In January 2026 US forces seized a Russia-flagged oil tanker linked to Venezuelan crude after a roughly two-week pursuit across the Atlantic, in an action Washington described as part of an expanded sanctions-enforcement campaign.[8] Russian officials and state-aligned media condemned the seizure, arguing that it violated international maritime law and amounted to unlawful interference with commercial shipping.[8] Badlands commentary noted that Russian military assets, including at least one submarine and surface warships, were reported in the vicinity shadowing the vessel without interfering, and argued the ship — the [[seizure-of-the-tanker-marinera|Marinera]], formerly the Bella 1 — had only recently switched to Russian registration as a false-flag device within the dark fleet.[8] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the episode as evidence that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] were cooperating to defuse a manufactured escalation rather than driving toward war, and suggested Russia was considering an analogous playbook in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[8]

## Energy leverage during the 2026 oil shock

During the March 2026 energy crisis triggered by the war involving [[iran|Iran]], the United States issued a temporary 30-day waiver allowing Indian refiners to purchase Russian oil already at sea, a measure officials said was intended to maintain global supply and prevent further price spikes.[9] Amid the same price surge, President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said Moscow could halt gas supplies to European markets and redirect exports elsewhere, arguing alternative markets might offer better returns; Russia's share of EU pipeline gas had already fallen from about 40% before the Ukraine war to roughly 6%.[9]

Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] was cited in a circulating social media post as saying additional relief from sanctions on Russian oil could be considered because of a temporary global shortage, though the claim was not independently verified within the post.[9] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the sequence as American policy indirectly enabling Russia to tighten an energy pincer on Europe, with [[india|India]] and Russia both benefiting while European industry — dependent on the very US–Russia energy dynamic it had spent years trying to destroy through sanctions — faced an energy crisis he said could eclipse anything seen in decades.[9]

## Nord Stream probe and the March 2023 Moscow summit

Following the publication of Seymour Hersh's report describing a [[cia|CIA]] and U.S. Navy covert operation conducted with Norwegian intelligence, Russia became more vocal in alleging that Washington was responsible for the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream pipeline sabotage]].[10] In February 2023 Moscow introduced a draft [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]] resolution calling for an international independent investigation commission and started the silence procedure on the text, only for the United States and other Western members to break silence and object; [[china|China]] publicly backed the Russian draft in March, and both governments read Western resistance as a sign of guilt.[10]

At the March 2023 Moscow summit, [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] told [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] that "change is coming that hasn't happened in 100 years and we are driving this change together," with Putin replying, "I agree."[10] Badlands commentary noted that the ruble was stronger than at the outset of the war in Ukraine even as the West faced a banking crisis cascade and looming labor unrest, treating the contrast as evidence that the [[multipolar-world|multipolar world order]] was consolidating around Moscow and Beijing.[10]


## Drone incursions and oil-tariff pressure (September 2025)

Following incursions of Russian drones into Polish airspace in September 2025, [[nato|NATO]] launched Operation [[eastern-sentry|Eastern Sentry]], under which British [[royal-air-force|RAF]] Typhoons joined Danish, French and German forces in air defense missions over [[poland|Poland]] to deter potential Russian aggression.[11] British Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Sir Keir Starmer]] said Russia's "reckless behaviour is a direct threat to European security."[11]

In parallel, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] pressed [[g7|G7]] and NATO partners as well as the [[european-union|EU]] to impose secondary tariffs of up to 100% on Chinese and Indian goods tied to purchases of Russian oil, arguing this could help end the Ukraine conflict.[11] [[china|China]] rejected the pressure, with Foreign Ministry spokesperson [[lin-jian|Lin Jian]] calling energy cooperation with Russia "fully legitimate and lawful."[11]

## Belgorod attack and Putin's response

On December 30, 2023, a strike on the Russian city of Belgorod killed at least two dozen people and injured more than 100, a day after Moscow's own large-scale air assault on cities across [[ukraine|Ukraine]] killed 23.[12] President Vladimir Putin said on January 1, 2024 that Russia would "intensify" strikes in response to the [[belgorod-attack|attack]], declaring that "no crime against civilians will rest unpunished," while asserting that Russia hits military rather than civilian targets.[12]

## Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Treaty with Iran

On September 18, 2024 President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] accepted the [[russian-foreign-ministry|Russian Foreign Ministry]]'s proposal to sign the [[comprehensive-strategic-partnership-treaty|Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Treaty]] with the Islamic Republic of [[iran|Iran]], per a decree published on Russia's official legal information portal.[13] The treaty had been finalized in June 2024 following roughly two and a half years of editorial work, and the decree specified that signing should occur "at the highest level," authorizing the ministry to make non-substantive changes during negotiations.[13]

Badlands analysis paired the decree with the [[fbi|FBI]]'s simultaneous claim of Iranian election interference on behalf of the Biden–[[kamala-harris|Harris]] campaign, arguing that formal Russia–Iran alliance produced "a total inversion of the Russia, Russia, Russia psyop."[13] The same coverage held that Russian military forces and the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] were dug in alongside [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] in Lebanon and had signalled that they knew how to defeat the blitzkrieg strategy the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] had telegraphed.[13]

## Support for Venezuela (December 2025)

Russia and [[china|China]] expressed support for [[venezuela|Venezuela]] in December 2025 as it confronted a US [[2025-venezuela-oil-tanker-blockade|blockade]] of sanctioned oil tankers, while President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] escalated his pressure campaign on [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolas Maduro]].[14] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the moment through his "Sovereign Alliance Maneuver Defense" concept, arguing that the US and Russian militaries had executed a pincer movement on ISIS in Syria in December 2024 and asking whether the same pattern was about to repeat in Venezuela.[14]

## May 2024 tactical nuclear drills

On Tuesday, 21 May 2024, Russia's military began drills involving tactical nuclear weapons that authorities had announced on 6 May, in an apparent warning to senior Western officials who had spoken about deeper involvement in the war in Ukraine.[15] It was the first time Russia had publicly announced drills involving tactical nuclear weapons, although its strategic nuclear forces exercise regularly.[15] According to a Defense Ministry statement, the first stage envisioned 'practical training in the preparation and use of non-strategic nuclear weapons,' including nuclear-capable Kinzhal and Iskander missiles.[15] The maneuvers took place in the Southern Military District, comprising Russia's southern regions including the border with Ukraine, Crimea — annexed from Ukraine in 2014 — and four Ukrainian regions Russia annexed in 2022 and partially occupies.[15] The ministry said the drills responded to 'provocative statements and threats of certain Western officials regarding the Russian Federation.'[15]

## Economic resilience under sanctions (2024)

In late October 2024, reporting relayed by ReMix News described Russia as experiencing its "fastest economic growth in the last decade."[16] Richard Connolly, a junior research fellow at the Royal Institute of Shared Services in London and an expert on the Russian economy, told CBS News that the number of small and medium-sized enterprises registered in Russia had reached an unprecedented level.[16]

As many Western companies left the country or suspended operations following the invasion of Ukraine, they were rapidly replaced by domestic equivalents — Stars Coffee in place of Starbucks, Maag in place of Zara, and Dobry Cola in place of Coca-Cola.[16] Dobry reported in April that its 2023 earnings were quadruple its 2022 profits.[16]

## April 2024 strikes on Ukraine's energy grid

On April 11, 2024, Russia launched a major missile and suicide-drone offensive against [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s power grid and underground natural gas storage sites.[17] Bloomberg reported that Russia air-launched six hypersonic Kinzhal missiles at critical infrastructure near Kyiv and around Lviv, none of which Ukrainian air defenses intercepted; of 42 slower conventional missiles, 18 were downed, and only one of 40 drones.[17] Energy Minister [[german-galushchenko|German Galushchenko]] said power plants near Kyiv and in the Kharkiv, Zaporizhzhia and Lviv regions were damaged, and President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] reported damage to a plant in Odesa; six missiles struck the [[trypilska-power-plant|Trypilska plant]] south of Kyiv, the largest in the region.[17] Badlands commentary argued that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] was deliberately targeting critical infrastructure to force Ukraine to the negotiating table, and that the penetration of U.S. and [[nato|NATO]]-supplied defenses by hypersonic weapons should alarm [[israel|Israel]] given [[iran|Iran]]'s similar capabilities.[17]

## Banking integration with Iran (2023)

In January 2023, Russia and [[iran|Iran]] integrated their interbank communication and transfer systems, linking 52 Iranian and 106 Russian banks through the Russian Financial Message Transfer System in an effort to bypass sanctions on their financial infrastructure.[18] Badlands contributor [[patriots-in-progress|Patriots in Progress]] framed the [[iran-russia-banking-integration|move]] as a response to [[swift|SWIFT]]'s historical use as an instrument of political coercion and as part of a broader strategy, alongside [[brics|BRICS]] currency-basket discussions, that could ultimately end the [[petrodollar|petrodollar]] system.[18]

## Badlands framing of the 2022 Poland strike

In "Death Blossom" (November 23, 2022), [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Russia was the intended target of a narrative operation surrounding the [[2022-poland-missile-strike|missile strike on Polish territory]], in which an errant strike was to be framed as the work of an "aggressive and unhinged Russia" and used to trigger [[nato|NATO]] escalation.[19] He described the framing as collapsing within roughly twelve hours under decentralized public scrutiny.[19]

Consistent with his [[righteous-russia|Righteous Russia]] series, he treated Russian forces as allied to the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] rather than adversarial, predicting that the "hot" factions exposed by the episode would be removed by American or, "more likely," allied Russian forces so that the next errant strike would be even less successful.[19] He also argued that the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]]'s February–March 2022 portrayal of Russians as "mustache-twirling villains" was already eroding among reachable audiences.[19]

## The 2022 energy war with Europe

Joe Lange's November 2022 essay "Money and Oil" describes [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s invasion of [[ukraine|Ukraine]] as "a brilliant chess move" that changed everything in Europe, arguing that Ukraine had been the [[cabal|cabal]]'s launchpad for a plan to destroy Russia.[20] In Lange's telling Putin exposed illegal bio labs, Nazi divisions and a money laundering operation in Ukraine, protected ethnically Russian Ukrainians from slaughter by their own government, and exposed [[nato|NATO]] as a puppet of the cabal alongside Europe's central banks.[20]

The essay treats the resulting sanctions as self-defeating for Europe, which depends on Russian energy: prices spiked, inflation rose, and the [[european-union|European Union]]'s economy was crushed while Russia was little affected.[20] When central banks attempted to destroy the ruble, Putin demanded payment for Russian oil and gas in rubles or gold, which strengthened it, while [[china|China]] and [[india|India]] increased their purchases of Russian oil and [[japan|Japan]] decided to proceed with a Russian pipeline.[20] Lange concludes that Putin is winning the economic war against the cabal in Europe, with European public sentiment turning against the war because of high energy prices and European countries entering recession.[20]

## Ban on Discord (October 2024)

Russia moved to ban [[discord|Discord]] in October 2024, a step Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] cited alongside [[turkey|Turkey]]'s access-ban decision as part of a broader trend of states blocking communications platforms they cannot readily surveil.[21] DeLarme grouped the Russian action with [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s move to limit [[telegram|Telegram]] use and with France's prosecution of [[pavel-durov|Pavel Durov]], arguing that governments had grown tired of pressuring social media platforms to censor and now rely on force.[21]
