# Saif al-Islam Gaddafi

Saif al-Islam Gaddafi is the second son of the late Libyan leader [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] and was widely regarded before the 2011 uprising as the reformist face of his father's government and a likely successor.

## Role in the Badlands corpus

The Badlands Media article A Conflict of Interest cites Saif al-Islam in connection with the release of [[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] from Libyan custody. Quoting Africa Intelligence, the article states that in 2009 Belhaj — the former emir of the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]], who had been interrogated in [[cia|CIA]] prisons in the early 2000s and then held in Libya's Abu Salim Prison — negotiated his liberation with the help of [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]] preacher [[ali-sallabi|Ali Sallabi]] and Gaddafi's son Saif al-Islam.

The article treats the timing of this release as significant, arguing that it followed [[barack-obama|Barack Obama's]] election in 2008 and that the elder Gaddafi, then seeking to maintain the cooperative relationship with Washington he had adopted after the September 11 attacks, agreed to free Belhaj and other LIFG figures as a goodwill gesture toward the incoming administration. Two years later, according to the article, the men released through this channel led the insurgency that toppled and killed Muammar Gaddafi.
