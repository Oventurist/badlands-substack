# Brad Schimel

Brad Schimel was the Republican-backed candidate in the 2025 Wisconsin Supreme Court race.[1]

[[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] played a major role in that contest, with Musk and affiliated groups spending at least $20 million in support of Schimel's candidacy.[1] Musk's [[america-pac|America PAC]] also offered $100 to voters who signed a petition opposing "activist judges" or who referred others to sign it.[1] Musk's separate offer of $1 million to voters in the same race later led the [[wisconsin-elections-commission|Wisconsin Elections Commission]] to refer complaints for criminal review.[1]

Schimel, a Waukesha County judge, faced Dane County Judge [[susan-crawford|Susan Crawford]] in the [[2025-wisconsin-supreme-court-election|April 2025 Wisconsin Supreme Court election]], a contest that drew unprecedented out-of-state funding from prominent political groups and megadonors in addition to grassroots contributions.[2] Although Wisconsin caps direct contributions to individual Supreme Court candidates at $20,000, unlimited sums may flow through political action committees or the state political parties, helping push total spending on the race to at least $81 million.[2]

## April 2025 defeat

Schimel, a Waukesha County circuit court judge and former Republican state attorney general, lost the April 1, 2025 Wisconsin Supreme Court election to Dane County judge [[susan-crawford|Susan Crawford]], leaving the liberal majority on the state's highest court intact.[3] The race, held to fill the seat of retiring Justice Ann Walsh Bradley, was widely cast as a referendum on [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], who poured millions into supporting Schimel, and Schimel was endorsed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and most Republicans while Crawford drew backing from Barack Obama and other prominent Democrats.[3] Though nonpartisan in form, the contest turned on labor rights, abortion access and Trump's January 6 pardons.[3]

## Turnout context

Election analyst [[seth-keshel|Seth Keshel]] wrote that Schimel was "suffocated" by ten points by [[susan-crawford|Susan Crawford]], despite himself setting a new Wisconsin spring-election record vote total on the losing side of a Supreme Court race.[4] Keshel argued the result illustrated Democratic dominance of mail-ballot turnout in off-year elections, noting that roughly 600,000 votes had been enough to win the equivalent Wisconsin contests in 2018 and 2019.[4]
