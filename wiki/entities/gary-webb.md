# Gary Webb

Gary Webb was an American investigative journalist invoked by Badlands Media as a benchmark of genuine journalistic practice.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

The article names Webb alongside [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]] as a reporter who challenged and exposed consequential lies, dark secrets and corruption within institutions such as the [[cia|CIA]], the [[fbi|FBI]], Wall Street and the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) He is contrasted with [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]], who Badlands argues has done nothing comparable and has instead pursued cancellation campaigns over language.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The comparison supports the article's central claim that the modern corporate press no longer performs the journalistic function protected by the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Dark Alliance and the Contras

Webb's 1996 "Dark Alliance" series alleged that traffickers linked to the [[nicaraguan-contras|Nicaraguan Contras]], among them Danilo Blandón and Norwin Meneses, funnelled cocaine into Los Angeles and fuelled the crack epidemic, with the profits flowing back into the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]-backed war against the Sandinista government.[2] The reporting built on the terrain mapped by the 1989 [[kerry-committee-report|Kerry Committee report]], which had found Contra suppliers and pilots deeply entangled in cocaine trafficking with the knowledge of U.S. officials.[2]

On 10 December 2004, Webb was found in his home with two gunshot wounds to the head; the autopsy concluded the wounds were self-inflicted.[2] His book *Dark Alliance* is listed among the principal sources for Badlands Media's account of the [[war-on-drugs|War on Drugs]].[2]
