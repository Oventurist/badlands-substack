# Central Florida Tourism Oversight District

The Central Florida Tourism Oversight District (CFTOD) is the special governmental district that oversees the land occupied by Disney World in Florida.[1] It was created in February 2023 when Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] signed legislation abolishing its predecessor, the [[reedy-creek-improvement-district|Reedy Creek Improvement District]], and appointed the new board's members himself.[1]

## Origins

The restructuring grew out of DeSantis's confrontation with [[disney]] after the company's leadership publicly opposed his Parental Rights in Education bill, labeled the "Don't Say Gay" bill by detractors.[1] DeSantis argued that no other business in Florida enjoyed a comparable arrangement, noting that Disney had appointed the members of the body that regulated it and had received various tax exemptions under the self-governing status it held since the district's creation in 1967.[1]

## Elimination of DEI programs

On August 1, 2023, District Administrator Glenton Gilzean announced the abolition of all diversity, equity, and inclusion programs at the district, including its DEI board.[1] The district's statement said the action followed an internal investigation and came after Reedy Creek had implemented hiring and contracting programs that "discriminated against Americans based on gender and race, costing taxpayers millions of dollars."[1]

## Limits and criticism

Commentators at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] observed that the decision came from the DeSantis-created oversight board rather than from Disney itself, so it would apply only to entities within the district's jurisdiction and not to the company's practices nationally.[1] Legal ambiguity remained over the board's power to affect Disney's internal policies, with pro-DEI analysts arguing the prohibition reached only government workers.[1] Michael McAuliffe, a former federal prosecutor and elected state attorney in Florida, told [[newsweek|Newsweek]] that the district's pronouncement "appears to be a mixture of political posturing and legal implementation," while companies including Disney were likely revamping DEI initiatives to avoid using race as an explicit remedial factor.[1]
