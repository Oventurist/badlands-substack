# Nikki Haley

Nikki Haley is an American Republican politician who, in Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s account of the aftermath of October 7, 2023, was "neck and neck" with [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] in competing over who was more fervently devoted to Israel.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

DeLarme cites Haley as an example of the rhetorical pattern by which those seeking to erode the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] preface their intentions: statements of the form "College campuses are allowed free speech, BUT…", where the true intention follows the "but."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) In Haley's case that intention was to take a law designed to narrow the parameters of free expression and, in her phrasing, "give it teeth."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

DeLarme also argues that Haley's framing overlooks the fact that a person can be both a Jew or "Semite" and an anti-Zionist simultaneously, which he says illustrates the amorphous and dubious nature of the term "[[antisemitism]]" and how its excessive application diminishes genuine cases.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) He further groups her with Lindsey Graham among those who have intimated support for the killing of Gaza's population, arguing that such repellent speech is nonetheless protected — the same protection he says should extend to Israel's critics.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## October 2023 response to the Hamas attacks

Following the [[october-7-attacks|October 7, 2023 attacks]] on Israel, Haley — then a Republican presidential primary candidate — called the assault "an attack on America."[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], described her as a "GOP fake primary candidate" and cited her remarks as evidence that the entire establishment had aligned behind Israel within hours.[2] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] characterized her reaction as "war mongering opportunism" typical of neocons, and argued there was "seemingly a lot more going on with Israel than just war hawks like Nikki Haley trying to gobble up some of the government graft."[2]

## December 2023 remarks on Gaza refugees and Putin

In December 2023, Haley made two statements about the Israel–Hamas war that [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] described as ultra controversial and somewhat bizarre.[3] In an ABC News interview she lashed out at what she called the "pro-Hamas" countries of [[iran|Iran]], [[qatar|Qatar]] and [[turkey|Turkey]], arguing that they should take in all Palestinians fleeing the war in Gaza given their well-known support for the terror group.[3] Asked where war refugees in Gaza should go, she answered that "they should be going to the Rafah gate and [have] [[egypt|Egypt]] take them," before adding that "what you should have is that they should go to pro-Hamas countries — Qatar, Iran, Turkey… send them there."[3] She asked why the conversation always returned to [[israel|Israel]] and the United States rather than Egypt, Turkey, Qatar or Iran, and said that if the October 7 attack had happened to America, "do you not think that we would have hit back?"[3]

Haley, who was attempting an improbable primary win over GOP frontrunner [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], had long been a staunch supporter of Israel and an advocate for maximal U.S. defense and foreign aid, with Israel remaining the largest recipient at over $3 billion annually.[3] The [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] government would likely have agreed with her position, having pressed Egypt to take in hundreds of thousands of Palestinians and place them in tent cities in the Sinai desert.[3] Separately, at a campaign rally, Haley told supporters that "Hamas invaded Israel on October 7th, October 7th is [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s birthday," implying a connection between the Russian president and the attackers.[3]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] wrote that a conservative transported back to 2002 might have felt at home cheering "every word of warmongering, zionist propaganda" in Haley's appeal, including her conspiracy-theory peddling about the Russian Federation.[3] He argued that the ability of the base to laugh or cringe at such statements in 2023 showed how far American conservatism had come in the age of Trump, MAGA and the [[america-first-movement|America First movement]], and that establishment figures propped up on "faux-MAGA stages" served both as a rapid red-pill delivery mechanism to normies and as a reminder of what the movement once was.[3]

## Pro-Israel primary contest (October 2023)

During the 2024 Republican presidential primary, Haley was described by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] as locked in a contest with Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] over who could demonstrate the most intense loyalty to [[israel|Israel]], in a spectacle the outlet said "would repel Washington and Jefferson."[4] Whereas DeSantis's status as a sitting governor allowed him to take executive action — including using state cargo planes to send drones, body armor, helmets and privately purchased guns and ammunition to Israel — Haley was "limited to warmongering, interventionist rhetoric."[4]

## Criticism over Gaza war advocacy (May 2024)

In May 2024 Badlands commentary named Haley together with [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] as archetypes of an "Age of Cowards," describing them as two people who had never seen combat or served in the military yet called for war and mass death in [[gaza|Gaza]].[5] The remarks appeared in coverage of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s threat to halt offensive weapons shipments to [[israel|Israel]] over a [[rafah|Rafah]] invasion.[5]

## Criticism of Trump's tariff plan

As of February 2024, Haley was [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s sole remaining challenger for the Republican presidential nomination.[6] When Trump confirmed in a February 4 interview that he was considering tariffs of 60% or higher on Chinese goods in a second term, and possibly a blanket 10% tariff on all US imports, the former UN ambassador criticized the proposal for the impact it would have on American pocketbooks.[6]

## 2024 Michigan primary

In the [[2024-michigan-republican-primary|Michigan Republican primary]] of February 27, 2024, Haley lost decisively to Trump, who appeared on a path to roughly 70 percent of the vote.[7] She nonetheless drew more than a quarter of the vote, a share significant enough to raise questions about Trump's general-election standing, though it fell short of her 40 percent in South Carolina and 43 percent in New Hampshire in the previous two head-to-head contests.[7] The result increased pressure on her to exit the race, but Haley said she planned to remain in at least through the March 5 "Super Tuesday" contests across 15 states.[7] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that establishment coverage of her campaign had fractured — half the corporate media lambasting her run while the other half sounded alarms about Trump on her behalf — which he read as outlets hedging their narratives.[7]

## February 8, 2024 contests

On February 8, 2024, Haley lost two more contests to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on the same day.[8] In the [[2024-us-virgin-islands-republican-caucuses|US Virgin Islands caucuses]] she received 64 votes, or 26 percent, to Trump's 182 votes and 74 percent, with Trump expected to net all four territorial delegates.[8] In Nevada, having chosen to compete in the state-run primary rather than the party's delegate-allocating [[2024-nevada-republican-caucuses|caucuses]], she finished second behind the ballot line "none of these candidates," an embarrassing defeat for a candidate presenting herself as a serious challenger; she received 14,328 votes in a primary that drew just 42,702 participants, 7.6 percent of registered Nevada Republicans.[8]

## Trump's threat to bar Haley donors (January 2024)

On Wednesday, January 24, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] railed against Haley on his Truth Social platform and warned that anyone contributing to her campaign would be "permanently barred from the MAGA camp."[9] Trump wrote that when he ran for office and won he had noticed that the losing candidate's donors "would immediately come to me, and want to 'help out,'" adding that while this was standard in politics it would "no longer" be so with him.[9] Using his nickname "Birdbrain" for Haley, he continued: "Anybody that makes a 'Contribution' to Birdbrain, from this moment forth, will be permanently barred from the MAGA camp. We don't want them, and will not accept them, because we Put America First, and ALWAYS WILL!"[9]

The Hill characterized the direct threat against Haley's supporters as a sharpening of Trump's rhetoric as he sought to compel support from all corners of the Republican Party.[9] Trump had beaten Haley in the New Hampshire primary the previous Tuesday by 11 points — a significant edge over his former United Nations ambassador, though closer than his dominant lead in many polls in preceding months.[9]

## 2024 South Carolina primary defeat

On February 24, 2024, Haley lost the [[2024-south-carolina-republican-primary|South Carolina Republican primary]] to Donald Trump in the state where she had served as governor from 2011 to 2017, a defeat that intensified pressure on her to leave the race.[10] She insisted she was "not going anywhere" and vowed to stay in through at least the March 5 Super Tuesday contests, but despite holding far more campaign events than Trump and arguing that the indictments against him would hamstring him against Joe Biden, she was unable to dent his momentum.[10] The Associated Press called the race for Trump the moment polls closed at 7 p.m., based on AP VoteCast survey data that confirmed pre-election polling showing Trump far ahead statewide.[10] Badlands contributor Ashe in America argued that ubiquitous crossover voting by Democrats was propping Haley up and that even with that assistance "she's being humiliated," calling her pledge to continue "so silly, you almost have to wonder if it's real."[10]

## March 2024 caucus losses

On Saturday, March 2, 2024, Haley lost the Idaho and Missouri caucuses and the Michigan Republican convention to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who took every delegate at stake that day.[11] The results brought Trump's delegate count to 244 against 24 for Haley, with 1,215 needed to clinch the Republican nomination.[11] The next contest was in the District of Columbia on Sunday, followed two days later by Super Tuesday, when 16 states would hold primaries on the largest day of voting of the year outside November.[11] The steep odds facing Haley were on display in Columbia, Missouri, where Republicans gathered at a church to caucus.[11]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Trump had taken all the delegates in Missouri and Idaho while losing to Haley in DC as of press time, arguing that "the Haley bros are desperate for her to have a win — any win — but this one puts a super fine point on the fact that Nikki Haley is a swamp creature."[11]

## 2024 New Hampshire primary

Haley lost the [[2024-new-hampshire-republican-primary|New Hampshire Republican primary]] on January 23, 2024 to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who was projected the winner within minutes of the final polls closing.[12] By that point she was Trump's last remaining major rival for the nomination, having watched him crush the field in the Iowa caucuses eight days earlier.[12] Speaking to supporters in Concord after the race was called, Haley said: "I want to congratulate Donald Trump on his victory tonight. He earned it and I want to acknowledge that."[12]

Badlands commentary read the contest structurally rather than as a straightforward defeat for Haley, with [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] arguing that New Hampshire — where Democrats are historically permitted to vote in the Republican primary — functions as the establishment's mechanism for reversing the harder-to-manipulate Iowa result and manufacturing momentum for an establishment-favored candidate.[12]

## Suspension of her campaign (March 2024)

In early March 2024, following decisive losses to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the early primary states and on Super Tuesday, the former South Carolina governor announced that she was suspending her White House bid.[13] Her withdrawal left Trump as the presumptive Republican nominee and prompted him, the following day, to call publicly for debates with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] "ANYTIME, ANYWHERE, ANYPLACE."[13] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed Haley and the rest of the primary field as "fake primary contenders" who were never a serious threat, noting that Trump had led by as much as 70 points and arguing that debating "that clown car" would have been beneath him.[13]

## 2024 Israel visit and "Finish Them!" inscription

On a Memorial Day 2024 visit to Israel, Haley signed Israeli artillery shells with the inscription "Finish Them!", accompanied by the words "America loves Israel."[14] The former South Carolina governor made the display on a trip to Israel's northern border with Lebanon, escorted by [[danny-danon|Danny Danon]], a former Israeli ambassador to the United Nations and a hawkish [[likud|Likud]] member of the Knesset.[14] The episode came while Israel was prosecuting an offensive in Gaza that had killed more than 36,000 Palestinians, an estimated 15,000 of them children.[14]

Speaking to reporters, Haley was unapologetic, criticising the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration for temporarily withholding weapons to discourage an Israeli assault on Rafah, and attacking both the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] — then seeking [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s arrest — and the International Court of Justice, which was weighing genocide charges against Israel.[14] "What America needs to understand is if Israel's fighting our enemies, how can we not help them," she said.[14]

Badlands commentary responded by asking "Finish what, exactly?", suggesting Haley might be referring to [[fatah|Fatah]] and [[hamas|Hamas]]'s displacement of it within the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], and citing Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]]'s argument that the IDF campaign had itself become Hamas's greatest recruiting tool.[14] The same writer contrasted Haley's rhetoric unfavourably with Saudi foreign minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Prince Faisal bin Farhan]]'s statement that Israel "doesn't get to decide whether or not the Palestinians have a right to self-determination," concluding that Haley "sounds like a bloodthirsty psychopath" beside him.[14]

## Crossover Democratic voting and donor flight (January 2024)

Reporting after the [[2024-new-hampshire-republican-primary|New Hampshire primary]] highlighted the role of non-Republicans in Haley's vote. Videos from New Hampshire showed Democratic voters attempting to game the Republican primary to derail [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s nomination, and Democrats were able to vote in the Republican contest in large numbers for Haley.[15] CNN found that while 70 percent of Trump's voters were registered Republicans, 70 percent of Haley's voters were registered undeclared voters; she took only 27 percent of registered Republicans.[15]

In the days after the loss, billionaire [[reid-hoffman|Reid Hoffman]] — among the first of her wealthy donors to head for the exit — was reported to have no plans to give further money to her campaign, after she said she would stay in the race despite defeats in New Hampshire and Iowa.[15]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed Haley through the concept of proxy candidates, arguing that parties and candidates are "little more than shells for the systematized interests that back them," and that Haley, "by hook or by crook," was being used to expose the fact that the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] is real and unified against Trump.[15]

## Withdrawal from the 2024 race

Following dismal results on Super Tuesday, Haley planned to end her presidential campaign as early as the morning of Wednesday, March 6, 2024, according to a Wall Street Journal report citing people familiar with her plans.[16] Of the 15 states holding Republican contests, she secured a victory only in Vermont, in addition to a win in the District of Columbia.[16] The NBC News delegate tracker showed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] leading with 1,057 delegates against Haley's 92.[16] Her departure effectively guaranteed Trump the Republican nomination and a November showdown with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[16] The Federalist's Sean Davis highlighted an exit-poll finding that 92 percent of Haley's voters approved of Biden's performance.[16]

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Haley beat Trump in Vermont by roughly 3,000 votes while losing the rest of the country, and pointed out that although Vermont had voted Republican in 71 percent of presidential elections since 1900, it had voted Democrat 100 percent of the time since 2000 — suggesting Vermont voters examine their elections in light of the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]] passed in 2002.[16] She concluded that Haley "never had a path because she's an unlikable war monger," and welcomed the end of her "humiliating 15 minutes."[16]

## Nevada primary loss to "None of These Candidates"

In the [[2024-nevada-republican-primary|2024 Nevada Republican primary]] held on February 6, 2024, Haley failed to win despite being the only major candidate on the ballot, finishing second to the ballot line "None of These Candidates" by roughly 22,000 votes and taking less than a third of the total.[17] More than 60 percent of participants voted for no one, while 2,705 cast ballots for [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]]; [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] tallied 26,038 votes for "none of these candidates" against Haley's 14,328, a margin of about 27 percent.[17] Turnout was just 42,702 voters — 7.6 percent of the state's registered Republicans — with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] absent from the ballot because Nevada was concurrently running a party caucus.[17] Trump joked that Haley would soon be claiming victory.[17]

## December 2023 opposition from Donald Trump Jr.

In a Newsmax interview aired December 25, 2023, [[donald-trump-jr|Donald Trump Jr.]] said he "wouldn't have" Nikki Haley as his father's running mate and would "go to great lengths to make sure that doesn't happen."[18] The remarks followed footage of Haley — a former U.S. ambassador to the United Nations and a GOP presidential candidate — telling ABC News Live Prime that "I don't think he should be president" and that "Donald Trump brings us chaos."[18] Trump Jr. accused her of wanting "never-ending wars" and of being "a puppet of the establishment in Washington, D.C.," and "the new favorite candidate of the billionaire class."[18]

## Ramaswamy clash over Israel (October 2023)

In October 2023, rival Republican presidential candidate [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] publicly criticized Haley for what he described as a "childish tantrum" regarding the attacks on Israel, telling [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] that her approach was "a formula for World War III."[19] The clip, shared by Carlson on X, was featured as a bonus item in the Badlands News Brief covering the opening days of the Israel–[[hamas|Hamas]] war.[19]

## Don Lemon's "past her prime" remarks (February 2023)

In February 2023, after Haley called for politicians over the age of 75 to take mental competency tests, [[cnn|CNN]] anchor [[don-lemon|Don Lemon]] said on air that Haley "isn't in her prime," adding that women are in their prime in their twenties and thirties.[20] The remarks drew a rebuke from CNN chief executive [[chris-licht|Chris Licht]] and preceded Lemon's removal from the network's morning show lineup, an outcome Badlands commentators attributed to Lemon having attacked a Republican politician from a "woke leftist" vantage point.[20]


## Endorsement of Trump and speculation about a role (May 2024)

On May 22, 2024, in her first remarks since dropping out of the Republican presidential race, Haley announced she would vote for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and described [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s presidency as a "catastrophe."[21] Asked days later by News 12 New York after a Bronx campaign rally whether Haley might join his team or his ticket, Trump responded positively: "Well, I think she's going to be on our team because we have a lot of the same ideas, the same thoughts... we had a nasty campaign, it was pretty nasty, but she's a very capable person and I'm sure she's going to be on our team in some form absolutely."[21] Haley had previously served as United Nations ambassador during Trump's first administration.[21]

## South Carolina primary stand (February 2024)

On February 20, 2024, Haley vowed to remain in the Republican presidential primary against former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], telling a crowd in her home state of South Carolina: "I'm not going anywhere."[22] She said she was "not afraid to say the hard truths out loud," felt "no need to kiss the ring," had "no fear of Trump's retribution," and that her own political future was "of zero concern," comparing the race to David versus Goliath.[22] Her hawkish position on war took centre stage in the primary, and the campaign followed a bruising weekend for her social media operation.[22] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] argued that, given her insurmountable deficit, the evident purpose of staying in was to prevent the right from consolidating behind Trump before November.[22]

## Coverage during the Michigan primary week

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that most headlines and commentary around the February 2024 Michigan primaries had been relegated to the memetic quality of Haley's "continued political death spiral," distracting from what he saw as the more significant signal in the Democratic [[2024-michigan-democratic-primary|uncommitted protest vote]].[23]

## Her son's dispute with Vivek Ramaswamy (October 2025)

In October 2025 Haley's Catholic son, [[nalin-haley|Nalin Haley]], became a prominent online critic of fellow Republican [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]], attacking him for likening the Holy Trinity to Hinduism's "330 million gods" and calling the comparison "blasphemous, disrespectful, and a slap in the face to every Christian."[26] Coverage of the exchange noted the family's Indian origin and Sikh grandparents, and described Nikki Haley as an Indian-origin GOP leader and former Governor of South Carolina.[26]

## Debate boycott before the New Hampshire primary

After finishing third in the January 2024 Iowa caucuses, Haley announced that she would not participate in any further primary debates unless [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] joined them, a stance that contributed to the cancellation of both the [[cnn|CNN]] and [[abc-news|ABC News]]/WMUR debates planned before the New Hampshire primary.[25] "We've had five great debates in this campaign. Unfortunately, Donald Trump has ducked all of them," she wrote on X, adding: "He has nowhere left to hide. The next debate I do will either be with Donald Trump or with Joe Biden. I look forward to it."[25] CNN instead scheduled a town-hall event with Haley for that Thursday.[25]
