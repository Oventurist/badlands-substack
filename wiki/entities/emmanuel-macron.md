# Emmanuel Macron

Emmanuel Macron is the President of France and one of the European incumbent leaders described in early 2026 as facing severe domestic unpopularity.[1]

## 2026 political position

Macron registered just 16% approval amid gains by far-right parties in France.[1] He was grouped with U.K. Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] and German Chancellor [[friedrich-merz|Friedrich Merz]] as leaders whose weakness has driven far-right parties to lead or near-lead polls in Germany, the U.K., France, and Italy over inflation, economic stagnation, and immigration backlash.[1]

This domestic fragility coincided with a broader transatlantic rift, in which European officials increasingly described the United States under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as a rival rather than an ally, and pursued "de-risking" through diversified ties and a rethinking of defense.[1]

## Refusal to join the Hormuz coalition

In March 2026 Macron rejected President Trump's call for an international coalition to secure the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] during the war with [[iran|Iran]], stating that France is "not party to the conflict" and "will never take part in operations" to reopen the strait.[2] His position was part of a broader refusal by [[nato|NATO]] allies — including Germany, Spain, Italy and [[greece|Greece]] — that declared the conflict "not our war."[2] Trump criticized the reluctance as a "one-way street" while insisting the United States no longer needed allied assistance.[2]

## Declaration that the Iran strikes violated international law

In early March 2026 Macron said publicly that the attacks on [[iran|Iran]] violated international law, a statement issued alongside a call by a Spanish member of the European Parliament for withdrawal from [[nato|NATO]] following the strikes.[3] Badlands analyst [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the remarks as evidence that transatlantic alignment behind the campaign was fracturing in plain sight, and read that fracture as a reverse indicator that the operation was heading toward a contained outcome under the [[venezuela-model|Venezuela Model]] rather than the open-ended conflict the "Forever War complex" expected.[3]

## "Wake up" address in Greece (April 2026)

During a visit to Greece in April 2026, Macron urged Europe to strengthen its unity and defend its own interests, saying the geopolitical moment required Europeans to "wake up" because the leaders of the United States, Russia and China were acting against European interests, according to POLITICO Europe.[4] He made the remarks alongside Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis, and the two said recent [[european-union|European Union]] moves to expand defense spending were meant to reinforce the alliance with [[nato|NATO]] rather than replace it.[4] Macron also defended the EU's mutual defense clause, saying it carried real weight and pointing to coordinated assistance provided to Cyprus after a regional security incident.[4] The comments were consistent with his long-running calls for European strategic autonomy and reduced dependence on outside powers.[4]

Badlands analyst [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the speech as a reverse indicator, arguing that "the enemy always tells you what they fear the most" and that Macron had effectively confirmed the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] is real, that the European continent knows it is real, and that it is winning.[4] In his reading, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] have been executing pincer moves against the globalist Western hegemon on kinetic, political, financial and energy grounds, with [[the-iranian-knot|the Iranian Knot]] representing the closing of those pincers at once.[4]

## Davos 2026

At the January 2026 [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] meeting in Davos, Macron warned of a dangerous "shift towards a world without rules," in which international law is trampled and "the law of the strongest" prevails, and rejected any return to imperialism.[5] He urged the [[european-union|European Union]] to consider deploying its [[anti-coercion-instrument|anti-coercion instrument]] against U.S. tariffs for the first time, while arguing that peace in Ukraine should take priority over an escalating trade war.[5] His remarks came amid the [[greenland-tariff-dispute-2026|tariff dispute triggered by the U.S. push to acquire Greenland]], on which European leaders presented a united front.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] mocked Macron's appearance at the forum, noting that he delivered his full speech, posed for group photos, and held one-on-one discussions while wearing sunglasses, and joking that he might be concealing an eye infection.[5] He described Macron as arriving fresh off another [[humiliation-ritual|humiliation ritual]] at the hands of [[donald-trump|President Trump]].[5]

## Niger crisis (2023)

In August 2023 Macron announced that France's ambassador to [[niger|Niger]], [[sylvain-itte|Sylvain Itte]], would remain at his post despite the ruling junta's demand that he depart within 48 hours.[6] Speaking to a gathering of French ambassadors in Paris, Macron condemned the coup leaders while insisting that [[france|France]] was not Niger's enemy, and dismissed suggestions that standing up to the junta was dangerous.[6] "Our policy is the right one," he said. "It depends on the courage of President [[mohamed-bazoum|Mohamed Bazoum]], the commitment of our diplomats, of our ambassador on the ground who is remaining despite pressure."[6] He added that "one shouldn't give in to the narrative used by the coup leaders that consists of saying France has become our enemy."[6] Badlands commentary read the episode as France conflating diplomatic solutions with military activity as the situation in Niger slipped from its control.[6]

## Warnings over the green agenda (2024)

In April 2024 [[david-djaiz|David Djaïz]], a former Macron advisor and the
government's rapporteur for the National Council for Refoundation, warned that
if France continued down the same path with the green agenda the country would
likely face a "social revolt" far greater than the
[[yellow-vest-movement|Yellow Vest movement]] or the recent farmer
protests.[7] Djaïz accused the government and political elites of being
"content to manage the decline" and described a "deficit of strategic thinking"
in the halls of power, saying he had seen from the inside "how intellectually
and operationally the state was exhausted."[7] Populist uprisings have been a
defining feature of Macron's government; the former Rothschild banker has been
branded the "president of the rich," with reporting noting that half of his
government ministers are millionaires.[7]

## Condemnation after the al-Ahli hospital blast (October 2023)

Hours after the [[al-ahli-baptist-hospital-explosion|al-Ahli Baptist Hospital explosion]] in Gaza on October 17, 2023, Macron's government issued a condemnation stating that "nothing can justify targeting civilians," a statement widely read as placing blame on Israel and adding to the pressure on [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to join the international chorus of condemnation.[8] The French foreign ministry added that "international humanitarian law is binding on all and must enable the protection of civilian populations," and that "humanitarian access to the Gaza Strip must be opened up without delay."[8]

## The "coalition of the willing" troop proposal (March 2025)

In March 2025 Macron, alongside the United Kingdom, led efforts to form a "coalition of the willing" to deploy peacekeepers to [[ukraine|Ukraine]] to secure an eventual truce between Kiev and Moscow, suggesting that "a few thousand troops per country" would be stationed at key locations.[9] Italian Prime Minister [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] publicly rejected the plan in an address to the Italian Senate, describing it as "a very complex, risky and ineffective option" and saying Italian participation had never been on the agenda.[9]

Badlands commentary argued that Macron and [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] were, perhaps unwittingly, establishing an offensive mandate for [[nato|NATO]] in a conflict involving a non-member state, and that Meloni's refusal marked the beginning of a fracturing of the alliance.[9]

## Recognition of Palestine (July 2025)

In a surprise statement posted to X on Thursday, July 24, 2025, Macron announced that France would recognize Palestine as a state in pursuit of what he called a "historic commitment to a just and lasting peace" in the region.[10] He said he would make the formal announcement at the United Nations General Assembly in New York that September, adding that "the urgent need today is to end the war in Gaza and to rescue the civilian population."[10]

Badlands commentary noted that roughly 140 countries already recognized Palestine, but argued the French move carried outsized weight because France and Britain had carved up the Middle East and Africa more than a century earlier and France remained central to the region's security architecture — making the announcement a signal that the geopolitical calculus of the West was rapidly changing and that Israel was no longer the priority it once was.[10] See [[palestinian-statehood]].

## Salvini's criticism and domestic unpopularity (August 2025)

In August 2025 Italian Deputy Prime Minister [[matteo-salvini|Matteo Salvini]] claimed that some 80% of France's population "can't wait" for Macron to leave office, and argued that the president's warlike rhetoric — including talk of sending troops to Ukraine — was aimed only at boosting his dwindling popularity at home.[11] Paris summoned Italian Ambassador Emanuela D'Alessandro over Salvini's earlier statements, in which he had mockingly urged Macron to take up arms and go fight for Ukraine alone since "not even [one] Frenchman would follow him."[11] Salvini reiterated the charge at a Lega event in Trentino, saying that in an attempt to raise his approval Macron "one day attacks [US President Donald] Trump, one day [Russian President Vladimir] Putin, one day [Israeli PM] Netanyahu, one day the Italian government," and that months of talk about "war, the nuclear umbrella, bazookas, missiles, and a European army" had been cooked up because "Macron's popularity at home is at its lowest."[11]

## "The EU could die" (October 2024)

Speaking at the Berlin Global Dialogue event on Wednesday, October 2, 2024, Macron warned that the [[european-union|European Union]] economy needed to become more competitive with the United States and China or it "could die."[12] He said the 27-nation bloc was falling behind Washington and Beijing, both of which had outstripped the EU in economic output and investment, and that the Union was in a very precarious situation if it failed to deepen its single market and resolve "fragmentation."[12] "The EU could die, we are on a verge of a very important moment," he said, adding: "Our former model is over – we are over-regulating and under-investing. In the two to three years to come, if we follow our classical agenda we will be out of the market."[12] He further warned that absent urgent regulatory reform the bloc would be forced to implement a rescue plan within five to ten years.[12]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] dismissed the speech as a "feigned moment of sobriety during an otherwise humiliating display of 'failed leadership,'" refusing to credit Macron — whom he mockingly nicknamed "Monsieur Enculé" — with genuinely diagnosing Europe's troubles.[12]

## March 2025 address on the Russian threat and the French nuclear umbrella

In a nationally televised address on Wednesday, March 5, 2025, Macron said France was considering extending its nuclear umbrella to the rest of Europe, in what ZeroHedge characterized as a hawkish anti-Russia turn.[13] The shift was notable because in the opening six months of the Ukraine–Russia war Macron had come under fire in Europe as one of the few major leaders to hold regular diplomatic calls with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in an effort to end the conflict.[13]

"We are entering a new era," Macron told his countrymen. "If a country can invade its neighbour in Europe and go unpunished, nobody can be sure of anything." He asserted that "beyond Ukraine, the Russian threat is real — it affects the European countries," and claimed that "President Putin is violating our borders to assassinate opponents, manipulate elections."[13] He added, "We need to prepare," and, in reference to shifting Washington policy, "I want to believe that the US will stand by our side, but we have to be ready for that not to be the case."[13] Macron also claimed that "France has to recognize its special status — we have the most efficient, effective army in Europe," saying France had nuclear weapons to provide to the broader Western alliance if called upon.[13]

The address came amid a broader European push to fill the gap left by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s suspension of US arms shipments and intelligence sharing with Kiev; days earlier, at a London security conference of European leaders, UK Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] had presented a plan for up to 30,000 British peacekeeping troops for Ukraine, supported by France.[13] ZeroHedge wrote that Macron "pulled out all the stops" and appeared to be making himself into the European version of Russia's Medvedev, going "maximal no limits jingoism."[13]

## Hanoi 'shove' video (May 2025)

A viral clip recorded after Macron's aircraft landed in Hanoi, Vietnam, during a Southeast Asia tour appeared to show his wife, [[brigitte-macron|Brigitte Macron]], shoving his face away as he stood in the open doorway of the plane; the president recovered, smiled and waved before disembarking alongside her.[14] The Élysée Palace and Macron himself dismissed the speculation, with the president telling reporters on 26 May 2025 that the couple had simply been "horsing around."[14] Badlands contributors were unconvinced: [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that Brigitte refused her husband's arm on the stairs, that Macron's fists were clenched during the tarmac press availability, and that a woman with a ripped shirt and bloody shoulder was visible in the footage.[14]

## EU pressure on Hungary

France and Germany joined a majority of EU member states in urging the European Commission to crack down on Hungary over Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]]'s plan to ban Pride celebrations in Budapest, according to a joint statement reported in May 2025.[14]

## Paris talks on Ukraine (December 2024)

In December 2024, Macron organized talks in Paris bringing together Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] and US President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to discuss the nearly three-year war in Ukraine, described as the biggest armed conflict in Europe since the defeat of Nazi Germany.[15] Zelensky publicly thanked Macron for arranging the meeting and Trump for his "strong resolve" to bring the war to a fair end.[15] Separately, Badlands commentary listed Macron among the leaders - alongside [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] and [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]] - it urged to "take the deal" in the face of Trump's tariff diplomacy.[15]

## Withdrawal from Niger (2023)

On Sunday, September 24, 2023, Macron announced that France would end its military presence in [[niger|Niger]] by the end of 2023, the latest major development amid high tensions between the two countries since a military junta seized control of the country in July.[16] "We are putting an end to our military cooperation with the de facto authorities of Niger because they don't want to fight terrorism anymore," he said of the military leaders who had taken over the northwest African country.[16] France had not recognized Niger's military authorities and insisted that ousted President [[mohamed-bazoum|Mohamed Bazoum]] remained the country's only legitimate authority.[16] Macron said the decision to end the cooperation was taken "because we are not there to deal with internal politics and be hostages of putschists," and that the withdrawal would be organized in the coming weeks.[16]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the announcement as the conclusion of French colonialism in a resource-rich nation and as a retreat scored by the triumphant shouts of the Nigerien people.[16] She noted that to the long-colonized, the French framing of Niger no longer wanting to "fight terrorism" read as the terrorists leaving, and that while [[ecowas|ECOWAS]] had yet to accept defeat, the conflict appeared finished and the West had lost.[16]

## Call for a new world order (September 2024)

On Sunday, September 22, 2024, Macron called for reform of the current "unjust" world order so that humans can coexist more peacefully, outlining his vision at the international "Imagining Peace" gathering in Paris hosted by the [[community-of-santegidio|Community of Sant'Egidio]].[17] He said Europe must be "imaginative enough to think about the peace of tomorrow, a peace in Europe in a new form," one that is "neither quite the EU, nor resolutely NATO," and that after the Ukraine conflict ends the continent "will have to think of a new form of organization for Europe and rethink our relationship with Russia."[17]

The speech followed a pattern of mixed signals on the Russia-Ukraine war: in 2022 Macron drew Western criticism for urging that Russia not be "humiliated," and in early 2024 he said the West should not rule out deploying NATO troops on Ukrainian soil, a proposal rejected by multiple other leaders.[17] His remarks came as Ukrainian leader [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] prepared to present a "victory plan" to President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], including permission for long-range strikes deep inside Russia with Western weapons.[17]

Badlands Media cited RT for the headline but pointed to the Associated Press account of Macron's nearly two-hour Sorbonne University speech, in which he said the continent is divided and "too slow and lacks ambition" at a time when the 27-member European Union must become a superpower, defend its own borders and speak with one voice; "Russia mustn't be allowed to win," he added.[17] The outlet read that framing as "the rhetoric of ascending tyrants," and compared Macron's line that "Europe must become capable of defending its interests, with its allies by our side whenever they are willing, and alone if necessary" to the rhetoric of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[17]

Macron also called for strong armies, a European rapid intervention force, tanks, a missile shield and other European-produced weapons, backed by "a joint diplomatic force that will speak with one voice and build bridges with Africa and Latin America."[17] Badlands rejected the African premise, arguing that African states have organized against European designs and that "bridges" is a euphemism for violent coups and destabilizing color revolutions; it credited the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]] and the Russian military with training security forces to counter NATO- and EU-trained forces on the continent.[17] It further noted that the Prime Minister of [[chad|Chad]] had said US troops who left in May 2024 were not returning, and that it is the French military the Chadian public considers a hostile adversary — with expulsion meaning that, for the first time since 1659, [[france|France]] would have no military presence in Africa.[17]


## Meeting with Trump in Paris (December 2024)

On Saturday, December 7, 2024, Macron received President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the Elysee Palace in Paris for a meeting Trump said was to discuss the world going "crazy."[18] Trump said the two leaders had "really great success working together on defense and offense" during his first administration, cited their "great relationship" and called the people of France "spectacular," describing the visit on Truth Social as "A GREAT HONOR."[18] Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] joined the meeting, greeting Trump with a handshake.[18] The visit coincided with the reopening of [[notre-dame-cathedral|Notre Dame Cathedral]], which Trump attended along with First Lady [[jill-biden|Jill Biden]], President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] having cited a scheduling conflict.[18]

## May 2024 remarks on sending troops to Ukraine

In early May 2024, Macron again raised the possibility of Western "boots on the ground" in Ukraine, months after first controversially saying the option could not be ruled out.[19] In an interview with *The Economist*, he said the question of injecting NATO troops into the conflict would "legitimately" arise if Russia broke through the front lines and if the Zelensky government specifically requested it, adding that "if Russia decided to go further, we will in any case all have to ask ourselves this question."[19] He characterised his earlier remarks to NATO-member defence ministers in Paris as a "strategic wake-up call for my counterparts."[19]

Days earlier, Macron had declared in a speech that Europe is "mortal" and could "die" if Russia won in Ukraine and continued on an expansionist path, an assumption that a number of analysts rejected as a description of Moscow's actual aims.[19] Speaking to *The Economist*, he further called Russia "a power of regional destabilization" and "a threat to Europeans' security," stating: "I have a clear strategic objective: Russia cannot win in Ukraine. If Russia wins in Ukraine, there will be no security in Europe."[19]



## French recognition of Palestine

At a summit organized by France and Saudi Arabia in New York ahead of the [[united-nations-general-assembly|United Nations General Assembly]] on September 22, 2025, Macron declared: "We must pave the way for peace… Today, France recognizes the state of Palestine."[20] The statement drew applause from attendees and a standing ovation from the Palestinian delegation.[20] Macron noted recent statements of recognition, some not yet formalized, by Britain, Canada, Australia, Portugal, Belgium and several others, in a diplomatic push over the opposition of Israel and the United States as the Gaza war neared its two-year mark.[20]

## Ukraine peace talks phone call

On Monday, December 1, 2025, Macron and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz held a phone call with Vladimir Zelensky and several other European leaders, including Finnish President Alexander Stubb and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, a day before Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner traveled to Moscow for talks with Vladimir Putin.[21] According to a transcript obtained by Der Spiegel, Macron said there was 'a possibility that the US will betray Ukraine on the issue of territory without clarity regarding security guarantees,' and warned that continued peace talks could pose a 'great danger' for Zelensky personally.[21]

## Recognition of a Palestinian state (July 2025)

Speaking at the [[2025-un-two-state-solution-conference|United Nations conference on the two-state solution]] on July 28, 2025, Saudi Foreign Minister Prince [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] welcomed Macron's stated intention to formally recognize a state of Palestine in September.[22] The French position was cited in Badlands commentary as an example of European governments moving toward Palestinian statehood in response to sustained Arab diplomatic and financial engagement.[22]

## June 2024 European elections

Badlands commentary named Macron, alongside Germany's [[olaf-scholz|Olaf Scholz]], as the leader most clearly rattled by the [[2024-european-parliament-elections|June 2024 European Parliament elections]], noting that both were soundly rejected by double-digit deficits amid record turnout and a strong showing by right and centre-right parties.[23] The result was described as ushering in a political earthquake in France, even though the vote did not directly alter national leadership in any member state.[23]

## Placement among sinking incumbents, June 2024

June 2024 coverage of [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]]'s by-election defeat placed France's Emmanuel Macron among the sinking post-pandemic incumbents, describing him as scrambling for survival.[24]

## Response to the Durov arrest, August 2024

After French authorities detained [[telegram|Telegram]] founder [[pavel-durov|Pavel Durov]] over the weekend of August 24–25, 2024, Macron came under sustained international criticism, with figures including [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] describing the case as selective persecution of a free-speech advocate.[25] In a rare direct intervention on Monday, August 26, Macron said: "The arrest of Telegram's president on French territory took place as part of an ongoing judicial investigation. This is in no way a political decision. It is up to the judges to decide," and condemned what he called "false information" circulating about the case.[25] The claim that the [[arrest-of-pavel-durov|arrest]] was not politically motivated was widely mocked, and ZeroHedge reported that Macron appeared to be feeling the pressure as the prosecutor's office indicated the detention might run until August 28.[25]
