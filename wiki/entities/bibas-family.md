# Bibas Family

The Bibas family — brothers Ariel and Kfir Bibas and their mother Shiri Silberman Bibas — became among the most prominent symbols of the Israeli hostage crisis following the October 7, 2023 attacks, and the disposition of their remains became a flashpoint in the 2025 hostage-ceasefire arrangement between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]].[1]

## Return of remains, February 2025

On Thursday, February 20, 2025, Hamas returned a set of remains to Israel. Specialists at the Abu Kabir Forensic Institute identified the bodies of the brothers Ariel and Kfir Bibas alongside those of slain hostage [[oded-lifshitz|Oded Lifshitz]], but the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] announced early the following morning that a fourth body sent by the group was not that of the boys' mother, Shiri Bibas, and could not be identified as any other hostage either.[1]

The transfer had been carried out in what Israeli reporting described as a propaganda-filled ceremony, and the return of the remains had already sparked anger and mourning across Israel before the identification failure was disclosed.[1] Hostages' families demanded the release of all captives still held in Gaza, alive and dead, and Israeli leaders vowed to destroy Hamas.[1] The IDF called the discrepancy "a very serious violation by the Hamas terrorist organization, which is required by the agreement to return four dead hostages," and demanded that Hamas "return Shiri home, along with all of our hostages."[1] The episode left the future of the ongoing hostage-ceasefire deal looking more uncertain than ever.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] situated the Bibas case within a broader skepticism of Israeli government hostage disclosures, noting that after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2024 election victory the Israeli government began acknowledging that many hostages had in fact been killed on October 7 — most notably [[omer-neutra|Omer Neutra]] — without citing new intelligence or recovered bodies, and questioning the logistics of Hamas maintaining possession of cadavers for eighteen months in a territory Trump had described as a "demolition site."[1]
