# Yuval Noah Harari

Yuval Noah Harari — rendered "Noah Yuval Harari" in the source article — is a historian and author frequently associated with globalist technocratic commentary on [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]].[1]

Harari is cited for the claim that 2024 would be the last human election, and that the capabilities of advanced technology would produce such substantial [[reality-collapse|reality collapse]] that political leaders would be selected by machines.[1] Badlands Media calls this a silly prediction, on the grounds that political leaders are already selected by machines, and treats the revelation of AI's election use cases as a further indictment of everyone who peddled the claim that 2020 produced the safest and most secure elections in history.[1]

Harari is also listed among the signatories of the [[ai-pause-open-letter|open letter of March 22, 2023]] calling on all AI labs to pause for at least six months the training of systems more powerful than GPT-4, alongside [[tristan-harris|Tristan Harris]], [[aza-raskin|Aza Raskin]], and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[1]

He falls within the group the article characterizes as sounding the alarm while denying the existence of a sovereign Creator and of a spiritual realm, holding climate-alarmist views that regard humans as a cancer on the planet and as merely another animal species, and aiming ultimately at saving the planet rather than humanity — a profile the author says warrants taking their directed hysteria with a grain of salt.[1]

## Bitcoin skepticism

In May 2024, Harari publicly expressed skepticism about [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]], describing it as "a currency of distrust."[2] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the remarks as a case of "narrative convergence," arguing that Harari — characterized as a WEF-aligned globalist figure among the "tapwater goblin bankers" — had inadvertently made the strongest available case for decentralizing the financial sector.[2] In Burning Bright's summary, Harari identified three threats to the system: decentralization and transparency, distrust of the government and banking apparatus, and digital gold; the commentary invited readers to "play the inversion game" and ask whether he was wrong on any count, provided one rejects his premise that he and his class are a net benefit to society.[2]

## Ehret's critique (2025)

In a November 2025 essay, [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] treated Harari as the leading "expert" transhumanist promoting the view that technological growth itself creates [[useless-eaters|"useless eaters"]].[3] Ehret quoted Harari as saying that with enough data and computing power "you can understand people better than they understand themselves, and then you can manipulate them in ways that were previously impossible", that democracy must be reinvented for an era in which "humans are now hackable animals", and that "the whole idea that humans have this 'soul' or 'spirit' and have free will… that's over."[3]

As a leading figure of the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] Great Narrative Project, Harari argued that the Bible offers no answer for what to do when humans are no longer useful to the economy, and that completely new ideologies and religions are likely to emerge from Silicon Valley — offering happiness, justice and even eternal life "here on earth, with the help of technology".[3] Ehret concluded that, having replaced God with Silicon Valley technocrats, Harari is being sold as a "Moses" of the new post-human age his masters wish to usher in.[3]

Harari also holds that humans have only two basic abilities, physical and cognitive, and that "if AI becomes better than us in that, there is no third field humans can move to".[3] He describes an emergent "high caste" that "won't exploit the poor. They just won't need them", adding that "it's much more difficult to rebel against irrelevance than against exploitation."[3] Asked what should be done with the resulting population, Harari said the problem is boredom and meaninglessness and that his "best guess at present, is a combination of drugs and computer games" — an answer Ehret traces to [[aldous-huxley|Aldous Huxley]]'s 1962 "Ultimate Revolution" lecture at Berkeley.[3]

Ehret situates Harari's outlook in the [[cybernetics|cybernetics]] lineage of Marshall McLuhan, [[julian-huxley|Julian Huxley]], [[norbert-wiener|Norbert Wiener]], [[pierre-teilhard-de-chardin|Pierre Teilhard de Chardin]] and [[ray-kurzweil|Ray Kurzweil]], and notes that Harari always excludes himself from the category of purposeless, hackable humans.[3] He contrasts Harari's unipolar vision of stasis and an end to history with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s St. Petersburg address on a technology-based economy and [[multipolar-world-order|multipolarity]].[3]

## In "Soul Governance"

The Badlands Media essay [[soul-governance|Soul Governance]] names Yuval Harari the "high priest of nihilism" of what it calls the digital grid, a religious order whose Vatican is the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]].[4] The essay treats his best-known assertions — that humans are hackable animals and that the soul is obsolete — as doctrinal claims aimed at sovereignty rather than at religion, arguing that a person persuaded his soul is obsolete ceases to be a threat to the system.[4]

## "Surveillance under the skin"

Badlands Media describes Harari as an adviser to [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] founder [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]] and credits him, among others, with popularizing the idea of taking "surveillance under the skin."[5] The article argues this is not an idle notion: there has been extensive discussion at WEF gatherings in Davos about implementing technology that harvests biometric data, with [[smart-clothing|smart clothing]] serving as a stepping stone — a bridge between the smartphone and an eventual push for a digital ID chip literally under the skin.[5] Harari is also named alongside [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]] and Schwab as an inheritor of the [[technocracy|technocratic]] tradition developed by [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]].[5]
