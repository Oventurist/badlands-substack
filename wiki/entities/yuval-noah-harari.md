# Yuval Noah Harari

Yuval Noah Harari — rendered "Noah Yuval Harari" in the source article — is a historian and author frequently associated with globalist technocratic commentary on [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]].[1]

Harari is cited for the claim that 2024 would be the last human election, and that the capabilities of advanced technology would produce such substantial [[reality-collapse|reality collapse]] that political leaders would be selected by machines.[1] Badlands Media calls this a silly prediction, on the grounds that political leaders are already selected by machines, and treats the revelation of AI's election use cases as a further indictment of everyone who peddled the claim that 2020 produced the safest and most secure elections in history.[1]

Harari is also listed among the signatories of the [[ai-pause-open-letter|open letter of March 22, 2023]] calling on all AI labs to pause for at least six months the training of systems more powerful than GPT-4, alongside [[tristan-harris|Tristan Harris]], [[aza-raskin|Aza Raskin]], and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[1]

He falls within the group the article characterizes as sounding the alarm while denying the existence of a sovereign Creator and of a spiritual realm, holding climate-alarmist views that regard humans as a cancer on the planet and as merely another animal species, and aiming ultimately at saving the planet rather than humanity — a profile the author says warrants taking their directed hysteria with a grain of salt.[1]

## Bitcoin skepticism

In May 2024, Harari publicly expressed skepticism about [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]], describing it as "a currency of distrust."[2] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the remarks as a case of "narrative convergence," arguing that Harari — characterized as a WEF-aligned globalist figure among the "tapwater goblin bankers" — had inadvertently made the strongest available case for decentralizing the financial sector.[2] In Burning Bright's summary, Harari identified three threats to the system: decentralization and transparency, distrust of the government and banking apparatus, and digital gold; the commentary invited readers to "play the inversion game" and ask whether he was wrong on any count, provided one rejects his premise that he and his class are a net benefit to society.[2]
