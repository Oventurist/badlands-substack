# Future Forward

Future Forward is the largest pro-Biden Democratic super PAC of the 2024 election cycle.[1]

## 2024 donor revolt

In July 2024, following [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s widely criticized debate performance, *The New York Times* reported that some major Democratic donors had told Future Forward that roughly $90 million in pledged donations was on hold for as long as Biden remained atop the ticket, according to two people briefed on the conversations.[1] Aggregators characterized the freeze as a "coordinated rebellion" against the president's continued candidacy.[1]

The freeze followed further public stumbles in which Biden referred to Kamala Harris as "Vice President Trump" and introduced Volodymyr Zelensky as "President Putin," with leftist media figures publicly disputing the extent of the president's cognitive decline.[1]

## Backing Kamala Harris

After Biden left the race, Future Forward became the main outside spending group backing [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s presidential bid, raising hundreds of millions of dollars ahead of the November 5, 2024 election.[2] The super PAC built a large polling and message-testing operation, creating and testing roughly 500 digital and television advertisements for Biden and some 200 for Harris and speaking with about 375,000 Americans in the weeks after Harris became the presumptive nominee on July 22, 2024.[2]

Speaking on August 19, 2024 at an event hosted by the University of Chicago Institute of Politics, group president [[chauncey-mclean|Chauncey McLean]] said the PAC's internal numbers were "much less rosy than what you're seeing in the public" and warned that Democrats faced much closer races in key states than public polling implied.[2] McLean said Future Forward had at least $250 million left to spend on advertising between Labor Day on September 2 and Election Day.[2] Badlands commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the remarks as super PAC leaders hedging on an unviable candidate and preparing to redirect donor money toward down-ballot races.[2]
