# Bernie Houghton

Bernard "Bernie" Houghton was a Texan with high-level ties to US and Australian intelligence who served as the [[nugan-hand-bank]]'s representative in Saudi Arabia.[1] The New York Times reported in 1982 that Houghton, then 62, and co-founder [[michael-jon-hand]] both had backgrounds in American intelligence before arriving in Sydney in 1967.[1]

Before his banking role Houghton was a barkeep, owning several bars in Australia that became popular hangouts for American soldiers on "R&R" leave during the Vietnam War. Those soldiers spent roughly $9 million a month, much of which Houghton collected, and Joe Lange writes that the same vice bars were where many US servicemen could obtain the heroin that became a widespread epidemic during the war.[1]

## Saudi Arabian operations

Almost 40 pages of a 240-page Australian police report were devoted to Houghton. It stated that when he ran the Saudi Arabian office the bank transferred millions of dollars out of Saudi Arabia, and that the money has yet to be traced.[1] WikiSpooks describes the bank's most brazen fraud as possibly the theft of at least $5 million — maybe more than $10 million — from American civilian and military personnel in Saudi Arabia, with Houghton the man in charge of those operations.[1]

The mechanism, as described in Joel Bainerman's "The Crimes Of A President," was to send "investment counselors" to installations where Americans worked in Saudi Arabia and persuade them to invest their salaries in secured government bonds through the bank's Hong Kong branch. None of the money was recovered.[1]

Lange concludes that Houghton both stole from American civilians and soldiers and profited from the heroin addiction he helped supply, yet faced no consequences because he was an "untouchable" — while the soldiers were expendable.[1]
