# Marion King Hubbert

Marion King Hubbert was a geologist, the originator of Peak Oil Theory, and — in the account of Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] — the principal author of the 1934 Technocracy Study Course, the founding manifesto of [[technocracy-inc|Technocracy Inc.]].[1] Ehret also calls him a godfather of modern ecology.[1]

## The Technocracy Study Course

Published in 1934 amid a Great Depression in which unemployment approached 30 per cent across North America, the Study Course functioned, in Ehret's words, as an immutable bible for technocrats for decades afterwards.[1] It explicitly banned moral considerations from the organization of human society, arguing that because large numbers of people under the same environmental circumstances behave in the same way, this "basic fact shows the futility of all moralistic approaches to the solution of social problems."[1]

Following the materialism of the radical Darwinians around Thomas Huxley and the behaviourism of B. F. Skinner, the manifesto denied the existence of mind or soul, stating that "the most minute anatomical dissection had never revealed anything that corresponded to a 'mind' or a 'conscience' or a 'will'," and that when such concepts are tested against objectively observable phenomena "they rapidly fade out of existence. When we observe a human being we merely perceive an object which makes a certain variety of motions and noises."[1] It further defined the human being as "an engine taking potential energy in the form of chemical combinations contained in food, and converting this potential energy into heat, work and body tissue," governed by thermodynamic processes "in no essential particular different from the corresponding processes in man-made engines."[1]

Ehret argues that this reduction of humanity to an automaton placed the movement in the same worldview as the eugenics-minded [[nikola-tesla|Nikola Tesla]], and that the technocratic priesthood's absolute submission to the law of entropy exposed its promises of abundance as empty rhetoric.[1]

## Peak Oil

Ehret notes that although since discredited, Peak Oil Theory became a foundational precept of the post-war growth of ecologism.[1] He cites the geologist Daniel Yergin, who wrote in *The Prize: The Epic Quest for Oil, Money & Power* that "the peak oil theory embodies an 'end of technology/end of opportunity' perspective, that there will be no more significant innovation in oil production, nor significant new resources that can be developed."[1]
