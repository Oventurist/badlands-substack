# Mujahideen

The Mujahideen were the Afghan fighters built up by the [[cia|CIA]] to fight the Soviets in the twilight of the Cold War, and are described by Badlands Media as the precursor to [[al-qaeda|al-Qaeda]].[1]

The article uses the Mujahideen as the founding example of a newer era of warfare in which localized proxy groups are used instead of sending U.S. troops directly — a pattern it says characterizes most conflicts since World War II.[1] That lineage runs forward through CIA and [[pentagon|Pentagon]] sponsorship of Syrian proxy groups, including [[timber-sycamore|Timber Sycamore]], to the rise of [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] and the December 2024 ouster of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]].[1]

## Operation Cyclone

The Mujahideen were the fierce Afghan guerrilla fighters who resisted the Soviet occupation, predating [[isis]], the [[taliban]], and even popular use of the term "Al Qaeda."2 The word, plural of *mujahid* or "doers of jihād," was never the official name of any particular group but became a Western media catch-all for the various militant factions opposing the Soviets.2

Though represented in the West as a single cohesive force, hardcore ideological lines separated the factions.2 Of the seven mujahideen groups supported by the Pakistani government, four espoused Islamic fundamentalist beliefs, and it was these fundamentalists who received most of the funding, weapons and training — the factions that would one day form the Taliban.2

Under [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]] the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] funneled roughly $3 billion to the Mujahideen between 1979 and 1992, routed through Pakistan's [[inter-services-intelligence|ISI]] and matched by [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], with weapons including at least 2,300 [[fim-92-stinger|FIM-92 Stinger]] missiles.2 Israeli military instructors also trained thousands of Mujahideen fighters and sold them weapons seized from the Soviets during the Yom Kippur War.2 [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] met with Mujahideen representatives in the Oval Office on multiple occasions, providing photo opportunities used to justify continued U.S. support.2
