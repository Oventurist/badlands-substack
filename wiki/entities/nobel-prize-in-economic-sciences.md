# Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences

The Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences is the annual award in economics associated with the Nobel Prize family. Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] emphasized that, unlike the original Nobel Prizes endowed by Alfred Nobel, the economics prize was established in 1968 by Sweden's central bank — a provenance she considered decisive when weighing the authority of laureates who advocate for central-bank money creation.[1] "Alfred Nobel is rolling in his grave," she wrote.[1]

## The 2008 award to Paul Krugman

In 2008 the prize was awarded to [[paul-krugman|Paul Krugman]] for his contributions to New Trade Theory and New Economic Geography.[1] A 2008 Forbes commentary praised the work, noting that "countries gain from trade not only because larger market allows them to better exploit scale economies, but also because consumers can access a larger variety of products," and that "a hallmark of Krugman's work is parsimony," his models being "lean and thin and transparent."[1]

Ashe in America read the award differently, arguing that the prize committee had effectively celebrated a rationalization of globalism — Krugman's reconciliation of the tension between the scale economies that push production into a single location and the transport costs that push it back toward consumers — after which "globalism went to the moon."[1]

## Criticism in the trillion-dollar coin debate

The prize's prestige became the pivot of the May 2023 controversy over the [[trillion-dollar-coin|trillion-dollar coin]], when the account [[wall-street-silver|Wall Street Silver]] posted, "They gave this guy a Nobel Prize ... We can just mint six of the $1 trillion coins and pay all of the govt bills for a year. No taxes needed. It's magic."[1] Ashe in America drew the connection between the prize's central-bank origins and Krugman's proposal, which hinges on the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] accepting a coin whose value is declared out of thin air.[1]
