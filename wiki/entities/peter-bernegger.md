# Peter Bernegger

**Peter Bernegger** is an election-integrity researcher whose analysis of online political fundraising has circulated widely in the independent-media ecosystem.[1]

## ActBlue statutory analysis

The June 12, 2026 [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] credited Bernegger with compiling the list of federal statutes potentially violated by the fundraising practices alleged against [[actblue|ActBlue]].[1] The catalogue cited by contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] included 18 U.S.C. § 1956 (money laundering), § 1957 (monetary transactions in property derived from unlawful activity), § 1001 (false statements to federal agencies), § 1343 (wire fraud), § 1341 (mail fraud), § 371 (conspiracy to defraud the United States), § 1028 (identity theft and document fraud), and § 1030 (Computer Fraud and Abuse Act), together with 52 U.S.C. § 30122 (contributions in the name of another, under FECA), the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act at 15 U.S.C. §§ 6801–6809, and the Bank Secrecy Act at 31 U.S.C. § 5311 et seq.[1]

Ashe in America argued that state analogues of these charges could also apply, ostensibly on a per-transaction basis, describing the totality as "a portfolio of crime."[1]

## Wisconsin ERIC litigation

In early January 2026 Bernegger reported a "huge win in court" after the [[wisconsin-elections-commission|Wisconsin Elections Commission]]'s motion to dismiss was denied in Waukesha County Case Number 2024CV001544, *[[janel-brandtjen|Janel Brandtjen]] v. Wisconsin Elections Commission, [[meagan-wolfe|Meagan Wolfe]]*, sending the case to full discovery.[2] He indicated that the plaintiffs planned to depose [[david-becker|David Becker]] of the [[electronic-registration-information-center|ERIC]] states.[2] Bernegger alleged that "the Election Commission and ERIC are unlawfully giving away voters['] personally identifiable information to CEIR (Becker's other company)," comparing the practice to a bank disclosing a customer's Social Security number, birthdate and driver's license number to a nonprofit without notice, and calling it criminal for residents of every ERIC state.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] said she had shared with Bernegger sworn trial transcripts in which a Colorado deputy secretary of state acknowledged that ERIC forwards non-citizens to states as "eligible but unauthorized" voters, describing the material as "an ERIC customer admitting knowledge of the 'potential' padding."[2]

## Ossoff campaign finance claims (2025)

On Monday, February 24, 2025 Bernegger posted on X that "US Senator @SenOssoff caught Smurfing," adding that "a quick data run on his campaign…doesn't show all of the laundering."[3] His records indicated 20 people made 7,380 contributions totalling $197,223, drawn from the top 20 donors via [[actblue|ActBlue]] alone.[3] Badlands writers, citing Bernegger's repeated appearances on [[why-we-vote|Why We Vote]], described [[smurfing]] as the laundering of money through stolen identities and characterized the pattern as a massive scandal that institutions had ignored.[3]

## Smurfing findings on WinRed and Ted Cruz

Described in June 2025 as one of the primary investigators of donation smurfing and a frequent guest on the program Why We Vote, Bernegger — based in Wisconsin — asserted that Republican Senator Ted Cruz is the number one recipient of smurfed donations in the nation.[4] His findings were cited by Badlands commentators arguing that the House and Justice Department probes into ActBlue should be matched by equivalent scrutiny of WinRed.[4]
