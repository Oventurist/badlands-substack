# Kremlin

The Kremlin is the seat of the Russian presidency and, by metonymy, the executive apparatus surrounding President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]. In Badlands coverage it appears principally as the institutional voice that confirms or denies Western reporting about Russian intentions in the war in Ukraine.[1]

## January 2024 Denial of Peace-Talk Reports

By late January 2024, with the war in Ukraine approaching its two-year mark, international press chatter grew that Moscow was looking to strike a peace deal with Ukraine and the West.[1] The New York Times had reported in December 2023 that Putin was "quietly signaling" to the West that he wanted a ceasefire, and Bloomberg revived the storyline on January 25, 2024, citing two anonymous sources described as close to the Kremlin who said Putin's officials had used diplomatic back-channels to "put out feelers" to senior Biden administration officials in December.[1]

On Friday, January 26, the Kremlin issued a firm denial of these reports. Addressing the Bloomberg article specifically, presidential spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] told a press briefing: "That's false information, it's completely untrue."[1]

## Badlands Interpretation

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the episode as evidence of "obfuscation and narrative reframing" ahead of what he termed the Sovereign Alliance endgame, arguing that the Media Industrial Complex's peace-feeler stories contradicted two years of observable reality in which Russia withstood sanctions and threats from the Globalist West.[1] In his framing Putin "isn't begging western bureaucrats for peace," and the Establishment's shifting of the Overton window on the Ukraine-Russia conflict amounted to a retreat along previously held narrative ground.[1]

## "Criminal gang" remark on EU asset seizure (October 2025)

In early October 2025, Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] said [[european-union|EU]] countries debating ways they could "steal" Russian assets to prop up Ukraine were behaving like a criminal "gang," likening the bloc to a group of mobsters planning their next heist.[2] The remark came after EU leaders met in Copenhagen to discuss a €140 billion ($164 billion) loan to Ukraine backed by Russia's immobilized central bank assets, most of which sit at [[euroclear|Euroclear]] in Belgium.[2]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry called it an "[i]nteresting choice of words from the Kremlin," extending the mafia metaphor into a claim that an organized crime syndicate hostile to God and Christianity controls Western civilization through "usury and tribalism," and concluding that "God wins" but "the journey is the destination."[2]
