# 101st Airborne Division

The 101st Airborne Division is a United States Army division headquartered at Fort Campbell, Kentucky, whose 2024 rotation to the Middle East became the subject of viral claims of escalation.[1] The division is described in Badlands commentary as one of America's most combat-ready, "always-first-to-go-in" military units, a tip-of-the-spear formation lionized in history books and in the miniseries "Band of Brothers."[2]

## 2022 deployment near the Ukrainian border

In October 2022 the [[white-house|Biden White House]] positioned the 101st Airborne Division on the border of the territories newly annexed by the [[russia|Russian Federation]] in [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[2] Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[brian-cates|Brian Cates]] argued that deploying such a unit to [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s doorstep was a not-so-subtle message to Moscow that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] would commit ground troops to the escalating conflict, and a dramatic escalation between the US/[[nato|NATO]] bloc and Russia.[2] He treated the move as evidence that a president who had refused to set any limit on the billions of taxpayer dollars or the quantity of weaponry sent to Ukraine was preparing to ship "America's sons and daughters" to the war theater as well.[2] The episode is treated at length in [[us-ground-war-over-ukraine-debate|the debate over a US ground war over Ukraine]].[2]

## 2024 Middle East rotation

In September 2024, posts circulating on X presented the movement of the division's "Wings of Destiny" brigade as evidence of an emergency escalation tied to the fighting between [[israel|Israel]] and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[1] The Army had in fact announced the deployment nearly two weeks earlier, describing it on its official website as part of a "regular rotation of forces" sending the unit from Fort Campbell to various parts of the Middle East.[1] The brigade was replacing the 10th Mountain Division's Combat Aviation Brigade, a fact corroborated by military trade journalists and by local media in the Fort Campbell area.[1]

## Badlands Media analysis

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the rotation as a case study in media distortion, arguing that neither the division's movement nor the concurrent sailing of a carrier group from Norfolk represented escalation by the [[pentagon|Pentagon]].[1] He tied the point to claims by Colonel [[lawrence-wilkerson|Lawrence Wilkerson]], in an interview with Judge [[andrew-napolitano|Andrew Napolitano]], that combatant commanders — specifically CENTCOM's General [[michael-kurilla|Michael Kurilla]] — had usurped the authority of the [[white-house|White House]] and [[state-department|State Department]] in conducting US foreign policy, and said the routine rotations did nothing to contradict that account.[1]
