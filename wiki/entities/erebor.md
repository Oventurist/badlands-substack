# Erebor

Erebor is a proposed United States bank being organized by a group of technology billionaires to serve start-ups and cryptocurrency businesses, filling the market gap left by the collapse of [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]].[1] Reporting by the Financial Times, summarized in the Badlands News Brief of July 3, 2025, described the venture as a digital bank purpose-built for crypto and technology companies.[1]

## Founding and backers

The effort is led by [[palmer-luckey|Palmer Luckey]], co-founder of the military contractor [[anduril|Anduril]].[1] Backers reportedly include [[joe-lonsdale|Joe Lonsdale]], founder of the venture capital firm [[8vc|8VC]] and a co-founder of [[palantir|Palantir]], the defence-oriented data company associated with [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]].[1] Thiel's venture capital vehicle [[founders-fund|Founders Fund]] would also be among the investors, according to people close to the matter cited by the Financial Times.[1]

## Purpose

Erebor is intended to serve the start-up ecosystem — including cryptocurrency firms that have struggled to obtain conventional banking relationships — after the 2023 failure of Silicon Valley Bank removed the sector's principal lender.[1] Badlands contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] highlighted the launch as a significant development, noting that PayPal co-founder Peter Thiel and Anduril founder Palmer Luckey were behind a digital bank built for crypto and technology start-ups.[1]

## Naming

Like Anduril and Palantir, the name Erebor is drawn from J. R. R. Tolkien's *The Lord of the Rings*. Erebor is the "lonely mountain" whose treasures are reclaimed from the dragon Smaug, a reference consistent with the Tolkien-derived branding favoured across the Thiel-adjacent defence and technology network.[2]

## Reception among independent commentators

Coverage of the bank's formation in the Badlands News Brief of July 2, 2025 emphasised the concentration of defence-technology capital behind a new financial institution.[2] Badlands contributor Chris Paul framed the venture as vindication of a long-standing argument, writing that it was "what I have been saying since 2022" while other commentators focused on electing Republicans and supporting what he characterised as the regime's proxy state in the Middle East.[2]
