# Alexander Galt

Sir Alexander Galt was a Father of Canadian Confederation who articulated the British policy rationale for westward expansion across British North America.[1]

On 22 May 1867, weeks before the [[british-north-america-act|British North America Act]] took effect, Galt stated: "If the United States desire to outflank us on the west, we must accept the situation and lay our hand on British Columbia and the Pacific Ocean. This country cannot be surrounded by the Unites States. We are gone if we allow it… 'From the Atlantic to the Pacific' must be the cry in British America as much as it has ever been in the United States."[1] Matthew Ehret reads the statement as an explicit declaration that Confederation's westward drive was intended to block the emerging rail and telegraph connection between the United States and Russia through [[british-columbia|British Columbia]] and Alaska.[1]
