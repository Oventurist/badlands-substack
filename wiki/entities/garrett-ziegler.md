# Garrett Ziegler

Garrett Ziegler is a former Trump White House aide who operates [[marco-polo|Marco Polo]], the nonprofit research organization that published the contents of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s abandoned laptop.[1]

## Lawsuit by Hunter Biden

In a filing entered on the night of Wednesday, September 13, 2023, Hunter Biden's legal team sued Ziegler in the Central District of California.[1] The thirteen-page complaint alleged that Ziegler and unnamed co-defendants violated federal and California privacy laws by "accessing, tampering with, manipulating, altering, copying and damaging computer data" taken from Hunter Biden's purported laptop and iPhone cloud storage without consent.[1] It further alleged that the defendants obtained sensitive materials by hacking encrypted data on the devices, uploaded them to Ziegler's website where they remain public, and refused requests to cease the activity and return private data belonging to the president's son.[1]

Ziegler responded to CBS News that neither he nor Marco Polo had been served, and said the complaint "should embarrass Winston & Strawn LLP" and was "not worth the paper it's written on."[1] He argued that numerous state and federal laws and regulations protect authors and publishers like Marco Polo, and noted that the suit was filed one day after the [[biden-impeachment-inquiry|impeachment inquiry]] into [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was announced, calling it a SLAPP action and describing the president's son as "a disgrace to our great nation."[1]

## Context

The material Ziegler published derives from the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]], the same device that fifty-one former intelligence officials characterized as having "all the classic earmarks of a Russian information operation," and which Antony Blinken — then a senior Biden campaign official — was reported to have played a central role in discrediting after the New York Post's reporting.[1] Badlands coverage frames the laptop as containing evidence relevant to the Biden family's business dealings alongside private photographs, emails and text messages.[1]
