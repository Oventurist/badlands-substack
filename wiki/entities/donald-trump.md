# Donald Trump

Donald Trump is the President of the United States and the central figure of the Badlands corpus, which reads his second term as the execution of a long-planned strategy to detach American interests from globalist institutions and restore [[pax-americana|Pax Americana]].[1]

## Ultimatum to Iran (March 2026)

On March 22, 2026 Trump threatened to strike [[iran|Iran]]'s power plants unless Tehran fully reopened the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] within 48 hours, escalating a conflict that had begun on February 28 and entered its fourth week by Monday, March 23.[1] Reuters reported that Iran's [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Revolutionary Guards]] responded that the strait would be "completely" closed if the United States followed through, and that Iranian officials warned energy infrastructure in countries hosting U.S. military bases would become legitimate targets if Iranian power plants were attacked.[1] The exchange coincided with Iranian ballistic missile strikes on Arad and Dimona in [[israel|Israel]] on March 22 that injured at least 31 people in Arad and five in Dimona, and with Brent crude rising to $112.19 a barrel.[1]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the episode as [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]] in action, arguing that Trump's seizure of the U.S. military reins from 2017 through 2026 — consistent with Jon Herold's Devolution research — neutered the Military Industrial Complex that had coerced the Sovereign Alliance under every administration from Bush Sr. through Obama, and that Trump is deliberately allowing chaos to expose the Western protectorate as "a paper tiger built on managed decline, narrative control and coerced collectivism."[1]

## The Ayatollah story

The New York Post reported that Trump had been briefed that Iran's new supreme leader [[mojtaba-khamenei|Mojtaba Khamenei]] — son of the late [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]] — was gay, prompting White House journalists to question him about it in the Oval Office.[1] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Trump's reply, "Well, nobody knows…", as delivered while visibly holding back laughter, comparing it to Trump's earlier announcement that he had approved *covert* operations in Venezuela — a contradiction the press failed to notice.[1] An IRGC video released the same weekend showed spokesman [[ebrahim-zolfaghari|Ebrahim Zolfaghari]] mocking Trump's signature phrases "You're fired" and "Thank you for your attention to this matter."[1]

## Meeting with the Japanese Prime Minister

Trump drew criticism for invoking [[japan|Japan]]'s role in the 1941 attack on [[pearl-harbor|Pearl Harbor]] during a White House meeting with Prime Minister [[sanae-takaichi|Sanae Takaichi]], using the reference to defend his administration's decision not to notify allies ahead of U.S. military action against Iran.[1] Despite the uneasy reaction among Japanese officials, the White House released a fact sheet emphasizing strengthened defense, energy and economic cooperation, and Trump announced Japanese investment of $40 billion in small modular nuclear reactor plants in Tennessee and Alabama and $33 billion in natural gas generation in Pennsylvania and Texas, on top of the earlier [[us-japan-strategic-trade-and-investment-agreement|2025 U.S.-Japan Strategic Trade and Investment Agreement]] commitments.[1] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry judged the press treatment crass, noting Takaichi's affectionate greeting and Trump's off-color humor, and called him "an absolute Legend."[1]

## The McCarthy endorsement and the 2023 debt ceiling

In a June 2023 Badlands essay, contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] used Trump's endorsement of [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] for Speaker of the House as a case study in the limits of confident interpretation, noting that Trump had simultaneously endorsed both McCarthy and the America First congressmen opposing him.[3] Carlson initially read the outcome — McCarthy's confirmation followed by the placement of his opponents on key committees — as evidence that Trump held leverage over the new Speaker, and pointed to the flurry of classified-document discoveries, train derailments, Chinese spy balloons and the Nord Stream sabotage revelations as signs the [[deep-state|Deep State]] had entered "full-on panic mode."[3] The [[2023-debt-ceiling-agreement|2023 debt ceiling agreement]], which raised the limit with virtually no spending cuts, forced a reassessment: Carlson asked whether Trump had endorsed McCarthy precisely so that he would be ousted, exposing the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] in the process.[3] He also observed that as of the morning before Sean Hannity's town hall, Trump had not publicly mentioned the debt ceiling deal at all, spending his commentary instead on light mockery of [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]].[3]

## Election administration order

A federal appeals court blocked key portions of Trump's executive order on election administration in 23 Democrat-led states, ruling the administration cannot enforce several provisions while litigation continues.[5] The order sought to impose new federal election requirements including proof-of-citizenship measures and restrictions affecting [[mail-in-voting|mail voting]], directing the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] to compile lists of confirmed citizens eligible to vote, the [[united-states-postal-service|US Postal Service]] to set new standards for mail-in ballots, and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] to prioritize investigations of state and local election officials issuing federal ballots to ineligible people.[5] The decision preserved Judge [[indira-talwani|Talwani]]'s injunction for the plaintiff states and set up a likely [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] showdown over the scope of presidential authority in regulating federal elections.[5] Badlands commentary held that the dispute is about presidential authorities, that such battles have largely been going Trump's way, and that this one should as well — asking who is constitutionally authorized to exercise the Postal Service's decision rights if neither the states nor the President may.[5]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that Trump is "very clearly pinning a good degree of his futurism goals" on the perpetuation and acceleration of AI, a fact he said much of the [[truth-community|Truth Community]] misses amid anxiety about the industry's leading personalities.[5] Trump's Commerce Secretary [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]] reportedly said the administration aims to "protect open source AI," and former AI and Crypto Czar [[david-sacks|David Sacks]] argued that [[openai|OpenAI]] and [[anthropic|Anthropic]] seek regulation to stave off competitors.[5] Burning Bright further argued that [[china|China]]'s timed releases of open-weight models grant Trump the narrative shielding required to resist the regulation push, closing with the remark that it is a "good thing Trump and [[xi-jinping|Xi]] are Sovereign Bros."[5]

## Israel and Turkey

Trump praised Turkey as a "tremendous ally" while publicly criticizing Israeli military operations during a meeting with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], underscoring visible policy differences even as the US-Israel alliance remained intact.[5] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read this as a repeat of a September 2025 pattern in which Trump invited [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] to the White House the day before Netanyahu addressed the UN General Assembly, and argued that Trump's plan is not kinetic war against Israel but the destruction of Israel's public reputation "by feeding into its greatest indulgences and allowing it the space to show the world what it truly is."[5]

## Cartels and Mexico

Media coverage increasingly portrayed the administration's intensified campaign against Mexican drug cartels — expanded intelligence operations, increased surveillance, and the designation of several cartels as foreign terrorist organizations — as laying groundwork for possible US military action inside [[mexico|Mexico]].[5] The administration maintained its focus is dismantling transnational criminal organizations responsible for fentanyl trafficking, human smuggling and violence, emphasizing cooperation with Mexico while declining to rule out further measures against cartel leadership.[5] GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted the administration had indicted ten Mexican government officials over alleged [[sinaloa-cartel|Sinaloa cartel]] ties amid growing tension with Mexican President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]], whom Trump accused of being a cartel puppet.[5]
