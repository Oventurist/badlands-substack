# Justin Trudeau

Justin Trudeau is the Canadian politician who led the [[liberal-party-of-canada|Liberal Party of Canada]] to power and served as Prime Minister, with [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]] as his Finance Minister and Deputy Prime Minister.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

In [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s account, Trudeau was prepped for the prime ministership as the "Barack Obama of Canada," with Freeland cast in the role of a Valerie Jarrett figure beside him.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) Her entry into his inner circle — and, in Ehret's framing, her position as his official handler — was engineered by the Rhodes Scholar [[bob-rae|Bob Rae]], who abdicated his seat to her.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems) The Liberal Party's preparation for power took place between 2011 and 2014 under the banner of the [[canada-2020|Canada 2020]] think tank.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-re-alignment-of-world-systems)

## December 2024 ski resort confrontation

While vacationing in British Columbia in late December 2024, Trudeau was confronted in a snow-filled parking lot at the Red Mountain Resort in Rossland by a local woman filming on her cellphone.[2] Wearing a snowboarder's helmet and ski goggles, Canada's 23rd prime minister walked over to shake her extended hand, whereupon she told him profanely to "get the f— out of B.C." and ended the exchange with "You suck."[2] Trudeau replied, "Ha, ha, have a nice day madam," before returning to his ski holiday with a child believed to be his 10-year-old son Hadrien.[2] Footage of the interaction went viral, though the Toronto Sun's Joe Warmington raised the possibility that it was an orchestrated gotcha video.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the episode as significant because the woman was a "farm mom" — a member of the very demographic Trudeau "was designed by his masters to appeal to," given his longstanding strong polling with women.[2] He called the targeted demographic's open rebellion against the operative a "big white pill," while asking why Canadian men were not in the vanguard of similar confrontations.[2]

## Replacement by Carney (March 2025)

On March 9, 2025, the [[liberal-party-of-canada|Liberal Party of Canada]] chose [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] to replace Trudeau as party leader and prime minister, amid a generational crisis in Canada's relationship with the United States.[3] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that a week after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] asked why Trudeau was still speaking for the Canadian people, Carney was succeeding him, and concluded: "Trump plays the tunes. They dance."[3]

## Mar-a-Lago meeting and the "51st state" remark (December 2024)

After President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened sweeping tariffs on Canadian products, Trudeau flew unannounced to Mar-a-Lago in late November 2024 for a meeting Trump called "very productive."[4] According to sources cited by Fox News, tariffs, border security and trade deficits dominated the discussion: Trump told Trudeau that [[canada|Canada]] had failed the U.S. border by allowing large volumes of drugs and people across, including illegal immigrants from more than 70 countries, and became animated over a U.S. trade deficit with Canada he estimated at more than $100 billion.[4] Trump said he would impose a 25% tariff on all Canadian goods on his first day in office if the border and deficit issues were not fixed; when Trudeau replied that such a tariff would kill the Canadian economy completely, Trump asked whether Canada could not survive except by "ripping off" the United States, then suggested Canada become the 51st state — prompting nervous laughter from the prime minister and others at the table.[4]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] called the resulting news cycle "paradigm-shattering," arguing that Trudeau had said the quiet part out loud in conceding Canada's status as a vassal state of the US empire while the United States gains little from the association given its own energy, steel and agricultural capacity.[4] The commentary held that the real victims of "engineered Canadian serfdom" are ordinary Canadians, and that pressure applied to Trudeau would ultimately turn them against the bureaucrats who sold them out.[4]

## Mar-a-Lago meeting with Trump (November 2024)

On Friday, November 29, 2024, Trudeau flew unannounced to Mar-a-Lago after President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened to impose sweeping 25% tariffs on Canadian goods over the flow of illegal immigrants and illicit drugs across the northern border.[4] Trump described the meeting as "very productive."[4] Sources present said Trump was cordial but blunt, telling Trudeau that Canada had failed the U.S. border by permitting large volumes of drugs and migrants — including people from more than 70 countries — to cross, and became more animated over a U.S. trade deficit with Canada he estimated at more than $100 billion.[4]

When Trudeau said the tariff could not be levied because it would destroy the Canadian economy, Trump asked whether Canada could not survive unless it was "ripping off" the United States, and then suggested that Canada become the 51st state, which drew nervous laughter from the prime minister and others at the table.[4]

## Badlands assessment

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that whatever the precise content of the exchange, the dominant international story — Trump holding firm on tariffs while Trudeau prostrated himself — was "paradigm-shattering."[4] The commentary argued that Trudeau had effectively conceded that Canada functions as a vassal state of the U.S. empire while the United States gains little from the arrangement, given its own capacity in energy, steel and agriculture.[4] It predicted that pressure applied to Trudeau would be felt by ordinary Canadians, who would ultimately blame the bureaucrats who sold them out and demand change.[4]


## Emergencies Act ruling and Canadian lawfare

On January 23, 2024, Federal Court Justice [[richard-mosley|Richard Mosley]] ruled that Trudeau's invocation of the [[emergencies-act|Emergencies Act]] against the 2022 [[freedom-convoy|Freedom Convoy]] was unreasonable and unjustified, finding it did "not bear the hallmarks of reasonableness—justification, transparency and intelligibility."[5] Convoy participants responded by preparing lawsuits against the federal government, the banks that froze their accounts, and the police.[5]

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] called the ruling the most precedent-setting decision for the Canadian public, holding it illegal for Trudeau to have used the act to "physically, psychologically and financially brutalize citizens for exercising their rights."[5] Esler argued it reopened avenues for citizen [[lawfare|lawfare]] that had appeared closed after Liberal Justice [[paul-rouleau|Paul Rouleau]]'s commission declared the invocation entirely justified.[5] He linked the reversal to a wider cluster of Canadian cases — the [[coutts-four|Coutts 4]] charge withdrawals, the ruling against [[deena-hinshaw|Deena Hinshaw]]'s Alberta COVID measures, and the fines imposed on nurse [[kristen-nagle|Kristen Nagle]] — arguing that the Trudeau government's losses and wins alike were redpilling ordinary Canadians about the politicization of the legal system.[5]

## Quiet Electoral Reform Push (January 2024)

In January 2024 CTV News reported that teams working for Trudeau and NDP leader Jagmeet Singh had quietly been in talks to table electoral reform legislation before the next federal vote, as part of the Liberal-NDP confidence-and-supply agreement.[6] Public Safety, Democratic Institutions and Intergovernmental Affairs Minister Dominic LeBlanc led the negotiations for the government, with NDP MP and democratic reform critic Daniel Blaikie for his party; Blaikie said "a fair amount of work" had been done toward drafting amendments to the Canada Elections Act.[6] The package fell short of the full overhaul of the federal voting system Trudeau had once promised, instead bundling proposals aimed at expanding "the ability for people to vote."[6]

Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] situated the move within a difficult week for Trudeau: within a seven-day span a world-class MMA fighter attacked his character, Canada's highest court ruled his use of the [[emergencies-act|Emergencies Act]] during the Freedom Convoy unlawful, and Tucker Carlson launched a "Liberate Canada" campaign against his platform.[6] Esler highlighted three headline proposals — an expanded three-day voting period during general elections, allowing voters to cast ballots at any polling place within their riding, and changes to the mail-in ballot process — and argued they resembled the "copy-paste" election-administration changes the globalist establishment had used elsewhere, calling them "the moves of someone scared to lose power."[6]

## December 2024 cabinet shuffle

In December 2024 Trudeau faced rising discontent over his leadership and announced he would shuffle his Cabinet on Friday, December 20, with the prime minister's office confirming he would participate in the swearing-in ceremony and chair a meeting with the new Cabinet later that day.[7] The abrupt departure of his finance minister [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]] earlier that week was described as a blow he might not recover from.[7]

A rising number of Liberal lawmakers called on Trudeau to resign, but new Finance Minister [[dominic-leblanc|Dominic LeBlanc]] said Trudeau had the "full support of his Cabinet," adding that he respected the views of those seeking resignation: "That's a view they are expressing. The prime minister listened carefully when that view was expressed to him... He listened, in some cases responded to specific things that were raised, and he said he would reflect carefully."[7] LeBlanc said the government would remain focused on addressing President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s threat to impose a 25% tariff on all Canadian products upon his inauguration the following month.[7]

## Resignation as Liberal Party leader (January 2025)

On Monday, January 6, 2025, Trudeau announced his resignation as leader of the [[liberal-party-of-canada|Liberal Party]] in the face of rising discontent over his leadership and after the abrupt departure of his finance minister signaled growing turmoil within his government.[8] He said "internal battles" meant he "cannot be the best option" in the next election, and planned to remain prime minister until a new party leader was chosen.[8]

An official familiar with the matter said Parliament, due to resume January 27, would be suspended until March 24 to allow a Liberal leadership race, and all three main opposition parties said they intended to topple the Liberals in a no-confidence vote when Parliament returned, making a spring election almost assured.[8] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the ouster as expected and even planned by the Canadian political class — which he glossed as the British Royals — not unlike the removal of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], and as the product of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s narrative campaign around Canada as the 51st state.[8] Full detail is at [[2025-trudeau-resignation|the 2025 Trudeau resignation]].[8]

## Apology for the Nazi veteran tribute (September 2023)

On Wednesday, September 27, 2023, Trudeau offered "Parliament's unreserved apologies" to the world after Canadian lawmakers mistakenly praised a man who had fought in a Nazi division during the Second World War, an episode covered at [[canadian-parliament-nazi-veteran-tribute|the Canadian Parliament Nazi veteran tribute]].[9] "This is a mistake that deeply embarrassed Parliament and Canada," he said in a televised address ahead of his apology in the House of Commons.[9]

Trudeau acknowledged that the incident, which occurred during Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]]'s visit to Ottawa, damaged Ukraine's public relations efforts as Moscow used the debacle to its propaganda advantage.[9] He said that "all of us who were in this House on Friday regret deeply having stood and clapped, even though we did so unaware of the context," calling the ovation "a horrendous violation of the memory of the millions of people who died in the Holocaust" and painful for Jewish, Polish, Roma, LGBTQI+, disabled and racialized people.[9] Trudeau did not take questions from reporters and pointed blame at the House of Commons speaker.[9]

## Hunka ovation and Ukraine funding, September 2023

During [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]]'s September 2023 visit to Ottawa, Trudeau joined the standing ovation for [[yaroslav-hunka|Yaroslav Hunka]], a veteran of the Nazi-aligned [[first-ukrainian-division|First Ukrainian Division]], in the [[house-of-commons-of-canada|House of Commons]].[10] [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] wrote that Hunka was "more than likely a hero" to Trudeau and Zelensky given his Third Reich service, summing the episode up as "birds of a feather flock together."[10] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] paired the ovation with Trudeau sending Zelensky home with hundreds of millions of dollars in fresh proxy-war funding "in the midst of an economic depression at home."[10]

## Online Harms Bill and the Scottish hate crime law (2024)

Badlands contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] observed in April 2024 that Scotland's new hate crime law, the [[hate-crime-and-public-order-scotland-act-2021|Hate Crime and Public Order (Scotland) Act 2021]], was "being implemented in lockstep with Trudeau's Online Harms Bill."[11] Esler argued the synchronization showed "an apolitical, globalist enemy attacking the very concept of the sovereign nation state," and noted that in his home province of Ontario laws treating misgendering as a hate crime had been in place since around 2017 without producing the predicted wave of arrests.[11]

## Freeland resignation (December 2024)

On December 16, 2024, Trudeau's finance minister and deputy prime minister [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]] — described as his chief lieutenant throughout his tenure — resigned from cabinet over differences on how to confront President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "America First" economic nationalism, threatened tariffs and a possible trade war.[12] Freeland's letter said Trudeau had told her he no longer wanted her as finance minister and had offered another cabinet role, leading her to conclude that resignation was "the only honest and viable path."[12] The Washington Post called the resignation the latest blow to an embattled Trudeau whose popularity had nosedived over the prior year, with polls projecting a wipeout for the [[liberal-party-of-canada|Liberal Party]] in any near-term federal election.[12] Badlands speculated that with Freeland ousted by a "capitulating" Trudeau, the "Proxy Prince of Canada" might be entering a Zelensky-style arc, turning on his globalist backers to accommodate Washington, and that the move would not please Brussels.[12]

## Mar-a-Lago dinner with Trump (November 2024)

Trudeau met President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on Friday, November 29, 2024 at Mar-a-Lago, where the two leaders discussed trade, the border and fentanyl — the subjects of Trump's tariff threat against Canada — over a dinner lasting roughly three hours.[13] Officials familiar with the meeting said the agenda also covered defense, [[nato|NATO]], Ukraine and China, along with several pipeline projects including the Keystone XL line killed by the Biden administration, and icebreakers.[13] Top Trump officials attended, including incoming National Security Adviser [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]], commerce secretary pick [[howard-lutnick|Howard Lutnick]], interior secretary pick Doug Burgum, newly elected Pennsylvania senator David McCormick and senior aide Dina Powell.[13] Days earlier Trump had vowed additional 10% tariffs on Chinese goods and 25% tariffs on all products from Mexico and Canada if the two neighbours failed to act on border security.[13]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that Trump had brought the leaders of Mexico and Canada "to heel in the span of 48 hours with little more than a narrative deployment" that tied together the international banking syndicate, the U.S. fentanyl crisis, drug cartels and free trade agreements.[13] He argued the currency flash crash triggered by the mere suggestion of tariffs amounted to a forced "blink," and characterized both the Trump–[[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]] phone call and the Trudeau dinner as face-saving efforts rather than negotiations — meaning Trump could obtain the benefits of tariffs without implementing them.[13]

## March 2025 tariff call with Trump

On Wednesday, March 5, 2025, after speaking with Trudeau by phone about newly implemented US tariffs, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the Canadian prime minister was trying to use the escalating trade war as a way to stay in power.[14] Writing on Truth Social, Trump said: "Justin Trudeau, of Canada, called me to ask what could be done about Tariffs. I told him that many people have died from Fentanyl that came through the Borders of Canada and Mexico, and nothing has convinced me that it has stopped. He said that it's gotten better, but I said, 'That's not good enough.'"[14] Trump described the call as partially cordial before adding: "The call ended in a 'somewhat' friendly manner! He was unable to tell me when the Canadian Election is taking place, which made me curious, like, what's going on here? I then realized he is trying to use this issue to stay in power. Good luck Justin!"[14]

Trump had previously delayed implementation of 25 percent tariffs on Canada and Mexico by a month following negotiations with Trudeau and Mexican President [[claudia-sheinbaum|Claudia Sheinbaum]], during which both countries agreed to additional border security measures; on Monday, March 3, he said there was no more room for dealmaking before the tariffs took effect at midnight.[14] Trudeau had announced his intent to resign in January 2025.[14] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that "the inversion of the globalist west continues unabated," arguing Trump was refusing to deal with Trudeau not out of political rivalry but because he considered him an illegitimate leader, and that across the [[globalist-hegemon|globalist hegemon]] "there are many such cases."[14]

## Retaliation against Trump tariffs, 2025

After President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] imposed levies of 25% on Canadian and Mexican goods and 10% on Chinese goods, effective that Tuesday, Trudeau announced he would impose a 25% tariff on more than $100 billion worth of American goods, covering everything from alcohol to perfume, clothing and produce.[15] Trump laid out his motive on Truth Social, writing that the United States pays "hundreds of Billions of Dollars to SUBSIDIZE Canada" for no reason, that America has unlimited energy and more lumber than it can use, and that without the subsidy "Canada ceases to exist as a viable Country" — proposing that Canada become "our Cherished 51st State" with lower taxes, better military protection, and no tariffs.[15]

Badlands commentary by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Trump's approach as "anchoring," leading with an endgame offer so that any subsequent concession favors the initial position, and argued that "Canada lost already — it only remains to be seen how badly."[15] A later entry said Trudeau had already blinked by telling Canadians to prepare for pain while Trump told Americans to prepare for winning.[15]

## Trump's "Governor of the Great State of Canada" jibe

In December 2024, President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly mocked Trudeau as the "governor" of the "Great State of Canada" in a Truth Social post following their dinner at Mar-a-Lago, writing that he looked forward to continuing "in depth talks on Tariffs and Trade."[16] Trump had recently warned of tariffs on Canada and had joked that the country should become the 51st US state, suggesting it directly to Trudeau at the dinner according to attendees.[16] Trudeau's office did not immediately respond to requests for comment, while Trump spokesman Steven Cheung said only, "It's so good."[16]

Badlands commentary argued that Trudeau capitulated publicly within 48 hours of Trump's 25% tariff threat, making commitments to crack down on Canadian border security, and framed the "governor" line as a memetic tactic that made the story viral while carrying an implicit threat.[16] The commentary's advice to Trudeau, [[claudia-sheinbaum|Sheinbaum]], [[emmanuel-macron|Macron]] and other leaders was to "take the deal."[16]

## Dispute with Elon Musk over CBC labeling (April 2023)

In April 2023, Trudeau publicly accused [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] of "attacking foundational Canadian institutions" after [[twitter|Twitter]] applied a "government funded" label to the [[canadian-broadcasting-corporation|CBC]], Canada's largely state-financed broadcaster.[18] Trudeau insisted that the CBC is an "independent media organization" notwithstanding its near-total reliance on government funding.[18] He grouped Musk with his domestic conservative opponents, charging that they had "run to American billionaire tech giants" in order to "constantly attack" Canadians, and stated that they were "choosing to constantly attack independent media organizations, journalists who are working hard to keep Canadians informed and support our democracy."[18]

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] rejected Trudeau's framing, arguing that the vehemence of the reaction to a factually accurate label indicated an agenda dependent on public confidence in media neutrality.[18] DeLarme characterized the incident as Twitter applying to mainstream outlets the same standards the platform had long applied to non-mainstream voices.[18]

## June 2024 by-election defeat and resignation pressure

In late June 2024 the heat on Trudeau intensified after his party's candidate suffered a surprise defeat in a special election for Toronto—St. Paul's, an urban seat the Liberals had held since 1993.[19] The staggering loss, amid Canada's affordability crisis and a swelling desire for political change, amplified pressure on him to resign for the good of his party, placing him among a long list of sinking incumbent governments that emerged from the pandemic politically wounded.[19] Pressed by CBC host David Cochrane on whether he accepted that he was the main reason his party was struggling so badly, Trudeau replied that he had discussed the matter with other leaders at the [[g7|G7]] and at the Ukraine peace conference in Switzerland, and that "everywhere, people are struggling with high inflation, cost of living issues, interest rates, housing challenges, childcare challenges," adding that Canada was "doing better than many countries."[19]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Trudeau was being pressured to leave by the end of summer so his party could choose a new leader before the following year's election, called his answer detached from reality, and argued that the conditions he described were the fault of the G7, Trudeau himself and their global handlers rather than an inexplicable accident.[19] She closed by saying she did not think the regime could replace [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], and was very curious whether it could replace Trudeau.[19]
