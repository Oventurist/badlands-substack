# Yoav Gallant

Yoav Gallant is the Israeli Defense Minister who, in May 2024, became the most senior official to publicly call out Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] over the government's failure to produce a "day-after" plan for the Gaza Strip following the defeat of [[hamas|Hamas]].[1]

## Public challenge to Netanyahu

Speaking in rare, blunt remarks while commemorating Israel's Memorial Day, Gallant said: "I call on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, to make a decision and declare that Israel will not establish civilian control over the Gaza Strip, that Israel will not establish military governance in the Gaza strip, and that a governing alternative to Hamas in the Gaza Strip will be raised immediately."[1]

It was the second time Gallant had issued such a direct challenge to the prime minister. In March 2023 he had warned that Netanyahu's pursuit of widely controversial judicial reforms was threatening the security of the country.[1] Some analysts held that the national division of that period fed into Hamas's calculation to launch its mass-casualty attack of October 7.[1]

Netanyahu responded to Gallant's televised address with a video statement declaring he was "not ready to replace Hamastan with Fatahstan," rejecting any handover of Gaza to the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] and its dominant [[fatah|Fatah]] faction.[1]

## Context

Gallant's intervention came amid growing frustration in Israel with Netanyahu's handling of the war against Hamas and his widening split with U.S. President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], raising fears that public tensions between the two governments could invite increased attacks from [[iran|Iran]] and its regional proxies.[1]

Badlands commentary framed the episode as evidence that "the knives are now out" in Israeli politics, noting that Netanyahu had produced no plan for holding or governing territory cleared by the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]], thereby allowing Hamas to regenerate in those areas.[1]

## Gaza City offensive (November 2023)

On Tuesday, November 7, 2023, Gallant issued a battlefield update announcing that for the first time Israeli troops were "in the heart" of Gaza City, declaring that "Gaza is the largest terrorist base ever built" and that "We are going to destroy Hamas."[2] He said the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] had [[yahya-sinwar|Yahya Sinwar]], the leader of [[hamas|Hamas]] in Gaza, surrounded in a bunker, and noted that fighting was taking place in residential areas.[2]

Gallant also appeared to reject U.S. pressure to implement a humanitarian "pause," emphasizing that "there will be no humanitarian truce without the return of the hostages."[2] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], speaking from the Kirya military headquarters in Tel Aviv, affirmed the same position, said Gaza City was surrounded, and warned Lebanon's [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] that opening a new full war front would be "the greatest mistake of its life."[2]

## Opposition to a military government in Gaza (2024)

In November 2024, after leaving office, former Defense Minister Gallant said in a post on X that "a military government in Gaza is not part of the goals of the war, but a dangerous and irresponsible political act."[3] He made the statement in response to cabinet discussions about distributing food to Palestinian residents of [[gaza|Gaza]] through private companies under [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] security, calling those plans "empty words for the beginning of a military government."[3]

Gallant warned that "the price will be paid by the blood of the IDF soldiers, and the State of [[israel|Israel]] will pay in its own poor order of priorities that will lead to neglecting more important security tasks," adding that "everything depends on the preparation ahead of time of an alternative entity that will replace the IDF in Gaza."[3] His remarks came as the IDF announced two more soldier deaths, crossing the 800-fatality threshold for the war to 801.[3]

Former war cabinet minister and IDF chief [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] seconded Gallant's comments, while Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] accused both men of weakness.[3] Badlands commentary framed the two retired generals as the vanguard of a growing political resistance to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s government.[3]

## August 2024 warning over hostage-deal terms

In early August 2024, Israeli media reported that Gallant, then defense minister, together with IDF chief [[herzi-halevi|Herzi Halevi]], told Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] that his insistence on new terms would sabotage the ceasefire and hostage-release deal under negotiation.[4] Both were quoted as accusing Netanyahu of knowing full well that his added conditions would doom the deal, while the premier countered that [[hamas|Hamas]], not he, was introducing new demands.[4] The disputed conditions reportedly included an inspection mechanism to prevent combatants moving north, continued Israeli presence on the [[philadelphi-corridor|Philadelphi Corridor]] during the first phase, and a list of all living hostages to be released.[4]

## 'New phase' of the war, September 2024

Speaking to troops at the Ramat David Airbase near Haifa on Wednesday, September 18, 2024, Gallant declared that "a new phase" of Israel's almost year-long war was beginning, shifting focus from Hamas to [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[5] "The center of gravity is moving north. We are diverting forces, resources, and energy toward the north," he said in remarks published by his office on social media.[5] The announcement followed the detonation of thousands of pagers and radios belonging to the Lebanese paramilitary group in an operation widely attributed to Israeli secret services.[5]

Gallant had repeatedly threatened a major offensive into Lebanon in response to Hezbollah's low-intensity campaign along the border, which Hassan Nasrallah framed as an effort to tie up Israeli forces and prevent their deployment to Gaza.[5] Less than two months earlier, Foreign Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] had said the state was preparing for "all out war" with Hezbollah.[5] Badlands commentary treated Gallant's "new phase" language as evidence that the perceived success of the [[pager-attack|pager attack]] had emboldened the Netanyahu government toward a ground invasion.[5]

## Reported dismissal plans, September 2024

In September 2024, Israeli national broadcaster Kan reported, citing an official within the prime minister's bureau, that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was preparing to fire Gallant as defense minister and was considering appointing New Hope chairman [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] as his replacement.[6] Channel 12 reported that if the defense portfolio did not pan out for Sa'ar, he might instead receive the foreign minister role while [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] took over for Gallant, and that New Hope MKs Ze'ev Elkin and Sharren Haskel could be offered ministerial portfolios.[6] The Prime Minister's Office denied negotiations with Sa'ar, and a spokesman for Sa'ar said there was "nothing new" on the issue.[6] Rumors of Gallant's replacement had circulated for months, with Sa'ar denying as early as July 2024 that he had received an offer that would return him to the coalition, though he declined to "tie my hands" on serving as defense minister.[6]

Badlands reported that Gallant wanted to avoid immediate escalation of the war with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] in the north, clashing with the army's Northern Command chief Ori Gordin, who was calling for the government to approve a large-scale operation in Lebanon to destroy Hezbollah.[6] Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized Gallant as [[joe-biden|Biden]]'s "inside man" in the Netanyahu government, turning on Netanyahu and demanding peace in a way that forced his own dismissal — a maneuver he compared to [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]]'s departure in June 2024.[6]

## Dismissal over the judicial overhaul (March 2023)

In late March 2023, Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] fired Gallant as Defense Minister one day after Gallant publicly called on him to halt the planned [[israeli-judicial-overhaul|judicial overhaul]] that had fiercely divided the country.[7] The dismissal was read as a signal that Netanyahu would press ahead with the overhaul that week, and it triggered massive protests across Israel, angered military and business leaders, and raised concerns among Israel's allies.[7] Amid the resulting street chaos, Netanyahu announced in a televised evening address that he would pause the plan to "avoid civil war."[7]

## ICC arrest warrant application (May 2024)

In May 2024 the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]] Prosecutor applied to Pre-Trial Chamber I for warrants of arrest against Gallant, then Israeli Defense Minister, and Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] over alleged war crimes in Gaza, alongside [[hamas|Hamas]] figures [[yahya-sinwar|Yahya Sinwar]], [[mohammed-deif|Mohammed Deif]] and [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]].[8] [[european-union|EU]] foreign policy chief [[josep-borrell|Josep Borrell]] stated that if Netanyahu or Gallant travelled to a European country they would face arrest, because all states that have ratified the [[rome-statute|Rome Statute]] are bound to execute the court's decisions.[8]

## May 2024 ICC warrant application

In May 2024, Gallant was named alongside Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] in [[international-criminal-court|ICC]] prosecutor [[karim-khan|Karim Khan]]'s application for arrest warrants.[9] [[norway|Norway]] announced through Foreign Minister [[espen-barth-eide|Espen Barth Eide]] that it would be obliged to arrest both men if warrants were issued and they arrived on Norwegian soil, though Israeli commentary noted the pledge was largely symbolic given the collapse in Norway–Israel relations.[9] Britain objected to the equivalence Khan's application drew between Gallant and Netanyahu on the one hand and [[hamas|Hamas]] leaders on the other.[9]

## Cancelled Washington visit (October 2024)

Israel cancelled a planned meeting between Gallant and U.S. Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] scheduled for Wednesday, October 9, 2024, as the two governments debated how Israel should respond to Iran's missile attack the previous week.[10] [[pentagon|Pentagon]] deputy press secretary Sabrina Singh told reporters, "We were just informed that Minister Gallant will be postponing his trip to Washington, D.C.," adding that "Secretary Austin looks forward to seeing him soon."[10] Gallant had also been due to meet White House National Security Adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]].[10]

Hours before Gallant was to depart, Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] reportedly told him he did not approve of the trip and that he should wait until Netanyahu spoke with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]; the two leaders had not spoken in nearly 50 days amid tensions over the war in Gaza and the conflict with Hezbollah in Lebanon.[10] Netanyahu also delayed the trip pending approval by the political-security cabinet of Israel's planned actions against Iran, and the prime minister's office told Israel's N12News that the move was a normal order of operations because the visit made no sense until Israel had reached a clear decision on Iran.[10]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry situated the cancellation within a longer feud, recalling that Netanyahu had fired and immediately rehired Gallant in spring 2023 after Gallant attended a rally calling for the prime minister's resignation.[10] He noted that after Benny Gantz resigned in June and the War Cabinet was dissolved, Netanyahu became dependent on Gallant's participation, and that Netanyahu subsequently elevated minister without portfolio [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] to the Security Cabinet and to a new three-man war cabinet including himself and Gallant — fuelling speculation that Sa'ar would eventually replace Gallant as Defense Minister.[10]

## Dismissal as Defense Minister (November 2024)

On November 5, 2024 — the day of the US presidential election — Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] announced that he was firing Gallant as Defense Minister, citing a lack of mutual trust with a longtime rival inside the [[likud|Likud]] party.[11] In a terse letter delivered to Gallant and then released by the prime minister's office, Netanyahu wrote that "your tenure will end 48 hours from the receipt of this letter," and closed by thanking him for his service.[11]

In a video statement, Netanyahu said that although "in the first months of the war there was trust and there was very fruitful work," that trust had "cracked" in recent months, and charged that Gallant had made statements and taken actions contradicting cabinet decisions.[11] He further accused Gallant of indirectly aiding Israel's enemies, saying the disagreements "came to the knowledge of the public in an unacceptable way, and worse than that, they came to the knowledge of the enemy — our enemies enjoyed it and derived a lot of benefit from it."[11] The resulting "crisis of faith," he said, "does not enable the proper continuation of the [military] campaign."[11]

Gallant was replaced by Foreign Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]], with minister without portfolio [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] taking Katz's place at the foreign ministry.[11] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the dismissal itself was not shocking — it had been speculated for months — but that its timing on US Election Day was "rather interesting," and that Sa'ar had widely been expected to be the replacement rather than Katz.[11]

## Criticism of Ben-Gvir (August 2024)

In August 2024 Gallant, as defense minister, joined the head of the [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]] in publicly criticizing National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] for encouraging settler attacks in the West Bank and for failing to deploy the police forces under his ministry to shut down the incidents or arrest those involved.[12]

## "War without a compass" letter (October 2024)

In late October 2024, Gallant sent a letter to Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and members of the security cabinet stating that "the war is being conducted without a compass" and that its objectives needed to be updated.[13] The letter, first reported by Channel 13 News, was dispatched shortly before Israel's overnight strike on [[iran|Iran]] and was copied to [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]] director [[ronen-bar|Ronen Bar]], [[mossad|Mossad]] chief [[david-barnea|David Barnea]], IDF Chief of Staff [[herzl-halevi|Herzl Halevi]] and cabinet ministers.[13] Gallant argued that operating without a valid compass and without updated war goals harmed both the management of the campaign and the cabinet's decisions, and that the direct exchanges of blows between Israel and Iran made a comprehensive review of objectives across all arenas urgent.[13]

He proposed four new war goals corresponding to the four arenas in which Israel operates — [[gaza|Gaza]], [[lebanon|Lebanon]], Iran and the West Bank.[13] For Gaza, the stated objective was "establishing a reality without a military threat, preventing the enhancement of terror capabilities, securing the return of all hostages, and promoting an alternative to [[hamas|Hamas]] rule."[13]

### Badlands analysis

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] situated the letter in the rift between Gallant and Netanyahu dating to March 2023, when Gallant attended an anti-Netanyahu protest against the judicial overhaul and was fired, only to be rehired the following day.[13] He characterized the public letter, issued hours before the Iran strike, as a strange move that underscored the fractured state of Israeli domestic politics at what he called the most critical moment in the nation's short history.[13] Importantly, in his reading Gallant — widely regarded as the Biden administration's "inside man" in the Netanyahu cabinet — was not criticizing the warmongering itself, only the absence of defined objectives.[13]

The commentary predicted that the most likely outcome would be Gallant's dismissal and replacement, a step fraught with risk for Netanyahu given competing pressures within his coalition, with [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] the long-rumored successor and [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] the option most likely to provoke mutiny among the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] brass.[13] It also noted that both Gallant and [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] are retired generals who, despite a long history as political rivals, found common ground in criticizing Netanyahu and appear to speak for an IDF leadership that has soured on the lack of strategic objectives.[13]

## Reported role in the June 2024 crisis

During the June 2024 coalition crisis, mainstream outlets reported that Gallant was [[joe-biden|Biden]]'s "inside man" in the Israeli government, and it was widely speculated that the Biden administration was pushing him to revolt against [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]].[14] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that if [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] could convince Gallant to resign and join his protest, Gantz would stand a decent chance of unseating Netanyahu in a new election.[14]
