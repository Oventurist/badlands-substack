# Apple

**Apple** is an American technology company and, as of 2025, the largest American company by market capitalization.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Market position

Apple ranked first among the top five U.S. companies by market cap in 2025 and 2020, and second in 2010 behind Exxon.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) In 2025 Apple was roughly ten times larger than the twentieth-largest company, Home Depot — a spread cited as evidence of extreme concentration in the [[sp-500-index|S&P 500 Index]], where the top five firms hold about a third of the index weighting.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Allegations in Badlands coverage

In *America Has a CIA-Run Economy*, [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] groups Apple with the other mega-cap technology firms he argues share "the same master, the [[cia|CIA]]," describing them as critical instruments of propaganda and global control.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) He suggests [[darpa|DARPA]] probably created Siri, and states that iPhones listen to everything said around them and track everywhere the user goes.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

Apple was among the companies — alongside Google, [[microsoft|Microsoft]], [[meta-platforms|Meta]], [[amazon|Amazon]] and [[openai|OpenAI]] — that donated a million dollars to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s inaugural fund, which Carlson characterizes as big tech "kissing the ring."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) He also argues it is hard to imagine an America without Apple, framing the company as a necessary component of the U.S. economy going forward.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

## Involvement in the 2023 Google antitrust trial

Court documents filed ahead of the September 2023 antitrust trial over [[google|Google]]'s search dominance indicated that Eddy Cue, one of Apple's highest-ranking executives, might be called to testify, reflecting the commercial relationship between the two companies over default search placement.[2] See [[united-states-v-google-antitrust-trial]].[2]



## 2024 Justice Department antitrust action

In March 2024 the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] brought an [[united-states-v-apple-antitrust-case|antitrust action]] against Apple that, on its face, concerned the company's secure communications network, with the government seeking to force Apple to share its services with other networks.[3] The [[brownstone-institute|Brownstone Institute]] argued the case was really the government taking sides in a competitive dispute on behalf of Samsung and other smartphone providers, who resent the way Apple products all work together.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the action as demonizing competition and trampling intellectual property rights in the name of antitrust, and as an attempt to extend to device hardware and networking infrastructure the informal control the bureaucracy once exercised over social media platforms.[3]

## Congo mineral litigation (December 2025)

Badlands coverage of the [[m23|M23]] offensive in eastern Congo in December 2025 singled out Apple for knowingly participating in a scheme through which rare earth ore mined in the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] — reportedly using child slave labour — is smuggled into [[rwanda|Rwanda]], refined, and issued forged certificates marking it as cleanly sourced before sale to Fortune 500 companies.[4] The company is the defendant in a civil action brought by the government of the DR Congo and is also under criminal investigation by European authorities, who have already arrested and jailed commodity asset executives from firms such as [[glencore|Glencore]] and [[trafigura|Trafigura]].[4] The rebel capture of [[uvira|Uvira]] was expected to make traceability of these minerals, valued at an estimated $24 trillion in the region, effectively impossible.[4]

## Vision Pro launch

Apple's nearly $4,000 mixed-reality headset, the [[apple-vision-pro|Apple Vision Pro]], reached store shelves on Friday, February 2, 2024, and early adopters were quickly documented using it in public, including in videos of users wearing the device while driving.[5] Coverage framed the launch as a step toward dystopia, noting that Black Mirror creator Charlie Brooker had said Apple's Vision Pro presentation struck him as unnervingly reminiscent of his own series.[5] Badlands commentary treated the product as a vehicle of gradual "disembodiment" conditioning leading toward [[transhumanism|transhumanism]].[5]

## Trump pressure over Indian manufacturing (2025)

In May 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said publicly that he had told Apple chief executive [[tim-cook|Tim Cook]] he had "a little problem" with the company's plan to manufacture iPhones in [[india|India]].[6] Speaking during his visit to [[qatar|Qatar]], Trump recounted telling Cook, "You're coming in with $500 billion but now I hear you're building all over India," and added, "We're not interested in you building in India. India can take care of themselves."[6]

The $500 billion figure referred to Apple's February 2025 pledge to spend that sum in the United States on work with thousands of suppliers across all 50 states, direct employment, Apple Intelligence infrastructure and datacenters, corporate facilities, and Apple TV+ productions in 20 states, along with a partner factory to build servers running the Apple Intelligence service.[6] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the episode as Trump signalling to Apple to "claim your share of the coming American Greatness" after decades in which American manufacturing and then white-collar work were offshored.[6]

## DRC mineral-laundering lawsuit (2024–2025)

The law firm [[amsterdam-and-partners|Amsterdam & Partners]], acting on behalf of the government of the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]], published a report accusing Apple — along with Intel, Sony and Lockheed Martin — of illegally smuggling rare earth minerals out of the Congo into neighbouring countries, primarily [[rwanda|Rwanda]], so they could be laundered into the global supply chain as "clean," meaning certified as not mined using child slavery.[7]

The report further alleged that these companies used UN-connected organizations such as the [[international-tin-supply-chain-initiative|International Tin Supply Chain Initiative]] and the [[responsible-minerals-initiative|Responsible Minerals Initiative]] to legitimize the supply chains with fraudulent certifications, and named individuals including French industrialist Vincent Bolloré.[7] Approximately two months before late February 2025, Amsterdam & Partners filed a lawsuit against Apple on behalf of the DRC government.[7]

## India airlift ahead of the April 2025 tariff deadline

In April 2025 Apple chartered flights to ship roughly 600 tons of iPhones — up to 1.5 million individual handsets — out of [[india|India]] to the United States before President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s tariffs took broad effect, in an apparent effort to build up U.S. inventory.[8] Analysts warned that U.S. iPhone prices could spike because the company relies on Chinese-produced parts and because its principal manufacturing hub is in [[china|China]], then facing a 125 percent tariff rate.[8] Indian imports faced a 26 percent rate, but Trump announced a 90-day moratorium on the reciprocal import taxes after markets fell sharply following his [[liberation-day-tariffs|"Liberation Day"]] announcement, replacing them with a 10 percent across-the-board tariff excluding China; a source familiar with the company told Reuters that Apple sought to exploit this temporary opening to "beat the tariff."[8]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the episode showed shifting U.S. trade policy already changing corporate behavior, noting that about 20 percent of Apple iPhones destined for the U.S. are manufactured in India rather than China, and that the company had reportedly set a goal of increasing production at its Indian iPhone plants by 20 percent — a long-term investment implying Apple expects large-scale decoupling from China to stick.[8] She tied this to Apple's February 2025 announcement of its largest-ever spending commitment, more than $500 billion in the United States over four years.[8] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] used Apple as an illustration that companies were fleeing the East and re-shoring production irrespective of Trump alone, and pointed to suicide nets outside Chinese factories as evidence that globalism's "cheap goods" dynamic did not benefit the Chinese people either.[8]

## Liberation Day tariff exposure (April 2025)

Apple was among the largest casualties of the market sell-off that followed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[liberation-day-tariffs|"Liberation Day" tariffs]], tumbling below recent lows on Thursday, April 3, 2025 alongside Amazon.com, Nvidia, and Meta Platforms as the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq suffered their worst one-day declines since 2020.[9] Because the company's hardware supply chain runs through the Chinese and south-east Asian manufacturing base targeted by the new duties, Apple became the standard illustration of the tariffs' consumer price effects: Reuters reported, citing projections from [[rosenblatt-securities|Rosenblatt Securities]], that a high-end iPhone could cost nearly $2,300 if Apple passed the costs on to buyers.[9]

## Italian antitrust fine over App Tracking Transparency (December 2025)

On December 22, 2025, [[italy|Italy]]'s antitrust authority fined Apple 98.6 million euros ($116 million) after determining that the operation of its [[app-tracking-transparency|App Tracking Transparency]] (ATT) privacy feature restricted App Store competition, finding that Apple had abused its dominant position.[10] Apple said it would appeal the sanction.[10]

ATT, rolled out beginning in April 2021 as part of an update to the operating system powering the iPhone and iPad, forces apps to obtain permission before collecting data to target users with personalized ads.[10] While designed to tighten privacy, it drew criticism from Big Tech rivals who argued it would make it harder for smaller apps to survive without charging consumers.[10] The Italian authority did not criticize the policy per se, but the fact that Apple's system requires third-party app makers to ask users for consent twice in order to comply with Europe's strict privacy rules, saying the "double consent requirement is harmful to developers, whose business model relies on the sale of advertising space, as well as to advertisers and advertising intermediation platforms."[10]

## Indian iPhone production and friendshoring

By April 2024 Apple's India-made iPhone output had topped $14 billion in value, with Bloomberg reporting that roughly 14 percent — about one in seven — of the company's iPhones were being made in India.[11] The rapid increase was read as evidence that Apple was accelerating efforts to reduce its reliance on China amid worsening Sino-US relations, part of the broader [[friendshoring|friendshoring]] trend that followed the trade war begun by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[11]

[[foxconn|Foxconn Technology Group]] assembled nearly 67 percent and Pegatron Corporation about 17 percent of India-made iPhones in the fiscal year ended March 2024, with the balance produced at the Wistron plant in Karnataka taken over by the [[tata-group|Tata Group]], which planned to build one of India's biggest iPhone assembly plants.[11] China nevertheless still produced the largest share of iPhones.[11] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the shift did not damage China, since [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] had been adversarial toward the US oligarchy represented by Apple and Microsoft and was prioritising domestic Chinese producers, and read the trend as reinforcing multipolarity.[11]

## AI search engines in Safari (May 2025)

In May 2025 [[eddy-cue|Eddy Cue]], Apple's senior vice president of services, testified in court that the company was considering adding artificial intelligence search engines to its Safari web browser as search traffic declined.[12] Cue said Safari search traffic had fallen for the first time the previous month because of AI, and that Apple would likely add providers such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity: "We will add them to the list — they probably won't be the default."[12] [[google|Google]]'s share price fell about 9 percent on the day of the testimony, recovering nearly 2 percent the following day.[12]


## American Manufacturing Program and $600 billion commitment (August 2025)

In August 2025 Apple announced a new $100 billion commitment to America, bringing its total planned US investment to $600 billion over four years, and launched the [[american-manufacturing-program|American Manufacturing Program]] (AMP) to bring more of its supply chain and advanced manufacturing into the United States.[13] Through AMP, the company said it would increase investment across America and incentivize global firms to manufacture more critical components domestically.[13] Chief executive [[tim-cook|Tim Cook]] said the announcement covered new and expanded work with ten companies across America producing components used in Apple products sold worldwide, and thanked the President for his support.[13] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], whose team covered the announcement live, argued that legacy media outlets highlighted the $100 billion figure rather than the $600 billion total in order to minimize the news.[13]
