# Amir H. Ali

Amir H. Ali is a judge of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia who became a focal point of litigation over foreign aid spending during the opening weeks of Donald Trump's second term.[1]

## Foreign aid payment order (February 2025)

In late February 2025 Judge Ali issued an order requiring the Trump administration to disburse roughly $2 billion in foreign aid funds to contractors by midnight, money tied to the [[usaid|U.S. Agency for International Development]].[1] Administration officials stated that they would not be able to comply with the deadline and sought emergency relief from the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[1]

In its emergency application, the administration argued that Ali's order had created "an untenable payment plan at odds with the President's obligations under Article II to protect the integrity of the federal fisc and make appropriate judgements(sic) about foreign aid – clear forms of irreparable harm."[1]

Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]] paused the order on Wednesday, February 26, 2025, with responses from the groups litigating against the administration due before Friday at 12 p.m., meaning the administrative pause was potentially short-lived.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] commentator Jon Herold framed the dispute as a test of the constitutional role of the president, and predicted that the Court would deliver similar rulings in cases attempting to block Trump administration actions.[1]

## USAID funding freeze TRO

In early 2025 Ali, sitting as a U.S. District Judge, issued a temporary restraining order forcing payment of roughly $2 billion in [[usaid|U.S. Agency for International Development]] funds that the Trump administration had sought to freeze.[2] On March 5, 2025, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] denied the administration's application for a stay of that TRO by a 5–4 vote in [[department-of-state-v-aids-vaccine-advocacy-coalition|Department of State v. AIDS Vaccine Advocacy Coalition]], leaving Ali's order in place.[2] Legal commentator [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] wrote that the administration was "down by one vote but hardly out" in its fight with the lower courts over control of the funding.[2]

## Foreign aid payment order stayed (February 2025)

In February 2025, Ali, a Washington-based U.S. District Judge, issued an order requiring President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s administration to pay foreign aid funds to contractors and grant recipients, imposing a deadline of 11:59 p.m. on Wednesday, February 26.[3] Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]] paused that order the same week through an administrative stay issued without stated rationale, giving the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] more time to weigh the administration's formal request to block the ruling.[3]
