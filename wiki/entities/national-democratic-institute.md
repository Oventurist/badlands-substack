# National Democratic Institute

The National Democratic Institute (NDI) is one of the four core surrogate organizations created beneath the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]].[1] Badlands Media describes its function as exercising CIA/NED influence over Democratic Party politics both in the United States and in developing countries.[1]

## Position in the network

NDI is the partisan counterpart to the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]], which performs the same role on the Republican side; the [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]], which covers unions and the workforce in affiliation with the [[afl-cio|AFL-CIO]]; and the Center for International Private Enterprise, which covers business and chambers of commerce.[1] NED sits at the head of this structure as the grantmaking body funding cut-outs specializing in regime-change operations.[1]

## Origins

The institute belongs to the wave of institutions created in the 1980s after [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]]'s CIA director [[william-casey|William Casey]] secured bipartisan support for moving Cold War influence work from the agency level down to the NGO level, multiplying the deniability layers separating operations from the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], the Department of Defense, and the [[state-department|State Department]].[1]

## Role in the information imperium

Badlands Media's "The Information Imperium" identifies the NDI as one of the four core grantees of the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]], alongside the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]], the [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]] and the [[center-for-international-private-enterprise|Center for International Private Enterprise]].[2] The article credits NDI and IRI with coaching the ostensibly spontaneous 2004 [[orange-revolution|Orange Revolution]] in Ukraine, a campaign it says was synchronised with [[internews|Internews]]' media-training work in the country.[2] Domestically it accuses the two institutes of grooming party operatives, citing Obama's 2008 campaign machine and the neutering of the Tea Party by 2012.[2] In February 2025 [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[department-of-government-efficiency|Department of Government Efficiency]] blocked Treasury disbursements to the NED, cutting NDI staffing by roughly two-thirds and forcing furloughs.[2]

## Role in the NED structure and CEPPS

Badlands Media characterizes the National Democratic Institute as the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|NED]]'s Democratic arm — the channel of [[cia|CIA]] influence over the Democratic Party — parallel to the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]] on the Republican side.[3] In 1995 the NDI joined the IRI and the [[international-foundation-for-electoral-systems|International Foundation for Electoral Systems]] to create the [[consortium-for-elections-and-political-process-strengthening|Consortium for Elections and Political Process Strengthening]], a [[usaid|USAID]]-funded joint venture later linked to counter-disinformation and censorship programming in more than 140 countries.[3]

## Domestic discipline and leadership

"Two Wings, One Ugly Bird" presents the NDI, founded in 1983 under the NED umbrella, as one half of a paired mechanism for keeping the American political class on a leash, with the IRI covering the Republican side.[4] The article argues the institutes are sold as independent partisan cheerleaders for liberty, but that tens of millions of dollars annually from the taxpayer-funded NED tie both to the same government spigot controlled by the intelligence community that learned to outsource its work after 1975.[4] Domestically, it says, NDI does not merely export democracy but imports discipline — training party operatives, shaping platforms and grooming candidates under the guise of "capacity building" — so that maverick voices are sidelined; in the article's phrase, "it's not about rigging votes—it's about rigging the options."[4]

Abroad, the article credits NDI with pouring cash into "voter education" and training opposition groups during Ukraine's 2004 [[orange-revolution|Orange Revolution]] in support of [[viktor-yushchenko|Viktor Yushchenko]]'s bloc, and with shaping the Serbian opposition coalition that displaced [[slobodan-milosevic|Slobodan Milosevic]] alongside [[otpor|Otpor]].[4] At home in the 1990s it says NDI worked with Democratic leadership to "modernize" organizing while sidelining populist outliers such as Jesse Jackson's Rainbow Coalition, and that the institute's overseas organising techniques were mirrored in [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s 2008 grassroots machine.[4]

The article's roster of NDI figures includes chair [[madeleine-albright|Madeleine Albright]], board members [[stacey-abrams|Stacey Abrams]] and [[donna-brazile|Donna Brazile]], president [[derek-mitchell|Derek Mitchell]] (2018–2023) and current president [[tamara-cofman-wittes|Tamara Cofman Wittes]], along with lesser-known operatives such as Harriet Babbitt, Robert Liberatore, Howard Dean and Paul G. Kirk Jr.[4] Following the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]]'s 5–4 ruling of March 5, 2025 that blocked [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s bid to freeze $2 billion in [[usaid|USAID]] funds, the article reports that NDI, the IRI and the rest of the cut-out network saw furloughs lifted and grants resume.[4]
