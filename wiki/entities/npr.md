# NPR

NPR (National Public Radio) is an American public radio network mentioned in Badlands Media's survey of the legacy media's contraction.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

In her February 2024 assessment, [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]] stated that "radio is essentially dead, aside from NPR, which has been gutted," placing the network among the survivors of an industry-wide collapse that also encompassed the obliteration of local news, the cratering of the newspaper business and strikes at [[conde-nast|Condé Nast]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Coverage of Badlands Media (November 2025)

In November 2025, NPR published a story headlined "Right-Wing Media Shrugs Off Latest Epstein Document Release," examining how influencers who had long demanded disclosure responded to thousands of newly released pages concerning [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[2] The piece quoted [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] saying on his Rumble livestream, "To me, these are nothingburgers. If they're even real," and described him as having gained an audience after the 2020 election by spreading QAnon-adjacent conspiracy theories.[2] It also cited fellow Badlands personality [[brian-lupo|Brian Lupo]], who argued the emails showed Trump was informing on Epstein and [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]], who is serving a 20-year prison term.[2]

Herold responded that the article was likely the first time Badlands had ever been mentioned by NPR, said the outlet was "mad we didn't go hook, line, and sinker like literally everybody else on the internet," and disputed the "QAnon-adjacent" label by pointing to a nonexistent post number.[2]

## Uri Berliner resignation (April 2024)

On April 17, 2024, NPR senior business editor [[uri-berliner|Uri Berliner]] resigned after 25 years at the network, citing the response of chief executive [[katherine-maher|Katherine Maher]] to an outside essay in which he accused NPR of losing the public's trust.[3] Berliner told Maher he could not work in a newsroom "where I am disparaged by a new CEO whose divisive views confirm the very problems at NPR I cite in my Free Press essay," while affirming his respect for his colleagues and his wish for NPR to thrive.[3] NPR and Maher declined to comment on the resignation.[3]

Berliner's essay, published by The Free Press, argued that NPR's coverage had increasingly reflected a rigid progressive ideology, and that the network's pursuit of workforce diversity under prior chief executive John Lansing had not been matched by diversity of viewpoints in its shows, podcasts, or online coverage.[3] NPR had suspended Berliner before his resignation, a step Badlands commentators argued proved his point about censorship and political bias at the outlet.[3] Those commentators also contended that NPR and allied outlets focused on Berliner's criticism of his employer precisely to avoid engaging with the substance of his argument, and predicted an exodus of "newly awakened former NPR listeners."[3]

## Coverage of insect consumption

In July 2023 NPR published a podcast episode titled "This right wing conspiracy theory about eating bugs is about as racist as you think," whose premise was that proponents of the theory lean on an antisemitic trope that "global elites" plot to control the masses — under the guise of climate change solutions — by forcing them to eat bugs.[4] Badlands contributor Ashe in America noted that NPR itself had promoted insect eating in 2016, 2017 and 2018, and argued that it was neither racist nor conspiratorial to observe that governments, corporations, pseudo-governmental entities, academics, NGOs and charitable organizations collaborate to push a shift away from animal protein.[4]

The commentary was prompted by [[tyson-foods|Tyson Foods]]' October 2023 partnership with the Dutch insect protein firm [[protix|Protix]], and asked rhetorically whether NPR would apply the antisemitism charge to the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] or apologize, concluding that the "taxpayer-funded gaslight" would simply continue.[4]

## 2023 layoffs

In March 2023 NPR carried out the [[2023-npr-layoffs|2023 NPR layoffs]], cutting 84 positions and halting production on four seasonal podcasts including *Invisibilia*, *Louder Than a Riot* and *Rough Translation*, after warning in February of a projected $30 million sponsorship shortfall.[5] Thirteen digital-team roles were also slated for elimination, but those employees had unionised through a separate broadcast union and could not be laid off until a contract or separate agreement was reached.[5]

The cuts triggered multiple tense all-hands meetings in which affected staff questioned executives; laid-off employees were given 30 days to transition their work but barred from NPR offices, so the meetings were held over Zoom, with some executives dialling in from home and speaking in scripted or vague terms.[5] Commenting in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] derided NPR's correspondents as sounding "freakishly unamused" regardless of subject, called the network "basically a cultural weapon at this point," and said he was thrilled to see it losing staff.[5]

## Signalgate commentary

In December 2025 commentary on the Signalgate report, Badlands host CannCon highlighted that NPR chief executive Katherine Maher simultaneously chairs the board of the Signal Foundation, suggesting the overlap warranted scrutiny of the messaging platform's adoption by US officials.[6]
