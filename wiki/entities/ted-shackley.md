# Ted Shackley

Theodore "Ted" Shackley was the CIA officer who ran the agency's Miami station, overseeing operations under the [[jmwave]] codename.[1] Under his leadership from 1962 to 1965, JMWAVE became the largest CIA station in the world outside Langley, with hundreds of professional operatives and an estimated 15,000 anti-Castro Cuban exiles on its payroll.[1] The Miami-based Cuban brigade commanded by [[manuel-artime]], of which [[felix-rodriguez]] was a member, operated within his area of responsibility.[1]

Shackley later went to Vietnam, where he oversaw the [[phoenix-program]], and was a central figure in the [[secret-war-in-laos]], where [[thomas-clines]] served as his deputy.[1]

In 1983 the Australian Joint Task Force on Drug Trafficking released a report on the [[nugan-hand-bank]] to Parliament stating that Shackley, [[richard-secord]], Clines, [[rafael-quintero]] and [[edwin-wilson]] were people whose background "is relevant to a proper understanding of the activities of the Nugan Hand group and the people associated with that group."[1] Joe Lange notes that none of these names appeared in media reporting on the bank's collapse, attributing the omission to their status as protected "untouchables."[1]
