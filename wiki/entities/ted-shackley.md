# Ted Shackley

Theodore "Ted" Shackley was the CIA officer who ran the agency's Miami station, overseeing operations under the [[jmwave]] codename.[1] Under his leadership from 1962 to 1965, JMWAVE became the largest CIA station in the world outside Langley, with hundreds of professional operatives and an estimated 15,000 anti-Castro Cuban exiles on its payroll.[1] The Miami-based Cuban brigade commanded by [[manuel-artime]], of which [[felix-rodriguez]] was a member, operated within his area of responsibility.[1]

Shackley later went to Vietnam, where he oversaw the [[phoenix-program]], and was a central figure in the [[secret-war-in-laos]], where [[thomas-clines]] served as his deputy.[1]

In 1983 the Australian Joint Task Force on Drug Trafficking released a report on the [[nugan-hand-bank]] to Parliament stating that Shackley, [[richard-secord]], Clines, [[rafael-quintero]] and [[edwin-wilson]] were people whose background "is relevant to a proper understanding of the activities of the Nugan Hand group and the people associated with that group."[1] Joe Lange notes that none of these names appeared in media reporting on the bank's collapse, attributing the omission to their status as protected "untouchables."[1]

## Career and the Bush connection

Theodore George "Ted" Shackley, Jr. (July 16, 1927 – December 9, 2002) was an American CIA officer involved in many important and controversial operations during the 1960s and 1970s and one of the most decorated officers in the agency's history; owing to his "light hair and mysterious ways" colleagues called him "the Blond Ghost."[2] In the early 1960s his work included serving as station chief in Miami during the Cuban Missile Crisis and directing the Cuban Project, also known as Operation Mongoose.[2] He was said to be director of the [[phoenix-program]] during the Vietnam War, CIA station chief in Laos between 1966 and 1968, and Saigon station chief from 1968 through February 1972.[2] In 1976 he was appointed Associate Deputy Director for Operations, second in charge of CIA covert operations.[2]

Joe Lange stresses that the Miami station Shackley ran was the hub of the CIA's relationship with a specially trained force of Cuban-American operatives — not only [[brigade-2506]] but also Operation 40, in which [[felix-rodriguez|Felix Rodriguez]] was one of the leaders.[2] In Vietnam Shackley directed the Phoenix Program while Rodriguez trained the [[provincial-reconnaissance-units|Provincial Reconnaissance Units]] and flew covert missions, with [[donald-gregg|Donald Gregg]] as Rodriguez's supervisor.[2] As station chief in Laos, Lange writes, Shackley presided over a [[secret-war-in-laos|secret war]] fought to protect and expand the agency's global heroin trade, with Rodriguez flying missions there at the same time.[2]

[[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] made Shackley the number-two man at the CIA on becoming director in 1976, and later, as [[ronald-reagan|Reagan]]'s Vice President, made Gregg his National Security Advisor — a pattern Lange presents as evidence that Shackley, Gregg and Rodriguez formed a trio of [[shadow-government]] figures with direct ties to Bush, linked to the Miami station during the [[bay-of-pigs|Bay of Pigs]], the Cuban Missile Crisis and the JFK assassination.[2]
