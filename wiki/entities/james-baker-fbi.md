# James Baker (FBI general counsel)

James Baker is a former general counsel of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] who occupied a senior position inside the bureau's leadership in May 2017, when discussions about removing President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from office are alleged to have taken place.[1] He is a distinct figure from the Republican statesman of the same name; within Badlands Media commentary he appears principally as a congressional witness whose testimony corroborated the account that senior Justice Department and FBI officials seriously entertained recording and removing the president.[1]

## Testimony on the "wear a wire" discussions

The July 2025 Badlands article "Shall We Play a Game?" by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]], citing The Hill, reports that Baker did not accept the later characterisation of the discussions about secretly recording Trump as a joke.[1] Baker held what The Hill called "quite the vantage point," having been inside the bureau's leadership in May 2017 when the conversations occurred, in the days after Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] was fired.[1] According to the account, Baker told Congress that his boss — then-acting FBI Director [[andrew-mccabe|Andrew McCabe]] — was "dead serious" about the idea of surreptitiously recording the 45th president and using the resulting evidence to argue that he should be removed from office.[1]

Lange presents Baker's testimony as one of two independent corroborations of the New York Times report of September 21, 2018 that Deputy Attorney General [[rod-rosenstein|Rod Rosenstein]] had proposed wearing a wire and had discussed invoking the [[twenty-fifth-amendment|25th Amendment]], the other being the account of former FBI lawyer [[lisa-page|Lisa Page]].[1] Because Rosenstein never denied that the meetings had occurred — only that he had been serious — Lange argues that the testimony establishes that meetings to discuss removing a sitting president took place at the top of the [[us-department-of-justice|Justice Department]].[1]

## Testimony on the appointment of Mueller

Q drop 2323 of October 3, 2018, quoted in the article, asks whether Baker had "just testified behind closed doors" that Rosenstein and Comey coordinated to appoint [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] as special counsel, and greets the prospect with "What a wonderful day."[1] Lange treats this closed-door testimony as evidence that the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller special counsel investigation]] was not an organic legal response but a coordinated placement, and that Baker's cooperation was part of a wider pattern of lower-ranking officials turning on their superiors as [[declas|DECLAS]] disclosures increased legal pressure.[1]
