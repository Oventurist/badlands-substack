# Atlantic Council

The **Atlantic Council** is a Washington-based foreign policy think tank that Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] describes as the single most relevant institution for understanding the Western establishment's posture toward [[iran|Iran]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) In his account it is "not unfair to say" that the Council is the "brains behind [[nato|NATO]]."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Character and description

The Council is characterized as an influential and "extremely militaristic" influence network staffed with military officers, [[cia|CIA]] personnel and their attendant politicians, together with chief executives from strategic industries such as oil and media, with strong connections to NATO and a central place in the modern military-industrial congressional complex.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) It is also frequently described as a "civil society institution," a label DeLarme criticizes: while "civil society" is popularly imagined as a third-party, oppositional force standing apart from government and big business, in practice the membership of such institutions is drawn from precisely those two worlds.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Funding

According to the article, the Atlantic Council is funded by the U.S. [[state-department|State Department]], the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], [[usaid|USAID]], the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]], a litany of other U.S. government agencies, and several United Kingdom government agencies.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) It is additionally funded by nearly every major Western energy firm — [[chevron|Chevron]], Exxon, British Petroleum and Royal Dutch Shell all donate and all had a vested commercial interest in the Iran deal.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) The Council's board has included at least seven different former CIA directors.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Role in the Iran question

DeLarme presents the Atlantic Council as the institutional embodiment of what he calls the "foreign policy establishment": the venue where the heads of industry, finance, military, intelligence and politics convene.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Its energy-sector funders, he argues, treated Iran as a much larger version of Ukraine, where Chevron had signed $10 billion agreements with the state gas company Naftogaz; through their donations they could lobby the State Department, the CIA and the Pentagon for policies that would open Iran to Western capital.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) These were the interests that fought hardest to keep the United States inside the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|JCPOA]], and that reacted against Israel when the [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] government broke with the Atlanticist consensus by backing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## GEC funding of the Digital Forensic Research Lab

According to revelations from the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]], the State Department's [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]] funded groups including the Atlantic Council's [[digital-forensic-research-lab|Digital Forensic Research Lab]], which compiled blacklists of Twitter accounts supposedly tied to foreign disinformation campaigns.[2] The lab sent those blacklists to Twitter so that the company could deactivate the accounts listed.[2]

## Role in the Transition Integrity Project

[[mike-benz|Mike Benz]] identified the Atlantic Council, alongside [[john-podesta|John Podesta]], as a leader of the [[transition-integrity-project|Transition Integrity Project]], the non-governmental effort that wargamed responses to the 2020 U.S. election outcome.[3]

## Moldova program

During the height of blowback over the Moldovan banking scandal in 2016, the Moldovan oligarch [[vlad-plahotniuc|Vlad Plahotniuc]] was a guest of the pro-NATO Atlantic Council in Washington, where he met with [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]] and other members of the Obama administration; on returning home he moved speedily to take full control of the [[democratic-party-of-moldova|Democratic Party]].[5] The 'Atlantic Council Moldova Program' also advanced a steady stream of pro-NATO commentary funded by Jashi's Trans Oil Group, on whose board sat former U.S. Ambassador [[asif-chaudhry|Asif Chaudhry]].[5]
