# Election Systems & Software

Election Systems & Software (ES&S) is described in the June 9, 2026 *Badlands Brief* as a "US voting equipment powerhouse" whose forced divestiture of a competitor created [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] as a national player.[1]

In 2010, the Obama-era [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] under Attorney General [[eric-holder|Eric Holder]] required ES&S to divest [[premier-election-solutions|Premier Election Solutions]], the former Diebold Inc. subsidiary that ES&S had purchased just six months earlier.[1] The DOJ order required ES&S to divest "all of the intellectual property associated with all versions — past, present, and in development — of the Premier voting equipment systems to another company," and to "grant a fully paid-up, irrevocable, perpetual license to use the AutoMARK, ES&S's ballot marking device," including the "right to modify and improve both Premier products and the AutoMARK."[1]

The department further specified that "ES&S must sell the divestiture assets to a buyer approved by the department," and required ES&S to grant the divestiture buyer an opportunity to compete for services to Premier customers then under contract with ES&S.[1] The approved buyer was Dominion Voting Systems.[1] Badlands presents this government-directed transfer as a pivotal step in the consolidation of American election infrastructure and in the propagation of [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]]-derived intellectual property through the market.[1]
