# Mario Ruiz Massieu

Mario Ruiz Massieu was a Mexican prosecutor and the brother of assassinated PRI official [[jose-francisco-ruiz-massieu|José Francisco Ruiz Massieu]]. Appointed late in his term by President [[carlos-salinas|Carlos Salinas]] as special prosecutor to investigate the September 1994 assassination, he resigned a few months later claiming ruling-party power brokers were obstructing him. Mexican investigators later declared they had uncovered evidence that he had altered witnesses' statements to delete numerous references to [[raul-salinas|Raúl Salinas]]'s role in the slaying — an accusation he denied.[1]

The article frames the central puzzle as why a man would falsify evidence to protect his brother's killer, and answers it with money: the payments came from the [[gulf-cartel|Gulf Cartel]], to which the [[salinas-family|Salinas family]] was connected.[1]

He was arrested in Newark, New Jersey, in March 1994 while attempting to board a flight to Spain carrying $46,000 in undeclared cash, and was held under house arrest there despite repeated Mexican efforts in U.S. courts to have him returned to Mexico City for trial.[1] Between December 1993 and February 1995 he made an initial $40,000 deposit and 25 further cash deposits of $98,000 to $800,000 at [[texas-commerce-bank|Texas Commerce Bank]] in Houston, the money carried in cardboard boxes and suitcases on commercial flights by his bagman Jorge Stergios, who remains a fugitive. The U.S. Justice Department contended the $9.9 million so accumulated consisted of bribes paid by drug traffickers in exchange for immunity from prosecution; Ruiz Massieu insisted it was family money or "bonuses" from the Mexican presidency, and held a further $3 million in Mexican accounts.[1] In 1997 a federal judge and jury in Houston confiscated $9 million from the Texas account as proceeds of the illicit drug trade.[1]
