# Mario Ruiz Massieu

Mario Ruiz Massieu was a Mexican prosecutor and the brother of assassinated PRI official [[jose-francisco-ruiz-massieu|José Francisco Ruiz Massieu]]. His conduct is the hinge of the Badlands Media article's argument, connecting Mexican cartel money to the Bush family's Houston bank.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

Late in his term, President Carlos Salinas appointed Mario as special prosecutor to investigate his brother's assassination. A few months later he resigned, saying his investigation was being blocked by ruling-party power brokers. Mexican investigators afterward declared they had uncovered evidence that he had altered witnesses' statements to delete numerous references to [[raul-salinas|Raúl Salinas]]'s role in the slaying — an accusation he denied.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The article's explanation is money: he was paid by the [[gulf-cartel|Gulf Cartel]] to shield the man who had his brother killed, and the appointment worked precisely because nobody would question a verdict from the victim's brother.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

He was arrested in Newark, New Jersey in March 1994 while attempting to board a flight to Spain, carrying $46,000 in undeclared cash, and was held under house arrest despite Mexico's repeated efforts in U.S. courts to extradite him.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) In 1997 a federal judge and jury in Houston confiscated $9 million from his Texas bank account, concluding the funds were proceeds of the illicit drug trade.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

The account itself was at [[texas-commerce-bank|Texas Commerce Bank]] in Houston. Beginning with $40,000 in December 1993 and continuing through February 1995, he made 25 further cash deposits of between $98,000 and $800,000, carried from Mexico in cardboard boxes and suitcases on commercial flights by his bagman Jorge Stergios, who remained at large.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The U.S. Justice Department contended the $9.9 million consisted of bribes paid by drug traffickers in exchange for immunity from prosecution; Ruiz Massieu insisted it was family money or "bonuses" paid by the Mexican presidency, and held a further $3 million in Mexican accounts.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)
