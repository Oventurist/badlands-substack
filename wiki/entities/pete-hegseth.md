# Pete Hegseth

Pete Hegseth is the U.S. Secretary of Defense in the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration, associated with efforts to place the American defense industrial base on what he has termed a "wartime footing."[1]

## Industrial mobilization

In the context of [[ford-motor-company|Ford Motor Company]]'s May 2026 announcement that it was exploring military applications for its commercial vehicles, Hegseth described the administration's broader push — including discussions with General Motors and Oshkosh about converting idle civilian capacity to defense output — as shifting the industrial base to a wartime footing, citing stockpiles depleted by conflicts across Eurasia.[1]

His department, the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] (referred to in some reports as the Department of War), also announced in the same period that it was pausing U.S. participation in the [[permanent-joint-board-on-defense|Permanent Joint Board on Defense]] with Canada.[1]

## Role in the March 2026 Iran campaign

As Secretary of War during the March 2026 US–Israeli strikes on [[iran|Iran]], Pete Hegseth said the United States did not intend to deploy ground troops in Iran but declined to rule it out if necessary.[2] He described the campaign, known in Badlands commentary as [[operation-epic-fury|Operation Epic Fury]], as focused on military objectives rather than governance change.[2]

## Media leaks task force (2026)

In July 2026, Hegseth announced that the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] had established a [[doj-pentagon-media-leaks-task-force|joint task force]] to identify and prosecute unauthorized disclosures of sensitive information to the news media.[3] In a video posted to [[x-platform|X]], he said he had delegated authority to the [[department-of-war|War Department]]'s Office of General Counsel to lead media leak investigations, empowering that office to request and receive all information, records and support across the department related to those investigations.[3]

## Europe force posture review (July 2026)

As Defense Secretary, Hegseth described European allies' refusal to provide U.S. base access for the campaign against [[iran|Iran]] as "shameful."[4] The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] under his leadership had already scaled back some U.S. forces in the region and launched a six-month review of the American military presence in Europe, a review that coincided with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s refusal to rule out further reductions ahead of the Ankara [[nato|NATO]] summit.[4]

## NATO collective defense (March 2026)

On March 31, 2026 Hegseth declined to reaffirm the United States' commitment to [[nato|NATO]]'s collective-defense clause, saying that the decision rested with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[5] The refusal came days before Trump said he was "strongly considering" withdrawing from the alliance.[5]

## Strike on Tren de Aragua leadership (June 2026)

In June 2026 Hegseth said a U.S. military strike had been carried out earlier that week against a [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] compound in [[venezuela|Venezuela]], killing the gang's leader [[hector-guerrero-flores|Héctor Rusthenford Guerrero Flores]], known as "Niño Guerrero."[6] Hegseth described the operation as evidence of cooperation between the United States and Venezuela against what he called narco-terrorist groups, and Venezuela's government confirmed its participation, saying clashes in Bolívar state resulted in Guerrero Flores' death.[6] The strike formed part of a broader administration campaign against groups linked to drug trafficking, in which AP reported at least 207 people had been killed since September.[6]

## AUKUS undersea drone announcement (June 2026)
Speaking in Singapore, War Secretary Hegseth announced that the United States, United Kingdom and Australia would jointly develop new undersea drones under the [[aukus|AUKUS]] defense pact.[7] He described the project as the first "Pillar Two" initiative focused on uncrewed undersea vehicles, intended to deliver "highly adaptable multi-mission UUV payloads" and maintain maritime advantage.[7] Hegseth said the United States remained committed to the pact — following earlier concerns over a [[pentagon|Pentagon]] review that eased when President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] reaffirmed support — and was moving quickly to expand submarine capabilities in the Pacific.[7] See [[aukus-undersea-drone-program|AUKUS undersea drone program]].[7]


## Censure of Mark Kelly

Hegseth formally censured Arizona Senator and retired Navy Capt. [[mark-kelly|Mark Kelly]] and launched [[pentagon|Pentagon]] proceedings that could demote Kelly's retired rank and reduce his military pension.[8] The action followed the November 2025 [[seditious-six|Seditious Six]] video urging service members to refuse "illegal orders."[8] Hegseth characterized Kelly's participation as "reckless misconduct" and "seditious," placed a letter of censure in Kelly's permanent military file, and initiated a retirement grade review.[8]

## Censure of Senator Mark Kelly (January 2026)

On January 5, 2026, Hegseth announced administrative actions against Senator [[mark-kelly|Mark Kelly]], a retired Navy captain and former astronaut, consisting of a formal letter of censure placed permanently in Kelly's military record for alleged "seditious statements" and "reckless misconduct," and the initiation of a 45-day retirement grade determination that could reduce Kelly's retired rank and retirement pay.[9]

Hegseth said the actions were based on a pattern of public statements from June to December 2025 in which, he argued, Kelly characterized lawful military operations as illegal, and wrote that Kelly and five other members of Congress had "released a reckless and seditious video that was clearly intended to undermine good order and military discipline."[9] The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] maintained that retired officers remain subject to the [[uniform-code-of-military-justice|Uniform Code of Military Justice]] and that Kelly's status as a sitting senator does not exempt him.[9]

## Strikes on Iran (June 2026)

In June 2026 Hegseth said that U.S. strikes on Iranian surveillance, communications and air-defense facilities were intended both to degrade [[iran|Iran]]'s military capabilities and to strengthen Washington's negotiating position in stalled ceasefire talks.[10] The strikes followed the downing of a U.S. Apache helicopter near the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]], which Washington blamed on Iran, and preceded President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s warning that he would "bomb the shit out of them" if Tehran did not agree to a deal.[10]

## Welcoming DOGE to the Pentagon (February 2025)

In February 2025 Hegseth welcomed the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] to the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], where [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] confronted an accounting system that has never passed an audit and decades of rampant price gouging by defense contractors.[11] The same week, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] floated cutting the defense budget in half after things "settle down" with Russia and China — a sharp turn from Hegseth's comments earlier that day that "nobody can or should test the extent of America's willingness to invest in national security."[11] Defense policy experts described the department's $850 billion budget as in sore need of oversight.[11]

## June 2025 Ukraine aid cut and Los Angeles litigation

Testifying before the [[house-appropriations-committee|House Appropriations Committee]] on June 10, 2025, Hegseth said the [[white-house|White House]] would slash military funding for Ukraine, stating plainly, "It is a reduction in this budget," and that "this administration takes a very different view of that conflict."[12] He tied the cut to the pursuit of a negotiated settlement, arguing that "a negotiated peaceful settlement is in the best interest of both parties and our nation's interests especially with all the competing interests around the globe," consistent with the administration's "America First" pivot away from foreign assistance.[12]

The same day, Hegseth was named alongside President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] as a defendant in Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]]'s suit seeking a temporary restraining order against the federalization of the National Guard and the deployment of Marines during the [[2025-los-angeles-immigration-enforcement-protests|Los Angeles unrest]].[12] Judge [[charles-breyer|Charles Breyer]] granted the administration's request for additional time to respond, deferring the TRO question.[12]

## Firing of the DIA director (August 2025)

In August 2025, Hegseth fired Lt. Gen. [[jeffrey-kruse|Jeffrey Kruse]], head of the [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]], whose agency's initial assessment of damage to Iranian nuclear sites from U.S. strikes had angered President Trump, according to two people familiar with the decision and a White House official.[13] That preliminary assessment, leaked to the media a few months earlier, found [[iran|Iran]]'s nuclear program had been set back only a few months, contradicting claims by Trump and [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[13] In a news conference following the June strikes, Hegseth had lambasted the press for what he called anti-military bias without offering direct evidence of the destruction of Iranian nuclear production facilities.[13] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] quipped that the headline should have read "Trump fires general."[13]

## Panama visit (April 2025)

On Tuesday, April 8, 2025, Hegseth met Panamanian President [[jose-raul-mulino|José Raúl Mulino]] for talks amid rising tension over global trade and alleged Chinese influence in the strategic [[panama-canal|Panama Canal]] waterway.[14] Afterward he used social media to hail "increased security cooperation" between the United States and Panama.[14] The visit came hours after Panama said it would call for an investigation into local executives of [[ck-hutchison|CK Hutchison]], the Hong Kong–listed firm that owns two ports at either end of the canal.[14]

## Boat strike "kill everybody" order allegation

A *Washington Post* report published on 28 November 2025 said that Hegseth had given a verbal order to kill everyone aboard boats suspected of smuggling drugs in the Caribbean, and that a military commander consequently carried out a second strike to kill survivors of an early-September attack.[15] The report prompted lawmakers of both parties to raise the possibility that a war crime had been committed, an episode covered further at [[caribbean-boat-strike-war-crime-allegations|Caribbean boat strike war crime allegations]].[15]

## April 2025 leak probe suspensions

On April 15, 2025 the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] placed two of Hegseth's top political appointees on administrative leave following a probe into potential leaks of sensitive information.[16] [[dan-caldwell|Dan Caldwell]], a senior adviser to Hegseth, was escorted out of the building by security officers and had his access suspended, while deputy chief of staff [[darin-selnick|Darin Selnick]] was suspended and escorted out in the same [[2025-pentagon-leak-investigation|investigation]].[16] The disclosures under review reportedly involved operational plans for the [[panama-canal|Panama Canal]], a second carrier headed to the Red Sea, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s Pentagon visit, and a pause in intelligence sharing with [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[16]

## Nigeria preparations (November 2025)

After President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] instructed the [[department-of-war|Department of War]] in a Truth Social post to prepare for possible military action in [[nigeria|Nigeria]] over the alleged killing of Christians by Islamist militants, Hegseth replied to the order on X, stating that his department "is preparing for action."[17]

## Pentagon press restrictions (October 2025)

Hegseth defended the [[pentagon-press-credentialing-policy-2025|new Pentagon press policy]] that took effect in October 2025, which conditioned credentials on journalists agreeing not to seek information unapproved by Pentagon officials, including unclassified material, saying the department "now has same rules as every U.S military installation."[18] Reporting noted that he had also falsely claimed reporters were previously allowed to roam the complex freely without badges.[18] Reporters from all major outlets turned in their badges and cleared out of the building on October 15 rather than sign; [[one-america-news-network|One America News Network]] was the only outlet to accept the terms.[18]

## Proposed Army force reduction (April 2025)

In April 2025 the [[united-states-army|U.S. Army]] was reported to be quietly weighing cuts of up to 90,000 active-duty soldiers, potentially reducing the force to as few as 360,000, as Hegseth pushed for an 8 percent cut to the Pentagon's budget.[19] The prospective reductions were described as consistent with defense leaders' aim of building a smaller, faster, more high-technology force, and accompanied a strategic shift away from Europe and counterterrorism toward the Pacific, where China is treated as the primary growing threat.[19]


## Caribbean strike on a Venezuelan drug vessel (September 2025)

On September 3, 2025, Hegseth issued a stark warning to drug traffickers as he described a US military strike that destroyed a Venezuelan drug boat in the Caribbean, killing 11 suspected [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] narco-terrorists.[20] "It's a new day. It's a different day, and… this is an activity the United States is not going to tolerate in our hemisphere," he said on "Fox & Friends."[20]

Venezuela's Minister of Communication and Information [[freddy-nanez|Freddy Ñáñez]] dismissed video of the attack as AI-generated, an accusation Hegseth rejected outright: "I can tell you that was definitely not artificial intelligence. I watched it live. We knew exactly who was in that boat. We know exactly what they were doing, and we knew exactly who they represented."[20] Hegseth declined to rule out regime change in Venezuela, saying it was "a presidential-level decision and we're prepared with every asset that the American military has."[20] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] dismissed the posture as "all so silly," while CannCon argued that the strike represented President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] legitimately protecting American citizens from foreign drug traffickers, a threat he characterized as greater to ordinary Americans than adversaries killed in Afghanistan or Iraq.[20]


## Insurrection Act Assessment (2025)

Hegseth, as Defense Secretary, was tasked together with Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] with assessing whether [[donald-trump|President Trump]] should invoke the [[insurrection-act|Insurrection Act of 1807]] at the southern border, with recommendations reported to be due in late April 2025.[21] He was also among the executive officials named as defendants in [[harvard-university|Harvard University]]'s suit over the federal funding freeze.[21]

## Pentagon pizza index remarks

In a Fox News interview aired Sunday, October 5, 2025, War Secretary Pete Hegseth addressed the [[pentagon-pizza-index|"pizza index"]] meme, which links spikes in pizza deliveries near the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] to imminent military operations.[22] "I'm aware of that account," said Hegseth, a former Fox News host, referring to the Pentagon Pizza Report X account.[22] Asked whether staff might simply use the cafeteria before an airstrike, he laughed and answered: "I haven't thought of just going to the cafeteria. I've thought of just ordering lots of pizza on random nights," suggesting deliberate deception of those tracking delivery patterns.[22]

## Global command overhaul (December 2025)

In December 2025 the Washington Post reported that the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] was drafting a sweeping overhaul of American military command structures that would downgrade several major headquarters and reshape the balance of influence among senior generals — a reshuffle driven by Hegseth in his capacity as Secretary of War and extending well beyond staffing and personnel changes.[23] The plan was described as being driven in large part by Hegseth's pledge to "break the status quo" and to reduce the number of four-star generals across the armed forces, alongside his long-stated aim of purging the "woke agenda" from within military ranks.[23]

Under the restructuring, [[us-central-command|US Central Command]], [[united-states-european-command|US European Command]] and [[us-africa-command|US Africa Command]] would be diminished in standing by being brought under a newly created [[us-international-command|US International Command]], while [[us-southern-command|US Southern Command]] and [[united-states-northern-command|US Northern Command]] would be placed under a new [[us-americas-command|US Americas Command]], reported as "Americom."[23] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued the overhaul suggests the earlier meeting of all military leaders convened by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was to some extent a purge, and read the international/domestic split as preparation for using the military at home against cartels.[23]

## Munitions acceleration drive

Hegseth co-led the June 2025 [[pentagon|Pentagon]] roundtable of top missile makers that launched the effort later known as the [[munitions-acceleration-council|Munitions Acceleration Council]], alongside Joint Chiefs Chairman General [[dan-caine|Dan Caine]].[24] The initiative presses defense contractors to double or quadruple missile output ahead of a potential conflict with [[china|China]], and Pentagon spokesman [[sean-parnell|Sean Parnell]] said President [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Hegseth were "exploring extraordinary avenues to expand our military might and accelerate the production of munitions."[24] Under shutdown contingency planning issued the same month, the defense secretary retains authority to designate additional activities as "excepted" during a lapse in appropriations.[24]

## Brussels remarks on European security (February 2025)

On Wednesday, February 12, 2025, Hegseth travelled to Brussels and told European allies to "take ownership of conventional security on the continent," a message CNN paired with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s same-day call with [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] as two "geopolitical thunderclaps" transforming transatlantic relations.[25] Although Hegseth recommitted to [[nato|NATO]], CNN judged that something fundamental had changed in the American posture toward Europe.[25]

Hegseth also made clear that Ukraine's hopes of regaining all of its lost land were unrealistic, a position CNN said pointed toward a settlement resembling the partition of Germany after World War II — Russian-occupied territory frozen under Moscow's control and the rest of Ukraine remaining a democracy behind a hard border — but without US troops to guarantee it.[25] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] used the episode to ask what the developments revealed about the history Americans were taught and the situation in Europe as it had been described to them.[25]

## Boat strike follow-up and fears of illegal orders

Reports that Hegseth ordered troops to "kill everybody" in a September 2025 boat strike, and that he authorized a follow-up strike, fed growing apprehension among service members that they might be asked to carry out an illegal order.[26] The concerns were reflected in an uptick in calls to the [[orders-project|Orders Project]], which provides free legal advice to military personnel, from staff officers involved in planning strikes on supposed drug-carrying boats and from personnel responsible for designating those aboard as threats.[26] Although a reported classified Justice Department memo from the summer of 2025 preemptively argued that U.S. troops involved in the strikes would not be in legal jeopardy, service members appeared unusually concerned; Frank Rosenblatt, president of the National Institute of Military Justice, said the questions were new because the issue "never came up" during the global war on terror in Iraq or Afghanistan.[26]

## "Seditious Six" video and Scouting America (November 2025)

On November 25, 2025, Hegseth publicly denounced the [[seditious-six-video|"Seditious Six" video]] — in which six Democratic lawmakers urged troops to refuse illegal orders — as a "politically-motivated influence operation" that injected doubt into the armed forces, writing on X that the message "may seem harmless to civilians" but "carries a different weight inside the military."[27] He faulted the video for never naming a specific illegal order, for creating "ambiguity rather than clarity," for its "carefully scripted, legal-sounding language," and for reframing military obedience "around partisan distrust instead of established legal processes."[27] On the same day the video appeared, his [[department-of-war|Department of War]] issued [[dodi-8025-01|DoDI 8025.01, "DoD Internet Content Blocking"]], a coincidence of timing noted by Badlands commentators.[27]

Separately, Hegseth was reported to have circulated a memo — first reported by NPR — seeking to cut military ties with [[scouting-america|Scouting America]], accusing the organization of attacking boy-friendly spaces and of being "genderless" amid his displeasure with its diversity, equity and inclusion programming.[27] The group responded that it was "surprised and disappointed," noting the military's support for the Scouts since 1937.[27]

## Pentagon inspector general report on Signalgate

On December 4, 2025, the Pentagon inspector general released its long-awaited report on Hegseth's role in the Signalgate affair.[28] Investigators concluded that his use of the Signal messaging app to send updates about strikes in Yemen broke rules for handling sensitive information and risked putting troops in danger, even though he has the power to declassify such information as he sees fit.[28] Reporting on the release listed six takeaways: that he broke the rules, kept a very tight circle, offered up incomplete records, produced findings open to interpretation, failed to satisfy Democrats, and had changed his tune; analysts judged that nothing in it would doom Trump's most embattled Cabinet chief, though it could complicate his political future.[28]

## January 2025 Senate confirmation hearing

On January 14, 2025, Hegseth emerged from a tense Senate Armed Services Committee confirmation hearing with the support of the Republican Party intact, following weeks of scrutiny over his fitness and qualifications to lead the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]; a vote of the full [[united-states-senate|Senate]] was expected as soon as the following week.[29] In a hearing that stretched more than four hours, Democrats pressed Hegseth on numerous allegations of misconduct, his views about women in combat, and his ability to lead a department with three million employees and an $849 billion budget.[29] Hegseth claimed to have been the victim of a "smear campaign" focused on an accusation of sexual assault and episodes of drinking to excess, repeatedly answering senators' questions with the phrase "anonymous smears."[29]

Senator [[joni-ernst|Joni Ernst]] of Iowa, a veteran and sexual assault survivor widely seen as critical to his confirmation chances, appeared satisfied with his responses on the role of women in the military and sexual assault prevention, and devoted much of her questioning to shared agreement that the Pentagon should be audited.[29]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] carried special live coverage of the hearing.[29] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the misconduct allegations "were all they had," that the nearly five-hour hearing produced no surprises or shocking revelations, and predicted flatly that Hegseth would be confirmed.[29] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] dismissed the proceedings as a "shiny object," arguing that Uniparty Republicans use such hearings to present themselves as MAGA defenders despite having turned on [[donald-trump|Trump]] after 2020.[29]

## Firing of the Navy chief of staff

On Friday, October 3, 2025, Hegseth reportedly fired [[jon-harrison|Jon Harrison]], chief of staff to the Secretary of the Navy, who had helped carry out a reshuffle of the service's bureaucracy.[30] The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] gave no reason, telling Politico only that Harrison "will no longer serve as Chief of Staff to the Secretary of the Navy" and that "we are grateful for his service to the Department."[30] The dismissal followed administration warnings of major trimming of the federal government during the [[october-2025-government-shutdown|October 2025 government shutdown]] and came the same week the [[us-senate|Senate]] confirmed Navy Undersecretary Hung Cao.[30]

## National Defense Area prosecutions (2025)

As Defense Secretary, Hegseth praised the establishment of the restricted National Defense Area along the U.S.-Mexico border, emphasizing its role in reducing illegal crossings and warning during a visit earlier in 2025 that "when you cross illegally, you will be prosecuted to the fullest extent of the law."[31] In May 2025 a federal judge in New Mexico, Gregory B. Wormuth, dismissed charges against 98 illegal immigrants who had entered the zone on the ground that they were unaware it was military property, among roughly 400 misdemeanor charges filed over the area.[31]

## Operation Southern Spear

On Thursday, November 13, 2025, Hegseth announced [[operation-southern-spear|Operation Southern Spear]] in a post on X, a military operation against "narcoterrorists" launched amid ongoing tensions with Venezuela and strikes on alleged cartel vessels.[32] He wrote that the mission, "led by Joint Task Force Southern Spear and [[southcom|SOUTHCOM]]," would defend the homeland, remove narco-terrorists from the hemisphere, and secure the United States "from the drugs that are killing our people," adding that "the Western Hemisphere is America's neighborhood – and we will protect it."[32] Hegseth did not specify whether the operation would expand on the strikes against alleged cartel vessels in international waters of the Caribbean Sea, in which the United States had destroyed at least 20 boats and killed 80 people since September 2025.[32]

## Confirmation hearing (January 2025)

Pete Hegseth's hearing to become Secretary of Defense was the first of the second Trump administration's cabinet confirmation hearings, scheduled for 9:30 a.m. ET on Tuesday, January 14, 2025 and carried as a special event by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]].[33] A month earlier the top Pentagon nominee had suffered through mounting questions about his personal and professional life, but by the eve of the hearings Republicans were more confident than ever that Trump's personnel slate was back on track.[33]

## Counter-drone task force

In 2025, Hegseth announced the creation of a new Department of Defense task force to counter drone threats and keep U.S. airspace safe, saying the department wanted to address a growing threat and improve protection of people and equipment at home and overseas.[34] "We're moving fast – cutting through bureaucracy, consolidating resources, and empowering this task force with the utmost authority to outpace our adversaries," he said. "We will innovate, we will lead and we will win."[34] He directed Secretary of the Army [[dan-driscoll|Dan Driscoll]] to establish [[joint-interagency-task-force-401|Joint Interagency Task Force 401]], which a [[pentagon|Pentagon]] memo said would gather top talent from across agencies to address the threat posed by unmanned aircraft systems.[34]


## Follow-up strike controversy (December 2025)

At a cabinet meeting on Tuesday, December 2, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] defended War Secretary Hegseth amid heavy scrutiny of the administration's conduct in the campaign against alleged drug-trafficking boats in the Caribbean, which had killed more than 80 people.[35] Trump said that neither he nor Hegseth had known in advance about the second strike on a suspected drug vessel — a follow-up attack carried out by the U.S. military on September 2 after an initial strike did not kill everyone on board.[35]

## 2024 confirmation attacks

During the November 2024 confirmation cycle, Hegseth's attorney [[timothy-parlatore|Tim Parlatore]] told Breitbart News that a [[hegseth-sexual-assault-allegation|2017 sexual assault allegation]] against his client had been "completely investigated" at the time and that Hegseth is "completely and totally innocent," asserting that the accuser "was the aggressor."[36] Parlatore described the incident as following a California Federation of Republican Women conference in Monterey at which Hegseth had spoken, saying video and eyewitness accounts showed the accuser sober and Hegseth drunk, and that police who reviewed the footage considered charging her.[36]

Badlands Media characterized the allegation as the third narrative deployment against the nomination, following claims that Hegseth was merely "that Fox News weekend anchor" lacking requisite experience — countered by his verifiable service record and his appearance on [[shawn-ryan|Shawn Ryan]]'s podcast — and criticism of his Jerusalem Cross tattoo, which some critics compared to a swastika.[36] The outlet argued that resorting to a [[metoo-movement|Me Too]] attack so early signaled opponents had nothing substantive, and predicted he would be confirmed.[36]

## October–November 2025 actions

At the end of October 2025 internal War Department memos showed the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] directing every state and territory [[national-guard|National Guard]] to establish [[quick-reaction-force|"quick reaction" forces]] by January, trained and equipped for riots and civil unrest; the initiative followed an August directive from President [[donald-trump|Trump]] to War Secretary Hegseth.[37]

On Friday, October 31, 2025, Hegseth held talks with Chinese Defense Minister [[dong-jun|Dong Jun]] at a gathering of Association of Southeast Asian Nations defense chiefs in Kuala Lumpur, with no readout provided; the meeting followed Trump's summit with [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] in South Korea, where the two agreed to reduce trade tensions.[37]

Hegseth also ordered the military to assign dozens of attorneys to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] for temporary duty in Memphis and along the U.S.–Mexico border, with the Defense Department detailing [[judge-advocate-generals-corps|Judge Advocate General's Corps]] attorneys to the U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Columbia to serve as Special Assistant United States Attorneys filling critical vacancies.[37]

## Nomination as Secretary of Defense (November 2024)

On November 12, 2024, President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] selected Hegseth, then a Fox News weekend host, as his nominee for Secretary of Defense, prompting an immediate outcry from Democratic legislators and legacy media commentators.[38] Representative [[dan-goldman|Dan Goldman]] (D-NY) told CNN's "OutFront" that he was "shocked, truly," arguing the pick exemplified fears that Trump would "appoint unqualified loyalists to shape this government into his own personal thiefdom, and get revenge on generals."[38] Goldman said he appreciated Hegseth's service in the armed forces but that being a serviceman did not qualify him to lead the Department of Defense or to have access to nuclear weapons.[38] [[don-lemon|Don Lemon]] mocked the choice in a social media post asking, "The Fox News weekend host?"[38]

Badlands coverage by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that backlash against these critics was swift, emphasizing that Hegseth attended Princeton and Harvard and served in active combat with the Minnesota National Guard, and that by the following morning [[cnn|CNN]] had updated its description of him to "Fox News host and veteran."[38] Reviewing Hegseth's interviews, including one with [[shawn-ryan|Shawn Ryan]], she argued the pick made sense in the context of Trump's stated plan to streamline the federal bureaucracy within an eighteen-month window: Hegseth had a reputation in military circles for exposing bureaucratic waste, and while [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] were running the [[department-of-government-efficiency|Department of Government Efficiency]], success would require mission-aligned agency heads willing to demolish internal fiefdoms.[38]

## Reagan National Defense Forum keynote (December 2025)

Speaking on a Saturday in December 2025 at the Ronald Reagan Presidential Library, Defense Secretary Hegseth delivered the keynote address at the Reagan National Defense Forum and defended U.S. strikes on alleged drug cartel boats, saying President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has the power to take military action "as he sees fit" to defend the nation.[39] Hegseth dismissed criticism of the strikes, which had killed more than 80 people and faced intense scrutiny over concerns that they violated international law, arguing they were justified to protect Americans and likening the campaign to the war on terror that followed the September 11, 2001 attacks.[39] Badlands host [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded to Hegseth's framing of the question to the public by asking whether Americans were "allowed to say no," noting that "if we're not allowed to say no, you're not asking, you're pretending we chose something we had no say in."[39]

## Reaction to the 2024 Secretary of Defense nomination

When Hegseth was named Secretary of Defense during the November 2024 transition, [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that his initial reaction was "not immediate joy" and set out the mental hurdles he was trying to navigate while remaining open-minded.[42] He noted that Hegseth, then head of the pro-war lobbying group [[veterans-for-freedom|Veterans for Freedom]], had appeared with [[george-w-bush|George W.
