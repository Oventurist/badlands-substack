# Director of National Intelligence

The Director of National Intelligence (DNI) is the head of the United States intelligence community and the office to which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] nominated former Democratic congresswoman [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] at the start of his second term.[1]

## The 2025 Gabbard nomination

Gabbard's nomination was treated by Badlands Media as a test case of whether an anti-establishment figure could be seated atop the intelligence apparatus.[1] Her confirmation hearing opened in early February 2025 amid what the publication described as an intensifying smear campaign built on misrepresentation, with the outcome still uncertain at the time of writing.[1] Former [[cia|CIA]] director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]] was among the first public critics, and the neoconservative magazine [[national-review|National Review]] attacked the nomination from the right over the same weekend.[1]

Attack lines against the nominee centered on her sympathy for [[edward-snowden|Edward Snowden]], her criticism of [[section-702|Section 702]] warrantless surveillance, her commentary on how U.S. foreign policy contributed to the Russian invasion of Ukraine, and her 2017 meeting with [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]].[1]

## Institutional stakes

Badlands Media argued that the DNI post, together with the directorship of the FBI and the Health and Human Services secretaryship, formed a triad of appointments — Gabbard, [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]], and [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|RFK Jr.]] — chosen precisely because Trump and his voter base believed the agencies concerned were fundamentally broken and required deconstruction.[1] In this reading, a Gabbard DNI represented an existential threat to entrenched [[deep-state|deep state]] interests both inside and outside government, which explained the intensity of senatorial opposition.[1]

In her opening statement, Gabbard outlined the record of falsehoods and abuses committed by U.S. intelligence institutions against the American people, which the article presented as the core explanation for public loss of faith in those agencies.[1]
