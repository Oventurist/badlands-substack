# Jay-Z (Shawn Carter)

Shawn Carter, known professionally as Jay-Z, is a rapper and music-industry executive who appears in Badlands Media coverage of the alleged entertainment-industry abuse network surrounding [[sean-combs|Sean "Diddy" Combs]].[1]

## Allegations reported by Badlands

The singer Jaguar Wright, who has performed on studio recordings with rappers including Jay-Z, has claimed that there are likely thousands of abuse victims harmed by Carter and his wife, Beyoncé Knowles.[1] "That couple is nasty," Wright said, visibly shaking and breaking down in tears, adding that she was already counseling hundreds of victims and believed thousands more existed.[1] These are allegations reported by the Brief and are not established findings.[1]

## Kanye West episode

On November 18, 2016 — ten days after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was first elected president — [[kanye-west|Kanye West]] interrupted his own concert to endorse Trump and address Jay-Z directly: "It's a new world, Hillary. It's a new world, Barack. It's a new world, Jay-Z."[1] West added, "Hey, bro, Jay-Z! I know you have killers, please don't send them at my head. Just call me! Talk to me like a man."[1] The Brief also cites reporting from March 2024, with video evidence, describing Jay-Z arriving at a West concert in Los Angeles seeking a private meeting, and West ignoring him.[1]

## December 2024 civil lawsuit

On December 8, 2024, Jay-Z, then 55, denied sexual assault accusations after his name was added to a civil lawsuit alongside [[sean-combs|Sean "Diddy" Combs]] alleging that the two men drugged and raped a 13-year-old girl more than two decades earlier.[2] The amended complaint lists the rapper by his legal name, Shawn Corey Carter, alongside Combs, who had been criminally charged in September 2024 with racketeering, sex trafficking, and other offenses.[2] The case was first filed in October 2024 in the Southern District of New York, naming Combs as the only defendant.[2]

The plaintiff, identified only as "Jane Doe," alleges she was drugged and sexually assaulted by both men at an after-party following an MTV Video Music Awards night in 2000.[2] Carter responded on the evening of December 8, saying in a statement issued through Roc Nation that the plaintiff's attorney, [[tony-buzbee|Tony Buzbee]], had sent him a letter demanding a settlement, and accusing Buzbee of attempting to force a settlement given "the nature of these allegations and the public scrutiny."[2] Carter said he would pay nothing and would "expose" what he characterized as fraudulent claims in court.[2] "These allegations are so heinous in nature that I implore you to file a criminal complaint, not a civil one!!" he said, adding that anyone who would commit such a crime against a minor "should be locked away" and that "these alleged victims would deserve real justice if that were the case."[2]

Buzbee replied on X the same day, saying that since he had sent the letter on behalf of his client, Carter had "filed his frivolous case under a pseudonym" and had "tried to bully and harass" both the attorney and the plaintiff.[2]

Badlands commentary by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that civil rather than criminal suits were being filed against Carter because the discovery process in a civil suit is significantly broader and more invasive than in a criminal proceeding, owing to Bill of Rights protections, and that material collected in civil discovery can be used to open a criminal investigation or support indictments.[2]
