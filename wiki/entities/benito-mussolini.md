# Benito Mussolini

Benito Mussolini was the fascist dictator of Italy, and appears in Badlands reporting chiefly in connection with the origins of the term "fascism" and with Italian sponsorship of Zionist paramilitary groups in Mandate Palestine.[1]

## Fascism and Giovanni Gentile

Badlands commentary notes that the term "Fascism" was first coined in 1922 by [[giovanni-gentile|Giovanni Gentile]], who served as Mussolini's chief political philosopher and Minister of Public Education.[1] It was Gentile — not Mussolini, to whom the line is widely misattributed across the internet — who said that "Fascism should rightly be called 'Corporatism,' as it is the merger of corporate and government power."[1] The commentary invokes this history to reject the mainstream framing of fascism as an inherently right-wing doctrine.[1]

## Support for the Irgun and the Jerusalem Agreement

According to the same account, the [[irgun|Irgun]] was armed by Mussolini's regime and received combat training in Italy, particularly in the use of explosives, with Italian observers remarking on the aptitude of Polish-Russian Jews shaped by generations of hardship in the Pale of Settlement.[1] In 1940 Avraham Stern and the breakaway [[lehi|Lehi]] militia sent Mussolini the Jerusalem Agreement, a document swearing allegiance to Italian Fascism and offering the deep-water port of Haifa as an Italian port in exchange for recognition of Palestine as a Jewish state.[1] Hitler's correspondence with Mussolini acknowledged receiving a parallel proposal, which the Axis powers ultimately declined.[1]

## Wall Street financing and American promotion

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that Mussolini, like [[adolf-hitler|Hitler]], was never "his own man," and that the financing behind his regime did not come from the Bank of Italy.[2] Two years before the [[young-plan|Young Plan]] began, [[jp-morgan|JP Morgan]] had already given a $100 million loan to Mussolini's newly established fascist regime, with Democratic Party kingmaker [[thomas-lamont|Thomas Lamont]] playing the role in Wall Street's Italian operation that [[prescott-bush|Prescott Bush]] played in its German one.[2]

Enthusiasm for Il Duce extended beyond banking: [[time-magazine|Time]] publisher [[henry-luce|Henry Luce]] put Mussolini on the magazine's cover eight times between 1923 and 1943 while promoting fascism as the "economic miracle solution for America" in *Time*, *Fortune* and *Life*.[2] Many Americans traumatized by the depression that began in 1929 embraced the idea that an American fascism would put food on the table.[2] [[winston-churchill|Winston Churchill]] is likewise described by Ehret as a Mussolini admirer.[2]
