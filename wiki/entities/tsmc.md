# Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) is the Taiwanese chip-making giant that is the world's biggest maker of advanced semiconductors, a technology described as the lifeblood of the global economy.[1]

## $100 billion US investment

In early March 2025, TSMC announced plans to invest $100 billion in the United States after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened to impose tariffs on overseas-made chips.[1] The announcement brought the total the company has pledged to invest in the United States to $165 billion, which TSMC characterized as the "largest single foreign direct investment in US history."[1] The pledge followed Trump's accusations that [[taiwan|Taiwan]] had stolen the US chip industry and his threats to impose tariffs of up to 100 percent, as well as Taipei's own promises to increase investment in the United States.[1]

## Strategic and geopolitical framing

At his investment press conference, Trump referenced strategic defense considerations regarding Taiwanese semiconductors, called TSMC a monopoly, and argued that on-shoring insulated the United States from escalation between China and Taiwan.[1] Badlands commentator Burning Bright read the framing as potentially significant signal, asking whether Xi Jinping had in effect been "granted permission" and whether the situation amounted to a re-run of the Russia–Ukraine dynamic.[1] Trump also proposed eliminating the [[chips-and-science-act|CHIPS Act]] and applying the savings to deficit reduction, arguing the law was a giveaway that harmed rather than helped US chip manufacturing.[1]

## Third Arizona fab and CHIPS Act awards (April 2024)

In April 2024 TSMC, the world's largest contract chip maker, announced plans to build a third chip factory in Arizona after receiving $6.6 billion in grants and $5 billion in loans through US government subsidies, part of an effort led by the Biden administration to rebuild American semiconductor manufacturing.[2] The [[white-house|White House]] said the Department of Commerce had announced a preliminary agreement with the company to support construction of leading-edge facilities in the United States, and that the third Phoenix factory would raise TSMC's total Arizona investment to $65 billion while creating over 25,000 direct construction and manufacturing jobs plus thousands of indirect ones.[2]

US Commerce Secretary [[gina-raimondo|Gina Raimondo]] told reporters that "it's a national security problem that we don't manufacture any of the world's most sophisticated chips in the United States," and noted that the 2-nanometer chips the third plant will produce — forecast for production by the decade's end — will be crucial for the artificial intelligence chips powering the defense industry.[2] Bloomberg reported that the other two Arizona plants were expected to begin production in 2025 and 2028.[2]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the [[chips-and-science-act|CHIPS Act]] and its fallout is one of the most observable "Actuals" mapping onto [[devolution|Devolution]] theory and his own "Switch" economic theory, suggesting the Biden administration was a proxy ultimately benefiting the side of the Awakening.[2] He predicted the plants coming online from 2025 would generate tens of thousands of high-paying jobs and reignite a US manufacturing boom unseen in two generations, built on semiconductors — which he called the most traded commodity on the planet, even before their full direction toward the AI era and decentralized energy production.[2] He concluded there is "much more to the Arizona chip factories than meets the eye," with benefits to real GDP growth, national autonomy and cyber-warfare defense capability.[2]
