# American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC)

The American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC) is described as the foremost pro-Israel lobbying group in the United States, and is treated in coverage of American politics as an organization whose endorsements and financial backing carry decisive weight in congressional primaries and general elections.[1]

## Role in American political influence

Commentary reproduced in the Badlands News Brief argues that American public consciousness of Israel's influence over US politicians — described as effectively determining policymaking, elections, and political appointments — is "approaching the Rubicon," with dissatisfaction over that influence becoming increasingly vocal.[1] The same analysis lists the bombing of Iranian nuclear sites at the behest of Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], prolonged support for the war in Gaza, and the handling of the [[epstein-files|Epstein files]] as episodes in which Israeli influence was said to be visible across the second [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] — a pattern characterized as contradicting the America First platform on which [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] campaigned.[1]

## Break with Randy Fine

In July 2025, AIPAC removed Representative [[randy-fine|Randy Fine]] from its list of endorsed candidates after Fine publicly called for the starvation of the population of Gaza.[1] Fine, elected to Congress in 2025 and characterized as perhaps the chamber's most overt Zionist, had repeatedly posted on X expressing satisfaction at Palestinian suffering; the American Jewish Committee had publicly condemned his comments on July 23, 2025, a week before AIPAC acted, and Fine continued posting regardless.[1] Analysts framed AIPAC's decision as a lobby at a crossroads, forced to balance maintaining its influence against distancing itself from positions that inflame the public perception threatening it.[1]

## Related developments

The break with Fine coincided with reporting that House Republicans, historically the more reliably pro-Israel caucus, had grown more critical of Israel amid reports of starvation among Gaza civilians.[1]

## Foreign agent registration history

Badlands commentary describes AIPAC as the head of a foreign lobby that ultimately rules Washington, noting that in May 2024 the group released a video boasting that it had been donating to essentially every politician in Washington since the middle of the 20th century — an advertisement about "bipartisan" support for Israel that was read as bragging that it had owned them all.[2] In 1957 Senator [[stuart-symington|Stuart Symington]] attempted to bring legislation to the Senate floor that would have forced AIPAC to register as a foreign agent; he failed to secure support, though Senator [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]] backed the proposal.[2] As president, Kennedy maintained a contemptuous relationship with AIPAC and the Israeli government and likewise sought to have the committee registered as a foreign agent, believing it was working behind the scenes to subvert his pursuit of nuclear disarmament.[2] Symington later passed an amendment to the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty framework in the 1970s barring U.S. foreign aid to any government found in violation of the treaty, a provision that Badlands notes has yet to be enforced against Israel.[2]
