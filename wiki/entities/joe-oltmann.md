# Joe Oltmann

Joe Oltmann is a Colorado podcaster and businessman whose uncorroborated allegations of 2020 election rigging spawned a series of defamation lawsuits against conservative political figures who publicly repeated that narrative.[1] He is the originator of the claim that Dominion Voting Systems executive [[eric-coomer|Eric Coomer]] participated in a September 2020 conference call with Antifa-aligned activists, a claim that became the central factual dispute in Coomer's defamation litigation.[1]

## Testimony in the Coomer defamation trial

Oltmann testified before a Denver jury in June 2025 in the defamation trial brought by Coomer against [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]], [[mypillow|MyPillow]] and FrankSpeech.[1] Under examination he admitted in federal court that he had misled a judge about his inability to sit for questioning, declined to reveal the identities of those who facilitated his alleged discovery of election manipulation, and accused unnamed individuals of having "wiped" relevant information from the Internet.[1] Attorney Brad Kloewer pressed him on the growth of his audience, asking, "You certainly started to get a lot more viewers after you started talking about Eric Coomer (on your podcast), didn't you?" — to which Oltmann responded, "We had more listeners, yes."[1] The examination was frequently interrupted by bench conferences called by U.S. District Court Judge Nina Y. Wang.[1]

Oltmann is himself among the defendants in the broader set of defamation suits Coomer is pursuing; other defendants include media figures, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]]'s 2020 campaign, and Trump's lawyers who amplified Oltmann's unproven accusations.[1]

## The "Antifa call" dispute

For years, Colorado legacy media reported that the call story had been fabricated by Oltmann, on the reasoning that "Antifa isn't an org, they don't have calls, this never happened."[1] Until June 2025 there was no evidence that any [[antifa|Antifa]]-linked call occurred in September 2020 beyond Oltmann's own testimony and his hand-written notes.[1] New evidence introduced during the trial's second week — an affidavit describing a local activist/BLM/Antifa Zoom call in September 2020 — confirmed several points in Oltmann's notes while still disputing his account of Coomer's participation.[1] Badlands correspondent [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], reporting from the courtroom, emphasized that the affidavit did not prove Oltmann's story true, but that the factual landscape surrounding the call had become materially muddier for the jury, which serves as the finder of fact.[1]
