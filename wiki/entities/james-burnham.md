# James Burnham

James Burnham was [[leon-trotsky|Trotsky]]'s former personal assistant and, in Christopher Hitchens' phrase quoted by Matthew Ehret, "the intellectual founder of the neoconservative movement."[1]

Burnham was among the leading American Trotskyists — with Sidney Hook, Max Schachtman, [[albert-wohlstetter|Albert Wohlstetter]] and Irving Kristol — who abandoned Trotskyite socialism after Trotsky's 1940 assassination and adopted the rabidly right-wing paradigm that became [[neoconservatives|neo-conservatism]].[1] As [[cynthia-chung|Cynthia Chung]] has argued, Burnham saw the resolution to the Manichean problem of class struggle and Cold War in a one-world fascist government.[1]

Shortly before Trotsky's death, Burnham wrote an essay renouncing dialectical materialism in favor of what he regarded as the superior philosophy of [[bertrand-russell|Bertrand Russell]], thereby ensuring his rebirth as a neoconservative.[1] In his February 1940 piece "Science and Style," Burnham offered Trotsky a reading list "ranging from the work of the brilliant mathematicians and logicians of the middle of the last century to one climax in the monumental Principia Mathematica of Russell and Whitehead (the historic turning point in modern logic)," and onward to the scientists cooperating in the new Encyclopedia of Unified Science.[1]

Burnham was later one of the leading organizers, alongside Russell, Sidney Hook and Irving Kristol, of the CIA–MI6 funded [[congress-for-cultural-freedom|Congress for Cultural Freedom]] launched in 1949.[1]
