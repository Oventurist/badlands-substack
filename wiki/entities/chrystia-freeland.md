# Chrystia Freeland

Chrystia Freeland is a Canadian journalist and politician who served as Canada's Minister of Foreign Affairs, Minister of Finance and Deputy Prime Minister. She is the central subject of the Badlands Media essay A Re Alignment of World Systems by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], which treats her removal from the Canadian cabinet in September 2025 as a marker of a broader shift in global power relations.

## Departure from cabinet

On September 16, 2025, according to the source, the Prime Minister's Office in Ottawa released an announcement in which [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] thanked Freeland for her service as Finance Minister and Deputy Prime Minister while signalling the end of her political career, writing: "I have asked Chrystia to serve as Canada's new Representative for the Reconstruction of Ukraine." The analyst John Helmer, quoted in the article, wrote that "Freeland's ouster was so rushed, there was no time for her to explain what the hurry was in her departure, nor for Carney to prepare what Freeland would be doing as his special envoy to the Ukraine without any staff or diplomatic rank."

The article argues that Freeland's anti-China hawkishness had become incompatible with a new Canadian agenda of befriending China under Carney. It states that her 100% tariff on Chinese electric vehicles provoked retaliatory Chinese tariffs on Canadian canola products that cost Canadian farmers billions.

## Formation and career

Freeland is described in the source as a Rhodes Scholar whose formation at Oxford prepared her, in Ehret's account, to be a "regime change apostle devoted to unipolarism and eventual war with Russia and China." After leaving Oxford in 1993 she worked for the London *Economist*, the *Washington Post*, the *Financial Times*, the *Globe and Mail* and *Reuters*, where she served as editor-at-large — a period the article characterizes as an apprenticeship in "perception management." She is said to have then been positioned to play the role of Valerie Jarrett to Canada's "Barack Obama," Justin Trudeau.

The source states that Freeland maintained high-level contacts with Ukrainian, Russian and Western oligarchs including Viktor Pinchuk and Mikhail Khodorkovsky, and counted Larry Summers, George Soros and Al Gore among her close friends, bringing them into Canada during the reorganization of the Liberal Party between 2011 and 2014 under the banner of the [[canada-2020|Canada 2020]] think tank. Her entry into electoral politics was managed by fellow Rhodes Scholar [[bob-rae|Bob Rae]], who abdicated his seat to her.

## Family background

The article places heavy emphasis on Freeland's descent from a family of Ukrainian nationalists, and specifically on her grandfather [[michael-chomiak|Michael Chomiak]], described as a Nazi propagandist. It claims this émigré network was brought to Canada after the Second World War by Anglo-American intelligence and cultivated as a force with continuing ties to pro-Nazi Ukrainian counterparts.

## Writings on plutocracy

Freeland's 2011 Atlantic essay *The Rise of the New Global Elite* and her November 2013 New York Times piece *Plutocrats vs. Populists* are examined at length in the source. In the former she distinguished today's plutocrats from a hereditary elite who "grow rich in their sleep," warned that the real threat to the super-elite was that "inchoate public rage could cohere into a more concrete populist agenda," and concluded that "super-elites have two ways to survive: by suppressing dissent or by sharing their wealth." In the latter she praised plutocratic political power for offering "the tantalizing possibility of policy practiced at the highest professional level with none of the messiness and deal making and venality of traditional politics … a technocratic, data-based, objective search for solutions to our problems," and encouraged "smart, publicly minded technocrats" to go to work for plutocrats whose values they share.

Ehret reads these writings as dividing plutocrats into "good" ones aligned with a green depopulation agenda — Bill Gates, Warren Buffett, Soros — and "bad" ones such as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and, after October 2013, Viktor Yanukovych.
