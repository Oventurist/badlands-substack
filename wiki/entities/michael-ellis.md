# Michael Ellis

Michael Ellis is an American national security lawyer who was announced by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on February 3, 2025 as his pick for deputy director of the Central Intelligence Agency.[1] The deputy director post does not require Senate confirmation, which allowed Ellis to begin serving immediately, and CIA director [[john-ratcliffe|John Ratcliffe]] had pushed for the appointment.[1]

## Background

Ellis previously served as a top lawyer for former Representative [[devin-nunes|Devin Nunes]] of California during Nunes's tenure leading the House Intelligence Committee.[1] Mainstream accounts characterize his work there as an effort to delegitimize the investigation into potential ties between Russia and the 2016 Trump campaign; Badlands commentary frames the same episode differently, holding that Ellis was among those who provided Nunes with the intelligence reporting showing that Trump and his associates had been surveilled by U.S. intelligence agencies.[1]

Ellis subsequently moved to the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]] as its general counsel, a position he held only briefly before the Biden administration placed him on administrative leave.[1] His installation at the NSA was contentious: after repeated delays, acting Secretary of Defense [[christopher-miller|Chris Miller]] ordered that Ellis be "immediately installed" just four days before Joe Biden's inauguration, a move critics described as an eleventh-hour irregularity that the incoming Biden team should presume improper and reverse on day one.[1]

Critics at the time also viewed his elevation as a reward for his role in the administration's contested legal effort to stop former national security adviser [[john-bolton|John Bolton]] from publishing a book damaging to the president; an inspector general's report later concluded there had been no undue pressure by the Trump administration to install him.[1]

## Significance in Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] treated the 2025 appointment as significant precisely because of Ellis's history with the surveillance disclosures, writing that "Ellis now at the CIA is certainly interesting."[1] In the same commentary Herold characterized the role as CIA general counsel, reflecting reporting confusion at the time over the exact position.[1]
