# Mojtaba Khamenei

Mojtaba Khamenei is the figure identified in mid-2026 reporting as [[iran|Iran]]'s new Supreme Leader, successor to [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]].[1] In a July 2026 interview with documentary filmmaker Javad Mogouee, Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] said he has never met Mojtaba Khamenei in person and stated that only a small number of people have met him.[1]

The Badlands Brief seized on that admission, noting that on the same day Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitri Peskov]] said Vladimir Putin had no current plans to speak with the Ayatollah by telephone, despite no world leader having yet spoken with him.[1] The commentary asked repeatedly whether the figure is "even real," and speculated that the public story might be a device to unwind the narrative of the theocratic system associated with Iran.[1] The Brief referred to him throughout with the bracketed epithet "[gay] Ayatollah," reflecting claims circulating in its audience.[1]

## Trump's proposed meeting (June 2026)

In June 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he would "probably" meet Iran's supreme leader if doing so helped secure a nuclear deal, and indicated the supreme leader was directly involved in approving the ongoing talks.[2] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the figure Trump signaled he would sit down with is not "the old Ayatollah whose image had been frozen in amber for decades as the necessary villain of the narrative," but Mojtaba Khamenei, operating within a reconfigured leadership structure shaped by the strikes said to have removed his father and by the surviving IRGC commanders.[2] He noted that the New York Times had reported in advance that the man in question "looks and sounds nothing like" the leader the original playbook required to remain in place, attributing the change to the strikes Trump ordered at the start of the war.[2]

## Trump's June 4, 2026 remarks

On June 4, 2026 President [[donald-trump|Trump]] said he was not currently seeking a meeting with Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei but would consider one if a deal were reached, and that he would approach such a meeting respectfully.[3] Responding to a question from Fox News correspondent Peter Doocy, Trump began by saying he did not want to meet before immediately adding that he would be "honored" to do so, and noted that "some people have suggested it."[3] He also stated that Iran had already agreed not to obtain nuclear weapons, describing that commitment as the central issue in the ongoing discussions.[3]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the change "a remarkable shift in rhetoric in the past 48 hours," moving from a framing of the Ayatollah as a crazy religious zealot who must be destroyed to Trump's remark that "he's probably a professional; in some circles, he has a very good reputation."[3] He described the sequence as "a rebranding exercise at Warp Speed," in which Trump reframes the Supreme Leader as a reasonable actor and seeds the expectation of an eventual meeting and deal.[3] After months of exchanging disparaging memes and AI videos with the Iranian government over social media, the writer argued, Trump's promise of respect reinforced the notion that there would be no formal attempt to overthrow Iran's system of government and that peace would instead be made with its existing leaders.[3]

## Trump's CIA claim (March 2026)

In a March 2026 interview on Fox News' "The Five," President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the [[cia|CIA]] had told him that Mojtaba Khamenei is gay.[4] Trump acknowledged the agency had relayed the information but indicated uncertainty, noting that others had made similar claims, before turning to his support among gay voters in the 2024 election.[4] Reporting connected the claim to broader geopolitical narratives and to internal Iranian power dynamics.[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] observed that Trump was now calling the Ayatollah gay after weeks of publicly speculating whether he was even alive or real, given the absence of footage of him appearing in public, and noted that by that standard his father had also been missing for roughly a year.[4] He concluded that the sequence amounted to Trump trolling his audience, writing, "Let's see how much sillier they can make all of this."[4]

## Trump's posture toward a meeting

In June 2026 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] struck a conciliatory tone toward Iran's leadership, saying he was not currently seeking a meeting with Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei but would consider one if a deal were reached, and that he would approach such a meeting respectfully.[5] Trump also stated that [[iran|Iran]] had already agreed not to obtain nuclear weapons, describing that commitment as the central issue in the ongoing discussions.[5]

## March 2026 disappearance and the "Cardboard Ayatollah"

Khamenei was appointed supreme leader on March 9, 2026, following the death of his father, [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]], who was killed in a U.S.- and Israeli-backed airstrike in Tehran on February 28, according to official accounts of the succession process.[6] As of reporting on March 21 he had not appeared publicly or released any verified audio or video statements; a March 12 address attributed to him was delivered by a state television anchor rather than by Khamenei himself, and subsequent communications were issued as written statements or broadcast substitutes.[6] The absence extended to key events including the Persian New Year, when he did not deliver the traditional Nowruz speech and instead released a written message.[6] U.S. intelligence agencies, including the [[cia|CIA]], monitored for indications of his activity and assessed that he is likely alive but operating under heightened security constraints, contributing to uncertainty about his visibility and role.[6]

The New York Post reported that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had been briefed that the new ayatollah was gay, prompting White House journalists to ask Trump about it in the Oval Office, where he replied "Well, nobody knows…" while appearing to hold back laughter.[6] After a video circulated showing a cardboard cutout of Khamenei on stage at a pro-government rally, the internet anointed him the [[cardboard-ayatollah|"Cardboard Ayatollah"]], launching a viral meme campaign.[6] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the elder Khamenei had likely died of cancer the previous summer and that after [[ali-larijani|Ali Larijani]] implemented a [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] plan in August, the office of supreme leader was removed from the chain of command and reduced to an irrelevant figurehead.[6]

## Selection as supreme leader (March 2026)

Iran's clerical leadership selected Mojtaba Khamenei as supreme leader following the death of his father, [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]], during the opening phase of the war involving [[iran|Iran]], [[israel|Israel]] and the United States; the [[assembly-of-experts|Assembly of Experts]] announced the decision after deliberations among senior clerics.[7] The elder Khamenei was killed in a U.S.–Israeli strike that helped trigger the broader regional conflict, after which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] demanded Iran's "unconditional surrender" and said the United States should have a role in determining the country's future leadership.[7]

In a phone interview with CNN's [[dana-bash|Dana Bash]], Trump affirmed he would be willing to accept a "religious leader" rather than a democratically elected one if that leader were friendly to the United States.[7] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that just days after Trump had called on the Iranian people to rise up against the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]], he was signaling willingness to accept Khamenei's son as long as the new leader was friendly to the US and Israel.[7] Rather than backpedaling, he read this as Trump using "chaotic plot twists and narrative whiplash" to prevent rival actors — above all [[reza-pahlavi|Reza Pahlavi]] and his neoconservative backers — from imposing their own agenda on Iran's succession.[7]

## Announced as supreme leader, March 2026

In March 2026, [[iran|Iran]] announced that Mojtaba Khamenei, son of the previous supreme leader, was its new leader.[8] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], speaking to CBS News from his golf club in Doral, Florida, said he had no message for him and that he had someone else in mind to lead the country.[8]
