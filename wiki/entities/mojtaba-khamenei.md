# Mojtaba Khamenei

Mojtaba Khamenei was appointed [[iran|Iran]]'s supreme leader on March 9, 2026, following the death of his father [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]], who was killed in a U.S.- and Israeli-backed airstrike in Tehran on February 28, according to official accounts of the succession process.[1]

## Absence from public view

Since assuming the role, Khamenei has not appeared publicly or released any verified audio or video statements, according to reporting as of March 21, 2026.[1] A March 12 address attributed to him was delivered by a state television anchor rather than by Khamenei himself, and subsequent communications were issued as written statements or broadcast substitutes.[1] The absence extended to key events: he did not deliver the traditional Nowruz speech for the Persian New Year, releasing a written message instead.[1] U.S. intelligence agencies, including the [[cia|CIA]], monitored for indications of his activity and assess that he is likely alive but operating under heightened security constraints, contributing to uncertainty about his visibility and role.[1]

## Media furor

The New York Post reported that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had been briefed that the new Ayatollah was gay, prompting White House journalists to ask Trump about it in the Oval Office; Trump answered "Well, nobody knows…" while apparently suppressing laughter.[1] The internet then anointed Mojtaba the [[cardboard-ayatollah|Cardboard Ayatollah]] after a video circulated showing a cardboard cutout of him on stage at a pro-government rally, launching a viral meme campaign.[1]

Commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], who has long speculated that the former Ayatollah died of natural causes (cancer) around the time of the Twelve Day War in mid-2025, calls the affair "one of the better" psyops of the Persian adventure.[1] He adds that once [[ali-larijani|Ali Larijani]] of the national supreme security council implemented a [[continuity-of-government|Continuity of Government]] plan in August 2025, the Ayatollah was removed from the chain of command and became "an irrelevant figure head" — leaving open the question of whether Mojtaba actually leads Iran at all.[1]
