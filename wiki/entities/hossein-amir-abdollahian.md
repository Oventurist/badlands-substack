# Hossein Amir-Abdollahian

Hossein Amir-Abdollahian was the Foreign Minister of the Islamic Republic of Iran who was killed alongside President [[ebrahim-raisi|Ebrahim Raisi]] in a helicopter crash in Iran's remote northwest on Sunday, May 19, 2024.[1]

Amir-Abdollahian and Raisi were returning from a ceremony for the opening of a dam on Iran's border with Azerbaijan when the aircraft went down, according to Iranian state media.[1] Among the others on board were three crew members, the governor of Eastern Azerbaijan province, an imam, the president's head of security, and a bodyguard, per the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]]-run outlet Sepah.[1]

As foreign minister, Amir-Abdollahian had been a principal voice in Iran's escalating confrontation with Israel in the months preceding his death, a period in which regional tensions were described as being at a dangerous high.[1] His death, together with that of Raisi, was expected to compound political uncertainty in a country already under significant economic and political strain.[1]

## November 2023 Gaza diplomacy

On Sunday, November 5, 2023, Amir-Abdollahian sent separate letters to his counterparts in the five [[brics|BRICS]] member states — Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa — urging them to intervene "in an active, constructive and responsible manner" to stop Israel's war crimes against Palestinians in the besieged Gaza Strip and to "hold the occupying aggressors to account."[2] In the letters he expressed "deep concern and sorrow" over the treatment of the people of Gaza.[2] The appeal followed the start of the Israeli bombardment on October 7, which Iranian state media framed as a response to Operation al-Aqsa Storm launched by Palestinian resistance groups.[2]

The following day, Monday, November 6, the foreign minister claimed that the United States was desperately seeking an end to the fighting despite Israel being nowhere near its stated aim of dismantling [[hamas|Hamas]], telling state television that "the Americans sent us a message in the past three days saying they are seeking a ceasefire."[2] The remarks accompanied an Iranian warning that Americans would be "hit hard" if a Gaza ceasefire were not reached, and coincided with [[cia|CIA]] Director [[william-burns|William Burns]]'s secretive regional trip.[2]
