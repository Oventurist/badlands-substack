# Bob Casey

Bob Casey is a Democratic United States Senator from Pennsylvania.[1]

## 2024 re-election campaign

Casey, who was seeking re-election in 2024, appeared alongside Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and Pennsylvania Governor [[josh-shapiro|Josh Shapiro]] at a union hall event in Pittsburgh on Labor Day, 2 September 2024.[1] The event accompanied the pair's attendance at Pittsburgh's Labor Day parade in the battleground state, and drew local and national leaders from major unions including the International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, the [[afl-cio|A.F.L.-C.I.O.]] and the United Steelworkers.[1] It marked the first time Harris and Biden had shared a speaking slot on the campaign trail since the election shakeup six weeks earlier.[1]

## Distancing from the Harris ticket

By late October 2024, Casey was among the Senate Democrats in "blue wall" states reported to be running away from Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] in hopes of winning over some of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s voters to hold their seats.[2] The Pennsylvania incumbent went as far as embracing Trump's tariff policies: a campaign ad launched the previous week described him as "independent" and touted how he "bucked" the Biden administration to protect fracking and "sided with Trump to end NAFTA."[2]

Berwood Yost, director of the Center for Public Opinion Research at Franklin & Marshall College, observed that "There's no party affiliation in Casey ads. I don't recall seeing any that say 'Democrat' or anything like that. He's running as an incumbent on his own record," adding that Casey was "trying to distance himself a little bit from an administration that is viewed negatively by the most part."[2] Badlands read the pattern as evidence both that "Trump Hate" had failed as a platform and that Trump's America-first policies were popular enough for down-ballot Democrats to adopt them, undercutting the claim that the United States is a 50/50 country.[2]

## Defeat and recount (November 2024)

Casey lost his re-election bid to Republican [[dave-mccormick|Dave McCormick]], though the margin was narrow enough that the [[2024-pennsylvania-senate-race|2024 Pennsylvania Senate race]] moved to a legally required statewide recount.[3] Unofficial returns published by the [[pennsylvania-department-of-state|Pennsylvania Department of State]] gave Casey 3,350,972 votes, or 48.50 percent, against McCormick's 3,380,310 votes, or 48.93 percent.[3] Secretary of the Commonwealth Al Schmidt announced the recount trigger on November 13, 2024, with counties required to complete the count by noon on November 26, 2024.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the race should have been called on election night and that the refusal to concede amounted to election denial, while welcoming the recount as a source of election-integrity data.[3]
