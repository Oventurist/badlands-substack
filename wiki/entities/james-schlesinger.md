# James Schlesinger

James Schlesinger was an American official described in Badlands Media commentary as "an important Trilateral Commission ideologue" who moved through a rapid succession of the most powerful national-security and energy posts in the United States government during the 1970s.[1] He served as Director of the Central Intelligence Agency in 1973, as Secretary of Defense from 1973 to 1976, and then as the first head of the Department of Energy from 1977 to 1979.[1]

## Role in the nuclear moratorium

In his tenure at the Department of Energy, Schlesinger is credited with overseeing the cancellation of nearly all nuclear projects that had been planned for the 1980s.[1] Writer [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] situates this within a broader pattern in which, under Presidents Nixon, Ford and Carter, not a single new US nuclear construction was permitted between 1977 and 2013, forcing the United States and Canada into an effective nuclear moratorium.[1] The account frames this not as an accident of markets or regulation but as the deliberate imposition of "artificial scarcity" onto a formerly pro-industrial-growth United States by a neo-Malthusian technocratic class.[1]

## The Political Economy of National Security

Ehret argues that the reason Schlesinger was selected for this assignment can be read directly from his 1960 book *The Political Economy of National Security*, in which Schlesinger defined economics as "the science of choice in a world of limited resources."[1] In the passage quoted, Schlesinger wrote that "we have gone around the world spreading the 'gospel of plenty' raising the level of expectations … [but] in the nature of things, these rising expectations can never be satisfied," and concluded that "we must in our strategic policy return to the days before the Industrial Revolution … [and] prepare to fight limited wars."[1]

For Ehret this statement is the ideological core of the post-industrial turn in US policy: a closed-system, zero-sum conception of economics in which scarcity management, population control and limited war replace scientific and industrial progress as the organizing principles of statecraft.[1]

## Context

Schlesinger is presented alongside Canada's [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]] as one of two kindred figures who dismantled North American nuclear capability from within government, and within the wider network of [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] agents who, in this telling, "took over the reigns of government in the 1970s" and shattered the culture of progress in nuclear fission and fusion, space technology and post-colonial industrial development.[1] The same argument links these cancellations to the collapse of NASA funding from roughly 4% of GDP per year in 1965 to under 1% by 1975, and to [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]]'s efforts to keep the world addicted to hydrocarbons under the petrodollar system.[1]
