# Robert Rubin

Robert Rubin is an American financier and government official who served as Secretary of the Treasury under President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] and later became a senior executive at [[citigroup|Citigroup]]. In Badlands Media's reporting he is presented as the clearest single embodiment of the revolving door between the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] and [[citibank|Citibank]].[1]

## Pressure to repeal Glass-Steagall

In February 1995, as a newly appointed Treasury Secretary, Rubin — together with his deputy Bo Cutter and senior advisers including [[john-podesta|John Podesta]] — gave President Clinton three days to decide whether to back repeal of the [[glass-steagall|Glass-Steagall Act]].[1] Cutter described the maneuver as "an action forcing event," writing to Clinton on 21 February that Rubin wanted the policy announced before the House banking committee took the matter up on 1 March.[1] Documents later released from the Clinton presidential library revealed two separate attempts, in 1995 and 1997, to hurry Clinton into supporting repeal and to allow investment banks, insurers and retail banks to merge.[1]

## The Treasury-to-Citibank pipeline

Badlands Media argues that Citibank had been recruiting leaders out of the Treasury Department for generations, so Rubin's move to lead Citibank after leaving the Clinton administration was the predictable end of a "direct pipeline" rather than a coincidence.[1] The deregulation Rubin pushed culminated in the [[financial-services-modernization-act|Financial Services Modernization Act]] of 1999, which gave retrospective clearance to the 1998 merger of Citicorp and the [[travelers-group|Travelers Group]] and unleashed a wave of Wall Street consolidation later blamed for forcing taxpayers to bail out the enlarged banks after the sub-prime mortgage crisis.[1]
