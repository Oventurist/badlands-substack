# David Rockefeller

David Rockefeller was an American banker whose name recurs in Badlands Media's account of the origins of the modern environmental and depopulation movement.[1] Citing historian [[f-william-engdahl|F. William Engdahl]], [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that in 1968 David Rockefeller founded the neo-Malthusian think tank the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]] along with [[aurelio-peccei|Aurelio Peccei]] and [[alexander-king|Alexander King]].[1]

## Networks

Engdahl's account, reproduced by Ehret, ties Rockefeller to the Italian industrial elite: Peccei was a senior manager of Fiat, owned by the Agnelli family, and Fiat's Gianni Agnelli sat on the International Advisory Committee of Rockefeller's Chase Manhattan Bank.[1] Agnelli and Rockefeller had been close friends since 1957, and Agnelli became a founding member of Rockefeller's [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] in 1973.[1] The same passage notes that Alexander King, head of the [[oecd|OECD]] Science Program, was also a consultant to [[nato|NATO]].[1]

## Significance in the depopulation thesis

In Ehret's framing, Rockefeller's sponsorship of the Club of Rome links the post-1968 Malthusian revival directly to Anglo-American financial power, and situates the later [[limits-to-growth|*Limits to Growth*]] study and the [[green-new-deal|Green New Deal]] within a continuous banker-directed program rather than a grassroots ecological awakening.[1]

## Trilateral Commission and Operation Cyclone

Badlands Media's retrospective on [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]] states that Rockefeller, together with [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]] and with the blessing of the [[bilderberg]] Group, founded the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] — a body created to coordinate imperialist strategy between Washington, Western Europe and Japan.2 Brzezinski is described in the same account as a "Rockefeller-acolyte" and the quintessential high-ranking deep-state operative.2

## Harvard control grid

"The Harvard Control Grid" describes Harvard's ties to the Rockefeller dynasty as "overwhelming," listing [[amy-falls|Amy Falls]]'s management of [[rockefeller-university|Rockefeller University]]'s endowment under David Rockefeller's oversight, [[martin-escobari|Martín Escobari]]'s seat on the advisory board of the [[david-rockefeller-center-for-latin-american-studies|David Rockefeller Center for Latin American Studies]], and [[rick-slocum|Rick Slocum]]'s management of [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller Foundation]] investments.[3] The article credits Rockefeller with founding the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] and the Rockefeller family with helping to found the [[council-on-foreign-relations|CFR]] and early global development pipelines including the UN and WHO.[3]

## Founding of the Trilateral Commission

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 2" places Rockefeller at the center of the creation of the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]]: by the late 1960s, as chairman of the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]], he reportedly grew concerned that the CFR had become too well known to the American public, and instigated a slightly more public offshoot organization that would formally bring Japan into the globalist fold.[4] The intellectual proposal originated with [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]], but it was Rockefeller who carried it to the [[bilderberg-group|Bilderberg group]] meeting at Knokke-Heist, Belgium, in April 1972, where it was reportedly met with enthusiasm.[4]

Organizing meetings began on July 23–24, 1972 at Kykuit, Rockefeller's 3,500-acre estate at Pocantico Hills in Tarrytown, New York, with the founding participants hand-selected by Rockefeller and Brzezinski.[4] The Commission was formally founded on July 1, 1973 with Rockefeller as chairman and Brzezinski as founding North American director.[4] Researchers Anthony C. Sutton and Patrick M. Wood, in *Trilaterals Over Washington*, described him as then chairman of the "ultrapowerful" Chase Manhattan Bank, a director of many major multinational corporations and endowment funds, and "a central figure in the mysterious Council on Foreign Relations."[4]

Rockefeller was also reported to be the source of the instruction that led [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]] to appoint [[paul-volcker|Paul Volcker]] to head the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[4] Answering critics in a 1980 edition of the *Wall Street Journal*, Rockefeller wrote that "far from being a coterie of international conspirators with designs on covertly ruling the world, the Trilateral Commission is, in reality, a group of concerned citizens interested in fostering greater understanding and cooperation among international allies."[4] The article observes that the ties binding American political leadership to both the CFR and the Trilateral Commission — with Rockefeller a leading luminary in each — have fueled conspiracy writing on both the Left and the Right.[4]

## Education and wartime service

"The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 5" traces Rockefeller's formation: after a B.S. from Harvard he enrolled at the London School of Economics, a school heavily funded by the [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller Foundation]], the Carnegie United Kingdom Trust Fund and the widow of J. P. Morgan's partner Willard Dickerman Straight, where he encountered the teachings of John Ruskin and socialists such as Harold Laski, who wrote that the state is "the fundamental instrument of society."[5] Returning home, he displayed his attachment to England in an April 1941 letter to the *New York Times* declaring that "we should stand by the British Empire to the limit and at any cost."[5] Just before the war he took a doctorate at the University of Chicago with a thesis entitled "Unused Resources and Economic Waste," in which he wrote that "of all forms of waste, however, that which is most abhorrent is idleness."[5]

He entered the U.S. Army as a private but soon moved to the [[office-of-strategic-services|Office of Strategic Services]] in North Africa and France, acquiring both a taste for foreign affairs and the high-level intelligence contacts that later served him in the [[council-on-foreign-relations|CFR]], the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]] and the [[bilderberg-group|Bilderberg Group]].[5]

## Rockefeller Institute and banking

By 1948 Rockefeller chaired the board of trustees of the [[rockefeller-institute|Rockefeller Institute]], whose president was the biophysicist [[detlev-bronk|Detlev Wulf Bronk]].[5] He joined the Chase National Bank of New York after the war under his uncle Winthrop Aldrich, played the major role in the 1955 merger that created [[chase-manhattan-bank|Chase Manhattan Bank]], and became board chairman and chief executive of Chase Manhattan Corp. in 1969.[5] He had been elected vice president of the Council on Foreign Relations as early as 1950.[5] His generation of brothers was the product of the union between the son of [[john-d-rockefeller|John D. Rockefeller]] and the daughter of [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson Aldrich]] of Federal Reserve and Jekyll Island fame.[5]

## Influence and death

Gary Allen recorded that in 1973 alone Rockefeller met 27 heads of state, including the rulers of Russia and Red China, and Ralph F. Epperson observed that he had never been elected or appointed to any position entitling him to represent the American people.[5] He maintained a friendship of more than fifty years with Lord [[jacob-rothschild|Jacob Rothschild]], the fourth Baron Rothschild.[5] He died in 2017 at the age of 101 with a Forbes-assessed fortune of $3.3 billion.[5] He was the youngest of the five brothers and became the most powerful.[5]
