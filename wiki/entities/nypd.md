# New York City Police Department

The **New York City Police Department (NYPD)** is the municipal police force of New York City. In March 2024 the department was thrust into the national presidential campaign after officer [[jonathan-diller|Jonathan Diller]] was murdered by a lifelong criminal during a routine traffic stop in Queens.[1]

Former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] attended Diller's wake, an appearance that drew a contrast in coverage with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s participation the same day in a $500,000-per-ticket celebrity fundraiser.[1] The episode became a recurring reference point in commentary about crime, policing and the optics of the 2024 campaign.[1]

## Pro-Palestinian airport caravan protest

During the [[pro-palestinian-airport-caravan-protest|pro-Palestinian caravan protest]] that snarled traffic around John F. Kennedy International and LaGuardia airports at the turn of 2024, demonstrators with a bullhorn chanted "NYPD, KKK, IDF they're all the same!"[2] Video posted to social media showed dozens of vehicles waving Palestinian flags, honking horns, and shouting at police officers, and protest organizers said the airport was "swarming with all kinds of law enforcement" and that organizers were "forcibly removed from Terminal 4."[2]

## Arrest of Jonathan Majors (2023)

On the morning of March 25, 2023, the department arrested actor [[jonathan-majors|Jonathan Majors]], then 33, following a 911 call from an apartment in the Chelsea neighborhood of Manhattan in what police described as an alleged domestic dispute.[3] In a statement the NYPD said the victim, a 30-year-old woman, reported being assaulted and had minor injuries to her head and neck, and that Majors faced charges of strangulation, assault and harassment.[3] An attorney for Majors said the actor was "completely innocent" and expected all charges to be dropped.[3]

## Met Gala protest arrests (2024)

On May 6, 2024, the NYPD began mass arrests after more than 1,000 pro-Palestine demonstrators marched through upper Manhattan toward the Metropolitan Museum of Art, which was hosting the Met Gala.[4] Officers intervened at the East 79th Street Transverse at Central Park as protesters moved down Fifth Avenue and blocked traffic, with one officer overheard telling his partner, "This is an exercise in futility at this point. There's nowhere for them to go."[4] See the [[2024-met-gala-pro-palestine-protest|2024 Met Gala pro-Palestine protest]].[4] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] contrasted the department's rapid response at the gala with what she described as its tolerance of comparable protest activity directed at ordinary Americans.[4]

## Investigation into the killing of Brian Thompson (December 2024)

On December 4, 2024, the department opened a manhunt after [[unitedhealthcare]] chief executive [[brian-thompson]] was shot dead outside a Hilton hotel in midtown Manhattan.[5] Police commissioner [[jessica-tisch]] told reporters the shooting was a "brazen, targeted attack" that did "not appear to be a random act of violence," adding that "every indication is that this was a premeditated, pre-planned targeted attack."[5] Chief of detectives Joseph Kenny said Thompson was found on the sidewalk with gunshot wounds to his back and leg and was pronounced dead at Mount Sinai hospital at 7:12 a.m.[5] Investigators reconstructed the sequence from surveillance video showing the masked gunman arriving on foot about five minutes before the victim and ignoring other pedestrians.[5] See [[killing-of-brian-thompson]].[5]

## Arrest in the UnitedHealthcare CEO shooting (December 2024)

At a news conference on Monday, December 9, 2024, NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch identified [[luigi-mangione|Luigi Mangione]], 26, as the person of interest arrested in Altoona, Pennsylvania, at a McDonald's in connection with the [[killing-of-brian-thompson|shooting death of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson]].[6] Chief of Detectives Joseph Kenny said Mangione was born and raised in Maryland, had ties to San Francisco, and listed a last known address in Honolulu, Hawaii, and that the department would work through extradition with the Manhattan district attorney's office while backtracking his movements from New York to Altoona.[6] NYPD officers and Manhattan prosecutors said they would interview him further.[6]
