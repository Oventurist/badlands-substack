# Anglo-Iranian Oil Company

The Anglo-Iranian Oil Company (AIOC), originally founded as the Anglo-Persian Oil Company (APOC), was the British enterprise that controlled Iranian petroleum for the first half of the twentieth century and the corporate ancestor of BP.[1]

## Origins

In the 1890s reports began to circulate speculating that an enormous oil belt lay beneath the Persian Gulf, running north into Iran. That speculation fueled an already intense rivalry between Britain and Russia, both of which saw Persia as critical to their imperial economic interests. Russia's geographic adjacency made its interest self-evident; for Britain, Persia represented the land bridge to India, where the East India Company — which had by then transformed into British intelligence, in the account given by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] — had established an empire increasingly threatened by rising nationalism.[1]

In 1901 [[william-knox-darcy|William Knox D'Arcy]] negotiated a petroleum concession with Mozzafar Al-Din, the Shah of Persia. The D'Arcy Concession granted the exclusive right to prospect for oil, which was finally discovered at the Masjed Soleiman field in 1908 — the first oil discovery in the Middle East, predating Iraq. D'Arcy formed the Anglo-Persian Oil Company to begin extraction, marking the first enterprise of its kind in the region. In 1914 the British government purchased 51% of the company, effectively nationalizing it.[1]

## Nationalization and aftermath

In 1950 [[mohammad-mosaddegh|Mohammad Mosaddegh]] won election in Iran on a promise to nationalize the oil industry and reclaim the country's natural resources from Britain. AIOC was displaced by the [[national-iranian-oil-company|National Iranian Oil Company]] as Britain withdrew its personnel and attempted to sue Iran in international court; the case was dismissed. A worldwide embargo was launched against Iran, and in 1953 the [[1953-iranian-coup|CIA-orchestrated coup]] removed Mosaddegh.[1]

The restored [[mohammad-reza-pahlavi|Mohammad Reza Shah]] ratified the Consortium Agreement of 1954, restoring British ownership over the oil fields in exchange for lifting the sanctions crippling the Iranian people. AIOC was subsequently renamed British Petroleum, known today as BP.[1]
