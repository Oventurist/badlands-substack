# Dick Durbin

Dick Durbin is a Democratic United States Senator from Illinois and a longtime member of the Senate's Democratic leadership.[1]

## Opposition to the SAVE Act

During the March 2026 Senate debate over the SAVE Act, Durbin argued that the proof-of-citizenship requirement would impose real costs and delays on voters, claiming the measure would require expensive passports costing $186 and weeks of processing time, and that it would create burdens for married women who would need extra documentation to reconcile a changed surname.[1]

Bill sponsor [[mike-lee|Sen. Mike Lee]] rebutted these claims on the floor, pointing to free sworn attestations, state verification using existing records, and interstate reciprocity agreements, and insisting the process "will not cost them a dime."[1]

## Questioning at the Bondi Hearing (January 2025)

As ranking member of the Senate Judiciary Committee, Durbin pressed attorney general nominee [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] on January 15, 2025 to say whether [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] lost the 2020 election, a question she declined to answer directly.[2] Durbin also devoted questioning time to [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]], Trump's FBI director nominee, raising Patel's past commentary about [[qanon|QAnon]] conspiracy theories during a hearing nominally about Bondi.[2]


## Criticism of the Hernández pardon (December 2025)

As the top Democrat on the Senate Judiciary Committee, Durbin rebuked President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s December 2025 pardon of former Honduran president [[juan-orlando-hernandez|Juan Orlando Hernández]], who had been serving a 45-year sentence for conspiring to import tons of cocaine into the United States.[3] Durbin said the evidence presented at Hernández's trial had established that the former president "orchestrated a vast trafficking conspiracy" that raked in millions of dollars for drug cartels.[3] His criticism formed part of a broader Democratic argument that Trump was acting hypocritically in claiming to have escalated the fight against illicit drugs while freeing a man convicted of using his office to aid traffickers.[3]
