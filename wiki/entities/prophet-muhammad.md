# Prophet Muhammad

Muhammad, the founder of Islam, appears in the Badlands Media essay [[arabian-nights]] as the grandson of [[hashim-ibn-abd-manaf|Hashim]] and the figure who, in the article's words, "would forever change the world."

## Lineage and setting

He belonged to the [[banu-hashim]] within the tribe of [[quraysh]], which controlled Mecca. The source notes that at the time of the tribal map it reproduces for 600 AD, the site of modern Riyadh was not a place of significance. Orphaned as a child, Muhammad was taken in by the father of [[ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]], whom he in turn raised from the age of five.

The article records the tradition that Muhammad had red hair and a fair complexion, as did many of his kin, and states that he emerged from one of his routine meditative excursions into the caves around Mecca claiming that the archangel Gabriel — identified as the same being who visited Mary on the night of Jesus's birth — had brought him a message from God. The ten-year-old Ali believed him when no one else did.

## The Constitution of Medina and religious tolerance

The essay gives particular emphasis to the [[constitution-of-medina]], written by Muhammad in 622, which it describes as a formal agreement between his followers and the Jewish clans that had historically ruled Medina, establishing the city as a *haram* or sanctuary, prohibiting violence between clans and guaranteeing religious freedom for non-Muslims and specifically Jews. It states that Muhammad later established similar laws in Jerusalem, where Jews had been banned from worship by the ruling Christian authorities, and argues that this record defies the modern narrative about Islam and its rival religions — while noting that his successors, especially under [[wahhabism]], took a far less tolerant line.

## Conquests and succession

Muhammad's forces defeated the Banu Bakr, the Christian clan from which the [[house-of-saud]] descends, in 627. The source recounts that their emir, tied to a pole and threatened with execution, refused to renounce Christ and calmly recited a wisdom three times; recognising him as a God-fearing man, Muhammad released and embraced him as a brother, and the emir later converted of his own volition.

Muhammad died without dictating a line of succession. Most of his followers supported his companion [[abu-bakr]], inaugurating the [[rashidun-caliphate]]. His designation of Ali as his "brother, guardian and successor," and his use of the ambiguous term *Mawla*, became the root of the division between [[shia-islam|Shia]] and [[sunni-islam|Sunni]] Islam. The Quran, the source notes, credits Jesus with at least six miracles while clarifying that Muhammad performed none.

The essay's conclusion names Muhammad, along with Hashim and [[mohammed-bin-saud]], as one of the key Arabian figures it argues were men of honour and reason rather than ruthless ideologues.
