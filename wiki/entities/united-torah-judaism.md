# United Torah Judaism

United Torah Judaism is an Israeli ultra-Orthodox (Haredi) political party represented in the [[knesset|Knesset]]. Its chairman, [[yitzhak-goldknopf|Yitzhak Goldknopf]], is a Hasidic politician who has led the party's resistance to the extension of mandatory military conscription to yeshiva students.[1]

## Role in the conscription dispute

The party's central political priority is the preservation of the Haredi exemption from Israel Defense Forces service. In July 2025, after Israeli authorities detained several draft dodgers, Goldknopf warned on the party's behalf that the ultra-Orthodox community would leave Israel en masse rather than accept conscription, stating that "if there is no choice and the ultra-Orthodox have to leave the country, then they will leave the country."[1]

Goldknopf argued that Israel's manpower shortfall — described as a gap of some 50,000 soldiers — could instead be filled from the roughly 100,000 draft dodgers he claimed were living in Tel Aviv and the Gush Dan coastal region.[1]

## Significance

Commentary published by Badlands Media treated United Torah Judaism's emigration threat as a potential existential pressure point for the Israeli state, arguing that the departure of the Haredi community would erode the demographic base of the Zionist project, deepen economic strain, and accelerate the political dissolution of the state through attrition.[1] See [[haredi-conscription-exemption-law]].

## Departure from the Netanyahu coalition, July 2025

United Torah Judaism's legislators submitted their resignations on Monday, July 14, 2025, set to take effect within 48 hours, leaving the coalition of Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] over the failure to codify the yeshiva conscription exemption.[2] With only seven seats, the Ashkenazi ultra-Orthodox party was not large enough to topple the government on its own; its exit cut Netanyahu's majority in the 120-seat [[knesset]] from 68 seats to 61 — the bare minimum required for him to remain prime minister.[2]

The Sephardic Haredi party [[shas]], holding eleven seats, was openly threatening to follow, a step that would leave the coalition around 50 seats and force snap elections.[2] Observers noted that the departure of both Haredi factions would strip the coalition of its majority without automatically triggering elections, and that neither party clearly wanted to bring the government down; the Knesset's July 27 recess for a three-month break gave Netanyahu time to negotiate privately.[2] The core UTJ objection was that no exemption bill was likely to pass, and that the version drafted by Netanyahu's committee excluded the thousands of yeshiva students who had received draft letters since the [[israeli-supreme-court|Israeli Supreme Court]] ruling, exposing them to arrest as draft dodgers.[2]
