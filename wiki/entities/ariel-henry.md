# Ariel Henry

Ariel Henry is a Haitian politician who served as Prime Minister of [[haiti|Haiti]] and was the outgoing premier during the country's 2024 collapse of state authority.[1]

## 2024 crisis

By March 2024, Henry was outside the country, and gang leader [[jimmy-cherizier|Jimmy "Barbecue" Cherizier]] declared that one of his express goals was to prevent Henry's return to the Caribbean nation, alongside capturing Haiti's police chief and government ministers.[1] Badlands Media commentary described Henry as the "former PM" whose return Cherizier was committed to blocking, while nearly 1.5 million Haitians faced famine and gang attacks on power substations left much of Port-au-Prince without electricity.[1]

Former Prime Minister [[laurent-lamothe|Laurent Lamothe]] publicly called for the restoration of law and order during the same period.[1]

## Resignation, March 2024

Henry resigned as Haitian prime minister on Tuesday, March 12, 2024, amid a collapse of state authority in which armed gangs had taken over much of Port-au-Prince.[2] His departure came as violence spread beyond the capital and prompted an immediate American security response: the following day US Southern Command announced the deployment of the Marine Fleet Anti-terrorism Security Team to the US embassy at the State Department's request.[2] Badlands commentary described the sequence of assassinations, resignations and all-out gang war as amounting to a transfer of power in Port-au-Prince.[2]

## Resignation announcement (March 2024)

In a statement released early Tuesday, March 12, 2024, Henry announced he would resign as prime minister of Haiti, bowing to international pressure amid turmoil that had overwhelmed the country.[3] He agreed to leave office once a transitional presidential council was created and an interim prime minister named, saying, "The government that I'm running cannot remain insensitive in this situation. There is no sacrifice that is too big for our country," and, "The government I'm running will remove itself immediately after the installation of the council."[3] The announcement came hours after Caribbean leaders and US Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] met in Jamaica for an emergency session on Haiti's spiraling crisis, which had been worsened by violent gangs burning police stations, attacking the main airport and raiding two of the country's largest prisons; some experts described the situation as a low-scale civil war.[3]
