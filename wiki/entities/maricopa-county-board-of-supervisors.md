# Maricopa County Board of Supervisors

The Maricopa County Board of Supervisors is the governing body of [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]], Arizona, the jurisdiction containing the Phoenix metropolitan area. As the county's governing body it holds statutory responsibility for administering elections and for meeting to canvass — that is, officially certify — election returns.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## Role in the 2022 general election

During the [[2022-arizona-general-election|2022 Arizona general election]] the Board became a focus of national attention after widespread equipment failures on Election Day. Its chairman, [[bill-gates-arizona|Bill Gates]], responded by posting a weekend series of explanatory videos on the county's official @MaricopaCounty Twitter account, answering voter questions about how the county runs elections, including a video on the transport trucks that run between the county recorder's elections operation and its ballot-printing vendor.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Kitty Gillespie, writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], derided the video series as a comedy show she titled "Arizona Elections for Dummies with Mr. Bill," describing Gates's manner as "folksy, singsongy, friendly" and saying the videos made her stomach turn.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

The Board's certification obligation was the operative deadline in the dispute that followed. Under A.R.S. § 16-642(A), the governing body holding an election must meet and canvass the election not less than six nor more than twenty days after the election, placing Maricopa County's deadline on or before November 28, 2022.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Assistant Attorney General [[jennifer-wright|Jennifer Wright]] demanded a response to her office's inquiry before the county submitted that official canvass, explicitly tying the outstanding questions to the county's "ability to lawfully certify election results."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

Gillespie also highlighted subsection (C) of the same statute, which provides that if returns from any polling place are found to be missing at the time of the governing body's meeting, the canvass shall be postponed from day to day until all returns are received or until six postponements have occurred — a provision she suggested placed Arizona in "uncharted territory."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## Named as defendant in the Lake contest

All five members of the Board — [[bill-gates-arizona|Bill Gates]], Clint Hickman, Jack Sellers, Thomas Galvin and Steve Gallardo — were named as defendants in their official capacities in the 70-page [[kari-lake-election-contest-2022|election contest]] filed by [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] on December 9, 2022, as was the Board as a body.[2] Also named were [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]], County Recorder [[stephen-richer|Stephen Richer]] and elections director [[scott-jarrett|Scott Jarrett]].[2]

The complaint accused the Board of misrepresenting the scale of the Election Day breakdown. Against its claim that tabulation issues had been fixed by mid-afternoon, the suit alleged that no fewer than 34 vote centers suffered tabulator breakdowns after 3 p.m. and throughout the day, and that 59 percent of the county's 223 vote centers experienced printer and tabulator failures.[2] It further alleged that the Board tried to cover up how many vote centers had lines longer than ninety minutes, and disputed the county's claim that there were only 16,724 uncounted "[[door-3-ballots|Door 3]]" ballots, arguing there was good reason to believe the true number was far greater.[2] The suit also stated that the county added 25,000 ballots to its totals after Election Day.[2]
