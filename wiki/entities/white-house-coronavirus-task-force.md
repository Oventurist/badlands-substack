# White House Coronavirus Task Force

The White House Coronavirus Task Force was the body established in late January 2020 to coordinate the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's response to the COVID-19 outbreak, chaired by Vice President [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]].[1] Its staffing and internal disputes became a central battleground in the political fight over the pandemic, and in Badlands Media commentary the task force is treated as one of the channels through which the [[deep-state|Deep State]] is said to have operated inside the Trump White House.[1]

## Staffing and Olivia Troye

[[olivia-troye|Olivia Troye]], Pence's homeland security and counterterrorism adviser, served as an aide to the task force and joined it when it was first established in late January 2020, before any American had died of COVID-19.[1] She was Pence's top aide on the body and, after her departure in the summer of 2020, became the first task force staffer to go public against the president.[1] Erik Carlson's December 27, 2023 article "The Sabotage of Trump's Administration" treats her path — from a George W. Bush appointment at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] through the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] to the task force — as the signature career arc of a [[rino|RINO]] operative placed near the president.[1]

## Disputes over the pandemic response

Troye told [[new-yorker|The New Yorker]] in September 2020 that the administration's response had been "derailed by the person at the very top," that Trump could not focus and was consumed by his November prospects, and that she feared a vaccine would be rushed to save the election.[1] She said career civil servants on the task force were reviled as "deep staters" by Trump's political appointees, a suspicion that deepened after mid-level White House staffers — including Pence's national security aide Jennifer Williams, who worked in the office next to hers — testified in the December 2019 impeachment proceedings.[1]

Carlson reads the same events in reverse: he argues that Trump's stated priorities of finding the origin of COVID, fast-tracking a vaccine and reopening the country were legitimate, that Troye's characterisation of them as selfish electoral calculation was a political attack, and that the task force's institutional resistance to the [[lab-leak-theory|lab leak theory]] reflected a broader unwillingness across government to identify the virus's source.[1] He points to what he calls a change of heart after the 2020 election — opposition to a fast-tracked vaccine before November giving way to enthusiastic promotion afterward, with [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] receiving her first shot on December 29, 2020 — as evidence the objection was to Trump rather than to the vaccine.[1]
