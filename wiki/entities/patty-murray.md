# Patty Murray

**Patty Murray** is a Democratic United States Senator from Washington and the senior Democrat on the [[senate-appropriations-committee|Senate Appropriations Committee]].[1]

In March 2025 Murray co-signed a letter initiated by committee chairwoman [[susan-collins|Susan Collins]] to the [[white-house|White House]], asserting that the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] had violated the six-month spending law approved by [[us-congress|Congress]] by refusing to spend $2.9 billion in appropriated funds.[1] The bipartisan letter argued that the law requires the administration to spend all emergency money or none of it.[1] See [[2025-emergency-spending-impoundment-dispute|2025 emergency spending impoundment dispute]].[1]

## FY2025 funding and the March 14 deadline

In late February 2025, as Senate Appropriations Vice Chair, Murray acknowledged that another short-term funding bill — a [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]] — would likely be needed to extend the March 14 funding deadline and avoid a partial government shutdown, even though negotiations on a topline fiscal year 2025 spending agreement had progressed.[2] [[congress|Congress]] was at that point nearly five months behind in passing a final FY25 spending agreement and had relied on a series of CRs to keep the government funded.[2]

## Blocking of standalone Israel aid, November 2023

As chairwoman of the [[senate-appropriations-committee|Senate Appropriations Committee]], Murray led floor arguments on November 7, 2023 against a Republican bill providing standalone aid to Israel without accompanying Ukraine funding.[3] "Our allies in Ukraine can no more afford a delay than our allies in Israel," she said, adding that "there is strong support here in Congress to address these urgent priorities in one package—and that is exactly what I am working with colleagues on both sides of the aisle to do now."[3] Senate Democrats blocked the measure, producing the [[2023-israel-aid-bill-standoff|2023 Israel aid bill standoff]].[3]
