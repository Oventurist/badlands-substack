# Ignatius of Loyola

**Ignatius of Loyola** was the founder of the Society of Jesus, the Catholic religious order known as the [[jesuits|Jesuits]].[1]

The Badlands Media essay *Throne of the Ivy Grid* names Loyola as the originator of what it calls a legal and linguistic "alchemy" perfected by his order.[1] In the essay's framing, the Jesuits founded by Loyola operate not merely as missionaries but as "deep-code social engineers," exercising silent influence over world leaders, academic institutions, and intelligence agencies under later Superior Generals including Hans Kolvenbach and Adolfo Nicolás.[1]

The essay attributes to the Jesuit oath of obedience a doctrine of "mental reservation," described as a tactic of concealed truth cloaked in selective silence, and concludes that the order "is not a brotherhood of God. It is an order of architects."[1] Loyola's order is positioned within the essay's account of [[vatican|Vatican]] power as the technical arm linking canon law, the [[crown-temple|Crown Temple]] legal bars, and the [[vatican-bank|Institute for the Works of Religion]].[1]
