# Facebook

Facebook is the flagship social network operated by [[meta-platforms|Meta Platforms]] and led by [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]].[1] In the independent-media record it appears principally as a conduit through which the federal government sought to shape the American information environment.[1]

## Government pressure and the Facebook Files

Communications obtained by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] and released in 2023 as the [[facebook-files|"Facebook Files"]] documented efforts by the [[joe-biden|Biden]] [[white-house|White House]] to influence the platform's moderation and ranking decisions.[1] White House digital strategy director [[rob-flaherty|Rob Flaherty]] asked the company whether it could change its algorithm to make users more likely to see [[new-york-times|The New York Times]], The Wall Street Journal and other "authoritative" outlets rather than the [[daily-wire|Daily Wire]], Tomi Lahren and other figures he described as polarizing.[1]

Facebook did not surrender the records voluntarily; the committee secured them only after applying considerable pressure, including a threat to hold Zuckerberg in contempt of [[congress]].[1]

## Assessment

Critics argued the exchanges demonstrated that the platform functioned as an instrument of state speech control, allowing officials to circumvent [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] limits through a private company — the pattern described as the [[censorship-industrial-complex]].[1] Commentators at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] stressed that although the arrangement had been exposed, there was no evidence the practices had stopped.[1]

## 2023 nudity policy change

In January 2023 Facebook and its subsidiary [[instagram|Instagram]] modified their rules to allow transgender and non-binary users to post photographs and videos featuring their bare breasts, following a decision published on the [[meta-oversight-board|Meta Oversight Board]] website on January 17, 2023.[2] The same permission was not extended to cisgender women, who remained barred from posting such images; the board wrote that "the same image of female-presenting nipples would be prohibited if posted by a cisgender woman but permitted if posted by an individual self-identifying as non-binary."[2] [[badlands-media|Badlands]] contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] framed the change as cultural warfare and "soft eugenics" pursued through [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation warfare]] rather than overt coercion.[2]

In the same month, NBC News reported that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was preparing to return to Facebook as well as [[twitter|Twitter]].[2]

## Suppression of the Hunter Biden laptop story

Facebook played a key role in censoring the Hunter Biden laptop story before the 2020 election on the basis of what proved to be false claims that the reporting was a Russian disinformation operation; some observers argued that this suppression, together with a multitude of others, could have changed the outcome of the vote.[3] In late August 2024 [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] warned in his book [[save-america|*Save America*]] that Facebook founder [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] would "spend the rest of his life in prison" if election meddling were proven, writing that Zuckerberg had told him "there was nobody like Trump on Facebook" while nonetheless steering the platform against him.[3] Zuckerberg had apologized the previous week for caving to government censorship requests.[3]

## Censorship of the Trump assassination photograph

Facebook wrongly censored a photograph of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] raising his fist after surviving the July 2024 assassination attempt, a company official acknowledged.[5] Users who posted the image were told they had shared an "altered photo," with a notice stating that "independent fact-checkers reviewed a similar photo and said it was altered in a way that could mislead people," and warning that those who share false information might see their posts ranked lower in the feed.[5] Users complained on X, a Facebook competitor, about being warned despite sharing an unaltered photograph.[5] Spokeswoman Dani Lever said in an X post: "This was an error. This fact check was initially applied to a doctored photo showing the Secret Service agents smiling, and in some cases our systems incorrectly applied that fact check to the real photo. This has been fixed and we apologize for the mistake."[5] Facebook and other [[meta-platforms|Meta]]-owned properties use a third-party fact-checking network the company says lets it reduce the spread of problematic content across its apps; some conservatives said the censorship should prompt lawmakers to question Meta CEO [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]].[5]

## Trump Doctrine: Peace Through Space

"[[trump-doctrine-peace-through-space|Trump Doctrine: Peace Through Space]]" identifies two Facebook ventures that failed during the period it examines.[7] The company was to be a major user of the [[amos-6|Amos-6]] satellite, owned by [[spacecom|Spacecom]] and part of Facebook's Internet.org program to deliver internet to the developing world, which was destroyed in the September 1, 2016 [[spacex|SpaceX]] pad explosion — the company's only failure against 273 successes, and a payload intended to provide capabilities similar to [[starlink|Starlink]].[7] Facebook also planned its own stablecoin, [[diem|Diem]] (formerly Libra), initially researched during 2017 by Morgan Beller before David Marcus and Kevin Weil joined the effort; the project was blocked after Treasury Secretary [[steven-mnuchin|Steve Mnuchin]] objected on July 15, 2019, and its assets were sold to [[silvergate-bank|Silvergate Bank]].[7]

## March 2024 global outage
Facebook was knocked offline on March 5, 2024 in a [[2024-meta-global-outage|global outage]] of [[meta-platforms|Meta]] services that lasted more than two hours.[4] At the peak of the disruption, the tracking site Downdetector recorded more than 550,000 reports for Facebook alone, with users complaining on X that they had been booted from their accounts and could not log back in.[4] Meta attributed the incident to a technical issue, and the White House National Security Council said it saw no evidence of malicious cyber activity.[4]

## Alleged CIA origins (Carlson, May 2023)

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] wrote in May 2023 that most people in his readership believe Facebook was started and is run by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], citing as evidence that the agency's citizen-information gathering program known as [[lifelog|LifeLog]] was shut down the same day Facebook began, and that a great deal of the early money behind Facebook is tied to the CIA.[8] Carlson treated the public's familiarity with these claims as itself significant: information of this kind circulates freely because it cannot bring down the [[deep-state|Deep State cabal]].[8]

In his account the platform's role, alongside [[amazon|Amazon]] and the agency itself, is not primarily surveillance aimed at catching dissenters but the mass accumulation of behavioural data — purchases, browsing, interests and fears — sufficient for supercomputers to predict probable outcomes and thereby steer or control future behaviour.[8]
