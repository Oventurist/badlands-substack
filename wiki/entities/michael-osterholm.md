# Michael Osterholm

Michael Osterholm is an American epidemiologist and the director of the Center for Infectious Disease Research and Policy at the University of Minnesota, described in reporting on his correspondence with federal health authorities as a "top epidemiologist."[1]

## November 2021 letter to the CDC

In November 2021, Osterholm and seven colleagues sent a letter to the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] informing the agency that it was promoting flawed data and excluding data that did not reinforce its narrative on masking.[1] The letter, later obtained among documents from the [[national-institutes-of-health|National Institutes of Health]], warned that misrepresenting data on trusted websites such as the CDC's and the COVID-19 Real-Time Learning Network — jointly created by the CDC and the [[infectious-diseases-society-of-america|Infectious Diseases Society of America]] — would "damage the credibility of science," endanger public trust by "misrepresenting the evidence," and give the public "false expectations" that masking would protect them from SARS-CoV-2.[1]

Osterholm wrote: "We believe the information and recommendations as provided may actually put an individual at increased risk of becoming infected with SARS-CoV-2 and for them to experience a serious or even life-threatening infection."[1] The authors urged the IDSA to remove from its website the suggestion that masking prevents severe disease, and asked the CDC to reconsider its statements about the "efficacy of masks and face coverings for preventing transmission of SARS-CoV-2."[1] Osterholm also noted a pattern of selectively choosing data that supported the desired narrative that masks prevent severe COVID-19 disease and transmission, claims he said were unsupported by the scientific evidence the CDC and IDSA themselves presented.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[the-epoch-times|The Epoch Times]] reported the letter in August 2023 as evidence that public health officials had used inaccurate information and misrepresented medical research to advance a policy objective despite contrary scientific evidence from experts.[1] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the disclosure as a "rerun," arguing that the ineffectiveness of masks had been evident at the time, that ample contemporaneous evidence existed — including video of [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] saying masks do not work — and rejecting the implication that scientists had merely been misled: "No way. No amnesty. No more lies."[1]
