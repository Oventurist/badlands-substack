# Abe Hamadeh

Abe Hamadeh (rendered "Abe Hamedeh" in one instance in the source) is an Arizona Republican who was the party's nominee for state attorney general in the [[2022-united-states-midterm-elections|2022 midterm elections]]. His race against Democrat [[kris-mayes|Kris Mayes]] was the closest statewide contest in Arizona history.

## Litigation and recount

Shortly after the November 2022 general election, Hamadeh and the Republican National Committee filed a lawsuit contesting the results. The court ruled the suit premature: under Arizona law, election results cannot be contested until they are certified. Certification occurred on Monday, December 5, 2022, at the hands of Secretary of State [[katie-hobbs|Katie Hobbs]], opening a five-day window in which candidates could file. Hamadeh refiled his challenge to the general election results within that window, on the same Friday that [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] and [[mark-finchem|Mark Finchem]] filed theirs.

His race was simultaneously subject to an automatic recount. "At 511 votes out of 2.5 million, our race is the closest statewide race in Arizona history, it is currently undergoing a recount. Every legal vote deserves to be counted," Hamadeh said in a statement. As *The Hill* reported, Mayes led by 511 votes as of certification, a margin under 0.1 points; Arizona law requires an automatic recount whenever the margin falls within 0.5 points. Recounts for the attorney general race, the state superintendent race, and a state House race began on Wednesday, December 7.

## Treatment in the corpus

The Badlands Media article [[arizona-earthquake|"Arizona Earthquake!"]] speculates that Hamadeh will win the recount, and frames the prospect of an America First attorney general serving as the state's top law enforcement official inside a Hobbs administration as a dramatic development in the unfolding political conflict in Arizona.
