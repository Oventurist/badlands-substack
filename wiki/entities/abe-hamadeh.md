# Abe Hamadeh

Abe Hamadeh (rendered "Abe Hamedeh" in some [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] copy) was the Republican candidate for Attorney General of Arizona in the [[2022-arizona-general-election|2022 general election]], and one of several America First candidates who contested the outcome of that election in court.[1]

## Premature litigation and the December filing

Soon after the November 8, 2022 election, Hamadeh and the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] filed suit contesting the results. The court ruled the action premature: under Arizona law election results cannot be contested until they have been certified. Certification occurred on Monday, December 5, 2022, after which candidates had five days in which to file a contest.[1] Hamadeh refiled his challenge to the general election results within that window.[1]

## The recount

Hamadeh's race was simultaneously subject to an automatic recount because the margin was too close to call. Democrat [[kris-mayes|Kris Mayes]] led him by 511 votes as of certification, a margin of less than 0.1 percentage points; Arizona law requires an automatic recount whenever a race falls within 0.5 points.[1] "At 511 votes out of 2.5 million, our race is the closest statewide race in Arizona history, it is currently undergoing a recount. Every legal vote deserves to be counted," Hamadeh said in a statement issued on Friday, December 9.[1] Recounts for the attorney general race, the state superintendent race and a state House race began that Wednesday.[1]

[[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] speculated that Hamadeh would prevail in the recount and framed the prospect of an America First attorney general — the state's top law enforcement officer — seated inside a Hobbs administration as a source of coming drama.[1]

## Runbeck referral and the 2026 FBI probe

As a member of Congress from Arizona, Hamadeh asked the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] to investigate [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]] and its handling of ballots during the 2024 election.[2] That referral preceded the March 2026 disclosure that the [[fbi|FBI]] had obtained election-related records tied to [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]] under a grand jury subpoena, as part of a federal criminal investigation into election administration from 2020 through 2024 spanning multiple states.[2]

## Request for a DOJ investigation of the 2024 election (June 2025)
In June 2025 Hamadeh, by then a Republican congressman from Arizona, asked U.S. Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] to investigate "credible claims" that an elections service provider breached protocols during the 2024 election.[3] According to a news release from his office, the request focused on the handling of ballots by Arizona-based [[runbeck-election-services|Runbeck Election Services]] in several western states.[3] Newsweek said it had sought comment from the Department of Justice, from Hamadeh's office and from Runbeck.[3] Commentary from [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] expressed hope that "this effort from Hamadeh goes somewhere," while acknowledging the possibility of being "on a rug" given the elections industry's record of obstruction.[3]
