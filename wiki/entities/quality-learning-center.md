# Quality Learning Center

The Quality Learning Center is a childcare facility on Nicollet Avenue in the [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]] area that became a key target of a sweeping federal fraud investigation in April 2026.[1] It had previously operated under the name Salama Child Care Center.[1]

## Federal raid (April 2026)

On Tuesday, April 28, 2026, agents of the [[fbi|FBI]] and [[homeland-security-investigations|Homeland Security Investigations]] executed search warrants at more than twenty locations across the Minneapolis area, primarily targeting Somali-linked businesses as part of a federal probe into what officials described as "rampant fraud of U.S. taxpayers dollars" in government-funded social-services programs.[1] The investigation focused especially on childcare centers billing for nonexistent or "ghost" services.[1]

The Quality Learning Center was described as a key target of the operation.[1] It had received $1.9 million in state funds in fiscal year 2025 and had previously been exposed in a viral video as operating as a shell operation.[1] No arrests were announced from the raids, though officials characterized them as "the tip of a very large iceberg."[1]

## Context in the Minnesota fraud investigations

The action fit into a much larger pattern of federal investigations in Minnesota, including the [[feeding-our-future|Feeding Our Future]] scandal that siphoned more than $250 million in federal child nutrition funds, alongside similar schemes involving autism services, housing assistance, Medicaid, and SNAP benefits.[1] Since January, authorities had issued more than 1,750 subpoenas and 130 warrants in these cases, many involving defendants from the Somali-American community.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that she hoped the raids marked a beginning, citing [[doge|DOGE]], [[usaid|USAID]] and independent investigator Nick Shirley as sources of staggering fraud revelations during the first fifteen months of the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] term.[1] Responding to complaints that no one had been arrested, she argued that the justice system prioritizes the rights of the accused and that prosecutors cannot skip investigative steps without risking collapsed cases, framing the effort as having moved from data collection and assessment into the design and execution of justice.[1]
