# John Quincy Adams

John Quincy Adams (1767–1848) was the sixth President of the United States and previously Secretary of State. In the Badlands Media corpus he appears as the principal architect of a republican, anti-imperial American security doctrine.

## The Monroe Doctrine

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s essay Avoiding WWIII credits Adams with drafting the [[monroe-doctrine|Monroe Doctrine]], extending the ideas of [[george-washington|George Washington]] about avoiding foreign entanglements. Adams is quoted for the principle that the doctrine could function only if America went "not abroad, in search of monsters to destroy" — a formula the article treats as the touchstone distinguishing legitimate continental development from imperial adventurism.

## Anti-slavery and the Whig tradition

Adams is listed among the "wiser anti-slavery voices" of the early republic alongside [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]], John Jay, [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]], Charles Sumner, William Seward and [[william-gilpin|William Gilpin]], all of whom the article says were repeatedly subverted by an Anglophile [[deep-state|Deep State]] operating through Wall Street in the north and the slave power in the south. For decades the nationalist grouping opposed to the slave power rallied primarily around Adams's [[whig-party|Whig Party]], which won presidential victories in 1840 with [[william-henry-harrison|William Henry Harrison]] and in 1848 with [[zachary-taylor|Zachary Taylor]].

## Legacy in the corpus

Ehret closes his essay by arguing that the spirit of Adams's security doctrine survives today not in the United States but in the Eurasian development programs of [[russia|Russia]] and [[china|China]].
