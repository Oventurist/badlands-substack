# John Quincy Adams

**John Quincy Adams** was an American statesman and president who, in the account given by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] in "Avoiding WWIII," drafted the [[monroe-doctrine|Monroe Doctrine]] as an extension of the republican tradition of [[manifest-destiny|Manifest Destiny]].[1]

## Security doctrine

Adams extended the ideas of [[george-washington|George Washington]] and [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] by drafting the Monroe Doctrine, which he understood could work only if America ventured "not abroad, in search of monsters to destroy."[1] Ehret places him among the "wiser anti-slavery voices" of the early republic alongside Franklin, John Jay, [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]], Charles Sumner, William Seward and [[william-gilpin|William Gilpin]], all of whom were repeatedly subverted by an anglophile [[deep-state|Deep State]] parasite class running Wall Street and the southern slave power.[1]

## The Whig grouping

For decades the nationalist, anti-slavery grouping rallied primarily around the Whig Party of John Quincy Adams, winning important victories in 1840 and 1848 with the presidencies of [[william-henry-harrison|William Harrison]] and [[zachary-taylor|Zachary Taylor]] — both of whom, Ehret writes, were poisoned in office.[1]

## Legacy

Ehret concludes his essay by observing that, viewed against Russia's Far Eastern Vision and China's [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]], it is a "rich irony" that the spirit of John Quincy Adams' security doctrine is alive in the world — "just not in the U.S."[1]
