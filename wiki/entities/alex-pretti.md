# Alex Pretti

Alex Pretti was a United States citizen fatally shot on January 24, 2026 during protest clashes connected to [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]], the federal immigration enforcement surge in Minneapolis and St. Paul, Minnesota.

Pretti's death, together with that of [[renee-good|Renee Good]] on January 7, 2026, featured prominently in the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]] hearing of March 3, 2026 described in the Badlands Brief of March 4. Democratic senators accused [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] and [[customs-and-border-protection|CBP]] officers of excessive force and criticized Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] for characterizing the two victims as "domestic terrorists"; Noem attributed the violence to protesters.

## Minneapolis shooting, January 2026

The January 26, 2026 edition of the Badlands Brief recounts that Pretti, a 37-year-old U.S. citizen, was fatally shot by federal immigration agents in Minneapolis on January 24 during an enforcement operation, an event that ignited protests and political controversy in Minnesota and nationally. The [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] stated that Pretti was armed with a handgun and resisted efforts to disarm him before a Border Patrol agent fired, killing him at the scene. Bystander videos and witness accounts described in the Brief suggest a different sequence: that Pretti was initially filming officers and was pepper-sprayed and pinned to the ground before the shots, with some footage appearing to show an agent taking his weapon before others fired.

Border Patrol Chief [[gregory-bovino|Gregory Bovino]] defended the agents, characterizing them as victims and asserting that Pretti had interfered with their actions. Minnesota Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] and Minneapolis Mayor [[jacob-frey|Jacob Frey]] denounced the operation as excessive and called for the withdrawal of federal agents and for accountability, while President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] used Truth Social to back the agents, question the absence of local police, and accuse Minnesota officials of "inciting insurrection."
