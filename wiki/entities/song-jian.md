# Song Jian

Song Jian was a Soviet-trained cyberneticist and, per Matthew Ehret's Badlands account, a devotee of the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]] who played the decisive technical role in the design of China's [[one-child-policy|one child policy]].[1]

Citing researcher Robert Zubrin, the article states that Song immediately translated the Club of Rome's [[limits-to-growth|Limits to Growth]] into Mandarin and used its linear computer models to project trends in population, pollution and resource loss over the course of a century.[1] From these models he concluded that China's optimal population — its "carrying capacity" — was on the order of 650–700 million people, nearly 300 million fewer than the country's population at the time.[1]

As head of China's State Science and Technology Commission, Song interfaced closely with [[zhao-ziyang|Zhao Ziyang]] in order to keep Chinese science policy tied to the Club of Rome's closed-system paradigm, according to Cambridge researcher Julian Gewertz's 2019 study "Futurists of Beijing."[1] Ehret uses Song's role to argue that the one child policy cannot be understood as a purely indigenous Chinese Communist Party program, but rather as an agenda effected through the Club of Rome and imposed on a weakened China by external forces.[1]
