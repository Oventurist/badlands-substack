# Washington Commanders

The Washington Commanders are the National Football League franchise based in the Washington, D.C. area, known as the Washington Redskins until 2020, when the team dropped that name after decades of criticism that it was a racial slur tied to the historical treatment of the Indigenous population of the United States.[1] The team's name became a flashpoint in July 2025 when President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened to interfere with the franchise's stadium plans unless it readopted the Redskins name.[1]

## Stadium deal and Trump's ultimatum

The franchise moved from Washington to suburban Landover, Maryland, in 1997, but in 2025 reached an agreement with the District of Columbia government to return to the city in a new stadium expected to open in 2030.[1] On Sunday, July 20, 2025, Trump wrote on Truth Social: "I may put a restriction on them that if they don't change the name back to the original 'Washington Redskins,' and get rid of the ridiculous moniker, 'Washington Commanders,' I won't make a deal for them to build a Stadium in Washington."[1]

Trump had previously urged both the Washington team and the Cleveland Guardians baseball club to revert to their former names, but this was the first time he suggested taking official action.[1] Under the home-rule law governing federal oversight of the District of Columbia, the president has limited authority to intervene, though Trump had raised the prospect of greater federal control, telling reporters in February 2025, "I think we should take over Washington, D.C."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media commentary treated the episode as more than "bread and circus," arguing that the Redskins name had been the "ultimate prize" of the woke movement because it was the most controversial and one of the oldest brand names in American popular culture.[1] The commentary noted that the team's logo had been designed by a Native American and that the franchise had been supported by numerous tribes, attributing the 2020 change primarily to the poor public-relations instincts of former owner Dan Snyder, who was widely disliked by both peers and fans and may have made the change out of spite before selling the team.[1] Trump's demand was characterized as a signal that woke ideology should be rejected in all its forms.[1]
