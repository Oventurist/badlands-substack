# Ryan Matta

Ryan Matta is a podcast host with more than 200,000 followers on X.[1]

## Background and crypto commentary

Matta is an independent journalist who built a following covering cryptocurrency before, in Badlands Media's account, being canceled for speaking the truth about global communism.[2] A former robotics engineer, he was mining [[ethereum|Ethereum]] when it traded at $2.64, and he discusses finance on his YouTube channel.[2]

Asked in 2023 to define [[decentralized-finance|DeFi]], Matta answered: "The first thing you need to understand is that there's no such thing as 'DeFi.'"[2] He argued that "there is nothing decentralized about [[proof-of-stake]] blockchain," because validators are selected according to how many coins they have staked, creating barriers to fair access for retail investors — a dynamic he examined in his October 2022 video "What is Solana??" about [[solana|Solana]].[2] He added that "when a project launches, the team owns enough stake in the company to where they can control and change the rules of a blockchain," conduct the article compared to antitrust violations and market manipulation.[2]

Despite this critique Matta was described as optimistic, grounding that optimism in human agency and personal responsibility: "With great reward comes great responsibility. The longer you take to educate yourself and get up to speed, the only person you are doing a disservice to yourself is you."[2] He also told readers to "think back to the first time you heard about bitcoin. What if you would have invested then? How much better would your life be today? The same will be true five years from now," adding that "it's definitely a painful learning curve, but once you fully understand crypto, you will 100% see the major issues with banks and traditional markets."[2] Along with [[gmoney|GMoney]], he insisted on the distinction "Bitcoin, not crypto."[2]

## Commentary on the Kirk assassination

In the hours after the [[assassination-of-charlie-kirk|assassination of Charlie Kirk]] at [[utah-valley-university|Utah Valley University]] in September 2025, Matta posted: "At this point does anyone not thi[nk] Charlie Kirk was assassinated by Mossad?", a reference to the Israeli spy agency [[mossad|Mossad]].[1] The Times of Israel cited his post as an example of allegations spreading online that Jews or Israel were responsible for the killing, a dynamic the outlet said recurs frequently after major news events.[1]
