# Adolf Heusinger

Adolf Heusinger was a German military officer cited in Badlands Media commentary as a leading example of the recruitment of former Nazi-era officers into the postwar Western security architecture.[1] According to that account he served as Inspector General of the Bundeswehr and as Operations Chief within the general staff of the High Command of the German Army during the Third Reich.[1]

Heusinger was subsequently appointed head of the West German military from 1957 to 1961, and then served as Chair of [[nato|NATO]]'s military committee from 1961 to 1964 — meaning, as the Badlands commentary emphasized, that during John F. Kennedy's presidency a former Nazi high commander headed NATO's military body.[1] The commentary paired his case with that of Alexander Dolezalek, a Race and Settlement Main Office and Waffen SS officer who after the war changed his surname to Bomhoff and was hired by NATO to run its "All-European educational project."[1]

The broader argument advanced in the article was that NATO's Cold War staffing deliberately drew on "the professionals who knew the communists the best" — former Waffen SS intelligence officers — and that this lineage, together with claims about the origins of the [[european-union|European Union]]'s founding documents, means "NATO is not what we have been told it is."[1] These claims are presented as Badlands commentary rather than independently verified history.[1]

## Wartime rank and NATO chairmanship (Ehret, 2024)

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s April 2024 essay "The SCO vs. NATO" identified Heusinger as Chief of the General Staff for the Nazis during the Second World War, and after it as chief of the West German army, a general of the Bundeswehr from 1957 to 1961, and Chairman of the [[nato|NATO]] Military Committee from 1961 to 1964.[2]

Ehret placed him within a documented roster of unreconstructed Nazis holding senior NATO posts between 1950 and the 1980s, including [[hans-speidel|Hans Speidel]], [[johannes-steinhoff|Johannes Steinhoff]], Johann von Kielmansegg, Jurgen Bennecke, Ernst Ferber, Karl Schnell, Franz Joseph Schulze and Ferdinand von Senger und Etterlin — noting that the post of NATO Commander in Chief of Allied Forces Central Europe was filled solely by former Nazis for sixteen straight years from 1967 to 1983.[2] This staffing pattern is presented as part of a wider apparatus overseen by [[allen-dulles|Allen Dulles]] and connected to [[reinhard-gehlen|Reinhard Gehlen]]'s West German intelligence service and NATO's stay-behind secret armies.[2]
