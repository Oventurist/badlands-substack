# Financial Industry Regulatory Authority

The Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) is the U.S. self-regulatory organization that oversees broker-dealers and compiles short-interest data on securities traded in American markets.[1]

## Role in the October 7 short-selling findings

FINRA short-interest data supplied the empirical backbone of the 2023 study by [[robert-jackson-jr|Robert J. Jackson Jr.]] and [[joshua-mitts|Joshua Mitts]], who cited it in reporting that short interest in the [[msci-israel-etf|MSCI Israel Exchange Traded Fund]] "suddenly, and significantly, spiked" on October 2, 2023, days before the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 Hamas attack]].[1]

The authors noted that while the identity of the traders responsible is not discernible from publicly available data, FINRA and the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]] would know their names, and they urged both agencies to investigate why and how financial markets may have anticipated the events of October 7.[1] They also argued that trading of this kind exploits gaps in U.S. and international enforcement of legal prohibitions on informed trading.[1] The matter forms part of the [[israel-october-7-foreknowledge-allegations|Israel October 7 foreknowledge allegations]].[1]
