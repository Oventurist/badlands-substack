# Nithya Raman

Nithya Raman is a Los Angeles City Council member who advanced to the November 2026 runoff election for mayor of Los Angeles, where she will face incumbent Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]].[1]

Raman entered the mayoral race shortly before the filing deadline and campaigned on housing affordability, homelessness, and city services.[1] Public polling before the primary showed her in a closely contested three-way race with Bass and former reality television personality [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]].[1] She finished ahead of Pratt in the 14-candidate nonpartisan primary, taking the second runoff slot.[1]

Her advancement over Pratt is the proximate event that prompted Badlands Media's June 9, 2026 argument that "elections are still fake," given Los Angeles's continued use of [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]] voting equipment.[1]

## Skid Row voter fraud allegations

Videos that surfaced after Election Day in 2026 featured Skid Row residents claiming they had been paid to sign registration forms and forge signatures, and that they had been encouraged to support incumbent Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]] and Raman; one account stated that challenger [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]] was not among the candidates promoted to voters.[2] Federal agents subsequently conducted an operation on Skid Row as part of the resulting [[skid-row-voter-fraud-investigation|voter fraud investigation]].[2]

Reporting that accompanied the probe noted that a homeless services drop-in center in Venice with 185 registered voters tied to its address had previously received a $600,000 taxpayer-funded grant while Raman chaired the Los Angeles City Council's Housing and Homelessness Committee.[2]

## June 2026 result reversal

In the [[2026-los-angeles-mayoral-election|2026 Los Angeles mayoral race]], early election-night returns showed Raman trailing challenger [[spencer-pratt|Spencer Pratt]] by roughly eight points, but the margin reversed as additional mail ballots were counted over the following days, positioning her to potentially advance to a runoff against incumbent Mayor [[karen-bass|Karen Bass]].[3] The swing was cited by Trump administration officials and Republican allies as an example of the [[red-mirage|Red Mirage]] pattern and as grounds for suspicion of fraud in California's slow, multi-day mail-ballot count.[3]
