# Jennifer Granholm

Jennifer Granholm is an American politician who served as United States Secretary of Energy, heading the [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] during the Biden administration. In that role she became a central spokesperson for federal policy on electricity generation, grid capacity and the energy demands of emerging technologies.[1]

## Small modular reactors and AI power demand

In a CNBC appearance in September 2024, Granholm signalled that [[small-modular-reactor|small modular reactors]] were effectively a settled part of the future energy mix, responding to questions about how the rapidly growing electricity appetite of data centers and generative artificial intelligence would be satisfied.[1] Interviewer David Faber posed the underlying tension directly: the long-running effort to replace conventional energy sources with renewables was colliding with an "incredible potential uptick in the need for power because of data centers and the growth of generative AI," at a time when discussion had turned to bringing a reactor at [[three-mile-island-nuclear-power-plant|Three Mile Island]] back online and recommissioning other nuclear sites, with national power needs potentially 20% higher within a handful of years.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media treated Granholm's remarks as a notable narrative shift, describing her as pivoting sharply away from the wind-and-solar rhetoric that had saturated Green New Deal framing over the preceding decade and toward nuclear generation — specifically small modular reactors, a technology the outlet noted is discussed frequently by Trump-aligned Space Force officers.[1] Placed alongside the involvement of Microsoft and Pennsylvania governor Josh Shapiro in restarting Three Mile Island, the outlet read the shift as evidence for its thesis that the "Great" Reset was being circumvented and converted into what it called a "Good" Reset.[1]
