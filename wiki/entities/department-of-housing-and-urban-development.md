# Department of Housing and Urban Development

The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) is the federal agency responsible for housing programs and fair-housing enforcement. In early 2026 it featured in two distinct strands of Trump administration policy: a civil-rights investigation in Minnesota and the implementation of an executive order restricting institutional investors in the single-family housing market.[1]

## Minneapolis housing investigation

HUD opened a probe into [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]] housing programs, alleging that race-based prioritization for certain ethnic groups violates the [[fair-housing-act|Fair Housing Act]] and the Civil Rights Act.[1] The probe formed part of a wider set of federal actions in Minnesota in early 2026 that also included the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]]-led [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]] and [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] grand jury subpoenas to state and local officials, and it was accompanied by broader federal concern about welfare fraud and corruption tied to state policies.[1]

## Institutional homebuyer executive order

Under [[executive-order-on-institutional-homebuyers|President Trump's executive order on institutional homebuyers]], HUD is directed to identify institutional investors participating in federal housing programs through ownership disclosure requirements.[1] The order more broadly instructs federal agencies to bar government programs from approving, insuring, guaranteeing, or facilitating sales of single-family homes to institutional investors.[1]

## Confirmation of Scott Turner (February 2025)

On February 5, 2025, the Senate confirmed [[scott-turner|Scott Turner]] as Secretary of Housing and Urban Development by a vote of 55-44.[2] During his confirmation hearing, Turner said HUD is "failing at its most basic mission," pointing to record-high homelessness and a shortage of affordable housing.[2]

## Role in Hurricane Helene recovery funding

HUD's grant-approval process was cited in early 2025 as a reason for slow disbursement of [[hurricane-helene|Hurricane Helene]] recovery money to Western North Carolina, since states must submit spending plans to HUD and await approval before funds flow.[2] North Carolina officials quoted by Cardinal & Pine in January 2025 said money was unlikely to move "until the summer" because of that review cycle.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] contrasted congressional Democrats' protests over foreign aid cuts with the absence of comparable outrage over the delayed domestic disaster relief routed through HUD and FEMA.[2]

## DOGE AI deregulation (July 2025)

The [[doge-ai-deregulation-decision-tool|DOGE AI Deregulation Decision Tool]] was used to review more than 1,000 regulatory sections at the Department of Housing and Urban Development in under two weeks, an early deployment of [[doge|DOGE]]'s automated system for identifying obsolete federal rules.[3]
