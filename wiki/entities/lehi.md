# Lehi (Stern Gang)

Lehi, commonly known as the Stern Gang, was a Zionist paramilitary militia in Mandate Palestine formed in the late 1930s by a faction that broke away from the [[irgun|Irgun]].[1]

## Formation

The split arose from a disagreement over strategy within the Irgun, which had been carrying out acts of terrorism and assassination against the British colonial government in Palestine.[1] [[zeev-jabotinsky|Ze'ev Jabotinsky]] wanted to make peace with the British so that a Jewish government could be negotiated, while a faction led by Avraham Stern insisted that National Socialism was Israel's natural political ideology and proposed an alliance with the Axis powers.[1] That division produced the breakaway Lehi militia.[1]

## The Jerusalem Agreement

In 1940 Stern and Lehi sent [[benito-mussolini|Mussolini]] a document known as the Jerusalem Agreement, swearing allegiance to Italian Fascism and offering to make the deep-water port of Haifa an Italian port in exchange for Italian recognition of Palestine as a Jewish state under a Jewish government.[1] The same proposal was sent to Hitler a year later and again in 1942.[1] From Stern's perspective Hitler was viewed favorably because he had helped the World Zionist Organization settle Israel under the Haavara Transfer Agreement of 1933.[1] Hitler's correspondence with Mussolini acknowledged receiving the proposal, observing that if accepted, Israel would become a valuable satellite of the Axis powers and the Nazi war machine; the offer was ultimately declined.[1]

## Legacy

Lehi and the Irgun reunited in 1948, together with several other paramilitary groups, to form the [[israel-defense-forces|Israeli Defense Force]].[1] One Lehi militant, Yitzhak Shamir, became prime minister in the 1980s and appointed [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] as his foreign minister in 1990.[1] Badlands commentary cites this lineage as evidence for its contention that the Israeli state has fascist origins, an argument omitted from mainstream discussion of Israeli politics along with related keywords such as Revisionism and Jabotinsky.[1]
