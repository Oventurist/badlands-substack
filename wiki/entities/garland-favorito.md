# Garland Favorito

Garland Favorito is a Georgia lobbyist and election-integrity advocate.[1]

Favorito appeared as a supporter of [[georgia-house-bill-397|House Bill 397]] during the Georgia Senate Ethics Committee's late-night March 2025 hearing on the measure, which would require an election night hand count among other changes to state election administration.[1] Summarizing his case for the bill, Favorito said: "If the elections aren't right, what else do we have? We rely on the elections to elect the right people, to make the right decisions."[1]

## Fulton County ballot litigation (2025)

As cofounder of [[voterga|VoterGA]], Favorito pressed [[fulton-county|Fulton County]] in February 2025 to release its 2020 election ballots under a new Georgia law, effective January 1, 2025, allowing the public to request high-resolution ballot copies.[2] After the county refused, Favorito — who has sought access through two pending lawsuits — said "Fulton obviously has something to hide" and that the refusal proved "all along that we were right — there was something seriously wrong about the 2020 election in Fulton County."[2] Multiple investigations have countered allegations of widespread fraud there, and three counts showed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] lost Georgia by roughly 12,000 votes.[2]

## Response to the unsealed Halderman Report (2023)

In June 2023, after the [[halderman-report|Halderman Report]] on Georgia's [[dominion-imagecast-x|Dominion ImageCast X]] machines was unsealed, Favorito appeared on Steve Bannon's [[war-room|War Room]] to summarize its contents.[3] He said the vulnerabilities discovered could be used to "alter the QR codes on the printed ballots to modify voter selections," noting that in Georgia the filled bubble does not determine the count — the QR code contains the voter's intent and the voter cannot verify it.[3] Favorito was described as holding one of the only existing lawsuits in the country regarding the 2020 election, after the Georgia Supreme Court remanded his case to the lower court having established that he had standing to bring it.[3]
