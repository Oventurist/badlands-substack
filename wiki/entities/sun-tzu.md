# Sun Tzu

**Sun Tzu** is the ancient Chinese strategist cited in the Badlands Brief article "Kekistani Code Talkers" as an early theorist of [[information-warfare|information warfare]] from the perspective of an entirely analog battlefield.[1]

The article closes its argument by invoking his maxim that "all warfare is based on deception," using it to justify the claim that the irregular and misunderstood sources and methods of information control in a [[fifth-generation-warfare|fifth-generation war]] are entirely expected rather than anomalous.[1]

## The golden bridge maxim

Sun Tzu's *Art of War* also supplied the organising metaphor for the January 28, 2025 Badlands Media feature "On Golden Bridges and the Art of War" by [[american-hypnotist|American Hypnotist]], which quoted the rule that "a surrounded army must be given a way out," with the ancient rule of the charioteers directing commanders to "surround them on three sides, leaving one side open, to show them a way to life... and then you can take advantage of this to strike them."[2] The author used this [[golden-bridge-strategy|golden bridge]] principle to interpret the public conduct of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] during the [[h-1b-visa-program|H-1B visa]] controversy as a feint that offers opponents an apparent escape in order to strike from advantage.[2] The same essay drew on the general principle of confusion as a weapon, treating obfuscation of an operation's finer details as a deliberate method of deployment rather than incompetence.[2]

## Citation in Q For Dummies Chapter 13

The Badlands Media "Q For Dummies" series invoked Sun Tzu and The Art of War while explaining Q's Drop #97 request that readers define [[game-theory-strategy|game theory]], naming both [[donald-trump|President Trump]] and [[michael-flynn|General Michael Flynn]] as proponents of the text.[3] Two maxims were quoted: "Let your plans be dark and impenetrable as night, and when you move — fall like a thunderbolt," and "Appear weak when strong."[3] The author used them to argue that direct confrontation with the [[deep-state|deep state]] would fail and that deception, patience, and exploitation of an adversary's ego are the viable path — the same logic he applied to the Second World War [[ghost-army|Ghost Army]] and to modern psychological operations.[3]
