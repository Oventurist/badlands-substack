# Russian Investigative Committee

The Russian Investigative Committee is the top investigative body of the Russian Federation, tasked with probing major crimes.[1] In April 2024 it moved beyond ordinary domestic criminal matters into an explicitly geopolitical arena, announcing a criminal probe into senior officials of the United States and of [[nato|NATO]] member countries suspected of "financing terrorism."[1]

## The April 2024 terrorism-financing probe

The Committee announced on Tuesday, April 9, 2024 — via a statement circulated on its Telegram press channel — that it had launched the criminal probe.[1] It said it had "established" that money originating with commercial organizations had been used to "eliminate prominent political and public figures" both inside and outside [[russia|Russia]] in recent years, and additionally to "inflict economic damage" against the country.[1]

The Committee did not specify which particular terrorist attacks it believed had been funded by Western organizations.[1] The announcement nevertheless arrived while senior Russian officials were doubling down on claims that Ukraine and the West had been involved in the [[crocus-city-hall-attack|Crocus City Hall attack]] of the preceding month, giving the probe an unmistakable contextual frame.[1]

Among the organizations the Committee named as implicated was the Ukrainian energy company [[burisma|Burisma Holdings]].[1] [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]], son of U.S. President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], served as a member of Burisma's board of directors between 2014 and 2019 — a fact that gave the naming of the company obvious political resonance in the United States.[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary treated the announcement as potentially the opening move in a public disclosure of the international financing of terrorism pursued through [[lawfare|lawfare]] by a purported member of a "Sovereign Alliance."[1] Writing for the brief, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] tied the theme to longstanding questions about 9/11, Saudi Arabia, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], and closed by wondering "what evidence Putin is going to present next to support his argument that US government agencies are funding terrorist activities."[1] The framing positions [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and the Committee as actors using formal legal process as an information-war instrument rather than as a conventional prosecution.[1]

## Il-76 shootdown findings, June 2024

On June 25, 2024, the Investigative Committee announced that senior military officials in Kiev were responsible for shooting down a Russian Ilyushin Il-76M transport plane carrying Ukrainian prisoners of war to an exchange on January 24, 2024, killing all 65 POWs, three Russian guards and six crew members.[2] It said the aircraft was brought down by a US-made [[patriot-missile-system|Patriot]] interceptor missile and that it had collected enough evidence to accuse Ukrainian commanders and the leadership of the GUR military intelligence directorate, who "knew for certain" a prisoner swap was due and "ordered the destruction of the plane" nevertheless.[2]
