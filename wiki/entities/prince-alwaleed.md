# Prince Alwaleed bin Talal

Prince Alwaleed bin Talal is a Saudi Arabian businessman and member of the Saudi royal family. In the Badlands Media article "A Conflict of Interest," Alwaleed is portrayed as a key financial and political node in what the source describes as a global cabal that controls U.S. taxpayer money through its influence over politicians and intelligence agencies.

According to the article, Alwaleed was born in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, and is of Lebanese descent through his mother, Mona El Solh, who was the daughter of Riad El Solh, the first Prime Minister of Lebanon. The source emphasizes that "a lot of roads lead to Jeddah," and that Alwaleed's background gave him "deep and wealthy connections in both Saudi Arabia and Lebanon" from the very beginning of his life.

The article claims that Alwaleed had "strong ties to the Bush family and the CIA," and that he was "one of [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]]'s biggest puppets." It specifically links Alwaleed to [[prince-alwaleed|Basel]], the naturalized American citizen of Lebanese descent who handled the $40 billion in Iraqi cash shipments, noting that both were born in Saudi Arabia and of Lebanese descent. The source speculates that Basel was affiliated with Alwaleed, and that the absence of documentation after the cash reached Baghdad was designed to allow Alwaleed and his network to steal the money.

According to the article, Alwaleed's value to the cabal was his ability to provide financial support to politicians such as [[john-mccain|John McCain]], whom the source claims was "pumping money into the McCain Institute." The piece asserts that Alwaleed's influence over Bush and McCain allowed him to shape U.S. foreign policy in ways that benefited his financial interests and the broader cabal's goal of controlling global money flows.

The article notes that Alwaleed's father, Talal bin Abdulaziz Al Saud, was a member of the Saudi Royal family, and that Alwaleed's maternal grandfather was the first Prime Minister of Lebanon, giving him unique connections across the Middle East that the source claims were exploited for corrupt purposes.
