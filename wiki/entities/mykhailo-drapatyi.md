# Mykhailo Drapatyi

Lt. Gen. Mykhailo Drapatyi is a Ukrainian military officer who was appointed commander-in-chief of [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s armed forces and who drew international attention in July 2026 for declaring that "Russia has no right to exist."[1]

## The July 2026 remarks

Drapatyi made the statement while outlining his broader vision for the future of the Ukrainian military, framing the war with [[russia|Russia]] in existential rather than territorial terms.[1] Badlands commentary characterized the remarks as part of a wider Ukrainian narrative that portrays ethnic Russians living in Ukraine as invaders rather than as a long-resident population, a framing the commentary rejected by noting that such communities have lived in the region for more than a thousand years.[1]

## Timing and diplomatic context

The declaration landed at a diplomatically sensitive moment. It coincided with Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]]'s meeting with Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] in Manila, at which Rubio said the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] remained prepared to play a "constructive role" in helping end the war while cautioning that no quick path to a negotiated settlement existed.[1] The remarks also followed the administration's decision to withhold portions of previously planned military assistance to Kyiv pending a review of US weapons stockpiles.[1]

In Badlands' reading, Drapatyi's rhetoric — arriving alongside the deployment of [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] to Ukraine for a pro-war publicity campaign — represented an effort by war-continuation factions to counteract American diplomatic outreach to Moscow.[1] Ukraine has separately maintained that any peace agreement must preserve its sovereignty and territorial integrity.[1]
