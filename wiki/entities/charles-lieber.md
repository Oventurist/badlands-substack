# Charles Lieber

Charles Lieber is a nanotechnology researcher and the former Chair of the Chemistry and Chemical Biology Department at [[harvard-university|Harvard University]], whose federal prosecution became one of the most cited examples of Chinese penetration of American academic research.[1]

## Conviction

In December 2021, Lieber was convicted on six felony counts, including lying to federal investigators and failing to report foreign bank accounts.[1] Prosecutors established that he had secretly accepted roughly $50,000 per month from the Wuhan University of Technology, over $1.5 million to establish a laboratory in China, and that he had simultaneously held more than $15 million in research grants from the [[national-institutes-of-health|National Institutes of Health]] and the Department of Defense.[1]

A press release issued by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] on December 21, 2021 stated that "there is now no question that Charles Lieber lied to federal investigators and to Harvard in an attempt to hide his participation in the Chinese [[thousand-talents-program|Thousand Talents Program]]," adding that "he exploited the openness of our academic system."[1]

## Sentencing

According to an April 26, 2023 Department of Justice update, Lieber was sentenced to time served — just two days — along with six months of home confinement and a $50,000 fine, an amount less than a single month of his Wuhan salary.[1] Critics characterized the outcome as a slap on the wrist wholly disproportionate to the conduct.[1]

## Significance

Lieber's research concerned nanowire battery systems and biosensors, technologies with dual-use potential in warfare and surveillance.[1] Badlands Media argued that the transfer of such work to China while deceiving U.S. defense investigators constituted a national security breach rather than a mere disclosure violation, and that Harvard's claim of ignorance was not credible given the scale of the payments involved.[1] The case is presented in *The Ivy Web* as evidence that Harvard's compromise was "not just financial or administrative, it was biological," linking the university's funding streams to Wuhan laboratories.[1]
