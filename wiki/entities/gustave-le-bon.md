# Gustave Le Bon

**Gustave Le Bon** was a French polymath whose seminal work *The Crowd: A Study of the Popular Mind* is cited by Badlands Media as foundational to understanding [[mass-psychosis]] and crowd behavior.[1]

## Cited passage

In [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]]'s January 20, 2023 essay, Le Bon is quoted as writing that "the masses have never thirsted after truth. They turn aside from evidence that is not to their taste, preferring to deify error, if error seduces them. Whoever can supply them with illusions is easily their master; whoever attempts to destroy their illusions is always their victim. An individual in a crowd is a grain of sand amid other grains of sand, which the wind stirs up at will."[1]

## Role in the Badlands argument

The quotation is used to establish that susceptibility to illusion is a durable property of crowds rather than a novelty of the digital era, framing the modern propagandist as an inheritor of a very old advantage.[1] Le Bon appears alongside [[carl-jung|Carl Jung]], who is quoted saying that the greatest threat to mankind lies not with the forces of nature or physical disease but with humanity's inability to deal with the forces of its own psyche, and alongside [[ken-wilber|Ken Wilber]] on scapegoating.[1] Together these citations support the essay's contention that current societal schisms parallel earlier mass psychoses, including the American and European witch hunts of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries — in which thousands, mostly women, were killed as scapegoats of societies gone mad — and the rise of twentieth-century [[totalitarianism]].[1]
