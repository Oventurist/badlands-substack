# Beny Steinmetz

Beny Steinmetz is a French-Israeli diamond and mining magnate whose international business dealings have repeatedly drawn criminal scrutiny in Europe and Africa.[1]

## Detention in Greece

On Sunday, January 26, 2025, the 68-year-old Steinmetz was detained in Athens, hours after arriving at Athens International Airport aboard a private aircraft; police and legal sources confirmed the arrest the following Monday.[1] The detention was carried out under a European arrest warrant issued on behalf of Romania, on accusations related to participation in a criminal organization, according to a police official speaking on condition of anonymity.[1] He was expected to appear before a prosecutor later that Monday.[1]

## Romanian conviction

Romania convicted Steinmetz in absentia of real estate fraud and, in December 2020, sentenced him to five years in prison.[1] An initial Interpol "red notice" seeking his detention was issued but subsequently canceled amid concerns that the Romanian prosecution was "politically motivated," according to his spokesman, who made the argument the previous year when Steinmetz was similarly detained in Cyprus and then released on bail.[1]

## Congolese mining ties

Badlands commentary situates Steinmetz within a wider network of Israeli-linked mining finance in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, drawing an explicit comparison to [[dan-gertler|Dan Gertler]] of [[katanga-mining|Katanga Mining]], [[glencore|Glencore]], Executive Order 13818 and the Congolese state mining company Gécamines.[1] According to that commentary, Steinmetz was a longtime business partner of [[george-soros|George Soros]] and the key investor who partnered with Gertler and Glencore in 2007 to merge Katanga Mining and Nikanor, creating what became the largest copper producer in Africa — a transaction covered at the time by the Financial Times of London.[1] The same commentary links this history to contemporaneous reporting on the [[m23|M23]] armed group and the Rwandan army entering the DRC and capturing the city of Goma, raising the question of who funds M23 and what role [[rwanda|Rwanda]] plays.[1]
