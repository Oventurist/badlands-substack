# Senate Judiciary Committee

The Senate Judiciary Committee is the standing committee of the United States Senate with jurisdiction over the federal judiciary and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]]. In "America for Sale — Part 2" it is treated as the institutional venue through which the anticipated declassification and prosecution phase will run, under Chairman [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Powers

The article lists the committee's oversight responsibilities as overseeing the DOJ, considering executive and judicial nominations, reviewing pending legislation, holding hearings and investigations, and exercising legislative jurisdiction.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) That legislative jurisdiction is described as broad, encompassing proposed constitutional amendments and legislation touching federal criminal law, human rights law, immigration, intellectual property, antitrust law and internet privacy.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Significance

Oversight of the DOJ is characterized as a particularly powerful lever given the author's contention that the department was corrupted and weaponized under [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The committee's legislative reach is said to map onto the goals of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second term — dismantling the two-tier justice system, addressing crimes against humanity, ending illegal immigration, protecting intellectual property, breaking up monopolies and enacting an internet bill of rights.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) After [[bob-goodlatte|Bob Goodlatte]] and [[trey-gowdy|Trey Gowdy]] concluded their House inquiries, their evidence was handed to Grassley in this committee.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## March 2026 DHS oversight hearing

On March 3, 2026, the committee held a hearing at which Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] faced sharp bipartisan criticism over the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s mass deportation operations and controversial [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] spending.[2] Members questioned aggressive [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] and [[customs-and-border-protection|CBP]] actions in Minnesota under [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]], during which two U.S. citizen protesters were fatally shot, and pressed the Secretary over a $220 million advertising campaign featuring Noem herself and a $143 million no-bid contract awarded to a company created eleven days earlier.[2] Senator [[thom-tillis|Thom Tillis]] called for her resignation, and Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]]'s critical opening statement was described as a surprise; the session ran nearly five hours and included the removal of protesters.[2]

## Investigation of Jack Smith's document practices (July 2026)

In July 2026 committee chair [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] disclosed that former special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s prosecutors had obtained and reviewed text messages from 44 members of [[us-congress|Congress]], including Grassley, 19 other senators, and House Republicans such as then-GOP Leader [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]].[3] Grassley and [[ron-johnson|Sen. Ron Johnson]] issued a joint report based on whistleblower information and internal DOJ records alleging that filtering protocols had been bypassed, and Grassley announced plans to compel Smith to testify before the committee.[3]

## Bondi Confirmation Hearing (January 15, 2025)

The committee heard attorney general nominee [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] on January 15, 2025, in a session that quickly turned into a proxy fight over FBI director nominee [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]].[4] Ranking member [[dick-durbin|Dick Durbin]] raised Patel's [[qanon|QAnon]] commentary, [[amy-klobuchar|Amy Klobuchar]] questioned his statements about targeting the media, and several Democratic members pressed on his purported "enemies list" of prospective investigative targets.[4] Democrats also repeatedly asked Bondi whether Joe Biden won the 2020 election; she conceded Biden was duly sworn in but would not say Trump lost, and referred to possible fraud in Pennsylvania.[4]
