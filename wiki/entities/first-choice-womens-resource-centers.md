# First Choice Women's Resource Centers

**First Choice Women's Resource Centers** is a New Jersey network of crisis pregnancy centers that became the petitioner in a 2026 [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] case over whether such organizations may bring federal constitutional challenges to state investigations.

## The Platkin subpoena

New Jersey Attorney General [[matthew-platkin|Matthew Platkin]] issued a subpoena to First Choice seeking donor and operational records. The organization argued that the demand for private donor information would chill speech and association rights protected by the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]], and sued in federal court rather than litigating solely in state proceedings.

## Supreme Court ruling (April 2026)

The Court ruled that First Choice may proceed with its federal lawsuit, holding that the organization had established injury to its associational rights: "An official demand for private donor information is enough to discourage reasonable individuals from associating with a group." The Court did not reach the merits of the subpoena itself, deciding only that the center can pursue its claims in federal court. The decision is regarded as a procedural victory for crisis pregnancy centers and similar organizations seeking access to federal courts when challenging state enforcement actions.

Executive director Aimee Huber said that because pregnancy centers such as First Choice do not perform or refer for abortion, they are targets for a government that disagrees with their views, warning that if New Jersey's attorney general could bully the group, the same could happen in other states.

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the opinion "flew under the radar" beside [[louisiana-v-callais|Louisiana v. Callais]] but was an important one, describing New Jersey as having harassed reproductive centers that decline to perform abortions and framing the ruling as part of the dismantling of the weaponization of government: crisis pregnancy centers, she argued, now have the same rights to judicial review as their counterparts.
