# Jamie Dimon

Jamie Dimon is the chief executive officer of [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]], the largest US bank by assets.[1]

## Trump debanking lawsuit

On Thursday, January 22, 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] filed a $5 billion lawsuit against JPMorgan Chase and Dimon personally, accusing the bank of cutting off his accounts for political reasons after decades of business ties.[1] The suit was filed in Florida state court in Miami by Trump attorney Alejandro Brito.[1]

The complaint alleges that JPMorgan abruptly terminated multiple accounts belonging to Trump and his hospitality companies in early 2021, despite the accounts being in good standing and having processed hundreds of millions of dollars in transactions over the years, and that the bank provided no warning, appeal process, or alternative banking arrangements.[1] Trump's legal team argues the closures were driven by political and social considerations rather than legitimate financial or regulatory concerns, and that the bank sought to distance itself from Trump and his conservative views at a moment when doing so was politically advantageous.[1]

The lawsuit further alleges that JPMorgan and Dimon caused Trump, the Trump Organization, affiliated entities, and members of the Trump family to be placed on an internal banking blacklist shared across federally regulated institutions — a move the complaint characterizes as false, malicious, and designed to prevent other banks from doing business with them.[1]

JPMorgan Chase responded in a statement: "While we regret President Trump has sued us, we believe the suit has no merit. We respect the president's right to sue us and our right to defend ourselves — that's what courts are for."[1]

## Epstein account allegations

In 2023, former JPMorgan executive [[jes-staley|Jes Staley]] claimed in legal filings that he and Dimon had communicated about [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] — including at the time of Epstein's 2006 and 2008 arrests and repeatedly through 2012 regarding whether to keep Epstein as a client — a claim the Wall Street Journal reported as setting the stage for a major legal battle.[3] Dimon maintains that no such conversations took place, and a JPMorgan spokeswoman said there was "no evidence that any such communications ever occurred," noting that nothing appeared in the reviewed documents or the nearly dozen depositions taken, including the CEO's own, and that "the one person who claims this to be true is currently accused of horrific acts and dishonesty."[3]

## Influence over regulators

Commentary published in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] in June 2023 asserted that Dimon, as chief of [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]], effectively sets the investigative priorities of the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]] and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], citing his suggestion that authorities crack down on investors shorting bank stocks — a priority the DOJ announced two weeks later.[2] The remark appeared alongside coverage of a $4 million SEC fine imposed on JPMorgan Chase after the permanent deletion of roughly 47 million emails, which [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized as a slap on the wrist for a bank with more than $3 trillion in assets under management.[2]

## Trump's Davos debanking rebuke (January 2025)

During his January 23, 2025 video address to the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly called out Dimon and [[bank-of-america|Bank of America]] over the "de-banking" of conservatives for their politics, saying: "I hope you and Jamie open things up because what you're doing is wrong."[4] Badlands contributor Jon Herold highlighted the exchange as one of the notable moments of the Q&A, and GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that Bank of America's denial of the allegation on X drew a Community Note.[4]

## Bitcoin reversal (May 2025)

At [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]]'s annual investor day on Monday, May 19, 2025, Dimon announced that the largest US bank would soon allow clients to buy [[bitcoin|bitcoin]], a stunning reversal given his persistent criticism of the cryptocurrency.[5] "We are going to allow you to buy it," Dimon told investors. "We're not going to custody it. We're going to put it in statements for clients."[5] Dimon, then 69 and nearly two decades into the top job, made the announcement amid continuing speculation about his succession.[5]

The Queens native had long been a vocal bitcoin skeptic, citing risks such as money laundering and unclear ownership; he called the coin "worthless" in 2021 and a "pet rock" that "does nothing" in a CNBC interview at Davos in 2024, and suggested in 2023 Senate testimony that the government should shut it down over its alleged links to criminal activity.[5] Badlands contributor GMONEY called the shift a "big upgrade" from Dimon's earlier vow to fire any JPMorgan employee who traded bitcoin.[5]

## Deposition in the Epstein litigation (2023)

In late March 2023 Reuters reported that Dimon would be deposed under oath in connection with [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase's]] relationship with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], the late sex offender and a former client of the bank.[6] [[brad-edwards|Brad Edwards]], a lawyer representing women who say Epstein sexually abused them, said the interview was expected to take place in early May.[6] The plaintiffs are suing the largest U.S. bank for allegedly enabling the financier's sex trafficking.[6]

Commentary published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that the significance of the [[deutsche-bank|Deutsche Bank]] and JPMorgan suits lay less in their legal detail than in the spectacle of major banks appearing in headlines beside the words "Epstein victims" during a banking crisis.[6]

## Meeting with Zelensky at Davos (January 2024)

In January 2024, Dimon met Ukrainian president [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] for the first time at the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] in Davos, Switzerland, greeting him with "God bless you" as they shook hands ahead of a sit-down with Wall Street financiers.[7] Zelensky used the meeting to pitch Ukraine as open for business, tweeting that "attracting private capital to rebuild Ukraine is important to us" and expressing hope that [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan]] would help draw global investors and corporations into the Ukrainian economy.[7] The encounter came amid continued wrangling in Washington over a proposed $61 billion package of military and financial support, and a day after Zelensky asked his Swiss hosts to organize a "high-level peace conference" on the war with Russia.[7]

## Bitcoin criticism

A January 2024 Badlands Brief essay marking fifteen years of the [[bitcoin-genesis-block|Bitcoin Genesis Block]] referred to Dimon as "Jeffrey Epstein's Banker" and named him alongside Senator [[elizabeth-warren|Elizabeth Warren]] as figures inside whose minds [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] "lives rent-free," because the protocol cannot be stopped and continually undermines what the author described as an abstract power hierarchy.[8] The essay placed both under the heading "those who scream the loudest," treating vocal hostility to Bitcoin as evidence of the threat it poses to incumbents of the monetary system.[8]
