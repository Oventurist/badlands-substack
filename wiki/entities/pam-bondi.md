# Pam Bondi

Pam Bondi is the United States Attorney General in the second [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]. In coverage from January 2026, Bondi featured in the dispute over the tenure of [[john-sarcone|John Sarcone III]] as US Attorney for the Northern District of New York.[1]

## The Sarcone appointment

After local judges declined to reappoint Sarcone at the expiration of his 120-day interim term, Bondi named him a "special attorney" with an indefinite term — an unconventional move that formed the basis of the legal challenge to his authority.[1] US District Judge [[lorna-schofield|Lorna Schofield]] subsequently disqualified Sarcone and invalidated subpoenas his office had issued to New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]].[1]

The episode was one of several in which federal judges rejected efforts to install US attorneys without Senate confirmation, with removals also occurring in New Jersey, Delaware, Nevada, Los Angeles, and Virginia.[1]

## Cities Church response

Following the storming of [[cities-church|Cities Church]] in St. Paul, Minnesota by left-wing activists in January 2026, Attorney General Bondi spoke directly with the affected pastor, affirming that attacks on law enforcement and the intimidation of Christians would face the "full force of federal law."[2] The [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] Civil Rights Division under Assistant Attorney General [[harmeet-dhillon|Harmeet Dhillon]], together with the [[fbi|FBI]], opened an investigation into possible violations of the [[ku-klux-klan-act|Ku Klux Klan Act]] and the [[face-act|FACE Act]].[2]

## Congressional Epstein subpoena (March 2026)

In early March 2026 the [[united-states-house-oversight-committee|US House Oversight Committee]] issued subpoenas in the [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] case, one of which targeted Bondi.[3] Lawmakers sought documents and communications related to Epstein investigations conducted in Florida during Bondi's tenure as that state's attorney general.[3] The subpoenas followed allegations that key records in the investigation had not been fully released.[3]

## Comer deposition subpoena

On a Tuesday in March 2026, House Oversight and Government Reform Committee Chair [[james-comer|James Comer]] (R-Ky.) formally subpoenaed Bondi, requiring her to appear for a deposition on April 14 regarding the Justice Department's handling of the Epstein investigation and its compliance with the [[epstein-files-transparency-act|Epstein Files Transparency Act]].[4] The subpoena followed an earlier committee vote prompted by Rep. [[nancy-mace|Nancy Mace]] (R-S.C.), who raised concerns that the DOJ had not turned over all required records related to the convicted sex offender.[4] It passed with full bipartisan support from every Democrat on the panel plus several Republicans, and top Democrat [[robert-garcia|Robert Garcia]] accused Bondi of leading a "White House cover-up" of the full Epstein files.[4]

Lawmakers highlighted more than 65,000 missing documents, over 2,000 unreleased videos, and problematic redactions that in some cases appeared to shield Epstein's associates while exposing victim identities.[4] The Justice Department called the subpoena "completely unnecessary," stating that lawmakers had already been invited to review unredacted files in person at the DOJ and that Bondi had made herself available for multiple briefings, calls and meetings with Congress on the transparency law — including an offer to brief the committee the following day.[4] Subpoenas are compulsory, but enforcement ultimately rests with the Justice Department if they are ignored.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the exchange was political theater: with the Epstein files having fallen out of the public conversation because of the war, Comer and Bondi were dramatically bringing them back, and House legislators dramatized their pursuit of justice online rather than accepting the offered briefing.[4] She noted that legacy media reporting tied the "missing files" claims to sexual assault allegations against [[donald-trump|President Trump]], making the subpoena a vehicle for new narrative cycles of public character assassination ahead of the midterms.[4] She also observed that [[congress|Congress]] costs Americans $16.7 million to $21.7 million per day when in session while being unable to pass the [[save-america-act|SAVE America Act]].[4]

## ABA and judicial nominations

In 2025 Bondi sent a letter to the [[american-bar-association|American Bar Association]] stripping its preferred access to the judicial nomination process, writing that "while the ABA is free to comment on judicial nominations along with other activist organizations, there is no justification for treating the ABA differently from such other activist organizations and the Department of Justice will not do so."[5] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the letter as the precursor to Rep. [[brandon-gill|Brandon Gill]]'s July 2026 move to end the ABA's role in law school accreditation.[5]

## Congressional testimony on Epstein (February 2026)

Bondi testified before Congress on Wednesday, February 11, 2026, in a hearing described as contentious.[6] Lawmakers questioned her about the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s handling of the [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] material, and the session also included questioning on broader Justice Department oversight and policy issues.[6] Following the hearing, Epstein survivors spoke publicly in response to Bondi's testimony, according to NBC video reporting.[6]

## Fifth Circuit detention ruling (February 2026)

After the [[fifth-circuit-court-of-appeals|Fifth Circuit]] reversed two district-court orders requiring bond hearings or release for detained noncitizens in *Buenrostro-Mendez v. Bondi*, Bondi — the named respondent — said [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] attorneys had secured a "crucial legal victory" supporting the administration's immigration agenda.[7]

## Voter-roll lawsuits (February 2026)

In February 2026 Bondi and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s Civil Rights Division announced federal lawsuits against Utah, Oklahoma, Kentucky, West Virginia, and New Jersey for failing to provide complete voter registration lists, bringing the total to 29 states plus the District of Columbia.[8] Bondi stated that the actions reflected enforcement of the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act]] and the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]] to ensure accurate and transparent voter rolls.[8]

## ISIS terrorism prosecution (March 2026)

Announcing charges in March 2026 against two [[isis|ISIS]] supporters accused of attempting to detonate explosive devices at a protest, Bondi said, "This was an alleged ISIS-inspired act of terrorism that could have killed American citizens," and added that the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] "will not allow ISIS's poisonous, anti-American ideology to threaten this nation — our law enforcement officers will remain vigilant, as they were when these devices were brought to a protest."[9]

## House Judiciary Committee Epstein hearing (February 2026)

Bondi testified at a contentious February 2026 hearing before the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] on the [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] matter, after which both she and the legislators faced criticism for their conduct.[10] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] defended her on Truth Social, writing that "AG Pam Bondi, under intense fire from the Trump Deranged Radical Left Lunatics, was fantastic at yesterday's Hearing on the never ending saga of Jeffrey Epstein," and asserting that the proceeding had conclusively exonerated him while implicating "SLIMEBALL Democrats, many of them big Donors and Politicians."[10] Trump used the same post to attack Representative [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] and to characterize the [[epstein-narrative|Epstein story]] as a Democratic distraction from Republican successes.[10]

## February 2026: Massie confrontation over redactions

In a further account of the same [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] hearing, Bondi was directly confronted by Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]], who pressed the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] to fully unredact and disclose the [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]] documents and highlighted suspicious redactions that the DOJ quickly corrected after his objections.[11] [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] separately accused the Trump administration of slow-walking the files and attempting a cover-up, demanding complete public disclosure and open congressional testimony.[11]

## Washington Post reporter search (January 2026)

Attorney General Bondi publicly defended the January 14, 2026 [[fbi|FBI]] search of the Virginia home of [[washington-post|Washington Post]] reporter [[hannah-natanson|Hannah Natanson]], saying the raid was conducted at the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]'s request and stressing a zero-tolerance approach to leaks of classified information.[12] She stated that the individual suspected of the leak — [[pentagon|Pentagon]] contractor [[aurelio-perez-lugones|Aurelio Perez-Lugones]] — was "behind bars."[12] Press freedom groups condemned the operation as highly unusual and a potential threat to [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] protections.[12]

## House Oversight testimony on the Epstein files (May 2026)

In a closed-door interview with the [[united-states-house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] on Friday, May 29, 2026, Bondi — by then the former Attorney General — said the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] had produced the Epstein-related materials required under the [[epstein-files-transparency-act|Epstein Files Transparency Act]], according to The Epoch Times.[13] Her written opening statement said then-Deputy Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] oversaw the file-release process; she acknowledged redaction errors while defending the department's compliance and transparency, in an account republished by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]].[13] Rep. [[james-comer|James Comer]] said transcripts of the interview would be released as quickly as possible.[13]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Bondi's "everything required" formulation echoed the election apparatus's defense of records wiped during the Trusted Build — "we preserved all the records we were required to under the law" — and read the phrasing as implicit shade directed at lawmakers to pass better statutes.[13] She rejected as tiresome the suggestion that the administration was covering up predatory networks, while agreeing that government dodging of inquiry and transparency in this manner was itself objectionable.[13]


## December memo on antifa and extremist financing

A December memo issued by Bondi instructed federal agencies to prioritize investigations of [[antifa|antifa]] and other extremist groups, highlighting potential violence, anti-American and anti-religious ideologies, and tax fraud as key concerns, and ordering law enforcement to compile intelligence on such groups for the [[fbi|FBI]].[14] The memo built on a September executive order from [[donald-trump|President Trump]] on [[domestic-terrorism|domestic terrorism]] issued after high-profile incidents including the 2025 assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[14] It underpinned the March 2026 FBI-[[internal-revenue-service|IRS]] joint task force targeting nonprofits suspected of funding political violence and a parallel [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] task force probing antifa-linked funding.[14]

## Departure from the Justice Department (April 2026)

Longstanding rumors of Bondi's imminent ouster as Attorney General were confirmed on 3 April 2026 by a statement [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] issued on Truth Social.[15] Trump called Bondi "a Great American Patriot and a loyal friend, who faithfully served as my Attorney General over the past year," credited her with "overseeing a massive crackdown in Crime across our Country, with Murders plummeting to their lowest level since 1900," and said she "will be transitioning to a much needed and important new job in the private sector, to be announced at a date in the near future."[15] The same statement named Deputy Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] — described by Trump as "a very talented and respected Legal Mind" — as Acting Attorney General.[15]

Badlands commentary characterized the announcement as following the "Noem model" of a soft firing dressed up as a promotion or lateral move.[15] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] asked whether Badlands hosts were permitted to question whether Trump's cabinet members are actually read in on his plan, are active opponents of it, or are simply superfluous, noting that Badlands remained the only outlet in the space to interrogate the loyalty and standing of such figures while still defending Trump's plan, on the view that the two positions are not mutually exclusive.[15] Credit was given to [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]], who had been called a doomer for two years for arguing that the "TV characters" in Trump's cabinet were exactly that and might be active opponents of his agenda.[15] The commentary concluded that Bondi — like most of the cabinet — mattered little on an actual level and only slightly more on a narrative one, likely serving as a narrative shield while Trump advanced his true agenda behind the scenes.[15]

## September 2025 enforcement actions

Minutes after the September 25, 2025 federal grand jury indictment of former FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] for false statements to [[congress|Congress]], Bondi said, "No one is above the law."[16] The same day, the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] announced identical lawsuits against California, Michigan, Minnesota, New York, New Hampshire and Pennsylvania for failing to produce their statewide voter registration lists in response to DOJ requests made under [[executive-order-14248|Executive Order 14248]].[16] Bondi said in a statement that "clean voter rolls are the foundation of free and fair elections," adding that "every state has a responsibility to ensure that voter registration records are accurate, accessible, and secure — states that don't fulfill that obligation will see this Department of Justice in court."[16]

## Unredaction of the Comey leak memos (2025)

In August 2025, Attorney General Bondi intervened to eliminate redactions in declassified memos concerning former [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[james-comey|James Comey]], dispatching unredacted versions to the House and Senate Judiciary committees.[17] The memos, discovered earlier that year by FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]], recorded evidence gathered by U.S. Postal Inspection Service agents from former FBI general counsel [[james-baker|James Baker]] and former Comey chief of staff James Rybicki indicating that Comey had authorized leaks of classified information to *The New York Times* in October 2016, before the election won by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[17] The passages had originally been redacted by the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] in the versions first sent to [[congress|Congress]].[17]

## Epstein memo backlash and Trump's defense (July 2025)

In July 2025 Bondi faced growing calls from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s supporters for her resignation after a memo released by her [[justice-department|Justice Department]] failed to deliver what many had hoped would be major revelations in the [[epstein-files|Epstein]] case.[18] Critics accused the attorney general of withholding information about the death of sex offender [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] and his so-called client list.[18] Trump defended her in a lengthy social media post, writing in all caps, "Let Pam Bondi do her job," and encouraging his supporters not to "waste time and energy" on Epstein.[18]

Badlands host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] responded that the willingness of some commentators to lie in service of a presumed plan was itself the problem, urging humility: "we don't know the full truth ... or we wouldn't be asking for the files."[18] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] contrasted the moment with an earlier Trump clip on Epstein and questioned why legacy media focused only on the later one, adding that "treason ends the game."[18] The controversy intensified after [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] suggested at [[turning-point-usa|Turning Point USA]]'s Student Action Summit that Epstein had been working for Israeli intelligence.[18]

## The Kirk case as a test (September 2025)

## Termination of ABA judicial-nominee vetting (May 2025)

On May 29, 2025, Attorney General Bondi sent a letter to the [[american-bar-association|American Bar Association]] informing it that the Justice Department would no longer vet judicial nominees through the group, citing its alleged bias in rating nominees.[21] Bondi wrote to ABA President William Bay that "the ABA no longer functions as a fair arbiter of nominees' qualifications, and its ratings invariably and demonstrably favor nominees put forth by Democratic administrations," and that there was "no justification for treating the ABA differently from such other activist organizations."[21] The policy revoked the association's access to certain non-public information about nominees and ended the practice of having nominees interview with or answer questions from the ABA, while leaving it free to comment publicly like any other group.[21] Badlands commentary treated the decision as a clear win and a precedent for stripping other partisan NGOs of privileged standing.[21]

## Colorado Springs nightclub raid (April 2025)

Bondi publicly linked the April 27, 2025 [[colorado-springs-nightclub-raid|Colorado Springs nightclub raid]] to organized crime, saying in a post on X that the club was frequented by members of the Venezuelan prison gang [[tren-de-aragua|Tren de Aragua]] and the international gang [[ms-13|MS-13]].[22] Badlands reporting noted that national social media deployments about the raid came from Bondi and border czar [[tom-homan|Tom Homan]], alongside local FBI, DEA and ICE channels.[22]

## Attorney General Confirmation Hearing (January 15, 2025)

At her confirmation hearing before the [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]], Bondi declined to say that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] lost the 2020 election when pressed by ranking member [[dick-durbin|Dick Durbin]].[23] She acknowledged that "President Biden is the president of the United States. He was duly sworn in," and characterized the post-2020 handover as "a peaceful transition of power" in which "President Trump left office and was overwhelmingly elected in 2024," but stopped short of the concession Democrats sought.[23] Bondi went on to raise possible fraud in Pennsylvania, where she had aided the Trump campaign in challenging the election result, and stated that the 2020 election remained an ongoing investigation.[23]

Badlands commentary framed Bondi as "a confirmed election denier" and treated that as a badge of honor, arguing that senators including Durbin, [[mazie-hirono|Mazie Hirono]] and [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] repeatedly asked whether Biden won in 2020 and then reclaimed their time to grandstand rather than let her describe what she "saw in Pennsylvania."[23] Much of the hearing was in practice devoted not to Bondi but to [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]], Trump's FBI director pick, whom Democrats appeared to view as an easier target than Bondi, who was seen as being on a glide path to confirmation.[23]

## Confirmation as Attorney General (February 2025)

The United States Senate confirmed Bondi on Tuesday, February 4, 2025, as Attorney General, tasked with steering the Justice Department through Donald Trump's second term.[24] The vote was 54 to 46 and fell largely along party lines, with all Republicans voting to confirm and every Democratic senator except John Fetterman voting against.[24] Bondi took charge at a tumultuous moment for the department, which had already been making sweeping personnel changes across senior leadership and the previous week had fired the prosecutors who worked on the criminal cases against Trump, at the direction of the president himself.[24] Critics characterized the appointment as part of a clear intent to turn the department into an extension of executive power, particularly against Trump's personal and political adversaries.[24]

Badlands commentator Paul Fleuret emphasized that in her confirmation hearing Bondi would not admit that Joe Biden won the 2020 election and referred to the 2020 election as an "ongoing investigation."[24] He noted that the Attorney General is the chief law enforcement officer of the United States, wields immense power, and sits on the National Security Council — significant, in his framing, because stolen elections are an exponential national security concern.[24] As Attorney General, Fleuret argued, Bondi would have access to all kinds of information including anything touching the 2020 election, about which she had been outspoken, including describing what she witnessed firsthand in Pennsylvania.[24] He tied the timing of her vote to Q Drop 3831, a February 5th delta featuring the "Panic In DC" meme and a message that those who tried to subvert the country would not escape unscathed.[24]

## ODNI Strike Force and Epstein grand jury filings (July 2025)

## July 2025 DOJ memo on the Epstein "client list"

On Monday, July 7, 2025, the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] issued a memo stating that sex offender [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] did not keep a "client list" linking celebrities and public officials to sex trafficking, angering MAGA commentators.[30] The declaration came on the heels of Bondi, as Attorney General, indicating to Fox News in February that such a document was "sitting on my desk" waiting to be scrutinized; for months she had claimed that significant information about the disgraced financier ignored by the Biden administration would come to light.[30] White House Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] and Justice Department spokesperson Chad Gilmartin told reporters that Bondi had meant to say all of the files regarding Epstein were on her desk — not a specific list of co-conspirators.[30] Badlands contributors responded caustically, with [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] writing that the "copium is off the charts" and [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] asking whether this version of the Epstein files could have been released on day one and whether investigators had spent five months confirming nothing.[30]

## Day-one directives as Attorney General (February 2025)

On her first day leading the Justice Department in February 2025, Bondi issued several major directives, including orders to combat the weaponization of the legal system, to require prosecutors to seek the death penalty where appropriate, and to work with the Department of Homeland Security to "completely eliminate" cartels and transnational criminal organizations.[31]

One directive addressed "zealous advocacy," stating that the responsibilities of Justice Department attorneys include "aggressively enforcing criminal laws passed by Congress, but also vigorously defending presidential policies and actions on behalf of the United States against legal challenges."[31] The memo added that prosecutorial discretion "does not include latitude to substitute their personal political views or judgments for those that prevailed in the election."[31]

Bondi also moved to establish a [[weaponization-working-group|Weaponization Working Group]] tasked with reviewing the activities of all federal law enforcement agencies over the preceding four years to identify instances of "politicized justice."[31] Bondi had been confirmed as Attorney General by the Senate before this point in the second Trump administration's confirmation sequence.[31]

## Russia collusion grand jury order, August 2025

On Monday, 4 August 2025, Bondi signed an order directing a federal prosecutor to present evidence to a grand jury to weigh charges related to the Russia collusion hoax, multiple sources confirmed to Just the News.[32] The step followed criminal referrals sent to the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], who in July 2025 said she had forwarded evidence that U.S. intelligence officials politicized intelligence connected to the Russia collusion investigation, calling it a "treasonous conspiracy."[32] Convening a grand jury does not guarantee charges; the panel decides whether sufficient evidence exists to recommend prosecution.[32]

Bondi had earlier established a Justice Department strike force to investigate the affair, and reporting on newly declassified 2016 material framed the central question before it as whether the [[hillary-clinton|Clinton]] campaign's plan to tie [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] was a political dirty trick or a criminal conspiracy.[32] See the [[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate hoax]].[32]

## Selection as attorney general nominee (November 2024)

Within hours of [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]]'s withdrawal from consideration as attorney general on 21 November 2024, [[donald-trump|Trump]] announced on social media that he had selected Bondi, the former Florida attorney general, to run the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]], and over the following weekend filled out the remainder of his Cabinet selections.[33] Bondi's selection reframed the search for an [[fbi|FBI]] director, with Missouri Attorney General [[andrew-bailey|Andrew Bailey]] and [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] both reported to be in contention.[33]

## Ten-year stain lawfare investigation (December 2025)

In written answers provided to [[just-the-news|Just the News]] on December 28, 2025 while recovering from eye surgery, Bondi disclosed that she had asked prosecutors to investigate the Obama-Biden era of [[lawfare]] as an ongoing election-meddling conspiracy that shielded Democrats from criminal investigation and infringed the civil rights of Republicans, including President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his supporters.[34] Her position aligned with [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]], whose earlier memo predicated an investigation into the weaponization of intelligence and law-enforcement powers dating to the Russia collusion case as a continuing conspiracy — a framing that permits charges for conduct otherwise barred by the statute of limitations, as in mafia and cartel prosecutions.[34] "At my direction, our U.S. Attorneys and federal agents are actively investigating instances of government weaponization nationwide," Bondi said, calling the period "a ten-year stain on the country committed by high-ranking officials against the American people."[34] The effort is documented at [[obama-biden-lawfare-conspiracy-investigation|the Obama-Biden lawfare conspiracy investigation]].[34]
