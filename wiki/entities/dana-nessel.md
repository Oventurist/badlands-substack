# Dana Nessel

Dana Nessel is the Democratic Attorney General of Michigan.[1]

## Prosecution of the 2020 alternate electors

In 2023, Nessel charged sixteen Michigan Republicans with eight felonies each — forgery and conspiracy counts — for signing documents claiming that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had won the state's 2020 presidential vote.[1] One defendant's charges were dropped after he agreed to cooperate with the prosecution.[1] The remaining case collapsed on September 9, 2025, when District Court Judge [[kristen-simmons|Kristen Simmons]] dismissed all charges for lack of evidence of intent, ruling that the signatories had been exercising a constitutional right to seek redress.[1] Badlands commentators cast the failed prosecution as an example of [[lawfare|lawfare]] in which the years-long legal process, and the millions in defense costs it imposed, functioned as the real punishment.[1]

## Charges against the 2020 Michigan Republican electors

In July 2023 Nessel, the Michigan Attorney General, charged 16 of the state's 2020 Trump electors with eight felonies each, including forgery, election law forgery, utterance and conspiracy; many of those charged were retirees in their seventies and eighties.[2] Badlands commentary described the prosecutions as an asymmetric application of the rule of law, noting that the [[john-podesta|Podesta]]-overseen Biden campaign had itself prepared alternate-elector contingency plans, and criticised media use of the term "fake electors."[2]

## FOIA disclosures on 2020 Michigan election fraud reports

In February 2024, FOIA documents reported by [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] were said to show that government officials had been notified of election fraud allegations in Michigan immediately after Election Day in 2020, undercutting Nessel's premise that the state's alternate electors had "no reason" to believe the election was anything other than "the most secure election in history."[3] Coverage noted that Nessel had charged the 16 Republican electors — many of them senior citizens — with eight felonies each in July 2023, exposure that critics said could mean spending the rest of their lives in prison, and that at her announcement press conference she stated the electors were guilty, a striking assertion from the state's top law enforcement officer before the defendants had had their day in court.[3] Commentators characterized the effort as a political persecution rather than a prosecution.[3]

The same reporting recalled that Nessel herself had been aware that in October 2020, a month before the election, a full-scale investigation was underway involving the Muskegon Police Department, the Michigan State Police, lead investigators from the Attorney General's own office, and Secretary of State [[jocelyn-benson|Jocelyn Benson]]'s office, concerning more than ten thousand voter registrations turned in by a single individual at the Muskegon city clerk's office.[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the persecution of both [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the Michigan electors rests on an alternative theory of intent — that because 2020 was supposedly beyond reproach, any challenge must have been nefarious — and that documented cause to doubt the results dissolves that theory.[3] She highlighted the disclosure that Department of Justice officials knew on November 5, 2020, two days after the election, of a very serious accusation of voter fraud reported at Detroit's TCF Center on election night, calling it potential proof of a cover-up implicating the highest levels of Michigan's state executive branch.[3]
