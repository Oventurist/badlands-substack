# Palisades Nuclear Plant

The Palisades Nuclear Plant is a nuclear generating station in Michigan, owned by Holtec International, that became the subject of the first attempt to restart a shuttered American nuclear plant.[1]

## 2024 federal loan and restart

On September 30, 2024, the [[department-of-energy|Energy Department]] announced it had finalized a $1.5 billion loan to stake the revival of the Holtec Palisades plant, which would make it the first American nuclear plant ever to be restarted.[1] The announcement came a week after [[microsoft]] and Constellation Energy unveiled a $1.6 billion power purchase agreement to restart a dormant reactor at Pennsylvania's [[three-mile-island-nuclear-power-plant|Three Mile Island]] plant in 2028.[1]

Both moves were reported as part of a broader [[nuclear-renaissance]] driven by the energy appetite of AI data centers, with U.S. electricity demand rising after fifteen flat years on the strength of new factories, data centers, electric vehicles and hotter summers.[1] At Climate Week in New York, fourteen of the world's largest banks and financial institutions pledged to support the COP28 goal of tripling global nuclear capacity by 2050.[1]

Badlands commentary read the Palisades loan as an "Actual Layer" data point in a convergence of nuclear narratives — Department of Energy and Department of Defense initiatives to kickstart nuclear restoration ahead of Trump's return, mostly proposed under Trump and actualized under Biden — set against a "Narrative Layer" fear campaign running from the film *Oppenheimer* to coverage of nuclear threats posed by the so-called New Axis.[1]

## 2024 federal loan and restart plan

In March 2024, the Loan Programs Office of the US Energy Department, directed by [[jigar-shah|Jigar Shah]], approved a loan to [[holtec-international|Holtec International Corp.]] to reopen the Palisades plant.[2] Holtec had bought the facility when it closed in 2022, intending to decommission it, but officials including Michigan Governor Gretchen Whitmer began calling almost immediately for the plant to reopen; the company subsequently shifted gears and planned for Palisades to begin producing power again in late 2025.[2]

The Palisades restart was reported as the leading edge of a broader shift in American energy policy. A month after the United States offered $1.5 billion to restart the shuttered plant, industry and government officials expressed a growing sense that it would not be the last such revival, with Shah saying that "there are a couple of nuclear power plants that we probably should, and can, turn back on," and observers expecting the precedent to spur other companies to follow suit.[2]

Badlands Media writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the Palisades financing as evidence for his "The Switch" thesis — that despite catastrophic narratives around energy transition, the concrete measures being implemented pointed toward an energy-abundant future, with new nuclear jobs and reduced reliance on solar and wind.[2]
