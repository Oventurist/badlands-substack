# Technical Alliance

The Technical Alliance was an organization founded by the economist [[thorstein-veblen|Thorstein Veblen]] in connection with his founding of the [[new-school-for-social-research|New School for Social Research]] in Greenwich Village, New York.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] describes it as the direct precursor of [[technocracy-inc|Technocracy Inc.]], which [[howard-scott|Howard Scott]] built out of it in 1932.[1]

## Activities

Scott joined Veblen's New School study group after the First World War, in 1919, and the Alliance subsequently received contracts from the Industrial Workers of the World and the Railroad Brotherhood to accumulate voluminous data on America's agriculture, industry, raw materials and workers, including worker behaviour and psychological profiles.[1] Ehret writes that despite the absence of advanced computers, the organization became obsessed with the challenge of mass data collection and surveillance of every element of the economy and of worker behaviour, in order to acquire enough information to "scientifically" control society.[1] Members of the Technical Alliance were photographed at [[columbia-university|Columbia University]] around 1930.[1]

## Doctrine and successor

Through the Alliance, Veblen advanced his concept of a "Soviet of Engineers" managed by a central directorate presiding over "sub-centers and local councils" — the structure from which Scott derived the Technate a few years later.[1] Ehret argues that the Alliance was one node of a broader transatlantic project, with the New School serving as the American entry point for [[fabian-society|Fabian Society]] and London School of Economics ideas, making it "part of the exact same agency" as the Canadian Fabian networks in which [[joshua-haldeman|Joshua Haldeman]] operated.[1]
