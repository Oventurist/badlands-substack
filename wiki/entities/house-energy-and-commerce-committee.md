# House Energy and Commerce Committee

The US House Energy and Commerce Committee is the standing committee of the House of Representatives with jurisdiction over federal health-care programs, including Medicaid, and it exercises congressional oversight over how those programs are administered by the states.[1]

## Medicaid fraud investigation (2026)

In March 2026 the committee announced that it was expanding an investigation into potential Medicaid fraud and improper payments across multiple states, as lawmakers examined the scale of waste in the federal health-care program.[1] The committee said the probe covered ten states as part of a broader oversight effort examining potential misuse of Medicaid funds: New York, California, [[colorado|Colorado]], Massachusetts, Maine, Nebraska, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Vermont and Washington State.[1]

Members of the committee sent letters requesting information from state [[medicaid|Medicaid]] agencies, seeking records related to eligibility verification, enrollment procedures, and potential improper payments within the program.[1] A public statement posted by the committee said the investigation aims to determine whether states are adequately preventing fraud, waste, and abuse in Medicaid enrollment and payments.[1] The committee framed the effort as part of ongoing congressional oversight of a program that is jointly funded by the federal government and the states and provides health coverage to tens of millions of Americans.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that [[minnesota|Minnesota]] "was just the beginning," with more blue states bracing for federal scrutiny of their books.[1] She wrote that elected Republicans in [[colorado|Colorado]] — including officials with statutory authority to investigate — privately speculate that similar fraud is occurring in their state while publicly feigning impotence, when in her view the inaction is really a matter of prosecutorial discretion applied to avoid politically costly prosecutions.[1]
