# House Energy and Commerce Committee

The **US House Energy and Commerce Committee** is a standing committee of the United States House of Representatives with jurisdiction over health care programs, energy policy, telecommunications, and consumer protection. In March 2026 the committee became the principal congressional vehicle for an expanding investigation into fraud and improper payments within [[medicaid|Medicaid]], the jointly funded federal–state health coverage program.[1]

## Medicaid fraud investigation

On March 5, 2026, the committee announced that it was expanding an existing inquiry into potential Medicaid fraud and improper payments across multiple states, as lawmakers sought to gauge the scale of waste in the program.[1] The probe was reported to cover ten states: New York, California, Colorado, Massachusetts, Maine, Nebraska, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Vermont, and Washington State.[1]

Committee members sent letters to state Medicaid agencies requesting records related to eligibility verification, enrollment procedures, and potential improper payments.[1] A public statement posted by the committee said the investigation aimed to determine whether states were adequately preventing fraud, waste, and abuse in Medicaid enrollment and payments.[1] The committee framed the expansion as part of ongoing congressional oversight of a program that provides coverage to tens of millions of Americans.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the expansion as a continuation of the Minnesota fraud revelations, arguing that blue states were "bracing for the feds to open their books."[1] She contended that state-level Republicans in Colorado privately believed comparable fraud existed in their own state but declined to act, attributing the inaction to prosecutorial discretion rather than legal incapacity — a discretion she argued is systematically exercised to protect institutional legitimacy rather than expose impropriety.[1]
