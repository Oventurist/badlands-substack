# Otpor!

Otpor! was a civic youth movement and political organization in Serbia, then part of the Federal Republic of [[yugoslavia|Yugoslavia]], which Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] presents as the quintessential demonstration of [[nonviolent-action|nonviolent action]] in subversive geopolitics.[1]

## Origins and character

Otpor! began as a civic protest group operating from 1998 to 2000 and evolved into a movement styling itself Narodni pokret, the People's Movement.[1] DeLarme writes that, "like any successful movement, it was infiltrated, co-opted, and steered by Western forces."[1] The group was not only filled with radicalized civilians but with trained operatives as well.[1]

## Western training and funding

For over a decade beforehand, [[gene-sharp|Gene Sharp]]'s [[albert-einstein-institution|Albert Einstein Institution]] had been reaching out to "Slovenian democrats" and "Albanian students," work that culminated in a workshop on nonviolent action held in March 2000.[1] Funding for that workshop came from the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]], which the article characterizes as the CIA's right-wing NGO.[1]

Sharp's *The Politics of Nonviolent Action* was translated and compiled as the "Otpor! User Manual."[1] [[freedom-house|Freedom House]], another U.S. "democracy promotion" NGO and consistent recipient of [[national-endowment-for-democracy|NED]] funds, financed the translation, production and distribution of 5,000 copies of Sharp's pamphlet *[[from-dictatorship-to-democracy|From Dictatorship to Democracy]]*, disseminated to 70,000 activists and operatives in Serbia; the article cites *A Force More Powerful* as its source for these statements.[1] NED would ultimately pour millions of dollars into Otpor.[1]

The link between Otpor! and the [[united-states-institute-of-peace|United States Institute of Peace]] ran through [[daniel-serwer|Daniel Serwer]], director of the institute's Balkans Initiative.[1] DeLarme concludes that the fingerprints of the IRI, USIP, NED and Freedom House are all over the movement — all major nodes in the NATO/CIA/Pentagon NGO proxy network erected to carry out functions once ascribed openly to the CIA.[1]

## Toppling Milosevic

Otpor! became the Western proxy whose "nonviolent action" finally toppled the regime of [[slobodan-milosevic|Slobodan Milosevic]], who was removed from office in September 2000 and apprehended in 2001 for war crimes before being transported — despite assurances to the contrary — to The Hague for trial.[1] The Guardian reported that the U.S. government officially spent $41 million organizing and funding the year-long operation to remove Milosevic beginning in October 1999, an episode known as the [[bulldozer-revolution|Bulldozer Revolution]]; the comparable Ukrainian figure was around $14 million.[1] DeLarme likens the result to "a sort of January 6th scenario on steroids."[1]

## Export and legacy

Otpor! became the model for similar movements in the United States and abroad, complete with the clenched fist, menacing aesthetics and psychologically manipulative slogans and chants.[1] Otpor activists trained by Robert Helvey became, in one Serbian analyst's phrase quoted by [[mark-r-beissinger|Mark R. Beissinger]], "a modern type of mercenary," traveling the world in the pay of the U.S. government or NGOs to train local groups in organizing democratic revolutions.[1] Leaders of the Ukrainian youth movement [[pora|Pora]] were trained in Serbia at the Center for Nonviolent Resistance, a consulting organization set up by Otpor activists.[1] Otpor! leaders Srdja Popović and Slobodan Djinović went on to co-found the [[canvas|Center for Applied Nonviolent Action and Strategies]] (CANVAS).[1] Serbian Otpor veterans and Georgians from Kmara were brought to Ukraine to coach organizers of the [[orange-revolution|Orange Revolution]].[1]

Today, the article notes, the countries of the former Yugoslavia — which enjoyed political freedom and a relatively high standard of living under socialism — are consistently among the most impoverished in Europe, while Otpor! has become a favored model for protest movements in the United States.[1]

## IRI and NDI backing

"Two Wings, One Ugly Bird" describes Otpor as the Serbian youth movement that ousted [[slobodan-milosevic|Slobodan Milosevic]] in the 1990s with funding funnelled by the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]] and additional help from [[george-soros|George Soros]]' [[open-society-foundations|Open Society]] network.[2] Training camps, cash and strategy sessions are said to have turned a ragtag resistance into a polished battering ram branded as "people power," with the [[national-democratic-institute|National Democratic Institute]] shaping the opposition coalition that took power afterwards and producing a government friendlier to NATO and United States interests.[2]
