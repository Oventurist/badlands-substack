# Ted Kaczynski

**Theodore "Ted" Kaczynski**, known as the Unabomber, is the subject of a two-part treatment by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d)

Ehret introduced Kaczynski in an earlier feature and returned to him in "On Ted Kaczynski and False Prophets," published 25 October 2025 and later highlighted in the 7 November 2025 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] retrospective.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d) The organizing claim of that treatment is that Kaczynski shared common axioms with the leading oligarchists running the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] and the [[great-reset|Great Reset]] — that is, that the Unabomber's anti-industrial philosophy converges with the outlook of the technocratic elite rather than opposing it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d) The "false prophets" framing positions Kaczynski as one of several figures whose apparent radicalism serves, in Ehret's reading, the same anti-industrial agenda he associates with [[transhumanism|transhumanism]] and [[eco-terrorism|eco-terrorism]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-look-at-recent-features-84d)

## Invoked as a comparison to Israeli device attacks

In September 2024, Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] invoked Kaczynski as a benchmark when assessing the [[2024-lebanon-pager-attacks|Israeli pager and walkie-talkie attacks]] in [[lebanon|Lebanon]], asking how the operations differed objectively from Kaczynski's mailing of bombs to people between 1978 and 1995 in an attempt to foment chaos and anarchy.[2] He noted that Kaczynski killed three people and injured 23 across that seventeen-year period, a toll he described as "lackluster" compared with the casualties produced in Lebanon within 24 hours.[2]

## The Harvard experiments and MK Ultra

In the second part of his treatment, Ehret argued that Kaczynski — increasingly sold as a modern visionary on the strength of the technology critique in his 1995 manifesto [[industrial-society-and-its-future|Industrial Society and Its Future]] — is much less a philosopher of technology than a disturbed product of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s [[mk-ultra|MK Ultra]] mind control project of the Cold War.[3] Writing in a June 2000 issue of *[[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]]*, Alston Chase reported that from the fall of 1959 through the spring of 1962, Harvard psychologists led by [[henry-a-murray|Henry A. Murray]] conducted "a disturbing and what would now be seen as ethically indefensible experiment on twenty-two undergraduates," selected for their high intelligence and referred to by code name only.[3] The student dubbed "Lawful" was Theodore John Kaczynski, who would one day be known as the Unabomber and who would mail or deliver sixteen package bombs to scientists, academicians and others over seventeen years, killing three people and injuring twenty-three.[3]

Ehret notes that Kaczynski's years as a subject coincide with the [[harvard-psilocybin-project|Harvard Psilocybin Project]] of 1960–62, run by [[timothy-leary|Timothy Leary]] with Murray's blessing and overseen directly by MK Ultra's [[sidney-gottlieb|Sidney Gottlieb]] — the same period, he writes, in which Kaczynski was going through his drug-assisted transformation.[3]

## Denial of free will and of ideas

Ehret isolates Kaczynski's core assumption: "The system does not and cannot exist to satisfy human needs. Instead, it is human behavior that has to be modified to fit the needs of the system. It is the fault of technology, because the system is guided not by ideology but by technical necessity."[3] In that statement, he argues, Kaczynski denies both free will and the causal role of ideas — right and wrong — in shaping history.[3] The fundamental assumptions of eco-terrorists such as the Unabomber, Andreas Malm, David Skribina, Roger Hallam and Derrick Jensen are therefore, in his reading, identical with those of death cultists like [[prince-philip|Prince Philip]], [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]] and [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]]: one side adapts to the supposed consequences of technological progress and becomes an alpha in the emergent [[brave-new-world|Brave New World]], while the other goes maximum John Savage and burns it all down.[3] Ehret treats this as a [[double-bind-theory|double bind]] and a false dichotomy leading to the same techno-feudal dystopia.[3]
