# Medmenham Abbey

Medmenham Abbey is the English property, leased by [[francis-dashwood|Sir Francis Dashwood]], identified in the Badlands Media essay "In Defense of Benjamin Franklin" as the meeting place of the [[hellfire-club|Hell-Fire Club]].[1]

The Abbey had formerly been a Cistercian house in the thirteenth century.[1] The article contends that, like all Mithraic cults, the site was chosen for the vast caverns beneath it, which had served as Mithraic altars built during Roman times; it was in these underground caverns that the ceremonies of the [[eleusinian-mysteries|Eleusinian Mysteries]] were performed by leading figures of London's satanic club.[1]

Above the caverns Dashwood restored the decrepit St. Lawrence Church in 1751, modelling it on the Sol Invictus (Mithraic) Temple recently discovered at Palmyra, and the society that met there took the name "The Order of Knights of St. Francis," sometimes called "The Medmenham Monks."[1] After the abbey was redesigned in the gothic style, a banner reading "Do What thou Wilt" was placed above the entrance.[1]

Hellfire Club member [[john-wilkes|John Wilkes]] described the secrecy of the site, stating that "No profane eye dared to penetrate the English Eleusinian Mysteries of the Chapter Room where the Monks assembled on solemn occasions […] secret rites performed and libations to the Bona Dea."[1]
