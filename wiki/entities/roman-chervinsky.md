# Roman Chervinsky

Roman Chervinsky is a decorated Ukrainian colonel, 48 years old at the time of reporting, who served in Ukraine's special operations forces and maintained deep ties to the country's intelligence services.[1] In November 2023 the Washington Post reported that Chervinsky played a central role in the bombing of the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream]] natural gas pipelines, according to officials in Ukraine and elsewhere in Europe as well as other people knowledgeable about the covert operation.[1]

## Role in the Nord Stream operation

According to the reporting, Chervinsky served as the "coordinator" of the Nord Stream operation, managing logistics and support for a six-person team that rented a sailboat under false identities and used deep-sea diving equipment to place explosive charges on the pipelines.[1] On September 26, 2022, three explosions caused massive leaks on the Nord Stream 1 and 2 pipelines, which run from Russia to Germany beneath the Baltic Sea, leaving only one of the four gas links in the network intact as winter approached.[1]

The account of Chervinsky's role was described as the most direct evidence to date tying Ukraine's military and security leadership to an act of sabotage that has spawned multiple criminal investigations and that U.S. and Western officials have characterized as a dangerous attack on Europe's energy infrastructure.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the report naming Chervinsky was "literally meant to provide Narrative Shielding" for the [[cia|CIA]], and should be treated as such.[1] He pointed to the earlier Seymour Hersh report, published nine months prior, which documented how elements within the Biden administration allegedly circumvented the U.S. chain of command to use proxies on the ground to sabotage German energy infrastructure as a false flag against Russia.[1] While conceding that Ukrainian assets likely were used, Burning Bright framed the admissions as an attempt by the media to pin agency operations onto proxies rather than on the planners in Washington, summarizing the dynamic as "Deflection = Defeat."[1]
