# Los Angeles Dodgers

The Los Angeles Dodgers are a Major League Baseball franchise based in Los Angeles, California. In May 2023 the club became a focal point of the broader backlash against corporate progressive activism after a reversal-and-re-reversal over the participation of the [[sisters-of-perpetual-indulgence|Sisters of Perpetual Indulgence]] in the team's annual Pride Night.[1]

## 2023 Pride Night controversy

The Dodgers had originally planned to host the Sisters of Perpetual Indulgence as honored guests at Pride Night and to present the group with the organization's Community Hero Award.[1] Following objections, the club disinvited the group, a decision that commentators framed as unusually courageous in a city whose political leadership is uniformly on the left and, in the view of the Red State account cited by Badlands, petty and retaliatory toward businesses that cross it.[1] The same account pointed to [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] closing beaches in a single county during the pandemic in response to local resistance, and to Los Angeles Mayor [[eric-garcetti|Eric Garcetti]] filling an outdoor skate park with sand and threatening to deputize city bureaucrats to enforce lockdown compliance, as illustrations of that retaliatory posture.[1]

The Red State writer argued that the disinvitation was driven not by Sen. [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]]'s public criticism but by the anger of the Dodgers' own fan base, which is heavily Latino and heavily Catholic and objected to honoring an organization widely described as anti-Catholic.[1] The organization subsequently reversed course again and reconciled with the group, which Badlands contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] characterized as the club "caving in" to an anti-Christian/anti-Catholic, pro-LGBTQ+ organization.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Johnny Q wrote that sports and public events had historically been a space where people across the political spectrum could set aside societal pressure and simply be themselves, and that this was no longer the case.[1] He argued that the episode had pushed players away and upset the majority of the club's faith-based Latino fans, noting that the nephew of longtime Dodgers play-by-play broadcaster Vin Scully had spoken out against the decision.[1] While stating he had no issue with sexual orientation so long as it did not harm or indoctrinate children, he described the outcome as a woke agenda forced on everyone regardless of consent, and predicted the same commercial damage that had followed [[bud-light|Bud Light]]'s virtue-signaling campaign — a catastrophic loss of ticket sales and attendance.[1] His closing prescription was to call out the hypocrisy and then "sit back and let cancel culture cancel itself," linking the episode to the broader dynamic described on [[cancel-culture|cancel culture]] and [[woke-capital|woke capital]].[1]

The Red State framing placed the Dodgers alongside Bud Light and Fox News as 2023 case studies in brands discovering the cost of insulting a large share of the people who pay their salaries.[1]

## Pride Night backlash

Badlands writer [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] cited the Dodgers' Pride Night controversy in June 2023 as evidence of widespread public resistance to drag-nun performers such as [[sister-roma|Sister Roma]], noting that the episode "wrought outrage from many in Los Angeles and beyond," with pushback from celebrities, baseball players, and fans alike.[2]
