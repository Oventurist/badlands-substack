# Halliburton

Halliburton is a major American oilfield services company. In the summer of 2025 it announced East Texas projects as part of a broader pivot by oil and gas firms into lithium and other critical minerals extraction, alongside [[chevron|Chevron]].[1]

## Texas critical minerals push

Reporting in October 2025 described major oil companies drilling again in East Texas — not for oil, but for lithium used in batteries and for other rare elements.[1] Halliburton and Chevron both announced East Texas projects that summer, while [[exxonmobil|Exxon]] holds acreage across the border in Arkansas.[1] Smackover Lithium, a joint venture of a Norwegian oil giant and a Canadian miner, reported in late September the most lithium-rich fluids ever measured in North America, in a deep brine deposit known as the Smackover Formation beneath its Texas claims.[1]

The activity forms part of federal efforts to establish domestic production of materials required for advanced manufacturing — batteries, solar cells, wind turbines, microchips and cruise missiles — with competition against China as the explicit backdrop.[1]


## Kuwaiti reconstruction

"Profiting From War" names Halliburton, alongside the [[carlyle-group|Carlyle Group]], as one of the firms that profited from rebuilding [[kuwait|Kuwait]] after the country was devastated by [[saddam-hussein|Saddam Hussein]]'s invasion.[2] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] argues the war served two purposes for the [[bush-family|Bush family]]: distracting from the [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] scandal and destroying evidence of their Kuwaiti dealings, with reconstruction contracts as the profit stage.[2]

## Q drop #117 and the Iraq invasion

Chapter 17 of the [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] series takes up Q drop #117's questions about why the United States attacked Iraq and which senior officials were affiliated with Halliburton.[3] It describes Halliburton as an oil company — specifically the second largest in the world — specializing in the acquisition and production of petroleum, and notes that Saudi money was invested in the firm.[3]

The chapter answers Q's question by naming [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] and [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney]] as the officials linked to Halliburton, with Cheney having served on its board, and summarizes the argument as the equation "Iraq + Oil + Halliburton + Cheney/Bush 2.0 = Iraq invasion."[3] On this reading the [[iraq-war|invasion of Iraq]] had nothing to do with Saddam Hussein or the weapons of mass destruction cited as its official justification and never found, and was always about oil.[3]
