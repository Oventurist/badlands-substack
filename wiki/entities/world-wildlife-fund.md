# World Wildlife Fund

The **World Wildlife Fund** (WWF) is described by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as an institution founded by UNESCO founder and Eugenics Society president Sir [[julian-huxley|Julian Huxley]] alongside [[prince-philip|Prince Philip Mountbatten]] and [[prince-bernhard-of-the-netherlands|Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands]].[1]

Ehret notes that all three founders were present at Bernhard's founding meeting of the [[bilderberg|Bilderberg]] group in 1954, and that Philip and Bernhard went on in 1970 to co-found the [[1001-nature-trust|1001 Nature Trust]] alongside 999 other wealthy figures to fund the blossoming environmental movement.[1] [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]], the Canadian who chaired the 1992 [[rio-earth-summit|Rio Summit]], is identified as a vice-president of the World Wildlife Fund under Prince Philip.[1]

Ehret argues that the oligarchs behind the fund did not in fact care about the environment, writing that Philip and Bernhard "have been recorded to have killed more endangered species on safari than most people have killed mosquitos," and that what they actually disliked was people — especially thinking people who question the arbitrary rules used to justify wars, poverty and oligarchism.[1]

## Founding and leadership

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that the World Wildlife Fund was created by [[prince-bernhard-of-the-netherlands|Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands]] alongside his close associates [[prince-philip|Prince Philip Mountbatten]] and Sir [[julian-huxley|Julian Huxley]], a few years before Bernhard founded the [[1001-nature-trust|1001 Trust]] in 1970 to finance the green paradigm shift.[2] Canada's [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]], a member of both the 1001 Club and the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]], became Vice President of the WWF under Prince Philip's presidency, replacing [[louis-mortimer-bloomfield|Louis Mortimer Bloomfield]] — a 1001 Club member whom New Orleans District Attorney Jim Garrison identified as central to the 1963 assassination of [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]].[2]

## Criticism

[[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]] listed the World Wildlife Fund among the neo-Malthusian institutions whose policies he blamed for the greatest single share of ecological destruction over the fifteen years preceding his 1982 [[there-are-no-limits-to-growth|*There Are No Limits to Growth*]], alongside the Club of Rome, the International Institute for Applied Systems Analysis, the Aspen Institute, the Ford Foundation, the Rockefeller Foundation and the U.S. [[sierra-club|Sierra Club]].[2]
