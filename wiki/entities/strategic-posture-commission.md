# Strategic Posture Commission

The Strategic Posture Commission is a congressionally appointed bipartisan panel tasked with assessing the long-term nuclear and conventional posture of the United States. In a report released in October 2023, the commission concluded that the United States must prepare for the possibility of simultaneous wars with [[russia|Russia]] and [[china|China]] by expanding its conventional forces, strengthening alliances, and accelerating its nuclear weapons modernization program.[1]

## October 2023 report

The report arrived amid tensions with China over Taiwan and worsening frictions with Russia over its invasion of [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[1] A senior official involved in the report declined to say whether the panel's intelligence briefings had revealed any Chinese and Russian nuclear weapons cooperation, stating on condition of anonymity: "We worry ... there may be ultimate coordination between them in some way, which gets us to this two-war construct."[1]

The commission's findings would upend the prevailing U.S. national security strategy, which calls for winning one conflict while deterring another, and would require large defense spending increases with uncertain congressional support.[1] The report's preface, signed by Democratic chair Madelyn Creedon — a former deputy head of the agency that oversees U.S. nuclear weapons — and vice chair Jon Kyl, a retired Republican senator, stated: "We do recognize budget realities, but we also believe the nation must make these investments."[1] At a briefing releasing the report, Kyl said the president and [[us-congress|Congress]] must "take the case to the American people" that higher defense spending is a small price to pay "to hopefully preclude" a possible nuclear war involving the United States, China, and Russia.[1]

The report contrasted with President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s position that the existing U.S. nuclear arsenal is sufficient to deter the combined forces of Russia and China.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized the report as a "(re)action plan" addressing the increasing coordination and power projection of Russia and China, arguing that a triumvirate of superpowers historically deters war by incentivizing open communication among all three, with the caveat that intelligence agencies and "Deep State disruptor factions" complicate that dynamic in the U.S. case. He concluded that the commission demonstrated that the only matter on which Republicans and Democrats agree is "the need for preemptive war posturing."[1]
