# JD Vance

JD Vance was selected by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as his vice-presidential running mate in July 2024, days after the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|attempted assassination of Trump]] at the Butler, Pennsylvania rally.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Place in the July 2024 sequence

Badlands Media commentary lists the Vance selection alongside the Republican National Convention and [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s withdrawal from the race as the three developments that followed the Butler shooting in rapid succession — a period [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] characterized as one in which "with every new day, a tectonic shift of the narrative occurs."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-week-to-remember)

## Big Tech and cryptocurrency ties

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] later urged readers to consider why [[donald-trump|Trump]] would choose Vance as his running mate, pointing to Vance's connections to Big Tech and cryptocurrency as a clue to the administration's intended economic direction.[2] The suggestion is offered as evidence for the thesis that the substantive story of mid-2024 was a rebuilding of the American financial system — a story Carlson said was obscured by the [[distraction-operations|distractions]] dominating the news cycle — rather than as investment advice, which he explicitly disclaimed.[2]

## Geneva signing ceremony (June 2026)

Vance was reportedly designated to attend the signing ceremony in Geneva for the US–Iran framework negotiated in June 2026, with four US Air Force C-17s departing Israel for Europe in anticipation of the event.[3] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the fact that Vance rather than President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was travelling — combined with the ceremony being hosted in Europe — as grounds for concern that the accord might not be the final deal and could yet fall apart at the hands of bad-faith actors who had infiltrated the process.[3]

## Olympics security incident (2026)

In early 2026, Vance was booed in Italy in connection with U.S. agents providing security at the Olympics, an episode cited alongside other frictions as evidence of a deepening transatlantic rift in which European officials increasingly regard the United States as a rival.[4]

## Announcement of a fraud-focused assistant attorney general (2026)

In January 2026 Vance announced that the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] was creating a new assistant attorney general position focused on investigating fraud tied to federally backed programs, following claims that taxpayers lost billions of dollars in Minnesota.[5] He said the official would coordinate investigations across federal agencies and pursue criminal charges where warranted, and that the role would not be housed within the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] but would instead operate out of the [[white-house|White House]] under the oversight of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the vice president.[5]

According to Vance, the assistant attorney general would likely serve through the end of the administration with nationwide jurisdiction over fraud investigations, with initial efforts concentrated in Minnesota.[5] The position requires Senate confirmation; Vance said a nominee was expected within days and that Senate Majority Leader [[john-thune|John Thune]] had pledged swift confirmation.[5] The announcement followed the administration's decision to halt more than $10 billion in federal funding for social services programs in Minnesota, New York, California, Illinois, and Colorado, and the [[department-of-health-and-human-services|Department of Health and Human Services]]' freeze of federal child care funding for Minnesota.[5]

## Defense of the 2026 Iran campaign

During the March 2026 [[iran-war|U.S.–Iran war]], Vance — previously identified with anti-interventionist positions — defended the operation as limited and necessary to prevent Iranian nuclear development.[6] Badlands contributor Burning Bright noted that the broader America First contingent of Vance-aligned voices had fallen into a telling silence, arguing that the Israel variable resisted simple pro- or anti-Zionist framing and demanded evaluation strictly against first principles.[6]

## Armenia visit and AI alignment (February 2026)

In February 2026 Vance arrived in [[armenia|Armenia]], becoming the first sitting US vice president or president to visit the country, according to AP News.[7] The trip followed a US-facilitated peace deal between Armenia and Azerbaijan that included an August 2025 White House summit and a transit-corridor accord.[7]

In the same period, Politico reported that Vance and Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] had publicly aligned with [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s deregulatory AI policy, while relying on anonymous sources to allege internal concerns over national security and political backlash.[7] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that the article's authors admitted they did not know where Vance and Rubio would ultimately land, arguing this revealed that the [[media-protectorate|Protectorate]] is uncertain about the real power centers inside the administration.[7]

## Defense of the US–Iran deal (June 2026)

In June 2026, Vance defended the [[us-iran-memorandum-of-understanding|US–Iran memorandum of understanding]] and criticized opposition from Israeli officials, delivering what Badlands described as a dressing-down of Israeli leadership from the podium at the White House.[8] He was reported to have cancelled or delayed his travel to the planned signing talks in Switzerland, a delay Badlands attributed to Israel's resumption of hostilities against [[iran|Iran]] via strikes in southern Lebanon.[8]

Appearing on a podcast in a separate appearance, Vance addressed the assertion that the Israelis would "kill everything" that stands in front of them.[8] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry called this a "heel turn," argued Vance was being sent on a media tour to run interference on unfavorable headlines, and dismissed the notion that the United States would ever "integrate" its military or culture with a foreign power.[8]

## Support for UK anti-immigration activists (2026)

In May 2026 Vance publicly backed British anti-immigration activists after the "[[unite-the-kingdom-rally|Unite the Kingdom]]" rally in London, saying it was "OK to defend your culture" and encouraging opponents of mass migration in the [[united-kingdom|United Kingdom]] to "keep on going."[9] He argued that Western governments have relied on "millions and millions of unvetted people" and framed tighter border controls as both an economic and a cultural issue.[9] The rally, organized by [[tommy-robinson|Tommy Robinson]], drew tens of thousands of demonstrators, and Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] condemned it as promoting "hatred and division."[9]

## Freedom 250 and the Iran de-escalation (June 2026)

Vance attended [[ufc-freedom-250|UFC Freedom 250]] on the South Lawn of the [[white-house|White House]] on June 14, 2026, alongside President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and other administration officials.[10] In the same period he told Fox News that [[iran|Iran]] had been planning to retaliate for Israel's strike on Beirut but backed down at the request of the United States.[10] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the Fox News clip as proof that Trump had negotiated peace with Iran while excluding [[israel|Israel]], and as evidence of Iranian restraint contrasted with continued Israeli belligerence.[10]

## Leading the War on Fraud (February 2026)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced during his February 2026 State of the Union address that Vance would lead a federal initiative described as a "[[war-on-fraud|war on fraud]]."[11] Vance acted the following day, suspending [[medicaid|Medicaid]] payments to [[minnesota|Minnesota]] over alleged fraud in the state's program, with roughly $259 million withheld pending review and the state given 60 days to implement corrective measures.[11] He announced the action alongside [[centers-for-medicare-and-medicaid-services|Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services]] Administrator [[mehmet-oz|Mehmet Oz]], promising to root out taxpayer money stolen through federal benefit programs, while Minnesota Democratic leaders called the suspension politically motivated.[11]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed Vance's appointment as the human follow-through phase of the technology transformation begun by [[doge|DOGE]], arguing that "DOGE was tooling" and that the monitoring systems installed under [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] now required a leader to act on their findings — "apparently that person is JD Vance."[11] She added that if the effort is more than theater, it should generate trackable cycles of disclosure and accountability, and speculated about whether the underlying tooling came from [[palantir|Palantir]].[11]

## Iran memorandum of understanding (June 2026)

Vance was among the signatories, alongside [[donald-trump|President Trump]] and Iranian Parliament Speaker [[mohammad-bagher-ghalibaf|Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf]], of the virtually signed memorandum of understanding intended to end the recent U.S.–Iran conflict, lift the naval blockade on the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and Iranian ports, and open nuclear negotiations.[12] He was subsequently dispatched on a media tour during the interval before the deal's details were released; asked by [[megyn-kelly|Megyn Kelly]] why the text was delayed, Vance acknowledged that even he did not fully understand the reason, while defending the sequencing and arguing that congressional approval of the agreement was not required.[12] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read Vance's remarks as consistent with a deliberate strategy in which Trump sends the MOU to [[congress|Congress]] anyway in order to expose lawmakers who would nullify it on Israel's behalf.[12]


## Islamabad talks with Iran (April 2026)

Vice President Vance said that talks held in Islamabad in April 2026 between US and Iranian officials lasted more than twenty hours but failed to bridge critical gaps, particularly over Tehran's nuclear ambitions.[13] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] confirmed the breakdown on Truth Social, stating that [[iran|Iran]]'s refusal to abandon its nuclear program was the central obstacle to any agreement.[13] The collapse of the negotiations preceded the announcement of a US maritime blockade in the Gulf of Oman and Arabian Sea.[13]

## Budapest visit and the Hungarian election (April 2026)

Vance traveled to Budapest to rally support for Hungarian Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] ahead of the [[2026-hungarian-parliamentary-election|April 12, 2026 parliamentary election]], a contest portrayed as an existential test for the MAGA movement's global nationalist model.[14] The visit was explicitly aimed at reinforcing Orbán as a key U.S. partner on migration, energy, technology and defense, aligning with the Trump administration's National Security Strategy of empowering nationalist leaders across Europe.[14]

## Task Force to Eliminate Fraud

Vance leads the [[task-force-to-eliminate-fraud|Task Force to Eliminate Fraud]], with which the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s [[national-fraud-enforcement-division|National Fraud Enforcement Division]] — announced by Acting Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] on April 7, 2026 — was said to coordinate closely as part of a "supercharging" of enforcement against those exploiting federal programs and taxpayer funds.[14]

## Anti-fraud task force (February 2026)

CBS News, citing multiple sources briefed on the plan, reported in February 2026 that Vance would lead a new [[federal-anti-fraud-task-force-2026|federal anti-fraud task force]] expected to be announced by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as an executive order, possibly later that month.[15] According to those sources the task force would initially focus on fraud in California state programs; the [[white-house|White House]] declined to comment and California officials disputed the story.[15]

## Maher's conditional 2028 endorsement

In July 2026 comedian and longtime Democratic voter [[bill-maher|Bill Maher]] said he could see himself voting for Vance in 2028 if the Democratic Party continued its leftward trajectory, replying "Yes. If this is the Democratic side, yes" when asked directly, while stressing that he would not support [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] himself.[16] Maher tied the shift to the rising influence of the [[democratic-socialists-of-america|Democratic Socialists of America]], whose platform he described as communism rather than democratic socialism.[16] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the de facto endorsement of Vance over Trump as a caution rather than a victory, writing that his trust does not automatically transfer from Trump to those around him and invoking Mike Pence as precedent.[16]

## Greenland talks and the Venezuela tie-breaker (January 2026)

In mid-January 2026, Vance met Danish officials in Washington following President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s Truth Social post asserting that US control of [[greenland|Greenland]] is necessary for American national security; Greenland was discussed and Danish officials reiterated that the territory is not for sale.[17] The Danish foreign minister said afterwards that "it's clear the president has a wish to conquer Greenland," and [[denmark|Denmark]] subsequently began moving military resources into the territory.[17]

On January 14, 2026, Vance cast the tie-breaking vote in the 51–50 [[united-states-senate|Senate]] decision to block the [[venezuela-war-powers-resolution|Venezuela war powers resolution]] led by Sen. [[tim-kaine|Tim Kaine]], which would have required congressional approval for further military action against [[venezuela|Venezuela]].[17]

## Dollar policy and baby-face memes (March 2025)

In March 2025 [[the-economist|The Economist]] reported that both Vance and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had argued for a weaker currency, saying the dollar's strength was a problem for American industry, in contrast with Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]]'s insistence that the strong-dollar policy remained in place.[18] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] disputed the framing, arguing that Trump has consistently backed the dollar as the world's reserve currency and that Vance "has nothing to do with any of it."[18]

The same month, memes Photoshopping Vance's face into cartoonishly chubby, baby-like caricatures spread virally across both left-wing and pro-Trump accounts on X, a phenomenon [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] examined at length and Badlands commentators celebrated as evidence of the right's superior grasp of internet culture.[18] See [[jd-vance-baby-face-memes|J.D. Vance baby-face memes]].[18]

## 2024 campaign trail

As the Republican vice-presidential nominee in August 2024, Vance shadowed the newly formed Democratic ticket across the Midwest, and his travel overlapped on a Wisconsin tarmac with [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] and [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] on their first full day campaigning together, narrowly avoiding a direct confrontation.[19] The episode was treated as an illustration of how hotly contested the region would be in the 2024 general election.[19]

## 2024 vice-presidential debate reception

Vance drew broadly favorable reviews for his October 1, 2024 debate against Minnesota Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]], with pundits describing him as "sharp" and delivering a "commanding performance."[20] New York Times columnist Ross Douthat wrote, "For Vance, it was a commanding performance. For Walz, it was a nervous ramble. For the audience, it was the most civil and substantive debate of the Trump era."[20] "The View" co-host and CNN commentator Alyssa Farah Griffin said she was "struck by the fact that JD Vance is a significantly more eloquent Donald Trump" and an "incredibly effective communicator" to MAGA audiences.[20] Michigan Governor [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]] told Jimmy Kimmel that "JD Vance was a better debater than Donald Trump," adding that Trump "should have taken some lessons from him."[20] See [[2024-vice-presidential-debate|2024 vice-presidential debate]].[20]

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] told Fox News Digital the next morning that "JD was fantastic last night — it just reconfirmed my choice," praising "a brilliance to what he did," while saying Walz "embarrassed himself and the Democrat Party" though he still ranked the governor "a large number of steps above Kamala."[20] Trump had already posted on Truth Social that his running mate "crushed it."[20]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that the degree of capitulation to Vance's win within the "Media Industrial Complex" was notable, as was Trump's strengthened public backing of his running mate ahead of the November 5 inflection point.[20] He counselled a "Bicameral approach" to the narrative pattern — not trusting Vance merely because he was being thrust into the spotlight, particularly given his support from what he called the "Con Inc. doom brigade" — while observing that Vance's presence had sent traditional media into a tailspin, a form of narrative chaos that typically benefits "the Infinite Player on the game board," usually Trump.[20]

## Hosting The Charlie Kirk Show (September 2025)

Following the assassination of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] at Utah Valley University, Vance guest-hosted *The Charlie Kirk Show* from the White House on Monday, 15 September 2025 as a tribute to the slain conservative activist.[21] In his opening monologue he said "we have to make sure that the killer is brought to justice" and claimed that "left-wing extremism" was part of the reason behind Kirk's death.[21]

Vance used the broadcast to amplify right-wing calls for a broadside against leftist groups, arguing that serving Kirk's memory meant pursuing left-leaning organisations he and other officials portrayed as bent on undermining national unity.[21] His guest, White House Deputy Chief of Staff [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]], said Kirk's final message to him called for a coordinated effort against unnamed left-wing groups and pledged that the federal government would use "every available resource" to dismantle them.[21]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] criticised the framing, writing that blaming the assassination on "The Left" rather than the Cabal, and defending what he called the rebrand of cancel culture, indicated that the reader "might be the problem."[21]

## July 2025 Netanyahu meeting

Vance joined President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Israeli prime minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] in the Oval Office on July 8, 2025, for a meeting of over an hour on the Gaza ceasefire and hostage release talks, which concluded without any public announcement of a breakthrough.[22]

## February 2025 Oval Office confrontation

Vance participated in the heated February 28, 2025 Oval Office exchange with [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], after which the Ukrainian leader's visit to sign a minerals deal was cut short and he was asked to leave the White House.[23] Zelensky was accused of ungratefulness, of disrespecting the Oval Office and of "gambling with World War III" by refusing to work towards peace with Russia.[23] Finnish President [[alexander-stubb|Alexander Stubb]] publicly likened the confrontation to a marital dispute and suggested the parties resolve it in a sauna.[23]

## MAGA infighting over antisemitism (December 2025)

Vance was widely regarded as the potential "boundary setter" in the [[republican-party|Republican Party]] as MAGA feuded over whether antisemitic figures such as [[nick-fuentes|Nick Fuentes]] would be allowed in the coalition once [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] leaves office.[24] Observers watched the December 2025 [[turning-point-usa|Turning Point USA]] convention to see whether Vance would draw a red line against bigotry in the party; he did not.[24]

According to the Associated Press, Vance ducked a chance to "condemn a streak of antisemitism" that had roiled the GOP in recent months, telling the convention's closing session, "I didn't bring a list of conservatives to denounce or to de-platform."[24] The remark came after commentator [[ben-shapiro|Ben Shapiro]] used his own address to criticize Fuentes, [[candace-owens|Candace Owens]] and [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] as "grifters" who "do not deserve your time."[24] Vance declined to take sides, saying MAGA Republicans have "far more important work to do than canceling each other."[24] The New York Times noted that Vance has repeatedly "refused to pick a side in interparty fights over bigotry."[24]

## Rescissions tiebreaker, July 2025

On Tuesday, July 15, 2025, Vance cast the tiebreaking vote in a 51–50 Senate decision to discharge the DOGE-inspired rescissions request out of the Appropriations Committee and bring the foreign aid clawback package to the floor.[25] Three Republicans — [[lisa-murkowski|Lisa Murkowski]], [[susan-collins|Susan Collins]], and [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]] — voted no, requiring Vance to return to the chamber in which he once served.[25] It was his sixth tiebreaker in the Senate, following his deciding vote on July 1 to pass the [[one-big-beautiful-bill-act|One Big Beautiful Bill Act]].[25] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] concluded that Republicans could afford to lose those three votes "and still win if JD Vance shows up to work."[25] See [[2025-rescissions-package]].[25]

## Epstein files defense (August 2025)

In an interview released on Sunday, August 10, 2025, Vance pushed back against criticism of the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s handling of the [[epstein-files|Jeffrey Epstein files]], accusing Democrats of political opportunism and of failing to act when they held power.[26] "President Trump has demanded full transparency from this. And yet somehow the Democrats are attacking him and not the Biden administration, which did nothing for four years," he told Fox News' *Sunday Morning Futures*.[26] "I laugh at the Democrats who are now all of a sudden, so interested in the Epstein files. For four years Joe Biden, the Democrats did absolutely nothing about this story," he added.[26] After the interview aired, clips of Vance's remarks gained traction across social media, with users across the political spectrum posting "Release the Epstein files!"; the vice president reiterated that full disclosure remains a goal of the administration.[26]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] responded that there is "an entire zeitgeist of people out there who have not only NOT learned to think for themselves like Trump wants them to, but have gone backwards," asking whether mandate is lent or given.[26] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that a sobering revelation had been realizing how many people invested in the movement have no principles, mistaking being "principled" for defending a specific opinion to the death even after it has been shown illogical, morally repulsive, or contradictory to their stated belief system.[26]

In November 2025, Vice President JD Vance was described as the 2028 GOP frontrunner and an ally of [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], placing him on a collision course with Senator [[ted-cruz|Ted Cruz]], who was leaning into a feud with Carlson while positioning himself as a traditional, pro-interventionist Republican.[27] The dispute centered on ideological differences over Israel that were straining President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s MAGA movement.[27] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] dismissed the whole contest as kayfabe, arguing that Cruz, Carlson, and Vance were merely pretending to compete to become the next rulers once Trump was outlasted.[27]

"Not only do I accept the CBS debate on October 1st, I accept the CNN debate on September 18th as well. I look forward to seeing you at both!"[29] His opponent [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] had accepted the October 1 date the same week.[29]

## Farmland warnings and Zelensky attack (September 2024)

Breitbart's September 2024 report on [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s farmland push noted that senators had warned for years about Chinese purchases of American land, including Trump's running mate Vance, who in a 2022 campaign ad vowed to end China's buying of US farmland on the grounds that "America should belong to Americans, not our enemies."[30] Later that month, Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] attacked Vance in an interview with The New Yorker while traveling in the United States on a US Air Force C-17, prompting policy consultant [[dan-caldwell|Dan Caldwell]] to accuse the Biden-Harris administration of "using military assets to fly a foreign leader into a battleground state in order to undermine their political opponents."[30]

Following the [[assassination-of-charlie-kirk|assassination of Charlie Kirk]], Vice President Vance met with Kirk's family and close friends in Salt Lake City and traveled with Kirk's casket to Phoenix aboard Air Force Two.[31] Kirk had lived in Arizona, where his youth organization [[turning-point-usa|Turning Point]] was based.[31]


## Most-blocked account on Bluesky (2025)

Vance held the position of most-blocked account on the social platform [[bluesky|Bluesky]] in October 2025, having accumulated 166,000 blocks since joining in June.[32] The [[white-house|White House]] account, which joined that month, was blocked by more than 92,000 users within 48 hours and was on pace to overtake him.[32]

## Fox News remarks on Israel, Iran and the Gaza war (July 2024)

Fresh off his nomination as the Republican vice-presidential candidate at the [[2024-republican-national-convention|Republican National Convention]] in Milwaukee, Vance accused President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] of "making it harder and harder" for Israel to vanquish Hamas, claiming Biden's policies unnecessarily prolonged the war and prevented an Israeli-Saudi rapprochement.[33] The freshman senator from Ohio said Israel should win and end the war in Gaza "as quickly as possible" so that the "Israelis and the Sunni Arab states" could form a united front against Iran.[33] Speaking to [[fox-news|Fox News]], he concurred with host [[sean-hannity|Sean Hannity]]'s statement that Biden had "surrendered the war on terrorism" by failing to sufficiently support Israel in its war against Hamas following the October 7 attack that killed nearly 1,200 people and saw 251 taken hostage.[33]

Commentators framed Vance's selection as cementing Trump's hold on the party, with some analysts suggesting Trump was effectively anointing his successor; in choosing a harsh Trump critic turned fierce loyalist, Trump had passed over more traditional conservatives in favor of a populist with a notable isolationist streak.[33]

## Badlands scrutiny of the pick

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that Vance had not been his first choice but that he deferred to Trump's judgment, while insisting that discernment and scrutiny be built into the movement's political ethos.[33] He distinguished the trust extended to [[donald-trump|Trump]] — earned over a decade and sealed by the [[attempted-assassination-of-donald-trump-2024|Butler shooting]] — from any automatic transfer of that status to a running mate, warning that granting Vance a free pass "through osmosis" would be an exercise in idolatry of the kind that produced the movement's earlier errors.[33] He cited the 2019 elevation of [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] and [[mitch-mcconnell|Mitch McConnell]] to hero status by MAGA audiences, despite their prior efforts to keep Trump out of office, as a cautionary precedent.[33]

He said he was concerned that Vance's first act as the vice-presidential candidate was to go on Fox News and "shill for war with Iran," calling the proposal to unite Israel and the Sunni Arab states against Iran "straight from the NeoCon playbook" and the product of neoconservative lobby think tanks.[33] He argued a Sunni-Shia war was unlikely given Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]]'s inroads with Iran, and objected to saber-rattling against a bloc in which Russia and China back Iran and Hezbollah.[33] He added that he could not be certain Vance was "read in" on the Q operation and therefore rejected the "trust the plan" response, concluding that politicians should have to work for public trust.[33]

## 2028 planning and the 2026 midterms

Reporting by [[axios|Axios]] on December 28, 2025 described a five-pronged strategy by which Vance intended to prepare for an expected 2028 presidential run while campaigning coast to coast in the midterms and remaining close to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]: be a loyal vice president, stay out of MAGA's civil war, raise big money, play big in 2026, and lean on an "influencer army."[34] The reporting noted the difficulty of his position — he must ready a national campaign without looking too eager, since Trump is not one to share the spotlight — while his aides maintained he was focused solely on the November 2026 midterms.[34] The episode is treated in detail at [[jd-vance-2028-presidential-planning|JD Vance 2028 presidential planning]], and [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands]] characterized the coverage as a coordinated Uniparty messaging operation.[34]

## 2024 vice-presidential shortlist

In July 2024 [[axios|Axios]] reported that Vance's Rust Belt appeal had helped rocket him into one of the finalist slots to be [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s running mate, at a moment when the campaign's central strategic task was to hold Pennsylvania and the wider Blue Wall against [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[35] The reporting noted that the nominee's first job, besides raising money, would be to park in Pennsylvania to deny Biden the biggest of those states.[35]

## AmericaFest 2025 remarks on DEI

Speaking at [[turning-point-usa|Turning Point USA]]'s AmericaFest 2025 in Phoenix, Arizona in December 2025, Vance declared that one of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s big wins since returning to office had been gutting federal [[diversity-equity-and-inclusion|diversity, equity, and inclusion]] laws that he said discriminated against white and Asian Americans.[36] He argued DEI is antithetical to American values and to a meritocratic society, which made it an easy target for the second Trump administration.[36] "We don't treat anybody different because of their race or their sex," Vance told the crowd. "So we have relegated DEI to the dustbin of history, which is exactly where it belongs."[36] The comment drew a big roar from the crowd, and coverage summarized his message as "You don't have to apologize for being white anymore!"[36]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] disputed the claim that DEI was finished, recounting that the head of "diversity, equity, inclusion, and belonging" at the [[colorado-department-of-corrections|Colorado Department of Corrections]] had denied her request to see [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]] and doubled down after she pushed back on being treated differently as a member of the media.[36] "JD can say whatever he wants, DEI is not only alive and well in Colorado, it's mutating again," she wrote, noting the term had become DEIB and asking why "belonging" was being pushed in a prison system.[36]

Separately, a December 2025 [[cnn|CNN]]/SSRS poll on the [[2028-united-states-presidential-election|2028 presidential election]] found Vance named by 22% of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents as the candidate they would like to see run — the largest share of any figure tested.[36]

## "The Talented Mr. Vance" (2025)

A July 2025 Atlantic profile titled "The Talented Mr. Vance" framed Vance as posing "a problem" whose core is "a question about character," describing his transformation in the years after the 2016 election from a center-right memoirist and public speaker offering a complex analysis of America's social ills and a sharp critique of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] into a right-wing populist politician whose illiberal ideas and vitriolic rhetoric frequently "out-Trump the original."[37] By Vance's own account and that of his supporters, the change followed a realization during Trump's first term that the president was lifting up the fallen working class of the heartland that had produced him, so that to help his people Vance had to make peace with their champion.[37] Critics countered that Vance cynically betrayed his true values to take the only path open to an ambitious Republican in the Trump era, pursuing it with the zeal of a convert under suspicion.[37] The magazine cast the competing readings as either a poor boy from the provinces who makes good in the metropole, turns on his glittering benefactors and goes home to fight for his people, or a poor boy who seizes every opportunity on the way up, loses his moral compass and is ruined by his own ambition.[37]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that Trump appeared to be the only prominent figure claiming to represent the America First movement who had decidedly not anointed Vance his heir apparent, and combined that with "JD chasing his shadow around the world" to conclude that "things get ... interesting," describing the dynamic as "wars within wars."[37]

## Ukraine negotiations and the mineral rights deal (March 2025)

On Monday, March 3, 2025, Vance said Trump administration officials were already in talks "with the Russians" to end the war Russia launched against Ukraine, and warned that Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] needed to accept [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s terms for a mineral rights deal and enter negotiations with Moscow.[38] The demand followed Zelensky's ejection from the White House the previous Friday after an Oval Office shouting match with Vance and Trump, and came just hours after the administration announced it was halting defense aid to Kyiv at a crucial moment in the war.[38]

Speaking to Fox News host Sean Hannity, Vance argued that placing American economic interests "on the ground" in Ukraine would provide a superior security guarantee to stationing American or European troops there to guarantee any future ceasefire with Moscow.[38] "If you want to actually ensure that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] does not invade Ukraine again, the very best security guarantee is to give Americans economic upside in the future of Ukraine," he said.[38]

## Adaline Deal transplant story (February 2025)

In February 2025 One America News reported that a 12-year-old girl who is a distant relative of Vance by marriage through his half-siblings had been denied a spot on a heart transplant list.[39] Adaline Deal was born with two rare heart conditions that her family knew would one day require a transplant, her mother Janeen Deal told The Cincinnati Enquirer, and she had been treated at [[cincinnati-childrens-hospital|Cincinnati Children's Hospital]] for nearly ten years.[39] Her parents said the hospital now requires transplant patients to be vaccinated against COVID-19 and the flu and declined to make an exception despite their religious objections as non-denominational Christians.[39] The [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] carried the report as a bonus item.[39]

## Republican National Convention address (July 2024)

Vance was welcomed by Republicans as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s running mate on the third night of the [[2024-republican-national-convention|2024 Republican National Convention]], an evening otherwise devoted to attacking President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s leadership on the world stage.[40] The 39-year-old Ohio senator used his national introduction to offer his life story as a son of Appalachia, reaffirming Trump's connections to Americans who feel alienated socially, economically, and politically.[40]

Coverage of the speech described Vance as underscoring a more populist, protectionist, and isolationist GOP, blending his working-class Appalachian roots with Trump's economic populism and nationalism, and effectively coronating him as the heir apparent to Trump's movement.[40] Vance blasted Wall Street and "multinational corporations," hailed Trump as a man who "will stand up for American companies" and "union and nonunion" workers, and spoke of raising wages, battling China in the global marketplace, and making other nations foot the military bill for international security and stability.[40] The mix of national security talk, relentless attacks on Biden, and the unveiling of a potential MAGA movement heir was said to encapsulate what Trump's "America First" agenda meant for the GOP and the US role as a global superpower.[40]

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] acknowledged not having predicted the Vance pick, having floated names ranging from a returned Mike Pence to Michael Flynn and Robert C. O'Brien, and having described Vance in the lead-up as too much of a "Normie Build-a-Republican" candidate.[40] Following the selection, [[just-human|Just Human]] devoted a lengthy segment of the [[devolution-power-hour|Devolution Power Hour]] to Vance's attributes.[40] What stood out most to Burning Bright was not the backing of [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]] — whom many anons believe is at least tangentially involved in patriot plans — but how far back that backing goes, suggesting there may be more to Vance's ability to force a paradigm shift in Silicon Valley than meets the eye, with the intervening speculation acting as smoke and shielding on the informational battlefield.[40]
