## 2026 Ebola outbreak response

During the [[2026-ebola-outbreak|2026 Ebola outbreak]] of the Bundibugyo strain in the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] and Uganda, WHO Director-General [[tedros-adhanom-ghebreyesus|Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus]] said the delay in detection meant authorities were "playing catch-up with a very fast-moving epidemic" and that the outbreak was outpacing scaled-up operations.[2] The WHO coordinated intensified surveillance, patient isolation and contact monitoring with the DRC and Ugandan governments and partners, with roughly $500 million pledged for response and prevention.[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the WHO and aid-group warnings as fearmongering, noting that "potential pandemics" keep arriving after global think tanks predicted increasingly frequent pandemics.[2]

## United States withdrawal (January 2026)

The United States officially withdrew from the World Health Organization effective Thursday, January 22, 2026, following the one-year notice period required by US law.[3] The notice period began on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first day in office, when he issued an executive order notifying the [[united-nations|United Nations]] of the US intent to exit.[3] The White House posted: "EFFECTIVE TODAY: The United States has exited the World Health Organization. This fulfills President Trump's commitment under an executive order signed one year ago, following the WHO's mishandling of COVID-19 and its ongoing lack of reform, accountability, & transparency."[3]

A joint statement by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] and Health Secretary Robert Kennedy, Jr. said the WHO "abandoned its core mission and acted repeatedly against the interests of the United States," pursuing "a politicized, bureaucratic agenda driven by nations hostile to American interests" despite the US being a founding member and its largest financial contributor.[3] The statement accused the organization of obstructing the timely and accurate sharing of critical information that could have saved American lives and then concealing those failures "under the pretext of acting 'in the interest of public health.'"[3] It also alleged the WHO refused to hand over the American flag that hung in front of it, arguing it had not approved the withdrawal and claiming the US owed it compensation.[3] Going forward, US engagement with the WHO would be "limited strictly to effectuate our withdrawal and to safeguard the health and safety of the American people."[3]

The planned exit triggered a financial crisis at the WHO, which had historically relied on the United States as its largest donor.[3]

In late February 2023 the [[state-department|Department of State]] under President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] announced that the United States was "committed to a #PandemicAccord" being negotiated at the WHO, saying it would "vastly improve the global health architecture for preventing, preparing for, and responding to future pandemic emergencies."[4] Representative [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] rejected that commitment, insisting Americans would live under the Constitution and laws made by the representatives they elect rather than by globalists.[4]

## 2024 mpox emergency declaration

On August 14, 2024, the WHO declared the ongoing mpox outbreak in Africa a public health emergency of international concern, the highest level of alarm under international health law, after its emergency committee met virtually to advise Director-General [[tedros-adhanom-ghebreyesus|Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus]].[6] The committee had been convened amid concerns that a deadlier strain, clade Ib, had reached four previously unaffected African countries after having been contained to the [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of Congo]].[6] The WHO's African contagion tracking reported more than 17,000 mpox cases on the continent since the beginning of that year.[6] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed the WHO and allied bodies as "not-at-all-credible global medical authorities" pushing "amped up fear porn," and rejected Tedros's call for a coordinated international response outright.[6] The episode is covered at [[2024-mpox-global-health-emergency|2024 mpox global health emergency]].[6]

## Khartoum biohazard declaration (2023)

On April 25, 2023, the WHO declared a "high risk of biological hazard" in Khartoum after a military group seized one of [[sudan|Sudan]]'s main bio-laboratories, a facility [[reuters|Reuters]] reported contained cholera, measles and several other deadly pathogens.[7] The WHO Representative for Sudan said the "main concern" was that there was "no accessibility to the lab technicians to go to the lab and safely contain the biological material and substances available."[7]

Badlands writer [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] treated the speed of the organisation's statement as significant in itself, concluding that whoever had taken the lab and for whatever purpose, "the timely response from the WHO indicates that, either way, they are deploying a narrative."[7] He raised the possibility that the episode could parallel the Ukraine biolab controversy or even serve as the seed of "another Wuhan," releasing a timely pandemic to justify mail-in ballots.[7]

## Loss of US funding and the measles laboratory network (2025)

Following [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Inauguration Day order withdrawing the United States from the WHO along with its funding, the organization warned in February 2025 that its largest global laboratory network was on the brink of collapse without replacement support.[8] The Global Measles and Rubella Laboratory Network — known internally as "Gremlin" — comprises 760 labs worldwide and tests about 500,000 patient samples annually, identifying outbreaks before they cross borders.[8] Its potential collapse was said to threaten measles elimination amid a growing outbreak in Texas that had infected at least 124 people and hospitalized 18.[8]

Matthew Ferrari, director of Pennsylvania State University's Center for Infectious Disease Dynamics, said "protecting this resource is absolutely imperative," noting that measles and rubella elimination set the standard for other programs.[8] The WHO-led program relies entirely on US funding, receiving about $8 million annually; the United States had been the WHO's biggest donor, contributing $1.3 billion between 2022 and 2023.[8] Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry responded by asking whether the loss of WHO funding would coincide with a decline in global pandemics, and explicitly accused the organization of creating bioweapons used against human populations as part of a [[hegelian-dialectic|Hegelian dialectic]].[8]

The WHO also featured in the controversy over the cancelled March 2025 [[vaccines-and-related-biological-products-advisory-committee|VRBPAC]] meeting, where panel member [[paul-offit|Paul Offit]] asked whether manufacturers would now turn to the WHO to determine influenza strains.[8]

## First confirmed human H5N2 death (June 2024)

On June 5, 2024, the WHO said a death had been caused by the first laboratory-confirmed human case of infection with the A(H5N2) subtype of avian influenza.[9] The organization said a 59-year-old resident of Mexico died on April 24 after developing fever, shortness of breath, diarrhea, nausea and general discomfort.[9] It was the first laboratory-confirmed human A(H5N2) infection reported globally and the first H5 virus infection reported in a person in Mexico.[9]
