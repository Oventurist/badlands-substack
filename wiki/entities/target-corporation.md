# Target Corporation

Target Corporation (NYSE: TGT) is a major American general-merchandise retailer whose quarterly results are widely treated as a barometer of U.S. discretionary consumer spending. In May 2024 the company became a focal point of the debate over the health of the American economy when it reported quarterly earnings that missed Wall Street estimates and issued a forecast for the current quarter that also disappointed investors.[1]

## May 2024 earnings miss

On Wednesday, May 22, 2024, Target reported quarterly results below analyst expectations, and investors sold off the stock. Shares were last down 7.7 percent at $143.84, putting the stock on track for its sharpest one-day drop since November 2022.[1]

The result contrasted sharply with that of Target's larger rival [[walmart|Walmart]], which the previous week had reported better-than-expected results and raised its annual outlook as shoppers prioritized food and essentials such as toilet paper and detergent.[1]

## Market share erosion

Analysts framed the miss as a company-specific loss of position rather than purely a macroeconomic signal. Neil Saunders, managing director of the research firm GlobalData, said that "Target's performance is significantly worse than the overall market, which underlines that Target is losing [market] share," adding that the figures painted a picture of "a business that has run out of steam."[1]

According to calculations by the data and analytics firm Kantar, Target held a 1.8 percent share of U.S. retail sales in 2023, down from 2.1 percent in 2020.[1] Charles Sizemore, chief investment officer of Sizemore Capital Management — which owns shares in Target — agreed the company was losing market share, but cautioned that it was "not accurate to compare" Target's results with those of Walmart, the biggest U.S. grocery retailer.[1]

## Commentary in the Truth Community

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] used the Target results as evidence against the [[bidenomics|Bidenomics]] narrative, contrasting official White House claims of a strong economy with grocery-aisle price complaints and citing Investopedia's observation that consumer spending accounts for roughly 65 percent of U.S. gross domestic product. In her framing, Target's numbers showed that "in Bidenomics, the majority of Americans are just trying to survive."[1]

## 2023 Pride collection boycott

Two years earlier, Target had been at the center of a different controversy when it introduced a "Pride collection" across its stores in May 2023, featuring merchandise, clothing, and books promoting transgender-friendly messages, including products aimed at young children and babies and displayed prominently at store entrances.[2] Facing mounting backlash, the company held an emergency meeting and directed certain locations, mainly in rural Southern areas, to relocate and shrink their Pride sections in order to avoid a repeat of the [[bud-light-boycott|Bud Light controversy]].[2]

The effort failed to contain the financial damage. Target stock had closed at $160.96 per share a week earlier for a market capitalization of $74.3 billion, but by early trading on Thursday, May 25, 2023, shares traded at $141.76 and market value had fallen to $65.3 billion — a 12 percent drop and a loss of roughly $9 billion in market capitalization.[2] The episode is treated in the Truth Community as a landmark demonstration of conservative consumer power, alongside the [[goya-foods|Goya]] "buycott" of 2020 and the [[bud-light|Bud Light]] decline of 2023.[2] See [[target-pride-collection-boycott|Target Pride collection boycott]].[2]
