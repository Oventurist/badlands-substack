# Elihu Yale

Elihu Yale was a Boston-born businessman whose donation to the fledgling collegiate school at New Haven gave [[yale-university|Yale University]] its name.[1] In 1718 several men involved with the college contacted him to ask for financial help in constructing a new building for the school.[1]

## East India Company career

Elihu Yale started his career as a clerk and eventually rose to the rank of President of the [[british-east-india-company|British East India Company]] settlement at Fort St George, Madras.[1] He later lost that position under charges of corruption for self-dealing, and was known for keeping slaves and for supporting slave export — described in the Badlands account as "a veritable cash cow" for the Company.[1] Ryan DeLarme argues that both Yale and Isaac Newton, who also donated books and money to the college, had ties to the secret societies operating behind the East India Company, and that consequently "from its very inception, Yale had connections to the moving forces of the world."[1]
