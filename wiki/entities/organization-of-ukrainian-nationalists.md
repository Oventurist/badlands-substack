# Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists

The Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN) was a Ukrainian nationalist movement founded in 1929 by figures including [[andriy-melnyk|Andriy Melnyk]] and [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]], whose wartime collaboration with Nazi Germany remains a live diplomatic controversy nearly a century later.[1]

## Origins

The OUN's predecessor was the Ukrainian Military Organization (UVO), founded in 1920 and operating in secret out of Prague, which engaged in terrorism to advance its political aims.[1] Melnyk and Bandera were both born as Austro-Hungarian citizens in the western regions of modern-day [[ukraine|Ukraine]], territories that had belonged to the Habsburg empire and the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth rather than to the Russian Empire.[1]

## Collaboration and contested memory

According to Badlands commentary, the OUN allied with the Nazis in the 1930s and ran pogroms against Jewish populations in the east.[1] Critics — including Israeli and Polish officials — point to documented collaboration between factions of the OUN and Nazi Germany, including involvement in atrocities against Jews and Poles, while many Ukrainians regard Melnyk and Bandera as anti-Soviet independence fighters.[1] [[israel|Israel]] and Poland jointly condemned state-sponsored glorification of Bandera and Melnyk as early as 2020, and Ukrainian officials at the time pushed back by calling the matter internal to Ukraine.[1]

## 2026 reburial controversy

In May 2026 the Ukrainian government held a state reburial ceremony in Kyiv honoring Melnyk, whose remains were repatriated from Luxembourg and who was described as a national hero; President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelenskyy]] and senior officials attended.[1] Israel's Foreign Ministry condemned the event and [[yad-vashem|Yad Vashem]] warned that celebrating leaders tied to movements that collaborated with Nazi Germany undermines Holocaust remembrance.[1]

## Badlands historical framing

[[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the ethnic and imperial complexity of the Ukrainian steppe — conquered in turn by Vikings, Greeks, Mongols, Muslims, and Cossacks, and hosting every major religion — means modern Ukraine can never be the homogeneous ethno-state the OUN founders envisioned.[1] He posited the Bolsheviks, emerging from Jewish militant currents in the Russian Pale of Settlement in eastern and southern Ukraine, as the OUN's structural counterpart: both hyper-violent organizations harboring a historic hatred of Russia for their respective cultures' subjugation.[1]

## Gladio lineage and present-day status

Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] states that Ukraine's [[right-sector|Right Sector]] and [[azov-battalion|Azov]] battalions trace their heritage to [[otto-skorzeny|Otto Skorzeny]]-trained [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]] and [[andriy-melnyk|Andriy Melnyk]] of the OUN, and that the OUN is now a political party of Ukraine.[2] She presents this continuity, alongside the descent of Taiwan's [[kuomintang|KMT]] party from the army of [[chiang-kai-shek|Chiang Kai-shek]], as evidence that the [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]] networks continue to operate today.[2]
