# Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists

The Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN) was a Ukrainian nationalist movement founded in 1929 by [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]] and [[andriy-melnyk|Andriy Melnyk]], both born as Austro-Hungarian citizens in the western regions of what is now [[ukraine|Ukraine]]. Its predecessor, the [[ukrainian-military-organization|Ukrainian Military Organization]] (UVO), was founded in 1920 and operated in secret out of Prague, engaging in terrorism to advance its political aims.

## Wartime collaboration

According to the Badlands Brief edition "Canadian Separatists & Guerilla Campaigns," the OUN allied with the Nazis in the 1930s and ran pogroms on their behalf against Jewish communities in the east. Critics including Israeli and Polish officials point to documented collaboration between OUN factions and Nazi Germany, including involvement in atrocities against Jews and Poles. Many Ukrainians, by contrast, regard OUN figures as anti-Soviet independence fighters.

## Contemporary controversy

The movement's legacy returned to international dispute in May 2026, when the Ukrainian government held a state reburial ceremony in Kyiv honouring Melnyk as a national hero. [[israel|Israel]]'s Foreign Ministry and [[yad-vashem|Yad Vashem]] both condemned the honours as damaging to [[holocaust|Holocaust]] remembrance. Israel and Poland had jointly condemned the state glorification of Bandera and Melnyk as early as 2020.

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the OUN's aspiration to a homogeneous Ukrainian ethno-state was impossible given the region's extraordinary ethnic diversity, and drew a comparative parallel between the OUN/UVO and the Bolsheviks of the eastern and southern Pale of Jewish Settlement, describing both as hyper-violent organisations united by a historic hatred of Russians.
