# Ronen Bar

Ronen Bar is the head of [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]], Israel's domestic security agency, whose attempted dismissal by Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] became a constitutional flashpoint in March 2025.[1]

## Dismissal dispute

On Monday, March 17, 2025, Netanyahu accused Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara of "abusing" her authority after she informed him that he could not proceed with Bar's dismissal unless a "factual and legal basis" for the move could be established.[1] It was not clear when the cabinet would hold a vote on the dismissal; Netanyahu sought to move the cabinet meeting up from Wednesday to Tuesday, but Israeli media reported that as of late Monday night ministers had not yet received a directive to convene on the matter.[1]

The Haaretz daily reported that the offices of both Netanyahu and the attorney general were examining whether the government required approval from a commission that vets candidates for senior civil service positions before firing Bar — a procedural question that could delay the removal by weeks.[1]

## Alleged conflict of interest

Badlands commentary framed the firing as legally vulnerable: the opposition minority coalition in the [[knesset|Knesset]] was expected to argue before a judge that Netanyahu held a conflict of interest, because Bar had been investigating Netanyahu's inner circle, the events surrounding the October 7 attack, and their potential connections to [[qatar|Qatar]].[1] The dispute was described as distinct from a separate police complaint Netanyahu filed against former Shin Bet director Nadav Argaman.[1]

## Gaza operations

Bar's agency participated in the resumption of hostilities in [[gaza|Gaza]]: the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] and Shin Bet said in a joint statement that they had begun attacking numerous [[hamas|Hamas]] targets across the enclave in strikes that began around 2 a.m. local time.[1]

## Feud with Netanyahu (March 2025)

The long-running conflict between Bar and Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] reached an unprecedented level on Thursday, March 13, 2025, when Netanyahu accused the [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]] chief of "blackmail."[2] The accusation came in a statement responding to an interview given by Bar's predecessor, [[nadav-argaman|Nadav Argaman]], who revealed that he was sitting on a trove of information compromising to the premier that he would release publicly if Netanyahu broke the law.[2]

Netanyahu was widely reported to have been working for months to oust Bar, seeking to place blame for the failures that allowed [[hamas|Hamas]]'s October 7 onslaught squarely on the security echelon rather than the political leadership.[2] Bar was said to be pushing back against the effort, fearing that acquiescence would let Netanyahu install a loyalist in his place.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the affair as an escalating soap opera, questioning whether Netanyahu's choice of the word "blackmail" implied he had skeletons to hide or was merely playing the victim, and argued that Argaman's disclosures supported the claim that Netanyahu had funded and propped up Hamas after Palestinian President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] tried to starve the group of resources — asserting Hamas would have collapsed in 2018 without that intervention.[2]

## March 2023 warning to Netanyahu

In early March 2025 the Haaretz daily reported that Bar had warned Prime Minister Netanyahu five months before the October 7, 2023 [[hamas|Hamas]] onslaught that a war in Gaza was inevitable.[3] The two met in late May 2023, after Operation Shield and Arrow against Palestinian Islamic Jihad in Gaza, and Bar reportedly told Netanyahu that the five-day campaign had been only "the first round against the Shiite axis," adding: "We must prepare for an opening strike, for a round of assassinations. Hamas is the next challenge before us, there will be no escaping from a campaign in Gaza."[3] Hamas did not actively take part in that 2023 round of violence, effectively helping keep it limited; Israel eliminated several senior Islamic Jihad leaders in airstrikes while the group fired some 1,500 rockets before an Egyptian-brokered ceasefire took hold.[3]

The report emerged amid an escalating war of words and strategic leaks between the premier and the [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]] chief, including widespread talk that Netanyahu might fire Bar.[3] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the two men as engaged in a narrative war: Bar leaking that he had warned of imminent war, and Netanyahu leaking that Bar had told him at the same meeting that Hamas was subdued and not a threat.[3] Given leaked reports from Aman, Israeli military intelligence, reaching conclusions similar to the Shin Bet's, the commentary judged it more probable than not that Netanyahu was the one lying.[3]

## Netanyahu's announced dismissal (March 2025)

On Sunday, March 16, 2025, Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] announced his intention to dismiss Bar as head of the [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]], bringing months of growing disagreement between the two men to a head; the attorney general responded that the prime minister could not do so before a legal review.[4] Netanyahu summoned Bar to an urgent meeting at his office that evening and informed him that the cabinet would vote on his dismissal later in the week.[4] In a video statement afterward, Netanyahu said that "at all times, but especially during an existential war such as the one we face, there must be full trust between the prime minister and the head of the Shin Bet," adding that "unfortunately, the situation is the opposite — I do not have such trust," while stressing that he remained "full of appreciation for the men and women of the Shin Bet."[4]

Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] situated the move within a wider confrontation, noting that Bar had signaled he might further expose Netanyahu's ties to [[hamas|Hamas]] by releasing a report showing that money Netanyahu directed to the group originated in [[qatar|Qatar]], and that the dismissal followed Netanyahu's police complaint accusing former Shin Bet director [[nadav-argaman|Nadav Argaman]] of blackmail.[4]


## Dismissal by the Israeli cabinet (March 2025)

As the Israeli cabinet deliberated Bar's dismissal in March 2025, an unnamed "Israeli official" — identified by Channel 12 as Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] himself — accused the [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]] chief of having known in advance about the [[hamas|Hamas]] attack of October 7, 2023, and doing nothing to stop it.[5] "Ronen Bar preferred not to attend the government meeting dealing with his case, simply because he was afraid of giving answers," the official said, "and especially of answering one question: Why, after you knew about the Hamas attack many hours before it happened, did you do nothing and did not call the prime minister?"[5] Netanyahu had sought throughout to place responsibility for the failure on the security establishment, arguing he was not woken when warning signs were picked up by Israel's intelligence services in the hours before the invasion.[5]

Around midnight on Thursday, March 20, 2025, the Israeli government unanimously approved Bar's ouster, with his removal set for April 10 or sooner if a successor was named, according to a statement from the prime minister's office.[5] Bar refused to attend the nighttime meeting, instead sending a letter to the government stating that the process was illegal and that the prime minister's motives were "fundamentally flawed."[5] The move was expected to be contested in Israel's Supreme Court.[5]

In the letter, Bar said he was investigating Netanyahu's inner circle over corrupt ties to [[qatar|Qatar]], where Hamas leadership resides.[5] Badlands commentary noted that Netanyahu and Bar were effectively accusing one another of foreknowledge or involvement in October 7, that former Shin Bet director [[nadav-argaman|Nadav Argaman]] had claimed knowledge of Netanyahu's Qatari dealings concerning the funding of Hamas, and predicted the imminent collapse of Netanyahu's government and a political civil war in Israel.[5]
