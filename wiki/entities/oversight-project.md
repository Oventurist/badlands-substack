# Oversight Project

The Oversight Project is a transparency and litigation organization that pursues records disclosure from federal officials. In July 2026 it published more than 70 hours of recorded 2016 and 2017 conversations between [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and his memoir ghostwriter [[mark-zwonitzer|Mark Zwonitzer]], material originally obtained during special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s classified documents investigation.[1]

## Release of the Biden tapes

The recordings were released after a lengthy legal fight in which Biden's attorneys spent months attempting to block publication before abandoning their lawsuit; a judge accepted the dismissal on a Monday, clearing the way for release.[1] On the tapes Biden is heard warning "some of this may be classified … So, be careful. I'm not sure … It's not marked classified but —" and at another point saying, "The next thing I have here is, um, this is classified …"[1] The audio also captures Biden frequently pausing, speaking haltingly, and struggling to recall events, details consistent with Hur's earlier characterization of Biden's memory.[1]

Oversight Project Vice President of Litigation [[jeff-clark|Jeff Clark]] said the recordings should force a full reexamination of the decision to give Biden a pass for retaining and disclosing classified information.[1]

## CIA conflict-of-interest records

In January 2024 the Oversight Project, the investigative arm of the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]], obtained through a Freedom of Information Act request records showing that the [[cia|CIA]] submitted 32 OGE Form 202 conflict-of-interest referral notifications to the [[office-of-government-ethics|Office of Government Ethics]] between January 2021 and May 2023.[2] The project provided the records to [[daily-caller|The Daily Caller]], which reported that the agency appeared to have referred dozens of employees to the Department of Justice over potential conflicts of interest during the Biden administration.[2] The OGE informed the project that multiple forms may be submitted for a single referral, so the number of Form 202s may not equal the total number of referrals.[2]
