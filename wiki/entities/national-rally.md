# National Rally

The National Rally (Rassemblement National, RN) is a French nationalist and anti-immigration political party led by [[jordan-bardella|Jordan Bardella]] and still heavily influenced by its former presidential candidate [[marine-le-pen|Marine Le Pen]].[1] By 2024 the party had become the principal vehicle of the French populist right and the chief beneficiary of youth disaffection with the country's establishment parties.[1]

## Youth support ahead of the 2024 European elections

Polling by Ipsos published in March 2024 showed 31 percent of French voters aged 18 to 24 intending to vote for the National Rally in the European Parliament elections scheduled for June.[1] That was a nine-point gain on the party's standing in December 2023 polling, an unusually rapid movement for a single quarter.[1] The party's principal competitor on the hard right, [[reconquete|Reconquête]], collapsed from 8 percent to 3 percent among the same voters over the same period.[1]

## Interpretation

Coverage of the polling emphasized that the underlying story was disillusionment with establishment parties, with young French voters drifting toward both ends of the spectrum rather than uniformly rightward.[1] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that this pattern pointed toward "strange alliances" between left and right populists, drawing an analogy to cooperation between Rep. Matt Gaetz and Rep. [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]] during the 2023 speaker fight in the U.S. House.[1]

## Response to the EU Migration Pact

The National Rally was among the nationalist parties that condemned the [[european-parliament|European Parliament]]'s April 2024 approval of the [[eu-asylum-and-migration-pact|EU Asylum and Migration Pact]], which they argued cedes national sovereignty to an increasingly centralized [[european-union|European Union]].[2] Party leader [[marine-le-pen|Marine Le Pen]] said the pact "organizes the tutelage and control of nations" and pledged to end policies encouraging mass immigration following the June 9 European elections, at which the party was expected to win the largest bloc of French seats.[2]
