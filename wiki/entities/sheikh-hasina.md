# Sheikh Hasina

Sheikh Hasina is the former Prime Minister of Bangladesh, who led the country for 15 years at the head of the Awami League party before being ousted in the [[2024-bangladesh-government-overthrow|2024 overthrow of her government]].[1]

## Fall from power

In August 2024, weeks of student-led protests against job quotas escalated into nationwide violence that claimed more than 700 lives according to the interim government's tally, and Hasina fled the country as crowds stormed her residence.[1] Nobel Peace Prize laureate [[muhammad-yunus|Muhammad Yunus]] became chief adviser of the interim government that replaced her.[1]

## Allegations by her former minister

[[mohibul-hasan-chowdhury|Mohibul Hasan Chowdhury]], who served in Hasina's cabinet and acted as chief negotiator during the crisis, later told RT that the riots which brought down her government were backed by [[usaid|USAID]] and the family of [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]], and that US-linked NGOs including the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]] had been running campaigns against the government since 2018.[1] He described the unrest as a "carefully planned" operation bankrolled by Western interests rather than a spontaneous youth revolt.[1]


## Resignation and flight in August 2024

On Monday, August 5, 2024, Hasina resigned as prime minister of Bangladesh and reportedly fled the country after protesters stormed her official residence, following weeks of deadly anti-government demonstrations.[2] Scenes of jubilation erupted in the streets as demonstrators celebrated the end of her fifteen years in power, climbing on tanks and scaling an imposing statue of her father, the independence leader Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, in Dhaka and attacking its head with an axe.[2] Images showed flames billowing from vehicles near her house and protesters inside the building, smashing walls and looting its contents; earlier in the day the military and police had attacked demonstrators rallying in the area, according to a journalist working for CNN in Dhaka.[2]

In a national address, Bangladesh's army chief, General Waker-uz-Zaman, confirmed that Hasina had resigned and announced that the military would form an interim government.[2] Addressing the protesters, who were largely young Bangladeshis and students, he said: "Whatever demands you have we will fulfil and bring back peace to the nation, please help us in this, stay away from violence," adding that "the military will not fire at anyone, the police will not fire at anyone, I have given orders."[2]
