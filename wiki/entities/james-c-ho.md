# James C. Ho

James C. Ho is a United States circuit judge on the [[us-court-of-appeals-for-the-fifth-circuit|U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit]], known for writing separately in high-profile cases to register concerns about judicial process and institutional legitimacy.[1]

## Concurrence in A.A.R.P. v. Trump

In May 2025, after the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] vacated the Fifth Circuit's jurisdictional dismissal in [[aarp-v-trump|A.A.R.P. v. Trump]] and remanded with instructions to move expeditiously, Ho joined the Fifth Circuit's order assigning the case to the next available oral-argument panel and wrote a seven-page concurrence.[1]

In it he affirmed the duty of an inferior court to follow Supreme Court rulings — "We don't have to like it. But we have to do it." — while stating "sincere concerns about how the district judge as well as the President and other officials have been treated in this case."[1] He defended district judge Wes Hendrix by name against what he framed as an unfair attack from the high court, and warned that "the disrespect they have been shown will not inspire continued respect for the judiciary, without which we cannot long function," citing In re Westcott, 135 F.4th 243, 250–51 (5th Cir. 2025), in which he had also concurred separately.[1]

## Reception in independent media

The May 21, 2025 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] highlighted the concurrence, with [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] writing that although the opinion was formally a concurrence, "Judge Ho isn't happy about it," and treating his legitimacy warning as validation of the outlet's argument that judicial authority rests on public belief in the courts' legitimacy.[1]
