# Hafizullah Amin

Hafizullah Amin was President of Afghanistan in the period between the April 1978 [[saur-revolution|Saur revolution]] and his killing by Soviet forces on December 27, 1979, the day Russia's military entry into Afghanistan officially began.[1]

## Character and alignment

According to [[paul-fitzgerald|Paul Fitzgerald]] and [[elizabeth-gould|Elizabeth Gould]] in [[valediction-three-nights-of-desmond|Valediction: Three Nights of Desmond]], Amin was not the sort of individual western media portrayed.[1] He was not a Marxist, was not in any way pro-Soviet, and was not even a serious Muslim.[1] The evidence the authors gathered indicated he was little more than an opportunistic [[cia|CIA]] tool, interfacing closely with his nominal enemy [[gulbuddin-hekmatyar|Gulbuddin Hekmatyar]] — another CIA asset — in an effort to bring global heroin production into Afghanistan.[1] Both men were in truth united as members of the same Ghilzai tribe, which had long sought to assert dominance over Kabul.[1]

That tribal goal went hand in hand with Amin's objective of undermining the nationalistic forces associated with King Daoud within the PRPD during the April 1978 Saur revolution that deposed the king.[1]

## Dubs and Amin

Amin held at least fourteen secret meetings with U.S. Ambassador [[adolph-dubs|Adolph Dubs]], who was negotiating a plan that would keep the Soviets out of an Afghan trap while still enriching Amin.[1] The authors argue that this negotiation threatened [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]]'s script and precipitated the ambassador's assassination.[1]

## Death

Amin's CIA connection ultimately proved his own undoing, and also resolves the paradox that, despite being a nominally pro-Soviet Afghan president, Soviet forces wasted no time killing him on December 27, 1979.[1] In the article's framing, Amin was the sacrificed asset who would play the role of an Afghan Lee Harvey Oswald, taking the primary blame for the chaos that erupted under Russia's soft underbelly.[1]

## The CIA-asset thesis and the Soviet invasion

"Who Is The Real Enemy?" places Amin at the center of the events that triggered the Soviet intervention in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]].[2] Born in 1929 outside Kabul, an ethnic Pashtun from an intellectual background, he studied education at [[columbia-university|Columbia University]] in New York City, where he also adopted his Marxist ideology.[2]

As leader of the radical Khalq faction of the [[peoples-democratic-party-of-afghanistan|People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan]], Amin brutally eliminated his party rivals, forcibly disappeared thousands of Afghans, confounded the [[soviet-union|Soviet Union]]'s leadership and forced a series of foreign interventions that sealed Afghanistan's fate for the next forty years.[2] Dissatisfied with the PDPA's rule after the 1978 coup, he outmaneuvered his predecessor and fellow Khalq member [[nur-mohammad-taraki|Nur Mohammad Taraki]] by packing the Politburo with loyalists; [[leonid-brezhnev|Leonid Brezhnev]] tried to prevent Taraki's murder, but Amin had him killed in October 1979.[2]

Afghans who lived through Amin's roughly 100 days of rule are quoted describing him as a "bloodthirsty ruler" on the payroll of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and an "American chess piece" whose actions split and weakened the PDPA — a U.S.-trained man and American pawn who resorted to forced disappearance and burying people alive in order to incite the population against the Soviet-backed communist regime.[2] Badlands Media treats these accounts not as conspiracy narrative but as accurate, arguing the CIA staged the coup.[2]

Soviet documents support the suspicion of Western contacts: in December 1979 KGB chief [[yuri-andropov|Yuri Andropov]] wrote to Brezhnev that Amin's behind-the-scenes activities might mean a political reorientation to the West and that he kept his contacts with the American chargé d'affaires secret.[2] Andropov and defense minister Dmitri Ustinov also cited the danger of American missiles being deployed in Afghanistan.[2] The Soviet Union sent special forces into Kabul to kill Amin and take control of the city as it invaded.[2] One witness compared Amin's savageries to those later repeated by [[gulbuddin-hekmatyar|Gulbuddin Hekmatyar]].[2]
