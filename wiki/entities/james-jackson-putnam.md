# James Jackson Putnam

James Jackson Putnam was an American neurologist and Professor of Diseases of the Nervous System at [[harvard-university|Harvard]] who became the leading early American advocate of [[psychoanalysis]].[1]

Putnam encountered the work of [[sigmund-freud|Sigmund Freud]], and his subsequent public endorsement of it represented a significant breakthrough for the psychoanalytic cause in the United States.[1] In 1911, using Freud's work, Putnam and fellow neurologist [[ernest-jones|Ernest Jones]] organized the founding of the [[american-psychoanalytic-association|American Psychoanalytic Association]]; Putnam was elected its president and Jones its secretary.[1]

Badlands Media commentary places Putnam's role within a broader account of the 1910s as the decade when systems of mass control — including control exercised through the unconscious mind — were installed in American life.[1]
