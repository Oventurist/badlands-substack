# Karin Immergut

Karin Immergut is a United States District Judge in Oregon, appointed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Portland National Guard injunction

In early October 2025, Immergut issued a temporary injunction blocking Trump's order to deploy [[national-guard|National Guard]] troops from other states to Portland, Oregon, amid protests outside a Portland [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] facility.[1]

[[white-house|White House]] Press Secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] sharply criticized the ruling on October 6, calling it "untethered in reality and in the law" and asserting that "the president has the right to call up the National Guard in cases where he deems it appropriate."[1] Leavitt accused protesters outside the ICE facility of "inciting violence."[1] The administration filed an appeal, with Leavitt expressing confidence that the president's powers would be upheld.[1] Trump publicly criticized the judge he had himself appointed for blocking the deployment.[1]

## Ninth Circuit stay of the Portland order

In October 2025 the [[united-states-court-of-appeals-for-the-ninth-circuit|Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals]] temporarily lifted Immergut's Saturday ruling, which had halted the deployment of Oregon [[national-guard|National Guard]] troops to Portland, while a broader ban she issued on any state's National Guard entering the city remained in place.[2] The administrative stay applied only to that first order, and the appeals court said it had not assessed either side's arguments, stating that the move preserved the status quo as it deliberated.[2]

Immergut's wider order, issued Sunday night, still prohibited National Guard forces from any state from entering Portland.[2] "The administrative stay will best preserve the status quo," the Ninth Circuit wrote, noting that Oregon troops had been federalized but not deployed before the initial order.[2] In her earlier ruling Immergut had found that Portland was "not significantly violent or disruptive" enough to justify federal intervention and said the president's claims were "simply untethered to the facts."[2] The appeals court scheduled arguments for that Thursday on whether to extend the stay, prompting Badlands commentary that the dispute would make a notable Supreme Court case.[2]
