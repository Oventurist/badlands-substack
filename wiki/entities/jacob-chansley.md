# Jacob Chansley

Jacob Chansley, widely known by the nickname "the QAnon Shaman," is an American man who became the single most photographed participant in the events of January 6, 2021 at the United States Capitol. His horned headdress and face paint made him the visual shorthand used by news organizations to depict the day, and he was subsequently prosecuted and imprisoned.[1]

## The surveillance footage dispute

In March 2023, [[tucker-carlson]] aired portions of the roughly 42,000 hours of Capitol security video that had been provided to his program. Carlson released footage that he said showed Capitol Police officers escorting Chansley peacefully through the building rather than confronting him as an insurrectionist, and argued that this contradicted the charge that Chansley had committed sedition.[1] The Chansley material was presented alongside claims regarding [[brian-sicknick]] and [[ray-epps]] as part of Carlson's broader argument that the [[january-6-committee|January 6 committee]] had propagated false narratives.[1]

Commentary published in the [[badlands-news-brief]] treated the Chansley footage as evidence that the full video archive should be released to the public — or to a figure such as [[elon-musk]] — rather than being controlled by [[fox-news]], and dismissed claims that a full release would create security risks for what is a heavily photographed public building.[1]

Footage aired by Carlson on the evening of Monday, March 6, 2023 appeared to show that [[united-states-capitol-police|Capitol Police]] made no attempt to stop Chansley from reaching the Senate floor, but instead escorted him there.[2] As described in reporting by the New York Post and Just the News, the video showed Chansley walking through the corridors of the U.S. Capitol flanked by two Capitol Police officers who had not subdued him and who did not appear to have restricted his movement with handcuffs.[2] Carlson said of the tapes that the Capitol Police "never stopped Jacob Chansley. They helped him. They acted as his tour guides."[2] Chansley received a 41-month sentence in connection with his role in the incident.[2]

Badlands contributor [[absolutetruth1776|AbsoluteTruth1776]] argued that the footage confirmed that the public had for two years received only edited and chopped video serving a narrative against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his supporters, that there had been no insurrection, and that the January 6 narrative would continue to crumble as more video was released.[2]
