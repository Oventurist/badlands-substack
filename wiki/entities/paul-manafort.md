# Paul Manafort

Paul Manafort is an American political consultant and lobbyist who served as chairman of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2016 presidential campaign and became an early target of the [[mueller-special-counsel-investigation|Mueller special counsel investigation]].[1]

## Indictment and Ukrainian connections

An indictment charging Manafort and his deputy Rick Gates with breaking foreign lobbying law was unsealed in late October 2017.[1] It accused Manafort of hiring the [[podesta-group|Podesta Group]] — identified as "Company B" — to lobby on behalf of an ostensibly independent nonprofit that was "under the ultimate direction" of the Ukrainian president, his party, and the Ukrainian government.[1] Special Counsel [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]]'s inquiry into Manafort's finances subsequently gave rise to the federal investigation of [[tony-podesta|Tony Podesta]] and his firm.[1]

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] emphasizes that the entire media narrative — that Manafort had Russian connections and was being indicted over Russian collusion, thereby tying Trump to Russia — was false: Manafort was not charged with anything relating to Russian collusion, and his connections, like the Podestas', were Ukrainian rather than Russian.[1] He characterizes this as a case study in [[disinformation]] and deception.[1]

## Q claims

Q drop 15 (October 31, 2017) asserted that "Manafort was placed into Trump's camp (as well as others)," framing him as a plant rather than a genuine Trump ally, and stated that the coming corruption disclosures were so serious that deals would have to be cut, since otherwise "70% of elected politicians would be in jail."[1] Lange uses the Podesta Group's abrupt closure following Manafort's indictment as evidence that Mueller's investigation had been redirected away from Trump and toward Democratic-aligned targets.[1] These claims reflect Badlands Media commentary and are not established fact.

## Q drop #15

Chapter 3 of [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] lists Manafort among the topics raised in [[qanon|Q]] drop #15 of October 31, 2017, describing him and others as "plants which were inserted into Trump's team in the hopes of sabotaging him or framing him."[2] The author notes that the subject recurs in greater detail later in the drops.[2]

## The "PLANT" framing in "Setting the Stage"

Joe Lange's article "Setting the Stage" uses Manafort as the reference case for what a "PLANT" means in a criminal investigation, arguing that he was purposely inserted into the 2016 [[donald-trump|Trump]] campaign so that the enemy would have a target through which to prove Russian collusion.[3] Lange cites Q drop 1508 of June 15, 2018 — "Integrity. Devotion. Loyalty. Will POTUS and Q allow Manafort to suffer the wrath of the cabal….for helping POTUS and the good guys? PLANTS need water!"[3]

In Lange's account the plan failed: Manafort was supposed to be charged with Russian collusion in order to build the case against Trump, but [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]] charged him only with tax evasion offences dating back many years, because there was no collusion evidence.[3] Lange then inverts the tactic, suggesting that [[david-bowdich|David Bowdich]] was Trump's own plant inside [[james-comey|Comey]]'s FBI — the same method turned back against those who used it.[3]

## The "Company B" indictment and the Podesta parallel

Special prosecutor [[robert-mueller|Robert Mueller]]'s federal criminal indictment of Manafort, unsealed in late October 2017, assigned the [[podesta-group|Podesta Group]] the pseudonym "Company B" and explained that the firm had represented the same Putin-allied, state-funded organisation Manafort did, at the same time, and had likewise apparently failed to disclose its lobbying properly.[4] Tony Podesta stepped down from the firm in the aftermath.[4]

Badlands writer Joe Lange stresses the asymmetry: both Manafort and the Podesta Group worked for the Ukrainian government without registering, but only Manafort was indicted under the [[foreign-agents-registration-act|Foreign Agents Registration Act]], while the Podesta Group was permitted to file retroactively and was never charged.[4] He argues the public framing of Manafort as a Russia-collusion figure obscured that his exposure — and the Podestas' — was Ukrainian, and that the firm's abrupt closure indicates something beyond a FARA violation was in play.[4]
