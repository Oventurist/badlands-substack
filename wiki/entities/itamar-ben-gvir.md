# Itamar Ben Gvir

Itamar Ben Gvir is [[israel|Israel]]'s National Security Minister. He has advocated surrounding prisons holding Palestinian security detainees with Nile crocodiles as a deterrent to escape attempts, saying the concept was inspired by Florida's "Alligator Alcatraz."[1]

The proposal advanced in July 2026 when Environmental Protection Minister [[idit-silman|Idit Silman]] reclassified the Nile crocodile into a category permitting security agencies to keep the animals under specified conditions.[1] The [[israel-prison-service|Israel Prison Service]] had previously sent officials to a crocodile farm to examine the practical aspects of the proposal.[1] Reporting indicated the regulatory change enables consideration of a pilot program but does not mean crocodiles have been deployed at prison facilities.[1]

## Sde Teiman protests

Ben-Gvir figures in Badlands Media's account of the [[sde-teiman|Sde Teiman]] detainee abuse case. When the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] arrested four soldiers accused of sexually assaulting a Palestinian prisoner, Israeli settlers led by members of the [[knesset|Knesset]] and by Ben-Gvir in his capacity as National Security Minister stormed the military base where the accused were being held and attempted to free them with excessive force, requiring IDF soldiers to physically repel demonstrators who tore down barriers.[2] The multi-day demonstrations became known as the "Right to Rape" protests.[2]

## Calls to assassinate al-Sharaa (2026)

As National Security Minister, Ben-Gvir made public statements calling for the assassination of Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]], formerly known as Mohammed Abu al-Jolani.[3] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that this sat awkwardly beside [[joe-kent|Joe Kent]]'s disclosure that the [[cia|CIA]] worked directly with [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] and [[islamic-state|ISIS]] in [[syria|Syria]] on Israel's behalf, given al-Sharaa's own past leadership in both groups.[3] He linked the shift in Israeli posture to unmet expectations that al-Sharaa would cede southern Syrian territory, and to reports of [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] forces raising the Israeli flag over southern Syrian towns as part of a [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] agenda.[3]

## Flotilla detainee video and Petro's response (2026)

Ben-Gvir posted video of detained [[global-sumud-flotilla|Global Sumud Flotilla]] activists, including Colombians, kneeling and handcuffed after [[israel|Israel]] intercepted the convoy, which reportedly carried about 430 international volunteers attempting to bring humanitarian aid to Gaza.[4] Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] accused him of acting "like a true Nazi" over the treatment of the detainees.[4] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] defended the interception itself but distanced himself from Ben-Gvir's handling of the detainees and ordered deportations expedited.[4]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that Ben-Gvir published the video himself and appeared proud of it, describing the display of hubris as an increasingly common pattern among Israeli leaders and linking it to [[knesset|Knesset]] member [[yitzhak-kroizer|Yitzhak Kroizer]]'s remarks about Palestinian civilians.[4]

## Rejection of the US–Iran agreement (June 2026)

On Monday, June 15, 2026, Ben Gvir publicly criticized the United States–[[iran|Iran]] agreement announced by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[5] According to AFP, he said Israel was not a party to the deal, argued that military operations should continue until [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] is dismantled, and opposed withdrawing from territory held by Israeli forces in Lebanon.[5] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read the statement as a declaration that Israel considers itself unbound by the accord and intends to continue pursuing war, contrasting it with Iran's demonstrated restraint and cooperation with Trump.[5]

## Misattributed viral video (March 2026)

In March 2026 a viral video widely described as showing Ben Gvir calling for more Islamic migration and takeover in the West was identified by Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] as actually depicting [[benzi-gopstein|Benzi Gopstein]], leader of the [[lehava|Lehava]] movement, with Ben Gvir standing behind him.[6] The writer noted that Ben Gvir had served as Gopstein's defense attorney after Gopstein's 2015 arrest for incitement to violence over calls to expel all Christians from Israel and burn all churches.[6]

## Exchange with Ahmad Tibi

In October 2024, Ben-Gvir, then Israel's National Security Minister, reacted furiously after being called a "terrorist" by Palestinian-Israeli politician [[ahmad-tibi|Ahmad Tibi]].[7] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] highlighted the episode, noting that the response from Ben-Gvir's side was that Jews cannot be considered "terrorists," which he presented as evidence of supremacism and double standards in Israeli politics.[7] The contributor nonetheless said the willingness of an Israeli politician to make the accusation publicly gave him hope that [[israel|Israel]] "can be saved from itself."[7]

## Denial of Palestinian nationhood (November 2025)

Ahead of a [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]] vote on implementing the next stage of the U.S.-brokered peace plan for Gaza, Ben-Gvir declared that the Palestinian people do not exist.[8] The Council was set to vote Monday, November 17, 2025, on a resolution drafted by the United States and backed by several Arab and Muslim countries, which its sponsors said "offers a pathway to Palestinian self-determination and statehood."[8]

In a lengthy X post on Saturday, Ben-Gvir — described as Israel's hardline security minister and leader of the ultranationalist [[otzma-yehudit|Otzma Yehudit]] party — claimed that "there is no such thing as 'Palestinian people,'" calling the nation "an invention without any historical, archaeological, or factual basis."[8] He wrote that "the collection of immigrants from Arab countries to the Land of Israel does not constitute a nation," that they "certainly do not deserve a reward for the terrorism, murder, and atrocities they have spread everywhere, especially in Gaza," and that the only real solution was "encouraging voluntary emigration."[8] Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] made a similar appeal, urging Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] to tell the world a Palestinian state "will never be established."[8]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] commented that this was precisely the position U.S. Ambassador [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] had espoused for decades, found it notable that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] chose Huckabee as ambassador at this moment, and called Ben-Gvir's claim "pure projection" given that most Israelis are of Eastern European descent with a mixture of Turkic heritage.[8] He closed by asking why identity politics is tolerated when practiced by the Zionist Right.[8]

## Yale protest, April 2025

On 22 April 2025 pro-Palestinian protesters erected eight tents on Beinecke Plaza at [[yale-university|Yale University]] to protest an upcoming talk near the campus by Ben-Gvir, described in coverage as the far-right Israeli security minister.[9] Around 200 people gathered by 9:30 p.m., but organizers disbanded the encampment before 11:30 p.m., citing threats of "retribution" from university administrators.[9]

## Leverage within Netanyahu's security cabinet

Badlands commentary describes Ben-Gvir as one of three hardline ministers — with [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] and [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] — who together hold three of the six seats in [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s security cabinet and can dissolve the government at any time by resigning.[10] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterizes these ministers as protégés of the Christian-hating [[lehava|Lehava]] movement who have spent months threatening to collapse the coalition if Netanyahu stops the war, which the commentary offers as the reason Netanyahu will subvert or sabotage every hostage and ceasefire deal put on the table.[10] Netanyahu's reliance on them is attributed to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] having courted centrists such as [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] away from him.[10]

## Gaza settlement push (September 2024)

In September 2024 Badlands commentary reported that Gaza was being prepared for settlement by Israeli citizens, an effort led by National Security Minister Ben-Gvir.[11] The observation appeared in analysis arguing that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s warnings to Lebanese civilians ahead of a possible invasion followed the same pattern the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] used in the West Bank in 1967 and more recently in Gaza, and situating both within the [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] project.[11]

## Coalition arithmetic comparison (May 2025)

Badlands commentary in May 2025 contrasted Ben-Gvir with [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]], noting that Ben-Gvir's Jewish Power Party controls only six [[knesset|Knesset]] seats, so when his faction resigned in January — and later returned — [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] still retained a 62-seat majority.[12] The author characterized Ben-Gvir as the louder and more obnoxious of the two zealots in Netanyahu's core security cabinet, while describing Smotrich as the more cunning and politically polished.[12]

## Criminal record and the Rabin hood ornament (October 2024 retrospective)

Badlands commentary in October 2024 described Ben-Gvir as one of the government ministers in "the deepest throes of bloodlust," circulating videos from the preceding six months in which the national security minister was said to pull a pistol on Arabs and, in one clip, to call in Hebrew on settlers and police around him to shoot Palestinians.[13] The piece stated he has been convicted on at least eight charges, including supporting a terrorist organization and incitement to racism, quoting former Shin Bet official Dvir Kariv's remark that "we had to change the ink on the printer."[13]

The same commentary recalled Ben-Gvir's 1995 appearance on Israeli television, interviewed about a [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] political rally he had attended, holding a Cadillac hood ornament he claimed had been taken from the car of then Prime Minister [[yitzhak-rabin|Yitzhak Rabin]].[13] Rabin had signed the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]] in 1993 with Mahmoud Abbas, the first attempt to establish a two-state solution, and had drawn the wrath of Zionists while Netanyahu campaigned to replace him using violent rhetoric.[13] In the interview Ben-Gvir said, "We got to his car, and we'll get to him too"; two weeks later Rabin was assassinated by Yigal Amir, beginning Netanyahu's tenure as prime minister.[13]

## Opposition to the 2024 hostage deal

On Sunday, August 11, 2024, Ben Gvir denounced continued talks toward a hostage-and-ceasefire deal with [[hamas|Hamas]] in Gaza, telling Radio 103 FM that such an agreement would amount to surrender to the terror group.[14] In the same interview he called for cutting off humanitarian aid to the Strip, a step Israel's allies had repeatedly warned would cross a red line and cost Israel their support.[14] His criticism followed an announcement by Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s office the previous Thursday that an Israeli delegation would attend ceasefire-for-hostage talks on August 15 to negotiate the release of hostages seized during the October 7 attack that killed 1,200 people in Israel.[14] "We are crushing Hamas," Ben Gvir said, claiming he had been receiving briefings from local commanders in the field in addition to cabinet updates, and asked: "So, now we should go to a conference and surrender? This is a serious mistake by the prime minister."[14]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the episode as another instance of Ben Gvir issuing public veiled threats to keep Netanyahu from concluding a deal, arguing that Netanyahu — though himself "a warmongering sociopath" — is beholden to more extreme religious figures in his cabinet, a configuration the writer believes was deliberately engineered by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during his first term to pin Netanyahu politically and avert a wider war.[14]

## Named by Biden in December 2023

In off-camera remarks on December 12, 2023, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] singled out Ben-Gvir as an example of far-right members of the Israeli government who "don't want a two-state solution with the Palestinians," arguing that changes to the coalition were necessary for progress.[15] Biden added that while Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] appeared to grasp that Israel's support extended beyond the United States to the European Union and most of the world, he was "not sure Ben-Gvir and his war Cabinet" understood that this support was eroding.[15]

## Sde Teiman base storming, July 2024

In July 2024 Ben Gvir was among the right-wing lawmakers who urged supporters to gather outside the [[sde-teiman-detention-facility|Sde Teiman]] military facility after Israeli Military Police arrested nine [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] soldiers suspected of the serious abuse of a Palestinian detainee held there.[16] Alongside Heritage Minister Amichay Eliyahu and [[religious-zionism-party|Religious Zionism]] MK Zvi Sukkot, he was described as whipping supporters into an angry frenzy, and mobs subsequently broke into both Sde Teiman and the IDF's Beit Lid base in central Israel.[16] Ben Gvir, whose ministry controls the [[israeli-police|Israel Police]] and the [[israel-prison-service|Israel Prison Service]], said that "the spectacle of Military Police officers coming to arrest our best heroes at Sde Teiman is nothing less than shameful."[16] IDF chief [[herzi-halevi|Herzi Halevi]] called the scenes "bordering on anarchy," and [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] tied the rioters to Ben Gvir's [[otzma-yehudit|Otzma Yehudit]] party and the teachings of [[meir-kahane|Meir Kahane]].[16]

## Al-Aqsa synagogue call and settler violence (August 2024)

Speaking to Army Radio in late August 2024, Ben-Gvir called for a synagogue to be built atop the [[al-aqsa-mosque|Al-Aqsa Mosque]] compound and said Jews should be permitted unrestricted prayer at the site, remarks that provoked outrage in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] and condemnation even from some Israeli groups as inflammatory and unrealistic.[17] Analysts noted that any such construction would require destroying the mosque, with the potential to ignite region-wide Islamic uprisings.[17]

Badlands Media reported that Ben-Gvir had been leading "prayer groups" onto the compound in violation of rules barring Israeli prayer and protest there, restrictions traceable to earlier attacks by extremists such as Meir Kahane and Baruch Goldstein, a portrait of whom Ben-Gvir reportedly keeps in his home.[17] The outlet situated his conduct within a broader campaign to clear the way for a Third Temple.[17]

Ben-Gvir was also criticized by the head of the [[shin-bet|Shin Bet]] and Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] for encouraging settler attacks and for failing to deploy the police forces under his ministry to halt incidents or arrest participants.[17] Badlands noted that religious zealots aligned with him stormed two [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] bases in protest at the arrest of soldiers accused of assaulting a Palestinian detainee.[17] The outlet also drew attention to his mentor [[benzi-gopstein|Benzi Gopstein]], who faced incitement proceedings in 2015 after calling for churches to be burned and Christians expelled from [[israel|Israel]], and argued that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had turned to such figures out of political desperation.[17]
