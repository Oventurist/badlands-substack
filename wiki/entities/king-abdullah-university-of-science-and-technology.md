# King Abdullah University of Science and Technology

King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST) is a Saudi Arabian research university whose scientists developed the direct lithium extraction technology at the center of the kingdom's effort to build a lithium mining sector.[1]

## Lithium extraction research

In 2021, researchers at the university announced what they believed to be an economically viable system to extract high-purity lithium from seawater.[1] The work was funded with investment from [[saudi-aramco|Saudi Aramco]], the Saudi national oil company, and [[maaden|Ma'aden]], the national mining company, with the goal of cultivating a domestic mining sector for lithium, the critical material used in advanced battery technology.[1] The principal obstacles were cost — the method is relatively expensive compared with conventional lithium mining — and the very low lithium content of brine and seawater.[1]

## Commercialization

The technology has since been commercialized through [[lihytech|LiHyTech]], also known as Lithium Infinity, a startup founded out of the university.[1] LiHyTech was named to lead a commercial pilot program for direct lithium mining announced by deputy minister of mining affairs [[khalid-al-mudaifer|Khalid bin Saleh Al-Mudaifer]] after lithium reserves were identified in Saudi oil fields.[1] A pilot at an Aramco oilfield found the brine there rich in lithium, yielding far more than typical brine, and reportedly prompted companies such as [[exxon-mobil|Exxon Mobil]] to consider similar projects.[1]

The research is framed within Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]]'s [[saudi-vision-2030|Saudi Vision 2030]] plan to move the kingdom's economy beyond oil and to position it as a leader in technology as well as energy.[1]
