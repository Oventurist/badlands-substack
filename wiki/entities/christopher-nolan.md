# Christopher Nolan

Christopher Nolan is the British-American filmmaker who wrote and directed *Oppenheimer* (2023), the three-hour biopic about [[j-robert-oppenheimer|J. Robert Oppenheimer]], the [[manhattan-project|Manhattan Project]] and the [[nuclear-arms-race|nuclear arms race]].[1]

## Oppenheimer and the box office

*Oppenheimer* opened the same weekend as Greta Gerwig's *Barbie*, a pairing the Badlands review compared to a moment out of *Idiocracy* while conceding it delivered studios much-needed box office success.[1] Per AP News, *Barbie* took the top spot with roughly $155 million from 4,243 North American theaters, the biggest opening of the year and a first-weekend record for a film directed by a woman, while Universal's *Oppenheimer* took in $80.5 million from 3,610 theaters — Nolan's biggest non-Batman debut and one of the best-ever starts for an R-rated biographical drama.[1] The reviewer noted that *Oppenheimer* did only about 52 percent of *Barbie*'s business and concluded that "our culture is broken."[1]

## Style and criticism

The review calls *Oppenheimer* the most boring Nolan film the author had seen, saying its storytelling felt closer to Oliver Stone's *JFK* (1991) than to the thrilling, mind-bending construction expected of Nolan.[1] Glimpses of the director survive in the use of sound and in psychological sequences where Oppenheimer (Cillian Murphy) is trapped inside his own mind, and the Los Alamos [[trinity-test|Trinity test]] is described as beautifully shot, providing updated visuals of the formidable power of nuclear weapons.[1]

## Narrative function

The central charge is that Nolan sacrifices entertainment value in favor of narrative value, doing heavy lifting for what Badlands calls the central narrative: that communists are merely misunderstood, that nuclear weapons are the destroyer of worlds, and that [[global-governance|global governance]] is the only solution.[1] By refreshing public memory of world-ending capability with an all-star cast and modernized mushroom-cloud imagery, the film — in the review's reading — strengthens the nuclear metaphor being deployed against [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] at precisely the moment the regime needs it.[1]
