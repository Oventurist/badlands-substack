# Abbas Araghchi

**Abbas Araghchi** is the Foreign Minister of [[iran|Iran]] and a lead negotiator in the country's talks with the United States.[1]

## Role in the 2026 negotiations

Araghchi participated in the talks that produced a tentative US–Iran memorandum of understanding in June 2026, reported by Axios and mediated jointly by [[qatar|Qatar]] and [[pakistan|Pakistan]].[1] Negotiators reached the tentative agreement on a Wednesday night in discussions involving Araghchi and Qatari mediator [[ali-al-thawadi|Ali Al-Thawadi]], according to diplomats and US officials cited by the outlet.[1]

The framework, reportedly to be known as the [[islamabad-agreement|Islamabad agreement]], would reopen the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] without tolls, provide compliance-linked sanctions relief, and extend a 60-day ceasefire including in Lebanon.[1] While President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he expected a signing ceremony over the weekend, an Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson said Tehran had not yet made a final decision on the proposal.[1]

## Oman talks and new sanctions (2026)

In February 2026, following indirect U.S.–Iran talks concluded in Oman, Araghchi stressed the need for calm dialogue without threats; the United States responded by announcing new sanctions.[2] The exchange occurred as [[iran|Iran]] maintained that its nuclear program is peaceful while having enriched uranium to 60% purity since the U.S. withdrawal from the 2015 deal, and as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] demanded limits on enrichment and Iran's ballistic missile program.[2]

## July 2026 interview and backlash

In a lengthy interview published in July 2026, Araghchi stated that Tehran had refused even temporary limits on uranium enrichment because doing so would sacrifice national "honor" and set a precedent of yielding to military pressure.[3] He said Iranian leaders were certain the United States and [[israel|Israel]] would attack regardless of negotiations, which he characterized as largely intended to demonstrate that diplomacy had been attempted.[3]

Araghchi claimed Iran closed the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] from the first day of fighting under a pre-agreed contingency plan triggered by any threat to the supreme leader, and asserted that Iran later achieved strategic parity with the United States, forcing mutual language on respecting sovereignty into a memorandum of understanding.[3] He identified the regime's greatest vulnerability as internal penetration by spies, noting that the Supreme National Security Council never convened in full during the war because the simultaneous loss of its members could collapse the system, and that meetings occurred in underground tunnels.[3] He said he has never met the new supreme leader, [[mojtaba-khamenei|Mojtaba Khamenei]], whose appointment he described as a signal of policy continuity.[3]

Speaking to establishment podcaster Javad Mogoei about the opening hours of the war — when coordinated strikes killed the supreme leader along with senior military, security, and political figures — Araghchi said the security breach was "not limited merely to infiltration and obtaining information," adding that "sometimes it also exists in influencing the decision-making process itself. And even more importantly, it extends to shaping our psychological environment and public perception."[3] [[iran-international|Iran International]] quoted DAWN analyst Omid Memarian suggesting one manifestation would be officials advocating policies that prolong the war against Iran's own interests.[3]

## The Gulf prince conversation

Araghchi recounted a heated pre-war dialogue with an unnamed Gulf prince who warned him: "They will destroy your nuclear facilities, and they will seize your missile capabilities."[3] He described the atmosphere as one in which the assembled leaders "were all waiting for this event," and replied that if Iran's nuclear facilities were struck, Iran would strike theirs, since "our missiles do not reach American territory, but they do reach their bases in the region."[3] He then laughed and told the prince, "Sir, God forbid, we do not want to strike you," and the prince closed with: "Endure for one more month at most, and this story will end forever."[3]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the exchange as congruent with the conduct of [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], arguing Iran was receiving a full warning from the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and that the prince's closing line was an allusion to [[narrative-warfare|narrative warfare]] and a [[fifth-generation-warfare|5GW]] psyop running against all parties, including the soldiers.[3]

## Hardline reaction

The interview drew sharp criticism inside Iran from hardline commentators and outlets affiliated with the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Revolutionary Guards]], who accused Araghchi of recklessly disclosing sensitive security details, wartime contingency planning, and leadership vulnerabilities that could aid foreign intelligence services.[3] Badlands commentary used the episode to revisit the [[devolution|Iranian devolution]] thesis and to ask whether [[qassem-soleimani|Qassem Soleimani]]'s role had been to escalate conflict with Israel through proxy militias and prevent peace, and whether Araghchi and [[masoud-pezeshkian|Masoud Pezeshkian]] could have taken office at all had Soleimani lived.[3]

## Caspian Sea strike protest (July 2026)

After [[iran|Iran]] accused [[ukraine|Ukraine]] of attacking an Iranian commercial vessel in the Caspian Sea — a strike that killed one sailor and wounded another — Araghchi said he had raised the issue with European Union foreign policy chief [[kaja-kallas|Kaja Kallas]] and Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov, describing the attack as a violation of international law and warning that it "cannot go unanswered," according to Iranian statements reported by AFP.[4] He also charged that "Ukraine attacked Iran at the behest of Israel in order to draw Europe deeper into their war" with the Middle East.[4] Iran summoned Ukraine's chargé d'affaires in Tehran to protest and said it would pursue the matter through diplomatic channels, while Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] claimed responsibility for long-range strikes on Iran-linked cargo shipping.[4]

## July 2026 interview

In a July 2026 interview with documentary filmmaker Javad Mogouee, Araghchi said Iran's [[supreme-national-security-council-iran|Supreme National Security Council]] is responsible for assessing whether the country should go to war or agree to a ceasefire, but that the final decision rests with the Supreme Leader.[5] He said he had faced criticism from rival political factions following the temporary understanding with the United States, and that Iran agreed to resume negotiations after Washington again demanded zero uranium enrichment and threatened military action — a step intended to demonstrate that Iran had exhausted diplomatic options.[5]

In the same interview Araghchi said he has never met [[mojtaba-khamenei|Mojtaba Khamenei]] in person and that only a small number of people have met him.[5] The Badlands Brief highlighted that admission alongside Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitri Peskov]]'s statement that Putin had no plans to telephone the Ayatollah, questioning whether the new Supreme Leader exists.[5] The Brief framed the timing within a broader regional realignment involving [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed Al-Sharaa]]'s Syria, Iraqi Prime Minister [[ali-al-zaidi|Ali Al-Zaidi]], and reported Iran–Iraq cooperation on de-escalation.[5]

## Response to the March 2026 strikes

Following the death of Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]] in the US–Israeli air campaign, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said that attempts at regime change through external force would be "mission impossible."[6] He added that Iran was interested in de-escalation but that there was at that time no direct communication with Washington.[6]

## St. Petersburg meeting with Putin (April 2026)

In April 2026 Araqchi met Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] in St. Petersburg, where Putin pledged strong support for [[iran|Iran]] and positioned [[russia|Russia]] as a key broker in efforts to end the escalating Middle East conflict.[7] Putin praised Iran's "resilience" against US and Israeli pressure and reaffirmed Moscow's willingness to help stabilize the region and mediate peace talks.[7] The meeting was described as highlighting a deepening strategic partnership between Moscow and Tehran, including long-term cooperation agreements and continued collaboration on energy and nuclear infrastructure, and came as US–Iran negotiations stalled and tensions around the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and Lebanon remained elevated.[7]

## Oman nuclear talks (February 2026)

US–Iran nuclear talks were expected to take place in [[oman|Oman]] in early February 2026 at [[iran|Iran]]'s request, with Araghchi and his team scheduled to meet [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]].[8] Araghchi arrived late Thursday, according to video footage posted by RT.[8] Axios reported, citing anonymous US officials, that at least nine Middle Eastern countries had contacted the [[white-house|White House]] at senior levels urging the administration to proceed with the talks after rumors of cancellation.[8] US officials said they remained skeptical of a breakthrough, with one anonymous official stating, "They asked us to keep the meeting and listen to what the Iranians have to say... But we are very skeptical."[8]

## Geneva talks (February 2026)

Araghchi represented Iran in the second round of indirect nuclear negotiations with the United States, which concluded in Geneva on Tuesday, February 17, 2026.[9] He said progress had been made and that Tehran would submit detailed proposals within the following two weeks, with Iranian officials reporting that the two sides had reached an understanding on core principles.[9] Iran made clear through the Oman-mediated channel that it would accept nuclear concessions in exchange for sanctions relief but would not abandon uranium enrichment or negotiate over its ballistic missile program.[9] The talks proceeded against a backdrop of continued confrontation, with Supreme Leader [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]] warning that Iran possessed weapons capable of sinking American warships and Iran, Russia and China launching the [[maritime-security-belt|Maritime Security Belt 2026]] naval exercises in the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] as the round wrapped up.[9]

## April 2026: "messages sent," but no negotiations

Speaking to [[al-jazeera|Al Jazeera]] amid the ongoing US-Israeli war on [[iran|Iran]], Araghchi said that messages had been exchanged with the United States but firmly insisted that Tehran was not engaged in any negotiations with Washington.[10] He said Iran has "zero" trust in the US and held no expectation that such communications would lead to meaningful results, stressing that Iran would not negotiate while under attack and had clear conditions for any potential talks.[10]

His remarks came as Al Jazeera reported continued US-Israeli strikes on Iranian infrastructure, including civilian sites such as steel plants and ports, alongside regional fallout including a Kuwaiti oil tanker hit in Dubai port and the killing of three UN troops, and as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] claimed the campaign against Iran could end in "two to three weeks."[10] Araghchi underscored that Iran was prepared to defend itself and would ensure safe passage through the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] for non-hostile vessels.[10]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the exchange as evidence of a backchannel, pairing it with Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]]'s remark about a "reshaping of the world order" and arguing that Lavrov's "we" encompassed Iran's cultivated relationships with [[russia|Russia]], [[china|China]] and the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[10]

## January 2026 remarks on provocateurs

During the January 2026 escalation between the United States and [[iran|Iran]], Foreign Minister Araghchi told Fox News' Bret Baier that operatives masquerading as protesters were shooting people in order to provoke US intervention.[11] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the interview while arguing that Araghchi and President [[masoud-pezeshkian|Masoud Pezeshkian]] are considered moderates representing a shift away from clerical zealotry, and that the wider Iran war narrative was a distraction operation covering the finalization of Phase Two of the Gaza plan.[11]

## April 2026 Iranian proposal and Saint Petersburg travel

In late April 2026 Iran delivered a new proposal to the United States offering to reopen the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and end the war, with nuclear negotiations postponed to a later stage, according to a U.S. official and two sources with knowledge.[12] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was expected to convene a Monday Situation Room meeting with senior advisers on Iran, and said Iran could phone him if they wanted to talk, as Araghchi arrived in Saint Petersburg for an expected meeting with Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[12]

The proposal was delivered to the White House via Pakistani intermediaries and reportedly focused on extending the ceasefire or ending the war outright, as well as lifting the US blockade of the strait — a chokepoint accounting for 20% of the world's seaborne oil trade — with nuclear talks not beginning until the blockade is lifted.[12] Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry predicted that Iran would ultimately be permitted a civil nuclear energy program with the enrichment needed to support it, arguing that [[israel|Israel]]'s artificial "zero enrichment" red line would have to be unwound incrementally, and that Araghchi's Moscow diplomacy would coincide with significant movement toward US–Iran peace even as Israel pursued escalation.[12]

## Retaliation warning (February 2026)

In February 2026, as [[iran|Iran]] continued to defy U.S. demands over its nuclear program, Araghchi warned that Tehran would retaliate against U.S. military bases in the Middle East if Washington attacked Iran, while maintaining that nuclear negotiations remained preferable to conflict.[13] Iranian officials reiterated at the time that Tehran would not surrender uranium enrichment, calling the program a legal right even under threats of military action, and insisted that indirect talks — including rounds held in [[oman|Oman]] and planned discussions in [[istanbul|Istanbul]] — remain narrowly focused on nuclear issues rather than ballistic missiles or regional proxy networks.[13]

## Rome talks, April 2025

Araghchi led the Iranian delegation at the second round of U.S.-Iran nuclear talks, which wrapped up in Rome after four hours on Saturday, April 19, 2025, opposite President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]].[14] The first round, held the previous weekend in Oman, had focused on setting the tone and format of the negotiations, while U.S. officials said their goal for the Rome round was to produce a framework for how talks would proceed; expert-level discussions were expected to continue on April 26 in Oman.[14] The negotiations took place amid an internal administration debate, and friction between Washington and Israel, over whether diplomacy or military strikes were more likely to prevent Tehran from obtaining a nuclear weapon.[14]
