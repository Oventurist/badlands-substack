# David Soren

**Dr. David Soren** is an archaeologist at the University of Arizona whose findings on [[carthage|Carthage]] were reported in a 1987 New York Times article and are used by Badlands Media as the historical foundation for its argument about elite [[child-sacrifice|child sacrifice]].[1]

## Findings on Carthaginian society

Soren is quoted describing a civilization in which "the thrust for commerce, prosperity and the good life were blended with a religion so intense that the richest Carthaginian could cheerfully consign a son or daughter to the flames of the sacrificial pit to redeem a pledge to the gods."[1]

The same reporting carries the observations of Dr. Lawrence Stager, who distinguished the Precinct of Tanit from ordinary Carthaginian necropolises: the precinct contained only cremated children from stillborn fetuses to four-year-olds, plus young cremated animals, with urns marked by dedications to Tanit and [[baal|Ba'al Hammon]].[1] Stager also reported that the ratio of children to animals rose from three-to-one in the oldest layers to ten-to-one by the third century BC, and that sacrifice was gradually "democratized" from elite parents in the fifth century BC to professional classes and artisans by the third.[1]

## Use in modern argument

[[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] invokes Soren's timeline to argue that elite fixation on child sacrifice grows with status and power, and that elites lead the normalization of the practice into the culture at large — a slow and steady process he compares to modern cultural developments.[1]
