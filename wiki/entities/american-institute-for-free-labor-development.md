# American Institute for Free Labor Development

The American Institute for Free Labor Development (AIFLD) was a labor institute founded in 1962 as an entity of the [[afl-cio|AFL-CIO]], funded by [[usaid|USAID]] and the [[cia|CIA]]. It was one of the labor institutes consolidated in 1997 to form the AFL-CIO's [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]]. In the Badlands Media article [[a-more-perfect-labor-union|A More Perfect (Labor) Union]], AIFLD is presented as a central instrument of covert U.S. labor operations in Latin America.

## Operations in Latin America

The article states that AIFLD's founding in 1962 was "bankrolled by none other than USAID and the CIA," and that it trained over 25,000 Latin American unionists in "anti-communist" tactics at camps in Virginia. AIFLD graduates are described as having led strikes that paralyzed leftist governments:

- **Guyana.** CIA-funded AFL-CIO actions triggered a three-month general strike against [[cheddi-jagan|Cheddi Jagan]] in 1962–63, "paving the way for U.S.-backed regime change."

- **Brazil.** In Brazil's 1964 coup against President [[joao-goulart|João Goulart]], AIFLD alumni in the metalworkers' union provided intelligence to the CIA and the [[pentagon|DoD]], justifying military intervention.

- **Chile.** From 1971 to 1973, AIFLD "channeled millions of dollars to right-wing unions and political parties opposed to [[salvador-allende|Allende's]] socialist agenda," per Tim Shorrock's 2003 *The Nation* piece cited by the article, helping finance the revolt by Chile's professional class that provided the pretext for Gen. [[augusto-pinochet|Augusto Pinochet's]] 1973 coup and seventeen-year dictatorship.

- **Central America.** During the [[lane-kirkland|Lane Kirkland]] era, AIFLD trained Salvadoran death-squad affiliates supporting Reagan's counterinsurgency.

The article describes AIFLD as one of "four US-government-funded labor institutes created during the cold war."
