# Efraín Ríos Montt

José Efraín Ríos Montt was a Guatemalan general, dictator, and president from 1982 to 1983, described in the source article as one of the most vicious tyrants in recent Guatemalan history.[1] He was a graduate of the U.S. Army's [[school-of-the-americas|School of the Americas]].[1]

Ríos Montt was proud of a political philosophy he summarized as "beans for the obedient; bullets for the rest."[1] His rule fell within a period of more than thirty years of civil war in which tens of thousands were slaughtered, with the total killed estimated to exceed 200,000 in a country of ten million; the indigenous Mayan population of roughly six million endured what the article calls the greatest suffering in Latin America.[1] Most of the ranking generals involved in the numerous coups and acts of terror and murder during this period were trained at the SOA.[1] Among them was General Manuel Antonio Callejas y Callejas, chief of army intelligence, whom the United Nations cited as the individual responsible for most of the assassinations of political activists and government opponents in the 1970s and early 1980s — also an SOA graduate.[1]

The article frames Guatemala as possibly the Central American country that suffered most at the hands of the SOA and of the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]-backed [[shadow-government|shadow government]] the author attributes to [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]], leaving surviving Mayan communities trapped in poverty with no power to change their circumstances.[1]
