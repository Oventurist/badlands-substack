# Elor Azaria

Elor Azaria is a former soldier of the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] whose 2016 killing of a wounded Palestinian became a defining test of Israeli military ethics and public opinion.[1]

In March 2016, while serving in the occupied West Bank city of Hebron, Azaria calmly walked over to Abdel Fattah al-Sharif — an injured Palestinian knife attacker lying on the ground — and shot him in the head.[1] Video of the killing, released by a human rights group, provoked political uproar.[1]

IDF commanders and many political figures, including Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], initially condemned the shooting as a breach of the military's ethical codes that had to be prosecuted.[1] The right-wing backlash was swift: polls suggested more than half of the Israeli public supported Azaria, and protests were held in his defence.[1] Netanyahu, playing to his base, appeared to change position, criticising his generals for investigating Azaria and eventually joining calls for a pardon.[1] Azaria served nine months of a 14-month manslaughter sentence and was released in 2018 to a hero's welcome.[1]

The case was cited in July 2024 as the obvious precedent for the far-right mob attacks on two IDF bases carried out in support of soldiers accused of sexually torturing a detainee at [[sde-teiman-detention-facility|Sde Teiman]], part of what The Guardian described as a "deep moral deterioration" being normalised in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.[1]
