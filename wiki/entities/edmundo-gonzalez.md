# Edmundo González

Edmundo González Urrutia is the Venezuelan opposition candidate who contested the country's July 28, 2024 presidential election against incumbent [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[1] On Thursday, August 1, 2024, the United States formally recognized González as the winner of the vote, discrediting the official results announced by Venezuela's electoral authority.[1]

## US recognition

U.S. Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] declared that "given the overwhelming evidence, it is clear to the United States and, most importantly, to the Venezuelan people that Edmundo González Urrutia won the most votes in Venezuela's July 28 presidential election."[1] The announcement followed calls from multiple governments — including close allies of Maduro — for Venezuela's National Electoral Council to publish detailed vote counts as it had in previous elections.[1] The main opposition coalition said it possessed contrary evidence in the form of more than two-thirds of the tally sheets printed by each electronic voting machine after the polls closed.[1] Maduro responded to the U.S. move with a terse admonishment: "The United States needs to keep its nose out of Venezuela!"[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media commentary treated Washington's endorsement of González as a "reverse indicator" — arguing that when the entire Western establishment aligns behind a single narrative about a foreign election, the opposite position is a better analytical starting point.[1] The commentary situated the Venezuelan dispute in a lineage of contested foreign elections and regime-change episodes, including the "show elections" framing applied to Saddam Hussein before the 2003 invasion of Iraq, the [[color-revolution|color revolution]] warning issued by [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]] in 2011, and the 2014 Maidan Revolution in Ukraine.[1]

## AP tally-sheet analysis, August 2024

An Associated Press review of vote tally sheets released by Venezuela's main opposition on August 2, 2024 indicated that González had won significantly more votes than the government acknowledged.[2] From nearly 24,000 tally-sheet images representing 79% of voting machines, the AP decoded QR codes to tabulate 10.26 million votes, finding 6.89 million for González and 3.13 million for [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]].[2] The [[national-electoral-council|National Electoral Council]]'s updated figures, by contrast, gave Maduro 6.4 million and González 5.3 million.[2]
