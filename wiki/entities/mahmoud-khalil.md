# Mahmoud Khalil

Mahmoud Khalil is a Palestinian graduate of [[columbia-university|Columbia University]] and a lawful permanent resident of the United States who was detained by federal immigration authorities in March 2025, becoming the central figure in a national controversy over the deportation of foreign-born participants in pro-Palestine campus protests.[1]

## Detention and deportation proceedings

Khalil was taken into custody by [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] agents on Saturday, March 8, 2025.[1] The administration of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] stated that it intended to deport Khalil — who is married to a US citizen — over his role in pro-Palestine protests at Columbia University.[1] A federal judge temporarily blocked the deportation, and Khalil was held awaiting proceedings in a federal jail in Louisiana.[1]

Khalil's lawyers and supporters argued that the administration was wilfully conflating criticism of Israel's war in Gaza with support for "terrorism."[1] Civil liberty groups roundly condemned the arrest and described Khalil as a "political prisoner."[1]

## Protests

The detention triggered a string of demonstrations. On Thursday, March 13, 2025, protesters flooded the lobby of Trump Tower in New York City in a show of solidarity with Khalil, resulting in arrests.[1]

## British government employment

Reporting by [[middle-east-eye|Middle East Eye]] revealed that Khalil had worked for the British government on what the outlet described as its "flagship soft power policy" for a period of years.[1] Khalil graduated with a master's degree from Columbia's School of International and Public Affairs in December 2024, and had been one of the main student negotiators during the pro-Palestine campus encampment in the spring of 2024.[1]

Before that, according to online records reviewed by Middle East Eye, Khalil worked from 2018 to 2022 as a programme manager at the Syria Office in the British embassy in Beirut, serving as a local manager for the [[chevening-programme|Syria Chevening Program]] — a UK government international scholarship scheme — as well as for the [[conflict-stability-and-security-fund|Conflict, Stability, and Security Fund]].[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Khalil "sounds like a spy," characterizing the Conflict, Stability, and Security Fund as resembling a money-laundering front of the kind associated with Operation Gladio.[1] She contended that subverting the US Constitution is grounds under multiple sections of the US Code for losing a green card and being deported, and held that the combination of a British security clearance, Syrian NGO projects, and Khalil's academic background debunked the narrative that he was a well-meaning activist held as a political prisoner.[1] She further noted the immediate availability to Khalil of a free, collaborative constitutional legal defense despite NGOs having recently taken a hit on dark money, concluding that the episode had been instructive regardless of its outcome.[1]

## Judicial block on deportation and protests (March 2025)

A federal judge temporarily blocked Khalil's deportation while weighing the petition challenging his arrest.[2] The decision came as several hundred protesters massed in Lower Manhattan on Monday, March 10, 2025 to demand his release, following his detention that Saturday.[2] "He was arrested for speaking out against genocide," said organizer Marian Osman. "We have a right to free speech. Political speech is protected speech in this country."[2] After President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted that Khalil would not be the last pro-Palestinian demonstrator arrested, Osman pledged that protesters would not be intimidated, saying "We are just getting started" and that protesters "should be upset and angry."[2] The case was reported by [[abc-news|ABC News]].[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the case needed to go through the courts to clarify the rights of what she called subversive foreigners relative to those of citizens, conceding that content-based restrictions on Americans' [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] rights are "pretty much dead on arrival" but questioning whether the same protection extends to green-card-holding foreigners accused of terrorizing American students and calling for violence.[2] She enumerated the statutes she considered applicable: 8 U.S.C. § 1227(a)(4)(A)(iii) on deportability for advocating the violent overthrow of the government; 8 U.S.C. § 1227(a)(4)(B) on deportability for terrorist activities; 8 U.S.C. § 1182(a)(3)(A)(iii) on inadmissibility for advocating violent overthrow; 18 U.S.C. § 2385, the Smith Act; and 8 U.S.C. § 1227(a)(4)(A)(i)-(ii) on deportability for membership in subversive organizations.[2] Her conclusion was that the protections do not extend.[2]


## Denial of Furlough for Son's Birth (April 2025)

Khalil, the [[columbia-university|Columbia University]] graduate detained by [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]], was denied permission to attend the birth of his first child, several outlets confirmed on Monday, April 21, 2025.[3] His attorneys had written on the Sunday to Melissa Harper, director of ICE's New Orleans field office where he was held, requesting a two-week release to be with his wife for the delivery; Harper denied the request about 30 minutes later, dismissing the furlough "after consideration of the submitted information and a review" of his case.[3] His wife, Dr. Noor Abdalla, said she welcomed their son into the world alone and called it "a purposeful decision by ICE to make me, Mahmoud, and our son suffer."[3]

## Cited in the UPenn antisemitism probe debate (2025)

Khalil's deportation was invoked in November 2025 commentary on the federal [[equal-employment-opportunity-commission|EEOC]] investigation into antisemitism at the [[university-of-pennsylvania|University of Pennsylvania]], where Jewish groups objected to authorities seeking personal information about Jewish staffers.[4] Badlands host GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted that Jewish groups had openly expressed concern about deportations ever since the Palestinian activist Khalil was removed for his perceived antisemitism, and argued it was strange that groups which had demanded action against activists like Khalil became alarmed once the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] investigative apparatus turned in their direction.[4]
