# Pat Ryder

Major General Pat Ryder is a United States Air Force officer who served as press secretary and principal spokesman for the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], regularly delivering the Defense Department's public statements on force deployments and Middle East operations.[1]

## THAAD deployment to Israel

On Sunday, October 13, 2024, Ryder announced in a statement that Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] had authorized the deployment of a [[thaad|THAAD]] missile defense battery to Israel at the direction of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], accompanied by roughly 100 American troops to operate the system.[1] Ryder said the battery would bolster Israel's air defenses following Iranian missile attacks on Israel in April and October of that year.[1]

"This action underscores the United States' ironclad commitment to the defense of Israel, and to defend Americans in Israel, from any further ballistic missile attacks by Iran," Ryder said.[1]

## Reception in Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the deployment Ryder announced was being overstated in public commentary, noting that a THAAD battery requires roughly 95 soldiers simply to operate, that similar systems had been sent to Israel and Arab states since 2019, and that no evidence indicated U.S. forces had been mobilized for offensive action on any front bordering Israel.[1] He characterized the resulting coverage as "another nothing burger" and an attempt to villainize [[us-central-command|CENTCOM]] and General [[michael-kurilla|Michael Kurilla]].[1]

## Announcement of the Austin transfer of duties (February 2024)

As [[pentagon|Pentagon]] press secretary, Maj. Gen. Ryder announced on Sunday, February 11, 2024 that Deputy Secretary of Defense [[kathleen-hicks|Kathleen Hicks]] had assumed the functions and duties of the top spot at the department shortly before 5 p.m., after Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] was hospitalized for symptoms "suggesting an emergent bladder issue."[2] Ryder said Austin had been taken to [[walter-reed-national-military-medical-center|Walter Reed National Military Medical Center]] around 2:20 p.m. and had travelled with the unclassified and classified communications systems required for his duties, and confirmed that the [[white-house|White House]], the chairman of the joint chiefs of staff and [[congress|Congress]] had been notified.[2] The Pentagon issued its first statement within three hours — a marked contrast with the delayed notifications surrounding Austin's December 2023 hospitalization.[2]

## Statements on Prigozhin and Wagner (August 2023)

As [[pentagon|Pentagon]] spokesman, Ryder gave the official U.S. assessment following the plane crash that killed [[yevgeny-prigozhin|Yevgeny Prigozhin]], saying an intentional explosion had brought the aircraft down and dismissing initial U.S. reports of a surface-to-air missile as "inaccurate."[3] He said the department assessed Prigozhin was likely on board and killed in the crash, without positing a precise cause.[3] Ryder also announced that the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]]'s presence on the ground in Ukraine was "no longer a significant factor."[3]
