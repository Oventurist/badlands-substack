# FTI Consulting

FTI Consulting is the business advisory firm where [[declan-kelly|Declan Kelly]] worked as a top executive before entering government service, and whose contractual terms the America for Sale series uses to explain the timing of [[teneo-holdings|Teneo]]'s launch.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

Citing Politico, the article reports that Kelly stepped down from FTI in July 2009 and signed a two-year non-compete agreement that barred him from doing consulting work for anyone else.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The agreement nonetheless offered him the chance to continue working for FTI part-time as a consultant on the Dow Chemical and Coca-Cola accounts, and Kelly opted to keep those lucrative relationships.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Between August 2009 and January 2011 — in the midst of his unpaid [[state-department|State Department]] work as [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s economic envoy to Northern Ireland — [[dow-chemical|Dow]] paid Kelly $2.4 million through FTI in consulting fees.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

The article draws a direct inference from the arrangement: Kelly, [[doug-band|Doug Band]] and [[paul-keary|Paul Keary]] began planning their joint firm in 2009 but waited until June 2011 to launch it because of the FTI non-compete.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) In the intervening two years Kelly used his official State Department position to make corporate connections that would later benefit both Teneo and the Clintons, while Dow and Coca-Cola became two of Teneo's first and biggest accounts.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)
