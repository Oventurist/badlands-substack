# Paul Rieckhoff

Paul Rieckhoff is an American media commentator and the founder of Iraq and Afghanistan Veterans of America and of Righteous Media. He appears as a talking head on [[msnbc|MSNBC]].[1]

## July 2023 remarks on a post-9/11 style crackdown

In late July 2023, Rieckhoff called on MSNBC for a post-9/11 style government crackdown as a means of winning what he described as "a battle for hearts and minds" among Americans who might turn against the leftist mindset and become, in the framing of the coverage, overly patriotic.[1] He compared the "threat" of such a scenario to the "number one threat" faced after the September 11 attacks — a comparison critics read as equating conservatives and Trump supporters with terrorists.[1]

"A lot of Americans are up for grabs. I wouldn't dismiss it as just something only people on the right or watching [[fox-news|Fox News]] latch on to. We are in a battle for hearts and minds and there people are on the fence," Rieckhoff said.[1] He continued that leaders, messages, messengers and programs were needed "to get to those people that bring them over into community organizing and into non-profit organizations and away from the Patriot Front and Oathkeepers."[1]

Rieckhoff further argued that "the same kind of tectonic shift" in culture experienced in the immediate aftermath of the 9/11 attacks — which ushered in what Summit News characterized as a draconian police-state style crackdown on liberties — was needed again, and suggested that the earlier measures had not gone far enough.[1]

## Reaction

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], responded that the establishment's grip on the central narrative was slipping so badly that anti-America First actors were "literally calling for another [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]] to prevent political speech with which they disagree."[1] She characterized Rieckhoff's position as a call for a whole-of-government effort to restrict the speech of his political opponents, and expressed surprise that the founder of a veterans organization would not treat the Bill of Rights as paramount.[1]

Ashe situated the remarks in a longer sequence of censorship disclosures running from the [[twitter-files|Twitter Files]] to the [[disinformation-governance-board|Disinformation Governance Board]] and the revelations in [[murthy-v-missouri|Missouri et al v. Biden]], arguing that the establishment's aggression reflected a battle for hearts and minds it had already lost.[1]
