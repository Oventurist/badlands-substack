# Gupta Brothers

The Gupta brothers are an Indian-born business family whose commercial empire in [[south-africa|South Africa]] became the central subject of allegations that the country's government had been "captured" by private interests during the presidency of [[jacob-zuma|Jacob Zuma]].[1] South African authorities accused the family of looting the state, and the Badlands News Brief of May 31, 2024 treated the family's fate as a case study in how United States sanctions policy reshaped African politics and mining.[1]

## Sanctions under Executive Order 13818

On October 10, 2019, Secretary of State [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]] announced in a public statement that the Trump Treasury was sanctioning the three Gupta brothers under [[executive-order-13818|Executive Order 13818]], the Global Magnitsky-derived order authorizing the blocking of property of persons involved in serious human rights abuse or corruption.[1] The designation document stated that "the Gupta family leveraged its political connections to engage in widespread corruption and bribery, capture government contracts, and misappropriate state assets," and described the designation as targeting the Guptas' "pay-to-play political patronage, which was orchestrated at the expense of the South African people."[1] The Treasury document specifically cited the family's corrupt dealings with former president Jacob Zuma.[1]

## Flight to the Emirates and arrests

The Guptas fled to the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] in 2018.[1] Two of the three brothers were arrested in Dubai in June 2022, and the third and eldest brother was arrested in India in late May 2024.[1] A Dubai court nevertheless refused to extradite two of the brothers to South Africa, citing incorrect paperwork — a ruling South Africa's justice minister called "shocking," and one that the Financial Times used to illustrate the Emirates' growing leverage on the African continent.[1]

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that the Financial Times framing was deceptive, since the brothers had in fact been detained by two states he identifies with the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] — the UAE in 2022 and India in 2024 — and that the arrests flowed directly from the 2019 sanctions rather than representing Emirati obstruction.[1]

## Role in state capture

At the heart of the [[zondo-commission|Zondo Commission]]'s inquiry was Zuma's "state capture" of the South African government, which some 300 witnesses testified had been facilitated in large part by the Gupta brothers, through disinformation disseminated by their media companies and through state contracts they had illegally procured for their portfolio of businesses.[1] Badlands commentary noted that an Associated Press feature on Zuma's 2024 electoral comeback never once mentioned the Gupta brothers, treating the omission as an indicator of the article's political sympathies.[1]
