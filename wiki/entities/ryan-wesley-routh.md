# Ryan Wesley Routh

Ryan Wesley Routh is the man convicted of the September 2024 attempted assassination of then-candidate [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at Trump's Florida golf course during the presidential campaign.[1]

## Conviction and sentence

Routh was convicted on charges including attempted assassination of a presidential candidate and firearms offenses.[1] On February 4, 2026, Judge [[aileen-cannon|Aileen Cannon]] of the US District Court for the Southern District of Florida sentenced him to life in prison plus seven years.[1]

## Significance

Badlands framed the sentencing as one of a cluster of early-February 2026 federal court actions — alongside the [[fulton-county|Fulton County]] election-records motion and litigation over the FBI's [[arctic-frost|Arctic Frost]] investigation — that it characterized as long-term legal remedies working through the district courts.[1]

## Arrest of his son (2024)

In September 2024, while Routh remained in custody on two federal gun charges — possessing a firearm as a convicted felon and possessing a firearm with an obliterated serial number — and under investigation over the apparent assassination attempt, his son [[oran-routh|Oran Alexander Routh]] was arrested on federal child pornography charges.[2] Investigators said they found "hundreds" of such files while searching the younger Routh's Guilford County, North Carolina residence in connection with an unrelated investigation.[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated the convergence of themes around the Routh family — political derangement, pro-Ukraine activism and child exploitation — as suspiciously neat, calling it "too easy and too cartoonish" and consistent with a deliberate narrative deployment.[2]

## Request for indefinite trial delay (October 2024)

On Wednesday, October 2, 2024, federal prosecutors requested an indefinite delay in scheduling Routh's trial, citing the massive volume of evidence gathered in the 17 days since his arrest.[3] In a filing asking Florida District Judge [[aileen-cannon|Aileen Cannon]] to formally designate the case "complex," prosecutors said that "over the past two weeks, the United States has interviewed hundreds of witnesses," had executed 13 search warrants in Florida, Hawaii and North Carolina, and had seized hundreds of items of evidence including multiple electronic devices.[3] Investigators reported more than 100 outstanding subpoena returns and estimated they had "thousands of videos to review" from the seized devices.[3]
