# Chagos Islands

The Chagos Islands, or Chagos Archipelago, are a group of seven atolls comprising more than 60 islands in the Indian Ocean, south of the Maldives.[1] An atoll is a ring-shaped island with a coral rim encircling a lagoon; most of the Chagos Archipelago is submerged, with only a few islands containing dry land.[1] The largest and most notable island is [[diego-garcia|Diego Garcia]], site of a joint UK–US airbase often described as "an unsinkable aircraft carrier."[1]

## Sovereignty dispute

The islands have been under British control, but in May 2025 London approved a plan to hand them over to [[mauritius|Mauritius]] — a small island nation near Madagascar — while signing a 99-year lease retaining access to the base at Diego Garcia.[1] The dispute over sovereignty was shaped by a 2019 advisory opinion from the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]], which found that the United Kingdom's separation of the islands from Mauritius in 1965 was unlawful.[1]

## Proposed US purchase

Per a report in *The Telegraph* cited by RT, U.S. officials have discussed a proposal that could lead to the United States purchasing the Chagos Islands from Mauritius after the transfer from British control.[1] [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] had previously criticized the UK–Mauritius agreement as "an act of great stupidity" and argued that it posed national security concerns.[1] Mauritius has not confirmed receiving any formal U.S. offer; citing Reuters, RT reported that the Mauritian government said it had received no official proposal and reiterated that its sovereignty over the archipelago is "non-negotiable."[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the proposal "an interesting wrinkle in the effort to bring our military home and stop acting like the World Police," favoring closure of the overwhelming majority of the 800-plus U.S. installations abroad while recognizing strategic value in retaining a few — Diego Garcia among them.[1] He argued that Trump now seeks to bypass the British and deal directly with Mauritius, which "would leave the British sidelined and at the mercy of the United States," and observed a broader "pattern of dismantling the remnants of the old British Empire" under Trump.[1]
