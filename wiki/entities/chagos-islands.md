# Chagos Islands

The Chagos Islands, or Chagos Archipelago, are a group of seven atolls comprising more than sixty islands in the Indian Ocean, situated just south of the Maldives. Most of the archipelago is submerged, with only a few islands containing dry land. The largest and best known is [[diego-garcia|Diego Garcia]], site of a joint United Kingdom–United States military base.

## Sovereignty dispute

As summarized in [[badlands-brief-25c|Badlands Brief: Elections are Fake; as is Trump's Friendship with Bibi]], the islands have been controlled by the [[united-kingdom|United Kingdom]], which separated them from [[mauritius|Mauritius]] in 1965 — a separation the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]] found unlawful in a 2019 advisory opinion. In May 2025 London approved a plan to transfer the archipelago to Mauritius while retaining access to Diego Garcia through a 99-year lease.

## Reported American purchase proposal

The Brief reports, citing The Telegraph via [[rt|RT]], that U.S. officials have discussed a proposal that could lead to the United States purchasing the islands from Mauritius after the transfer from British control. President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had previously criticized the U.K.–Mauritius agreement as "an act of great stupidity" and argued that it posed national security concerns. Mauritius said it had received no official proposal and reiterated that its sovereignty over the archipelago is "non-negotiable."

[[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] describes the development as "an interesting wrinkle in the effort to bring our military home and stop acting like the World Police," arguing that while most of the 800-plus American installations abroad should be shuttered, Diego Garcia is among the few worth retaining. He reads the proposed direct dealing with Mauritius as part of "a pattern of dismantling the remnants of the old British Empire."
