# Central Intelligence Agency

The Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) is the United States foreign intelligence service. In [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]]'s April 2025 essay it occupies a central position in an alleged hierarchy of organized crime, standing above the [[jewish-mafia|Jewish Mafia]] and, through it, the [[italian-mafia|Italian Mafia]], while itself answering to British [[mi6|MI6]] and the [[city-of-london|City of London]].[1]

## Alleged role in music and crime

Carlson writes that the late-1980s shift in rap music from themes of positivity, unity, and ending violence toward shooting police and selling drugs was not organic but spurred by the [[fbi|FBI]] and the CIA, which he says were by then running record labels.[1] He asserts that artists who did not rap about committing crimes and killing cops had no place in the genre, and that [[gangster-rap|gangster rap]] was designed to influence young Black men toward a life of crime in order to fill newly privatized prisons at a time when crime rates were falling.[1]

## Alleged narcotics trafficking

Carlson states that the CIA smuggled drugs from South and Central America to be sold on the streets of South Central Los Angeles, and that [[rick-ross|Rick Ross]] — an aspiring young tennis player who could not read — became fabulously rich distributing them.[1] He describes the arrangement as trading guns in Central America for drugs, transporting those drugs to American cities, and thereby filling US private prisons, remarking that no one can accuse the CIA of not being good at their job.[1] The composite objective he attributes to the agency is creating racial division, selling guns and drugs to promote foreign wars and unrest, promoting a hood mentality in inner cities, and filling prison cells while being compensated for it.[1]

## Alleged relationship with the Jewish Mafia

Carlson argues the CIA could not allow the American public to learn that "their guy," [[meyer-lansky|Meyer Lansky]], ran the agency's drugs out of the Golden Triangle during the Vietnam War, and that [[the-godfather|The Godfather]] — published in 1969 and released as a film in 1972, at the height of that war — served to redirect public attention onto the Italian Mafia.[1]
