# Central Intelligence Agency

The Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) is treated throughout the article as the operational arm of what the author calls the shadow government, responsible for creating, funding and protecting the armed groups the United States publicly fights.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

The article states that terrorists have no need to rob banks because they are funded by the CIA and the shadow government, and that billions of dollars were flown straight to America's enemies under the nose of the American public.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) It recalls a previous instalment's claim that the Mujahideen were a private army the CIA created and funded to protect its heroin trade in Afghanistan — the same milieu from which the [[libyan-islamic-fighting-group|Libyan Islamic Fighting Group]] emerged in 1995.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

[[abdelhakim-belhaj|Abdelhakim Belhaj]] fought with the CIA-backed Mujahideen in Afghanistan in the 1980s, was interrogated in CIA prisons in the early 2000s, and was then handed to Libya; the article's conclusion is that he was a CIA asset from the beginning.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) It argues the agency needed to relocate Al-Qaeda's training infrastructure out of Afghanistan after 9/11 so as to keep supplying fighters, and that [[benghazi|Benghazi]] became that replacement hub and "a big part of their overall plan."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

The article also assigns the CIA a media function, asserting that a CIA-controlled press consistently painted [[muammar-gaddafi|Gaddafi]] as a sponsor of terrorism in the same light as Saddam Hussein, because "the CIA has always used the media to paint their targets as the worst enemies in the world."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) Its summary charge is that the corrupt CIA and shadow government have been arming and training the very terrorists the United States has been at war with since the 1980s, and that American troops were purposely sacrificed because the never-ending war on terror was by design, for money.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest) It further ties [[prince-alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed]] to both the Bush family and the CIA.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-conflict-of-interest)

## Penetration of organized labor

A separate Badlands piece documents the agency's use of American labor unions as instruments of foreign policy.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The practice began with the CIA's precursor, the [[office-of-strategic-services|Office of Strategic Services]], which collaborated with U.S. unions late in World War II to counter communist influence in Europe; once the agency itself existed, interventionists recognized that working through the AFL would succeed where direct CIA action would not, because workers abroad were more likely to trust fellow union members.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The resulting secret partnership earned the [[afl-cio|AFL-CIO]] the nickname "AFL-CIA."[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

By 1949 the agency was funnelling millions through the [[free-trade-union-committee|Free Trade Union Committee]] to split European labor federations, funding Force Ouvrière in France and non-CGIL unions in Italy, and backing strikes and propaganda that helped the Christian Democrats hold power in 1948.[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) From 1962 it co-financed the [[american-institute-for-free-labor-development|American Institute for Free Labor Development]] with [[usaid|USAID]], feeding operations against [[cheddi-jagan|Cheddi Jagan]] in Guyana, [[joao-goulart|João Goulart]] in [[brazilian-coup-1964|Brazil]] and Salvador Allende in [[chilean-coup-1973|Chile]].[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) Ramparts magazine and a 1967 New York Times series exposed CIA funds flowing through unions such as the UAW, prompting congressional hearings, but the article says the apparatus pivoted rather than reformed, later routing labor work through the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] and the [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]].[[3]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Alleged control of the American economy

"America Has a CIA-Run Economy" argues that the five largest American companies by market capitalization — [[apple|Apple]], [[nvidia|Nvidia]], [[microsoft|Microsoft]], [[alphabet|Alphabet]] and [[amazon|Amazon]] — all share the same master, the CIA, and are used to influence Americans and the world through propaganda and control.[[5]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) Because these firms hold about a third of the [[sp-500-index|S&P 500 Index]] weighting, the agency's alleged grip on them is presented as a grip on the economy itself.[[5]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

The mechanism described is a pipeline from [[darpa|DARPA]]: the agency takes DARPA-created technology and uses it to influence and monitor Americans, and because Americans would not voluntarily hand their information to the CIA, the agency creates companies and assigns people to serve as their public faces.[[5]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) Specific claims include that much of Facebook's early funding came from CIA shell companies and that Facebook launched on 4 February 2004, the day DARPA's [[lifelog|LifeLog]] program was cancelled; that Google Earth is an old CIA satellite handed to the company; and that one of [[amazon-web-services|Amazon Web Services]]' first big contracts was a $600 million CIA deal in early 2013, months before [[jeff-bezos|Jeff Bezos]] bought the CIA-run *[[washington-post|Washington Post]]* for $250 million.[[5]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

The article grounds this in the 1975 [[church-committee|Church Committee]] revelation of [[operation-mockingbird|Operation Mockingbird]], arguing that the CIA's easy control of Hollywood, music, television, radio and print collapsed with the arrival of the internet, and that creating and controlling social media and search engines was the agency's replacement strategy.[[5]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy) [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] concludes that Deep State and CIA strings over these corporations are now being cut, that some companies will be broken up, and that he will take that as a win.[[5]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-has-a-cia-run-economy)

In May 2024, CIA Director [[william-burns|William Burns]] travelled to Doha for an emergency meeting with Qatari Prime Minister Mohammed Al-Thani in an attempt to salvage the Gaza hostage deal, a mission described by an official briefed on the talks as aimed at exerting maximum pressure on Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]] to keep negotiating.[58] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used the episode to argue that the CIA and the [[state-department|State Department]] had become functionally synonymous, with the agency openly performing the State Department's diplomatic role, and drew a parallel to Burns's January 2023 meeting with Ukrainian officials, which produced no ceasefire.[58] He described the agency as an organisation specialising in destabilisation and mass murder, and noted a measure of satisfaction in watching it fail publicly on a global stage.[58]

## Alleged agency role in proxy conflicts

Commenting on the arrival of Russian military personnel at an air base in [[niger|Niger]] hosting US troops, the same writer advanced a framing in which the conflicts of the Middle East are best understood as the US military versus CIA proxy militias, asserting that ISIS was created by the CIA under the stewardship of then-Secretary of State Hillary Clinton.[58] He extended that framing to nearly every theatre where the US military operates against "rebel factions," including Africa, and argued that a Russian and American military presence sharing a base was therefore coherent rather than alarming — particularly given his claim that ISIS, which he equates with the CIA, was behind the recent high-profile terror attack in Moscow.[58] He also contended that when [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] calls the media "the enemy of the People," the real referent is the CIA, which he asserts controls the corporate media, citing Adam Riva's documentary *Time of Deceit*.[58] He recalled the November 11, 2017 meeting between Trump and Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of the APEC conference in Vietnam, which produced a joint statement on fighting ISIS together, as the origin point of that framing.[58]

## Conflict-of-interest referrals to the Office of Government Ethics

Records reviewed by [[daily-caller|The Daily Caller]] and reported in January 2024 indicated that the CIA reported dozens of its employees to the Department of Justice over potential conflicts of interest during the Biden administration.[59] Between January 2021 and May 2023 the agency filed 32 OGE Form 202 conflict-of-interest referral notifications with the [[office-of-government-ethics|Office of Government Ethics]], the mechanism by which agencies report alleged ethics violations referred to the DOJ.[59] The records were supplied to the Daily Caller by the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]]'s [[oversight-project|Oversight Project]], which obtained them through a Freedom of Information Act request; the OGE cautioned that multiple forms may correspond to a single referral, so the number of Form 202s may not equal the number of referrals.[59]
