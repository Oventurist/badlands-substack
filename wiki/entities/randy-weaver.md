# Randy Weaver

Randy Weaver was the Idaho man at the centre of the [[ruby-ridge|Ruby Ridge]] standoff, cited in the Badlands Media essay "Patsies of the CIA and FBI" as an example of a target who did not take the bait offered to him by federal informants.[1]

## The church picnic account

The article recounts that at Ruby Ridge, [[fbi|FBI]] informants went to church picnics pushing race hate, and that Weaver responded with incredulity.[1] The framing matters to the essay's argument: rather than presenting Weaver as a radical whom agents discovered, it presents him as an ordinary person to whom radicalism was marketed and who found the pitch absurd.[1]

## Significance

Weaver's reaction is used to illustrate the mechanism the article ascribes to the [[cia|CIA]] and FBI generally — that the agencies infiltrate anti-government, limited-government or accountability-minded groups and try to turn them into hate groups, or at least make them appear to be hate groups, so that the public will equate anti-government sentiment with hatred.[1] Where the manufactured extremism fails to take root, as the article suggests it did with Weaver, the confrontation nonetheless proceeds.[1] Ruby Ridge and Waco together form the historical template the essay applies to [[january-6-capitol-protest|January 6]].[1]
