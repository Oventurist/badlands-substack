# Gavin Wilde

Gavin Wilde is a former US intelligence official who co-authored the January 2017 [[intelligence-community-assessment|Intelligence Community Assessment]] on alleged Russian meddling in the 2016 election, and who emerged in May 2026 as a newly disclosed witness before congressional investigators examining the origins and handling of the Trump–Russia probe.[1]

## Background and intelligence career

Wilde, who by 2026 worked at think tanks in Washington, DC, co-authored the January 2017 assessment on Russian interference before serving on the [[donald-trump|Trump]] National Security Council in 2018 and 2019.[1] He has also said he worked on US intelligence assessments concerning alleged Russian influence efforts ahead of the 2020 election.[1] Badlands framed the 2017 assessment he helped write as "flawed," placing him at the keyboard for multiple overt acts in what commentators characterized as a treasonous grand conspiracy against Trump following the 2016 election.[1]

## "Witness 2" and the Ukraine impeachment

Wilde was previously referred to as "Witness 2" in the Ukraine impeachment documents.[1] According to reporting cited by Badlands, the 2019 claims by Witness 2 were critical in helping Intelligence Community Inspector General [[michael-atkinson|Michael Atkinson]] push Ukraine whistleblower [[eric-ciaramella|Eric Ciaramella]]'s complaint forward, and Wilde's "Russiagate"-linked biases were concealed from House investigators during the impeachment process.[1] [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] discussed that obstruction in his book *Government Gangsters*.[1]

## Significance in the Durham-era fallout

Wilde's disclosure to congressional investigators added another layer to scrutiny of how Russia-related assessments were developed and communicated, an inquiry tracing back to the FBI's 2016 counterintelligence investigation [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] and Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]]'s review of its origins.[1] Badlands contributor Ashe in America suggested Wilde appeared to be a long-standing cooperating witness rather than a recent flip, given the speed of subsequent disclosures.[1] The commentary noted that the assessment Wilde co-wrote was written at the direction of then-President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and largely overseen by then-FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]], former CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]], and former Director of National Intelligence [[james-clapper|James Clapper]] — raising the question of who was giving Wilde orders from 2017 to 2019.[1]
