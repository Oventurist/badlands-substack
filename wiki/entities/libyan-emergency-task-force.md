# Libyan Emergency Task Force

The Libyan Emergency Task Force (LETF) was a U.S.-based lobbying and advocacy organization active during the 2011 Libyan civil war. In the Badlands Media article "A Conflict of Interest," the LETF is identified as a lobbying group set up to channel U.S. taxpayer money to rebel groups fighting [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]], groups that the source claims were affiliated with [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]].

According to the article, [[mouza-moustafa|Mouza Moustafa]] served as Executive Director of the LETF during the Libya intervention, working closely with officials within the [[transitional-national-council|Transitional National Council]]. The source claims that the LETF was "set up by our enemies to fund the very groups we are at war with," and that it used the same playbook as the [[syrian-emergency-task-force|Syrian Emergency Task Force]], which Moustafa later directed.

The article asserts that the LETF was designed to make it easy for the enemy to steal taxpayers' money through lobbying, and that [[john-mccain|John McCain]] was directly involved in its operations. It claims that McCain and Moustafa "had been working together to fund our enemies for a long time," first in Libya through the LETF and then in Syria through the SETF. The source notes that the LETF's name was similar to the SETF's, which it interprets as evidence of a deliberate pattern.

According to the article, the LETF was part of a broader network that included the Libyan Council of North America (LCNA) and the Coalition for a Democratic Syria (CDS), all of which the source claims were fronts for funneling taxpayer money to terrorist organizations with the approval of the Obama administration.
