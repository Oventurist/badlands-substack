# Tony Dokoupil

Tony Dokoupil is an American television journalist who became anchor of the "CBS Evening News" at [[cbs|CBS News]], debuting in the role on January 5, 2026.[1]

## Debut monologue on media trust

In his debut broadcast, Dokoupil directly addressed the erosion of public trust in legacy media, acknowledging industry failures on stories including Hunter Biden's laptop, Russiagate and COVID-19 policy coverage, where in his telling elite perspectives had overshadowed the concerns of average Americans.[1] He pledged transparency, equal accountability for public figures regardless of party, and a commitment to prioritizing viewers over corporate or political interests, stating "you come first... I report for you."[1] In a monologue that circulated widely online, he catalogued missed stories running from Iraq to COVID to the Hunter Biden laptop and implored the audience, "Don't just trust me. Make me earn it."[1]

## Backlash

Dokoupil's debut drew sharp criticism for on-air errors, including repeated self-introductions, awkward segues, misnaming Minnesota's state nickname and visible control-room confusion.[1] Critics also accused him of being "MAGA-coded" under new CBS Editor-in-Chief [[bari-weiss|Bari Weiss]], whose arrival was portrayed in the press as shifting the network toward conservative appeal.[1] Outlets including The Daily Beast branded the performance "rocky" and error-strewn, and alleged it insulted the storied legacy of CBS.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated Dokoupil as a "cognitive cypher" for the accelerating death spiral of the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]], arguing that his only apparent sin — acknowledging the industry's trust hemorrhage and pledging to hold power to equal standards — was instantly reframed by his peers as partisan contagion.[1] In that reading, the dogpiling amounted to internal cannibalization by a protectorate devouring its own as its narrative shield wall fractures.[1]
