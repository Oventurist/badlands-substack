# Emerich de Vattel

Emerich de Vattel was the jurist and author of *The Law of Nations or Principles of the Law of Nature*, the treatise cited by Badlands Media as the authority establishing that natural freedoms cannot be alienated without consent.[1]

## Citation in the Federal Reserve analysis

Badlands Media's article on the legal relationship between Americans and [[federal-reserve|the Federal Reserve]] quotes Vattel from the English translation of 1805: "IT IS EVIDENT FROM THE LAW OF NATURE, THAT ALL MEN BEING NATURALLY FREE AND INDEPENDENT, THEY CANNOT LOSE THOSE BLESSINGS WITHOUT THEIR CONSENT."[1] The quotation appears at the point in the argument where the author asks whether the American people work for the government under [[natural-law|Natural Law]] or are instead bound as customers of a private credit issuer under Corporate Law.[1]

Vattel's formulation is treated as decisive: because consent is required, arrangements entered into without public disclosure — and, in the article's framing, by way of fraud — cannot lawfully bind the people.[1] See [[law-of-nations]] and [[throckmorton-v-united-states]].
