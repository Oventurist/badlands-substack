# Chen Zhi

Chen Zhi, also known by the alias "Vincent," is the founder and chairman of [[prince-holding-group|Prince Holding Group]], a multinational conglomerate based in [[cambodia|Cambodia]].[1] On October 14, 2025, an indictment was unsealed in federal court in Brooklyn charging him with wire fraud conspiracy and money laundering conspiracy for directing Prince Group's operation of forced-labor scam compounds across Cambodia.[1] At the time of the unsealing, the defendant was reported to be at large.[1]

## Allegations

According to the indictment and an accompanying civil forfeiture complaint, Chen Zhi has served as founder and chairman of Prince Group since approximately 2015, presiding over a conglomerate that operates dozens of business entities in more than 30 countries and that is ostensibly focused on real estate development, financial services, and consumer services.[1] Prosecutors allege that in secret, Chen and his top executives grew Prince Group into one of Asia's largest transnational criminal organizations, generating enormous profits from scam compounds that perpetrated fraudulent cryptocurrency investment schemes.[1]

The alleged scheme followed the pattern known as [[pig-butchering-scams|"pig butchering"]]: malicious actors contacted unwitting victims through messaging or social media applications, built relationships and trust over time, and convinced the victims to transfer cryptocurrency to specified accounts on false promises of investment returns, after which the funds were stolen and laundered.[1] Prosecutors allege that the workers executing the scams were themselves trafficking victims — hundreds of people trafficked into the compounds and forced to work, often under the threat of violence.[1]

## Forfeiture action

The [[us-attorneys-office-eastern-district-of-new-york|United States Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of New York]] and the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department's]] National Security Division filed a civil forfeiture complaint against approximately 127,271 [[bitcoin|bitcoin]], worth roughly $15 billion, described as proceeds and instrumentalities of Chen's fraud and money laundering schemes.[1] The coins had been stored in unhosted cryptocurrency wallets whose private keys the defendant possessed, and are now in the custody of the U.S. government; the Justice Department described the complaint as the largest forfeiture action in its history.[1]

Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] called the action "one of the most significant strikes ever against the global scourge of human trafficking and cyber-enabled financial fraud," crediting the work of [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] and the bureau.[1] United States Attorney Joseph Nocella said the defendant "directed one of the largest investment fraud operations in history."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] urged readers not to dismiss the case as a distant Cambodian matter, arguing that the structure described — a global corporate conglomerate ostensibly delivering professional services while operating offshore centers that defraud Americans — should be read as a "template" applicable elsewhere.[1] Contributor [[gmoney|GMONEY]] noted that the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve executive order bars the government from selling seized BTC except with Treasury approval, by court order, or to return funds to verified victims of crime.[1]
