# Kursk Nuclear Power Plant

The **Kursk nuclear power plant** is a Russian nuclear generating station in the Kursk Region, near the area penetrated by Ukrainian forces during the August 2024 [[kursk-incursion|Kursk incursion]].[1]

## Alleged targeting

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote in October 2024 that "DC/NATO/Israeli" forces had become desperate enough to begin bombing the plant, and recalled that during a Labor Day discussion with [[canncon|CannCon]] on Badlands Daily he had argued the intended objective of the Kursk invasion was to reach the plant and destroy it, in the hope that resulting fallout would render a vast region of Russia uninhabitable.[1] He circulated a map showing the extent of the Ukrainian incursion in Kursk alongside a satellite image depicting miles of trenches that Russian forces had dug to defend the facility, presenting the defensive works as corroboration that Moscow anticipated the plant to be the operation's true target.[1]

## SVR allegations of an Anglo-American sabotage plot

In October 2024 [[sergey-naryshkin|Sergey Naryshkin]], head of Russia's [[foreign-intelligence-service|Foreign Intelligence Service]] (SVR), alleged that British and American spies had helped Ukraine develop plans to blow up the plant, an act he said would have plunged Europe into another radiation nightmare.[2] Ukrainian troops crossed into the Kursk Region in August 2024 but were stopped short of the facility at Kurchatov.[2] Speaking at a meeting of intelligence and security principals of post-Soviet states in Astana, Naryshkin said that "according to intelligence obtained by the SVR, the planned terrorist attack included taking and mining the Kursk NPP," and that had it succeeded "Europe would have faced an environmental and humanitarian disaster comparable to Chernobyl."[2]

He further claimed that Western services, "primarily the British [[mi6|MI6]]," had systematically prepared Ukrainian sabotage and reconnaissance groups to stage provocations at several Russian nuclear plants, and were developing an operation to blow up the power lines connecting those plants to the national energy grid.[2] The comparison invoked the 1986 Chernobyl accident, in which a reactor exploded and caught fire, forcing the evacuation of Pripyat, the creation of a 30-kilometre exclusion zone on the present-day Ukraine–Belarus border, and radioactive fallout carried by wind as far as Scotland.[2] Badlands commentary treated the disclosure as confirmation of its thesis that terrorism as an industry is primarily sustained by UK and US intelligence agencies.[2]

## Alleged targeting during the 2024 Kursk incursion

During the August–September 2024 [[kursk-incursion|Ukrainian incursion into Kursk]], President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] said that NATO was trying to strike the plant at Kurskaya, which lies deep inside Russian territory.[3] Badlands commentary judged it unlikely that Ukrainian ground troops would penetrate that far, while noting they retained the capacity to strike the site with missiles and had apparently attempted to do so; Russian forces dug massive trench lines to defend the plant from the invading force.[3] The same commentary listed an attack on the plant as one of three strategic objectives it ascribed to [[nato|NATO]] planners, alongside diverting Russian troops from the eastern front and terrorising Russian civilians, and linked it to a May 2024 drone attack on the Russian-controlled Zaporizhia plant and to alleged threats against the families of Ukrainian workers there.[3]
