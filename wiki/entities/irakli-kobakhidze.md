# Irakli Kobakhidze

Irakli Kobakhidze is the Prime Minister of Georgia and a leading figure of the ruling [[georgian-dream|Georgian Dream]] party. He is best known internationally for his government's decision to suspend European Union accession talks, a move that triggered sustained street protests in the Georgian capital of Tbilisi.[1]

## Suspension of EU accession talks

Kobakhidze defended his government's suspension of EU accession negotiations by saying that his party refuses to allow the European Union to keep Georgia in a "constant state of blackmail and manipulation."[1] The European Union had granted Georgia formal candidate status in December 2023, but accession talks and associated financial aid were frozen shortly afterward following Tbilisi's passage of a "foreign influence" law that critics described as Russia-inspired.[1]

## Tbilisi protests

Following the disputed parliamentary election of 26 October 2024 — widely treated as a referendum on Georgia's potential EU membership — Georgian Dream retained control of parliament, and demonstrations in Tbilisi ran for at least eleven consecutive days with tens of thousands of participants and at least 300 arrests.[1] Police responded with water cannons, tear gas and batons, while journalists and protesters reported being pursued by masked gangs, producing numerous injuries.[1] Protesters on Rustaveli Avenue built barricades, set off fireworks, and displayed pictures of those they believed had been injured or detained by the authorities.[1] Opposition parties and President [[salome-zourabichvili|Salome Zourabichvili]] accused the ruling party of electoral fraud carried out with alleged Russian assistance.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media commentary placed Kobakhidze's position within a broader pattern of Western pressure on governments that adopt NGO funding-transparency laws. The brief recalled that Slovakian Prime Minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]] was shot five times in the chest in May 2024 after his government moved legislation requiring foreign NGOs to disclose their funding sources, legislation modelled on existing Russian law.[1] In the immediate aftermath of that assassination attempt, with comparable legislation pending in Georgia, Kobakhidze said that a member of the European Parliament had contacted him and warned him to be careful, invoking what had just happened to Fico — which Badlands characterized as a thinly veiled threat.[1] The brief argued that a party which had just won a national election for the second time in twelve months would have no need to beat up journalists, and that the Georgian public appeared to be broadly on Kobakhidze's side.[1]

## Cited in NGO-transparency commentary (2024)

Badlands commentary in July 2024 invoked Kobakhidze's treatment by Western officials as evidence of a double standard in coverage of non-governmental organizations.[2] Writing about the [[security-service-of-ukraine|SBU]]'s claim to have foiled a coup plot organized by "NGO's known for anti-Ukrainian actions," the piece noted that the same Western establishment had castigated and, it alleged, threatened the Georgian prime minister for passing foreign-agent legislation that would expose the funding of such NGOs to the public — legislation critics credited to Russian legislative inspiration.[2]

## Reported EU threat

Badlands coverage in August 2024 recounted that EU officials had threatened Kobakhidze "with the same fate of [Slovakian Prime Minister] [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]]," a warning delivered roughly a week after Fico was shot five times in the chest.[3] EU official [[oliver-varhelyi|Olivér Várhelyi]] subsequently said the comments had been "taken out of context," a claim [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] dismissed as implausible.[3] The episode was cited as evidence of Western pressure on [[georgia-country|Georgia]] as its government drifted away from the EU and [[nato|NATO]] orbit.[3]


## Foreign agents law and the alleged EU threat (May 2024)

In May 2024 Kobakhidze defended [[georgia-country|Georgia]]'s newly passed foreign agents law — the [[transparency-of-foreign-influence-act|Transparency of Foreign Influence Act]] — as necessary to curb external influence and to avoid repeating the fate of Ukraine, which he said the West had turned into a battlefront against Russia.[4] He likened the prevailing situation to "muddy water," in which foreign influence flows unhindered and those seeking to shape Georgian governance can easily "catch fish" by uniting opponents of state policy into revolutionary movements.[4] Without the law, he argued, Georgia risked becoming another Western proxy as Ukraine had before the 2014 Maidan coup in Kiev.[4]

In a Facebook post that week, Kobakhidze claimed an unnamed European commissioner had warned him by telephone that the West would take "a number of measures" against him if his government pressed ahead with the law, and, while listing those measures, said: "you see what happened to Fico, and you should be very careful."[4] The reference was to Slovak prime minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]], shot multiple times in Handlova on May 15, 2024.[4] Kobakhidze responded publicly that "both America and the European Union need deoligarchization," branding them the "Global War Party" and accusing them explicitly of attempting a Maidan-style coup against his government.[4]

## December 2024 presidential transition

Thousands of Georgians protested in Tbilisi as [[mikheil-kavelashvili|Mikheil Kavelashvili]], a former professional footballer allied with the ruling [[georgian-dream|Georgian Dream]] party, was inaugurated president in late December 2024, during a critical period following the government's suspension of Georgia's European Union accession application.[5] Georgian Dream had won October parliamentary elections in a victory mired in fraud allegations that sparked repeated street protests.[5] Outgoing president [[salome-zourabichvili|Salome Zourabichvili]] refused to step down, calling herself the "only legitimate president," but ultimately left the presidential palace while branding her successor illegitimate: "This building was a symbol only as long as a legitimate president was sitting here."[5] Kavelashvili was sworn in at a private ceremony down the street with Prime Minister [[irakli-kobakhidze|Irakli Kobakhidze]] in attendance.[5]

Badlands commentary described Georgia as the target of intense [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] color revolution operations over the preceding year, beginning after Kobakhidze's party pushed legislation requiring NGOs receiving more than 20% of their funding from foreign sources to disclose their donors — the same type of law associated with the shooting of Slovak Prime Minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]].[5] Georgia's strategic value was tied to the [[baku-tbilisi-ceyhan-pipeline|Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline]], which carries Azerbaijani oil to Turkey's Ceyhan terminal and onward to Israel, supplying a reported 40% of Israeli oil, and to its role as leverage against Russia in the Caucasus.[5]
