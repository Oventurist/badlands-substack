# Blackwater

**Blackwater** is a private military company founded by [[erik-prince|Erik Prince]].[1]

## Role in the Purple Revolution narrative

Badlands Media's [[purple-revolution|Purple Revolution]] analysis raises Blackwater in the context of the 2016 [[donald-trump|Trump]] transition, asking why the Trump team would seek counsel from a figure in the private military space rather than from the US Military, and answering with the suggestion that the military itself was too compromised.[1] Prince initially denied any role in the transition before acknowledging his presence at the Trump Tower meeting of August 2016.[1]

## Pardons and contracts

Trump pardoned four former Blackwater employees who had been convicted in connection with a 2007 incident in Iraq in which they opened fire and killed 17 people.[1] The article also observes that Blackwater's contracts with the US government declined sharply after Barack Obama took office, describing Obama as a CIA asset.[1] Prince's sister [[betsy-devos|Betsy DeVos]] was appointed Secretary of Education by Trump.[1]

## Presence in Saudi Arabia during the 2017 purge

Chapter 9 of the Badlands series [[q-for-dummies|Q for Dummies]] identifies Blackwater as the answer to Q's question, in drop #71 of November 4, 2017, about which U.S. operators were then present in [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]].[2] The chapter places the company inside the Kingdom while the [[saudi-purge|Saudi purge]] was under way and the country was on lockdown under martial law, and tells readers that Blackwater's relevance to the wider narrative "will become clear in the coming chapters."[2] No further detail on the company's mission or client is supplied in that installment.[2]

## The "special contractors" in Syria

The Badlands Media feature "Transparency and Prosecution" identifies Blackwater as the "special contractors" referenced in Q drops #1090 and #1091 as the force that shadowed Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] during his May 2013 trip into Syria.[3] The company provided McCain's security and escorted him into and out of the country despite the absence of U.S. diplomatic relations with Damascus at the time.[3]

Quoting a Vanity Fair profile, the article states that between 2001 and 2009 Blackwater gleaned more than $1.5 billion in government contracts, acting among other things as an overseas Praetorian guard for CIA and State Department officials, while its chief executive [[erik-prince|Erik Prince]] simultaneously operated as a CIA asset crafting, funding and executing covert operations.[3] Because Blackwater personnel shadowed McCain's every move, the article treats the firm — and Prince in particular — as holding definitive knowledge of the identities of everyone present at the Syria meeting, including the man it identifies as [[abu-bakr-al-baghdadi|Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]].[3]
