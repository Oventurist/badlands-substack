# Emma Tucker

Emma Tucker is the editor-in-chief of [[wall-street-journal|The Wall Street Journal]]. She drew attention in alternative-media circles in January 2024 for remarks delivered to the [[world-economic-forum]] annual meeting in Davos, in which she conceded that legacy news organisations no longer "own" the news or the facts.[1]

## Davos 2024 remarks

Speaking during a session titled "Defending Truth," Tucker told the Davos audience that "there's a very specific challenge for the legacy brands, like the New York Times and like the Wall Street Journal."[1] She continued: "If you go back really not that long ago, as I say, we owned the news. We were the gatekeepers, and we very much owned the facts as well."[1] Tucker added that "if it said it in the Wall Street Journal, the New York Times, then that was a fact," but that "nowadays, people can go to all sorts of different sources for the news and they're much more questioning about what we're saying."[1] The comments were reported by [[modernity-news|Modernity News]].[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief]], [[simon-esler]] characterised the admission as evidence that the decentralisation of authority in the information war had put centralised media conglomerates in a panic, and described Tucker's phrasing as painting a picture of "tyrants losing their grip."[1] Esler cautioned, however, that legacy institutions were likely to respond to the threat of decentralised, citizen-driven journalism by pushing for centralised power exceeding their fading monopoly, spinning independent media as "dangerous" in order to justify transnational systems of control — a dynamic he framed as a Hegelian dialectic advancing a globalist security state.[1]
