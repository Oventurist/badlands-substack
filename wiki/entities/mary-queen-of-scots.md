# Mary, Queen of Scots

**Mary, Queen of Scots** is the subject of the second historical case study in the Badlands Brief article "Kekistani Code Talkers," which uses her correspondence to illustrate the trust problems that accompany centralized power.[1]

## Imprisonment and cipher correspondence

In 1586 Mary was held prisoner by a Protestant loyal to Elizabeth I.[1] Her contact with the outside world was limited to correspondence encrypted by her cipher secretary and smuggled out in casks of ale.[1] Different ciphers, using unique combinations of symbols, letters, words, and nulls, were used for different recipients.[1]

## Interception

Every letter was intercepted by a Catholic double agent.[1] Letters between Mary and her allies in England and abroad were painstakingly deciphered and copied, then carefully re-sealed and forwarded to their intended recipients.[1] The article reproduces a portion of the ciphers she used.[1]

## Framing

The case is introduced with the observation that centralization brings trust issues, frequently demonstrated by politics and actions affecting one's ability to project and scale real-world physical power — the example given being a king commanding an army.[1] It is offered as an early instance of [[cryptography|cryptographic]] [[information-warfare|information warfare]].[1]
