# Populist Party

The Populist Party, formally known as the People's Party, is identified in the article as a key player in the late nineteenth-century movement for a federal income tax and monetary reform. According to the source, the party was formed by former slave owners in the aftermath of the Civil War.

The article states that during the late nineteenth century the Populist Party favored the establishment of an income tax at the federal level. It claims the Long Depression was "doing more damage to the rich and powerful" and that they needed something to permanently suppress citizens. The Populist Party's 1892 platform demanded a graduated income tax, a position the article says was mirrored by the Democratic Party led by William Jennings Bryan, who advocated for the income tax law passed in 1894 and proposed an income tax in the Democratic Party's 1908 platform.

The article makes the striking claim that in 1910, leaders of the Populist Party convinced [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson W. Aldrich]], then leader of the Republican Party, to transform the entire monetary system of the United States. This conviction allegedly led to the [[Jekyll Island meeting]], where the plan for the Federal Reserve was hatched. The source presents the Populist Party's role as ironic: a movement initially formed to advance citizen interests allegedly became complicit in a plan that the article claims ultimately enslaved the American people to a fiat monetary system.
