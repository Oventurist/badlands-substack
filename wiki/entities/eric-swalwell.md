# Eric Swalwell

Eric Swalwell is a Democratic member of the U.S. House of Representatives from California. In the Badlands corpus he is referenced almost exclusively in connection with the counterintelligence episode involving the suspected Chinese intelligence operative known as [[fang-fang|Fang Fang]], who cultivated relationships with Bay Area politicians including Swalwell before the connection was severed following an FBI warning.

The July 22, 2026 Badlands Brief invokes the affair as evidence that [[congress|Congress]] has long tolerated foreign-intelligence exposure among its members and staff. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cites "Swalwell and Fang Fang," alongside [[dianne-feinstein|Feinstein]]'s driver and other staffer scandals, in expressing astonishment that criminal background checks for House employees were still not required at the time [[kat-cammack|Kat Cammack]] pressed for a vote on H.Res. 500. In that framing, Swalwell functions less as a subject of analysis than as the standing example of an unresolved counterintelligence failure that the institution never corrected.

## 2026 file-release push

In March 2026, FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] moved to release a decade-old investigative file involving Swalwell, directing agents in the FBI's San Francisco field office to accelerate redaction of the documents in preparation for possible public disclosure. The file concerns Swalwell's past contacts with [[fang-fang|Fang Fang]], the woman the [[fbi|FBI]] investigated as a suspected Chinese intelligence operative. Current and former officials said the push raised internal concerns about departing from standard bureau practice and politicizing investigative records; the earlier examination produced no charges against Swalwell.

Swalwell responded sharply: "Most troubling about this is that we are now literally at war. We also face threats against the homeland… Kash Patel should be spending every moment trying to keep us safe, not scoring political points. A lot of people have bent the knee to this administration. But I will not, and neither will the people of California."

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] welcomed the disclosure effort, noting that Swalwell was not removed from the [[house-intelligence-committee|House Intelligence Committee]] until the two-year investigation concluded, and arguing that the exculpatory official account had been delivered by the Biden-era Justice Department and therefore merited fresh scrutiny. She framed the episode as one of many national-security "loose ends" now being reopened.
