# Legislative Assembly of Costa Rica

The Legislative Assembly is [[costa-rica|Costa Rica]]'s national legislature, cited in Badlands contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s "Illusions of Democracy" both as a structural safeguard against career politics and as the body that honoured [[jose-figueres-ferrer|José "Pepe" Figueres Ferrer]].[1]

## Structure and term limits

Quoting [[freedom-house|Freedom House]], the article records that elections for the 57-seat unicameral Legislative Assembly occur every four years, with deputies elected by proportional representation. Deputies "may not run for two consecutive terms, but may run again after skipping a term."[1] Lange treats this as the legislative counterpart to the presidential ban on consecutive terms: the same safeguard that prevents a president from consolidating power also prevents members of the legislature from making a career out of politics.[1]

## Historical role

The Assembly's predecessor body is also central to the origins of the modern republic in Lange's telling. In 1948 the legislature, controlled by the losing incumbent's political party, annulled the presidential election at his direction so he could remain in power — the act that triggered the [[costa-rican-civil-war|Costa Rican Civil War]].[1] After the war, the provisional junta headed by Figueres oversaw the election of a new constitutional assembly, which adopted a constitution embedding the safeguards that have since protected the country.[1]

## "Hero of Peace" declaration

Citing CulturaCR.net, the article reports that the legislative plenary approved on first debate bill 22,289, declaring former president José Figueres Ferrer **"Hero of peace."**[1] The project was approved just two months after the same deputies eliminated the title of traitor of the Homeland that had weighed on former president [[rafael-angel-calderon-fournier|Rafael Ángel Calderón Fournier]].[1] Only deputies José María Villalta of the Frente Amplio and Paola Vega of the PAC voted against.[1]
