# Worldcoin

Worldcoin is a cryptocurrency and biometric digital-identity venture co-founded by [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]] of [[openai|OpenAI]]. Its business model pairs the distribution of free crypto tokens — worth roughly $50 per participant — with the collection of iris biometrics, captured by a spherical scanning device the company markets as the "Orb."[1]

## Kenyan suspension

In early August 2023 the Kenyan Ministry of the Interior suspended Worldcoin and "any similar entities" from operating in the country, halting the token-for-eyeball campaign that had drawn long queues in Nairobi.[1] The Ministry stated that "relevant security, financial services and data protection agencies have commenced inquiries and investigations to establish the authenticity and legality of the aforesaid activities, the safety and protection of the data being harvested, and how the harvesters intend to use the data."[1] Kenyan Cabinet Secretary [[alfred-mutua|Alfred Mutua]] denounced the scheme, urging support for "the stoppage of Kenyans being used as guinea pigs and their data being harvested."[1]

## Commentary in the Badlands corpus

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the Worldcoin episode as a data-sovereignty story rather than a crypto story, arguing that "the digital revolution — the bringing online of every aspect of our existence — is about tracking," and that transactional data on humanity permits the intimate modelling and prediction of behavior. In her formulation, "data means control."[1] She linked the Kenyan rejection of Worldcoin to the broader African political ferment of that month, including the [[2023-niger-coup|2023 Niger coup]], casting both as instances of national populations pushing back against what she described as a globalist surveillance agenda.[1]
