# Worldcoin

Worldcoin is a cryptocurrency and biometric digital-identity venture co-founded by [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]] of [[openai|OpenAI]]. Its business model pairs the distribution of free crypto tokens — worth roughly $50 per participant — with the collection of iris biometrics, captured by a spherical scanning device the company markets as the "Orb."[1]

## Kenyan suspension

In early August 2023 the Kenyan Ministry of the Interior suspended Worldcoin and "any similar entities" from operating in the country, halting the token-for-eyeball campaign that had drawn long queues in Nairobi.[1] The Ministry stated that "relevant security, financial services and data protection agencies have commenced inquiries and investigations to establish the authenticity and legality of the aforesaid activities, the safety and protection of the data being harvested, and how the harvesters intend to use the data."[1] Kenyan Cabinet Secretary [[alfred-mutua|Alfred Mutua]] denounced the scheme, urging support for "the stoppage of Kenyans being used as guinea pigs and their data being harvested."[1]

## Commentary in the Badlands corpus

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the Worldcoin episode as a data-sovereignty story rather than a crypto story, arguing that "the digital revolution — the bringing online of every aspect of our existence — is about tracking," and that transactional data on humanity permits the intimate modelling and prediction of behavior. In her formulation, "data means control."[1] She linked the Kenyan rejection of Worldcoin to the broader African political ferment of that month, including the [[2023-niger-coup|2023 Niger coup]], casting both as instances of national populations pushing back against what she described as a globalist surveillance agenda.[1]

## Funding round and AI framing (May 2023)

One day before [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]] testified to Congress on artificial intelligence, the *Financial Times* reported that he was "close to securing around $100 million in funding for his plan to use iris-scanning technology to create a secure global cryptocurrency called Worldcoin," with three people familiar with the deal saying the team was in "advanced talks" to raise the funds within weeks.[2] The project was marketed as preparation for "the disruption A.I. is anticipated to cause to a number of industries," and as a means of distinguishing humans from bots by issuing a unique ID while providing a universal basic income to offset AI-driven job losses.[2]

Altman and his collaborators described Worldcoin as an "inclusive" global cryptocurrency available to anyone who verifies their "unique personhood" with the Orb.[2] Worldcoin's own documentation states that the Orb "checks that an individual is real and is unique or has not previously signed up for Worldcoin," doing so "by capturing and processing images of an individual and their unique iris pattern."[2]

## Critique in "Dark Equation"

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the AI-disruption rationale gave Altman a second motive for publicly warning about artificial intelligence: beyond advancing a regulatory licensing agenda that would weed out competitors to [[openai|OpenAI]], it reinforced the purported need for his own digital currency.[2] He characterized Worldcoin as "a repackaging of the same old [[great-reset|Great Reset]] / [[fourth-industrial-revolution|fourth industrial revolution]] malarky" emanating from Davos, and as a continuation of a push by the United Nations, the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] and a growing number of governments toward a global biometric identification system attached to a [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currency]].[2] DeLarme clarified that he was not arguing all CBDCs or cryptocurrencies are inherently harmful, only that the involvement of the [[bilderberg|Bilderberg Group]], Silicon Valley and the World Economic Forum is cause for concern: "It's not necessarily the tool that is the problem; it is allowing the same international cabal of ruthless failures to be in charge of how it's used."[2]
