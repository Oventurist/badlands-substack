# National Security Agency

The **National Security Agency (NSA)** is the U.S. signals-intelligence agency responsible for collecting and analyzing foreign communications intercepts.

## Criminal referral over intercept leak (2026)

The NSA issued a criminal referral concerning the disclosure of information drawn from one of its overseas intelligence intercepts, which in 2026 prompted an [[fbi|FBI]] investigation into possible leaks by Democrats or staff on the [[senate-intelligence-committee|Senate Intelligence Committee]].[1] The intercept in question captured a conversation between two [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] figures discussing Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]]'s 2017 visit to Syria, and surfaced in press coverage during her confirmation process.[1]

The agency concluded that the published material accurately reflected the intercept's contents but found that Gabbard had not in fact met Hezbollah leaders.[1] It further identified potential leakers among Democratic committee staff who had access to the intercept before publication.[1] The referral reportedly remained pending for months until FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] learned of it, after which the bureau's counterintelligence and criminal investigators expanded the inquiry to other suspected leaks and contacts with reporters.[1]

## Recall of the Venezuela report (2025)

Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] directed the NSA to recall a classified report on Venezuela even though agency staff affirmed that it was accurate and complied with all NSA policies, a step that raised major questions inside the intelligence community.[2] The report concerned Venezuela and special presidential envoy [[richard-grenell|Richard Grenell]], and included conversations Grenell had with [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] as well as information about Grenell collected by the agency.[2] An unclassified addendum issued in April instructed staff to "recall this report" at the DNI's direction, stating: "The report is accurate and in accordance with all NSA policy, directives, and guidance; however, the DNI directed NSA to recall the report. Please remove the original report from all manual and computer files."[2]

## Dismissals over explicit chatrooms (February 2025)

In February 2025, Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]] announced the imminent dismissal of more than 100 intelligence community employees, drawn from the NSA and the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]], for participating in sexually explicit and graphic discussions of sexual fetishes and transgenderism on an intragovernmental server during work hours.[3] Gabbard called the episode "an egregious violation of trust" with potential national security and blackmail implications, and stated that she had issued a directive that all those involved would be terminated and their security clearances revoked.[3] Badlands commentary observed that the story had been overshadowed by the Epstein files controversy and argued that, given the agency's surveillance capabilities as revealed by Edward Snowden and dramatized in [[enemy-of-the-state|Enemy of the State]], the presence of more than a hundred compromised employees was "the stuff of nightmares."[3]
