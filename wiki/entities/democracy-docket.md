# Democracy Docket

**Democracy Docket** is the election-law platform founded by Democratic attorney [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]], described in Badlands commentary simply as the outlet of "the founder of 'democracy docket.'"[1] The organization functions as a clearinghouse for Democratic-aligned election litigation and for public advocacy on how elections should be administered, and its founder's positions are treated by Badlands writers as a proxy for the legal strategy of the Democratic Party's election apparatus.[1]

## Positions described in Badlands commentary

Writing in the Badlands Brief in February 2026, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized Elias's legal proposal — advanced through his platform — as one in which "the Federal Legislative bans the Federal Executive from investigating the State Executive after the State Legislative bans all local authorities and their people from investigating or questioning the State."[1] In her reading, the effect would be to insulate state election administration from both federal investigation and local scrutiny, which she summarized sarcastically as removing "all the checks and balances from democracy to save democracy."[1]

She also reported that Elias had argued local election certification "should just be done by Microsoft Excel," a position she read as treating both local officials and federal authorities as threats to state election administration.[1] Badlands framed the whole posture as evidence that transparency in elections is viewed by the platform's founder as "an existential threat to his existence," which the author called "the most encouraging thing I've seen."[1]

## Context

The commentary appeared alongside coverage of the [[virginia-supreme-court|Virginia Supreme Court]]'s February 13, 2026 decision clearing the way for the [[virginia-redistricting-referendum-2026|April 21, 2026 redistricting referendum]] and broader Democratic anxiety about the [[2026-midterm-elections|2026 midterm elections]], with Badlands presenting Democracy Docket's activity as part of a defensive legal posture ahead of that cycle.[1]

## Scope in 2023

By April 2023 [[marc-elias|Marc Elias]] described Democracy Docket as encompassing 45 voting-rights cases across 18 states, a caseload he publicized directly and which had made him a frequent target of Republican criticism given his record of winning such suits.[2] The scale of the litigation was cited as one possible explanation for the [[democratic-national-committee|Democratic National Committee]]'s decision that month to end its representation arrangement with him.[2]
