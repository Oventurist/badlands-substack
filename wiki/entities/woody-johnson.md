# Woody Johnson

**Robert Wood "Woody" Johnson** is an American billionaire, a minority owner of the pharmaceutical giant [[johnson-and-johnson|Johnson & Johnson]] through the founding family, and a longtime friend of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] He is best known publicly for buying the National Football League's New York Jets for $635 million in January 2000.[1]

## Diplomatic service

In 2017 Johnson was appointed by President Trump as United States Ambassador to the United Kingdom.[1] Once his post was confirmed, his brother Christopher Johnson became a Jets co-owner and assumed daily operations of the franchise; when Woody Johnson's ambassadorial term ended in 2021 he returned to the Jets and took ownership duties back from his brother.[1]

## Role in the Badlands "supervax" thesis

Badlands Media contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]], in her December 29, 2022 essay "Why Trump Pushed the Vax," treats Johnson as the key "thread" explaining Trump's continued public endorsement of COVID-19 vaccination.[1] She notes that while Johnson was serving in London, the Trump administration signed contracts worth roughly $900 million with Johnson & Johnson — a $689,525,867 award to the company's Janssen Pharmaceuticals unit on December 19, 2019 from the Office of the Assistant Secretary for Preparedness and Response, plus a further $216,169,222 in February 2020, for a total of $905,645,089.[1] The original December 2019 award was framed as influenza-vaccine work under an executive order aimed at "modernizing influenza vaccines in the United States to promote national security and public health," which Gillespie reads as Trump effectively anticipating a pandemic and partnering the federal government with a company owned by his friend's family.[1]

Gillespie further points to Trump's introduction of Woody and Suzanne Johnson at the Save America rally in Greensburg, Pennsylvania on May 6, 2022, where the crowd booed the mention of Big Pharma.[1] She argues Trump expected the reaction and staged the introduction deliberately to showcase his relationship with Johnson & Johnson — a "comm" in the Badlands idiom.[1] Her speculative conclusion is that Trump and Johnson may have arranged for millions of vials distributed as vaccine to contain either saline or the U.S. Army's experimental "supervax."[1] These claims are the author's speculation and are presented as such, with the standard Badlands disclaimer that contributor views are their own.[1]
