# Josh Hawley

Josh Hawley is a Republican United States Senator identified in February 2026 reporting as the most aggressive Republican critic of artificial intelligence deregulation.[1]

## AI policy

Hawley has criticized AI as a threat to workers and social order, and condemned a [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration executive order seeking to preempt state-level AI regulation.[1] Politico grouped him with Florida Governor [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] and Utah Governor [[spencer-cox|Spencer Cox]] as Republicans breaking from the administration's deregulatory approach, and suggested AI could become a defining and potentially fracturing issue for the party heading into 2028.[1]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the [[media-protectorate|Media Protectorate]] was casting Hawley and DeSantis as defenders of workers, families, and traditional values in order to frame Trump as opposed to those values.[1] In that analysis the underlying concerns — automation, family values, human dignity amid rapid change — are legitimate, but the resistance risks slowing the accelerationist momentum Trump has pursued through executive orders on nuclear energy, AI, and supersonic flight.[1]

## Venezuela war powers vote (January 2026)

Hawley was among the senators who voted on January 14, 2026 against the [[venezuela-war-powers-resolution|Venezuela war powers resolution]] led by Sen. [[tim-kaine|Tim Kaine]], after having previously supported its advancement in the 52–47 procedural vote of January 8.[2] The measure, which would have required President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to obtain congressional approval before further military action against [[venezuela|Venezuela]], was blocked 51–50 with Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] casting the tie-breaking vote; Sen. [[todd-young|Todd Young]] made the same switch.[2]

## Perjury assessment of Jack Smith (July 2026)

After [[chuck-grassley|Sen. Chuck Grassley]]'s July 2026 disclosures that former special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s team had reviewed text messages from 44 members of [[us-congress|Congress]], Hawley said Smith's earlier testimony appeared to constitute perjury.[3] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] went further, calling the conduct treason rather than mere perjury.[3]

## PELOSI Act (January 2023)

In January 2023 Hawley introduced the [[pelosi-act|PELOSI Act]] — the Preventing Elected Leaders from Owning Securities and Investments Act — which would ban members of [[congress|Congress]] and their family members from holding or trading stocks.[4] The bill's acronym referenced former House Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]], whose husband [[paul-pelosi|Paul Pelosi]] had bought up to $5 million in semiconductor stock as the Senate moved to subsidize the industry.[4] Hawley, who had introduced similar legislation the previous year, said the practice must end.[4]

## MATURE Act (February 2023)

In February 2023 Hawley introduced the [[mature-act|Making Age-Verification Technology Uniform, Robust, and Effective (MATURE) Act]], which would bar children under 16 from creating social media accounts by requiring platforms to verify age using a user's full legal name, date of birth, and government-issued identification.[5] In an accompanying statement Hawley said children "suffer every day from the effects of social media," that Big Tech companies are at best neglecting children's health and monetizing their personal information and at worst "complicit in their exploitation and manipulation," and that it was "time to give parents the weapons they need to strike back."[5] The introduction was one of two bills he announced that week to protect children online and push back against Big Tech.[5]

## Congressional stock trading ban (July 2024)

In July 2024 Hawley led a bipartisan group of senators — with Democrats [[jon-ossoff|Jon Ossoff]], [[jeff-merkley|Jeff Merkley]] and [[gary-peters|Gary Peters]] — in announcing a negotiated deal to ban members of [[congress|Congress]] from trading stock.[6] "Congress should not be here to make a buck," he said at a Capitol Hill press conference. "There is no reason why members of Congress ought to be profiting off of the information that only they get."[6]

The amendment to an existing stock trading ban bill would immediately forbid members of Congress, the president and the vice president from purchasing stocks and other covered investments, and give lawmakers 90 days to sell existing holdings.[6] It was the first such measure to receive formal committee consideration, scheduled before the Homeland Security & Governmental Affairs Committee on July 24, 2024.[6]

## Ukraine classified briefing (2023)

Hawley, known for his efforts to force the Biden administration to declassify information related to COVID-19's possible laboratory origins at Wuhan, emerged from a classified briefing on Ukraine in September 2023 and expressed shock at what he had heard.[7] "If there is some path to victory in Ukraine, I didn't hear it today. I also heard that there's going to be no end to the funding requests," he said.[7] He indicated Americans would be asked to spend hundreds of billions more dollars in the region under an indefinite blank check justified by the protection of "US standing" on the world stage, at a time when [[volodymyr-zelensky|Zelensky]] had asked [[joe-biden|Biden]] and Congress for another $24 billion, warning Ukraine would lose the war without it.[7] Hawley further said the public was being lied to about the war footing and that Ukraine was definitely "not winning" — a claim relayed alongside polling showing 55% of the American public opposed to more aid.[7]

## Secret Service whistleblower report (September 2024)

On September 16, 2024, Hawley released a whistleblower report detailing security failures by the [[us-secret-service|U.S. Secret Service]] and other federal agencies during the July 13, 2024 assassination attempt against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at a rally in Butler, Pennsylvania.[8] Based on information from multiple whistleblowers, the report described a pattern of incompetence, mismanagement and inadequate preparation by the agencies charged with protecting one of the most high-profile figures in American politics, and characterized the episode as a near-catastrophic breach of presidential security caused by systemic failures, poor decision-making and inadequate resource allocation.[8] Among its most critical allegations were that the Secret Service declined to conduct a standard evaluation of the rally site, that the Counter Surveillance Division was absent from the event, and that the agency turned down multiple offers from local law enforcement to deploy drone surveillance — a decision made more consequential by the fact that the would-be assassin used a drone to survey the site hours before the attack.[8] The findings prompted calls for urgent oversight and reform of the agency.[8]
