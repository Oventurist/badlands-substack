# Gulf Cooperation Council

The Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) is the regional bloc of Arab Gulf monarchies whose members coordinate on economic and collective-security matters.[1] It appears in Badlands coverage of the 2026 U.S.–[[iran|Iran]] crisis as an institution whose perceived failure to protect a member state helped trigger a major realignment in global energy politics.[1]

## Role in the 2026 UAE withdrawal from OPEC

When the [[united-arab-emirates|United Arab Emirates]] announced on April 28, 2026 that it was leaving [[opec|OPEC]] and OPEC+, Emirati Energy Minister [[suhail-al-mazrouei|Suhail Mohamed al-Mazrouei]] explicitly cited frustrations over insufficient Gulf Cooperation Council support against Iranian attacks and threats during the conflict.[1] The grievance placed the Council at the center of the reasoning for a decision described as a blow to OPEC unity amid the historic energy shock produced by disruptions at the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]].[1]

The episode illustrated a wider theme in the coverage: that the wartime pressure on Gulf shipping and production was straining the collective institutions of the region, with individual states increasingly setting policy unilaterally rather than through bloc consensus.[1]

## First EU–GCC summit, October 2024

On Wednesday, October 16, 2024, the six GCC states met the [[european-union|European Union]] in Brussels for the first-ever summit between the two blocs.[2] Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] was the highest-profile Gulf attendee, arriving on the red carpet in conversation with French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] and showing little sign of his earlier pariah status after the 2018 assassination of [[jamal-khashoggi|Jamal Khashoggi]].[2] Both sides used the meeting to press each other over the wars in [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and the Middle East.[2] Badlands commentary argued that the substantive business of such summits takes place in the bilateral meetings on the sidelines, noting MBS's meetings with Macron and Spanish Prime Minister [[pedro-sanchez|Pedro Sánchez]] days after both called for an arms embargo on [[israel|Israel]].[2]

## Russia–GCC Strategic Dialogue in Sochi (September 2025)

On September 11, 2025, Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] addressed a Russia–Gulf Cooperation Council Strategic Dialogue session in Sochi, using the meeting to condemn [[israel|Israel]]'s missile and bomb attacks on Doha two days earlier.[3] Lavrov told the Council that the session was taking place "against the backdrop of a sharp increase in military-political tensions in the Middle East" and characterised the [[2025-israeli-strike-on-doha|strike on Doha]] as "a gross violation of international law, primarily the UN Charter, an encroachment on the sovereignty and territorial integrity of an independent state."[3] He stressed [[qatar|Qatar]]'s role as a key mediator in indirect Israel–[[hamas|Hamas]] ceasefire negotiations, calling it "particularly cynical" that a mediating state had been attacked.[3] Badlands commentary framed the Sochi session as evidence that Russia was stepping in to confront [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and asked what Moscow would do to help Gulf leaders — including Qatari Emir Tamim, Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], and UAE President Mohammed bin Zayed — navigate the crisis.[3]

## September 2024 Riyadh ministerial meetings

The GCC — comprising [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], Bahrain, [[kuwait|Kuwait]], Oman, Qatar and the United Arab Emirates — held its annual regional meeting in Riyadh on September 9, 2024.[4] Saudi Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] scheduled stacked Joint Ministerial Meetings of Strategic Dialogue with both [[russia|Russia]] and [[india|India]], held in immediate sequence; the Russian session was the seventh GCC–Russia Joint Ministerial Meeting of Strategic Dialogue and was attended by Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]], while India sent its External Affairs Minister.[4] Kuwaiti Crown Prince [[mishal-al-sabah|Mishal Al-Sabah]] was among those seated with Faisal bin Farhan.[4]

Badlands commentary treated the sequencing as evidence of a broader realignment, in which "the Christian nation of Russia and the Hindu nation of India are in a deep alliance with the Muslims of both Arabia and Persia," and of a healing Middle East in which Deep State efforts to turn Sunni against Shia would fail.[4] During the same gathering, Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] called for an emergency meeting of all Islamic world leaders, after which Faisal bin Farhan flew directly to [[egypt|Egypt]].[4]
