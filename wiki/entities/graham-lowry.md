# Graham Lowry

Graham Lowry was a historian, described by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as the author of the magnum opus *How the Nation Was Won: Volume 1 (1630–1757)*.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

Ehret credits Lowry with brilliantly demonstrating that the networks led by [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] had been planning out the conditions for declaring independence for decades before the American Revolution, rather than reacting to events of the 1770s.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Lowry's book also treats at length the work of [[william-penn|William Penn]] in developing the Pennsylvania Colony along lines set forth by John Winthrop Jr and John Winthrop, rather than as a vehicle for imperial wealth extraction.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## On Franklin and the Hell-Fire Club

Lowry is quoted extensively in the Badlands essay "In Defense of Benjamin Franklin," which credits him with a rigorous proof of Franklin's role as a young counter-intelligence agent of [[cotton-mather|Cotton Mather]]'s republican resistance, laid out in his essay "Who Was the Real Benjamin Franklin" and his 1987 masterpiece *How the Nation Was Won vol 1*.[2] Lowry wrote that in 1721, at the age of fifteen, [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] joined the battle against these forces in the middle of one of the most intensive campaigns to destroy the Mathers that their opponents had ever mounted, with his half-brother [[james-franklin|James Franklin]] and the bookseller [[john-checkley|John Checkley]] on the enemy side.[2]

Lowry also argued that following George I's accession in 1714 the nature of the beast had changed culturally and thus politically, as satanic cults proliferated among Britain's elite — the most notorious being the branches of the [[hellfire-club|Hell-Fire Club]], modelled after [[bernard-mandeville|Bernard Mandeville]]'s doctrine that evil, vice and corruption were the ideal means of the state's controlling its servants.[2] He described the role of the clubs in the 1720 [[south-sea-bubble|South Sea Bubble]] year, the 1721 King's Order-in-Council banning them in public form, and the promulgation of the satanic notion that there is no distinction between good and evil by George I's chief minister Robert Walpole.[2] Lowry concluded that when Franklin arrived in London, "a most vital intelligence task would have been to dissect this new form of the beast."[2]
