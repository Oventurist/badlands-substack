# Tartus Naval Base

Tartus is the site of a Russian logistics support center on Syria's Mediterranean coast, operated by Moscow together with the [[khmeimim-air-base|Khmeimim Air Base]].[1] Under a 2017 agreement between Russia and Syria, Russian troops were to be stationed at both facilities for 49 years.[1]

The collapse of the Assad government placed the future of Tartus in doubt. After militant groups led by [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] took Damascus and forced [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] to resign, the fate of the Russian bases became an open question.[1] Syria's de facto leader [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed Hussein al-Sharaa]] indicated his government did not want Russia "to exit Syria in a way that would not be befitting for its long-standing relations" with the country.[1]

Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] said the bases rest on valid interstate agreements, while acknowledging that Assad's overthrow had brought adjustments to Russia's military arrangements; he noted that no formal requests to revise the agreements had been made, and that the transition period declared until March 1, 2025, under the interim Syrian government imposed limitations on its policy-making.[1] Badlands analysis framed the coastal bases as the anchor of Russia's logistics network for operations in Africa, and predicted Moscow would retain them.[1]

## Reported evacuation of naval assets, December 2024

As the Syrian civil war turned against the Assad government in late 2024, the Russian base at Tartus appeared under imminent threat and Moscow began taking precautions.[2] Russia at that point had five naval ships and a submarine based at Tartus: two Gorshkov-class frigates, one Grigorovich-class frigate, two auxiliaries and an Improved-Kilo-class submarine.[2] The auxiliary Yelnya, a Project 160 Altay-class oiler important to sustaining Russia's Mediterranean force, was observed departing Tartus on the morning of December 2, 2024, with information suggesting some or all of the other vessels also left; naval analyst Droxford Maritime noted "a realistic possibility departure is related to the worsening situation in Syria."[2]

Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] disputed the reporting, saying Russian ships had not left Tartus after the jihadist offensive and that rumors could stem from incorrect assumptions about ongoing exercises: "Military exercises are being conducted in the Mediterranean Sea. Perhaps the satellite images were mistaken for something else."[2] He spoke on the Sunday after meeting the Iranian and Turkish foreign ministers in Qatar, the three having convened in Doha to discuss the rebel advance.[2] Badlands commentary read Lavrov's restraint as deliberate power projection in the contest between the globalist hegemony and the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], and framed an eventual Russian drawdown in Syria as a scripted parallel to the 2021 Kabul withdrawal rather than a defeat.[2]
