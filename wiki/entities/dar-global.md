# Dar Global

**Dar Global** is a luxury international real estate developer and the international arm of the Saudi mega-developer [[dar-al-arkan|Dar Al Arkan]].[1] Listed on the London Stock Exchange, the company had approximately US$5.9 billion worth of projects under development across six countries as of mid-2024 — the United Arab Emirates, Oman, Qatar, the United Kingdom, Spain, and Bosnia.[1] Its chief executive officer is [[ziad-el-chaar|Ziad El Chaar]].[1]

## Partnership with the Trump Organization

Dar Global's relationship with [[trump-organization|The Trump Organization]] originated in a licensing and management deal signed with Dar Al Arkan in 2022, first announced in November of that year.[1] The partnership's flagship product is [[trump-international-oman|Trump International Oman]], a $500 million development within the AIDA project near Muscat, described as one of the largest premium mixed-use real estate projects in the world.[1]

On July 1, 2024, Dar Global announced a second collaboration with the Trump Organization: the future launch of [[trump-tower-jeddah|Trump Tower Jeddah]] in Saudi Arabia, the company's debut project in Jeddah and a move targeting the luxury Saudi market and international investors.[1] El Chaar said the agreement would "leverage the strengths of both organizations to attract more international investors and vacationers" and underscored a commitment to "elevating the standards of luxury living in the region."[1]

## United States expansion

In late June 2024 it was reported that Dar Global — described in some coverage as Dubai-based — would expand into the United States for the first time, investing $300 million in residential projects in New York, Miami, and Los Angeles.[1] El Chaar said the firm hoped to announce its first U.S. development before year-end and had been meeting with top American developers, but declined to identify potential partners.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] speculated that the unnamed partner was likely the Trump Organization, while suggesting that Gulf principals would deliberately keep their dealings with [[eric-trump|Eric Trump]], [[donald-trump-jr|Donald Trump Jr.]], and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] discreet out of deference to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s presidential campaign.[1]

## Criticism

Critical coverage cited in the brief argued that the Trump Organization continued to profit from foreign companies while Trump mixed business interests with his political agenda, noting that the Oman licensing and management arrangement would generate significant brand-licensing fees plus management fees for a hotel, golf course, and luxury villas to be built over the following decade.[1] Trump's 2023 financial disclosure covering 2022 reported $5.35 million in licensing fees earned from the Oman golf project.[1]
