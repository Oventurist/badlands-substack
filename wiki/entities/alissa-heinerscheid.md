# Alissa Heinerscheid

Alissa Heinerscheid was the vice president of marketing for [[bud-light|Bud Light]], the [[anheuser-busch-inbev|Anheuser-Busch InBev]] brand at the center of a major consumer backlash in the spring of 2023.[1]

## Bud Light partnership and leave of absence

Heinerscheid oversaw the marketing effort that sought to reposition Bud Light through a promotional partnership with transgender TikTok influencer [[dylan-mulvaney|Dylan Mulvaney]], a move ZeroHedge characterized as an attempt to make the brand the "queen" of woke beers.[1] Three weeks after the campaign began, Ad Age reported on Friday, April 21, 2023 that Heinerscheid had taken a leave of absence and that Anheuser-Busch InBev had abruptly changed the brand's marketing leadership.[1]

She was replaced by [[todd-allen|Todd Allen]], who had recently served as global marketing vice president for [[budweiser|Budweiser]].[1] A company spokesperson said that, in order to prevent a market disaster similar to the one sparked by Heinerscheid, top-level executives would closely monitor Bud Light's marketing activities, adding that "these steps will help us maintain focus on the things we do best: brewing great beer for all consumers, while always making a positive impact in our communities and on our country."[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that the controversy had lasted well over a week and a half in the national news cycle, drawing coverage from far more outlets than those usually engaged in culture-war disputes, and that the legacy financial press was grudgingly conceding the company might have made a catastrophic marketing blunder with consequences lasting several quarters or more.[1]

## Departure confirmed (June 2023)

On June 27, 2023, a source inside [[anheuser-busch|Anheuser-Busch]] confirmed in text messages obtained by [[daily-caller|The Daily Caller]] that Heinerscheid and Group Vice President for Marketing [[daniel-blake|Daniel Blake]] were "gone gone" and no longer worked for the brand.[2] The source said the company had described the exits as a leave of absence rather than a firing because using the word "fire" would open the door to litigation, and added that "the wholesalers would have had an absolute HAY DAY with leadership if they didn't remove her."[2] Wholesalers were told in person by leadership that both executives were gone for good, and their direct reports had already been reassigned.[2]

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] wrote that it was unfortunate Heinerscheid lost her job but assumed that, as a Harvard graduate who had worked with American mega-corporations including Johnson & Johnson and General Mills, another woke S&P 500 company would employ her.[2] He questioned the point of the firings, coming immediately after Anheuser-Busch doubled down by sponsoring the Toronto Pride Parade, and argued that even if the [[dylan-mulvaney|Dylan Mulvaney]] campaign was her idea, someone higher up had to approve it, so the removals changed nothing.[2] He urged readers to keep the "little victory" in mind when buying from [[esg|ESG]]/DEI-centric companies, suggesting the [[2023-bud-light-boycott|Bud Light boycott]] might mark the start of a consumer revolution.[2]
