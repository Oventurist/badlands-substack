# Daniel Hagari

Daniel Hagari is a rear admiral of the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] who served as the military's chief spokesman during the war that followed the [[october-7-attacks|October 7, 2023 Hamas attack]].[1]

## Wartime statements

As heavy Israeli bombardment of the Gaza Strip entered its sixth day, Hagari said the IDF was degrading [[hamas|Hamas]]'s ability to govern, stating: "In certain areas, the organization can no longer rule and we will continue until this is the case throughout."[1]

He also announced that Israel was specifically targeting those who filmed and broadcast the invasion of southern Israel, saying the military had killed Mustafa Shahin, a Hamas operative identified from footage circulating online.[1] That claim drew scrutiny from commentators who asked how an intelligence apparatus capable of identifying individual operatives and their hideouts in the middle of a war had failed to foresee the October 7 incursion at all.[1]

Hagari's operational messaging ran in parallel with the rare admission of failure issued by IDF Chief of Staff [[herzl-halevi|Herzl Halevi]].[1]

## Statements on the January 2024 Gaza drawdown

As spokesman for the Israeli military, Rear Admiral Hagari addressed the January 1, 2024 announcement that [[israel|Israel]] would begin withdrawing several thousand troops from [[gaza|Gaza]].[2] He emphasized that demobilizing some soldiers did not suggest any compromise of Israel's intention to continue fighting until it destroys [[hamas|Hamas]], and that fighting across Gaza remained intense.[2] Having previously said he expected "warfare throughout this year," Hagari indicated that some troops would be called back to service during 2024.[2]

## Confirmation of the February 2024 Ghaziyeh strikes

On February 19, 2024, Hagari confirmed in a statement that the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] had carried out large airstrikes on a town just south of Sidon, in the Lebanese region of Ghaziyeh — the first Israeli strikes near a major city deep inside Lebanon, some 45 to 60 kilometers from the border.[3] He said the strikes targeted [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] weapons depots and also served as a response to an explosive-laden attack that had struck northern Israel earlier that day.[3] The strikes marked a significant widening of a conflict that had previously been confined to the immediate border area since October 2023.[3]
