# John Checkley

John Checkley was a Boston bookseller and Anglican polemicist described by historian [[graham-lowry|Graham Lowry]], as quoted in the Badlands Media essay "In Defense of Benjamin Franklin," as the figure at the centre of the Anglican operation against the Mathers in early eighteenth-century Boston.[1]

Born in Boston in 1680, Checkley was subsequently trained in England at what the essay calls the Anglican stronghold of Oxford.[1] After an "oddly-accounted-for decade in Europe 'collecting art,'" he surfaced in Boston again in 1710 — the same year [[cotton-mather|Cotton Mather]] launched his broadened republican organising effort with *Essays to Do Good*.[1] Setting himself up as a bookseller, Checkley became a major propagandist against the Mathers' congregational doctrines and the leading lay advocate for the Church of England in Boston, working in a milieu that also included the printer [[james-franklin|James Franklin]] and the radical Whigs of Elisha Cooke, Jr.[1]
