# Chase Oliver

Chase Oliver is an American political activist who was nominated as the presidential candidate of the [[libertarian-party|Libertarian Party]] in late May 2024, becoming the standard-bearer of what the Washington Post described as the third-largest political party in the United States.[1] Prior to the nomination he had been a candidate in the 2022 Georgia Senate election.[1]

## 2024 nomination

Oliver secured the nomination late on Sunday, May 26, 2024, after seven rounds of voting over what reporters characterized as a long and contentious day in which factions of the party feuded over both the presidential nomination and internal party leadership positions.[1] Fewer than 900 delegates participated in the contest.[1] Once the last standing contender was eliminated, Oliver prevailed with 60 percent of the vote against the ballot option "none of the above."[1] In his acceptance speech he pledged to unify the fractured party, telling delegates, "I'm extending my hand. Take it and be a part of liberty."[1]

Oliver was backed by the Classic Liberal Caucus, a left-leaning faction within the party, and his candidacy functioned in part as a protest vote against the [[mises-caucus|Mises Caucus]], the right-wing bloc that had taken over party governance in 2022.[1] His final opponent was [[michael-rectenwald|Michael Rectenwald]], the Mises Caucus-endorsed former New York University professor who had led for most of the balloting before losing the head-to-head round.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the nomination as evidence of the party's irrelevance, arguing that Oliver was "worse than Gary Johnson" and that with his selection "the Libertarians look exactly like the so-called Left."[1] The commentary predicted Oliver's nomination would have "zero impact" on support for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] while leaving [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] facing two challengers heading into the November 5 vote.[1] The same piece characterized the party under Oliver as remaining "the party of open borders and societal licentiousness," regardless of the claims of Mises-aligned reformers.[1]
