# Felix Rodriguez

Felix Rodriguez was a Cuban exile recruited and trained by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and a member of the covert exile force [[brigade-2506]], which participated in the [[bay-of-pigs|Bay of Pigs]] invasion and later covert operations.[1] Joe Lange classes him among the network's "untouchables" — operatives shielded from prosecution regardless of exposure.[1]

The Washington Post reported in June 1987 that a money launderer for the major Colombian cocaine cartel had told Congress in secret testimony that he funneled nearly $10 million to the Nicaraguan Contras through Rodriguez.[1] That launderer, [[ramon-milian-rodriguez]], testified that the money was delivered in several installments after his own 1983 arrest, and that he passed the funds in Central America to couriers selected by Felix Rodriguez.[1] Lange treats this as evidence that the [[medellin-cartel]] was a CIA-connected cartel in every respect.[1]

Rodriguez served in the Miami-based brigade commanded by [[manuel-artime]], within the CIA station run under [[jmwave]] by [[ted-shackley]].[1]

## Early life and CIA career

Felix Ismael Rodríguez Mendigutía was born on 31 May 1941, a Cuban of Spanish Basque ancestry who, after the Cuban Revolution, went into exile with his family in the United States.[2] In September 1960 he joined a group of Cuban exiles in Guatemala, supported by the CIA, for military training — the force known as [[brigade-2506]].[2] He became a U.S. citizen in 1969 and soon enlisted in the United States Army, using the pseudonym Máximo Gómez during his CIA career.[2] He was awarded the Intelligence Star for Valor by the CIA — a decoration given for voluntary acts of courage under hazardous conditions and one of the rarest valor awards in the US government, held by only a few dozen people — and nine Crosses for Gallantry by the South Vietnamese government.[2] He is also known for his role in the interrogation and execution of the Marxist guerrilla Che Guevara.[2]

In the Vietnam War Rodríguez flew over 300 helicopter missions and was shot down five times.[2] In 1971 he trained [[provincial-reconnaissance-units|Provincial Reconnaissance Units]], the CIA-sponsored units that worked for the [[phoenix-program]] and which Joe Lange describes as death squads.[2] The Walsh Report states that during the Vietnam War [[donald-gregg|Donald Gregg]] supervised Rodriguez and that they kept in contact after the war; Rodriguez also reported to [[ted-shackley|Ted Shackley]] during the Phoenix Program.[2] He was in Laos during the [[secret-war-in-laos|secret war]] at the same time as Shackley, flying the missions for which Lange says the Intelligence Star was awarded.[2]

## Iran-Contra

During the most violent years of the war in [[nicaragua]], Rodriguez — described by Rolling Stone as a retired CIA agent of many talents and pseudonyms, a characterization Lange rejects on the grounds that "nobody ever retires from the CIA" — was the logistics officer for airlifts of weapons and supplies from the [[ilopango-air-base|Ilopango]] air base in El Salvador to the jungle hide-outs of the contras.[2] On October 5, 1986 one of his cargo planes, a [[southern-air-transport|Southern Air Transport]] C-123K loaded with 10,000 pounds of ammunition, failed to return from a scheduled drop, and Rodriguez made a series of frantic phone calls to Washington that evening.[2]

What Rolling Stone called unusual was that Rodriguez notified neither the Defense Department nor the CIA, but instead tried to reach Gregg, the national-security adviser to Vice President [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]]; failing that, he telephoned Gregg's deputy, army colonel Samuel Watson.[2] The following morning, learning that Sandinista artillerymen had downed the plane, killing the pilot and copilot and leaving [[eugene-hasenfus|Eugene Hasenfus]] captured, Rodriguez again called the Vice President's office and the search-and-rescue mission was called off.[2] Lange treats these calls as proof that Bush was in the loop.[2]

The Walsh Report records that Gregg introduced Rodriguez to Bush in January 1985 and that Rodriguez met the Vice President again in Washington in May 1986 and briefly in Miami on May 20, 1986 — at least three meetings during the Iran-Contra operation.[2] Lange disputes the word "introduced," arguing Bush had known Rodriguez for a very long time and that Rodriguez was heavily involved in [[shadow-government]] operations.[2] Rodriguez was also one of the leaders of Operation 40, the Cuban-American force run out of the Miami station during the Bay of Pigs period.[2]
