# Yaroslav Hunka

Yaroslav Hunka is a Ukrainian-born Canadian veteran of the Second World War who became the center of an international controversy in September 2023 after he was honored in Canada's House of Commons during a visit by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky.[1]

## The Ottawa tribute

Immediately following Zelensky's address to Canadian lawmakers on Friday, September 22, 2023, Speaker [[anthony-rota|Anthony Rota]] drew the chamber's attention to Hunka, then 98 years old, and introduced him as a war hero who had fought for the "First Ukrainian Division."[1] Members of Parliament, along with Prime Minister [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] and the visiting Ukrainian delegation, rose in a standing ovation.[1] The unit Rota referenced was subsequently identified as a Nazi military formation, and the [[first-ukrainian-division|First Ukrainian Division]] had operated as a Waffen-SS unit accused of war crimes.[1]

Rota apologized on Sunday, September 24, stating that he had "subsequently become aware of more information which causes me to regret my decision" to recognize the individual, and noting that neither his fellow parliamentarians nor the Ukrainian delegation had known of his plan in advance.[1] Rota also noted that Hunka was a constituent from his own riding.[1]

## Attempt to expunge the record

Following the standing ovations given to Hunka by the Canadian [[house-of-commons-of-canada|House of Commons]] — with Speaker [[anthony-rota|Anthony Rota]] and Prime Minister [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] present, during the visit of a guest party accompanying [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] — members of Parliament sought to erase all official records of the incident, including video records and the original text of Rota's remarks.[2] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] reported that the effort was ultimately blocked by House conservatives, and described it as a Stalinist attempt to memory-hole inconvenient history rather than confront the question of Nazi influences in Ukraine.[2] Hunka's wartime service was with the [[14th-waffen-grenadier-division-of-the-ss|14th Waffen Grenadier Division of the SS]], described by the Friends of the [[simon-wiesenthal-center|Simon Wiesenthal Center]] as a unit whose crimes against humanity during the Holocaust are well documented.[2] Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued the affair exposed a catastrophic vetting failure by Canadian officials and staffers who are meant to serve as trustees of the public.[2]
