# United States Senate

The United States Senate is the upper chamber of the U.S. Congress. In May 2024 it voted 60-38 to strike down the [[securities-and-exchange-commission|Securities and Exchange Commission]]'s [[staff-accounting-bulletin-121|Staff Accounting Bulletin 121]], the rule governing accounting for cryptocurrency custody.[1]

The Senate action followed earlier passage of the same resolution by the [[us-house-of-representatives|House of Representatives]], meaning the full Congress had finalized a bicameral rebuke of the agency's creation of the rule.[1] The measure was forwarded to the desk of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], where the [[white-house|White House]] had already indicated it would be vetoed.[1]

Badlands commentary treated the vote as a signal event in the "Info War," notable for unifying the crypto space and the banking sector in opposition to the Biden presidency.[1]

## Rival funding measure (November 2023)

During the November 2023 government funding standoff, House Republicans told reporters they feared being forced to back a rival funding measure originating in the Democratic-led Senate, a concern that shaped their internal debate over how to structure a stopgap [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolution]] before funding expired on November 17.[2] Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] was expected to unveil a House alternative within days to pre-empt the Senate's approach.[2]

## July 2025: Powell testimony and passage of the Big, Beautiful Bill

[[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Chair [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]]'s Senate testimony about the central bank's planned headquarters renovations became the basis of [[federal-housing-finance-agency|FHFA]] Director [[bill-pulte|Bill Pulte]]'s July 2025 demand that [[congress|Congress]] investigate him, Pulte calling the testimony "deceptive" and grounds for removal "for cause."[3]

On Tuesday, July 1, 2025, the Senate narrowly passed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s domestic policy package, the [[one-big-beautiful-bill-act|One Big Beautiful Bill]], sending it to the [[us-house-of-representatives|House]] ahead of a self-imposed July 4 deadline.[3] Representative [[chip-roy|Chip Roy]] said the [[medicaid|Medicaid]] cuts in the Senate-passed version were "a bit better" than he had expected, while adding that he still had concerns.[3]

## Bipartisan delegation to Israel (October 2023)

A bipartisan Senate delegation of five Republicans and five Democrats, led by South Carolina Republican [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]], travelled to Israel in October 2023, having originally planned the trip to promote Israeli-Saudi normalization.[4] At a Tel Aviv press conference the senators declared that destroying [[hamas|Hamas]] was "non-negotiable," threatened a war front on [[iran|Iran]]'s borders should Tehran broaden the conflict, and articulated a post-war vision in which Israel thrives and the Palestinian people have a better life.[4] The group met Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] and urged him to fund Gazan civilians until Hamas was destroyed, and also met families of hostages taken on October 7.[4]

Badlands criticized the mission as senators "doing everything they can to antagonize Iran" and questioned the expense of sending legislators abroad "to advertise for war" while Americans suffered economically.[4]

## Delays over Trump nominees, 2025

In early February 2025 President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] accused Democrats of "purposefully delaying virtually all of my Nominees," writing on Truth Social that no matter how well qualified a nominee might be, senators were "taking maximum time for approval — and laughing about it."[5] Just eight of Trump's more than 20 nominees had been confirmed by the Senate at that point, with many undergoing intense questioning as Democrats highlighted their concerns.[5] Trump argued the delays came at the country's expense and safety, said that "if George Washington and Abraham Lincoln were nominated for a position, the Democrats would take it out to the last moment before having to approve," and attributed the obstruction to Democrats' inability to accept their loss in the previous November's election.[5] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] used the episode to question the claim that nominees were deliberately telling mainstream falsehoods in committee testimony on Trump's behalf.[5]
