# Oleksandr Syrskyi

**Oleksandr Syrskyi** is a Ukrainian general who, since 2019, served as Commander of the Ukrainian Land Forces before being elevated to the top post in the Ukrainian armed forces.[1]

## Appointment as commander in chief

On Thursday, February 8, 2024, Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] announced the dismissal of General [[valerii-zaluzhnyi|Valerii Zaluzhnyi]] and named Syrskyi as his replacement, the largest military shakeup since the beginning of Russia's full-scale invasion nearly two years earlier.[1] The change followed the failure of Ukraine's much-publicized counteroffensive, a renewed Russian onslaught, and mounting manpower and ammunition shortages, with additional United States aid stalled in [[us-congress|Congress]].[1]

Zelensky said in a Telegram post shortly before the formal announcement that he had met with Zaluzhnyi and "discussed what kind of renewal the Armed Forces of Ukraine need," adding that "the time for such a renewal is now."[1] Zaluzhnyi, for his part, wrote on his own Telegram channel that "the tasks of 2022 are different from those of 2024."[1]

## Commentary

Writers at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] treated the timing of Syrskyi's elevation as an information-war event rather than a purely military one, noting that the shakeup landed on the same news cycle as the [[tucker-carlson-putin-interview|Tucker Carlson–Putin interview]] and arguing that the "Media Industrial Complex" was reacting to a Russian narrative offensive.[1]

## Frontline deterioration, April 2024

In a post on the Telegram messaging service on Sunday, April 28, 2024, Syrskyi — by then Ukraine's commander-in-chief — stated bluntly that "the situation at the front has worsened" in the face of multiple Russian attacks.[2] He confirmed that Ukrainian forces had withdrawn from positions in the eastern Donetsk region, in an area that had formed part of a defensive line established after Russia captured Avdiivka in February 2024.[2] Reporting attributed the pressure to Russia's attempt to exploit its superiority in manpower and artillery before newly approved American weapons could reach the front.[2] The United States had the previous week agreed a $61 billion military aid package for Ukraine, but the new weapons had yet to make their way to the frontlines, where Ukrainian troops had struggled for months with shortages of ammunition, troops and air defences.[2]

## French instructors initiative (May 2024)

On Monday, May 27, 2024, Syrskyi said he had signed paperwork allowing French military instructors to visit Ukrainian training centres soon, announcing the step on Telegram after talks by video link with French defence minister [[sebastien-lecornu|Sebastien Lecornu]].[3] "I am pleased to welcome France's initiative to send instructors to Ukraine to train Ukrainian servicemen," he said, adding that the signed documents would enable the first French instructors to familiarise themselves with training centre infrastructure and personnel shortly.[3] He gave no further details but said he believed France's determination would encourage other partners to join the "ambitious project."[3]

Ukraine's Defence Ministry later issued a "clarification" on X stating that Kyiv had been expressing interest in receiving foreign instructors since February and that discussions with France and other countries were still ongoing.[3]

## "The Butcher" reputation (March 2024)

Reporting in March 2024 described Syrskyi, then the newly installed head of the Ukrainian army, as being nicknamed "the butcher" by his own troops, with fears that his aggressive style would produce massive losses in the east on top of already high casualties.[4] While President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] claimed Ukraine had suffered only 31,000 soldier deaths, most analysts and observers inside Ukraine considered that figure far from reality; the United States estimated Ukraine had lost 70,000 men as of the previous summer, in addition to many more severely wounded, psychologically traumatized, or missing.[4] In an interview with Politico, one soldier who wished to remain anonymous warned of the 58-year-old commander, "He will kill us all."[4] Politico reported that Ukrainian troops regarded him as willing to put his men in harm's way to achieve his objectives, and noted that Syrskyi, who was born in central Russia, was known for hard-driving Soviet-style tactics.[4]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the emergence of such reporting from Ukraine-friendly outlets showed "the Turn on Ukraine" reaching the media establishment from too many directions to counter, with sympathy for Ukrainian soldiers driven by the character of the war machine rather than by Russian conduct.[4]
