# US Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit

The United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit is the federal appellate court with nationwide jurisdiction over specialized subject matter, including appeals from the Court of International Trade. In 2026 it became a central forum in litigation over [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]]'s tariff program.[1]

## 2026 tariff stay

In May 2026 the Federal Circuit issued a short-term administrative stay temporarily pausing a lower court ruling that had blocked Trump's 10% global tariff, imposed under Section 122 of the Trade Act.[1] The stay followed an appeal by the Trump administration of a trade court decision issued the preceding Friday; that lower court had ruled against the tariffs but stopped short of broadly halting their collection nationwide.[1]

Under the appellate court's order, challengers were given seven days to oppose a longer-term stay while the court considered whether the lower court ruling should remain blocked for the duration of the case.[1] The 10% tariff had been imposed in February 2026 after the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] struck down most of the tariffs Trump enacted in 2025, and was set to expire in July unless [[congress|Congress]] voted to extend it.[1]

## 2025 tariff ruling

In August 2025 the Federal Circuit determined that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] lacked the authority to impose his emergency-powers tariffs, but placed the decision on hold to give the administration time to appeal.[2] The administration asked the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] to take up the case on an expedited basis, and on September 9, 2025 the high court agreed to hear it that fall, scheduling oral argument for the first week of November.[2]

## Temporary reinstatement of the Trump tariffs (May 2025)

On Thursday, May 29, 2025, the Federal Circuit temporarily reinstated tariffs imposed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], granting the administration's request to pause a lower-court ruling that had struck them down.[3] The decision put on hold a Wednesday-night order from the [[us-court-of-international-trade|U.S. Court of International Trade]] that had blocked enforcement of most of the contested tariffs; the appellate court said the ruling was "temporarily stayed until further notice while this court considers the motions papers."[3] The administration had warned it would seek emergency relief from the Supreme Court as soon as Friday if the tariffs were not promptly restored.[3]

The underlying litigation comprised two suits — one from a coalition of companies including V.O.S. Selections, Inc., and another from states including Oregon, New York, and Illinois — arguing that the tariffs were improperly enacted via executive order.[3] Under the Federal Circuit's schedule, plaintiffs were to respond to the government's motion by June 5 with a reply due June 9, and both sides were required to keep the court updated on related activity in the lower court.[3]
