# Doug Burgum

Doug Burgum is a United States cabinet secretary in the second [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] administration who appeared as the administration's principal spokesman for its nuclear energy agenda in May 2025.[1]

## Nuclear executive orders

At a [[white-house|White House]] event publicized on May 22, 2025, Secretary Burgum framed a package of four executive orders on nuclear power as a historic corrective to decades of regulatory constraint, saying the action was "going to turn the clock back on over 50 years of overregulation of an industry" and that "a series of four Executive Orders — each of these helps attack separate issues that have held back this industry."[1] The White House promoted the signing as a "HUGE Day for the Nuclear Industry," and the [[nuclear-renaissance|nuclear renaissance]] was listed among the administration's weekly accomplishments alongside the [[one-big-beautiful-bill-act|One, Big, Beautiful Bill]], the [[take-it-down-act|Take It Down Act]], the MAHA Report, and [[golden-dome|Golden Dome]].[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the nuclear orders as evidence that Trump's invocation of a "Golden Age" was meant literally, tying the energy push to a broader "Timeline War" thesis and to the ideas of retired Lt. Gen. [[steve-kwast|Steve Kwast]]: energy independence and dominance were described as "a power card to usher in the latitude to start bringing in some of these other things."[1]

## Interior confirmation hearing (January 2025)

North Dakota Governor Doug Burgum's confirmation hearing to head the Department of the Interior was held on January 16, 2025 before the Senate Energy Committee, and was the opening item of Badlands Media's Senate confirmation coverage.[2] Most questioning concerned energy policy, with Republicans focused on land access and Democrats on green solutions.[2] A central topic was "baseload" — the minimum level of electricity demand that must be met consistently over time — and Burgum made clear that while he supports renewable energy, US energy demand cannot be reliably supplied with renewables at present technology levels.[2] Badlands commentary summarized his position as prioritizing reliability and declining to put Americans at risk to pursue green energy policies that do not solve America's energy demand, and described Burgum as calm and credible, with even the most combative Democrats such as Hawaii's [[mazie-hirono|Mazie Hirono]] backing off.[2] Representative [[ryan-zinke|Ryan Zinke]] said on Newsmax that he could not "think of a better individual for interior."[2]
