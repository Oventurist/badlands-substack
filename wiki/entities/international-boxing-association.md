# International Boxing Association

The International Boxing Association (IBA) is the international governing body for amateur boxing.[1] It became central to a 2024 Olympic controversy after it emerged that Algerian boxer [[imane-khelif|Imane Khelif]] had been banned from the 2023 world championships by the IBA for failing tests intended to establish gender qualification.[1]

Despite that disqualification, the [[international-olympic-committee|International Olympic Committee]] permitted Khelif to compete at the 2024 Paris Games over the objections of female athletes, setting up the 46-second bout in which Italy's [[angela-carini|Angela Carini]] abandoned the match shouting "This is unjust!"[1] The divergence between the IBA's ruling and the IOC's admission decision framed much of the ensuing public dispute over eligibility standards in women's boxing.[1]

## Paris 2024 press conference (August 2024)

The IBA held a press conference on Monday, August 5, 2024, at which it categorically confirmed that the two fighters then competing in Olympic women's boxing — [[imane-khelif|Imane Khelif]] of Algeria and [[lin-yu-ting|Lin Yu-ting]] of Taiwan — are "male" on the basis of a previously failed gender chromosome test; both had earlier been disqualified from the women's category by the association.[2] Chief executive Chris Roberts said the 2022 results, produced by an independent laboratory in Istanbul, "demonstrated the chromosomes we refer to in competition rules that make both boxers ineligible," and emphasised that both fighters had been informed of positive XY findings and offered an appeal to the [[court-of-arbitration-for-sport|Court of Arbitration for Sport]] with costs covered by the IBA.[2] Lin declined to appeal and Khelif withdrew hers; both were re-tested during the 2023 Women's World Boxing Championships in India with identical results.[2] The IBA's position stood in direct conflict with that of the [[international-olympic-committee|International Olympic Committee]], which allowed both boxers to compete at the [[2024-paris-summer-olympics|Paris Olympics]].[2]
