# Claude Dansey

Claude Dansey was a British intelligence officer who, in the account of [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], served simultaneously as a founding deputy director of Britain's MI5 and as the architect of at least two American secret intelligence agencies, operating from the [[sleepy-hollow-club|Sleepy Hollow Club]] in New York.[1]

## Career

Dansey rose to prominence during the Boer War, working closely with [[alfred-milner|Lord Milner]] and [[winston-churchill|Churchill]], and in 1909 became a founding deputy director of Britain's new spy agency MI5 alongside Viscount Richard Burdon Haldane and William Melville.[1] He was the permanent resident of the Sleepy Hollow Club, tasked with establishing a parallel security agency in the United States outside the influence of the American government, and according to [[anton-chaitkin|Anton Chaitkin]] he ran that agency from the club between 1911 and 1914.[1]

His protégé [[ralph-van-deman|Ralph Van Deman]] founded the shadowy military intelligence organization known as [[black-chamber|The Black Chamber]] in 1918, which excelled at signals intelligence and was rebranded in 1930 under the banner of the [[national-security-agency|National Security Agency]].[1] After World War I, Van Deman recommended Dansey for a Distinguished Service Medal "for guiding, planning and implementation of an American intelligence service."[1] Ehret argues that the fact that Dansey created at least two American secret intelligence agencies while serving as deputy director of the British Secret Service should cause any thinking person to question who has actually been running the United States over the past century.[1]

## The Z Organization

During World War II Dansey was assigned to create another secretive body, the Z Organization, which interfaced between British occult intelligence and Nazi occult networks and once again counted [[aleister-crowley|Aleister Crowley]] and [[george-sylvester-viereck|George Sylvester Viereck]] among its active members.[1] In the 1988 book All the King's Men, the historian Robert Marshall argued that Dansey's pseudo-private intelligence apparatus was behind the sabotage of several networks of French and Dutch anti-Nazi resistance fighters who believed they could trust their British intelligence contacts.[1]

## Intervention on behalf of Trotsky

Described as a Cecil Rhodes disciple, deputy chief of the newly formed MI6 and founder of US military intelligence in 1917, Dansey intervened directly to liberate [[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]] after Trotsky was arrested by Canadian authorities while en route back to Russia carrying tens of thousands of dollars of Wall Street money.[2]
