# National Guard Bureau

The National Guard Bureau is the federal military organization that oversees the Army and Air National Guard units of the individual U.S. states and territories, serving as the channel of communication between the Department of Defense and state Guard organizations.[1]

## Role in the 2025 "Quick Reaction Force" directive

In late October 2025 the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] directed every state and territory [[national-guard|National Guard]] to establish [[quick-reaction-force|"quick reaction" forces]] by January, trained and equipped to respond to riots and civil unrest across the United States, according to internal War Department memos.[1] The National Guard Bureau was assigned responsibility for training and equipping the forces for "civil disturbance operations."[1]

Under the plan, the Bureau would supply each unit with 100 sets of crowd control gear, including face and body shields, expandable riot batons, and shin guards, intended for both crowd control and checkpoint operations.[1] Each unit could include up to 500 soldiers prepared for rapid deployment on short notice, with personnel receiving nonlethal training in crowd control, the handling of detainees, and the use of batons, stun guns, and body shields.[1]

The memos set readiness benchmarks requiring forces in all 50 states, as well as Guam, the Virgin Islands, and Puerto Rico, to deploy 25% of personnel within hours, 50% within 12 hours, and 100% within 24 hours.[1] Washington, D.C., was to maintain a specialized military police battalion for civil unrest response separate from the nationwide units.[1] The initiative followed an August directive from [[donald-trump|President Trump]] to War Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]], and memos sent to state Guard organizations provided guidance for implementing the specialized units on an expedited, large-scale timeline.[1]
