# Samuel Chu

Samuel Chu is a Hong Kong–born American democracy activist who serves as Managing Director of the [[hong-kong-democracy-council|Hong Kong Democracy Council]] and is one of the most prominent overseas advocates for the [[hong-kong-pro-democracy-movement|Hong Kong pro-democracy movement]].[1]

## Background and family

Chu has participated in the [[hong-kong|Hong Kong]] pro-democracy movement for years, a movement in which his father was a leader.[1] His father was tried in Hong Kong courts for organizing the peaceful protests of 2014 and received a two-year sentence; Chu has said that had the case been heard on the Chinese mainland, the sentence would have been for life.[1]

## Assessment of the movement's destruction

Chu has described the collapse of the movement in stark terms, saying that the pro-democracy movement had "been so central and vital for the 30+ years in Hong Kong," but that "today, all of those leaders of the pro-democracy movement are either in jail, under house arrest, living in exile, or just stripped of their rights to speak out."[1]

## Targeting under the National Security Law

Although Chu has been an American citizen since 1996 and, by the account of Badlands Media, is "really only guilty of advocating for Basic Law in Hong Kong," he has been placed on a list of fugitives wanted by the [[chinese-communist-party|Chinese Communist Party]].[1] His case illustrates the extraterritorial reach of Article 38 of the [[hong-kong-national-security-law-2020|National Security Law]], which purports to allow anyone anywhere — citizen or not — to be charged for conduct deemed threatening to the CCP.[1]

## Policy prescriptions

In contrast to the resigned position voiced by [[richard-haass|Richard Haass]], Chu argues that the United States and the [[united-kingdom|United Kingdom]] — both instrumental in drawing up the framework for Hong Kong's short-lived autonomy — should return to the table and help uphold the promises made to the territory.[1] He maintains that a great deal remains possible: helping fight censorship and circumvent a coming internet firewall, helping activists leave the territory when China moves against them, strengthening civil society groups, and pressuring businesses in Hong Kong not to cooperate with repression.[1] Badlands Media noted that, despite Chu's optimism, the administration of [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] appeared uninterested in upholding free speech rights at home, let alone in a faraway city.[1]
