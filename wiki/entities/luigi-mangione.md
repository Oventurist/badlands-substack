# Luigi Mangione

**Luigi Mangione** is the man identified by police on Monday, December 9, 2024, as a "strong person of interest" in the [[killing-of-brian-thompson|shooting death of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson]] in Manhattan.[1]

## Arrest

Mangione, 26, was arrested in Pennsylvania on gun charges and was to face questioning, New York City officials said.[1] Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] told a news conference, "We believe we have a strong person of interest."[1] [[nypd|NYPD]] Commissioner Jessica Tisch identified him by name at a Monday briefing, and Chief of Detectives Joseph Kenny said he had been apprehended by authorities in Altoona, Pennsylvania, at a McDonald's.[1]

## Background as reported

Kenny said Mangione was born and raised in Maryland, has ties to San Francisco, and had a last known address in Honolulu, Hawaii.[1] Police officials said he would face gun charges in Altoona, with Kenny adding that "at some point, we'll work out through extradition to bring him back to New York to face charges here, working with the Manhattan district attorney's office."[1] Noting that "this just happened this morning," Kenny said investigators would be "backtracking his steps from New York to Altoona, Pennsylvania."[1] Authorities said NYPD officers and prosecutors from the Manhattan District Attorney's Office would interview him further.[1]

## September 2025 court ruling

In September 2025, Judge [[gregory-carro|Gregory Carro]] dismissed the terrorism charges against Mangione in New York State's case over the killing of [[unitedhealthcare|UnitedHealthcare]] chief executive [[brian-thompson|Brian Thompson]], while keeping the state's second-degree murder charges in place.[2] Mangione's lawyers had argued that the New York case and a parallel federal death penalty prosecution together amounted to double jeopardy, but Carro rejected that argument as premature, leaving Mangione exposed to prosecution at both the state and federal levels for the time being.[2]

The hearing marked Mangione's first court appearance in the state case since February 2025, and the prosecution was described as one of the most closely watched murder cases in recent years.[2] Mangione, then 27, had attracted a following — mostly on the political left — as a stand-in for public frustration with the health insurance industry.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the lionization of Mangione after Thompson's killing made subsequent celebrations of the death of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] entirely predictable, writing that when people show you who they are, you should believe them.[2] She identified the rejection of the double jeopardy argument as the most interesting element of the decision.[2]
