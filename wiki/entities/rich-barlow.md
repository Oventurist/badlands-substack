# Rich Barlow

Rich Barlow is a former covert [[cia|CIA]] operative and the agency's recognized expert on the trade in weapons of mass destruction, whose career was destroyed after he documented U.S. complicity in [[pakistan|Pakistan's]] acquisition of the nuclear bomb.[1] Badlands Media contributor Joe Lange calls him "a patriot and a hero," and cites him as evidence that not everyone inside the three-letter agencies was corrupt when what he terms the [[shadow-government|shadow government]] was being built.[1]

## Career

Barlow prepared briefs for Dick Cheney during Cheney's tenure at the Pentagon, for the upper echelons of the CIA, and even for the Oval Office.[1] The Guardian described him decades later idling outside a silver trailer on a remote campsite in Montana — itinerant and unemployed, with only his hunting dogs and a borrowed computer for company.[1]

## The Pakistan nuclear scandal

In the late 1980s, while tracking smugglers of WMD components, Barlow uncovered reams of material relating to Pakistan.[1] It was known that Pakistan had been covertly striving to acquire nuclear weapons since India's 1974 test, and the prospect alarmed the West given a nation that had suffered three military coups in under thirty years.[1] "Pakistan was the kind of place where technology could slip out of control," Barlow said.[1]

He then discovered that senior officials in government took the opposite view: they were breaking U.S. and international non-proliferation protocols to shelter Pakistan's ambitions and even to sell it banned WMD technology.[1] In the closing years of the Cold War, Pakistan was considered strategically vital as a springboard into neighbouring [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] and a route for passing U.S. weapons and cash to the [[mujahideen|mujahideen]].[1] "We had to buddy-up to regimes we didn't see eye-to-eye with, but I could not believe we would actually give Pakistan the bomb," Barlow said.[1] He next discovered that the Pentagon was preparing to sell Pakistan jet fighters capable of delivering a nuclear weapon.[1]

## Retaliation and consequences

Barlow was relentless in exposing what he saw as U.S. complicity, and in the end he was sacked and smeared as disloyal, mad, a drunk and a philanderer.[1] Many believe that had he been heeded, Pakistan might never have obtained its bomb, South Asia might not have been pitched into three near-nuclear confrontations, and the nuclear weapons programs of Iran, Libya and [[north-korea|North Korea]] — which British and American intelligence acknowledge were all secretly enabled by Pakistan — would never have got off the ground.[1] Lange argues Barlow is one of many such targets, and that the [[nuclear-proliferation|proliferation]] he uncovered was deliberate policy designed to protect Pakistan's role as terrorist safe haven and to shield the CIA's heroin trade and BCCI money laundering.[1]
