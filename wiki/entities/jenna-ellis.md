# Jenna Ellis

Jenna Ellis is an American attorney who served as legal counsel to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during and after the 2020 presidential election and who was later charged in the Georgia racketeering case brought by Fulton County District Attorney [[fani-willis|Fani Willis]].[1] The public image most observers had of Ellis was her "remarkably cheerful mugshot" taken after her arrest in that RICO prosecution.[1]

## Guilty plea in Georgia

In October 2023 Ellis appeared in Georgia state court and pleaded guilty to intentionally interfering in the election process in the state, entering a plea to a single count of aiding and abetting false statements in writing.[1] As with other former Trump counsel who resolved their cases, she avoided jail time.[1]

Under the terms of the plea agreement, Ellis was to serve five years of probation, pay $5,000 in restitution to the Georgia Secretary of State's office — then led by [[brad-raffensperger|Brad Raffensperger]] — within 30 days, complete 100 hours of community service, write an apology letter to the voters of Georgia, and testify truthfully in future hearings connected to the ongoing cases.[1]

## Legal significance

Commentary by law professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] characterized the Ellis plea as carrying more ominous implications for the former president than earlier pleas by figures such as [[sidney-powell|Sidney Powell]].[1] Powell had pleaded to comparatively minor charges involving unauthorized access to voting machines and restricted areas, offenses that are easy to prove but that do not obviously tie Trump to a conspiracy or racketeering scheme.[1] By contrast, Ellis pleaded guilty to false statements that could conceivably implicate Trump himself if she were to claim that he knew of their falsity and facilitated the crime.[1] Turley described hers as "the type of plea that tends to concentrate the mind."[1]

## Break with Trump

Ellis publicly broke with Trump shortly before the plea, calling him a "malignant narcissist" who cannot admit mistakes and stating that she would never vote for him again — a rupture that heightened the perceived risk to the former president from her cooperation obligations.[1]
