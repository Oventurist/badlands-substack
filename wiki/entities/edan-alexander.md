# Edan Alexander

Edan Alexander is an American-Israeli hostage held in Gaza whose potential release became the centerpiece of a United States proposal to revive the Gaza ceasefire in March 2025.[1]

## March 2025 ceasefire proposal

Qatar presented [[hamas|Hamas]] with a new US proposal to restore the ceasefire in Gaza through Alexander's release, according to a senior Arab diplomat who spoke to The Times of Israel on 27 March 2025. In exchange, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would issue a statement calling for calm in Gaza and the resumption of negotiations toward a permanent ceasefire.[1]

The Arab diplomat expressed skepticism that Hamas would agree to free Alexander in return for only a statement from Trump, suggesting the group would likely demand something more substantial given its doubts about Israel's willingness to adhere to agreements. Alexander was described as one of the remaining 59 hostages, 24 of whom were still alive according to Israeli intelligence assessments.[1]

## Badlands analysis

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read the offer as Trump seizing an opening amid Israel's domestic political chaos, describing it as effectively tossing a live grenade to Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] with the pin already pulled. Objectively, the commentary noted, the deal looked like a lifeline: with fury directed at Netanyahu across the political spectrum over his judicial appointments bill, a fresh ceasefire with released hostages could have won him goodwill among political moderates.[1]

The analysis argued instead that Netanyahu's radical coalition partners — including National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] and his [[otzma-yehudit|Otzma Yehudit]] party — had no interest in a ceasefire, having resigned in protest over [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]]'s earlier deal and returned only when Netanyahu resumed the bombing campaign. The focus on American-Israeli hostages was also framed as delivering on the promises of [[adam-boehler|Adam Boehler]], who had met Hamas in Doha earlier that month and told Jake Tapper a deal was only weeks away.[1]

Netanyahu's allies were said to have made clear that any deal releasing only dual citizens would be unacceptable, fearing that once American citizens were freed the Trump administration would lose its appetite for war — a fear the commentary judged probably correct. Compounding a ceasefire with the loss of the American-Israeli hostages as bargaining chips was expected to make the coalition's radicals anxious about the prospects of the expanded regional war they viewed as necessary to push Israel's borders into neighboring Arab states.[1]

## Release facilitated by Witkoff (May 2025)

In May 2025, Hamas confirmed it would release Alexander as a gesture of goodwill toward [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] during his visit to the region that week.[2] Israeli journalist Barak Ravid reported that US special envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] would arrive in Israel the following day to facilitate the process and "finalize the details ahead of the release of Edan Alexander," according to a source familiar with the matter.[2] Witkoff was at the time in Oman, where he had been mediating [[2025-oman-nuclear-talks|nuclear negotiations between the United States and Iran]].[2]
