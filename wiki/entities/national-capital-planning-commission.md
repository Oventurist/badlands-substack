# National Capital Planning Commission

The National Capital Planning Commission is the federal panel responsible for reviewing development and construction projects in the Washington, D.C. region. In April 2026 it voted 8-1 to approve President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s plan to build a new [[white-house-ballroom|White House ballroom]].[1]

## The ballroom vote

The commission approved the project on a Thursday by a margin of 8-1.[1] Commission chair [[will-scharf|Will Scharf]] said that many of the public comments submitted to the panel fell outside its authority, including objections concerning funding, demolition, and general political criticism of the president, remarking, "We are not some sort of free-ranging ballroom justice commission."[1] The comment underscored the commission's position that its jurisdiction is limited to planning and design review rather than the political or fiscal merits of a proposal.[1]

Following the vote, Trump thanked the commission on [[truth-social|Truth Social]], saying the project had been approved "overwhelmingly, 8-1," and noting that even Sen. [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] had voted in favor.[1] He said presidents for more than 150 years had envisioned a White House ballroom, describing it as a long-needed addition for hosting state visits, grand events, and inaugurations.[1]

## Role in the pressure campaign against Jerome Powell

In July 2025 the National Pulse reported that Trump had quietly replaced a number of Biden-era appointees on the commission with key figures from his [[white-house|White House]], in what was described as a maneuver to escalate pressure on [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] Chairman [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]].[2] Because the commission holds authority over government buildings — including the Federal Reserve's headquarters — the appointments were read as a means of fueling a negative media campaign against Powell over a lavish renovation of the central bank's offices that had far exceeded its approved budget.[2] The play was likened to President Richard Nixon's efforts to push his Fed chairman, Arthur Burns, to cut interest rates, with the aim of either ousting Powell or forcing him to yield to pressure to slash rates.[2]
