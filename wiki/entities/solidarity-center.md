# Solidarity Center

The Solidarity Center is the international arm of the [[afl-cio|AFL-CIO]], described in the article as the mechanism by which the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] and the [[cia|CIA]] exert influence over labor unions abroad.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The article reduces its function to a formula: "the Solidarity Center = NED/CIA influence over labor unions."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Formation

The AFL-CIO established the Solidarity Center in 1997 through the consolidation of four labor institutes: the [[american-institute-for-free-labor-development|American Institute for Free Labor Development]], the [[asian-american-free-labor-institute|Asian-American Free Labor Institute]], the African-American Labor Institute and the Free Trade Union Institute.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) It sits alongside other NED-funded cut-outs such as the International Republican Institute, the National Democratic Institute and the Center for International Private Enterprise.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

Its in-country staffers, drawn from union organizing backgrounds, act as conduits between American unions and their foreign counterparts while serving as the AFL-CIO's eyes and ears on the ground.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The article highlights a contradiction between the Center's claim to advance foreign workers' interests and the fact that three-quarters of its funding comes from the corporately dominated U.S. government, explaining it as Washington's attempt to influence a force it cannot fully repress.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Funding and targets

The Center receives an estimated $30–50 million annually from NED and [[usaid|USAID]], and targets "strategic" nations — those significant for resources, geography or ideological resistance to Western hegemony.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) These taxpayer-sourced funds train labor activists, fund strikes and shape union leadership to align with Washington's objectives, applying a Cold War playbook globalized and digitized for the era of hybrid warfare and information control.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Case studies

In [[ukraine|Ukraine]], the 2014 Maidan uprising is presented as a textbook case: the Center supported pro-Western labor groups with NED grants exceeding $500,000, amplifying strikes and protests against the Yanukovych government in line with [[state-department|State Department]] and [[pentagon|Department of Defense]] objectives to pull Ukraine into NATO's orbit.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) Ukrainian unions trained in Gene Sharp-derived "nonviolent resistance" tactics became foot soldiers in the operation, and post-Maidan funding reshaped Ukraine's labor laws toward EU standards.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

In Hong Kong during 2019–2020, the Center quietly funnelled NED grants to pro-democracy unions, notably the Hong Kong Confederation of Trade Unions, as part of a broader $2 million NED package; declassified State Department cables are said to show coordination with U.S. consulate officials, with the goal being destabilization of Beijing's control rather than worker empowerment.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

After the 2021 military coup in Myanmar, the Center ramped up support for anti-junta labor groups with USAID and NED funds, with the real aim described as weakening a government perceived as too close to China; "labor organizing" training doubled as intelligence gathering.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) In mineral-rich African states such as the Democratic Republic of Congo and Zambia, NED grants often exceeding $1 million per country fund training that steers unions toward pro-Western policies and undermines Chinese-backed labor federations, coordinated with AFRICOM's "stability operations."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

## Mechanisms of control

Two mechanisms are identified. The first is digital surveillance: through encrypted platforms, social media monitoring and data-sharing agreements with U.S. agencies, the Center collects real-time intelligence on labor unrest, political dissent and even military activity, with organizers in Venezuela and Ukraine reportedly tasked to report to U.S. embassies via secure apps — data feeding CIA and DoD analyses.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union) The second is conditional funding: grant agreements require alignment with U.S. foreign policy, and unions that deviate toward local wage demands or nationalization find their funding drying up.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)

The article's closing argument is that unmasking the Solidarity Center and its NED patrons is the first step toward labor reclaiming its independence.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-more-perfect-labor-union)
