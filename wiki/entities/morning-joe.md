# Morning Joe

*Morning Joe* is [[msnbc|MSNBC]]'s weekday morning news and commentary program, and a recurring venue for establishment-aligned political analysis of the sort routinely criticized in [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] coverage.[1]

## December 2023 14th Amendment segment

In December 2023 the program hosted [[new-york-times|New York Times]] editorial board member [[mara-gay|Mara Gay]] for a discussion of the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]]'s decision disqualifying [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from the 2024 election ballot.[1] Asked how she would respond to those who had questioned the constitutional basis for using the [[fourteenth-amendment|14th Amendment]] to bar Trump from ballots, Gay answered: "Why are you standing with confederates who betrayed this country?"[1] She continued that objectors were channeling "the spirit of those confederates rather than the Americans who came together after a long and brutal Civil War that was fought to keep the Union together," a generation that "clearly saw a threat in ex-confederates running for office" and amended the Constitution "to prevent those traitors from running for office."[1]

Law professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]], writing about the appearance, said the remarks "captured the unhinged coverage" of the Colorado ruling, and observed that the underlying 14th Amendment theory had been rejected by jurists in various states, including many Democrats.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] identified the segment as an instance of a previously subtle theme becoming explicit heading into 2024: casting [[jefferson-davis|Jefferson Davis]] and the Confederacy as an apt analogue for Trump, a framing she noted had occupied a significant portion of the Colorado trial and was reflected in the court's ruling.[1] Gay's extension of the analogy to critics of the ruling, she argued, implied that January 6 was a civil war, an escalation she attributed to establishment failure to stop Trump and expected to worsen.[1]
