# Sunrise Savings and Loan

Sunrise Savings and Loan Association of Boynton Beach, Florida was a $1.5 billion thrift whose 1985 failure Badlands Media contributor [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] links directly to intervention by Vice President [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]].[1]

Investigators alleged in July 1985 that bad deals and under-the-table arrangements doomed the institution, and a federal grand jury examined its lending practices including the possibility that borrowers paid kickbacks to get loans.[1] Regulators from the [[federal-savings-and-loan-insurance-corporation|Federal Savings and Loan Insurance Corporation]] had taken over the operation two weeks earlier after learning its losses for June totalled $22 million; at least four class-action suits were filed by shareholders who saw their stock plunge from $25 a share the previous year to 25 cents.[1]

The Buffalo News, reporting on a Village Voice investigation, said Bush derailed a federal investigation of the thrift while vice president, allowing it to fail from dubious loans to a Houston associate — described as part of a pattern of helping major figures in the savings and loan scandal who enriched themselves at taxpayers' expense but were never held accountable.[1] In 1984 Bush summoned a thrift regulator to his office and, in front of Sunrise chief executive Robert Jacoby, reprimanded her and told her to back off her investigation of Sunrise's bad loans.[1] The thrift failed a year later at a cost to taxpayers of $680 million; the incident was first revealed at a 1989 trial of Sunrise executives.[1]

Lange argues Bush only delayed the inevitable, allowing his friend to continue the fraud and making the eventual collapse far bigger, and speculates that Bush intervened because it was a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] operation shielded by a "national security" pretext — $680 million in agency funding paid for by taxpayers.[1] A major factor in Sunrise's failure was $15 million in defaulted loans to [[john-riddle|John Riddle]], a friend of Bush's longtime political fundraiser, Houston banker [[walter-mischer|Walter Mischer]], and one of the five largest defaulters on thrift loans in the nation.[1]
