# Tennessee Valley Authority

The **Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA)** was the southeastern zone of megaprojects within the "Four Quarters" grand design of [[franklin-d-roosevelt|Franklin Roosevelt]]'s infrastructure program, alongside the Columbia River Treaty zone, the St. Lawrence Seaway zone and the Hoover Dam/Colorado zone.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] writes that these projects, funded through the [[reconstruction-finance-corporation|Reconstruction Finance Corporation]], were transformative in ways money could never measure: literacy in the Tennessee area rose from 20% in 1932 to 80% in 1950, and what he calls racist backwater holes of the south became the bedrock of America's aerospace industry thanks to abundant and cheap hydropower.[1]

## Place among New Deal public works

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] lists the Tennessee Valley Authority first among the vast public works created under the [[new-deal|New Deal]], alongside the Grand Coulee and Hoover dams, the St. Lawrence development and countless hospitals, schools, bridges, roads and rail projects.[2] He compares the role these projects played in the 1930s to that of China's Belt and Road Initiative in the modern age.[2]
