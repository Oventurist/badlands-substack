# American Hypnotist

American Hypnotist is the pen name of a contributor to [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] who writes about hypnosis, persuasion technique, and mass propaganda, and who also publishes independently at the American Hypnotist Substack.[1]

## Analytical approach

The author's central premise is that hypnotic influence operates through emotional arousal: a hypnosis teacher's maxim that "if I can heighten someone's emotions then I can give them a hypnotic suggestion" is presented as the key to understanding modern media manipulation, from which it follows that the defense against being hypnotized is to remain "as emotion-neutral as possible in the midst of chaos."[1] On that basis American Hypnotist defines [[disinformation|disinformation]] as the deliberate blending of truth and falsehood into a web designed to stir the passions of the masses and steer them toward specific actions.[1]

A recurring theme is the technical vocabulary of hypnosis applied to news coverage — in particular [[nominalization|nominalizations]] and hypnotic repetition, described as "two of the main pillars of modern propaganda."[1] The author argues that learning to recognize nominalizations in real time makes a person "relatively invincible to all forms of Mockingbird propaganda," and frames this literacy as a matter of collective survival.[1]

## Stated position on Israel-Palestine

Writing on October 23, 2023 about the [[israel-palestine-conflict|Israel-Palestine conflict]], American Hypnotist declined to take a side, stating two principles for the record: that criminal enterprises masquerading as "government" are without exception wrong, and that innocent civilians must not be held personally culpable for the actions of those governments.[1] The stated goal is not for one side to decimate the other but for the removal of criminal elements from these governments, and the author explicitly refuses "to be goaded into taking a stance without sufficient reason for doing so."[1] The essay opens by quoting [[clif-high|Clif High]]'s satirical line, "Listen up, butthead! If you don't choose a side, you won't be able to have your emotions torqued & be manipulated!"[1]

The author questions how anyone living halfway across the world could know precisely what is happening when propagandists from every corner of the Earth skew the story in different directions, and cites a 2015 Photoshop mini-course as the source of a lasting realization that "anybody can make anything look like anything" — a lesson that, given deepfakes, now warrants near-categorical skepticism.[1]

## Thesis on the collapse of media programming

American Hypnotist argues that the [[mockingbird-media|Mockingbird Media]]'s conditioning is visibly breaking down, using the physics of an avalanche as a metaphor: a few patches of snow slip, then the whole structure comes down at once.[1] The essay suggests the Israel-Palestine conflict may be the point of no return for the edifice of Mockingbird programming, and connects this to the [[q|Q]] movement's role in "showing the people" what is going on.[1] Because a broken social environment can no longer deliver coherent instructions to its members, the author predicts the "spell" will break — and that [[world-war-iii|WWIII]] "will be like a car that won't start," prompting people to look under the hood and rediscover what [[smedley-butler|Smedley Butler]] said in the 1930s.[1]
