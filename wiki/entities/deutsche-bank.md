# Deutsche Bank

Deutsche Bank is a German multinational investment bank that, alongside [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]], became a defendant in civil litigation arising from the financial relationships maintained by the late sex offender [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[1]

## Epstein-related litigation

In February 2023, Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] cited lawsuits against JPMorgan Chase and Deutsche Bank as part of a renewed wave of Epstein-related legal activity, together with the unsealing of names and documents, newly surfaced emails, an appeal filed by [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]], and the subpoena of [[les-wexner|Les Wexner]] by the [[us-virgin-islands|US Virgin Islands]].[1]

## Rakoff ruling on Epstein liability (May 2023)

On May 1, 2023, U.S. District Judge [[jed-rakoff|Jed Rakoff]] declined to dismiss claims against Deutsche Bank arising from its relationship with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], holding that one plaintiff — a woman who said she was abused by Epstein and his associates — had "pled sufficient facts to support her allegation that Deutsche Bank knew or recklessly disregarded that Jeffrey Epstein ran a sex-trafficking venture."[2] The decision came in the same 54-page opinion that allowed claims to proceed against [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] and its former executive [[jes-staley|Jes Staley]], in litigation brought by alleged Epstein victims and by the [[us-virgin-islands|United States Virgin Islands]] government.[2]

## Testimony in the Trump New York civil fraud trial (2023)

Deutsche Bank occupied a central place in New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]]' civil fraud suit against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], which portrayed the German lender as Trump's biggest victim in an alleged scheme to inflate asset values in order to obtain favorable loan and insurance terms.[3] On November 28, 2023, managing director [[david-williams-deutsche-bank|David Williams]], who had worked directly on at least one of the loans, testified in Manhattan that it is "atypical, but not entirely unusual" for a bank to internally cut a client's stated asset values by 50% and approve the loan anyway, adding, "It just depends on the circumstances."[3]

Internal credit memos showed the bank reduced Trump's stated net worth in 2011 and 2012 from about $4.2 billion to $2.3 billion yet still approved hundreds of millions of dollars in loans for properties in Miami, Chicago and Washington, because it expected the loans to be profitable based on Trump's record of successful developments and other criteria.[3] Trump's defense called four current and former Deutsche Bank employees that week, including the family's former private banker [[rosemary-vrablic|Rosemary Vrablic]], seeking to flip the script on the state's account by showing the bank had courted his business.[3] ZeroHedge argued the testimony undermined James' premise that Trump had defrauded the bank.[3]

## Epstein settlement comparison (October 2023)

A federal judge approved Deutsche Bank's $75 million settlement with accusers of [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] on October 20, 2023.[4] Days later, state attorneys general objecting to [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]]'s larger $290 million settlement cited the Deutsche Bank agreement favourably, noting that it did not contain the language barring "any sovereign or government" from seeking damages arising from Epstein-related sex trafficking that they found objectionable in the JPMorgan accord.[4]

## Epstein trafficking lawsuits survive dismissal (March 2023)

On March 20, 2023, U.S. District Judge [[jed-rakoff|Jed Rakoff]] ruled that Deutsche Bank, along with [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase & Co.]], would face lawsuits alleging the banks enabled [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] to traffic his victims.[5] Two women suing as "Jane Doe" had brought federal class actions against the banks in November 2022.[5] Rakoff's four-page order permitted claims that the defendants "knowingly benefited from participating in a sex-trafficking venture," "obstructed enforcement of the [[trafficking-victims-protection-act|Trafficking Victims Protection Act]]," and "negligently failed to exercise reasonable care to prevent physical harm," together with a claim of negligent failure to exercise reasonable care as a banking institution providing non-routine banking; the remaining claims were dismissed.[5]
