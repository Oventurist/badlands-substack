# Jonathan Steele

Jonathan Steele is a British journalist and author of *Ghosts of Afghanistan: Hard Truths and Foreign Myths*, a book reviewed by [[the-guardian|The Guardian]] and cited by Badlands Media as evidence against the conventional account of the Soviet intervention in [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]].[1]

## Argument on the 1979 intervention

Steele based his view on declassified [[soviet-union|Soviet]] documents. According to the review quoted in the article, the Russians invaded in December 1979 not as part of a plan for the world victory of communism, as Western propaganda had it, but to protect the security of their southern frontier against Islamic unrest and American intrigue.[1]

Steele's account records that senior Soviet generals and officials had warned against getting mixed up in an Afghan civil war, pointing to the unhappy precedent of Vietnam, but that their leaders went ahead anyway largely because they could not think of a better alternative — hoping to change the Afghan leader, train his army and police, and withdraw within months. Instead the Soviets were quickly sucked into the quagmire the skeptics had feared and were soon looking for a way out.[1]

## Use in Badlands coverage

Badlands Media reads Steele's phrase "Islamic unrest and American intrigue" as a description of terrorism sponsored by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]], and treats his research as corroboration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s much-criticized 2019 remark that "terrorists were going into Russia" and that the Soviets "were right to be there."[1]
