# The Vanguard Group

The Vanguard Group is a major American asset manager named by Badlands Media among the institutional investors whose common shareholdings unify the mainstream media conglomerates.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

Badlands argues that the apparent competition among the "Big Six" media companies is illusory because Vanguard, [[blackrock|BlackRock]], [[state-street|State Street]] and [[berkshire-hathaway|Berkshire Hathaway]] hold overlapping ownership stakes across all of them.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) The article further notes that representatives of these asset managers remain regular participants in elite policy forums such as the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] and the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], linking media ownership to the wider establishment policy network.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## Part of the old guard

[[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] names the Vanguard Group, with [[blackrock|BlackRock Inc.]], among the "gargantuan investment firms" that form part of the old-guard [[foreign-policy-establishment|foreign policy establishment]] — the interagency bureaucratic amalgam and think-tank network that has long controlled and spread Western hegemony and that opposed withdrawal from the [[joint-comprehensive-plan-of-action|Iran Deal]].[2]

## SPLC donation pause (2026)

In May 2026, Vanguard Group and [[fidelity-investments|Fidelity Investments]] were reported to have halted charitable donations to the [[southern-poverty-law-center|Southern Poverty Law Center]] following a federal indictment involving an individual allegedly connected to the organization.[3] The pause was read as evidence of reputational-risk management among large asset managers facing increased scrutiny over the destinations of corporate philanthropy, even though the SPLC itself had not been charged as an organization.[3]

## Texas antitrust suit survives dismissal (2025)

Vanguard was named alongside BlackRock and State Street in Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton's antitrust suit over alleged coordination to curb coal production; a federal judge denied the managers' motion to dismiss in 2025, clearing the way for full discovery and a potential trial.[4]

## Universal ownership (January 2023 analysis)

In a January 17, 2023 Badlands Media feature, contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described the Vanguard Group and [[blackrock|BlackRock, Inc.]] as the two largest investment companies in the world, noting that a look at the biggest shareholders of virtually any major parent company turns up the same two names at the top of the institutional holders list.[5] He observed that the inner workings of Vanguard are more obscure than BlackRock's, and therefore focused his detailed analysis on the latter, while treating the two as an inseparable pair throughout.[5]

DeLarme traced the pair's position across the packaged food industry — [[pepsico|PepsiCo]], [[coca-cola|Coca-Cola]] and the grocery parent [[spartannash|SpartanNash]] — and then through media, technology and pharmaceuticals, arguing that the resulting cross-holdings constitute [[horizontal-shareholding|horizontal shareholding]] and have deteriorated any genuine sense of competition.[5] Among the specific holdings named are [[comcast|Comcast]], [[disney|Disney]], AT&T's Time Warner, [[news-corp|News Corp]], [[new-york-times|The New York Times]], [[meta-platforms|Meta]], [[alphabet|Alphabet]], Apple, Microsoft, Intel, IBM, [[pfizer|Pfizer]], Monsanto-[[bayer-ag|Bayer]], [[johnson-and-johnson|Johnson & Johnson]], [[moderna|Moderna]], [[novavax|NovaVax]], [[zoom-video-communications|Zoom]], [[walmart|Walmart]] and [[target-corporation|Target]].[5]

Asked who owns the largest shares of Vanguard and BlackRock themselves, the article answers that the largest holders of each are Vanguard and BlackRock, closing the ownership loop.[5] DeLarme concludes that Vanguard- and BlackRock-owned media persuaded the public of the efficacy of Vanguard- and BlackRock-owned experimental drugs, and that COVID-19 produced the largest upward transfer of wealth in human history, a claim supported with [[oxfam|Oxfam]]'s "Power, Profits and the Pandemic" report.[5]
