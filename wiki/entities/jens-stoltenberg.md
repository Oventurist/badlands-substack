# Jens Stoltenberg

Jens Stoltenberg is a Norwegian politician who served as Secretary General of [[nato|NATO]], the position he held during the period of the [[russian-invasion-of-ukraine|Russian invasion of Ukraine]].[1]

## Remarks on NATO expansion and the invasion of Ukraine

In a September 2023 speech to the European Parliament's foreign affairs committee, Stoltenberg was reported to have clearly and repeatedly acknowledged that [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] decided to invade Ukraine because of fears of NATO expansionism.[1] His comments, initially flagged by journalist Thomas Fazi and highlighted by [[caitlin-johnstone|Caitlin Johnstone]], recounted that Putin had declared in the autumn of 2021 and sent a draft treaty asking NATO to promise no further enlargement, presenting this as a precondition for not invading Ukraine.[1]

Stoltenberg said the alliance did not sign that document and that "the opposite happened."[1] He described Putin as also demanding that NATO remove military infrastructure from all allies that had joined since 1997 — effectively half the alliance, comprising Central and Eastern Europe — which Stoltenberg characterised as introducing a form of second-class membership, and which NATO rejected.[1] He concluded that Putin "went to war to prevent NATO, more NATO, close to his borders."[1]

The remarks were treated in alternative media as a significant admission because they conflicted with the prevailing Western framing of the war's origins.[1]

## Ukraine membership remarks on the war's second anniversary

In a video message released on Saturday, February 24, 2024 — the exact two-year mark since Russian forces crossed the Ukrainian border — the [[nato|NATO]] Secretary General said: "Ukraine will join NATO. It is not a question of if, but of when."[2] The remarks were characterized by ZeroHedge as ultra-provocative predictions about Kiev's future with the Western military alliance.[2]

Responding the following day, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] warned that European countries would "automatically be pulled into a war with Russia" if Ukraine were allowed to enter NATO, stressing that in such a scenario "there will be no winners" and that "you will be pulled into this conflict against your will. You won't even have time to blink your eye when you execute [[article-5|Article 5]]. I don't want it."[2]

## 2023 Vilnius summit

As NATO Secretary-General ahead of the July 2023 Vilnius summit, Stoltenberg promised to push for a fast-tracked accession process for Ukraine at the future point when the alliance was ready to seriously consider its application, a commitment tied to dropping the [[membership-action-plan|Membership Action Plan]] requirement.[3] The assurance did not satisfy President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]], who publicly criticized [[nato|NATO]]'s refusal to specify a timeline.[3]

## Chief of staff's land-for-peace remark (August 2023)

Stoltenberg had consistently declined to say what Ukraine should do to end the war, replying that the decision on whether to open negotiations with Russia belongs to Ukraine alone rather than to third countries.[4] That line was disrupted in August 2023 when his chief of staff, [[stian-jenssen|Stian Jenssen]], told Norwegian journalists that a territorial concession in exchange for peace and NATO membership had already been discussed within the alliance and "could be a possible solution."[4] Jenssen retracted the statement the next day, calling it "a mistake" made in the context of a broader discussion of future scenarios.[4]



## Winter missile-stockpile warning (November 2023)

At the Brussels meeting of NATO foreign ministers in late November 2023, Stoltenberg warned that Russia had amassed a large missile stockpile ahead of winter, a caution echoed by other sources and cited by Reuters.[5] His warning accompanied Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]]'s insistence that there was "no sense of fatigue" among allies over supporting Ukraine, even as the front line remained stalemated and the Israel-Gaza conflict dominated global attention.[5]
