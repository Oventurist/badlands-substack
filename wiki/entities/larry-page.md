# Larry Page

Larry Page is a co-founder of [[google|Google]]. He became a subject of public controversy in the "Truth Community" in April 2023 after [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], in a long-anticipated sit-down interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], said that Page had once told him he planned to build a "[[digital-god|digital god]]."[1]

Musk recounted that he used to stay at Page's house in Palo Alto, California, and that he frequently warned Page about the dangers of [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]].[1] According to Musk, Page did not take heed of the warnings about AI safety and instead called him a "speciesist."[1] Musk characterized Google's end game as creating a "digital super intelligence" — in other words, a digital god.[1]

## Succession at Google

Larry Page's 2019 departure from the chief executive role at [[alphabet|Alphabet]] left Sundar Pichai as chief executive, a succession noted in coverage of the September 2023 antitrust trial in which Pichai was expected to be a prominent witness.[2] See [[united-states-v-google-antitrust-trial]].[2]

## Epstein-related subpoena efforts

In May 2023, federal court filings revealed that the [[us-virgin-islands|US Virgin Islands]] could not subpoena Page, then 50, over potential ties to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] because he could not be physically located.[3] A motion filed by the territory's attorney general stated that investigators had identified four possible addresses for Page, none of which were valid.[3] Prosecutors asked the court to allow service by alternative means through [[alphabet|Alphabet]], Google's parent company, on whose board Page remained a member.[3] The motion was part of a case alleging that [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] facilitated Epstein's sex-trafficking ring by handling his payments to recruiters and victims.[3]

Page's whereabouts were reported to be largely unknown; as of December 2022 he was said to have spent the bulk of the COVID-19 pandemic on one of a growing number of private islands he owned, some of them located within the US Virgin Islands.[3] Badlands Media contributor [[paul-fleuret|Paul Fleuret]] argued Page had "conveniently" gone missing because "the walls are closing in," and predicted the Epstein matter would only grow.[3]

## Service in the U.S. Virgin Islands Epstein litigation

On May 4, 2023, U.S. District Judge [[jed-rakoff|Jed Rakoff]] ruled in federal court in Manhattan that the [[us-virgin-islands|U.S. Virgin Islands]] could serve legal papers on Page as part of its lawsuit against [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] over the bank's ties to the late sex offender [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[4] The ruling did not specify what information the territory sought from Page, a co-founder of Google, the company that grew into [[alphabet|Alphabet Inc.]] with Google as a unit.[4]

The territory's suit accuses JPMorgan of missing red flags about Epstein's abuse of women on [[little-st-james|Little St. James]], the private island Epstein owned there.[4] Commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the case, filed in December 2022, alleges that JPMorgan executives "developed a close relationship with Epstein" and that Epstein "brought additional high net worth clients" to the bank — Page being identified as one such client.[4] The subpoena was said to seek correspondence between Page and Epstein as well as between Page and JPMorgan.[4]
