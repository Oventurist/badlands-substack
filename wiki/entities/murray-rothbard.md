# Murray Rothbard

Murray Rothbard was an economist cited in the article for his revisionist reading of the [[long-depression|Long Depression]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

In his book *History of Money and Banking of the United States*, Rothbard argues that the Long Depression was only a misunderstood recession, since real wages and production were increasing throughout the period.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) He attributed the era's falling prices to the deflationary [[gold-standard|gold standard]] in the United States in the aftermath of the Civil War.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The article uses Rothbard's argument to support its central claim that deflation under hard money benefited ordinary citizens while harming banks and lenders.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Recommendation as a libertarian thinker

A Badlands Media essay of 5 October 2025, "On Nations and Natural Law," names Rothbard among the "great Libertarian thinkers" who have contributed a wealth of thought and writing on [[natural-law|Natural Law]], remarking that "anything by Murray Rothbard is no waste of time."[2] He is cited there alongside [[lysander-spooner|Lysander Spooner]] as evidence that contemporary readers of the subject are "not without wise guidance or cursed with a blank slate."[2]

## Opposition to fractional reserve banking

A February 2023 Badlands Media feature grouped Rothbard with the Spanish economist [[jesus-huerta-de-soto|Jesús Huerta de Soto]] as [[austrian-school|Austrian School]] detractors of [[fractional-reserve-banking|fractional reserve banking]], noting that both men called for the practice to be outlawed and criminalized.[3] Their argument held that bank money creation causes macroeconomic instability under the Austrian Business Cycle Theory and additionally constitutes embezzlement or financial fraud, legalized only through the influence of wealthy bankers over corrupt governments.[3]
