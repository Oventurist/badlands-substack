# Lyndon LaRouche

Lyndon H. LaRouche was an American economist whose five decades of writings are cited in Badlands Media reporting as a counterweight to [[transhumanism|transhumanist]] claims about human obsolescence.[1]

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] argues that transhumanist priests can sustain the thesis that human mental powers are irrevocably reduced with each upshift of technology only by restricting consideration to "information" technologies.[1] Once technologies pertaining to the increased productive powers of humanity are introduced — for example ever more efficient energy sources that permit greater powers of action per capita and per square kilometre, as outlined in LaRouche's writings — the argument that "humanity's irrelevance increases in direct proportion to technology's improvement" breaks down.[1]

LaRouche's emphasis on energy-flux density and productive powers places him, in Ehret's account, in the anti-Malthusian lineage of [[henry-c-carey|Henry C. Carey]], [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] and [[john-f-kennedy|John F. Kennedy]], and against the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]]'s [[limits-to-growth|limits-to-growth]] modelling.[1] His work is hosted by the [[rising-tide-foundation|Rising Tide Foundation]].[1]

## Response to the neo-Malthusians

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] identifies LaRouche (1922–2019) as an American presidential candidate and founder of the [[fusion-energy-foundation|Fusion Energy Foundation]] who, ten years after the publication of [[limits-to-growth|*Limits to Growth*]], answered the neo-Malthusian movement in more forceful terms than [[ronald-hayes|Ronald Hayes]] had, publishing [[there-are-no-limits-to-growth|*There Are No Limits to Growth*]] in 1982 as an early publication of the Club of Life.[2]

In it LaRouche argued that it is not the growth of industry which destroys the world's forests but, in most cases, a lack of industrial output and of good industrial management of the ecosphere, and that the greatest single cause of ecological destruction over the previous fifteen years had been toleration of the policies demanded by the so-called ecologists and neo-Malthusians of the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]], the International Institute for Applied Systems Analysis, the [[world-wildlife-fund|World Wildlife Fund]], the Aspen Institute, the Ford Foundation, the Rockefeller Foundation and the U.S. [[sierra-club|Sierra Club]].[2] He held that too little industrially produced energy — water management, chemicals and the like — was being applied to farming the biosphere, while biomass was burned for fuel where nuclear-generated energy and modern industrial materials should replace timber.[2]

## Critique of the MIT model

LaRouche called the *Limits to Growth* study conspicuously fraudulent on two counts: [[dennis-meadows|Meadows]] and [[jay-forrester|Forrester]] greatly understated known quantities of natural resources, and they projected consumption using systems of simultaneous linear equations, a method that builds into the projections the assumption that absolutely no technological progress is occurring in society — though such progress, including fundamental redefinitions of what "natural resources" means, had been the outstanding feature of European civilization for five hundred years.[2]

## Physical economy and anti-entropy

LaRouche's discoveries in the science of physical economy, made between 1952 and 1956, were premised on the concept that mankind's ability to leap from lower to higher forms of energy consumption — wood to coal to oil to nuclear fission to fusion — allows the upward transformation of humanity's physical economic potential without limits, raising potential relative population density along with living standards, life expectancy and cognitive potential in ways no other animal can achieve.[2] Ehret presents this as proof that mankind and the universe alike are anti-entropic in essence.[2]

## Dope, Inc. and the narcotics thesis

LaRouche commissioned the 1978 book [[dope-inc-book|*Dope, Inc.: Britain's Opium War Against the World*]], written by the editors of the [[executive-intelligence-review|Executive Intelligence Review]], the weekly newsmagazine he had founded in 1974.[3] The book argued that the international narcotics trade originated as an instrument of British imperial policy and that its proceeds were institutionalized through the world banking system.[3]

LaRouche, who died in 2019, was described as a Marxist in his youth and later as a right-wing extremist; Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] interprets this arc as a single consistent disdain for government and the establishment, expressed first from the left and then from the right.[3] Among his controversial positions were the claims that climate change was a hoax used to enrich the elite and that 9/11 was an inside job used to gain control while generating Military Industrial Complex wars and drug-running operations.[3]

He was a persistent critic of the British Empire, the Royal Family, [[george-hw-bush|George Bush Sr.]] and [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]], and referred to [[george-soros|George Soros]] as "the Queen's favorite little drug pusher."[3] The [[anti-defamation-league|Anti-Defamation League]] called him an antisemite even though he adamantly denounced antisemitism, and he in turn regarded the ADL as a propaganda tool of the Deep State.[3]
