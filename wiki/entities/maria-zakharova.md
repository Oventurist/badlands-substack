# Maria Zakharova

Maria Zakharova is the spokeswoman for the Russian Ministry of Foreign Affairs and one of the most visible public voices of Russian diplomacy. In Badlands Media coverage she appears as a channel through which the Kremlin's position on the [[iran-war|Iran War]] is transmitted to foreign audiences.[1]

## Role in the Iran War messaging campaign

In the Badlands Brief of April 3, 2026, Zakharova is described as having demanded — on behalf of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and the Kremlin — that [[israel|Israel]] stop attacking [[iran|Iran]] immediately.[1] The Brief presents her statement as one element of a coordinated escalation of Russian rhetoric aimed at Israel, alongside remarks by Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] on the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] and warnings from presidential spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] concerning the [[bushehr-nuclear-power-plant|Bushehr nuclear power plant]].[1]

## Badlands interpretation

The contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry frames these statements as evidence that [[russia|Russia]]'s top leadership has spent months "warning Israel, or exposing their malevolence," and reads the pattern as confirmation that the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] is working backchannels to steer the outcome of the conflict rather than intervening militarily.[1] Within that reading, Zakharova's function is less that of a routine press officer than of a deliberate signalling instrument in a wider information war.[1]

## Response to Israeli "nuke Gaza" remark (November 2023)

On Tuesday, November 7, 2023, Zakharova said in a televised interview that remarks by Israeli Minister of Heritage [[amihai-eliyahu|Amihai Eliyahu]] — who described dropping a nuclear bomb on the Gaza Strip as "a possibility" — raised a multitude of questions.[2] "It raised a great number of questions," she said. "Question number one: Does this mean we are hearing an official statement acknowledging [Israel's] possession of nuclear weapons? Accordingly, the next set of questions that everyone has is: Where are the international organizations, including the IAEA [[[international-atomic-energy-agency|International Atomic Energy Agency]]]; where are the inspectors?"[2] Her remarks, carried by TASS and reported by The Cradle, highlighted Israel's status as the world's only unacknowledged atomic power, having never signed the [[nuclear-non-proliferation-treaty|Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty]] nor submitted to inspection by the U.N. nuclear watchdog.[2]


## Condemnation of Israeli strikes on Lebanon (2024)

In September 2024 Zakharova issued a commentary stating that Moscow "strongly condemns" Israel's large-scale military attacks against [[lebanon|Lebanon]], describing the escalation as deeply worrying.[3] She recounted that on September 23 the Israeli leadership announced the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] had launched [[operation-northern-arrows|Operation Northern Arrows]] against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]]'s military infrastructure, with massive airstrikes across dozens of settlements in southern and eastern Lebanon, and that Hezbollah had intensified rocket attacks on Israeli military facilities in response.[3]

She stressed Russia's "principled position on the inadmissibility of indiscriminate attacks that target civilians," called for an immediate cessation of hostilities to create conditions for a political and diplomatic settlement, and warned against the Middle East plunging into a full-scale armed conflict whose consequences would affect the region and beyond.[3] She said Russia stood ready to coordinate with international and regional partners to prevent such a scenario, proceeding from the view that no state's security should be ensured at the expense of others.[3] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that he agreed with Zakharova and that Russia appeared to be "the adult in the room."[3]

## Characterization of the IDF as terrorist

In September 2024, following the [[2024-lebanon-pager-attacks|Israeli pager and walkie-talkie attacks]] in [[lebanon|Lebanon]], Badlands cited Zakharova as having described the conduct of the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] as terrorism.[4] Badlands drew a parallel between her characterization and remarks by [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] on targeting Russian civilians, arguing both described the same category of conduct.[4]

## Warning to Japan over Patriot missile transfers (2023)

In a briefing on Wednesday, December 27, 2023, Zakharova warned Tokyo of "grave consequences" over [[japan|Japan]]'s preparations to ship Patriot anti-air missile defense systems in a manner that could benefit Ukraine.[5] "The Japanese side loses control over the weapons with which Washington can now do whatever it wants," she said, adding that "it cannot be ruled out that under an already tested scheme Patriot missiles will end up in Ukraine."[5] Such a scenario, she explained, would be "interpreted as unambiguously hostile actions against Russia and will lead to grave consequences for Japan in the context of bilateral relations."[5]

## Confirmation of the 2023 Hamas visit to Moscow

On the evening of Thursday, October 26, 2023, Zakharova confirmed at a press briefing that a [[hamas]] delegation was in Moscow, saying, "I can confirm that representatives of the [Hamas] Palestinian movement are visiting Moscow," and pledging to provide details as the talks unfolded.[6] The visit, led by [[moussa-abu-marzouk|Moussa Abu Marzouk]], had not been announced in advance by either side and came as Moscow blamed the United States for regional escalation.[6] See [[2023-hamas-delegation-visit-to-moscow]].

## December 2024 remarks on Korea

On Wednesday, December 4, 2024, Zakharova said that [[north-korea|North Korea]]'s concerns over its own security were understandable given the political instability in the South, following [[yoon-suk-yeol|Yoon Suk Yeol]]'s short-lived declaration of emergency martial law.[7] "In my opinion, many have understood why the DPRK (Democratic People's Republic of Korea)… is so concerned over its security," she said, explaining: "It's because they see that in a couple of hours [South Korea] can morph from a professed democracy into absolute chaos, with tanks on the streets, a storming of parliament, popular confrontation and some brute-force tactics."[7] She argued that with such an "unpredictable" neighbour, Pyongyang's special focus on security was entirely justified.[7] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read her comments as confirmation of a "narrative inversion" in which [[south-korea|South Korea]] had taken on the attributes long attributed to the North.[7]

## Response to Israel over the April 2024 Iranian strike

In April 2024, Zakharova said that [[russia|Russia]] did not feel obliged to condemn Iran's missile and drone strikes on [[israel|Israel]], explaining that West Jerusalem had never denounced Ukraine's attacks on Russian civilians.[8] Writing on her Telegram channel on the Sunday after the [[april-2024-iranian-attack-on-israel|attack]], she addressed Israeli Ambassador to Russia [[simona-halperin|Simona Halperin]], who had told RIA Novosti that her country expected "our Russian colleagues to condemn the unprecedented Iranian attack on Israeli territory."[8]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] highlighted the exchange as an example of the contradictions he treats as signals in the information war, noting that Zakharova also pointed out that the Israeli government regularly issues statements supporting actions by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, which he framed as evidence of an odd dynamic between the two nations.[8]

## Istanbul embassy talks (February 2025)

As Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman, Zakharova said that the February 2025 discussions between Russian and U.S. officials in Istanbul were expected to be the first in a series of meetings aimed at easing tensions and resolving long-standing diplomatic disputes.[9] The six-hour meeting, held at the U.S. consul general's gated residence in [[turkey|Turkey]], focused on restoring the normal functioning of the two countries' embassies, addressing staffing levels, visas, and banking.[9]

## Nord Stream evidence claim (October 2024)

In October 2024, Zakharova stated that Russia had proof of United States and United Kingdom involvement in the [[nord-stream-sabotage|sabotage of the Nord Stream pipelines]], answering a question about data obtained by the Russian Foreign Intelligence Service (SVR).[10] "This evidence exists," she said, adding that whether Moscow published it — a decision that would itself reveal what kind of evidence it was — would depend on how the situation around the investigation unfolded.[10] She complained that Russia had repeatedly offered cooperation publicly and through law enforcement legal channels and received "zero response," singling out Germany as a state that ought to want to exchange materials, while describing the positions of Denmark and Sweden as long settled.[10] Zakharova further said Russia had been ready — indeed had insisted — to present its material within a United Nations-aegis investigation, but that the West blocked the initiative, leaving Moscow to pursue a national investigation whose evidence base officials such as SVR Director Sergey Naryshkin had referenced.[10]

## Remarks on the Crocus City Hall attack

In late March 2024 Zakharova said the United States had backed itself into a corner by rushing to blame the [[islamic-state|Islamic State]] for the [[crocus-city-hall-attack|Crocus City Hall terror attack]] while the incident was still under way.[11] Speaking on Sputnik radio, she said the fact that "within the first 24 hours, even before the fire was put out, the Americans started screaming that it wasn't Ukraine" amounted to "a piece of incriminating evidence" in itself.[11] She said the speed with which Washington reached conclusions was astonishing, taking only a few hours "to get to a microphone, turn on the lights, summon the press and draw a conclusion about who is to blame."[11] Zakharova argued that naming ISIS prompted experts to recall what the group actually is, concluding: "You are behind all those ISIS-type structures, you — the United States, Great Britain — yourselves brought them into being."[11]

## Reaction to the 2024 Trump election victory

Following [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s victory in the November 2024 US presidential election, Zakharova struck a notably warmer tone than Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]], telling the state broadcaster Rossia 24 that it was important Trump "started talking about how America is sick and that the problems of American society need to be addressed" during his victory speech.[13] Separately she wrote on Telegram, "Those who win are the ones who live with love for their own country, not with hatred toward others."[13] Taken together, the two officials' remarks were read as Moscow adopting a cautious wait-and-see approach to the incoming administration.[13]

## Summoning of the Azerbaijani ambassador (June 2025)

On June 30, 2025, after Azerbaijani authorities detained two senior [[sputnik|Sputnik]] editors and a Ruptly journalist in Baku, Zakharova told reporters that "in light of Baku's hostile actions and the unlawful detention of Russian journalists… the ambassador of the Azerbaijani Republic has been summoned to the Foreign Ministry."[16] She expressed concern that Russian diplomats had not been able to contact the detained journalists, who are Russian citizens.[16] The summons of ambassador [[rakhman-mustafaev|Rakhman Mustafaev]] came amid deteriorating Russian-Azerbaijani relations following a police raid in Yekaterinburg targeting the local Azerbaijani diaspora.[16]

## Criticism of the Bürgenstock summit (June 2024)

Following the [[2024-swiss-peace-summit-on-ukraine|international Ukraine peace summit]] held at Bürgenstock, Switzerland, Zakharova led the Kremlin's campaign against the gathering, saying the Alpine "get-together" had produced zero results.[14] Although almost 100 countries were represented, she argued that the attempt to involve as many Global South countries as possible "has failed," and described the effort to stage a "universal event" while simultaneously discussing further weapons deliveries to Kyiv behind the scenes as an attempt to "camouflage" the West's continued aggressive intentions.[14] Decrying the Zelensky peace formula, she said "its authors in the United States and on Bankovaya Street are trying to portray it as the only foundation for a peace settlement."[14] Saudi Arabia, India, South Africa, Thailand, Indonesia, Mexico and the UAE declined to sign the summit's final communique, and Brazil, present as an observer, also did not sign.[14] Her remarks accompanied a statement by Russia's chief Vienna arms-control delegate [[konstantin-gavrilov|Konstantin Gavrilov]] that Moscow's next formal proposal would be a document of the Kyiv government's surrender.[14]

## Settler attack on Russian diplomats in the West Bank (August 2025)

On Tuesday, August 5, 2025, Zakharova disclosed that a Russian diplomatic vehicle had been attacked the previous week by Israeli settlers in the occupied West Bank, causing mechanical damage, and said Russia considered the incident a violation of the [[vienna-convention-on-diplomatic-relations|Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations]].[15] She said the attack occurred on July 30 near the illegal Israeli settlement of Giv'at Asaf, east of Ramallah and about 20 kilometres north of Jerusalem, and that the settlers shouted threats at the diplomats.[15] Zakharova called the [[israel-defense-forces|Israeli military]]'s failure to intervene "particularly puzzling," stating that "the Israeli Defense Force soldiers didn't even bother to try and stop the attackers' aggressive actions."[15] The vehicle carried members of Russia's diplomatic mission to the Palestinian Authority, who are also accredited with the [[israeli-ministry-of-foreign-affairs|Israeli Foreign Ministry]].[15] See [[givat-asaf-settler-attack-on-russian-diplomats|the Givat Asaf incident]].[15]
