# Zbigniew Brzezinski

Zbigniew Brzezinski was the United States National Security Advisor during the late 1970s and a central architect of American Cold War strategy in the Middle East and Central Asia. He is the father of television host Mika Brzezinski, referred to in Badlands commentary as "Morning Mika."[1]

## Role in Deep State chronology

Badlands Media's reconstruction of 1978 places Brzezinski at the hinge of several simultaneous operations: he served as National Security Advisor while the Iran-Contra apparatus was taking shape, while the [[cia|CIA]] was arming and training mujahideen in Afghanistan against the Soviets through [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]], and while Washington prepared to depose the Shah of Iran.[1] The Badlands account attributes the move against the Shah to his growing criticism of Israel following the Yom Kippur War and the coercive petrodollar arrangement struck with Saudi Arabia in 1974.[1]

That petrodollar arrangement was negotiated by Henry Kissinger, whom the Badlands narrative describes as Brzezinski's "master."[1] The same account holds that a year afterward the CIA murdered the Saudi King, who had been personally endeared to and inspired by John F. Kennedy.[1] See [[petrodollar]].

## Significance in the Venezuela analysis

Brzezinski's tenure is invoked in Badlands commentary as the marker of "the most critical time period in Deep State history," used to cast suspicion on Venezuelan opposition figure [[edmundo-gonzalez|Edmundo González]], who served as Venezuela's ambassador in Washington in 1978 and was therefore, in that reading, an operative resident in the capital during Brzezinski's ascendancy.[1]

## Named among the "Four Horsemen of the Climate Cult"

Badlands commentary in February 2025 on the [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]]'s withdrawal from the [[network-of-central-banks-and-supervisors-for-greening-the-financial-system|NGFS]] cited commentator [[warhamster|WarHamster]] naming Brzezinski as one of the "Four Horsemen of the Climate Cult," alongside [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]], [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] and [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]].[2] The piece argued that since the 1970s these figures and their acolytes had sought to kill the human spirit through psychological warfare built on the Malthusian scarcity thesis.[2]

## The Grand Chessboard and the Russia-China-Iran alliance

Badlands commentary in March 2025 invoked Brzezinski's 1998 book *The Grand Chessboard* as having set out the globalist vision for the future of the Middle East, arguing that Brzezinski identified an alliance between [[russia|Russia]], [[china|China]] and [[iran|Iran]] as the one development capable of threatening the globalist agenda, and therefore to be stopped at all costs.[3] Contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that with the launch of the trilateral Security Belt-2025 naval exercise near Iran's Chabahar Port, "we are now living in Big Z's worst nightmare, as that unholy alliance has now officially emerged," coinciding with Washington and Tel Aviv saber-rattling about war with Iran.[3] See [[maritime-security-belt|the exercise series]].[3]

## Operation Cyclone and bin Laden

Badlands' July 2024 commentary described [[operation-cyclone|Operation Cyclone]] as Brzezinski's 1979 [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] plan to arm the mujahideen in Afghanistan against the Soviets, under which he recruited the Saudi national [[osama-bin-laden|Osama bin Laden]], provided him with the best available military training and weapons, and gave him the resources to build a network of fighters drawn from across the Middle East.[4] The article noted that Brzezinski is the father of broadcaster Mika Brzezinski and referenced a 1981 photograph of Brzezinski together with bin Laden.[4]

## The Grand Chessboard and the Middle East

Badlands commentary attributes much of the alleged NeoCon and globalist design for the Middle East to Brzezinski's 1998 book [[grand-chessboard|The Grand Chessboard]], which is described as laying out the region's future as the establishment envisioned it.[5] On that reading, the plan was to build Iran into a legitimate military power and then trigger a war between the Sunni and Shia branches of Islam, drawing in the United States, NATO, and Russia — a sequence characterized as "the recipe for World War 3."[5]

The analysis stresses that Brzezinski explicitly identified a triumvirate alliance between Iran, Russia, and China as the single development that could defeat the plan, and stated that it must be prevented at all costs.[5] It also notes that neither [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] nor his father appears in the book, and that Saudi Arabia scarcely figures in its projected future at all — an omission presented as the reason MBS's rise upended the design.[5]

## Between Two Ages and the technetronic elite

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] calls Brzezinski the "neo-con whisperer" and a close friend of [[francis-fukuyama|Francis Fukuyama]], noting that he had defined the new technocratic elite two decades before the [[the-end-of-history|End of History]] thesis in his 1970 manifesto *Between Two Ages: America's Role in the Technetronic Age*.[6] There Brzezinski wrote that the coming era would see "the gradual appearance of a more controlled society," one "dominated by an elite, unrestrained by traditional values."[6]

Asked whom these specialists would represent, Ehret answers from the book itself: "The nation-state as a fundamental unit of man's organized life has ceased to be the principal creative force: International banks and multi-national corporations are acting and planning in terms that are far in advance of the political concepts of the nation-state."[6] In Ehret's reading this post-nation-state "masterclass" operated through both left and right mainstream parties across the Trans-Atlantic, using "noble lies" and psychological warfare to keep populations fearful, ignorant and complacent.[6]

## Architect of Operation Cyclone

Badlands Media's September 2024 retrospective casts Brzezinski as the quintessential high-ranking deep-state operative, often referred to as the "Democrat counterpart to Kissinger," a member and product of the international think-tank class and one of only 46 people known to have attended both [[bilderberg]] and [[le-cercle|Le Cercle]] meetings.7 With the blessing of the Bilderberg Group, he and [[david-rockefeller|David Rockefeller]] founded the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]], a body created to coordinate imperialist strategy between Washington, Western Europe and Japan, whose membership ranged from Antony Blinken to Jeffrey Epstein.7 The late author Jim Marrs observed that President Carter's administration "was so filled with members of the Trilateral Commission that conspiracy researchers had a field day."7

It was Brzezinski who convinced [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]] of the importance of funding the Afghan resistance, after Pakistani intelligence officials privately lobbied Washington for assistance.7 In a 1998 interview with *Le Nouvel Observateur* he conceded that, contrary to the official version of history, Carter signed the first directive for secret aid to opponents of the pro-Soviet regime in Kabul on July 3, 1979 — before the Soviet invasion of December 24, 1979.7 Once the Soviets took the bait, Brzezinski traveled to the Khyber Pass, where he was recorded giving a religiously charged pep talk to Pashtun rebels in early 1980.7

Asked in 1998 whether he regretted an operation that had indisputably led to the creation of the [[taliban]], Brzezinski replied: "What is more important in world history? The Taliban or the collapse of the Soviet empire? Some agitated Moslems or the liberation of Central Europe and the end of the Cold War?"7 He had predicted that Afghanistan would become a "Vietnamese quagmire" for Moscow, and the [[soviet-afghan-war|war]] did indeed strain the Soviet economy until the last troops withdrew in early 1989.7

## Between Two Ages and the technetronic society

Badlands Media describes Brzezinski as a Deep State politician whose seminal work *Between Two Ages: America's Role in the Technetronic Era* developed the concept of a "technetronic society," in which technology and control over it play a central role in shaping social and political dynamics.[8] The article stresses that Brzezinski did not originate the idea of [[technocracy|technocracy]] but built upon a concept that had already captivated the ruling class for half a century — traceable to the 1930s promotion of technocracy by [[william-randolph-hearst|Randolph Hearst]] and [[nicholas-murray-butler|Nicholas Murray Butler]].[8]

His legacy, the article argues, is carried on today by figures such as [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]], [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]] and [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]], though the author doubts even Brzezinski could have fathomed where the technology would ultimately go.[8]

## Father of Trilateralism

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 2" credits Brzezinski, rather than [[david-rockefeller|David Rockefeller]], with originating the idea for the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]], which he developed while head of the Russian Studies Department at [[columbia-university|Columbia University]] and while researching closer cooperation between Europe, North America and Asia at the [[brookings-institution|Brookings Institution]].[9] In the early 1970s he argued in the [[council-on-foreign-relations|CFR]] journal *Foreign Affairs* that "a new and broader approach is needed—creation of a community of the developed nations," proposing "a council representing the United States, Western Europe, and Japan, with regular meetings of the heads of governments as well as some small standing machinery."[9]

Less than a year later he published *Between Two Ages: America's Role in the Technotronic Era*, which forecast a society "shaped culturally, psychologically, socially, and economically by the impact of technology and electronics," and stated that "regionalization is in keeping with the Tri-Lateral Plan which calls for a gradual convergence of East and West, ultimately leading toward the goal of one world government. National sovereignty is no longer a viable concept."[9] He further anticipated "movement toward a larger community by the developing nations . . . through a variety of indirect ties and already developing limitations on national sovereignty," suggested such a community be financed by "a global taxation system," and reasoned that "though the objective of shaping a community of developed nations is less ambitious than the goal of world government, it is more attainable."[9]

The proposal was presented to the [[bilderberg-group|Bilderberg group]] at Knokke-Heist, Belgium, in April 1972, where financiers were preoccupied with Nixon's devaluation of the dollar, import surcharges, the emerging détente with China, deteriorating relations with Japan, and the energy shock driven by OPEC price increases.[9] Organizing began that July at Kykuit, the Rockefeller estate at Pocantico Hills, and the Commission was formally founded on July 1, 1973, with Rockefeller as chairman and Brzezinski as founding North American director.[9]

## Recruitment of Carter and the 1977 administration

Brzezinski personally recruited Georgia governor [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]] into the Trilateral Commission in 1973, and after Carter won the presidency the group became the subject of extensive mainstream debate.[9] The *Washington Post* noted in January 1977 that the president-elect, vice-president-elect Walter Mondale, and the incoming secretaries of State, Defense and Treasury were all Commission members, as was Brzezinski himself, "a former Trilateral director and Carter's national security advisor."[9] Sutton and Wood's *Trilaterals Over Washington* described him as "a brilliant prognosticator of one-world idealism" whose books served as "policy guidelines" for the CFR, and noted that he served as the Commission's executive director from 1973 until late 1976, when Carter appointed him assistant to the president for national security affairs.[9]

His conduct in office drew direct criticism: Secretary of State Edmund Muskie charged that Brzezinski was making foreign policy rather than coordinating it, and William Sullivan, the U.S. ambassador to Iran, accused him of sabotaging efforts to ease relations with Tehran after the Shah's departure, complaining that "by November 1978, Brzezinski began to make his own policy and establish his own embassy in Iran."[9] Author Patrick Wood later claimed that [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] was groomed by Brzezinski, who served as his principal foreign policy advisor.[9]
