# Dilyana Gaytandzhieva

Dilyana Gaytandzhieva is an investigative journalist known for documenting the scale of the United States military's overseas biological laboratory network.[1]

In 2018 she published research describing the [[pentagon|Pentagon's]] multibillion-dollar budget sustaining bioweapons laboratories in 25 nations, plus 11 facilities within the United States itself.[1] Her reporting placed the expansion of this complex in the context of the December 2001 weaponized anthrax attack that killed five Americans, an event used to justify a steep increase in biological warfare spending — from roughly $5 billion when [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney's]] Bioshield Act passed in 2004 to more than $50 billion subsequently.[1]

Gaytandzhieva's work is cited by Chinese officials and commentators, including Foreign Ministry spokesperson [[hua-chunying|Hua Chunying]], when arguing that scrutiny of COVID-19's origins should extend to [[fort-detrick|Fort Detrick]] and America's overseas labs rather than resting solely on the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute of Virology]].[1] It is invoked by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as one of five factual pillars in his argument that China is being framed, alongside estimates by Alexei Mukhin, Director General of Russia's Center for Political Information, that 65 American secret bio-laboratories operate across the post-Soviet space.[1]
