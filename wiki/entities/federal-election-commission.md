# Federal Election Commission

The Federal Election Commission (FEC) is the United States regulatory agency responsible for administering and enforcing federal campaign finance law, including disclosure requirements and limits on contributions.

## The 2013 Obama campaign fine

In the April 21, 2026 Badlands Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cites the FEC's enforcement record as part of her argument that small-donor fundraising claims have long masked structural fraud. Quoting POLITICO's 2013 report, she notes: "President Obama's 2008 campaign was fined $375,000 by the Federal Election Commission for campaign reporting violations — one of the largest fees ever levied against a presidential campaign." She contrasts the timing of that conciliation agreement with the 2011–2012 communications campaign that rehabilitated [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s small-donor narrative, arguing that the underlying scandal was litigated and decided while a corrective study was being publicized.

The FEC's role is invoked alongside the statutes said to be implicated by [[actblue|ActBlue]] and [[winred|WinRed]] practices, including 52 U.S.C. § 30122, the Federal Election Campaign Act provision prohibiting contributions in the name of another — the legal core of what the corpus calls [[smurfing|smurfing]].
