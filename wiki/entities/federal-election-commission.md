# Federal Election Commission

The Federal Election Commission (FEC) is the U.S. agency responsible for enforcing federal campaign finance reporting requirements.[1]

## Obama 2008 fine

In January 2013, POLITICO reported that President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]]'s 2008 campaign was fined $375,000 by the FEC for campaign reporting violations — described as one of the largest fees ever levied against a presidential campaign.[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cited the fine as part of a longer arc in which small-donor claims by the Obama campaign were debunked by The New York Times in 2008, re-litigated through 2012 with a corrective study from the Campaign Finance Institute, and quietly settled in 2013.[1]

## Relevance to ActBlue

The FEC's reporting regime sits alongside the Federal Election Campaign Act provision 52 U.S.C. § 30122 (contributions in the name of another), one of the federal statutes cited as potentially violated by [[actblue|ActBlue]] and [[winred|WinRed]] in the ongoing congressional and criminal scrutiny of small-donor "smurfing."[1]

## The Cohen FEC letter (2023)

In March 2023, as the [[manhattan-district-attorneys-office|Manhattan District Attorney's office]] prepared a criminal case against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over the 2016 hush-money payment, a purported 2018 letter from a lawyer for [[michael-cohen|Michael Cohen]] to the FEC circulated widely.[2] The letter stated that Cohen had used his own personal funds to pay Daniels and that the Trump campaign was neither a party to the transaction nor a source of reimbursement — material characterized as "totally exculpatory" and as potentially fatal to the theory that the payment constituted an unreported campaign expenditure.[2]

## Foreign volunteer guidance in the 2025 Mamdani review

In November 2025, FEC guidance became relevant to a [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] review of British MP [[jeremy-corbyn|Jeremy Corbyn]]'s participation in a phone bank for New York City mayoral candidate [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]].[3] While federal law prohibits foreign nationals from contributing money to campaigns or engaging in campaign decision-making, Commission guidelines allow unpaid volunteer work in limited circumstances, and legal experts concluded Corbyn's participation likely did not violate federal law.[3]

## 2024 Trump campaign complaint against Labour

In October 2024 the campaign of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] filed a complaint with the Commission in Washington accusing the United Kingdom's [[labour-party|Labour Party]] of "blatant foreign interference" in the U.S. presidential election, after party volunteers travelled to the United States to campaign for Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[4] The filing called for an investigation into what the campaign termed apparently illegal contributions from Labour to the Harris campaign, placing the agency's jurisdiction over foreign national contributions at the centre of a transatlantic political dispute days before the vote.[4]
