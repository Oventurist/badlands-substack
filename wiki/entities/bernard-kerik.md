# Bernard Kerik

Bernard Kerik is a former New York City Police Commissioner who, in the aftermath of the 2020 presidential election, ran the investigative operation supporting Donald Trump's election challenges.[1]

## Role in the 2020 election challenge

Kerik conducted the investigation for [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]], the former New York City mayor who served as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s lead election challenge litigator.[1] Kerik and a small team working under Giuliani collected affidavits from voters and election observers alleging wrongdoing, commissioned academics to produce data analyses identifying possible statistical voting anomalies, and marked up government documents identifying potential election system vulnerabilities that had worried experts before the 2020 election.[1]

## Production of documents to the special counsel

Just days before Trump's August 2023 indictment, Kerik turned over to Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] a trove of documents showing that the Trump legal team was still conducting a sprawling, police-like probe that had not resolved most of the allegations of 2020 election fraud it had collected before the January 6 Capitol riot broke out.[1]

A lawyer involved in producing the documents told [[just-the-news|Just the News]] that the memos could prove valuable evidence of the state of mind of the former president and his legal team, undercutting the prosecution theory of an intentional fraud conspiracy between Trump and six other unnamed co-conspirators to overturn the 2020 election results.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that because Smith's case rests on the premise that Trump knew his fraud claims were false, the claims themselves will have to be argued in court — a prospect many in the MAGA coalition welcomed as a first opportunity to present election-fraud evidence before a court that had previously dismissed most such cases on procedural grounds.[1] She added that if Trump genuinely believed the claims, his statements would be protected by the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[1]
