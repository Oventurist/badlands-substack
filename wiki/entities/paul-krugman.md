# Paul Krugman

Paul Krugman is an American economist and commentator who for many years wrote a column for The New York Times and who, by early 2025, was publishing instead on his own Substack account.[1] In Badlands-adjacent coverage he appears as an emblem of establishment opinion journalism on public health and economics.[1]

## Criticism over the 2025 public health freeze

When the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration imposed a communications freeze across the federal public health bureaucracy in its first days, Krugman claimed that Trump wanted people to die — a charge the Brownstone Institute rejected outright, noting that he had reserved the "rather extreme view" for his Substack rather than a mainstream outlet.[1] See [[2025-public-health-communications-freeze|the 2025 public health communications freeze]].[1]

Writing for the [[brownstone-institute|Brownstone Institute]], the author recalled that Krugman had been "100 percent for lockdowns and all the rest including the fake science behind vaccine mandates," and that while much of the world was under lockdown restrictions he had proclaimed the dawn of the great reset; with that program now reversed, the piece argued, he had "reverted to form."[1]

## Career and academic positions

Paul Robin Krugman is an American economist with an extensive resume, the author or editor of 27 books spanning economic theory, academic textbooks, and general texts for broader public audiences, and the author of more than 200 scholarly works in professional journals.[2] He has been a widely celebrated columnist for The New York Times, Fortune and Slate.[2] At 70 years old in 2023, he was Distinguished Professor of Economics at the Graduate Center of the City University of New York, having earlier taught economics at MIT and then Princeton University, from which he retired in June 2015 while retaining the title of professor emeritus; he also holds the title of Centennial Professor at the London School of Economics.[2]

## Nobel Prize and New Trade Theory

Krugman is considered among the most influential economists in the world, and in 2008 he was awarded the [[nobel-prize-in-economic-sciences|Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences]] for his contributions to New Trade Theory and New Economic Geography.[2] A 2008 Forbes assessment praised the elegance of his models — "lean and thin and transparent," with "all the required parts but no unnecessary fat" — and summarized his insight that countries gain from trade both through scale economies and through consumer access to greater product variety.[2] Krugman combined product differentiation and scale economies with transport costs, the opposing forces giving rise to large population concentrations such as the East Coast corridor of the United States.[2]

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized this body of work as a rationalization of globalism, arguing that the prize committee celebrated it and that "globalism went to the moon" thereafter.[2]

## Revival of the trillion-dollar coin, 2023

In May 2023 Krugman revived the [[trillion-dollar-coin|trillion-dollar coin]] proposal — the theory that a $1 trillion platinum coin could be minted to avert a [[debt-ceiling|debt ceiling]] crisis — and added the claim that doing so would not be inflationary.[2] The account [[wall-street-silver|Wall Street Silver]] mocked the position: "They gave this guy a Nobel Prize ... We can just mint six of the $1 trillion coins and pay all of the govt bills for a year. No taxes needed. It's magic."[2] Ashe in America observed that the proposal hinges entirely on central banks declaring value out of thin air, and that the Nobel Prize in economics lending Krugman his authority was itself established in 1968 by Sweden's central bank.[2] Treasury Secretary [[janet-yellen|Janet Yellen]] had already dismissed the idea as a "gimmick" in January 2023.[2]
