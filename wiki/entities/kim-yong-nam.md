# Kim Yong-nam

Kim Yong-nam was a North Korean official described as the last ceremonial leader of the country, whose death at the age of 97 was reported by state media on Tuesday, November 4, 2025.[1] The Korean Central News Agency reported that he died on November 3 from multiple organ failure following an illness.[1] He was not related to North Korean leader [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong-un]].[1]

## Career

From 1998 to 2019, Kim headed the Presidium of the Supreme People's Assembly, the parliament of [[north-korea|North Korea]], a post regarded as the highest ceremonial position in the country.[1] Before that he served as North Korea's foreign minister from 1983 to 1998.[1] According to APTN, people who met him described him as mild-mannered but firm in his views.[1]

## Abolition of the ceremonial headship

After Kim's retirement in 2019 and subsequent constitutional amendments, the functions once associated with the North Korean head of state were absorbed by the State Affairs Commission, chaired by Kim Jong-un.[1]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said he had researched what a "ceremonial leader" actually is and found it notable that the arrangement ended in 2019, framing North Korea's ceremonial figures as puppet leaders now deceased.[1]
