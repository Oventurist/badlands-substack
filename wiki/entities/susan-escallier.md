# Susan Escallier

Susan Escallier is a US Army Brigadier General who served as the convening authority for the military commissions at Guantanamo Bay and who, in July 2024, signed plea agreements with three prisoners accused of plotting the September 11, 2001 attacks.[1]

## Plea agreements and their reversal

The agreements Escallier approved covered [[khalid-sheikh-mohammed|Khalid Sheikh Mohammed]], Walid bin Attash and Mustafa al-Hawsawi, and would have removed the possibility of death sentences in their cases.[1] Two days after the deals were signed, then–Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]] voided them and reinstated the death-penalty prosecutions.[1] In July 2025 the [[dc-circuit-court-of-appeals|D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals]] ruled that the agreements Escallier had entered into were not binding on the government, holding that the Secretary of Defense possessed indisputable legal authority to withdraw from them.[1]

## Removal from the September 11 case

On July 31, 2024, Escallier — by then a retired brigadier general serving as the senior Defense Department official responsible for military commissions — signed pretrial agreements with [[khalid-sheikh-mohammed|Khalid Sheikh Mohammed]], [[walid-bin-attash|Walid bin Attash]] and [[mustafa-al-hawsawi|Mustafa al-Hawsawi]] that exchanged guilty pleas for sentences of at most life in prison.[2] On Friday, August 2, Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd J. Austin III]] overruled her, issuing a memorandum relieving her of oversight of the capital case, assuming direct authority himself, and withdrawing from all three agreements — a step that effectively reinstated the matter as a death-penalty prosecution.[2] Austin left Escallier in charge of [[guantanamo-bay|Guantánamo]]'s other cases, and wrote that because of the stakes involved, "responsibility for such a decision should rest with me."[2]
