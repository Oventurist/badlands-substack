# Vivek Ramaswamy

Vivek Ramaswamy is an American entrepreneur and political figure cited as a foil in the Badlands Media essay A New Hollywood. [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] writes that, "unlike Vivek Ramaswamy," he does not believe most young children should be dreaming of someday writing software code or winning math contests.

Carlson's counter-position is that young children should dream "about being brave and selfless" — pulling a person out of a burning building, fighting off an invading army, standing up for someone being bullied, or scoring a 90-yard touchdown — without ruling out later careers at Microsoft or Google. The disagreement is presented not as a dispute about the value of technical achievement but about the proper imaginative life of a six-year-old, who in Carlson's view "should still be dreamers and not overly pragmatic."

## 2026 Ohio gubernatorial primary

On May 5, 2026, Ramaswamy won the Republican primary for Governor of Ohio and was widely expected to win the general election. The Badlands writer Ashe in America summarized the arc — a presidential run, a brief stint hyping DOGE, then a pivot to the Ohio governorship — with characteristic skepticism, remarking that "elections are still fake" and that with Ramaswamy as chief executive of the Buckeye State they "might be even more fake than we thought." Her verdict was a single word: "Accelerate."

The same primary night saw Trump-endorsed challengers unseat at least five incumbent Republican Indiana state senators who had opposed a Trump-backed [[mid-decade-redistricting]] push in the 2025 legislative session — Blake Fiechter, Tracey Powell, Michelle Davis, Brian Schmutzler and Trevor De Vries, with Jeff Ellington taking an open seat. The results were read nationally as a measure of [[donald-trump]]'s hold on Republican primary voters heading into the [[2026-united-states-midterm-elections]].
