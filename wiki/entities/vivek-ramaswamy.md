# Vivek Ramaswamy

Vivek Ramaswamy is the American political figure invoked in "A New Hollywood" as a foil on the question of what children should aspire to.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] writes that, unlike Ramaswamy, he does not believe most young children should be dreaming of someday writing software code or winning math contests.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He argues instead that young children should dream about being brave and selfless — pulling a person out of a burning building, fighting off an invading army, standing up for someone being bullied, or scoring a 90-yard touchdown.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) Carlson clarifies that this does not mean children should never aspire to work at Microsoft or Google, nor that they must become firefighters, police officers, soldiers or NFL players; the point is that at six years old, children should still be dreamers and not overly pragmatic.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He summarizes the position by saying young boys should aspire to be Batman, not an accountant.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## Ohio gubernatorial run

Ramaswamy won the Republican primary for Governor of Ohio in May 2026 and is expected to win the general election.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] summarized his trajectory as running for president, spending "five minutes hyping doge," and then deciding to run for governor of Ohio.[2] She tempered the expectation of victory with the observation that "elections are still fake," suggesting that with Ramaswamy as the ostensible chief executive of the Buckeye State they might be even more so, and closed with the accelerationist one-word verdict, "Accelerate."[2] See [[2026-ohio-gubernatorial-election|2026 Ohio gubernatorial election]].[2]

## H-1B controversy (December 2024)

As one of the twin heads of the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] alongside [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], Ramaswamy appeared at the end of December 2024 to call for expansion of the [[h-1b-visa-program|H-1B visa program]], which allows employers to use foreign labor when they cannot find qualified Americans.[3] The position put him at odds with [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] and a host of prominent America Firsters who all but called for ending the skilled foreign worker program, producing a week of public infighting within Trump World described as a MAGA "civil war."[3] Columnist David Marcus argued the episode was healthy politics, with both sides listening, compromising and arriving at clearer positions for the Republican Party.[3]

## DOGE co-chairmanship (2024)

In November 2024, Ramaswamy and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] were named co-chairs of a new government efficiency panel, the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]], and announced that they would identify "thousands" of federal regulations for President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to eliminate — a step they argued would justify "mass head-count reductions" across the federal bureaucracy.[4] Writing in a Wall Street Journal op-ed, the pair said they would pursue "regulatory rescissions, administrative reductions and cost savings," working "through executive action based on existing legislation rather than by passing new laws," and citing recent Supreme Court decisions constraining the administrative state.[4] They described themselves as "entrepreneurs, not politicians" serving as "outside volunteers, not federal officials or employees," with a mandate to deliver results by July 4, 2026.[4]

## 2024 campaign: end of TV advertising

In late December 2023, Ramaswamy's presidential campaign stopped spending money on television advertising and had no further TV time booked, according to an ad-tracking firm and the campaign itself.[5] Spending had fallen from roughly $200,000 in the first week of December to only about $6,000 later in the month.[5] The campaign said money would still be spent on advertising, but not on television: press secretary Tricia McLaughlin said the operation was "focused on bringing out the voters we've identified" through addressable advertising, mail, text, live calls and door-knocking.[5] The shift came less than a month before the January 15 Iowa caucuses and the January 23 New Hampshire primary, while rivals including [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] and [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] continued to run TV ads.[5] "As you know, this isn't what most campaigns look like," McLaughlin said. "We have intentionally structured this way so that we have the ability to be nimble and hyper targeted in our ad spending."[5]

Trump responded on Truth Social by sharing the news and predicting that Ramaswamy "will, I am sure, Endorse me. But Vivek is a good man, and is not done yet!"[5] Badlands Media writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the move as a cultural signal that traditional media was dead and that Ramaswamy was going "where the eyeballs are," running an insurgent campaign that disrupted uniparty election-year norms.[5] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] offered a wry counterpoint: "Note to self: never be called 'a good man' by Donald Trump in the midst of an Info War."[5]

## August 2023 surge in the Republican primary

An [[emerson-college-polling|Emerson College]] national poll released in August 2023 showed Ramaswamy tied for second place in the Republican presidential field with [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] at 10 percent each, having risen from just 2 percent in June while DeSantis fell from 21 percent; [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] led at 56 percent.[6] The poll's release coincided with a leaked memo from the DeSantis super PAC [[never-back-down|Never Back Down]] urging the governor to "take a sledgehammer" to Ramaswamy ahead of the first debate.[6] Ramaswamy replied on X that it was "another boring, establishment attack from Super PAC-creation 'Robot Ron' who is literally taking lame, pre-programmed attack lines against me for next week's debate."[6]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentators split on his significance. [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] wrote that Ramaswamy carried "a lot of the same energy that Trump brings to the table," describing him as a self-made man of principle able to articulate complex concepts plainly, and grouping him with Trump and [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|RFK Jr.]] as candidates capable of authentic and trustworthy performances in an era when image-over-substance politics was ending.[6] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] took the opposite view, arguing that "Vivek seems to be the next [[con-inc|Con Inc.]] lily pad now that the DeSantis campaign has run out of juice," with establishment-aligned figures softly defending Trump while "prepping the Ramaswampy shift"; he nonetheless allowed that both DeSantis and Ramaswamy were helping "separate the MAGA wheat from the chaff."[6]

## Proximity to Trump during the 2024 age debate

In February 2024, amid intensified scrutiny of the ages of both [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] described Ramaswamy as the youngest major Republican candidate in history and asked whether Trump's choice to cultivate a public closeness with him represented an opportunity to show he was injecting a youthful source of leadership into his campaign.[7] Esler framed the pairing as a counter-move against an anticipated attempt to project Biden's removal for age onto Trump.[7]

## Ohio Senate seat appointment push (January 2025)

In January 2025, President-elect Donald Trump encouraged Ramaswamy to consider filling Ohio's vacant Senate seat should the post be offered to him, according to two people with knowledge of private deliberations.[8] Ramaswamy had publicly withdrawn his name from consideration in November 2024 after Trump chose him to lead the Department of Government Efficiency panel alongside [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], but the Ohio native reemerged as one of the leading contenders and entered advanced discussions with GOP leaders about the position.[8] The seat became vacant when [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] resigned to serve as Trump's vice president; under Ohio law Governor [[mike-dewine|Mike DeWine]] appoints a replacement for the next two years, with a special election on November 3, 2026 deciding who serves until the term expires in 2029.[8] While the decision rested with DeWine, Trump personally appealed to Ramaswamy to accept the appointment if offered.[8] Badlands contributor Chris Paul was dismissive, characterizing Ramaswamy's qualifications as saying nice things online, attending a few rallies, and being a close friend of JD Vance and his wife.[8]

## 2024 campaign withdrawal and Trump endorsement

Ramaswamy dropped out of the 2024 Republican presidential race on the night of the [[2024-iowa-caucus|Iowa caucuses]] and immediately endorsed [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], urging Republican voters to put an "America First patriot" in the White House.[9] A day later he appeared with Trump at a rally in Atkinson, New Hampshire, where Trump thanked him for the endorsement and Ramaswamy told the crowd, "There is not a better choice left in this race than this man right here. And that is why I am asking you to do the right thing as New Hampshire and to vote for Donald Trump as your next president."[9] The pairing generated the hashtag #TrumpVivek2024 among conservatives on X, who floated Ramaswamy as a running mate.[9] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that although Ramaswamy appeared cozy with Trump in the public narrative, he and [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] had been intended by the narrative-setters as the future of the Uniparty — an arrangement Burning Bright described as already dead.[9]

## November 2023 Miami debate

At the third Republican presidential debate in Miami on November 8, 2023 — held while [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] rallied a short drive away in Hialeah — Ramaswamy used his opening remarks to demand the resignation of [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] chairwoman [[ronna-mcdaniel|Ronna McDaniel]] instead of making the case for his own nomination, also turning his ire on the debate moderators.[10] Referring to the previous day’s off-year elections, he said, "I think there’s something deeper going on in the Republican Party here, and I am upset about what happened last night. We’ve become a party of losers at the end of the day. Is it cancer in the Republican establishment?"[10] He catalogued GOP defeats since McDaniel took over in 2017 — 2018, 2020, 2022 with "no red wave," and 2023 — and offered to yield his time if she would come onstage and resign before GOP voters.[10]
