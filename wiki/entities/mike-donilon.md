# Mike Donilon

Mike Donilon is a longtime political adviser to [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] and one of the principal architects of Biden's 2024 re-election message.[1] Reporting in June 2024 identified Donilon, together with Biden himself, as the author of the campaign's central theory of victory — a strategy resting on voter concern about January 6, political violence, the preservation of democracy, and the personal character of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Role in the 2024 campaign strategy debate

By mid-June 2024 senior Democrats, including some of Biden's own aides, had grown "increasingly dubious" about the Donilon-designed approach, according to [[axios|Axios]].[1] Biden's core inner circle had not lost faith in the strategy, but that left the circle isolated within a party that increasingly believed the election would be decided on other grounds.[1] Polling cited in the same reporting showed that while voters expressed deep concern about democracy, they were most worried about inflation and the economy, and that Biden's support was slipping among Black, Latino, young and union voters, with the democracy message resonating mainly with older voters.[1]

Biden's former chief of staff [[ron-klain|Ron Klain]], who had known Donilon for decades, summarised his own position to Axios with the phrase "In Mike I trust."[1] A Democratic strategist in contact with the campaign was less sanguine, telling Axios it was "unclear to many of us watching from the outside whether the president and his core team realize how dire the situation is right now, and whether they even have a plan to fix it."[1]

## Badlands Media commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] News Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] treated the Axios account as confirmation that Biden's advisers expected January 6 to be the dominant voter concern in November, describing this as the most telling element of what she called "an absolute bloodbath of an article."[1] She linked the Donilon strategy to a broader "the insurrection is ongoing" narrative she had anticipated since the [[anderson-v-griswold|Anderson v. Griswold]] arguments, and argued that the strategy of demonising and criminalising political opponents was the only avenue left to the administration.[1]
