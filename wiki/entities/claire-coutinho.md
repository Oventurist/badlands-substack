# Claire Coutinho

Claire Coutinho is a British Conservative politician who served as the United Kingdom's Energy Secretary, a role in which she publicly warned that Britain risked becoming dependent on foreign suppliers as domestic North Sea oil and gas output declined.[1]

## North Sea decline warning

Speaking on a Wednesday in October 2023, Coutinho warned that Britain would see North Sea oil and gas production halve by 2030, a trajectory she argued would place the country at the mercy of external forces if a [[labour-party|Labour Party]] government took office and refused to license new offshore drilling.[1] She said the United Kingdom would be forced to import up to 80 percent of its oil and gas by 2030, rendering it "subservient to foreign regimes" and "decimating the same people and communities that we need to come with us on this green transition journey."[1]

Addressing the annual Energy UK conference, Coutinho cited new data from the [[north-sea-transition-authority|North Sea Transition Authority]] indicating that the number of offshore UK oil and gas wells had dropped by 83 during 2022, leaving 1,629 active wells compared with 2,052 in 2019.[1] Drawing on the same data, she stated that UK production had fallen to a record low of 38 million tonnes in 2022 and would fall further to 22 million tonnes by 2030 in the absence of new drilling, a supply gap that she said would require a significant and expensive increase in imports.[1]

## Political context

Coutinho's warning was framed against Labour's pledge to halt any new offshore drilling in pursuit of its 2050 climate goals should it win the next general election.[1] Her remarks followed Conservative Prime Minister [[rishi-sunak|Rishi Sunak]]'s decision in late September 2023 to walk back a series of net zero emissions targets and to green-light [[equinor|Equinor]]'s North Sea Rosebank field, one of the largest new UK oil and gas projects in recent years, on energy security grounds.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] treated Coutinho's statement as an example of what he termed "Exposure" rather than "Disclosure" — the gradual public discrediting of governing philosophies through visible crisis rather than through documentary evidence — arguing that the green movement and open borders were being exposed as destabilizing to would-be independent nation states, and that governments were being forced to walk back such programs one by one.[1]
