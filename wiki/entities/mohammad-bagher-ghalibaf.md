# Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf

Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf is the Speaker of the Iranian Parliament and one of the signatories to the June 2026 US–[[iran|Iran]] agreement.[1]

## Role in the 2026 agreement

According to the [[white-house|White House]], President [[donald-trump|Trump]], Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], and Ghalibaf digitally signed the agreement on a Sunday following negotiations aimed at ending the recent conflict.[1] Administration officials described the deal as a pathway toward regional de-escalation and a broader restructuring of Middle East security arrangements.[1] American Jewish organizations and Israeli commentators voiced concern that key details of the framework remained unclear, particularly reports that sanctions relief and security guarantees might be included.[1]

## Terms of the memorandum

Later reporting described the memorandum virtually signed by Ghalibaf, [[donald-trump|Trump]] and Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] as providing for the lifting of the U.S. naval blockade on the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] and Iranian ports, free transit through Hormuz for an initial 60-day period, and the launch of nuclear negotiations, with a formal signing scheduled for that Friday in Geneva.[2] Trump emphasized that [[iran|Iran]] had agreed never to acquire a nuclear weapon and characterized Iranian leaders as rational partners in the process.[2]

## Post-signing profile in US politics

Following the early remote signing of the [[us-iran-memorandum-of-understanding|memorandum of understanding]] in June 2026, commentator [[ben-shapiro|Ben Shapiro]] publicly objected to rumors that Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] was planning a photo opportunity with Ghalibaf.[3] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded that he hoped President [[donald-trump|Trump]] would go further and invite the Ayatollah to the White House for a "World Peace Summit."[3] Addressing videos of Ghalibaf leading chants of "Death to America," the same commentary noted that Ghalibaf and [[ali-khamenei|the Ayatollah]] have both said on multiple occasions that the chants are directed specifically at America's corrupt political elite rather than at the American people, whom Iranian leadership describes as unfairly oppressed by their own government.[3]
