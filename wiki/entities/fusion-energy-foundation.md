# Fusion Energy Foundation

The Fusion Energy Foundation was an organization founded by American presidential candidate [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]] (1922–2019), identified by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] as part of the institutional response to the neo-Malthusian movement of the 1970s.[1]

## Role in the anti-Malthusian counterattack

Ten years after the publication of [[limits-to-growth|*Limits to Growth*]], LaRouche — the Foundation's founder — replied to the neo-Malthusians in more forceful terms than [[ronald-hayes|Ronald Hayes]] had, publishing *There Are No Limits to Growth* in 1982 as an early publication of the Club of Life.[1] In it he argued that it is not the growth of industry which destroys the world's forests but, in most cases, a lack of industrial output and of good industrial management of the ecosphere, and that the greatest single cause of ecological destruction over the preceding fifteen years had been toleration of the policies demanded by the so-called ecologists and neo-Malthusians of the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]], the International Institute for Applied Systems Analysis, the [[world-wildlife-fund|World Wildlife Fund]], the Aspen Institute, the Ford Foundation, the Rockefeller Foundation and the U.S. [[sierra-club|Sierra Club]].[1]

The Foundation's programmatic outlook — reflected in LaRouche's argument that too little industrially produced energy in the form of water management and chemicals is applied to farming the biosphere, and that nuclear-generated energy and modern industrial materials should replace biomass fuel and timber — expressed the physical-economy doctrine LaRouche had developed between 1952 and 1956, in which humanity's leaps from lower to higher energy flux densities raise its potential relative population density without limit.[1]
