# Saint Paul

Saint Paul is the capital of Minnesota and, with [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]], one half of the Twin Cities metropolitan area targeted by the federal immigration enforcement campaign [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]].[1]

## Role in Operation Metro Surge litigation

Federal agents from [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] and [[customs-and-border-protection|CBP]] conducted arrests, raids and enforcement actions across the Minneapolis–Saint Paul metro area and statewide beginning in December 2025, with DHS reporting more than 10,000 arrests by January 19, 2026.[1] On January 12, 2026, the city of Saint Paul joined Minneapolis and Minnesota Attorney General [[keith-ellison|Keith Ellison]] in filing a federal lawsuit (Case No. 0:26-cv-00190) against the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] and federal officials, including Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]] and ICE and CBP officials.[1] The complaint alleged that the operation and its deployment of armed agents violated constitutional rights, and sought declaratory relief, a temporary restraining order and an injunction against certain enforcement tactics.[1]

Saint Paul was also among the communities addressed by the "ICE Out of Minnesota: Day of Truth and Freedom" statewide strike and economic blackout called for January 23, 2026 by a coalition of faith leaders, community groups and labor unions.[1]

## Federal sanctuary lawsuit, September 2025

St. Paul was sued on 29 September 2025 by the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], together with the state of [[minnesota|Minnesota]] and [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]], over [[sanctuary-cities|sanctuary city]] policies.[2] Like Minneapolis, St. Paul maintains a city ordinance prohibiting cooperation with federal immigration enforcement, and the [[doj-lawsuit-against-minnesota-sanctuary-policies|34-page complaint]] asked a judge to strike those provisions as unconstitutional.[2]

## 2025 cyberattack

In July 2025 the city detected a hacker breach of its systems, and by August stolen St. Paul data had been posted online while thousands of municipal employees queued at Roy Wilkins Auditorium for in-person password resets.[3] The FBI, Homeland Security and the [[minnesota-national-guard|Minnesota National Guard]] assisted the city in scouring and restoring its systems; see [[2025-st-paul-cyberattack|2025 St. Paul cyberattack]].[3]
