# Dianne Feinstein

Dianne Feinstein was the incumbent United States Senator from California whose seat became the focus of an early and unusually public Democratic succession fight in January 2023.[1] Aged 89 in early 2023, she was the oldest sitting member of the upper chamber and the longest-serving senator from her state, having first been elected to the Senate in 1992 after a public career spanning more than 50 years.[2]

## Retirement announcement (February 2023)

On Tuesday, February 14, 2023, Feinstein announced in a statement that she would retire from Congress at the end of 2024, closing out three decades in the Senate.[2] "I am announcing today I will not run for reelection in 2024 but intend to accomplish as much for California as I can through the end of next year when my term ends," she said.[2] A number of House Democrats had already announced 2024 campaign bids for her job, including Representatives [[katie-porter|Katie Porter]] and [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]], while Representative [[barbara-lee|Barbara Lee]] had also expressed interest in the race.[2]

## 2024 Senate seat and the Schiff challenge

On Thursday, January 26, 2023, California Representative [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] launched a 2024 campaign for the United States Senate, a move reported as potentially setting up a Democratic primary challenge against Feinstein, who at that point remained the incumbent and had not resolved the question of her own re-election.[1] Coverage framed the announcement as the opening of an "epic Democrat primary" in California, with Schiff moving before the incumbent's intentions were clear.[1]

Schiff's first campaign advertisement leaned on his anti-Trump record rather than on any direct contrast with Feinstein, using a voice-over line — "When a dangerous demagogue tried to undermine our democracy, I wasn't about to let him" — over footage of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributors treated the contest for Feinstein's seat primarily as a story about Schiff rather than about Feinstein herself, with [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] arguing that Schiff's pursuit of "a key seat for California representation in the US Senate" showed how little accountability had followed his role in promoting the [[russiagate-hoax|Russiagate]] narrative.[1]

After the retirement announcement, [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that while many were celebrating her departure, 50 years of damage had already been done and those seeking to replace her might prove far worse than Feinstein ever was.[2] He questioned whether she was stepping down willingly given her age, or being told to step down by what he called the puppet masters to make way for Schiff, and asked whether the Chinese Communist Party had "ended her contract."[2] DeLarme predicted the left and the media would treat her as a saint when, in his account, her legacy was that of a congressional stooge and a leaker who employed a Chinese spy and swindled the public for decades.[2] He also cited her role in the attempt to derail [[brett-kavanaugh|Brett Kavanaugh]]'s confirmation through what he described as the Christine Blasey Ford [[wrap-up-smear|wrap-up smear]], and noted that on a Senate salary of $174,000 a year she had, like [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]], accumulated a fortune he put at $110 million.[2]
