# McCain Institute

The McCain Institute is a US academic and policy institution based at Arizona State University, named after the late US senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] of Arizona.[1]

## Profile

The institute carries the name of a senator described in coverage as a renowned foreign policy hawk who backed the Western-supported change of government that overthrew the Ukrainian government in 2014.[1] Among its programs is the Kissinger Fellowship, named for former US Secretary of State [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]].[1]

## 2025 Kissinger Fellowship

In January 2025 the McCain Institute announced that former Georgian President [[salome-zourabichvili|Salome Zourabichvili]] had been chosen for the 2025 Kissinger Fellowship, a decision announced in a statement issued on a Monday.[1] The appointment drew immediate criticism from Georgia's parliament speaker Shalva Papuashvili, who said Zourabichvili was going back to "the entity that employed her" and compared the fellowship to the academic position previously extended to former Georgian President Mikheil Saakashvili at Tufts University roughly twelve years earlier.[1] Badlands commentary framed the fellowship as evidence that the institute functions as a landing pad for pro-Western officials removed from power abroad.[1]

## Founding

The institute originated at an April 2012 meeting in Sedona, Arizona, at which Sen. [[john-mccain|John McCain]] gathered longtime political allies to discuss his vision for a nonprofit aimed at preserving his political legacy.[2] Attendees included then-Sen. Jon Kyl (R-Ariz.), Sen. [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] (R-S.C.), then-CIA Director [[david-petraeus|David Petraeus]], former U.S. ambassador to NATO Kurt Volker and the actor Ben Affleck.[2] The McCain Institute for International Leadership was formally created in December 2012 with an $8.7 million donation of unused funds from McCain's 2008 presidential campaign — an arrangement the Badlands account characterizes as McCain donating the money to himself.[2]

## Saudi donation controversy (2016)

In March 2016 *Bloomberg* reported that the Saudi government had donated $1 million to the institute's fundraising arm.[2] After a town hall event in Phoenix, McCain distanced himself from the nonprofit, saying, "I'm proud that the institute is named after me, but I have nothing to do with it — except that they use my name just as the Goldwater Institute uses Goldwater's name."[2] The *Washington Post* fact checker examined the claim and awarded it two Pinocchios.[2]

The Post documented that McCain regularly attended institute events and fundraisers, that his wife [[cindy-mccain|Cindy McCain]] served on the institute's Human Trafficking Advisory Council, that many of his longtime political allies sat on the board of directors, and that tax records showed a longtime McCain fundraiser, Carly Eudy, maintained the McCain Institute Foundation's financial records.[2] His spokeswoman Rachael Dean said McCain "intended to convey that he had nothing to do with the solicitation of the Saudi donation," and confirmed he held no position with the institute or role in its governance.[2] *USA Today* had reported in 2014 that companies and foundations, some with business before [[us-congress|Congress]], had contributed at least $100,000 each.[2]

## Badlands interpretation

Because foreign contributions to U.S. election campaigns are banned while donations to politician-linked nonprofits are not, the Badlands account treats the institute as the loophole that McCain's own [[campaign-finance-reform|campaign finance reform]] left open.[2] It cites [[qanon|Q]]'s question — "What is the John M INSTITUTE? Notice any patterns relating to the CF?" — to link the institute to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] as parallel money laundering operations, both allegedly receiving millions from Prince [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Alwaleed bin Talal]] without reporting the true amounts or naming him as the contributor.[2] The article suggests McCain placed allies in control of the financial records precisely to keep such ties hidden.[2]
