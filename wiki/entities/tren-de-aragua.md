# Tren de Aragua

**Tren de Aragua** ("Train of Aragua") is a transnational Venezuelan criminal organization that originated inside a prison in Venezuela's Aragua state and expanded across Latin America and into the United States during the mass migration of Venezuelans driven by the country's prolonged economic crisis. Authorities in several Latin American countries have linked the group to violent criminal activity including extortion, human trafficking, kidnapping, and narcotics smuggling. The United States has designated the gang a terrorist organization.

## US designation and enforcement

By 2026 Tren de Aragua had become a central object of the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's hemispheric counter-narcotics campaign. The group's leader, [[hector-guerrero-flores|Héctor Rusthenford Guerrero Flores]] — known as "Niño Guerrero" — had been charged in federal court in New York with racketeering conspiracy and related offenses, and the [[state-department|U.S. State Department]] had posted a reward of up to $5 million for information leading to his arrest.

In June 2026 President Trump announced that a U.S. military strike had killed Guerrero Flores. Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] said the strike had been carried out earlier that week against a Tren de Aragua compound in [[venezuela|Venezuela]], and characterized the operation as evidence of cooperation between Washington and Caracas against narco-terrorist groups. Venezuela's government confirmed its participation, stating that clashes during the mission in Bolívar state resulted in Guerrero Flores' death.

The strike formed part of a wider campaign against groups the administration links to drug trafficking, which has also included military strikes on vessels accused of smuggling narcotics. The Associated Press reported that at least 207 people had been killed in those operations since the campaign began the previous September.

## Contested origins

Badlands commentary treats the gang's provenance as the crux of the story. Writing as GhostofBasedPatrickHenry, the Brief argued that the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] narrative — that Tren de Aragua was created and directed by Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] — collapses under the fact of Maduro's own government cooperating with the U.S. military to eliminate the gang's leadership. The alternative claim advanced is that the organization was a creation of the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]], stood up to destabilize and ultimately overthrow Maduro.

Maduro himself made this argument publicly. At the January 2025 inauguration of the "Bolivarian Shield 2025" military and police exercises, he described the "trains" — Tren de Aragua and Tren del Llano — as "a construct instrument of policies to attack the people, destabilize our country and facilitate interventionist scenarios to dominate and colonize Venezuela." In the same address he blamed former Colombian presidents [[alvaro-uribe|Álvaro Uribe]] and [[ivan-duque|Iván Duque]], naming them "public enemies number one" of Venezuela's armed forces, and offered to share Venezuela's counter-gang experience with other states.

The Brief connected this to a later statement by Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]], who alleged that his predecessor Duque had collaborated with the American [[deep-state|Deep State]] and intended to use Colombia as a staging ground for an invasion of Venezuela and the ignition of a civil war — a template the Brief compared to the 2011 Syrian conflict and the emergence of [[isis|ISIS]]. The essay closed by asserting that "the number one state sponsor of terrorism in the world is the US State Department."
