# Tren de Aragua

Tren de Aragua is a transnational criminal organization that originated in a prison in [[venezuela|Venezuela]]'s Aragua state and expanded beyond the country's borders during the mass migration of Venezuelans driven by the nation's economic crisis.[1] Authorities in several Latin American countries have linked the group to violent criminal activity across the region, and the United States has designated it as a terrorist organization.[1]

## Leadership and the June 2026 strike

The gang's leader, [[hector-guerrero-flores|Héctor Rusthenford Guerrero Flores]] — known as "Niño Guerrero" — was killed in a U.S. military strike on a Tren de Aragua compound in Venezuela, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] announced in June 2026.[1] Defense Secretary [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] said the strike had occurred earlier that week and described the operation as evidence of cooperation between the United States and Venezuela against what he called narco-terrorist groups.[1] Venezuela's government confirmed its participation and said clashes during the mission in Bolívar state resulted in Guerrero Flores' death.[1]

## Contested origins

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that the episode undercut the [[uniparty|Uniparty]] narrative that Tren de Aragua was created and directed by Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]], noting that Maduro's own government cooperated with the U.S. military to eliminate the gang's leadership.[1] In this framing the gang was most likely created by the [[cia|CIA]] to subvert and overthrow Maduro — a claim Maduro himself made publicly, describing the "trains" (Tren de Aragua and Tren del Llano) as "a construct" designed to destabilize Venezuela and to facilitate interventionist scenarios.[1] Maduro made those remarks at the inauguration of the "Bolivarian Shield 2025" military and police exercises, and blamed former Colombian presidents Álvaro Uribe and Iván Duque, whom he declared enemies of the Venezuelan armed forces.[1]

The Badlands commentary linked this account to statements by Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]], who said his predecessor Iván Duque had collaborated with the American [[deep-state|Deep State]] and intended to use Colombia as a staging ground for an invasion of Venezuela — a pattern the author compared to the creation of [[isis|ISIS]] in Syria in 2011, in which a manufactured villain is used to justify expanded state power.[1]

## Wider campaign

The strike was described by AP as part of a broader Trump administration campaign against groups it links to drug trafficking, including military strikes on vessels accused of smuggling narcotics; at least 207 people had been killed in those operations since the campaign began in September.[1] Guerrero Flores had previously been charged in federal court in New York with racketeering conspiracy and other offenses, and the [[state-department|U.S. State Department]] had offered a reward of up to $5 million for information leading to his arrest.[1]

## 2025 deportation flights

Suspected members of Tren de Aragua were among the Venezuelan migrants deported to El Salvador on the March 2025 flights carried out under the Alien Enemies Act, which triggered U.S. District Judge James Boasberg's criminal contempt probe of Trump administration officials.[2] That probe was ordered ended by a divided D.C. Circuit panel in April 2026.[2]

## Border crossing attacks (December 2024)

A Texas law enforcement memo leaked in late December 2024 reported that knife-wielding Tren de Aragua members were mobbing border crossings at El Paso, Texas, in an attempt to break into the United States, and had said they would attack border guards who tried to stop them.[3] According to the Texas Department of Public Safety memo, 20 of the Venezuelan gang members — armed with blades, tire irons and broken liquor bottles — tried to force their way into the US at a border gate, and another attempt was expected on New Year's Day.[3]

Victor Avila, a retired agent for Homeland Security Investigations, said the gang was becoming increasingly desperate to seed more members into the US before President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] took office: "You're seeing that violence at the border because they know that it's going to change in 27 days. It's going to change. It's going to be different, and they're going to be sought after."[3]

## Murder of Laken Riley

In November 2024, [[jose-antonio-ibarra|Jose Antonio Ibarra]], described in reporting as an illegal alien from Venezuela and a Tren de Aragua gang member, was convicted on all counts of murdering 22-year-old nursing student [[laken-riley|Laken Riley]] in Athens, Georgia, in February of that year.[4] Ibarra had been released into the U.S. interior on parole after crossing the border near El Paso in September 2022 and had been granted a work permit, making the case a prominent example cited by critics of the Biden-era border policy.[4]


## National Intelligence Council assessment (April 2025)

A newly released intelligence assessment found that some Venezuelan government officials "facilitated" the illegal migration of members of Tren de Aragua into the United States "to advance what they see as the Maduro regime's goal of destabilizing governments and undermining public safety."[5] However, the April memo from the [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]], released by the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]], stated that while Venezuela's "permissive environment enables TDA to operate," the [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] regime "probably is not systematically directing Venezuelan outflows, such as to sow chaos in receiving countries."[5] The memo waters down an earlier [[fbi|FBI]] assessment that the Maduro government was specifically directing TdA activities in the United States.[5]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued that the entire premise for the assertion that the Maduro regime assists Tren de Aragua rests on unverifiable sources according to the National Intelligence Council, and that those seeking to pin the gang on Maduro are supporters of a global regime long intent on removing him.[5] Commentary in the brief also noted the claim that Maduro was clearing out prisons and that some gang leaders escaped with assistance from the FBI and the [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]].[5]

Approximately 130 Venezuelan migrants alleged to be gang members were deported to El Salvador on March 15, 2025, aboard aircraft operated by [[globalx|GlobalX]]; a federal judge ruled on May 1 that the reasoning behind the deportations was illegal.[5]


## Aurora, Colorado presence (September 2024)

In late August 2024 rumours circulated that an apartment complex in [[aurora-colorado|Aurora, Colorado]] was being run by Tren de Aragua after a video went viral showing armed men entering the apartments; the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] confirmed to NewsNation that the men in the video were Tren de Aragua members.[6] The City of Aurora confirmed in an X post on August 30 that there was a "small Tren de Aragua (TdA) presence in Aurora" and that the police department had "been taking it seriously," while insisting that Aurora was a safe community and that media had "conflated and considerably exaggerated incidents that are isolated to a handful of problem properties alone."[6] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the episode as a seeded narrative, noting that the mainstream media had claimed a month earlier that [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]]'s disputed victory would flood the United States with Venezuelan criminals.[6]

## San Antonio crackdown (October 2024)

On October 5, 2024, twenty suspected gang members were detained in a predawn sweep of the Palatia Apartments in San Antonio, Texas, under [[operation-aurora|Operation Aurora]], with nineteen facing charges.[7] The [[san-antonio-police-department|San Antonio Police Department]], Texas Department of Public Safety, FBI, Homeland Security Investigations, ATF, and Texas Anti-Gang Unit inspected roughly 300 empty apartments suspected of being used by the gang, and ICE detainers were lodged against those arrested.[7] Police said the initiative grew from intelligence gathered by undercover officers within the Migrant Resource Center.[7]

The gang was founded in Venezuela's state of Aragua, and law enforcement believes it entered the United States mixed in with undocumented migrants; in San Antonio it is thought to be involved in prostitution, cocaine sales, and other violent crime.[7] Badlands contrasted the Texas response with Aurora, Colorado, quoting Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]]'s pledge that "we will not let them use Texas as a base of operations to terrorize our citizens" against Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]]'s August dismissal of the "purported invasion" as "largely a feature of Danielle Jurinsky's imagination" — a month before Aurora police began making arrests.[7] Alleged conduct in both cities included human trafficking, drug violations, and threats against apartment employees.[7]


## April 2025 RICO Indictments

On Monday, April 21, 2025, federal authorities announced charges against more than two dozen people described as associated with Tren de Aragua, covering conduct from robbery and sex trafficking to the murder of women who tried to escape.[8] Prosecutors brought two New York indictments: one against six alleged current TdA members and another against 21 people said to belong to Anti-Tren, a splinter group.[8] The [[justice-department|Justice Department]] said these were the first indictments against TdA under the [[racketeer-influenced-and-corrupt-organizations-act|Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act]], and Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] called the group "not just a street gang" but "a highly structured terrorist organization."[8] The charges were framed as a rebuttal to immigration-rights advocates who argued the government was overselling the TdA threat.[8] Badlands commentary cited [[michael-flynn|General Flynn]]'s statement that TdA is "a Foreign Terrorist organization directly backed by elements inside of the Venezuelan (VE) government," and drew an analogy to [[kilmar-abrego-garcia|Abrego Garcia]] being backed by elements inside the US government rather than by the US government as such — a distinction the commentator called critical when assessing adversarial states.[8]

## Aurora apartment complex takeover (August 2024)

Footage that emerged on August 28, 2024 showed a heavily armed group storming an apartment complex in [[aurora-colorado|Aurora, Colorado]], with one man carrying a high-powered rifle and pounding on a door while others brandished pistols.[9] Local council member Danielle Jurinsky wrote that "A GANG HAS TAKEN OVER several apartment complexes in Aurora!" and told Fox 31 that the building had been overrun by a Venezuelan gang, adding that "this isn't just Americans. Other Venezuelans are being extorted by this gang."[9] Police declined to confirm whether the gunmen belonged to Tren de Aragua, though reporting noted the group had established footholds across the United States, including in Colorado, amid the migration surge overseen by border czar [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]].[9]

## New Mexico harboring case (2025)

In April 2025 former Doña Ana County Magistrate Judge [[joel-cano|Joel Cano]] and his wife [[nancy-cano|Nancy Cano]] were arrested in Las Cruces, New Mexico on evidence tampering charges amid allegations that they harbored [[cristhian-ortega-lopez|Cristhian Ortega-Lopez]], an alleged illegal alien believed to be a member of Tren de Aragua.[10] Ortega-Lopez had been apprehended at the Cano residence in February after being hired for home repairs and later housed in the couple's guesthouse.[10]

## Trump's October 2024 Aurora rally

When [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s campaign confirmed on October 7, 2024 that he would hold a rally at the Gaylord Rockies Resort & Convention Center in [[aurora-colorado|Aurora, Colorado]] the following Friday, it described the city as a "war zone," claiming it had seen an "influx of violent Venezuelan prison gang members from Tren de Aragua."[11] Colorado Governor [[jared-polis|Jared Polis]] rejected that framing, saying Trump would instead find "an incredible city with two straight years of rapidly declining crime."[11]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] countered that Colorado's institutional press was gaslighting residents about migrant crime, pointing to store closures, merchandise locked behind glass and rising car thefts, and cited open records published by the *Denver Gazette* which she said proved the government knew about the gang takeover of Aurora apartment buildings and lied to citizens about it.[11]

## 2025 NYPD sweep

In raids conducted on the night of Tuesday, January 28, 2025, the [[nypd|New York Police Department]] took down ten high-ranking members of the Venezuelan gang Tren de Aragua in a nationwide sweep, with the members either in custody or on the run.[12] Police seized a cache of more than 30 illegal guns, including AR-15 assault rifles and a Glock 9mm modified to fire automatically.[12] The crew had been so brazen about its illegal gun profits that its members posted a photo of a baby — the child of one of those arrested — sitting on a pile of their ill-gotten cash.[12] NYPD Commissioner [[jessica-tisch|Jessica Tisch]] said, "Tren de Aragua is one of the most dangerous gangs in the country, and the NYPD has taken significant action to shut down their operations in New York City," adding that the indictment made clear the department would not let the gang terrorize the streets with illegal guns, assault weapons and dangerous narcotics.[12]

The gang's rapid growth in the United States featured in the debate over [[temporary-protected-status|temporary protected status]] for Venezuelan migrants, which the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration ended that same week.[12]

## Named in the 2025 Maduro reward announcement

Announcing a $50 million reward for information leading to the arrest of Venezuelan President [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] on August 7, 2025, Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] said Maduro "uses foreign terrorist organizations like Tren de Aragua, the [[sinaloa-cartel|Sinaloa Cartel]], and the [[cartel-de-los-soles|Cartel of the Suns]] to bring deadly drugs and violence into our country."[13] The [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]] was said to have seized thirty tons of cocaine tied to Maduro's network, much of it laced with fentanyl.[13]

## March 2025 mass deportation under the Alien Enemies Act

In mid-March 2025 the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] made Tren de Aragua the target of the first modern invocation of the [[alien-enemies-act|Alien Enemies Act]], deporting hundreds of alleged members over the weekend of March 15–16 despite an order from U.S. District Judge [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]] temporarily blocking use of the law.[14] Government lawyers told the court that the statute was being used specifically against members of the gang, described in reporting as a violent Venezuelan organization whose members had reached the United States in recent years and which had also faced raids in Chile.[14] White House press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] said the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] had arrested nearly 300 members of the gang that weekend, and Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] framed the removals as sending "hundreds of violent criminals" out of the country.[14]
