# Wyoming House of Representatives

The Wyoming House of Representatives is the lower chamber of the Wyoming Legislature and, in January 2025, the originating body for a package of election-integrity bills aimed at barring noncitizens from voting.[1]

## Noncitizen voting legislation, January 2025

On Monday, January 20, 2025, the House passed legislation designed to prevent noncitizens from voting in Wyoming elections.[1] Secretary of State [[chuck-gray|Chuck Gray]] commended the chamber's action, describing the bills as "priority number one of our election integrity agenda" and pledging continued work with the Legislature toward "meaningful, conservative reform."[1]

The central measure, HB 156, requires that a "qualified elector" have "been a bona fide resident of Wyoming for not less than thirty (30) days before the date of the election at which he offers to vote."[1] It also directs that county clerks "shall not" register a person to vote if the identification or proof of residence presented "contains any indication that the person is not a citizen of the United States," unless that person also presents proof of citizenship as specified by rule of the secretary of state.[1]

## Reception

The bills were reported by The Federalist as an example of red states moving quickly on election law after [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s second inauguration.[1] Badlands Media commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the reforms needed to be replicated nationwide before the midterm elections, contrasting Wyoming's statutory clarity with the political and legal risks of raising the same issue in Colorado.[1]
