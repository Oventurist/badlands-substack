# The Daily Beast

**The Daily Beast** is an American online news and opinion outlet, described in Badlands commentary as a leftist media property and mocked as "the menstrual rag of record."[1]

## 2024 buyouts and staff collapse

In June 2024 *The Wrap* reported that The Daily Beast was gutting its senior editorial team after implementing voluntary buyouts the previous month, with nearly 70 percent of unionized staffers leaving the outlet. About 25 unionized staffers took the buyouts. Senior staff departing included reporter Justin Baragona, political investigations reporter Jose Pagliery, senior national reporter Pilar Melendez and senior reporter Emily Shugerman. Further layoffs affecting non-union editorial staff were expected later that month.[1]

One person with direct knowledge told *The Wrap*, "We're currently watching the collapse of The Beast," adding "there is no doubt the site won't be able to recover from this." The same source invoked a business maxim: "One of the first lessons of any company: Don't alienate your core customer and piss them off so much they begin to call for a boycott against your firm."[1]

[[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], reporting on the story, situated it within a broader journalism downturn — more than 8,000 job cuts across the US, UK and Canada in the prior year and over 1,000 layoffs in the first half of 2024 across newspapers, online outlets and nonprofit watchdog organizations — while noting that some conservative outlets were thriving in an election year.[1] ZeroHedge attributed reader attrition to the outlet's reporting posture toward [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and recalled that the Beast was among the defendants sued by the repairman who blew the whistle on the [[hunter-biden-laptop|Hunter Biden laptop]] over alleged disinformation.[1]

## Coverage of the Korean DMZ incident

Separately, the outlet was singled out in the same brief for running the headline "Is Kim Jong Un Starting a War to Impress Visiting Friend Vladimir Putin?!" after several dozen North Korean troops crossed the demilitarized zone and exchanged fire with South Korean troops during [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s Pyongyang visit — an episode Badlands writers treated as evidence the outlet was easily duped.[1]

Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] summarized the outlet's predicament tersely: "you don't need lots of employees if you don't have any customers."[1]
