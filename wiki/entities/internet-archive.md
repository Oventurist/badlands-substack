# Internet Archive

The Internet Archive is a San Francisco–based nonprofit dedicated to preserving the record of human activity on the internet, operating out of an old church building and running on an annual budget smaller than that of the D.C. Public Library.[1] It is the largest such repository in existence, and its archival service, the Wayback Machine, preserves more than 900 billion webpages.[1]

## 2024 cyberattack

In October 2024 the organization came under sustained attack. Hackers struck the Internet Archive, leaking the information of millions of users and defacing the site with a message taunting the nonprofit for running on a shoestring budget.[1] To prevent further leaks, the Archive's team took the site — including the Wayback Machine — offline, the first outage longer than a few hours in the institution's nearly 30-year history.[1] Most of the site remained offline a week after the intrusion, and the cyberattack set off what was described as a frenzied race to restore access to the archive's holdings.[1]

Founder [[brewster-kahle|Brewster Kahle]] told the [[washington-post|Washington Post]] that he found it hard to imagine why hackers would target a free repository of digital history, apparently without an agenda or a ransom demand, asking simply: "Why kick the cat?"[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

Badlands Media commentary framed the attack as an attempt by "nefarious entities" to erase the history of the internet, linking it rhetorically to broader efforts to erase historical memory.[1] Commentators highlighted that the Internet Archive was among several groups that preserved deleted tweets by former president [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], a fact the Archive itself noted in 2017, treating that preservation role as a plausible motive for hostility toward the nonprofit.[1]
