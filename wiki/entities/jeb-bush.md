# Jeb Bush

Jeb Bush — whose legal name is John Ellis Bush, from which the nickname "Jeb" is derived as an acronym — is the son of [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] and a former Florida commerce secretary and governor. In the Badlands account he is presented not as a conventional businessman-politician but as a career intelligence asset whose banking, real-estate and healthcare dealings functioned as extensions of his father's networks.[1]

## Recruitment into the CIA and Texas Commerce Bank

According to the Intrepid Report, one of the last acts of George H.W. Bush as director of the [[central-intelligence-agency|Central Intelligence Agency]] was to arrange for his son Jeb to be hired by the international division of [[texas-commerce-bank|Texas Commerce Bank]] as a CIA "non-official cover" officer, or NOC. The international divisions of banks and investment firms were the customary placement for NOCs, and the bank had been founded by the family of [[james-baker|James Baker]], a close friend of the elder Bush who needed only to make a phone call to secure the position.[1]

A letter Jeb Bush wrote to CIA operative Robert Gambino, who helped train him as an expert in overseas banking, was unearthed by the Wayne Madsen Report; no mainstream media outlet has described Jeb Bush as a CIA operative, yet the article argues his first job was an intelligence cover.[1]

## Caracas posting

In 1977, shortly after his father left the CIA directorship, Jeb Bush — fluent in Spanish from an exchange-student stay in Guadalajara — was sent with his Mexican wife Columba to Caracas, Venezuela, to serve as "branch manager" and "vice president" at the age of 24, despite having no banking experience.[1] The article contends he was in reality the CIA's principal financial liaison to the Venezuelan oil industry and the Colombian narcotics cartels, reporting regularly to his CIA "official cover" counterpart posted to the U.S. embassy in Caracas as a State Department diplomat.[1]

In this role he is said to have laid groundwork for the Reagan-Bush administration's 1980s covert war against Nicaragua and leftist guerrillas in El Salvador by establishing banking and money-laundering links between the CIA and the [[medellin-cartel|Medellín]] and [[cali-cartel|Cali]] cartels; cartel money, notably from [[pablo-escobar|Pablo Escobar]], would help finance the Nicaraguan contras in exchange for CIA-supplied weapons. While in Venezuela he disguised Colombian cartel drug revenues as oil-industry revenues of front companies.[1] The article treats [[carlos-andres-perez|Carlos Andrés Pérez]], president during part of this era, as the corresponding political half of the arrangement, noting that Jeb was connected to two later-disgraced, self-exiled presidents — Pérez and [[carlos-salinas|Carlos Salinas]] — which it argues is no coincidence.[1]

## Miami years

After Venezuela, Jeb Bush established his base in Miami, the same city to which Pérez later self-exiled.[1] There he built connections with the Cuban exile community that the CIA had long cultivated.

- [[miguel-recarey|Miguel Recarey]]: Jeb Bush did business with the Medicare fraud suspect and telephoned a federal agency to urge "fair treatment," producing an exemption from the rule capping HMO Medicare enrollment at 50 percent. Recarey's [[international-medical-centers|International Medical Centers]] then collapsed amid forged enrollments and phony billings, leaving Florida to sort through $230 million in unpaid claims. Recarey paid Bush's real-estate company $75,000, a fee some described as payment for the intervention; Bush said it was for real-estate work, and IMC never selected a building he showed.[1]
- [[armando-codina|Armando Codina]]: the Coral Gables Cuban-American developer and early George H.W. Bush supporter gave Jeb his first Florida job, put the Bush name on the company and granted him 40 percent of its profits without Jeb contributing capital. A $1,000 stake in the Museum Tower office building in 1984 was sold in 1990 for roughly $346,000.[1]
- [[mark-guzzetta|Mark Guzzetta]]: when IBM hired the Codina Bush firm to sell its Boca Raton office park — two million square feet on some 565 acres, assessed at $100 million — the property sold in early 1997 for $46.1 million to Blue Lake Ltd., a group including Guzzetta, a Republican fundraiser at whose wedding Jeb Bush was best man and who became finance co-chairman of his gubernatorial campaign. Codina Bush declined to disclose its commission.[1]
- [[richard-lawless|Richard Lawless]]: a 15-year CIA veteran who won state contracts to promote Asian exports while Jeb Bush was Florida commerce secretary, and who later paid Bush commissions for finding real estate for Japanese investors, including $213,000 on a single 1993 deal.[1]

The article contrasts these arrangements with the contemporaneous scrutiny of Hunter Biden, arguing Jeb Bush's 40 percent no-capital stake far exceeded any ten-percent cut.[1]

## Lehman Brothers

For roughly seven years Jeb Bush earned more than $14 million as an adviser to [[lehman-brothers|Lehman Brothers]] and, after its 2008 bankruptcy, to Barclays, which absorbed the firm. As Lehman slid toward insolvency in 2008 he participated in the firm-wide rescue effort known internally as "Project Green"; his portion, "Project Verde," involved persuading Mexican billionaire [[carlos-slim|Carlos Slim]] to make a life-saving investment. The Valukas examiner's report records that on July 5, 2008, Bush reported the meeting had failed because Slim "did not express interest in jv (joint venture) or stock purchase." Slim's spokesman said the two had been friends for twenty years but never did business together.[1]
