# Jeb Bush

John Ellis Bush — "Jeb" being an acronym of his name — is the son of [[george-hw-bush|George H.W. Bush]] and a former Florida commerce secretary and governor. The Badlands Media account presents him as his father's operational "point man," a CIA non-official cover officer whose career in banking, real estate and Wall Street repeatedly intersected with drug cartels and politically connected fraud.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Texas Commerce Bank and the NOC posting

One of the last things George H.W. Bush did as Director of Central Intelligence was to have Jeb hired by the international division of [[texas-commerce-bank|Texas Commerce Bank]] as a CIA "non-official cover" officer, or NOC — the international division being the usual placement for NOCs inside banks and investment firms.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The hire required only a phone call to [[james-baker|James Baker]], whose family founded the bank.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) A letter Jeb sent from the bank to CIA operative Robert Gambino, who had helped train him in "overseas banking," was surfaced by the Wayne Madsen Report; no mainstream outlet has described Jeb Bush as a CIA operative.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Caracas

In 1977, shortly after his father left the CIA, Jeb — fluent in Spanish from a student exchange in Guadalajara — was sent with his Mexican wife Columba to Caracas, Venezuela as a "branch manager" and "vice president" at age 24, despite having no banking experience.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The article asks why a Texas bank would have a Caracas branch at all, and answers that Jeb was unofficially the CIA's main financial liaison to the Venezuelan oil industry and the Colombian narcotics cartels, reporting to his official-cover counterpart posted at the U.S. embassy as a State Department diplomat.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

In that role he is said to have laid groundwork for the Reagan-Bush administration's 1980s covert war against Nicaragua and leftist guerrillas in El Salvador by establishing banking and money-laundering links between the CIA and the [[medellin-cartel|Medellín]] and [[cali-cartel|Cali]] cartels; [[pablo-escobar|Pablo Escobar]] and others would help finance the Nicaraguan contras in exchange for CIA-supplied weapons.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) His technical specialty was disguising Colombian cartel drug revenues as oil industry revenues of front companies — which the article offers as a core explanation for the CIA's and the "shadow government's" closeness to the oil business, [[zapata-offshore|Zapata Offshore]] being the archetype.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) [[carlos-andres-perez|Carlos Andrés Pérez]] was president of Venezuela during this period.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## The Escobar approach

In 1989 the Medellín cartel dispatched an emissary, Vallejo, to Miami to present a negotiated settlement to a lawyer who had previously resolved legal difficulties for cartel member Jorge Ochoa and who was working with a team of corporate attorneys headed by Jeb Bush, then the president's son. The idea was for the lawyer to persuade Bush's son to raise the proposal privately with the president.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The assassination of Colombian presidential candidate Luis Galán in August 1989 helped end any chance of a deal.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Miami: Recarey, Codina, Lawless, Guzzetta

After Venezuela, Jeb based himself in Miami — the same city to which the disgraced Pérez later self-exiled.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) He did business with [[miguel-recarey|Miguel Recarey]] and telephoned a federal agency to lobby for an exemption from the rule capping Medicare patients at 50 percent of an HMO's enrollment, an intervention for which Recarey's company paid Bush's real estate firm $75,000 — a fee Bush maintains was for real estate work, though IMC never selected a building he showed.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

[[armando-codina|Armando Codina]] gave Jeb his first job in Florida, put the Bush name on the company and handed him 40 percent of its profits; a $1,000 stake in the Museum Tower office building in 1984 was sold for about $346,000 by 1990.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The article contrasts this with Hunter Biden's reported ten percent cut, calling Biden "an amateur."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) Codina Bush was hired by IBM Corp. to sell its Boca Raton office park — some 2 million square feet on about 565 acres, assessed at $100 million — which sold in early 1997 for $46.1 million to Blue Lake Ltd., a group including Republican fundraiser [[mark-guzzetta|Mark Guzzetta]], at whose wedding Jeb had been best man; the firm declined to disclose its commission.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

As Florida commerce secretary, Jeb awarded state contracts promoting Asian exports to [[richard-lawless|Richard Lawless]], a fifteen-year CIA veteran, who later paid Bush commissions for finding real estate for Japanese investors — one 1993 deal alone yielding $213,000.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Lehman Brothers

Jeb earned more than $14 million over roughly seven years as an adviser to [[lehman-brothers|Lehman Brothers]] and, after its 2008 bankruptcy, to Barclays.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) During Lehman's 2008 collapse he participated in the firm-wide rescue effort known internally as "Project Green"; his own piece, "[[project-verde|Project Verde]]," involved persuading Mexican billionaire [[carlos-slim|Carlos Slim]] to make a life-saving investment. On July 5, 2008 Bush reported the meeting had failed because Slim "did not express interest in jv (joint venture) or stock purchase."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) Slim's spokesman said the two had been friends for twenty years but never did business together.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## The Broward Federal savings and loan deal

In "Never Let a Crisis Go to Waste," Joe Lange details a Miami transaction he says exemplifies the corruption of the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|savings and loan scandal]].[2] A savings and loan became insolvent after lending Bush and a partner about half the money toward the purchase of a $9 million office building, and the federal government ended up repaying most of the loan; the New York Times described it as an example of the poor lending practices that ruined the thrift industry, while noting it involved no criminal behavior.[2]

In 1985 Bush, then 37, and Codina — through a partnership called 1390 Brickell — bought a Miami office building at that address for $9 million, using a $7 million mortgage from an insurance company and a $4.6 million loan from [[broward-federal-savings-and-loan|Broward Federal Savings and Loan]] of Sunrise, Florida, with the surplus earmarked for improvements and a "reserve account."[2] The Broward loan was acquired by a third partner, [[j-edward-houston|J. Edward Houston]], owner of South Florida Savings, who defaulted when his own thrift went bankrupt.[2]

Broward Federal became insolvent in 1988 at a cost to taxpayers of roughly $235 million, with the government paying more than $4 million to make good the loan on the Miami property.[2] Bush and Codina negotiated a settlement with regulators in which they repaid $505,000 and retained control of the building.[2] Lange argues the terms amounted to a gift: regulators ordered a reassessment valuing the building at only $6.5 million — just under the $7 million mortgage — and the $505,000 came out of the "reserve account" funded by the defaulted loan itself, so the partners paid nothing and gained instant equity.[2] He also notes that Miami, Bush's base, is among the biggest centers of [[real-estate-money-laundering|real estate money laundering]], with over 60 percent of $2 million-plus transactions by international investors done in cash.[2]

Jeb Bush was named by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the [[2023-trump-cpac-speech|2023 Trump CPAC speech]] as one of the establishment figures — alongside [[paul-ryan|Paul Ryan]] and [[karl-rove|Karl Rove]] — whose party the Republicans would "never go back to."[4] Badlands contributor [[brad-getz|Brad Getz]], a longtime Bush family supporter, wrote that watching Jeb Bush — "the candidate whom I expected to get the nod before Trump entered the arena" — be exposed during the 2016 primaries by "someone who wasn't afraid to be real with the American People" laid the groundwork for his own political awakening.[4]


## Miami networks and BCCI

"Profiting From War" quotes a 2004 [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] account stating that in the early and mid-1980s Jeb Bush spent most of his time among ex-Cuban intelligence officers, Nicaraguan Contras and others plugged into the orbit of Miami-area front groups for [[central-intelligence-agency|the CIA]], while also maintaining Middle East connections.[4] Two of his business associates, Guillermo Hernandez-Cartaya and Camilo Padreda, both indicted for financial dealings, were longtime associates of the Middle Eastern arms dealer, [[bank-of-credit-and-commerce-international|BCCI]] investor and Iran-Contra figure [[adnan-khashoggi|Adnan Khashoggi]].[4] Prosecutors dropped the case against the two and a federal judge ordered Padreda's name expunged from the record, though Padreda — a former Miami-Dade County GOP treasurer — was later convicted of fraud over a federally insured housing development that Jeb Bush had helped facilitate.[4] Jeb Bush also socialized with Adbur Sakhia, the Miami BCCI branch chief and later the bank's top U.S. official.[4]

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] characterizes Jeb Bush as a long-serving CIA operative heavily involved in agency drug trafficking and money laundering in Latin America and the Miami area, in scams involving CIA-connected Cuban Americans, and in the [[savings-and-loan-crisis|Savings and Loan scandal]] that transferred taxpayer money through a federal bailout during his father's presidency.[4]
