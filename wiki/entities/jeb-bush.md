# Jeb Bush

John Ellis Bush (born 1953), known as Jeb Bush, is an American politician who served as the 43rd governor of Florida from 1999 to 2007 and was a candidate for the Republican presidential nomination in 2016. In the Badlands Media article [[a-family-affair]], Jeb Bush is portrayed as a central node in a multigenerational network of CIA covert operations, drug trafficking, money laundering, and political corruption spanning Venezuela, Mexico, and Florida.

## CIA non-official cover at Texas Commerce Bank

According to the [[wayne-madsen-report]], Jeb Bush was hired by the international division of [[texas-commerce-bank]] as a CIA non-official cover (NOC) officer at the request of his father, [[george-hw-bush]], through [[james-baker]], whose family founded the bank. A letter from Jeb to CIA operative Robert Gambino, uncovered by the Wayne Madsen Report, is cited as evidence of his CIA training in "overseas banking." Jeb was officially listed as branch manager and vice president of the bank's Caracas, Venezuela, operation at age 24 in 1977.

## Venezuela and Latin American operations

The article claims that while in Caracas, Jeb Bush served as the CIA's main financial liaison to the Venezuelan oil industry and Colombian drug cartels, regularly reporting to a CIA official cover counterpart at the U.S. embassy. He is alleged to have established money-laundering links between the CIA and the [[medellin-cartel]] and [[cali-cartel]], hiding drug revenues as Venezuelan oil industry revenues in CIA front companies. The source ties his presence in Caracas to the presidency of [[carlos-andres-perez]], described as a corrupt leader installed with CIA assistance who presided over oil-boom corruption and later fled to miami.

## Miami network and Cuban exile connections

After his Venezuela posting, Jeb Bush established a base in Miami, building relationships with Cuban exile networks. The article connects him to [[miguel-recarey]], a Cuban-American who ran International Medical Centers, then the largest Medicare fraud case in U.S. history. Jeb allegedly intervened on Recarey's behalf with a phone call to a federal agency, securing an exemption that allowed Recarey's HMO to exceed the 50 percent Medicare enrollment cap. Recarey subsequently paid Bush's real estate company $75,000, which the source describes as payment for the intervention. Recarey fled to Venezuela and later Spain, where he was freed from jail rather than extradited.

## Florida real estate and quid pro quo

While Florida commerce secretary, Jeb Bush gave state contracts to former CIA officer [[richard-lawless]], who later paid Bush a $213,000 commission on a 1993 real estate deal. Through a partnership with Coral Gables developer [[armando-codina]], Bush acquired a 40 percent stake in Codina's company without contributing capital, turning a $1,000 investment in Museum Tower into a $346,000 profit by 1990. The 1997 sale of IBM's Boca Raton office park—assessed at $100 million but sold for $46.1 million to a group including Republican fundraiser [[mark-guzzetta]]—is cited as another example of insider dealing benefiting Bush associates.

## Lehman Brothers and the 2008 financial crisis

According to [[fox-business]], Jeb Bush earned more than $14 million over approximately seven years as an adviser to [[lehman-brothers]], the investment bank whose 2008 collapse triggered the global financial crisis. In 2008, Bush led "Project Verde," an effort to convince Mexican billionaire [[carlos-slim]] to inject capital into the failing firm. Slim declined. The article notes that Slim and the salinas family were close associates, and that Slim had already lost money as Lehman's stock collapsed. After Lehman's bankruptcy, Bush continued as an adviser to [[barclays]], which acquired the firm.
