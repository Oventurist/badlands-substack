# Sarah Ransome

Sarah Ransome is an accuser of [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] whose evidence and deposition testimony formed part of the record in the civil litigation whose documents were unsealed in early January 2024.[1]

## Evidence and testimony

Photographs taken at Epstein's Little St. James Island in 2006 were provided by Ransome and were included in a court filing unsealed on January 8, 2024, before Judge [[loretta-preska|Loretta Preska]] ordered them struck and redacted.[1] In a deposition reported alongside the filings by the New York Post, Ransome described the recruitment methods used around Epstein's operation, saying, "They were really naughty. You know, they took girls from very underprivileged families. They gave them accommodation, they gave them food, gave them money for transportation, you know, private planes, etc, etc."[1]

## 2016 emails to Maureen Callahan

A further tranche of court documents unsealed on Monday, January 8, 2024 included a string of 2016 emails Ransome sent to then-New York Post columnist Maureen Callahan.[2] In them she claimed that an unnamed friend had "sexual relations" with former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at Epstein's New York mansion "on regular occasions," writing that the friend "confided in me about her casual 'friendship' with Donald" and that Trump "kept going on about how he liked her 'pert nipples.'"[2] Ransome also asserted in the emails that she possessed copies of tapes Epstein had made of high-profile associates — Trump, former President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]], [[prince-andrew|Prince Andrew]] and British business magnate [[richard-branson|Richard Branson]] — allegedly having sex with an unnamed woman.[2]

In an email dated October 23, 2016, Ransome retracted the allegations, telling Callahan, "I would like to retract everything I have said to you and walk away from this," and adding that only "bad things" and "pain for my family" would come from going public.[2] The retraction was reproduced in the unsealed filing alongside the original claims.[2]

## Reception in independent media

Writers at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that the descriptions of the released images sounded comparatively benign and that the recurring framing of victims as "young girls" functions as a softening tactic that obscures the involvement of children of all ages, a point they attributed to [[patrick-gunnels|Patrick Gunnels]].[1]

Commenting on the Callahan emails, [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] emphasized the retraction and argued that the reporting mattered less as evidence than as an instance of what he described as a "mass conflation campaign" by the media directed at Trump, intended to redirect the Epstein boomerang toward him.[2]
