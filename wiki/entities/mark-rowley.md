# Mark Rowley

Sir Mark Rowley is the head of London's [[metropolitan-police|Metropolitan Police]], who in August 2024 warned that "keyboard warriors" could face terrorism charges for inciting riots online, even if they were living abroad.[1]

## August 2024 remarks

Rowley made the comments in response to waves of rioting across the United Kingdom following the murder of three young girls at a Taylor Swift dance class in Southport by a 17-year-old of Rwandan migrant origin through his parents.[1] Asserting that the "full force of the law" would be used against offenders, Rowley made clear that this included not only people physically involved in the riots but those making inflammatory comments about them on social media.[1] "And whether you're in this country committing crimes on the streets or committing crimes from further afield online, we will come after you," he said.[1]

A Sky News reporter raised [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] as a "high profile figure" who was "whipping up hatred," although Musk had merely asked Prime Minister [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]], "Why aren't all communities protected in Britain?"[1] Asked what he was considering for people "whipping up from behind a keyboard" and possibly located in a different country, Rowley replied that "being a keyboard warrior does not make you safe from the law," citing offences of incitement, stirring up racial hatred, and "numerous terrorist offences regarding the publishing of material," and saying police would come after such individuals just as they would physically confront "the thugs and the yobs" on the streets.[1]

## Badlands reaction

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] addressed Rowley directly in reply, calling him a despot whose "control words have no power," urging anons to keep fighting and not to fear "the primal screams of weaklings like Mark Rowley."[1]

## Threat of international arrests for online speech (August 2024)

In August 2024, Metropolitan Police Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley said the police would not necessarily confine arrests for speech offences to London or even the United Kingdom, suggesting that Americans and other foreign nationals could be extradited and brought to London over online postings.[2] "We will throw the full force of the law at people," he warned. "And whether you're in this country committing crimes on the streets or committing crimes from further afield online, we will come after you."[2]

The statement came after days of violent protests over immigration policies in Britain, including attacks and arson directed at immigration centres, fuelled by false online reports about the person responsible for an attack at a Taylor Swift-themed dance event that left three girls dead and others wounded; despite claims that the culprit was an asylum seeker, he was an 18-year-old British citizen born to Rwandan parents.[2] Police moved to arrest those repeating false claims or engaging in inflammatory speech.[2] Asked about criticism from [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], who had posted a video of someone allegedly arrested for offensive online comments with the question "Is this Britain or the Soviet Union?", Rowley reiterated the threat; British pundits and politicians had called for an investigation or the arrest of Musk for speaking publicly on the controversy.[2] The episode was documented by law professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]], who framed it under the headline "London Calling."[2]
