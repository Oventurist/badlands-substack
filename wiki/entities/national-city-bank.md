# National City Bank

National City Bank, later First National City Bank and today [[citibank|Citibank]]/[[citigroup|Citigroup]], is the New York institution at the center of Joe Lange's account of banker control over the United States government.[1]

Under heavy pressure from National City Bank, the United States elbowed France aside and became the dominant power in [[haiti|Haiti]] for decades, dissolving the Haitian parliament at gunpoint, killing thousands of people, and controlling the country's finances for more than 30 years while shipping a large portion of its earnings to bankers in New York.[1] In a 1932 filing to the Senate Finance Committee, the bank stated that it had secured one of its largest margins during the 1920s from a debt it controlled in Haiti.[1]

The bank's securities practices under chairman [[charles-e-mitchell|Charles E. Mitchell]] were central to the speculation that produced the [[stock-market-crash-of-1929|Crash of 1929]] and to the subsequent passage of the [[glass-steagall|Glass-Steagall Act]].[1] Testimony before the [[pecora-commission|Pecora Commission]] revealed that National City Bank had taken on bundles of bad loans, packaged them as securities, and unloaded them on unsuspecting customers.[1] Lange treats this as the original version of a fraudulent template later repeated in the [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 financial crisis]].[1]

## Exposure by the Pecora Commission

In his first two weeks as chief counsel, [[ferdinand-pecora|Ferdinand Pecora]] audited the books of major Wall Street banks and summoned National City's president [[charles-e-mitchell|Charles Mitchell]], described as pro-fascist and then preparing to advise [[benito-mussolini|Benito Mussolini]].[2] Mitchell admitted to short-selling his own bank's stock during the depression, scamming depositors with purchases of Cuban junk debt and avoiding taxes for years, and was forced to resign in shame.[2]
