# Marco Wanderwitz

Marco Wanderwitz is a German politician and member of the [[bundestag|Bundestag]] for the [[christian-democratic-union|Christian Democratic Union]] (CDU), best known for leading efforts to have the [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany]] (AfD) party banned.[1]

## Campaign to ban the AfD

In October 2023, Wanderwitz — who had personally been defeated in his constituency by an AfD candidate — announced plans to introduce a motion in the Bundestag seeking a ban on the party.[1] Speaking on the state-run ARD television program "Panorama," he said, "We are dealing with a party that seriously endangers our free democratic basic order and the state as a whole," and concluded that "it is high time to ban them."[1]

Wanderwitz said he was seeking other MPs to support his motion, though the ultimate authority over any such ban rests with the German Constitutional Court.[1] Observers noted that the legal hurdles for banning a party in Germany are very high and that the political implications of banning the AfD — then the second most popular party in the country at roughly 21 percent in Politico's poll of polls — would be enormous.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the episode as evidence of a worldwide populist awakening, framing calls to ban a party polling near a quarter of the electorate "in order to protect Democracy" as an absurdity exposing the establishment.[1]

## June 2024 developments

By June 2024 Wanderwitz said he had assembled enough support to table a motion, telling the newspaper taz that he had gathered 37 MPs who would back an [[afd-ban-effort|AfD ban]].[2] He was still waiting on the Münster Higher Administrative Court, which had agreed in May with the classification of the [[alternative-for-germany|AfD]] as a "suspected right-wing extremist" organisation but had not released the written justification for its decision.[2] "Once the reasons for the ruling are available, we will take a close look at it and then submit our updated and well-founded application for a ban," he announced, with the court entitled to take at least five months to publish its report.[2] If the [[bundestag|Bundestag]] voted for a ban, Germany's Constitutional Court would make the final determination on its legality.[2]
