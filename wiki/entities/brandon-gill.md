# Brandon Gill

Brandon Gill is a member of the United States [[congress|Congress]] who appears in the Badlands corpus as a questioner at the [[minnesota-somali-fraud|Minnesota Somali fraud]] hearings.

## Role in the fraud hearings

In the January 9, 2026 Badlands Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] describes watching Gill "rush through his questions" during the hearings. She uses his performance to make a structural criticism rather than a personal one, writing that "five minutes isn't enough. Five minutes can only be for show. Campaign clips. Five minutes is an unserious amount of time for the People's representatives' pursuit of truth." The observation is offered in support of her broader argument that congressional oversight of alleged multibillion-dollar fraud in [[minnesota|Minnesota]] has been structurally inadequate.


## Move to end ABA law school accreditation

The July 27, 2026 Badlands Brief reports that Gill, R-Texas, said he intends to pursue legislation that would remove the [[american-bar-association|American Bar Association]]'s authority to accredit U.S. law schools. Gill chairs the [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]]'s Task Force on Defending Constitutional Rights and Exposing Institutional Abuse, and said lawmakers are examining federal legislation that could reduce the ABA's accreditation role while leaving states free to decide whether to work with the organization.

His comments followed a House hearing at which ABA President [[michelle-behnke|Michelle Behnke]] defended the organization's diversity, equity and inclusion practices, testifying that diversity and excellence are "not mutually exclusive." Gill criticized the ABA's prior DEI requirements as discriminating against certain students and alleged that the organization has exercised significant influence over law school admissions, curricula and continuing legal education. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who wryly noted that Gill "sort of looks like Clark Kent," welcomed the effort, placing it alongside Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]]'s earlier letter stripping the ABA of preferred access to the judicial nomination process.
