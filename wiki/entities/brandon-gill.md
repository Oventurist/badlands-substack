# Brandon Gill

Brandon Gill is a Republican U.S. Representative from Texas who chairs the House Oversight Committee's Task Force on Defending Constitutional Rights and Exposing Institutional Abuse.[1]

In July 2026 Gill said he intends to pursue legislation that would remove the [[american-bar-association|American Bar Association]]'s authority to accredit U.S. law schools, adding that lawmakers are examining federal legislation that could reduce the ABA's accreditation role while leaving states free to decide whether to work with the organization.[1] His comments followed a House hearing at which ABA President [[michelle-behnke|Michelle Behnke]] defended the organization's diversity, equity and inclusion practices.[1] During the hearing Gill criticized the ABA's previous DEI requirements as discriminatory against certain students, and alleged that the organization has exercised significant influence over law school admissions, curricula and continuing legal education.[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who noted that Gill "sort of looks like Clark Kent," welcomed the move as part of a broader dismantling of institutional corruption.[1]

## Impeachment articles against Judge Boasberg

On November 4, 2025, Gill introduced articles of impeachment against Chief Judge [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]] of the federal district court in Washington, D.C., over his role in approving orders that barred telecommunications carriers from notifying members of Congress that their call records had been turned over to the Justice Department in the [[arctic-frost|Arctic Frost]] investigation.[2] [[verizon|Verizon]] acknowledged that same day that it had secretly provided Republican lawmakers' call records to the Justice Department and said it would adopt new safeguards requiring top-level review of congressional subpoenas.[2]

## March 2025 Boasberg impeachment resolution

Gill had earlier moved against the same judge. On Tuesday, March 18, 2025, hours after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called for Boasberg's impeachment over a ruling against the deportation of hundreds of Venezuelans, Gill announced that he was introducing articles of impeachment, arguing that Boasberg had "overstepped his authority, compromised the impartiality of the judiciary, and created a constitutional crisis."[3] The move formed part of an unprecedented flood of long-shot impeachment articles filed by House Republicans against federal judges obstructing the administration's agenda, with Boasberg at least the fifth such target.[3]

Gill's resolution grounded the case in the [[alien-enemies-act|Alien Enemies Act]], asserting that the Act commits to the President's sole and unreviewable discretion the authority to invoke its provisions, including the power to determine whether an invasion has taken place.[3] Citing *Ludecke v. Watkins*, 335 U.S. 160 (1948), the resolution argued that for a court to second-guess such a presidential determination "would be assuming the functions of the political agencies of the Government," since these are "matters of political judgement for which judges have neither technical competence nor official responsibility."[3]
