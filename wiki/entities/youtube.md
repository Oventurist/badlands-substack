# YouTube

YouTube is the video-sharing platform owned by [[google|Google]]. In Badlands Media coverage it appears primarily as an instrument of content moderation and deplatforming directed at independent and alternative media.[1]

## Leadership

[[susan-wojcicki|Susan Wojcicki]], one of Google's earliest employees and its 16th hire, served as YouTube's chief executive officer until stepping down; she died of non-small cell lung cancer on August 10, 2024, at age 56.[1] Badlands Media characterized her as "the chief architect of YouTube's dystopian ban hammer."[1]

## Deplatforming of Badlands Media

On Friday, August 9, 2024, [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]'s YouTube channel was permanently removed from the platform for "harmful conspiracy theories."[1] The outlet described the channel as having been "murdered," and noted the timing — Wojcicki's death came the following day — as an extraordinary coincidence.[1] The August 12, 2024 news brief used the episode to argue that speculation about matters of public interest, including the circumstances of Wojcicki's own death, could no longer be conducted on the platform: "We just can't do it on YouTube."[1]

## Settlement of Trump lawsuit, September 2025

In September 2025 YouTube became the latest technology and media company to settle a lawsuit brought by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] after the start of his second term.[2] Court papers filed on Monday 29 September showed the Alphabet-owned company agreeing to pay $24.5 million to resolve litigation over the suspension of Trump's account following the 2021 attack on the US Capitol by a mob of his supporters.[2] Of that sum, $22 million is directed to a trust for the National Mall, the entity handling construction of a $200 million [[white-house-ballroom|White House ballroom]], with the remainder earmarked for other plaintiffs including the American Conservative Union, Andrew Baggiani, Austen Fletcher, Maryse Veronica Jean-Louis, Frank Valentine, Kelly Victory and Naomi Wolf.[2] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded to the [[youtube-settlement-of-trump-lawsuit|settlement]] by asking when ordinary censored users would get paid.[2]
