# Alina Chan

Alina Chan is a molecular biologist at the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard who became one of the earliest and most prominent scientific voices arguing that the COVID-19 pandemic could plausibly have originated from a laboratory accident rather than a purely natural spillover event.[1]

## Advocacy on COVID-19 origins

In a 2021 interview with WBZ-TV, Chan described receiving online abuse and death threats in response to her position that COVID-19 could have emerged from a laboratory in Wuhan, China.[1] She characterized the reaction of the scientific establishment and public health leadership to the lab-leak hypothesis as an "allergic response," saying that colleagues "just could not accept the idea that a scientific research accident might have caused a pandemic."[1]

Chan has emphasized the unusual biological characteristics of the virus as it first appeared, stating that "the virus was highly adapted for human transmission" and that "in December 2019, the virus we saw was ready to go, ready to cause a pandemic."[1]

## Calls for oversight of high-risk research

Five years after the start of the pandemic, Chan argued that substantially more needs to be done to prevent high-risk biological research from triggering a future global health crisis, framing laboratory biosafety and oversight as unresolved policy problems rather than settled ones.[1] Her position was reported alongside coverage of a Trump administration executive order restricting federal funding of dangerous [[gain-of-function-research|gain-of-function research]], including work of the kind conducted on bat coronaviruses by the [[ecohealth-alliance|EcoHealth Alliance]] and the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute of Virology]].[1]
