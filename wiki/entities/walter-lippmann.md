# Walter Lippmann

Walter Lippmann was an American writer whose book *Public Opinion*, published a century before the Badlands Media essay "God and the Declaration of Independence," is cited there as a key authority on the limits of democratic self-government.[1]

## The mirage of majority will

The essay invokes Lippmann while criticizing secular political philosophy, which it argues can offer no objective standard of morality beyond the preference of the majority and therefore elevates "democracy" to the status of highest ideal.[1] Against this, the author reports Lippmann's finding that the will of the majority is largely a mirage, and that the only true power in a democratic society comes from the ability to sway the masses — a capacity the essay notes is "extremely easy" to exercise for whoever owns the media.[1]

This point supports the essay's broader warning that a society can vote its way into a totalitarian state, negating its own supposedly democratic character, and that without guardrails against the encroachment of individual rights a democratic system can devolve into mob rule in which any majority may oppress a political minority at will.[1] In the argument's structure, Lippmann's analysis is what deprives majoritarianism of its claim to be a substitute for the God-given rights asserted in the [[declaration-of-independence|Declaration of Independence]].[1]
