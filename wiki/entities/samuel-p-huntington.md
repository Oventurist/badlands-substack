# Samuel P. Huntington

Samuel P. Huntington was an American political scientist who authored one of the most consequential early reports of the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]].[1]

## The Crisis of Democracy

In 1975 the Commission published Huntington's contribution to its Task Force Reports — known as "The Triangle Papers" — under the title *[[the-crisis-of-democracy|The Crisis of Democracy]]*.[1] In it Huntington claimed that what America really needed was "a greater degree of moderation in democracy," arguing that democratic institutions were incapable of taking prompt action against crises and citing the Three Mile Island nuclear accident as an example.[1] The paper suggested that leaders possessing "expertise, seniority, experience, and special talents" were needed to "override the claims of democracy."[1] Badlands Media characterizes this as an attempt to popularize the idea of globalists circumventing the Constitution.[1]

## Government service and the creation of FEMA

Within the decade Huntington was named coordinator of security planning for [[jimmy-carter|Jimmy Carter]]'s National Security Council.[1] In that capacity he prepared Presidential Review Memorandum 32, which led to the 1979 presidential order creating the [[fema|Federal Emergency Management Agency]] — described in the Badlands account as a civilian organization with the power to take totalitarian control of government functions in the event of a declared national emergency.[1] The article presents the sequence as a demonstration of how quickly Trilateral Commission recommendations became federal policy: from a commission paper to a new federal agency in just five years.[1]
