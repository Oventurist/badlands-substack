# Pierre Trudeau

**Pierre Trudeau** was the Canadian Prime Minister whose entry into politics in 1963 is attributed by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] to the patronage of [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]].[1]

Ehret writes that from 1963 to 1970 Strong oversaw a vast re-organization of the [[liberal-party-of-canada|Liberal Party]], and that Strong bragged to his biographer Elaine Dewar in 1994 that he had sat on the selection committee which brought Trudeau — whom Ehret calls a [[fabian-society|Fabian Society]] agent — into politics in 1963.[1]

Trudeau is the father of [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]], whose replacement by [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]] frames Ehret's account of Canada's role as an anti-republican hub in world affairs.[1]

## Backer of the Limits to Growth project

Ehret writes that the [[club-of-rome|Club of Rome]]–MIT study that became [[limits-to-growth|*Limits to Growth*]] was not begun in the United States but at Montebello, Quebec in 1971, when Club of Rome backer Pierre Trudeau allocated taxpayer money to start the project.[2] A network of Rhodes Scholars and Privy Councillors — centred on [[alexander-king|Alexander King]], [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]], [[maurice-lamontagne|Maurice Lamontagne]] (founder of Environment Canada), Michael Pitfield (Privy Council Clerk and founder of Canada's CSIS) and Governor General Roland Michener — presided over that meeting; when the Canadian funds had served their role the project continued on funding from the Volkswagen Foundation.[2]

Lamontagne, whom Ehret describes as a long-time London-trained asset and close collaborator of Trudeau, used his Senate Committee Reports of 1968–1972 to reform Canadian science policy along zero-growth lines.[2] Dalhousie professor [[ronald-hayes|Ronald Hayes]] attacked the resulting program as an attempt to destroy the National Research Council, calling it "a fault of the Trudeau administration which Lamontagne echoes."[2]
