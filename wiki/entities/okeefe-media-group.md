# O'Keefe Media Group

O'Keefe Media Group (OMG) is the undercover journalism outlet founded by [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]], known for hidden-camera investigations into political and nonprofit organizations.[1]

## March 2026 Los Angeles registration drive footage

In March 2026, the group published undercover documentation of a Los Angeles voter registration drive targeting homeless people, showing money changing hands and fake names being registered.[1] The material circulated widely on social media and was re-shared by O'Keefe in June 2026 following disputed results in the Los Angeles primary.[1]

Badlands writers treated the footage as a precursor to the Department of Justice's May 18, 2026 indictment of a California signature collector accused of paying Skid Row residents to register to vote, and to the subsequent federal operation on Skid Row by roughly twenty agents including personnel from Homeland Security Investigations.[1]

## December 2023 Phoenix airport reporting

In December 2023, O'Keefe Media Group founder [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]] posted video shot at the drop-off terminal of Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport in which he interviewed a bus driver working for a limo service contracted by an unidentified entity to haul migrants from the southern border to the airport; asked "Are these migrants from the border?", the driver answered "Correct."[2] The footage followed a surge of citizen-journalist reports on X about migrants being flown around the country, including a post by [[ashley-st-clair|Ashley St. Clair]] alleging that migrants were being moved from processing centers onto [[delta-air-lines|Delta]] aircraft at Sky Harbor, which drew a Community Note saying there was no evidence the people at the gate were migrants.[2] [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] described O'Keefe's video as the best evidence yet that the travelers were in fact in the country illegally.[2]

## Prince Andrew video (2025)

In May 2025 O'Keefe Media Group released an undercover video featuring British royal family advisor [[john-bryan|John Bryan]], who claimed that [[prince-andrew|Prince Andrew]] "was f*cking underage girls" and had close ties to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[3] The video described Bryan's role providing crisis management at Andrew's private residence two weeks after the prince's BBC interview, and closed with [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]] teasing further material the following week.[3] Badlands writer Ashe in America criticised the release as a hyped non-revelation monetising public outrage over child sexual abuse, contrasting it with Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]]'s same-day statement on the volume of [[epstein-files|Epstein files]] evidence.[3]
