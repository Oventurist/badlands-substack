# Helmuth von Moltke

**Helmuth von Moltke** was a 19th-century Prussian field marshal described as "embodying Prussian military organization and tactical genius."[1]

## Transformation of Prussian doctrine

Moltke fundamentally transformed Prussian military doctrine by implementing the idea of [[maneuver-warfare|maneuver warfare]].[1] Unlike [[blitzkrieg]] — which seeks total annihilation through rapid brute force — Moltke continued to value the prolonged siege; his insight was that railways constituted a unique technology allowing commanders to deploy troops quickly and outflank enemies in a double envelopment, which then set the conditions for a longer siege.[1]

## Legacy

The Nazis took Moltke's maneuver-warfare concept and combined it with [[frederick-the-great|Frederick the Great]]'s [[preventive-war|preventive war]] doctrine to create blitzkrieg, a synthesis that Badlands analysis argues eventually vindicated Frederick's late-life skepticism.[1] Moltke's double-envelopment idea also supplies the metaphor used in Badlands commentary on political "pincer movements" against the [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] government.[1]
