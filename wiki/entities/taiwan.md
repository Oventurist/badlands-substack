# Taiwan

Taiwan is the self-governing island whose surrounding waters became the focus of a Chinese "special maritime law enforcement operation" reported in the June 9, 2026 *Badlands Brief*.[1]

China launched the operation east of Taiwan on a Saturday, according to China's Ministry of Transport and the state news agency Xinhua, saying it was intended to exercise maritime administrative law-enforcement jurisdiction and protect national rights and interests.[1]

Taiwan objected to a separate diplomatic development that preceded the operation: a May 28 summit in Tokyo at which Japanese Prime Minister [[sanae-takaichi|Sanae Takaichi]] and Philippine President [[ferdinand-marcos-jr|Ferdinand Marcos Jr.]] announced plans to begin maritime delimitation talks concerning their exclusive economic zones and continental shelves.[1] Taipei stated that the area overlaps with waters in which it claims rights and interests and urged Japan and the Philippines to consult with it.[1]

Taiwan's coast guard said it deployed at least five vessels in response to the Chinese operation and monitored four Chinese government vessels departing from Xiamen.[1] The episode unfolded amid ongoing disputes involving China, Taiwan, Japan, and the Philippines over maritime claims and jurisdiction in the waters surrounding the island.[1]

## Trump-Xi Beijing summit (May 2026)

Taiwan emerged as the clearest flashpoint at President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Beijing summit with [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]], even as both sides publicly emphasized stability, trade and cooperation.[2] Xi warned Trump that mishandling Taiwan could lead to "clashes and even conflicts," while US statements largely avoided the issue and said policy remained unchanged.[2]

Badlands analysis characterized the outcome as a signal in the noise: Beijing placed Taiwan at the center of the meeting, Washington publicly conceded no ground, and Taipei said there were "no surprises" while urging China to end military pressure.[2] The result was described not as a breakthrough but as a visible marker that Taiwan is moving higher on the board as Trump and Xi test whether economic détente can survive the hardest security question in US-China relations.[2]

In the [[multipolar-margin-call|Multipolar Margin Call]] framework advanced by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], Taiwan is cast as the last remaining globalist forward operating base of real consequence and the capstone of an accelerating sovereign disentanglement cascade.[2] A Taiwan crisis, in that reading, would complete a trilogy of lessons about dependence on distant nations — after [[iran|Iran]] on energy security and Russia on national security — by demonstrating the folly of relying on distant nations for logistical and technological security across semiconductors, rare earths and advanced manufacturing.[2]

## Sole pacing scenario in US defense planning (2025)

A March 2025 internal [[pentagon|Pentagon]] document, the [[interim-national-defense-strategic-guidance|Interim National Defense Strategic Guidance]], reportedly narrowed U.S. defense strategy in the Indo-Pacific to a single scenario: preventing Beijing from seizing full control of Taiwan by military means while simultaneously defending the U.S. homeland.[3] [[china|China]] was designated the Department of Defense's "sole pacing threat" and the only potential adversary in a major power war for which Washington needed contingency plans.[3]

Badlands commentary rejected the premise, with contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] arguing that there is "nothing of significance on the island of Taiwan that is worth American lives and American treasure," that the manufacturing base exported there decades earlier should be repatriated, and that the island's political conflict with Beijing could not be resolved by overwhelming military force.[3]

## TSMC investment and tariff pressure (2025)

In March 2025, after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened tariffs of up to 100 percent on overseas-made chips and accused Taiwan of having stolen the US chip industry, [[tsmc|Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co]] announced a $100 billion investment in the United States, bringing its total US pledge to $165 billion — described by the company as the largest single foreign direct investment in US history.[4] Taipei had promised to invest more in the United States amid the tariff pressure.[4] Trump referenced strategic defense considerations around Taiwanese semiconductors, called TSMC a monopoly, and argued that on-shoring insulated the United States from China–Taiwan escalation; Badlands contributor Burning Bright asked whether Xi Jinping had in effect been granted permission and whether a Russia–Ukraine re-run was in prospect.[4]

## September 2024 strait transit incident

On September 17, 2024 a U.S. P-8A Poseidon patrol aircraft flew through the Taiwan Strait, prompting the [[peoples-liberation-army|People's Liberation Army]]'s Eastern Theater Command to scramble fighter jets to tail the aircraft and vow to "resolutely defend national sovereignty."[5] Beijing regards self-ruled Taiwan as a renegade province and claims jurisdiction over the body of water separating the island from the Chinese mainland, while the U.S. Navy's Seventh Fleet described the P-8A as transiting in international airspace.[5] Badlands commentary read the episode as one element of an accelerating global crisis convergence in September 2024.[5]

## Chinese balloon incursions and the January 2024 elections

Taiwan's defence ministry said it detected three more Chinese balloons flying over the Taiwan Strait on Sunday, January 7, 2024, one of which crossed the island, the latest in a spate of such sightings over the preceding month.[6] In a strongly worded statement the previous day, the ministry accused [[china|China]] of threatening aviation safety and waging psychological warfare on the island's people with the balloons, days before key Taiwanese elections; China's defence ministry, which had declined to comment on the balloons in December, did not immediately respond to a request for comment.[6] The potential for balloons to be used for spying had become a global issue in February 2023, when the United States shot down what it said was a Chinese surveillance balloon that Beijing described as a civilian craft that had accidentally drifted astray.[6]

Taipei was on high alert for Chinese military and political activity ahead of the presidential and parliamentary elections of Saturday, January 13, 2024, saying Beijing was exerting military and economic pressure in an attempt to interfere in the vote.[6] China views the island as its own territory, a claim Taiwan's government rejects.[6] Commentators at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] framed the contest between Vice President [[lai-ching-te|Lai Ching-te]] and opposition leader [[hou-yu-ih|Hou Yu-ih]] as a coming "Current Crisis" replacing a Ukraine proxy war that had, in their assessment, run out of narrative energy.[6]

## Drone cooperation with Ukraine and Poland (2025)

In September 2025 a Taiwanese defense company signed a memorandum of understanding with Ukrainian and Polish delegations on cooperation in UAV technology, announced at the International Defense Industry Exhibition (MSPO) in Kielce, Poland, amid heightened US–China tensions in the South China Sea.[7] The document was signed by Taiwan Defense Industry Development Association President Tony Hsu, Polish-Taiwanese Chamber of Commerce founder Bartlomiej Dobosz, and Dmitry Dymyd of the Lviv Tech Cluster in Ukraine.[7]

Under the arrangement Ukraine contributes research and development, Poland provides expertise, and Taichung-based [[thunder-tiger|Thunder Tiger]] supplies technology and components; the firm had earlier in the year unveiled an FPV drone able to carry an 81mm mortar and a naval kamikaze drone.[7] Taiwan's office in Poland said the agreement "strengthens technological exchange, develops industrial potential, and enhances our security as partners united by democratic values."[7] Beijing regards all diplomatic ties with the self-governing island as a violation of its sovereignty and has opposed military aid to Taipei.[7] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the deal as evidence that Taiwan functions as "the Asian Ukraine."[7]

## US-China friction over Taiwan (August 2024)

Taiwan was the central point of contention during US national security adviser [[jake-sullivan|Jake Sullivan]]'s August 2024 trip to Beijing.[8] Chinese Central Military Commission vice chairman [[zhang-youxia|Zhang Youxia]] told Sullivan that reunification with the mainland was the "mission and responsibility" of the [[peoples-liberation-army|People's Liberation Army]], and China's defense ministry publicly demanded that the United States "stop military collusion between the U.S. and Taiwan, stop arming Taiwan and stop spreading false narratives about Taiwan."[8]
