# Memphis

Memphis is the largest city in the U.S. state of Tennessee and, in September 2025, became the focus of a federal law enforcement and military deployment ordered by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## September 2025 federal deployment

On Monday, 15 September 2025, Trump signed an executive order extending federal law enforcement action to Memphis, a move that included sending [[national-guard|National Guard]] troops into the city and establishing a [[memphis-state-task-force|Memphis Safe Task Force]] to address crime.[1] Trump justified the order by citing what he called the "tremendous levels of violent crime that have overwhelmed its local government's ability to respond effectively."[1] Local police, however, reported that overall criminal offences in the city were at a 25-year low.[1]

Trump described the Memphis operation as a "replica" of his August 2025 crackdown in Washington, D.C., which had likewise seen National Guard troops deployed on the streets of the U.S. capital.[1] He had pushed for similar military involvement in policing in Baltimore and Chicago — cities which, like Memphis and Washington, D.C., are Democratic strongholds.[1]

## Political reaction

Tennessee's Republican governor [[bill-lee|Bill Lee]] backed the task force and joined Trump at the White House for the announcement, saying at the signing ceremony, "I have been in office for seven years. I'm tired of crime holding the great city of Memphis back."[1] The mayor of Memphis publicly distanced himself from the deployment, posting on X that he had learned only the previous morning that the governor and president had decided to place the National Guard and other resources in the city, conceding that they had the authority to do so but adding, "I did not ask for the National Guard and I don't think it is the way to drive down crime."[1]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded by noting that Memphis ranks as the most dangerous large city in the nation by overall violent crime per 100,000 residents, and asked why the mayor would want to keep it that way.[1]
