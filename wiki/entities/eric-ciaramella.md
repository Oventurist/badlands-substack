# Eric Ciaramella

Eric Ciaramella is the individual identified in reporting as the Ukraine whistleblower whose complaint triggered the first impeachment of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## The whistleblower complaint

Reporting cited by Badlands states that 2019 claims by "Witness 2," later identified as [[gavin-wilde|Gavin Wilde]], were critical in helping Intelligence Community Inspector General [[michael-atkinson|Michael Atkinson]] push Ciaramella's complaint forward.[1] Wilde's "Russiagate"-linked biases were concealed from House investigators during the impeachment process.[1]

## Criminal referral (April 2026)

In April 2026 the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] referred the whistleblower behind the 2019 impeachment complaint, along with former Inspector General [[michael-atkinson|Michael Atkinson]], to the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] for potential criminal violations tied to 2019 briefings before the [[house-permanent-select-committee-on-intelligence|House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence]].[2] Badlands contributor Ashe in America framed the disclosure as evidence that a broad RICO case is progressing, noting that the public naming of Ciaramella was immediately followed by criminal referrals.[2] The commentary argued that [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]]'s 2019 insistence that the whistleblower's name could not be spoken was a purposeful lie that helped maintain what it called a Ukrainian money and secrets apparatus while undermining the elected president's agenda.[2]

## Role in the first impeachment

The Badlands article "Setting the Stage" identifies Ciaramella as the whistleblower whom [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] recruited and helped to file a complaint against President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and whose secret testimony was used to pursue impeachment and removal.[3] The complaint claimed that a phone call between Trump and Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] showed Trump pressuring the Ukrainian leader to gather evidence against a coming political opponent.[3]

Joe Lange emphasises the central defect in the account: although several officials normally listen in on presidential calls with foreign leaders, Ciaramella was not among them.[3] He was a CIA analyst at the time, was not involved in the call in any way, and was not legally privy to its contents — which means whoever supplied him those contents was illegally leaking classified information, even if the whistleblower himself might be protected by statute for passing it along.[3] Red State reported that he did not hold the clearance needed to access the call, whose rough transcript was first classified secret and later top secret.[3]

Lange argues that Ciaramella was close friends with someone who was listening to the call, and that the released impeachment transcripts point to [[alexander-vindman|Alexander Vindman]] as the source, after Schiff and [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] blocked [[jim-jordan|Jim Jordan]] from asking Vindman whom outside his chain of command he had spoken to.[3] The whole structure collapsed, in Lange's telling, once Trump declassified the transcript of the Zelensky call.[3]
