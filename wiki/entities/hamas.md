# Hamas

Hamas is the Palestinian militant organization governing Gaza whose disarmament is the central unresolved condition of the October 2025 U.S.-brokered [[gaza-ceasefire|Gaza ceasefire]].[1]

## Phase 2 of the Gaza ceasefire

An Israeli source indicated that Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] would inform President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] that Phase 2 of the ceasefire — requiring Hamas disarmament and Israeli troop withdrawal — "is not moving" because of Hamas's refusal to relinquish weapons.[1] [[israel|Israel]] argued that a renewed military operation is needed to enforce demilitarization in line with Trump's Gaza vision, amid ongoing incidents and IDF strikes on suspected violators.[1]

## Alleged pre-2018 Israeli knowledge and Qatari funding

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that a newly released Israeli government report claims Netanyahu knew about Hamas's plan to invade southern Israel as far back as early 2018.[1] On that account, Netanyahu possessed this knowledge when he arranged for tens of millions of dollars per month to reach Hamas after the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] in the West Bank cut the group off financially.[1] The commentary alleges Netanyahu appealed to [[qatar|Qatar]] on the grounds that a humanitarian crisis would follow if cash were not sent to Gaza, and that Qatari money was then walked into Gaza by his people and distributed to prominent families with known ties to Hamas — concluding that if true, Netanyahu knowingly funded October 7th.[1]

## Reaction to West Bank measures

Following the Israeli Security Cabinet's approval of measures advancing [[west-bank-annexation|de facto sovereignty]] in the West Bank, Hamas called for kinetic escalation from the region against Israel, which the Badlands commentary characterized as exactly the response Netanyahu wanted.[1]

## Talks with the Gaza Peace Board, March 2026

In March 2026, representatives of Hamas met with members of the [[board-of-peace|Gaza Peace Board]] to discuss governance and security arrangements for [[gaza|Gaza]].[2] The board forms part of a framework supported by US President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and is intended to establish a transitional governing structure for the territory after the conflict.[2]

The meeting reportedly addressed potential security coordination and administrative control in Gaza during a transition period, and involved regional mediators and representatives connected to the peace board structure, although details of participants and outcomes were not fully disclosed.[2] The talks came amid broader international efforts to settle post-war governance for Gaza following the conflict between [[israel|Israel]] and Hamas.[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] joked about the prospect of Hamas joining the Board of Peace and about the organization being represented at the meeting by [[aryeh-lightstone|Aryeh Lightstone]].[2] He noted that Israel subsequently opened a Gaza–Egypt crossing that had been closed since the [[iran|Iran]] conflict began, and argued the decisive shift was that the meetings are no longer run by Israel but by a coalition of nations seeking a deal with Hamas.[2]

## October 7, 2023 attacks

On October 7, 2023, Hamas launched an unprecedented air, land and sea incursion into southern Israel from [[gaza|Gaza]] — the most significant breach of Israel's borders since the 1973 [[yom-kippur-war|Yom Kippur War]].[3] Two days later, Hamas and [[palestinian-islamic-jihad|Palestinian Islamic Jihad]] reportedly controlled swathes of southern Israeli territory and even military outposts, with town-by-town and farm-by-farm fighting still underway.[3] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] pledged that the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] would use "all its strength" to "destroy" Hamas.[3]

The [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]] reported that Iranian security officials helped plan the assault and gave the green light at a meeting in [[beirut|Beirut]], with [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|IRGC]] officers working with Hamas since August 2023; U.S. officials including Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] said they had not seen evidence Iran directed the attack.[3] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterized Hamas as "a Deep State terror cell" that had "gone dramatically kinetic in their most direct assault ever," on the heels of months of fomented [[color-revolution|color revolution]] tactics inside Israel.[3]

## Abbas condemnation (April 2025)

In April 2025 Palestinian Authority president [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] called Hamas "sons of dogs" in a speech to a meeting in the occupied West Bank, demanding that the group release the hostages it still held, disarm, and hand over control of Gaza to end the war with Israel.[4] Abbas said Hamas had given Israel "excuses" to continue its attacks on Gaza and told the group to "release the hostages and be done with it," his strongest remarks against Hamas in the 18 months since the war began.[4] A Hamas official condemned what he called Abbas's "derogatory language" toward "a significant proportion... of his own people."[4] The previous week the group had rejected an Israeli proposal for a new ceasefire in Gaza that included a demand to disarm in return for a six-week pause in hostilities and the release of 10 of the 59 remaining hostages.[4]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] situated the remarks in a long conflict between Hamas and the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], noting that Hamas rose to power in Gaza in 2006 and waged kinetic war on the PA, expelling it from the territory, and that a 2017 agreement for the PA to resume government services in Gaza collapsed after Hamas attacked the Palestinian prime minister's caravan in 2018.[4]

## 200 days of war (April 2024)

Marking 200 days since the start of the Israel–Hamas war in October 2023, Abu Obeida, spokesperson for Hamas's armed wing the Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades, called on April 23, 2024 for an escalation across all fronts in a televised speech.[5] The call coincided with intensified [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] strikes across [[gaza|Gaza]] and fresh evacuation orders in the north, roughly four months after the Israeli army had announced it was drawing down troops in northern Gaza on the grounds that Hamas no longer controlled those areas.[5]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] speculated that the true purpose of the Gaza offensive was "not to eradicate Hamas, but to grow it," arguing that carpet-bombing a city cannot eliminate an ideology and that the campaign was generating radicalized recruits — the inverse of the containment strategy he credited [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with using against [[isis|ISIS]].[5]

## Direct talks with the United States (March 2025)

In March 2025 a senior Hamas delegation led by [[khalil-al-hayya|Khalil al-Hayya]] met US hostage envoy [[adam-boehler|Adam Boehler]] in Doha to discuss the potential release of American hostages from Gaza, an unprecedented direct engagement conducted without advance notice to Israel.[6] Discussions reportedly covered the number of Palestinian prisoners Israel would release in exchange for the five Israeli-American hostages still in Gaza, one alive and four believed dead.[6] Israel had opposed any such meeting without preconditions for Hamas, and Strategic Affairs Minister [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]] confronted Boehler by phone; the meeting was leaked hours later in a move US officials attributed to Israel.[6]

## Nova music festival attack and the friendly-fire admission

Hamas fighters and other Palestinians crossed the border fence from Gaza into Israel on October 7, 2023 and attacked the Nova music festival near the Gaza border.[7] An Israeli police investigation reported by Haaretz in November 2023 concluded that an [[israel-defense-forces|Israeli]] attack helicopter from the Ramat David base, firing at the attackers, also fired on some of the Israeli festival-goers, complicating the earlier official account that all 260 Israeli dead at the site had been killed by Hamas and Palestinians in a deliberate massacre; police put the total killed at 364.[7] A similar dynamic was reported at the settlement of Be'eri, where Hamas fighters held Israelis captive in homes and Israeli forces opened fire with tank shells, killing captives and fighters alike.[7]

## Instrument thesis and the Fatah rivalry (2024 commentary)

In May 2024 Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] advanced the thesis that Hamas functions as an instrument wielded by Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] like a weapon, with the intended target being Palestinian President [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] and the [[fatah|Fatah]] faction of the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]].[8]

Under this account, after [[yasser-arafat|Yasser Arafat]] was assassinated with poison in 2004, Hamas began engaging in real political activity in Gaza — then controlled by Fatah and occupied by the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] — using Arafat's death to rally support for a more militant activism against their ostensible oppressors.[8] Hamas defeated Fatah in the 2006 election, immediately attacked Fatah security forces and drove them out of Gaza, then turned its attention to the IDF, winning that war thanks in part to funding from Netanyahu.[8]

The commentary holds that Hamas is a weapon of insurgency intended to unseat Fatah not only in Gaza but also in the West Bank, and that the optics of the IDF's siege have driven public sympathy for Hamas worldwide to an all-time high — with implications for its potential political support in any upcoming West Bank election.[8] From the perspective of a warmonger, the argument runs, a Hamas-controlled West Bank is far superior to a peace-seeking Fatah-controlled one.[8]

Netanyahu's own May 2024 formulation — that he would not "replace Hamastan with Fatahstan" — was read as an obvious attempt to conflate the two rival factions.[8] He justified the position by citing polling that 80 percent of Palestinians in Judea and Samaria supported the October 7 massacre, and by insisting that "as long as Hamas remains intact, no other party will step in to manage civilian affairs in Gaza."[8] Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] had triggered the exchange by demanding a day-after plan and the immediate raising of a governing alternative to Hamas in Gaza.[8]

## Rafah and the recruitment dynamic (May 2024)

By May 2024 Hamas remained the governing power in [[gaza|Gaza]] and the object of Israel's stated war aim, with Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] insisting that destroying the group required going after its leaders hiding in [[rafah|Rafah]].[9] The group was described as having orchestrated the October 7 attacks that killed more than 1,100 Israelis.[9]

Badlands commentary argued that the Rafah campaign was strengthening rather than weakening Hamas: footage released by Israel of tanks and armoured bulldozers entering the city was being used by the group to recruit fighters, guerrilla activity continued in northern Gaza areas the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] claimed to have cleared of Hamas, and worldwide sympathy for Hamas had reached unprecedented levels.[9] The commentary also observed that although Hamas had never had a political presence in the West Bank, support for its militancy was beginning to grow there as the IDF conducted operations in Samaria and Judea.[9] Hamas explicitly warned that any foreign personnel introduced to fight alongside the IDF — including the American contractors slated to oversee the [[rafah-border-crossing|Rafah crossing]] — would be treated as targets.[9]

## October 2023 ground clash in Gaza

In late October 2023, Hamas claimed to have repelled a brief Israeli ground incursion in what was reported as the first direct ground fight between the two sides inside [[gaza|Gaza]].[10] The [[al-qassam-brigades|Al-Qassam Brigades]] said their fighters destroyed two Israeli military bulldozers and a tank in an ambush near Khan Younis as the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] conducted "limited" incursions into the strip.[10] The IDF did not confirm the claims but acknowledged that its forces were operating inside Gaza at the time, and a later IDF statement appeared to back the Hamas account.[10]

The group's October 7 attack — in which fighters paraglided into an Israeli music festival and killed hundreds of attendees — continued to shape American politics, including the controversy over a [[black-lives-matter|Black Lives Matter]] Chicago chapter post depicting a paraglider with the caption "I stand with Palestine."[10]

## Hostage claims after the Neutra announcement (December 2024)

Following the Israeli military's December 2, 2024 statement that [[omer-neutra|Omer Maxim Neutra]] had been killed on [[october-7-attacks|October 7, 2023]] and that his body remained in Gaza, the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] questioned whether Hamas would have prioritized retaining a soldier's remains through more than a year of intense bombardment while engaged in what it described as existential combat.[11] Neutra was one of seven American citizens held in Gaza, four of whom had been pronounced dead.[11] The Brief cited the reported use of the [[hannibal-directive|Hannibal Directive]] by the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] on October 7 in arguing that the hostage narrative attributed to Hamas may be unreliable.[11]

## Global escalation warnings (October 2023)

In late October 2023, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] warned that Israel's conflict with Hamas could spread well beyond the Middle East, saying it was wrong that innocent women, children and old people in [[gaza|Gaza]] were being punished for other people's crimes.[12] Speaking at a Kremlin meeting with Russian religious leaders, he said bloodshed in the region had to stop and that he had told other world leaders in phone calls that failure to do so risked a much wider conflagration, warning of "grave and extremely dangerous and destructive consequences."[12] He also charged that certain unnamed forces were seeking to provoke further escalation and draw as many other countries and peoples into the conflict as possible.[12]

[[gallup|Gallup]] polling in the same period tied the conflict to American domestic politics: the October 7 surprise attack by Hamas militants, which resulted in at least 1,400 deaths and more than 200 kidnappings, precipitated an Israeli counteroffensive that had produced an estimated 5,000 deaths in Gaza and a humanitarian crisis, and coincided with [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s approval rating falling to a record-low 37 percent.[12]

## Gaza City encirclement (November 2023)

By November 7, 2023, Israeli Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] announced that Israeli troops were "in the heart" of Gaza City, describing Gaza as "the largest terrorist base ever built" and stating, "We are going to destroy Hamas."[13] Gallant said the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] had Hamas leader [[yahya-sinwar|Yahya Sinwar]] surrounded in a bunker and that fighting was underway in residential areas.[13] He rejected U.S. pressure for a humanitarian "pause," saying there would be no humanitarian truce without the return of the hostages.[13] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said the IDF had reached deeper into Gaza than Hamas ever imagined and that pressure on the group was deepening "every hour, every day," ruling out fuel entry or a ceasefire absent the release of those kidnapped.[13]


## Trump threat over Gaza executions (October 2025)

In October 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] threatened action against Hamas over public executions carried out by the group after Israel withdrew from portions of the Gaza Strip under a US-backed ceasefire deal.[14] "If Hamas continues to kill people in Gaza, which was not the deal, we will have no choice but to go in and kill them," he warned in a Truth Social message.[14] The post appeared to mark a reversal from his stance two days earlier on Hamas's summary execution of Palestinians from rival gangs and Gazans it accused of collaborating with Israel — Trump had claimed on the Tuesday that he had given Hamas "approval for a period of time" to carry out the killings, asserting that the organization was targeting some "very bad gangs."[14] The threat also came as an Israeli-backed militia in southern Gaza appealed to the international community in a video, urging the American president to protect residents of the Strip from Hamas.[14]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that what was not being disclosed to the public is that there are ISIS-affiliated gangs, rivals of Hamas, funded and armed by [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]], and that these — not innocent Palestinians — are the people Hamas is targeting.[14]

## Killing of Sa'ad Ra'ad (December 2025)

Sa'ad Ra'ad, the former deputy commander of Hamas's military wing and one of the architects of the October 7 attacks, was killed in an Israeli strike over the weekend of December 13-14, 2025, prompting a White House rebuke of Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] over what Washington described as a violation of the Gaza ceasefire agreement.[15] Israel denied breaching the ceasefire and asserted that Hamas had broken the agreement by planning an explosive attack that wounded two Israeli soldiers.[15] The episode is covered in detail at [[killing-of-saad-raad|Killing of Sa'ad Ra'ad]].[15]

## Israeli escalation, March 2025

On March 30, 2025, Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said that ministers had voted overnight to increase military pressure on Hamas, claiming the approach was "working" to bring about a deal freeing hostages held in the Gaza Strip.[16] He said the strategy simultaneously crushed Hamas's military and governmental capabilities while creating conditions for the hostages' release.[16]

Netanyahu identified three Hamas "false claims" about Israel's negotiating posture, insisting Israel was still negotiating but doing so "under fire," which he argued made talks more effective, and claiming "there are suddenly cracks."[16] At that point Hamas had not agreed to Israel's demands for a new, extended ceasefire.[16] The Israeli security cabinet's vote coincided with massive nationwide protests in Israel against the government's handling of the hostage file.[16]

## Hostage releases, October 2023

During the pause before Israel's delayed ground invasion of Gaza in late October 2023, four hostages were freed as a result of negotiations involving Qatari mediation with Hamas, even as Israeli airstrikes intensified and the reported Palestinian death toll passed 6,500.[17]

## July 2025 proximity talks and the Rafah relocation plan

In July 2025 Hamas was engaged in indirect proximity talks with Israel in Doha, which U.S. Mideast envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] had been scheduled to join before delaying his flight to remain in Washington for meetings with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Qatari officials.[18] Badlands commentary characterized Netanyahu's insistence that the IDF continue fighting until Hamas no longer exists as an unattainable goal, given the way ideology proliferates in the face of violent warfare.[18] Israeli defense minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] said Palestinians would be screened before admission to a proposed Rafah "humanitarian city" specifically to ensure that no Hamas operatives slipped in.[18] See [[humanitarian-city-plan|Humanitarian City Plan]].[18]


## Gaza City offensive (September 2025)

Israel's new military push in Gaza City in September 2025 was framed around the stated goal of eradicating Hamas, an objective endorsed by U.S. Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] during his 15 September visit to Jerusalem, where he said [[israel|Israel]] could "count on our unwavering support."[19] Witnesses described heavy, relentless bombing of Gaza City hours later.[19] A week earlier President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had chided Israel for carrying out air strikes against Hamas leaders in the U.S. partner state of Qatar.[19]

## UN silence and the messaging war (November 2023)

In November 2023 the watchdog [[un-watch|UN Watch]] criticized the [[united-nations|United Nations]] for adopting eight resolutions condemning [[israel|Israel]] while issuing no condemnation of Hamas or other groups, at a time when antisemitism was rising and one month after what UN Watch described as the largest massacre of Jews since the Holocaust.[20] Separately, a US Embassy cable from [[oman|Oman]] obtained by CNN reported profound concern among American officials about anger in the Arab world that erupted after Israel launched operations against Hamas following the militant group's October 7 attacks in Israel, which left over 1,400 Israelis dead.[20]

## August 2024 ceasefire negotiations

In early August 2024, negotiations over a hostages-for-ceasefire arrangement between Israel and Hamas remained stalled after many months.[21] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] told President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] that Israel was moving forward and would soon send a delegation to resume talks, prompting Biden to tell him to "stop bullshitting me."[21] Israeli security officials, including [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] and [[herzi-halevi|Herzi Halevi]], said Netanyahu's added conditions — an inspection mechanism for movement into northern Gaza, continued Israeli control of the [[philadelphi-corridor|Philadelphi Corridor]], and a list of living hostages Hamas would release — would sabotage the deal, while Netanyahu blamed Hamas for introducing new demands.[21]

The killing of Hamas chief [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]] in Tehran in late July 2024 raised expectations of an [[iran|Iranian]] retaliation against Israel, with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] exchanging fire along the northern border and Netanyahu declaring Israel to be in a multi-front war.[21]

## January 2025 Ceasefire and Hostage Deal

On January 15, 2025, Israel and Hamas struck an agreement, approved in Qatar, that secured the release of all hostages held by the group; President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced the deal on TruthSocial, saying the captives "will be released shortly."[22] Multiple outlets reported the framework involved a 42-day ceasefire and a prisoner exchange covering all Israelis taken captive during the October 7, 2023 incursion from Gaza.[22] Negotiations in Doha brought together representatives of both the outgoing Biden administration and the incoming Trump team, with Brett McGurk and [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] jointly working the final push.[22] In Israel the framework triggered a political crisis, with President [[isaac-herzog|Isaac Herzog]] publicly urging the cabinet to approve it and Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] threatening to bolt the governing coalition.[22]

## October 7 attack and its regional aftermath

On October 7, 2023, Hamas launched a mass assault on Israel that killed more than 1,400 people, most of them civilians slaughtered in their homes or at a music festival near the Gaza border, and took over 200 people hostage.[23] In the weeks that followed, a bipartisan US Senate delegation led by [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] visited Israel and declared that "destroying Hamas is non-negotiable," urging Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] to fund the Gazan population until the group was destroyed.[23]

The attack also triggered a large US force build-up in the region, which Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] said raised the danger of escalation, and prompted [[pentagon|Pentagon]] warnings that [[iran|Iran]]-backed groups were preparing intensified attacks on US forces.[23] By late October the Gaza death toll had surpassed 5,000 amid UN and EU calls for a ceasefire.[23] In the United States, pro-Palestinian demonstrations spread to cities including [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]], which Badlands commentary framed as domestic activist networks rallying around Hamas-linked causes in tension with the administration they had helped elect.[23]

## Netanyahu's May 2025 war aims

At his first press conference in five months on May 21, 2025, [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] listed Israel's aims as defeating Hamas, "which carried out the atrocities of October 7," bringing back all hostages, and ensuring Gaza does not present a threat to Israel.[24] He said Israeli forces in [[operation-gideons-chariots|Operation Gideon's Chariots]] were "landing powerful blows that will get stronger against Hamas strongholds that still exist in Gaza," and promised Hamas would be "totally defeated" by the operation's end.[24] Badlands commentary elsewhere noted that South Africa's [[international-court-of-justice|ICJ]] petition concerned the IDF offensive launched immediately after the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack.[24]

## Israeli drawdown and the stated war aim (January 2024)

When [[israel|Israel]] announced on January 1, 2024 that it would withdraw several thousand troops from [[gaza|Gaza]], military spokesman Rear Admiral [[daniel-hagari|Daniel Hagari]] emphasized that the move did not suggest any compromise of Israel's intention to keep fighting until it destroys Hamas.[25] The [[israeli-troop-withdrawal-from-gaza-2024|drawdown]] was attributed to the economic toll of mobilization rather than to any change in war aims, with Hagari saying he expected "warfare throughout this year."[25]

## Response to the Trump Gaza plan (October 2025)

A source familiar with the negotiations told *The Times of Israel* in early October 2025 that Hamas would reply "positively" to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s proposal for ending the war in [[gaza|Gaza]], while submitting a series of amendments, with a response possible within hours.[26] Arab mediators from [[egypt|Egypt]], [[qatar|Qatar]], and [[turkey|Turkey]] had been holding productive talks with Hamas leaders in Doha about the plan, which Trump unveiled that Monday and which Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] accepted.[26]

The amendments were said to aim at softening terms Netanyahu had added at the eleventh hour regarding the Israeli withdrawal from Gaza and Hamas's disarmament; Netanyahu had secured changes slowing and limiting Israel's pullout while tightening requirements on disarmament and the demilitarization of Gaza.[26] The U.S. proposal required Hamas to release all hostages within 72 hours, disarm, and have no future role in Gaza's governance.[26] A source close to Hamas said the plan was "completely biased to Israel" and imposed "impossible conditions that aim to eliminate the group," while another Palestinian official told Reuters it amounted to "the full adoption of all Israeli conditions."[26]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry summarized the choice facing Hamas leadership as agreeing to the deal and leaving Gaza to live in peace elsewhere, or rejecting it and seeing [[israel|Israel]] take and annex Gaza.[26] He restated his theory that Hamas was created as part of a Hegelian dialectic to justify militant Zionism and the eventual annexation of Palestine, but noted that with the IDF having repeatedly wiped out Hamas leadership, whoever now led the group likely never expected the responsibility, and might "choose wisely" with guidance from Arab and Muslim leaders.[26]

## German arrests (October 2025)

German authorities detained three suspected Hamas members in Berlin during a police operation targeting a weapons handover, according to security sources cited by *Der Spiegel*; [[germany|Germany]]'s Federal Prosecutor General confirmed the provisional arrests.[26] All three were accused of membership in Hamas — designated a foreign terrorist organization by the [[european-union|European Union]], the United States, and Israel — and of preparing serious acts of violence endangering the state, with investigators alleging they were tasked by Hamas leaders with acquiring firearms and ammunition in Germany for attacks on Jewish and Israeli targets.[26] Prosecutors said the arrests yielded "various weapons, including an AK-47 assault rifle and several pistols, and a significant amount of ammunition," with additional searches in Leipzig and Oberhausen.[26] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry read the case as a deployment of "Hamas terrorist" narratives across the West to sell "the idea of a forever war with Islam."[26]

## February 2025 hostage-prisoner exchange

On Saturday, February 1, 2025, Hamas gunmen released three more hostages and Israel freed more than 180 Palestinian prisoners, conducting the latest exchange under the ceasefire deal and avoiding the chaos that had marked a drawn-out transfer earlier that week.[27] Hamas released Yarden Bibas, 35, and Ofer Kalderon, 54, in a highly theatrical ceremony in Khan Younis, then released Keith Siegel, a 65-year-old American Israeli, in a separate ceremony in Gaza City; the three were escorted by Red Cross workers to hospitals in Israel after 15 months in captivity.[27] Israel said it had released 183 Palestinian prisoners, with buses reaching Ramallah in the occupied West Bank and the Red Cross bringing another group to the European Hospital in Khan Younis.[27]

Badlands commentary by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] questioned the optics of the release, noting that the freed hostages appeared well fed and in good spirits, smiling, waving and shaking hands with their captors, while many released Palestinian prisoners — including some detained for 24 months before the October 7 attack without due process — appeared emaciated.[27] The same commentary noted that indirect negotiations on the deal's second phase faced a fundamental gap, with Hamas seeking to end the war and remain in power in Gaza while Israel opposed ending the war while Hamas governs.[27] It also addressed [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s insistence that Gazans be relocated to Egypt and Jordan, a proposal rejected by surrounding Arab nations including Saudi Arabia as tantamount to ethnic cleansing, which the author read as a feint within a larger negotiation.[27]

## Rising Gazan support during the 2024 war

In a June 2024 Foreign Affairs op-ed summarized by [[tass|TASS]], Audrey Kurth Cronin, director of the Carnegie Mellon Institute for Strategy and Technology, argued that Israel's military operation in Gaza had been a "strategic disaster" because support for Hamas among Gazans was increasing rather than collapsing.[28] Cronin held that although dismantling Hamas was the operation's primary objective, the Israeli armed forces had merely reduced the number of Hamas fighters.[28] She cited a March 2024 opinion poll by the Palestinian Center for Policy and Survey Research showing support for Hamas among Gazans topping 50%, a 14-point rise since December 2023.[28] "Simply put, despite some tactical victories, the Israeli war in Gaza has been a strategic disaster," she concluded, attributing the shift to the deaths of tens of thousands of civilians, restrictions on humanitarian aid, and Israeli operations in [[rafah-invasion|Rafah]].[28]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the outcome looked like failure only if one assumed Israel's endgame was Hamas's destruction; growing support for Hamas, in his reading, served [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s stated expectation of a decade-long war and the subversion of an earlier peace framework.[28]


## Israeli eradication pledge (November 2023)

On November 30, 2023, Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] warned the United States that "nothing will stop" Israel's army from "eradicating" Hamas, speaking after a Jerusalem meeting with Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]].[29] He vowed to continue the war until all hostages were freed and the group destroyed, while [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] reportedly pressed concerns about an Israeli advance into southern Gaza, where large numbers of Palestinians had fled.[29] A U.S. official said Netanyahu regarded a southern operation as essential to eliminating Hamas.[29]

## Qatari hosting and the September 2024 Mar-a-Lago meeting

Ahead of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s September 23, 2024 meeting with Qatari leaders at Mar-a-Lago, [[qatar|Qatar]] had come under attack from many Republican lawmakers over its hosting of Hamas and its bankrolling of [[al-jazeera|Al Jazeera]].[30] Qatar argues that the request to open a Hamas bureau in Doha came from the United States, which under both Democratic and Republican administrations supported its continued operation, and the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration was particularly complimentary of Qatar's mediation between Israel and Hamas during the Gaza war.[30] Badlands Media added its own assessment that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], despite publicly accusing Doha of financing Hamas, "was likely working with specific elements in Qatar to support Hamas over Fatah."[30]

## October 2025 Egypt negotiations

By early October 2025, Hamas had accepted parts of Washington's proposal for ending the Gaza war, prompting a fresh round of negotiations that began in Egypt on Monday, October 6, 2025.[31] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said he was "pretty sure" a deal would be reached and confirmed he had red lines for Hamas in the talks, stating: "If certain things aren't met, we're not going to do it."[31] Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had reportedly characterized Hamas's ambivalent response as "nothing to celebrate," a remark that triggered private friction with Trump.[31]

## Moscow delegation (October 2023)

On October 26, 2023, the [[russian-foreign-ministry|Russian Foreign Ministry]] confirmed that a Hamas delegation led by senior figure [[moussa-abu-marzouk|Moussa Abu Marzouk]] had arrived in Moscow for unannounced talks.[32] Although Hamas is designated a terrorist organization in the United States, the European Union and other countries, it maintains official relations with [[iran]], [[turkey]], [[syria]] and — following this visit — [[russia]].[32] Russian state media reported that the delegation "commended Putin's position and the efforts of Russia's diplomacy," while the Kremlin said it was conducting crisis diplomacy with both Hamas and Israeli interlocutors at a time when more than 220 hostages remained in [[gaza]], of whom four had been released largely through Qatari mediation.[32] Badlands commentary argued that Hamas was no victim but had made victims of Palestinians and Israelis alike, appearing intent on prolonging the conflict.[32] See [[2023-hamas-delegation-visit-to-moscow]].

## Amnesty offer, September 2025

In late September 2025 Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said he was open to granting Hamas amnesty if the group freed the remaining hostages and left [[gaza|Gaza]], a departure from his long-held insistence that the war could end only with the complete dismantling of Hamas and its leadership.[33] Under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s peace plan, Hamas leaders would be granted safe passage out of Gaza if the 48 remaining hostages were released within 48 hours of Israel's public acceptance, in exchange for Israel freeing hundreds of Palestinian prisoners serving life terms, over 1,000 Gazans detained since the war began, and the remains of several hundred Palestinians killed in the conflict.[33]

The plan stated that Hamas members committing to "peaceful coexistence" would receive amnesty, while those choosing to leave Gaza would be given safe passage to third countries.[33] When Trump unveiled the finalised 20-point plan at the White House on 29 September, he said Arab and Muslim countries had committed to disarming Hamas and that "Now it's time for Hamas to accept the terms of the plan that we put forward today."[33] Egyptian and Qatari mediators delivered the proposal to Hamas negotiators late that night, with a response expected to take several days at least.[33] Badlands analysts noted that amnesty and exile were precisely what the Arab League and Palestinian Authority had been demanding of Hamas since July.[33]

## ICC warrant applications, May 2024

[[international-criminal-court|ICC]] prosecutor [[karim-khan|Karim Khan]]'s May 2024 announcement sought arrest warrants covering Hamas leaders [[yahya-sinwar|Yahya Sinwar]], Muhammad Deif and [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]] alongside Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]].[34] Britain criticized the decision specifically for the equivalence it drew between the two sets of leaders.[34]

## Hostage negotiations after the ceasefire collapse (December 2023)

Following the collapse of the week-long Israel–Hamas ceasefire on December 1, 2023, mediation efforts resumed with a December 18 meeting in Poland between [[cia|CIA]] Director [[william-burns|William Burns]], [[mossad|Mossad]] chief [[david-barnea|David Barnea]] and Qatari Prime Minister [[mohammed-bin-abdulrahman-al-thani|Mohammad bin Abdulrahman al Thani]] aimed at freeing dozens of hostages still held in Gaza.[35] U.S. and Israeli officials said previous talks had stalled over an impasse concerning the remaining female hostages, whose release Israel insisted on before considering elderly and infirm men; Hamas claimed the women it continued to hold were IDF soldiers, an assertion the United States and Israel denied.[35]

Separately, the [[european-commission|European Commission]] cited the dissemination of illegal content in the context of Hamas' attacks against Israel among the grounds for its first [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]] proceedings against [[x-corp|X]] that same month.[35]

## January 2025 ceasefire finalization dispute

As mediators announced a phased deal on January 15, 2025 to end the 15-month war in Gaza, Israel's Prime Minister's Office accused Hamas of backing out of some agreements and creating a "crisis" in finalizing the deal, alleging it was throwing last-minute wrenches into negotiations to extort concessions.[36] No evidence was provided for the allegation and Hamas denied it.[36] Agreements on the hostage release were reported concluded in Doha in the early hours of January 17.[36]

## May 2024 negotiations and US assessments of eradication

In May 2024, reporting indicated a "widespread belief" among US officials that Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s goal of "eradicating" Hamas in Gaza was unattainable.[37] The March 2024 annual threat assessment of the US intelligence agencies stated that Israel would face "lingering armed resistance" for "years to come" and that the Israeli military would struggle to destroy Hamas's underground infrastructure, while earlier reporting indicated the United States doubted from October 2023 that Israel had a definable and achievable military objective — one source describing an October 27 Biden administration meeting said, "we never had a clear sense that the Israelis had a definable and achievable military objective."[37]

During the same period, Hamas fired rockets at the Kerem Shalom Crossing, injuring ten people, as the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] prepared for an operation in Rafah.[37] A Hamas delegation held a day of talks in Cairo with [[cia|CIA]] Director [[william-burns|William Burns]] and with Egyptian and Qatari officials before departing, with a return expected; mediators described the Cairo round as near collapse.[37] Hamas reiterated its demand for an end to the war in exchange for freeing hostages, a condition Netanyahu flatly rejected, while a three-phase proposal under discussion would have released 33 of the 132 remaining hostages for a 40-day pause.[37]

## Response to the Trump Gaza peace proposal

On Friday, October 3, 2025, Hamas returned what Axios characterized as a "yes, but" answer to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Gaza peace proposal.[38] In its official response, the group said it was willing to release all remaining hostages in return for an end to the war and a full Israeli military withdrawal from [[gaza|Gaza]], but requested negotiations on many of the details.[38] Trump treated the reply as an opening for a deal, having feared an outright rejection, while Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] told associates in private consultations that he viewed it as a rejection of the plan.[38] Badlands host [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] asked wryly whether Trump would have to sign a defense cooperation agreement with Hamas to end the war.[38]


## July 2025 ceasefire talks

Ahead of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s July 2025 visit to Washington, the Israeli prime minister vowed that Hamas's military and governing capabilities would be eliminated and that the group "will not be there," rejecting any hostage deal that would leave it in power in Gaza.[39] An Israeli negotiating team travelled to Doha for indirect talks with the group at the same time.[39] Hamas was reported to have responded "positively" to recently proposed ceasefire terms, while President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly stated that he expected to negotiate an end to the Gaza war during his meeting with Netanyahu.[39]

## Abbas's record of opposition (2007–2018)

Badlands Media compiled a sequence of confrontations between Hamas and [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] to argue that Abbas has been the principal Palestinian figure attempting to curb the organization.[40] In 2007, after Hamas drove both [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] security forces and the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] out of Gaza, Abbas declared that his government would hold no dialogue with the group's "murderous terrorists."[40] In 2017 he refused to appoint Hamas officials to the Palestinian government unless they recognized the state of Israel.[40] In 2018, Hamas attempted to assassinate his prime minister with a roadside bomb during a mission to negotiate the peaceful transfer of the Gazan government to the Palestinian Authority, after which Abbas sanctioned the organization and cut it off financially in an attempt to destroy it.[40]

According to the same commentary, Hamas survived that financial squeeze because [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] arranged for Israel to carry briefcases containing tens of millions of dollars in cash into Gaza and distribute them to families with ties to the organization — a scheme exposed when a cabinet minister resigned in protest and went public.[40] The outlet argued on this basis that the United States and Israeli governments had directly funded the group's rise to power.[40] In September 2024, Israel's UN ambassador [[danny-danon|Danny Danon]] accused Abbas of failing to condemn the October 7 Hamas attack that triggered the Gaza war.[40]

## Calls for escalation after the April 2024 Israeli strike on Iran

In April 2024, after Israel struck Iranian soil and both [[iran|Iran]] and Israel signalled that their exchange of strikes was finished, Hamas was reported as the only party pressing for a wider conflict: a Hamas official called the Israeli aggression on Iran "an escalation against the region" and called for expanding engagement against Israel over the war in Gaza.[41] Iranian Army Commander-in-Chief Abdolrahim Mousavi indicated Tehran did not feel compelled to react, and President [[ebrahim-raisi|Ebrahim Raisi]] made a planned speech that ignored the overnight attack, praising "Operation True Promise" instead.[41] ZeroHedge assessed that Hamas leadership likely believed the episode marked the start of a "big war" in Gaza's defense.[41]

Badlands commentary described Hamas as throwing a "temper tantrum" on the same day that [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben Gvir]] and allies within the Israeli cabinet did the same, arguing it was strange that two ostensible enemies shared an identical vision of kinetic escalation and questioning the media narrative that Iran controlled Hamas.[41]

## Context of the April 2024 escalation

By April 2024, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] had publicly criticized Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] over the civilian toll of [[israel|Israel]]'s campaign against Hamas, particularly after a strike killed seven aid workers, even while pledging "ironclad" support for Israel against threatened [[iran|Iranian]] reprisals.[42] Israeli troops and warplanes began an overnight operation in central Gaza on April 11 that the military said was aimed at destroying the infrastructure of armed Palestinian groups.[42]

## Hostage pressure after the December 2023 friendly-fire killings

After [[israel-defense-forces|Israeli troops]] mistakenly shot and killed three Israeli hostages carrying a white flag in Gaza's Shijaiyah neighborhood on December 15, 2023, pressure mounted on Israel to negotiate a new agreement for the release of the more than 130 hostages still held by Hamas.[43] Hamas had previously claimed that some hostages were killed in Israeli airstrikes on Gaza, though the December incident marked the first IDF acknowledgment of accidentally killing hostages during its ground operation.[43]

## Gantz resignation and the state of the war (June 2024)

In June 2024 [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] withdrew his party from Israel's emergency government, accusing Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] of preventing Israel from achieving "true victory" in the war against Hamas.[44] Contemporary reporting summarized the war's origin as the October 7, 2023 assault in which thousands of Hamas-led terrorists stormed into southern Israel, killing some 1,200 people, mostly civilians, and taking 251 hostages into the Gaza Strip.[44]

## Hostage families and the Netanyahu coup claim

In June 2024 [[sara-netanyahu|Sara Netanyahu]] reportedly told families of hostages taken by Hamas that [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] leadership was "staging a coup" against her husband, prompting one bereaved father to say publicly that no hostage deal would be reached while [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] remained in power.[45]

## Riyadh two-state alliance meeting (October 2024)

At the first meeting of the [[international-alliance-to-implement-the-two-state-solution|International Alliance to Implement the Two-State Solution]] in Riyadh on October 30, 2024, Saudi Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] accused Israel of conducting a genocide aimed at evicting the Palestinian people from their land.[46] Israel rejected the accusation, stating that the goal of its ongoing operation was to eliminate Hamas and to rescue the 251 hostages kidnapped on October 7, 2023, an assault in which some 1,200 people were killed.[46]

By the end of October 2024, Israeli and US officials assessed that [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], after severe losses including the assassination of Hassan Nasrallah, was finally willing to disconnect itself from Hamas in Gaza, opening the way for a possible Lebanon ceasefire brokered by Biden advisers [[amos-hochstein|Amos Hochstein]] and [[brett-mcgurk|Brett McGurk]].[46]

## Killing of Yahya Sinwar (October 2024)

Israeli forces killed Hamas leader [[yahya-sinwar|Yahya Sinwar]] — described as a chief architect of the October 7, 2023 attack — in southern Gaza on October 16, 2024, with the military announcing the death the following day after troops realized a body in the rubble was Israel's most-wanted man.[47] Israeli leaders framed the killing as a turning point in the campaign to destroy the organization, urging it to surrender and release the roughly 100 hostages still held, while Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] declared, "Hamas will no longer rule Gaza."[47]

Observers cautioned that Sinwar's removal might not end the war, in which Israel had destroyed much of the Gaza Strip and killed more than 42,000 Palestinians according to the Gaza Health Ministry, more than half of them reportedly women and children.[47] Although the death was a crippling blow, Hamas — which receives support from [[iran|Iran]] — had proven resilient to past losses of leaders, and offered no immediate confirmation.[47] With both [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]] and Sinwar dead, President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] told Netanyahu it was time to end the war and pushed for a hostage-and-ceasefire deal covering the 101 remaining captives.[47]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] restated his view that figures such as Sinwar are cultivated by intelligence services like the [[cia|CIA]] and [[mi6|MI6]] through lifelong indoctrination and radicalization to serve as the villains needed to justify wars of conquest, with financing routed through intermediaries to preserve deniability.[47]

## October 2025 ceasefire flare-up

On October 29, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] declared that "nothing is going to jeopardize" the Gaza ceasefire, while adding that Israel "should hit back" if its soldiers were killed, a day after an exchange of fire in which Jerusalem and Hamas accused each other of violating the deal.[48] "They killed an Israeli soldier. So the Israelis hit back. And they should hit back," Trump told reporters aboard Air Force One, adding that "Hamas is a very small part of peace in the Middle East, and they have to behave," and warning that if the group did not behave its members would "be terminated."[48] A senior aide had earlier said US special envoy [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]] and adviser [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] gave Hamas verbal assurances that Washington would hold Israel to the deal so long as Hamas kept its side.[48]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that the IDF soldier had been killed not by Hamas but by [[palestinian-islamic-jihad|Palestinian Islamic Jihad]], a militant group founded six or seven years before Hamas that has no political or diplomatic component and seeks only Israel's annihilation.[48] He noted that Hamas, by contrast, maintains a diplomatic mission — evidenced by the peace talks in Doha and Egypt — and a civic political infrastructure that has administered Gaza, however poorly, since 2006.[48] Hamas issued statements denying involvement, saying it had lost contact months earlier with its operators in the area where the attack occurred; he judged it implausible that the group would orchestrate the killing of a single soldier only to deny it, since the denial forfeits sympathy among Gaza militants while inviting significant retribution.[48] The prudent course, he wrote, would be for Hamas to stand down and let [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] be the party to disrupt Trump's negotiations.[48]

## October 2023 attack and the US response

Following the weekend assault by Hamas militants into southern [[israel|Israel]] on 7 October 2023, US officials described the group's tactics as brutal and unprecedented, and the combined death toll exceeded 1,500 within days.[49] Israel's government ordered a full-scale siege of the [[gaza|Gaza Strip]] on Monday, 9 October, and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] began "surging support" to Israel, with air defense equipment and munitions already in the air.[49] The attack also prompted Syrian missile fire toward the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]] and an exchange of fire between Israel and likely [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] positions in northern Lebanon, raising fears of a multi-front war.[49] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that the burden of proof for the factual component of such events falls on those using them to guide the collective mind toward endorsing a new worldwide conflict, describing the moment as a "War of Stories."[49]

## Contested origins and radicalization argument (2024 commentary)

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote in September 2024 that he was "firmly on record" in believing Hamas to be a creation of Western and Israeli intelligence agencies, intended to subvert the authority of the [[fatah|Fatah]] faction of the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] by promoting militant attitudes among the population, while treating the question of whether [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] shares a similar origin as separate.[50] He drew an analogy to American groups such as the [[oath-keepers|Oath Keepers]], [[proud-boys|Proud Boys]] and [[patriot-front|Patriot Front]], which he argued had at minimum been infiltrated by federal agents and used to demonize the nationalist-populist movement, and speculated that such groups could become "the Hamas/Hezbollah of America" in a collapsed United States.[50] He further argued that people joining Hamas in September 2024 might be doing so less from indoctrination than from desperation under occupation, describing the situation in Gaza and the West Bank since Israel's 1967 military occupation as a farm for growing terrorists.[50]

## August 2025 refusal to disarm

Hamas stated in August 2025 that it would not disarm unless an independent Palestinian state was established with Jerusalem as its capital, issuing the statement in response to media reports quoting U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff claiming the group had shown willingness to disarm.[52] The group declared that "resistance and its arms are a legitimate national and legal right as long as the occupation continues," a right recognized by international laws and norms and relinquishable only through full restoration of national rights.[52] It also dismissed Witkoff's visit to a Gaza aid distribution centre as "a premeditated staged show," while Israeli fire killed 18 people in Gaza the same period.[52] Badlands host Chris Paul framed the sequence as a long divisive narrative cycle running from the October 7 attack to eventual recognition of a Palestinian state and the end of "Hamas," arguing that terrorist groups function as private armies of a global regime.[52]

## US veto of the February 2024 ceasefire resolution

On February 20, 2024, the United States cast the lone veto against an Algerian-drafted [[united-nations-security-council|UN Security Council]] resolution calling for a ceasefire in Gaza, the third such veto, on a 13–1 vote with the United Kingdom abstaining.[53] Washington argued that a complete ceasefire would jeopardise negotiations involving Egypt and Qatar to release the hostages Hamas abducted during its October 7 assault, which killed some 1,200 people and precipitated the Israeli campaign in Gaza.[53]

## US ceasefire-revival offer via Qatar (March 2025)

On March 27, 2025, a senior Arab diplomat said Qatar had presented Hamas with a new US proposal to restore the Gaza ceasefire through the release of American-Israeli hostage [[edan-alexander|Edan Alexander]], in exchange for which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would issue a statement calling for calm in Gaza and the resumption of negotiations toward a permanent ceasefire.[54] The diplomat doubted Hamas would free Alexander for only a statement and expected the group to seek something more substantial, given its skepticism about Israel's willingness to adhere to agreements; Alexander was one of 59 remaining hostages, 24 of them believed alive by Israeli intelligence.[54]

Badlands analysis tied the offer to [[adam-boehler|Adam Boehler]]'s direct meeting with Hamas in Doha earlier that month, after which he had predicted a deal within weeks, and noted that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s allies had already declared unacceptable any arrangement releasing only dual nationals.[54]

## Assassination of Ismail Haniyeh

Hamas leader [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]] was assassinated in Tehran, Iran's Revolutionary Guard said early on July 31, 2024.[56] Suspicion fell immediately on [[israel|Israel]], which had vowed to kill Haniyeh and other Hamas leaders over the group's October 7 attack that killed 1,200 people and saw some 250 taken hostage.[56] The killing came as the Biden administration was pressing Hamas and Israel to agree at least a temporary ceasefire and hostage-release deal.[56]

## ICC warrant applications against Hamas leaders (May 2024)

On May 20, 2024, ICC chief prosecutor [[karim-khan|Karim Khan]] sought arrest warrants for three Hamas figures — [[yahya-sinwar|Yehiya Sinwar]], Izzedine al-Qassam Brigades leader [[mohammed-deif|Mohammed Diab Ibrahim al-Masri]], and political leader [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]] — alongside Israeli leaders [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]], on charges of war crimes and crimes against humanity.[57] Colonel [[douglas-macgregor|Douglas Macgregor]] characterized the simultaneous targeting of both sides as evidence of the court's "even-handedness," a reading Badlands commentary framed as both sides of a controlled opposition dynamic being pursued at once.[57]

## Hostage remains handover, February 2025

On Thursday, February 20, 2025, Hamas returned four sets of remains to Israel in what Israeli media described as a propaganda-filled ceremony.[58] Specialists at the Abu Kabir Forensic Institute identified [[oded-lifshitz|Oded Lifshitz]] and the brothers Ariel and Kfir of the [[bibas-family|Bibas family]], but the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] announced early the following morning that a fourth body was not the boys' mother Shiri Bibas and could not be matched to any other hostage.[58] The IDF called this "a very serious violation by the Hamas terrorist organization, which is required by the agreement to return four dead hostages," and demanded that Hamas "return Shiri home, along with all of our hostages."[58] The failure left the future of the hostage-ceasefire deal more uncertain than ever, as hostage families demanded the release of all captives and Israeli leaders vowed to destroy the group.[58]

## Declaration that the Gaza war had ended (October 2025)

On October 9, 2025 senior Hamas official [[khalil-al-hayya|Khalil al-Hayya]] said in a televised address to Gaza residents that the Gaza war was over, and that the peace plan put forward by US President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would mark the start of a "permanent ceasefire."[59] He said the group had received "guarantees" from Washington and other mediators that hostilities would not resume.[59]

Al-Hayya said Hamas "dealt responsibly with the American president's plan" and submitted a response aimed at preventing further bloodshed, and that the deal reached at Sharm el-Sheikh, Egypt, provided for humanitarian aid to the enclave, the opening of the [[rafah-border-crossing|Rafah border crossing]], and a prisoner exchange.[59] "Everyone confirmed that the war has completely ended," he said, pledging to work with all national and Islamic forces on the agreement's next steps.[59] The statement came while the Israeli cabinet was still voting on ratification, with National Security Minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] threatening to leave the government if Hamas retained control over Gaza.[59]

## Warning of ceasefire collapse (February 2025)
In February 2025 a senior Hamas official warned that the fragile [[gaza-ceasefire|ceasefire in Gaza]] was at risk of "collapse," according to Agence France-Presse, with the group accusing Israel of failing to uphold its commitments under a truce that had halted intense fighting nearly three weeks earlier.[51] The ceasefire, brokered by Qatar, Egypt and the United States, was structured in three phases; in the first, Hamas was to release 33 hostages — including children, female soldiers, the wounded and the sick — in exchange for 1,904 Palestinians imprisoned by Israeli authorities.[51] On that Saturday, 183 Palestinian detainees held in Israeli prisons were released while Hamas freed three Israeli captives, whose emaciated appearance West Jerusalem described as "shocking."[51]
Badlands commentary reported that Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had altered the terms of the deal, demanding that Hamas leadership leave Gaza permanently, and argued the demand's timing — directly after the hostage release and during Phase Two — risked collapsing the agreement brokered under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[51]

## Reported Hamas handover to the PA (February 2025)

Unconfirmed Arab media reports in mid-February 2025 said Hamas had agreed to cede governance of Gaza to the West Bank-based [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], a decision Sky News Arabia attributed to pressure from Egypt, a mediator of the group's month-old truce and hostage deal with Israel.[55] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] rejected the prospect outright, stating that "there will be neither Hamas nor the Palestinian Authority" in Gaza after the war, while his spokesman Omer Dostri wrote on X: "Not going to happen."[55] An Israeli official said Israel would not permit Hamas to stay in Gaza under any circumstances.[55]

## Aftermath of the October 2023 assault

By the end of Monday, October 9, 2023, the number of Israeli hostages held by Hamas had been revised upward to an estimated 150, while at least 900 Israelis had been reported killed and nearly 2,400 wounded since the Saturday surprise assault from Gaza; Palestinian emergency sources reported 560 dead and over 2,700 wounded in Gaza under heavy Israeli bombardment.[60] Hostages were seized from homes in border towns including Kibbutz Nahal Oz, from military bases, and from a large outdoor dance party, and included civilians, soldiers, people with disabilities, children, grandparents and a nine-month-old baby, as well as at least one Palestinian resident of Israel — a bus driver who had driven Israelis to the party.[60] The New York Times assessed that the capture of so many Israelis had taken the conflict into uncharted territory, both in the sheer number of hostages and in the dire threats Hamas was making against them.[60] The Washington Post verified footage indicating that at least four hostages were killed soon after being taken, with bodies filmed in the street at [[kibbutz-beeri|Kibbutz Be'eri]] yards from where militants had been recorded walking with those same captives.[60] The Wall Street Journal subsequently reported that officers of the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]] had planned and signed off on the operation.[60]

## Carlson on origins and American opinion

Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] wrote in November 2023 that the Israeli government and [[mossad|MOSSAD]] helped create Hamas in the late 1980s as a counterweight to the [[palestine-liberation-organization|PLO]], and that MOSSAD retains controllable factions within the organisation even if the whole is no longer under its command.[63] He framed the 2023 conflict as one in which both sides contain [[deep-state|Deep State]] components working together, with ordinary people treated as acceptable collateral damage in a contrived effort to produce a larger war.[63]

An October 18–19, 2023 [[harvard-harris-poll|Harvard/Harris]] survey of 2,116 Americans found 48% of respondents aged 18–24 siding more with Hamas than with Israel, against 5% of those 65 and over, and 51% of the youngest cohort saying the killing of 1,200 Israeli civilians could be justified by Palestinian grievances, against 9% of the oldest.[63] Carlson read the generational split as evidence of targeted propaganda rather than considered judgment.[63]
