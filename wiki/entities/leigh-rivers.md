# Leigh Rivers

Leigh Rivers is a Canadian academic who teaches chemical and physical sciences at the [[university-of-toronto|University of Toronto]], and who became a public figure in Canadian debates over academic freedom after being sanctioned by his department for a classroom exercise intended to encourage open debate.[1]

## The classroom exercise

Rivers conducted an experiment in [[spectrum-street-epistemology|Spectrum Street epistemology]], a discussion method in which a series of lines inscribed on the floor represent a spectrum running from agreement through neutrality to disagreement, and participants physically position themselves according to their view of a stated proposition.[1] The method is presented by its advocates as a tool for fostering critical thinking and self-examination rather than for advancing any particular political conclusion.[1]

According to accounts of the incident, Rivers opened with the claim "Vegetarians are morally superior to omnivores," which drew no institutional objection.[1] Subsequent prompts did. A statement referring to Prof. [[jordan-peterson|Jordan Peterson]] — "Prof. Jordan Peterson requires media training" — was, per Rivers, deemed by his department chair to be too stressful for University of Toronto students, a judgment described as absurd given Peterson's own prior status as a U of T professor and his prominence in Canadian public discussion.[1] Rivers was also told that the propositions "Western science is hampered by political bias" and "There are other ways of doing science beyond the Western tradition" were inappropriate.[1]

The most serious objection, in Rivers' telling, concerned the statement "Men can become women," which his chair characterised as traumatic and which, he was told, should have been accompanied by trigger warnings and safe spaces.[1] Rivers quoted his chair as saying, "We can sit here and pretend, but you and I both know that you overstepped the boundaries," a remark cited as evidence that the ideology in question is held by its adherents to be self-evidently correct.[1] When Rivers asked what sort of claim would be acceptable in future, the chair reportedly suggested "Pineapple belongs on pizza."[1]

One of Rivers' students, described as a chemical and physical sciences major working toward a graduate degree, responded to the prompt on political bias in science by asserting openly that Western science is infected by patriarchal, Euro-centric ideas — a response cited as illustrating how far ideological framing has penetrated even the hard sciences.[1]

## Public profile and the Heterodox screening

Rivers subsequently went viral over his sanction, including through an opinion piece under the framing that he was a University of Toronto professor punished for encouraging debate.[1] He spoke at a Toronto screening of [[the-coddling-of-the-american-mind|The Coddling of the American Mind]] organised by the Toronto chapter of the [[heterodox-academy|Heterodox Academy]], attended by [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]], who reported on the evening.[1]

At that screening Rivers was the first professor present to raise what became a recurring theme of the discussion: a sudden wave of students submitting "accommodation letters" demanding preferential treatment and extended deadlines.[1] His account was echoed by other faculty, including one who had taught at the [[ontario-college-of-art-and-design|Ontario College of Art and Design]] and reported an earlier and sharper spike there.[1]

Rivers' case has been used as a case study in the argument that certain topics have become taboo within academia and that even the physical sciences are no longer insulated from political orthodoxy, particularly since gender theory's entry into human biology.[1]
