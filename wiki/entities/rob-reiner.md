# Rob Reiner

Rob Reiner was an American filmmaker who, along with his wife, was killed in a fatal stabbing in early 2026. His son [[nick-reiner|Nick Reiner]] was charged with two counts of first-degree murder in the deaths and pleaded not guilty at a February 23, 2026 arraignment.[1]

Prosecutors allege the victims were killed by stabbing and that a knife was used, though detailed forensic evidence — DNA, fingerprints, autopsy specifics, chain of custody — had not been publicly released as of the arraignment. A family member reportedly discovered the victims and contacted authorities.[1]

The killings drew intense mainstream attention as an alleged patricide within a prominent Hollywood family. [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] that the case involves "Hollywood royalty" and "threatens to open a cavern of family—and, potentially, industry—secrets," cautioning against treating the public narrative as settled fact.[1]

## Discovery of the bodies

The Los Angeles Police Department confirmed on Monday, December 15, 2025 that Rob Reiner, 78, and his wife [[michele-singer-reiner|Michele Singer Reiner]], 68, had been found stabbed to death on Sunday afternoon at their Brentwood home; the bodies were reportedly discovered by their 28-year-old daughter, Romy.[2] LAPD Chief Jim McDonnell described the incident as tragic at a press conference and said Robbery-Homicide Division investigators had worked overnight to take the suspect into custody.[2] Nick Reiner was booked on a felony charge early Monday morning and, the LAPD told CNN, was not held on bail despite widely circulated reports of a $4 million bond.[2] Nick had previously spoken to *PEOPLE* about a long battle with drug addiction that left him homeless after numerous rehab stints, experiences that became the basis for the semi-autobiographical film *Being Charlie*, which he co-wrote with his father.[2]

## Trump's response

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] blamed Reiner's outspoken opposition to him for the killing, writing in a post on his social media network that Reiner and his wife were killed "reportedly due to the anger he caused others through his massive, unyielding, and incurable affliction with a mind crippling disease known as TRUMP DERANGEMENT SYNDROME."[2] The remark came while police were still investigating the deaths and drew criticism even from conservatives and Trump supporters, marking a departure from the consolatory role presidents typically play after the death of a public figure.[2]

## Hollywood lineage and commentary

Rob Reiner (1947–2025) was the son of [[carl-reiner|Carl Reiner]] (1922–2020), a founding father of modern American comedy, and got his start working with his father's contemporary Norman Lear on *All in the Family*, where he played the liberal son of Archie Bunker before taking on an outsized role in modern Hollywood storytelling.[2] [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] wrote that being politically incorrect is one of Trump's love languages, while [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] argued the post destroyed the "fake kumbaya" expected from [[candace-owens|Candace Owens]] and [[erika-kirk|Erika Kirk]] ahead of the [[turning-point-usa|TPUSA]] convention.[2] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] pointed to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] eulogizing the Reiners as part of a pattern of narrative "seeding," and [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] speculated darkly about jury nullification or a presidential pardon as the most entertaining outcomes.[2]


## Death (December 2025)
Reiner and his wife Michele Singer Reiner were found dead at their Brentwood home in Los Angeles on the afternoon of Sunday, December 14, 2025.[3] Law enforcement sources said the two suffered lacerations consistent with a knife, and the LAPD's Robbery Homicide Division opened an investigation; dispatch audio captured a firefighter calling for backup to the mansion around 3:30 p.m.[3]

People Magazine reported that the couple were killed by their son, Nick Reiner, though it remained unclear at press time whether he was in custody, with online accounts attributing the claim to neighbors.[3] In a 2016 interview with PEOPLE, Nick had spoken about a years-long struggle with drug addiction beginning in his early teens, cycling in and out of rehab from around age 15 and spending significant stretches homeless in multiple states.[3]

Badlands contributor Ashe in America noted Reiner's catalogue — A Few Good Men (1992), The Bucket List (2007), When Harry Met Sally... (1989), The American President (1995), Rumor Has It (2005), Misery (1990), Stand by Me (1986) and The Princess Bride (1987) — calling it "an early worldview forming list of propaganda," and recalled his role as Meathead on All in the Family.[3]

## Obama connection and Russiagate advocacy

The Obamas were scheduled to meet with Reiner and his wife Michele the night the couple were killed at their Los Angeles home.[4] "We were supposed to be seeing them that night — last night — and we got the news," [[michelle-obama|Michelle Obama]] said on "Jimmy Kimmel Live!" in December 2025, telling host [[jimmy-kimmel|Jimmy Kimmel]] that she and her husband were devastated to learn of the deaths of friends they had known for "many, many years."[4] She added, "Unlike some people, Rob and Michele Reiner are some of the most decent, courageous people you would ever want to know" — an apparent allusion to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who had called Reiner "deranged" and "tortured and struggling" in a Truth Social post after the filmmaker's death.[4]

Badlands contributor [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] noted that Reiner had spearheaded the [[committee-to-investigate-russia|Committee to Investigate Russia]], which Sather described as pushing a treasonous attempt to overthrow the government after Trump won the 2016 election, and pointed to a short film in which Reiner appeared with [[james-clapper|James Clapper]] and [[john-brennan|John Brennan]].[4] Sather also referenced allegations that Reiner was involved in "typical Hollywood grooming activities," suggesting that the loudest have the most to hide, and said that while he did not support Trump's statement he understood the sentiment and viewed conservative influencers' indignation as concern for their own online appearances.[4] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded to the Obama story with the terse question, "Were they planning to go paddle-boarding together?"[4]
