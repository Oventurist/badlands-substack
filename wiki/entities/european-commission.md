# European Commission

The European Commission is the executive body of the [[european-union|European Union]], led in 2026 by President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]].[1]

## Turnberry trade deal dispute

In May 2026, President [[donald-trump|Trump]] publicly pressed the Commission over what he described as unfulfilled obligations under the trade agreement reached at Turnberry, Scotland, which he called the largest trade deal ever.[1] In a Truth Social post, Trump said he and von der Leyen had a "great call" and were "completely united that Iran can never have a Nuclear Weapon," while stating that the EU had promised to cut its tariffs to zero and that he had given her until the United States' 250th birthday before tariffs would "immediately jump to much higher levels."[1]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the message as Trump warning the [[collectorate|Collectorate]] that timing on the Iranian question favored him, framing the episode within Trump's broader [[sovereign-disentanglement|Sovereign Disentanglement]] operations and [[power-paradigm|Power Paradigm]].[1]

## Cricket-derived food additive authorization

In January 2023 the European Commission passed a ruling authorizing a food additive made from powdered, defatted house crickets for sale across the European Union, citing the scientific opinion of the [[european-food-safety-authority|European Food Safety Authority]].[2] The decision permitted the additive in a wide range of products, "including but not limited to cereal bars, biscuits, pizza, pasta-based products, and whey powder," with the requirement that the insects "discard their bowel content" before being frozen.[2] Coverage stressed that the additive began appearing in ordinary European foods without the awareness of most consumers, and Badlands contributor Ryan DeLarme raised health objections tied to bacterial contamination and viral vectors associated with [[edible-insects|edible insects]].[2]

## Hate speech advocacy at Davos (2023)

At the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] meeting in January 2023, the Commission's Vice President for Values and Transparency, [[vera-jourova|Věra Jourová]], predicted that the United States would soon follow Europe in enacting laws against so-called hate speech, saying: "Illegal hate speech, which you will have soon also in the U.S. I think that we have a strong reason why we have this in the criminal law."[3] The statement was widely criticised in American alternative media as disregarding Supreme Court precedent holding that there is no hate-speech exception to the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[3]

## Response to Trump's 100% tariff proposal (September 2025)

In September 2025, reporting indicated that United States President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had asked the [[european-union|European Union]] to impose tariffs of up to 100 percent on China and India over their purchases of Russian oil, a request made when he was called into a meeting with senior U.S. and EU officials in Washington and one that the U.S. said it was prepared to "mirror."[4] Asked to comment, a European Commission spokesperson declined to disclose meeting details on confidentiality grounds, stating only that "the EU has engaged with all relevant global partners, including India and China, in the context of its sanctions enforcement efforts" and that "this engagement will continue."[4] Analysts on both sides of the Atlantic viewed Europe as unlikely to accede to the request, which was understood as an attempt to punish Indian and Chinese trade with Moscow and pressure Russia toward ending the war in Ukraine.[4]

## December 2015 Ukraine progress report

On December 18, 2015 the European Commission published a progress report on Ukraine that praised Prosecutor-General [[viktor-shokin|Viktor Shokin]] for his anti-corruption efforts — nine days after then-Vice President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] demanded Shokin's ouster.[5]

The report, obtained and published by the New York Post in September 2023, found that Ukraine had achieved "noteworthy" progress including in "preventing and fighting corruption," and was therefore eligible for visa-free travel in Europe.[5] It specifically noted that Shokin had just appointed the head of a specialized anti-corruption prosecution office, which the Commission called "an indispensable component of an effective and independent institutional framework for combating high-level corruption," and said the new office would help the newly established [[national-anti-corruption-bureau-of-ukraine|National Anti-Corruption Bureau]] combat corruption; it urged Ukrainian leadership to ensure both bodies were "fully operational" by the first quarter of 2016.[5]

The document was presented as contradicting Biden's claim that the European Union had joined his demand that Shokin be removed for corruption and for obstructing anti-corruption reform — and, in Badlands commentary, as evidence that the aid conditionality served the interests of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] and [[burisma]] rather than U.S. foreign policy.[5]

### Messaging discipline under von der Leyen's second term
A February 2025 Politico report described a marked centralization of authority inside the Commission under President Ursula von der Leyen at the outset of her second five-year term.[6] On the day of Donald Trump's inauguration, the communications advisers of EU commissioners were instructed by von der Leyen's cabinet that they could only repost the Commission president's January 20 message to Trump on social media and under no circumstances add words of their own.[6] Observers described the institution as evolving toward a fully presidential model, with von der Leyen and a close-knit circle of largely German advisers running the show while shunning scrutiny from the European Parliament and keeping a tight grip on all messaging and communication.[6]

## First DSA infringement proceedings against X (December 2023)

On December 18, 2023, the European Commission opened infringement proceedings against [[x-corp|X]] under the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]], the first such probe brought under the law.[7] The Commission said it acted on the basis of its preliminary investigation, including analysis of the risk assessment report X submitted in September, the platform's transparency report published on November 3, and X's replies to a formal request for information which concerned, among other matters, the dissemination of illegal content in the context of [[hamas|Hamas]]' terrorist attacks against Israel.[7] Commissioner [[thierry-breton|Thierry Breton]] framed the action as a response to suspected breaches of X's transparency obligations, its duties to counter illegal content and disinformation, and a "deceptive" user-interface design relating to blue check marks.[7]
