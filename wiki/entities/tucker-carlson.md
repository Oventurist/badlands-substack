# Tucker Carlson

Tucker Carlson is a conservative American broadcaster and commentator who, by March 2026, had become a central figure in a domestic dispute over speech, foreign-agent law and criticism of Israel during the United States' war with [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## FARA allegations

Carlson said publicly that he had learned the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] was preparing a criminal referral concerning his contacts with people in Iran, describing the supposed offense as "talking to people in Iran before the war" and stating flatly, "They read my texts."[1] A post attributed to Carlson circulating on X read: "When you discover the CIA has been reading your texts in order to frame you for a crime."[1] The claims were reported by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] on March 16, 2026.[1]

ZeroHedge subsequently updated its report to note that "top admin officials" had told Axios reporter Marc Caputo there was "no CIA investigation of Carlson."[1] Former CIA officer [[john-kiriakou|John Kiriakou]], speaking on an RT program aired March 15, 2026, said he was "genuinely worried" about Carlson's safety following the journalist's claim that the agency might pursue a referral under the [[foreign-agents-registration-act|Foreign Agents Registration Act]].[1]

## Campaign against Carlson

Badlands writers place the episode within a longer campaign. At the Republican Jewish Coalition summit in November 2025, Florida politician [[randy-fine|Randy Fine]] labeled Carlson "the most dangerous antisemite in America," a designation attributed less to Carlson's actual statements than to the size of his platform and its reach into mainstream and middle-American audiences.[1] On March 5, 2026, Fine called Carlson a "cancer" after Carlson published a podcast episode examining [[chabad-lubavitch|Chabad-Lubavitch]], the Ultra-Orthodox community whose Brooklyn headquarters had been raided by the NYPD two years earlier over "secret tunnels" found beneath the facility.[1]

Israeli-aligned online influencers called for Carlson's imprisonment for allegedly inciting violence against Jews, blaming him for a school shooting in Michigan.[1] [[mark-levin|Mark Levin]] amplified a Mediaite article reporting Republican Congressman [[max-miller|Max Miller]]'s call for Carlson to be investigated and jailed as an Iranian agent.[1] Activist [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] posted that she would take credit if Carlson were charged, saying she had been "relentless" in contacting GOP representatives, law enforcement and the Justice Department about him.[1]

## Interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted the irony that one of Levin's biggest public critics was now reportedly under FARA investigation, and criticized portions of the "truth community" for cheering the prospect of Carlson's imprisonment.[1] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] framed the affair as a Hegelian dialectic intended to criminalize dissent toward the government of [[israel|Israel]] by equating it with hatred of Jews — a slippery slope he argued would eventually be turned against criticism of the U.S. government itself.[1] In this reading, [[donald-trump|President Trump]] was deliberately baiting the pro-Israel faction and the corporate press into a collision over the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[1]

## Huckabee interview (February 2026)

In February 2026 Carlson conducted the televised interview in which US Ambassador to Israel [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] said [[israel|Israel]] has a biblical right to a large swath of the Middle East "from the Nile to the Euphrates" and that "it would be fine if they took it all," while adding that Israel was not currently seeking to expand its borders.[2] The remarks triggered condemnation from Arab and Muslim governments and regional bodies including [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], Egypt, Jordan, the [[arab-league|League of Arab States]] and the [[organization-of-islamic-cooperation|Organization of Islamic Cooperation]], and a US Embassy spokesperson said they did not reflect any change in US policy.[2] Israeli opposition leader [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] later expressed sympathy in Hebrew for the [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] concept Huckabee had articulated in the interview.[2]

## Claims of Mossad false-flag plots (2026)

In March 2026 Tucker Carlson claimed that both Qatar and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] had arrested [[mossad|Mossad]] agents who were planning to commit false-flag bombings in those countries and blame them on [[iran|Iran]].[3] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the truth of the claims remained to be seen, given that Gulf State monarchies are notoriously private and would not necessarily announce such arrests publicly, but argued it would make little sense for Carlson to invent something that could damage the very states he was defending.[3]

## Ben Gurion Airport incident (February 2026)

In February 2026 Carlson said he was "detained" and "interrogated" while attempting to depart [[israel|Israel]] at Ben Gurion Airport, stating on his X account that Israeli authorities had pulled him aside for questioning before his departure; Russian media characterized the episode as a detention and interrogation.[4] The Israeli government rejected that characterization, with officials countering that Carlson had not been detained but had undergone standard airport security screening procedures.[4] [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]], the US ambassador to Israel, also publicly disputed the claim, saying Carlson was neither detained nor interrogated beyond routine procedures.[4] As of press time the only documented facts were the competing public statements, with no independent documentation of the airport interaction publicly released.[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had created the entire dialectic by appointing Huckabee — a longtime advocate of the view that Palestine does not exist — as his first major administration appointment, a move that inflamed [[nick-fuentes|Nick Fuentes]] and the [[groypers|Groypers]].[4] He noted that Carlson's subsequent interview of Fuentes drew significant backlash and calls for censorship, to which Trump responded by defending open access to information, and concluded that Trump wanted the country having a public conversation about Israel and Zionism.[4]

## Chabad claim about the Iran war (March 2026)

In March 2026 Carlson claimed that the Jewish religious movement [[chabad-lubavitch|Chabad-Lubavitch]] was responsible for the war involving [[iran|Iran]], describing the movement as a "hidden hand" behind the conflict and suggesting it was pushing for a broader religious war connected to rebuilding the Jewish Temple in Jerusalem.[5] Critics characterized the claim as a conspiracy theory, and Jewish security organizations warned that such rhetoric could contribute to an increase in antisemitic narratives online.[5] Carlson has been among the commentators criticizing US and Israeli military actions against Iran, arguing that the conflict serves Israeli strategic interests rather than American ones and that it was initiated at the urging of Israeli leadership.[5] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said the episode brought his long-running work on the [[third-temple|Third Temple]] to an audience of millions.[5]

## Huckabee interview (February 2026)

Carlson interviewed U.S. Ambassador to Israel [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] in Israel in an episode aired February 20, 2026.[6] Carlson raised Genesis 15:18 and asked whether the modern state of Israel has a right to the lands described in that scripture, drawing Huckabee's reply that Israeli control of territory from the Nile to the Euphrates "would be fine" — a remark that produced diplomatic backlash.[6] Across the interview Carlson repeatedly pressed Huckabee on crimes committed against Christians in the Holy Land, questions Badlands writers said the ambassador failed to address.[6]

## Joe Kent interview (March 2026)

A podcast episode featuring former [[national-counterterrorism-center|National Counterterrorism Center]] Director [[joe-kent|Joe Kent]] aired on Wednesday, March 18, 2026, in which Kent gave his side of the story following his resignation and the reported [[fbi|FBI]] leak investigation into him.[7] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] compared the interview with an earlier January 2024 clip of Kent answering Carlson's questions about a war with [[iran|Iran]], concluding that the messaging sounded intentionally deployed by the administration rather than subversive.[7] Following the interview, [[donald-trump|President Trump]] posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] about [[israel|Israel]]'s subversive behavior and warned [[iran|Iran]] against retaliating on [[qatar|Qatar]].[7]

## Elon Musk interview (April 2023)

While still a [[fox-news|Fox News]] host, Carlson conducted a long-anticipated sit-down interview with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] that aired in April 2023.[8] In it Musk said [[google|Google]] co-founder [[larry-page|Larry Page]] had once told him he planned to build a "[[digital-god|digital god]]," that he had repeatedly warned Page about the dangers of [[artificial-intelligence|AI]] while staying at Page's Palo Alto home, and that Page dismissed him as a "speciesist."[8] The interview aired the same week Fox settled the [[fox-dominion-defamation-settlement|Dominion defamation suit]], a settlement that spared Carlson from being called to the witness stand.[8]

## Hungary speeches (2023)

In August 2023 Carlson traveled to [[hungary|Hungary]] and delivered two speeches apologizing for the United States' lack of diplomacy and its "cultural imperialism."[9] He opened by apologizing on behalf of the country after U.S. Ambassador [[david-pressman|David Pressman]] publicly lectured the Hungarian government over LGBTQ rights.[9] "The point of diplomacy is not to hector other nations for its own sake," Carlson said, "to show up in someone else's country and scream at them because they're different from you."[9] He added that although he was "not in the habit of apologizing for the United States," Pressman's behavior was "disgusting and inexcusable" and far outside American diplomatic norms.[9] Carlson described Pressman as "a creep" who was "not a diplomat" but "a political activist and Biden donor," and said his conduct hurt the United States and was "a grave embarrassment."[9]

## Devon Archer interview (August 2023)

On Wednesday, August 2, 2023, Carlson released Episode 12 of his Twitter/X program: a roughly twelve-minute interview with [[devon-archer|Devon Archer]], recorded two days after Archer's closed-door congressional testimony about his business with [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]].[10] Carlson opened by asking whether Hunter Biden could have succeeded in business without being the son of the vice president, drawing the admission that "the brand of Biden adds a lot of power when your dad's the Vice President."[10]

Carlson pressed Archer on what [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] understood about the speakerphone calls, saying "So Joe Biden, who is very much a product of Washington, of course must have known that he was calling into effectively a business meeting... He must have understood that that was kind of what his son was selling."[10] Archer declined to speculate but confirmed that at particular dinners and meetings the sitting vice president knew business associates were present.[10] Near the close, Carlson shared a letter Joe Biden had written to Archer thanking him for going into business with Hunter and apologizing for being unable to break away from the President of China; a longer interview was promised.[10]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] situated the release within a "Narrative Convergence" between [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Carlson and the [[united-states-house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]], arguing that Carlson's particular role in the layered information war was reaching disillusioned centrists and independents while MAGA voices spoke to the base and Trump addressed all layers at once.[10]

## January 6 surveillance footage broadcasts (2023)

In late February 2023 Carlson was granted access to roughly 42,000 hours of surveillance video from the January 6, 2021 events at the U.S. Capitol, and told his audience the previous Thursday that his team's analysis would air the following week.[11] His Monday, March 6 program was described as explosive: Carlson argued that many of the narratives advanced by the [[january-6-committee|January 6 committee]] were false, presenting footage he said showed [[brian-sicknick]] healthy and walking after allegedly being murdered, [[jacob-chansley]] being escorted peacefully through the Capitol by police rather than committing sedition, and evidence that [[ray-epps]] had perjured himself in sworn testimony.[11]

Senate leaders [[chuck-schumer]] and [[mitch-mcconnell]] responded immediately, with Schumer going to the Senate floor to condemn Carlson and [[fox-news]] for airing the video and McConnell aligning himself with that criticism.[11] Tuesday night's program was noticeably subdued: Carlson released no further explosive video and his interview with a Capitol Hill police officer contained no significant reveals, prompting speculation reported by [[the-gateway-pundit]] that [[rupert-murdoch]] and the Fox News leadership had intervened to change the show's plan.[11] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief]] argued the footage should instead have been handed to a figure such as [[elon-musk]] or released in full to the public, dismissing security objections on the grounds that the Capitol is a heavily photographed public building.[11]

## Defense of RFK Jr. (June 2023)

In a June 2023 episode of his program, Carlson attacked what he called a "media hysteria typhoon" directed at [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]], who was assailed by the press immediately upon announcing a primary challenge to President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in 2024.[12] Carlson noted that [[cbs|CBS News]] had denounced Kennedy's views as "misleading" and "dangerous," that the [[los-angeles-times|Los Angeles Times]] called him "a threat to democracy," and that [[npr|NPR]] had devoted an entire segment to savaging Kennedy not merely as a candidate but "as a human being," describing him as making debunked claims that undermine trust in vaccines and as giving moral support to "crazed extremists" rallying under the banner of liberty or freedom.[12]

Carlson also observed that People Magazine had reported none of Kennedy's actual statements, instead publishing a story about his relatives disliking him, and that Kennedy had faced censorship on Instagram and YouTube for raising questions about rises in allergies, asthma, autism and other conditions he linked to vaccines, for which the media and medical establishment labeled him a lunatic, a Nazi and an extremist supporter.[12] Carlson closed by defending Kennedy's right to ask such questions, saying, "Bobby Kennedy won't stop asking, and that's why they hate him."[12] In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that regardless of which "side" Kennedy was on, the episode showed unawakened but reachable audiences that questions themselves are what the establishment fears most.[12]

The Journal's editors can prove that. But they're not. Instead they're protecting Yermak," Carlson wrote.[13] He argued the information was being suppressed because Yermak, as Kiev's top negotiator, was "leading efforts to scuttle" the US-drafted plan to end the Russia–Ukraine conflict, and claimed the Murdoch family, which owns the paper, wants "to continue the war with Russia."[13]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] responded that the Ukrainian government was being exposed as "a bunch of bloodsucking parasites" and the American media as complicit, urging readers to "accelerate."[13]

## "Russia would be the best ally" (December 2025)

Speaking on his YouTube podcast in December 2025, Carlson argued that Russia would be the "best" ally for the United States, judging the question "purely through the lens of what's good for the United States, an America First perspective."[15] He cited the country's status as the largest in the world, its "enormous mineral deposits, energy deposits" and "a formidable military, by far the biggest on that continent": "If you needed an ally to help you in a conflict... Russia would be, like, the best, of course."[15]

The remarks were reported against the backdrop of US–Russia relations, which sank to a major low during Joe Biden's presidency amid the Ukraine conflict and Western sanctions, and which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has sought to mend since returning to office, pursuing a settlement in Ukraine along with cooperation in the Arctic, investment opportunities, and a proposed rail tunnel under the Bering Strait.[15] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] responded that Russia already is America's best ally — depending on which version of the United States is meant — and pointed to the "Righteous Russia" series and the reality of what he calls the Sovereign Alliance.[15]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the episode's significance lay in the fact that the conversation was happening on the biggest show in new and old media, and that serious figures were finally "calling the revolution by its name," concluding that for Hanson and Carlson it was "better late than never."[16]

## January 6 Capitol footage access (February 2023)

In February 2023 Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] reached an apparent deal granting Carlson access to thousands of hours of Capitol security footage from January 6, 2021, an arrangement first reported by [[axios|Axios]] on Monday, February 20 and covered by [[politico|Politico]] the following day.[17] The deal surprised officials with oversight responsibility over the files, including Capitol Police Chief [[thomas-manger|Thomas Manger]] and House Sergeant at Arms [[william-mcfarland|William McFarland]], both of whom told associates they learned of it only when it became public.[17] [[united-states-capitol-police|Capitol Police]] had been extremely reluctant to release large swaths of the footage, citing risks to lawmakers, aides and officers.[17]

Speaking on Friday, July 11, 2025 at [[turning-point-usa|Turning Point USA]]'s Student Action Summit, Carlson suggested that [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] had been working for Israeli intelligence services throughout the years in which he was allegedly sexually abusing and trafficking underage girls.[18] The Times of Israel described the remarks as adding fuel to a controversy that had re-erupted after the Trump administration decided to withhold investigative records and claimed that Epstein's much-hyped "client list" had never existed, a decision by the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] and [[fbi|FBI]] that angered conservative personalities and influential members of the president's base who had long contended that deep state actors were hiding information about Epstein's elite associates.[18]

### "Liars" and "low IQ lunatics" (September 2023)

In a short clip released in September 2023, Carlson argued that the American system of governance does not reflect the majority's will but instead serves a small group of the powerful: "Most governments are run by a small group of people for their own benefit without reference to what the majority wants."[19] Citing Gallup polling, he invited viewers to compare the public's top ten issues with the priorities of their leaders.[19] He singled out Michigan governor [[gretchen-whitmer|Gretchen Whitmer]], whom he called a "low IQ lunatic," for prioritising social questions such as "misgendering" over constituents' concerns, and Texas governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]], whom he called a "liar, and betrayer of your own people," for declining to use the National Guard to seal the border.[19] Carlson concluded that the gap between public will and governmental action was "a betrayal of the core promise of the country, which is the people rule" and amounted to a "criminal act," in remarks some observers likened to a campaign stump speech.[19]

### UFC 295 with Trump (November 2023)

On November 11, 2023, Carlson was part of the entourage that accompanied former president [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] into Madison Square Garden for [[ufc|UFC]] 295, alongside [[kid-rock|Kid Rock]], UFC president [[dana-white|Dana White]] and [[donald-trump-jr|Don Jr.]].[20] The group took ringside seats moments before the pay-per-view event began as the crowd gave Trump a loud ovation, an appearance Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read as a deliberate fusion of politics and counter-culture rather than a casual outing.[20]

## "Tucker on Twitter" Episode 3 (June 2023)

On June 13, 2023, the day of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s federal indictment in Miami, Carlson released the third episode of [[tucker-on-twitter|"Tucker on Twitter"]], the show he launched after leaving [[fox-news|Fox News]].[21] He proceeded despite a cease-and-desist notice from Fox over the Twitter program, having drawn more than 115 million views on the first episode and nearly 60 million on the second.[21] The third episode accumulated five million views within a couple of hours of posting.[21]

Carlson argued that the indictment "was always going to happen" and had been "inevitable since February 16, 2016," the date he identified as the moment Trump "made a blood enemy of the largest and most powerful organization in human history — the US federal government."[21] He traced that moment to the South Carolina Republican debate at which Trump denied the existence of Iraqi weapons of mass destruction beside a horrified [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]], saying, "By saying that, he sealed his fate. That was the one thing you weren't allowed to say, because it implicated too many people on both sides which, on this topic is really just one side."[21] He named [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]] and [[nikki-haley|Nikki Haley]] among the enemies Trump accumulated, and reserved his harshest language for [[mike-pompeo|Mike Pompeo]], whom he said "undermined Trump's often-stated commitment to peace and non-intervention abroad, at every turn" and whose flattery of Trump he called "indelibly repulsive."[21] He concluded that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] "is doing what no US President has ever dared to do... using law enforcement to lock up his chief political rival," and described the United States as a caste system that only Trump could set right.[21]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the episode showed "Tucker off the corporate media leash," noting that he had begun labeling permanent Washington a [[uniparty|uniparty]] and thereby normalizing the view that partisan conflict is a staged production.[21] She emphasized that Carlson's audience is not the Truth Community but the general public, which is what made the reach of the show significant and, in her reading, explained Fox's attempts to stop him.[21]

## Fallout from the Fox departure (May 2023)

In the week following his exit from [[fox-news|Fox News]], Carlson's attorneys reportedly sent the network a letter accusing it of breach of contract and fraud, and alleging that Fox had fired him as part of its $787.5 million settlement with [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]].[22] Carlson, who was under contract with Fox until January 2025, announced that same week that he would bring "a new version" of his show to [[twitter|Twitter]].[22]

The allegation prompted Texas Governor [[greg-abbott|Greg Abbott]] to demand that Dominion "answer" questions about any role it played in "silencing a prominent conservative journalist" before doing further business in Texas.[22] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that none of the disclosures from the Fox–Carlson battle was more significant than Dominion's role in the network's decision to exit its most profitable prime-time talent, and criticized Abbott for treating free speech and First Amendment protections as negotiation levers.[22] She noted that Fox News Tonight, Carlson's replacement program, had reclaimed prime time's top spot with 1.4 million viewers while remaining nearly 1.2 million below both its own debut and Carlson's roughly 2.6 million average in that slot.[22]

## Budapest visit and Orbán interview (August 2023)

During a visit to Budapest in August 2023, Carlson gave a speech apologising on behalf of America for the "disgusting" behaviour of the US Ambassador to Hungary, then sat down for an interview with Hungarian Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]].[23] ZeroHedge framed the interview as an attempt to learn what was actually happening in Ukraine given "the propagandic opacity of news in America," noting Orbán's first-hand experience of Russian occupation and Hungary's shared border with Ukraine.[23]

Orbán told Carlson that the American media claim that Ukraine is winning the war "is not just a misunderstanding, it is a lie," warned that attempting to remove [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] could produce dangerous instability, and criticised Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] for suggesting Ukraine could join [[nato|NATO]].[23] The interview was headlined by Orbán's warning that "a 3rd World War is knocking on the door."[23]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that Carlson's narratives at the time seemed to run "as opposing mirrors to parallel Deep State political deployments," and that the Media Industrial Complex was being forced to brand Orbán a Russia sympathiser despite his having been jailed by Soviets in his youth — in his assessment, "not a great plan."[23]

## Removal from Fox and move to Twitter (May 2023)

On Tuesday, May 9, 2023, Carlson's legal team said he had been told by a member of the [[fox-news|Fox]] board that he was taken off the air abruptly the previous month as part of the network's settlement with [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]] over 2020 election claims, according to two people briefed on the conversation who spoke to [[axios|Axios]].[24] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that Carlson had been fired for doing his job — investigating and reporting on election meddling — and that the episode would deliver "a healthy dose of societal disillusionment" contributing to the [[great-awakening|great awakening]].[24]

## April 2023 departure from Fox News

On Monday, April 24, 2023, Fox News announced that it and Carlson had "severed ties," a decision that the network disclosed without explanation and that CNN described as a stunning announcement that "rocked the media and political worlds."[49] The network's brief statement said only, "We thank him for his service to the network as a host and prior to that as a contributor," and noted that his last show had aired on Friday, April 21.[49] Carlson, then the highest-rated single host at [[fox-news|Fox News]], did not respond to requests for comment; according to people familiar with the matter, the decision was made on the evening of Friday, April 21 by Fox Corporation chief executive [[lachlan-murdoch|Lachlan Murdoch]] and Fox News chief executive [[suzanne-scott|Suzanne Scott]], and Carlson was informed on Monday morning.[49]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] treated the exit as a major inflection point in the information war. ## Move to Twitter and 2023 disclosures

Carlson's firing from [[fox-news|Fox News]] was announced on April 24, 2023, after which the network's ratings fell and it frequently landed second in primetime behind [[msnbc|MSNBC]].[74] He launched [[tucker-on-twitter|Tucker on Twitter]], where his viewership dwarfed his cable numbers: his best-rated Fox shows drew about four million live-plus-same-day views against tens of millions per episode on Twitter, a platform he pointedly refused to call X.[74] His first episode drew 121 million views, and the two-part [[andrew-tate|Andrew Tate]] interview a combined 154 million.[74]
