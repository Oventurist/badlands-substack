# Congressional Budget Office

The Congressional Budget Office (CBO) is the nonpartisan federal agency that produces economic and budgetary projections for [[congress|Congress]]. Its January 2026 projections were a central reference point in reporting on the expected path of [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] policy.[1]

## 2026 projections

According to the CBO, the federal funds rate is expected to decline to approximately 3.4% by the end of 2026 as the Federal Reserve begins cutting interest rates.[1] Inflation is projected to remain above the Fed's 2% target in the near term before gradually moving closer to that level in subsequent years.[1] At the same time, longer-term Treasury yields are projected to remain elevated or rise modestly, which could keep long-term borrowing costs — including mortgage rates — relatively high even as short-term rates fall.[1]


## 2025 deficit and debt projections

Congressional Budget Office projections released in the spring of 2025 indicated that the US budget deficit and federal debt were both bound to grow, though at slower speeds than the agency had projected a year earlier.[2] Former New York mayor [[michael-bloomberg|Michael Bloomberg]] read the forecasts as sending an "unambiguous message" that "there'll be a reckoning, and it will be grim" unless [[congress|Congress]] urgently changed course, warning of a "fiscal breakdown" given that public borrowing was expected "to remain at this elevated level or higher for decades."[2]
