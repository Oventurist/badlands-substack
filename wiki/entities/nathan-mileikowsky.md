# Nathan Mileikowsky

Nathan Mileikowsky was the grandfather of Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and, in the account offered by Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], a formative figure in the ideological lineage that produced the modern Israeli right.[1]

Mileikowsky attended the [[world-zionist-organization|World Zionist Organization]]'s Sixth Zionist Congress in Basel, Switzerland, in 1903, at which the Uganda Programme was submitted for a vote.[1] That proposition called for the creation of a Jewish nation in British East Africa, in modern-day Kenya; Mileikowsky vocally opposed the measure and it was defeated.[1]

He immigrated to Mandatory Palestine in 1920 and became the director of a school.[1] There he joined the [[revisionist-zionism|Revisionist Movement]] — the strain of [[zionism|Zionism]] calling for territorial maximalism on both sides of the Jordan River, with the ambition to annex both Palestine and Jordan as parts of Israel — and became one of its major advocates under [[zeev-jabotinsky|Ze'ev Jabotinsky]].[1] He and his sons affiliated with the [[irgun|Irgun]].[1]

## The Netanyahu name

Mileikowsky began signing his published articles as "Netanyahu," a Hebrew form of his first name.[1] One of his sons, Benzion, liked it enough to adopt it as his surname, giving the family — and eventually the prime minister — its name.[1] The Badlands account notes as an aside that Argentine President [[javier-milei|Javier Milei]]'s paternal name is also Mileikowsky, describing it as "a rabbit hole for another day."[1]
