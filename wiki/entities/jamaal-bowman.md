# Jamaal Bowman

Jamaal Bowman is a Democratic member of the U.S. House of Representatives from New York who lost his 2024 primary after an unprecedented wave of outside spending by pro-Israel political action committees.[1] Reporting cited in the *Badlands News Brief* described Bowman as one of two "top liberals in Congress" wiped out by pro-Israel super PACs during the summer of 2024.[1]

## 2024 primary defeat

Groups led chiefly by the super PAC affiliated with the [[american-israel-public-affairs-committee|American Israel Public Affairs Committee]] spent a combined $25 million on advertising to defeat Bowman and Representative [[cori-bush|Cori Bush]] of Missouri, producing the two most expensive House primaries in history.[1] The spending was prompted by both members' criticism of Israel during its war with [[hamas]].[1] Bowman's defeat cleared the way for George Latimer, a more mainstream Democrat, to advance in a safe blue district.[1]

Coverage noted that Bowman "had plenty of his own political flaws unrelated to the Middle East," which initially left progressives uncertain whether the AIPAC playbook could be replicated; Bush's subsequent loss removed that doubt and rattled the progressive bloc in [[congress]].[1] Senator [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]] characterized the campaign against Bowman and Bush as evidence of an "intimidating presence" AIPAC holds over every member of Congress.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued in the brief that Bowman and Bush were "nefarious actors," likely funded by oligarchs such as [[george-soros|George Soros]], and framed their removal as part of a longer controlled-opposition dynamic surrounding Israel in American politics.[1]

## Cannon Building fire alarm incident (2023)

On September 30, 2023, Bowman was recorded pulling a fire alarm in the Cannon House Office Building while Republicans were working to pass a stopgap spending bill to avert a government shutdown, an act critics characterized as an attempt to delay the vote.[2] Representative [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]] (R-GA) immediately called on the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] to prosecute Bowman under the same obstruction statute used against January 6 defendants, writing on X that he "interrupted the official proceedings of the House as Republicans worked to keep the government open."[2] Greene added that "the Democrats literally will do anything to shut OUR government down because billions aren't going to their favorite country: Ukraine."[2]

According to Axios Capitol Hill reporter Juliegrace Brufke, Bowman told reporters, "I thought the alarm would open the door," and said Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] (D-NY) "understood that it was a mistake"; Bowman confirmed the explanation in a written statement issued that Saturday night.[2] The episode went viral, with Representative George Santos (R-NY) filming a mock public service announcement about fire alarms and X users recirculating Bowman's own January 2021 posts about the gravity of obstructing an official proceeding.[2] Representative Nicole Malliotakis (R-NY) promised legislation to expel him, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called the actions egregious on Sunday, and by Sunday evening Bowman was reported to be under investigation.[2] Writing for Badlands, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the affair as the capstone to a week of Democrat hypocrisy that had begun with the [[biden-impeachment-inquiry|Biden impeachment inquiry]] hearing.[2]

## Guilty plea and House Resolution 771 vote (October 2023)

On Thursday, October 26, 2023, Bowman pleaded guilty to a misdemeanor charge of falsely pulling the fire alarm at a congressional office building ahead of the House vote on the stopgap spending bill the previous month.[3] In a statement issued after the charge was filed, he said he was "thankful for the quick resolution from the District of Columbia Attorney General's office" and "grateful that the [[united-states-capitol-police|United States Capitol Police]] General Counsel's office agreed I did not obstruct nor intend to obstruct any House vote or proceedings," adding that he was responsible for activating the alarm, would pay the fine, and looked forward to the charges ultimately being dropped.[3] He also predicted that "Republicans will attempt to use this to distract everyone from their mess," saying he wanted to put the matter behind him and keep "working hard to deliver for New Yorkers."[3] After his court appearance he told ABC News, "I regret Capitol Police resources needed to be used to respond to that. I'm glad no one was hurt."[3]

The day before his plea, Bowman was one of nine Democrats who voted against [[house-resolution-771-standing-with-israel|House Resolution 771]], the resolution expressing support for [[israel]] that passed the House 412–10.[3]
