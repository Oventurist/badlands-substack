# Shas

Shas is a Sephardic ultra-Orthodox (Haredi) political party in Israel and one of the two Haredi factions whose participation has underpinned Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s governing coalition. As of mid-July 2025 the party held eleven seats in the 120-seat [[knesset]], making it the larger of the two Haredi partners alongside the Ashkenazi party [[united-torah-judaism|United Torah Judaism]].[1]

## Role in the 2025 coalition crisis

In July 2025 Shas was reported to be "openly threatening" to quit the government after UTJ's legislators submitted resignations on Monday, July 14, set to take effect within 48 hours.[1] Commentary in the Badlands News Brief noted that UTJ's seven-seat departure alone reduced Netanyahu's majority from 68 seats to 61 — the bare minimum required for him to remain prime minister — while a further Shas withdrawal of eleven seats would drop the coalition to roughly 50 seats, well below the threshold, producing a government collapse and snap elections expected toward the end of the year.[1]

Analysts stressed that the exit of both Haredi factions would strip the coalition of its parliamentary majority but would not automatically trigger new elections, and that it was not clear either party actually wanted to bring the government down.[1] The Knesset's scheduled recess on July 27 for a three-month break was seen as giving Netanyahu time to resolve the crisis behind closed doors.[1]

## The conscription dispute

The proximate cause of the rupture was the long-running fight over military conscription of yeshiva students. After the [[israeli-supreme-court|Israeli Supreme Court]] ruled in 2024 that Haredi yeshiva students are not exempt from the mandated two-year conscription to the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]], the ultra-Orthodox parties pressed Netanyahu to codify the long-practiced exemption into law.[1] Shas and UTJ objected that the bill drafted by Netanyahu's committee failed to cover the thousands of yeshiva students who had already received draft letters since the ruling; if the bill passed as written and those students did not report, they would be treated as draft dodgers subject to arrest and imprisonment.[1] Badlands commentary framed the crisis as a continuation of the [[haredi-conscription-exemption-law]] fight that had nearly collapsed the coalition a month earlier.[1]

## July 2025 exit from the government

In July 2025 Shas decided to exit the government following a meeting of its ruling Council of Torah Sages in Jerusalem, leaving Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] with a diminished government; the party's ministers were expected to resign their positions the following morning.[2] Crucially, Shas said it would remain part of Netanyahu's coalition for the time being and was not pushing for elections.[2]

The party had announced the previous day that, "following the serious and unacceptable harm to the status of Torah scholars" brought about by the current government, the Council of Torah Sages would convene for "a crucial discussion regarding [the party's] continued path in the government."[2] The Shas party newspaper HaDerech blamed the crisis on what it called "[[likud|Likud]] and Edelstein's blatant violation of agreements on the law regulating the status of yeshiva students."[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted on [[badlands-daily|Badlands Daily]] that many outlets misreported the story as Shas leaving the coalition, which was not true: the party limited its protest to resigning cabinet positions, meaning Netanyahu's government would remain in power for the time being.[2] The move followed [[united-torah-judaism|United Torah Judaism]]'s departure from the government the prior Monday night.[2] He argued that Netanyahu, with his back against the wall as in the previous month when he bombed Iran, had again pulled a rabbit out of his hat — this time by bombing [[syria|Syria]] — and that the prime minister must "continue to serve up war on a platter" to hold his factions together, wars he said are bleeding [[israel|Israel]] dry financially, politically and literally amid record emigration.[2]
