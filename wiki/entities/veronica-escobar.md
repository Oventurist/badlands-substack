# Veronica Escobar

Veronica Escobar is a Democratic member of the United States House of Representatives from El Paso, Texas. In June 2025 she disclosed that her name appeared on the target list carried by the perpetrator of what she described as the "political assassination" of lawmakers in Minnesota.[1]

## Minnesota shooting target list

Escobar said she was notified by the [[united-states-capitol-police|Capitol Police]] on Sunday morning, June 15, 2025, that her name was among the targets identified in connection with the Minnesota attacks, and she made the disclosure in a statement issued on Monday, June 16.[1] The suspect, [[vance-boelter|Vance Luther Boelter]], 57, was arrested late on the Sunday following the largest manhunt in Minnesota history; he is accused of stalking and killing state Representative [[melissa-hortman|Melisa Hortman]] and her husband Mark, and of shooting State Senator [[john-hoffman|John Hoffman]] and his wife Yvette, who were seriously wounded.[1]

## Statements on political violence

Escobar condemned political violence "of any kind," citing not only the Minnesota killings but also a fatal shooting at an anti-Trump "[[no-kings-protests|No Kings]]" protest in Salt Lake City, threats against Democratic lawmakers at the protest in Austin, and the arrest of an armed man at the No Kings protest in El Paso.[1] She characterized the pattern as evidence that "right-wing extremism has a foothold in our country."[1] Escobar has been a staunch and vocal critic of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and of his hardline policies.[1]

## Commentary and criticism

Commentary published in the Badlands News Brief framed Escobar's response as a US representative "calling on President Trump to compel social media CEOs to censor Americans' First Amendment Right," and questioned the framing of the attacker's ideology by noting reporting that the alleged shooter had next to no political speech in his social media posts.[1] The commentary, attributed to [[canncon|CannCon's Newsletter]], closed by paraphrasing the maxim that "he who would give up a little liberty for a little security deserves neither," positioning the episode as a [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] question rather than solely a security one.[1]
