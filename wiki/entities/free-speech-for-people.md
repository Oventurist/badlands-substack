# Free Speech For People

Free Speech For People is a legal advocacy organization that served as counsel for the plaintiffs in [[naacp-v-useip|*NAACP et al. v. USEIP et al.*]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back)

On Inauguration Day, January 20, 2017, the organization launched a campaign to impeach President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], announcing the effort in a *Washington Post* article titled "The Campaign to Impeach President Trump Has Begun."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back) Defendants cited this history to argue that the organization's later characterization of election-questioning speech as voter intimidation was selective, given its own record of contesting the legitimacy of a sitting president.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back)

The litigation it brought ended in July 2024 with judgement on partial findings for the defendants and an award of costs, after Judge [[charlotte-sweeney|Charlotte Sweeney]] found no evidence of intimidating or threatening conduct — a result defendants described as a damning rebuke of the plaintiff organizations and their lawyers.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/always-fight-back)

## Campaign Against Kurt Olsen (2026)

In June 2026 Free Speech For People (FSFP) publicly objected to the appointment of attorney [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]] to a senior [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] post, arguing that lawyers who advanced claims challenging the 2020 election should not hold positions overseeing election-related investigations and policy, and that his role threatened public confidence in DOJ election enforcement.[2] NBC News, reporting on the group's letter, described FSFP as a nonprofit focused on "free and fair elections and rooting out corruption in government" while labelling Olsen an "election denier."[2] Badlands writers noted that FSFP had itself campaigned for Trump's impeachment the day after Inauguration Day in 2017.[2]

[[canncon|CannCon]], writing in *The Gateway Pundit*, pointed out that FSFP co-founder and president [[john-c-bonifaz|John Bonifaz]] had previously raised concerns in a letter posted on the National Institute of Standards and Technology website about voting software supplied by a "foreign-controlled company" with "ties to the Venezuelan government" — a position difficult to reconcile with the group's later framing of election-machine skepticism as denialism.[2] The Gateway Pundit also recalled FSFP's involvement in litigation against Colorado canvassers after the 2020 election under the [[ku-klux-klan-act|Ku Klux Klan Act]], a case that hinged almost entirely on a single witness and ended in a directed verdict for the defendants.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], a defendant in that Colorado case, wrote that discovery revealed through the privilege log that FSFP had approached the plaintiffs with a novel legal theory, after which the plaintiffs sued despite knowing nothing about the defendants; more than two years later the plaintiffs' star witness admitted she had named the defendants only because FSFP's lawyers told her to.[2] She characterized the organization as, in her opinion, a [[lawfare|lawfare]] strategist working to undermine the First Amendment and abuse legal process against ideological opponents.[2]
