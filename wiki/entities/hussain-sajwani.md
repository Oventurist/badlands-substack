# Hussain Sajwani

Hussain Sajwani is an Emirati billionaire and the head of the Dubai-based DAMAC Group, the parent of [[damac-properties|DAMAC Properties]].[1] He came to renewed prominence in American political coverage in January 2025 when he appeared alongside President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at a press conference at [[mar-a-lago|Mar-a-Lago]] in Florida to announce an investment of at least $20 billion to build new data centers in the United States.[1]

## The January 2025 data center announcement

At the January 7, 2025 press conference, Trump framed the Sajwani commitment as critical to keeping the United States competitive in emerging technologies, saying the money would support "massive new data centers across the Midwest, the Sun Belt area, and also to keep America on the cutting edge of technology and artificial intelligence."[1] Trump said the first phase of the investment would land in Texas, Arizona, Oklahoma, Louisiana, Ohio, Illinois, Michigan and Indiana, and he vowed to help move the deal quickly through environmental and permitting review.[1] Sajwani himself said, "We're planning to invest $20 billion and even more than that if the opportunity, the market, allow us," while Trump suggested the figure might go "double, or even somewhat more than double."[1]

Trump cast the pledge as a direct consequence of his election victory, arguing that his return to power was encouraging foreign business leaders to invest in the United States.[1]

## Relationship with the Trump Organization

Sajwani's DAMAC has an established commercial relationship with [[trump-organization|The Trump Organization]], having worked with the company to develop golf courses in the United Arab Emirates and Oman, with additional regional projects planned.[1] Badlands Media commentary grouped the Sajwani announcement with a similar December 2024 event at which Trump appeared with Japanese billionaire [[masayoshi-son|Masayoshi Son]] to announce a $100 billion [[softbank|SoftBank]] investment in US projects — a pledge Trump publicly pressed Son to double to $200 billion.[1]

Badlands commentators noted that the wave of Gulf-linked construction and investment commitments would be at risk if the war in the Middle East were not brought to an end, framing the choice facing the incoming administration as "war, death and famine; or peace and prosperity."[1]
