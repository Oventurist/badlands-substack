# Republican Party

The Republican Party is one of the two major United States political parties. In March 2026 its strategists and officials were reported to be divided over whether President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] should continue emphasizing claims about the 2020 election or pivot to current policy issues ahead of the midterms.[1]

## Internal messaging split

[[politico|Politico]] reported that some Republican strategists and lawmakers were urging Trump to stop focusing on [[2020-election-fraud-claims|claims about the 2020 election]] and instead emphasize issues they believe will resonate more with voters in upcoming elections.[1] Multiple Republican officials and strategists told the outlet that continuing to focus on election fraud in 2020 and beyond could distract from policy issues ahead of the [[2026-midterm-elections|midterm elections]].[1]

Other Republicans remained supportive of Trump raising election integrity concerns, viewing the issue as important to many Republican voters.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the Republican position as fear of losing the midterms if Trump keeps talking about 2020–2024 election fraud, satirizing the posture as "Stop saying true things! We're gonna lose the next fake election!"[1] She argued that no one wins a fake election and that the same election systems and processes are currently being exposed in Georgia and Arizona, with investigations and legal action underway in a couple dozen more states.[1] Noting that Trump himself never stopped calling elections fake, she questioned why some of his supporters and political allies insist everyone pretend elections are suddenly legitimate despite no meaningful change to the underlying systems — concluding that "incentives explain behavior" and that there can be no real elections without honesty about, and accountability for, the fake ones.[1]
