# Iqbal Jassat

Iqbal Jassat is a writer whose June 2024 article "'Negative Neutrality' – Palestinian Authority 'Weak and Irrelevant'" was published by the [[palestine-chronicle|Palestine Chronicle]].[1]

## The 2024 Palestinian Authority article

Jassat's piece argued that the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], led by [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]] — whose four-year term expired in 2009 and who has refused demands for fresh elections — had shut the door on reform and become weak and irrelevant.[1] The article described the PA as a construct of the [[oslo-accords|1993 Oslo Accords]] with limited self-rule powers in the occupied West Bank, comparing it to South Africa's apartheid-era "Bantustan" formations, and noted that South African solidarity movements had consistently raised concerns about the PA's role.[1] It further reported the view of many commentators that the Palestine Liberation Organization prematurely gave up armed resistance against [[israel|Israel]] in exchange for unfulfilled promises of an independent Palestinian state.[1]

## Badlands analysis

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] selected the article as a demonstration of open-source intelligence methodology rather than for its content, arguing that both Jassat and Palestine Chronicle founder [[ramzy-baroud|Ramzy Baroud]] met a checklist of indicators he associates with [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]]-linked media assets.[1] Those indicators included publication through Qatar-, London-, or US-based Middle East outlets, support for the 2011 Arab Spring, and public insistence that the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s findings on the death of Jamal Khashoggi be accepted and that Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] be deposed.[1] The commentary framed Jassat's call for rejection of Abbas and embrace of armed resistance as suspicious given the author's prior published assessment that Abbas was aligned with the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] and among [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s chosen agents of peace in the Middle East.[1] These are Badlands Media opinion claims, not established fact.
