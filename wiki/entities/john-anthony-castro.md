# John Anthony Castro

**John Anthony Castro** is a Texas-based, little-known Republican presidential candidate who became the most prolific filer of lawsuits seeking to remove former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] from state presidential ballots ahead of the 2024 election.[1]

## Ballot-disqualification litigation

Castro filed at least two dozen lawsuits against Trump in late 2023, each seeking to bar the former president from a respective state's ballot.[1] His central legal theory was that Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|Fourteenth Amendment]] disqualifies from the presidency anyone who has engaged in an insurrection against the United States.[1]

The campaign of suits met near-uniform failure. A judge in Arizona dismissed a similar Castro suit earlier in December 2023, and the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] declined in October 2023 to hear his appeal in a comparable Florida case.[1]

## West Virginia dismissal

On December 21, 2023, U.S. District Judge [[irene-berger|Irene Berger]] rejected Castro's bid to strike Trump from the West Virginia ballot, holding that Castro lacked standing to sue.[1] Berger sided with attorneys for Trump, Secretary of State [[mac-warner|Mac Warner]], and the [[west-virginia-republican-party|West Virginia GOP]] in dismissing the suit.[1] She wrote that the evidence Castro himself submitted removed "any doubt that Mr. Castro's purported 'campaign' exists as a vehicle for pursuing litigation, not votes," noting that he could show no political activity in the state apart from the lawsuit itself.[1]

## Rhode Island dismissal (November 2023)

On Monday, November 27, 2023, Chief Judge [[john-j-mcconnell|John J. McConnell]] of the U.S. District Court in Rhode Island summarily dismissed a Castro complaint seeking to disqualify Trump from the 2024 ballot, marking another defeat in a campaign of lawsuits Castro had filed in more than two dozen states.[2] The dismissal reasoned that the plaintiff had failed to show an "injury in fact," a required component for a Section Three claim.[2] McConnell's ruling followed a November 21, 2023 decision on the same matter, also brought by Castro, from the Court of Appeals for the First Circuit, whose rulings bind the Rhode Island district court.[2]

By that point courts in Florida, Colorado, New Hampshire, Minnesota, and Michigan had already dismissed Castro's claims, mostly on procedural or jurisdictional grounds such as lack of standing or refusal to rule on a political question.[2] Badlands commentator [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] counted 22 states with Fourteenth Amendment Section 3 challenges to Trump's candidacy and noted the coincidence that the Rhode Island plaintiff behind a couple dozen of those challenges was named Castro.[2]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Castro is "probably not qualified for the job" of president given his continued pursuit of standing in federal court, and that his challenges sought federal relief in conflict with Article I, Section 4 of the Constitution, which commits the manner of elections to the states.[1] She described it as curious that Castro continued the effort after more than two dozen failed cases, and more curious still that federal courts continued to hear a candidate who had raised no money, built no campaign infrastructure, and was unknown to most voters — treating, in her framing, "this glorified troll with greater respect and dignity" than the Republican frontrunner and former president.[1]

## Supreme Court Rejection, October 2023

In early October 2023 the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|U.S. Supreme Court]] rejected Castro's challenge seeking to remove Donald Trump from the 2024 presidential ballot, an outcome reported as a landmark rebuff of the ballot-disqualification theory.[3] Castro, described as a Republican presidential candidate and tax consultant who had unsuccessfully sought several political offices, argued that Trump should be disqualified over his alleged involvement in the January 6, 2021 riot at the U.S. Capitol.[3]

Castro's standing argument held that "a primary candidate has judicial standing to bring a claim challenging the eligibility of a fellow primary candidate for competitive injury in the form of a diminution of votes and/or fundraising" where the candidate believes a rival is ineligible to hold office.[3] The substantive claim rested on Section 3 of the [[fourteenth-amendment|14th Amendment]], a Civil War-era provision originally used to bar former Confederates from holding office.[3] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that establishment forces in Colorado had filed a parallel suit on the same "fringe legal theory" and argued the Court's refusal to take up Castro's case should damn the Colorado action, while cautioning that the broader lawfare campaign was unlikely to stop.[3]
