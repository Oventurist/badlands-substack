# Jacob Frank

Jacob Frank was an 18th-century religious leader who, roughly one hundred years after [[sabbatai-zevi|Sabbatai Zevi]], took [[sabbateanism|Sabbateanism]] "to the next level," according to [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[1]

## Doctrine

Frank drew from mystical traditions in Judaism, Christianity and Islam, among others, and pushed a form of antinomianism which argued that redemption could be achieved through sinning and then repenting.[1] The famous quote attributed to him — "Since we cannot be saints, let us be sinners" — captures the doctrine.[1]

## Associations

Frank palled around with some of the more influential players in Europe of his era, rubbing elbows with men who would found the [[bavarian-illuminati|Bavarian Illuminati]] and other geopolitical movements.[1] Deschamps notes with irony that these movements also harbored the ambition of restoring the Holy Land to God's people.[1]

## Modern claims

Deschamps argues that a faction of crypto-Sabbateans or Frankists — "or Satanists, for lack of a better term" — became embedded within the United States over 200 years ago and has grown increasingly active since the end of the Second World War, forming part of what he calls the [[cabal|Cabal]].[1] He offers this lineage, which begins with [[neoplatonism|Neoplatonism]] and runs through the Crusades to modern [[zionism|Zionism]], as historical context for the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]].[1]
