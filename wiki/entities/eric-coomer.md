# Eric Coomer

Eric Coomer is a former Dominion Voting Systems executive who became the plaintiff in a series of defamation lawsuits arising out of allegations that he participated in a September 2020 conference call with Antifa-aligned activists and boasted of rigging the 2020 presidential election against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] The allegation originated with Colorado podcaster [[joe-oltmann|Joe Oltmann]], whose uncorroborated claims were subsequently amplified by conservative media figures.[1]

## Litigation

Coomer's defamation suits name a wide field of defendants, including Oltmann, various media figures, Trump's 2020 campaign, and Trump's lawyers who repeated the accusations.[1] The most prominent of these went to trial in the U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado in June 2025 against [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]], [[mypillow|MyPillow]] and FrankSpeech, presided over by Judge Nina Y. Wang.[1] Jurors in Denver heard two days of testimony from Oltmann, described by courtroom observers as the most engaged the jury had appeared during the trial.[1]

The trial's second week was scheduled to feature testimony from Lindell, his former employee Brannon Howse, and Dennis Montgomery, whose deposition was to be entered through video clips covering PCAPs, China, and the Hammer and Scorecard programs.[1] Anticipated public interest led the Clerk of the Court to reopen the courthouse overflow room.[1]

## The central dispute of fact

The so-called "Antifa call" is the central dispute of fact underpinning Coomer's defamation claims.[1] Prosecutors of the narrative in Colorado legacy media long maintained the call never happened at all. New evidence introduced on the Friday of the trial's first week — an affidavit describing a local activist/BLM/Antifa Zoom call in September 2020, filed by Coomer's own attorneys in another matter — corroborated multiple points in Oltmann's hand-written notes while continuing to dispute his account.[1] Badlands correspondent [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted the oddity that such an affidavit had not been disclosed in discovery in the Lindell case despite its direct relevance, and observed that the jury, lacking years of external context, had been presented first with a narrative that no call occurred and then with evidence that a call did occur but was a different one.[1]

## Public-figure status and the Lindell trial

During the June 2025 [[coomer-v-lindell|Coomer v. Lindell]] defamation trial in Denver, Coomer's status as a public figure became a central legal question.[2] Badlands trial correspondent [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] assessed that as Vice President of Product Strategy and Security for a major US voting vendor during the 2020 election — and given his own heavily biased public statements — Coomer was involved in public affairs in a way that subjected him to increased scrutiny, making him a public figure in the context of the case.[2] That classification obliged him to prove "actual malice": that [[mike-lindell|Mike Lindell]]'s statements were made with knowledge of their falsity or with reckless disregard for the truth.[2]

Coomer filed suit in April 2022, accusing Lindell of defaming him in a series of statements and media appearances that caused emotional and physical distress and cost him his career in election security.[2] On the stand, Lindell attacked Coomer as merely seeking money and grinding a political ax, saying, "(I) cost Dr. Coomer what? He's out there suing people for money."[2] Coomer's attorneys spent most of the day pressing Lindell on all the ways he should have known his claims were false, and worked to tie the [[frankspeech|FrankSpeech]] broadcasts to [[mypillow|MyPillow]] promo codes in order to establish joint liability.[2]

## Cross-examination and friendly witnesses

Earlier in the Denver trial the jury saw Coomer cross-examined by [[mike-lindell|Lindell]]'s defense attorneys and read his #UnTrumpMe Facebook posts, material central to the defense's argument about his own partisanship.[3] Courtroom sources told [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] that the jury was mostly engaged during his testimony, though one juror appeared to doze.[3]

Coomer was followed on the stand by his friend [[matt-crane|Matt Crane]], who appeared in an exhibit photograph taken at a barbecue at Coomer's house alongside state and local election officials, among them Hillary Rudy of Colorado Secretary of State [[jena-griswold|Jena Griswold]]'s office.[3] According to courtroom sources, Crane conceded he was invested in the outcome of the case, struggled with questions about his wife's relationship with Coomer as colleagues at [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]], and defended the risk-limiting audit as a security measure without knowing that only four states used one in 2020.[3] Coomer was expected to call [[joe-oltmann|Joe Oltmann]] — whose 2020 statements comprise the central defamation claims — the following day.[3]
