# Elise Stefanik

Elise Stefanik is a New York congresswoman who was tapped by President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in November 2024 to serve as U.S. ambassador to the United Nations.[1]

Retired CIA intelligence officer and State Department official [[larry-johnson|Larry Johnson]] criticized the selection, saying Stefanik "comes in with a very strident pro-Zionist point of view," and cited her appointment as part of a broader pattern in Trump's foreign policy staffing that he argued would repeat the errors of the first term.[1]

## Ethics complaint against Jack Smith

Stefanik, the House GOP conference chair and a close Trump ally, filed an ethics complaint against special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] on April 30, 2024, accusing the prosecutor overseeing the federal investigations into Trump of trying to "unlawfully interfere with the 2024 presidential election."[2] The complaint was lodged with the Justice Department's [[office-of-professional-responsibility|Office of Professional Responsibility]] and argued that Smith was attempting to "rush" Trump's federal election subversion case to trial.[2] "It's obvious to any reasonable observer that Jack Smith is trying to interfere with the 2024 election and stop the American people from electing Donald Trump," she said in a statement. "At every turn, he has sought to accelerate his illegal prosecution of President Trump for the clear (if unstated) purpose of trying him before the November election."[2]

Stefanik argued Smith should be censured for violating the Justice Department's manual, citing a section stating that attorneys may "never select the timing of any action … for the purpose of affecting any election," and said his conduct "has brought disrepute to the Department of Justice and the entire federal government."[2] She pointed to Smith's actions in court to support her claims of political animus, including his request that the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] weigh Trump's immunity claims before they had been considered by an appeals court.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that both wings of the [[uniparty|uniparty]] were now making open accusations of election interference in official forums against the two main candidates in the presidential election — Judge Merchan's New York proceeding being dubbed the "NY Election Interference Trial" by the right, and Stefanik alleging interference by Smith and, by extension, the Biden Justice Department — and argued that what the official allegations fail to consider is that elections are rigged and interference is the status quo.[2]

## December 2023 university antisemitism hearing and resolution

As chair of the House Republican Conference, Stefanik questioned the presidents of [[harvard-university|Harvard University]], the [[massachusetts-institute-of-technology|Massachusetts Institute of Technology]] and the [[university-of-pennsylvania|University of Pennsylvania]] at a December 2023 House hearing, asking whether students calling for the genocide of Jews violated their schools' codes of conduct; the presidents' evasive answers went viral and drew bipartisan condemnation.[3] Exactly one week later she authored a [[2023-house-resolution-condemning-college-presidents-response-to-antisemitism|resolution condemning campus antisemitism and that testimony]], joined as lead sponsors by Majority Leader [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]] and Democratic representatives [[josh-gottheimer|Josh Gottheimer]] and [[jared-moskowitz|Jared Moskowitz]]; her office said it was expected to receive a floor vote that week.[3]

## Questioning of FBI Director Wray, March 2024

At the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence's Annual Threat Assessment hearing on March 12, 2024, Stefanik, the third-ranking House Republican, pressed FBI Director Christopher Wray on whether the bureau was conducting counterintelligence investigations into President Joe Biden or former President Donald Trump.[4] She first noted that former FBI Director James Comey had testified he did not follow proper protocol in notifying Congress of the opening of Crossfire Hurricane, the 2016 counterintelligence investigation into Trump, and cited the Intelligence Authorization Act, which directs the FBI to inform congressional leadership of any counterintelligence investigation into a federal candidate for office.[4] When Wray, after a long pause, tried to refer her to Special Counsel Robert Hur, Stefanik demanded a direct answer, prompting his reply that "there is no investigation that I could confirm here, no."[4]
