# Benny Gantz

**Benny Gantz** is a retired general of the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] and an Israeli politician.[1]

## Commentary on a ground invasion of Iran

During the March 2026 escalation between the United States, [[israel|Israel]], and [[iran|Iran]], Gantz publicly confirmed the possibility that a ground invasion of Iran could be undertaken by either Israel or the United States.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited Gantz's remarks as supporting a longstanding theory that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s interest in controlling Syria was aimed at establishing "David's Corridor," a land bridge from Israel to the Euphrates River and onward into Kurdistan, from which the [[kurds|Kurds]] could assist in moving soldiers and equipment into Iran.[1]

The same commentary noted that a ground invasion was the single course of action [[erik-prince|Erik Prince]] had said should never be undertaken.[1]

## Departure and dissolution of the war cabinet (June 2024)

On Monday, June 17, 2024, Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] dissolved his six-member Israel–[[hamas|Hamas]] war cabinet following Gantz's departure from the government.[2] Netanyahu told his security cabinet the preceding day that "the cabinet was in the coalition agreement with Gantz at his request" and that "as soon as Gantz left – there was no need for a cabinet anymore."[2] With the body dissolved, Netanyahu was left to rely on a smaller group of ministers to guide the war, including former war cabinet members [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]], the minister of defense, and [[ron-dermer|Ron Dermer]], the minister of strategic affairs.[2]

## Opposition to military government in Gaza (2024)

In November 2024, as an opposition leader and former war cabinet minister, Gantz seconded [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]]'s public rejection of an incremental Israeli military government in [[gaza|Gaza]], writing: "Israeli security control of Gaza - necessary. Israeli rule, with our soldiers handling sewage and trash – a disaster."[3] Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] accused the two former generals of weakness.[3]

Badlands commentary observed that Gantz and Gallant — two retired generals who had defected from [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s government and whom [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had reportedly encouraged to run against Netanyahu in 2018 — were now publicly opposing the government, and read the convergence of a "centrist" (Gantz), a Zionist (Gallant) and a Labor figure ([[ehud-barak|Ehud Barak]]) against the same opponent as evidence of an organized political resistance.[3]

## Cited in Trump's Gaza plan messaging (October 2025)

In a Truth Social post responding to reactions to his Gaza peace plan, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] included commentary from Israeli opposition leader Gantz alongside criticism from Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]].[4] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry found the juxtaposition notable, recalling remarks Trump made about Gantz in a December 2021 interview with Israeli journalist Barak Ravid, and reading the post as a signal that Trump expected [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s security cabinet to reject or undermine the plan.[4]

## Departure from the war cabinet, June 2024

On Sunday, June 9, 2024, Gantz announced his party's departure from Israel's emergency war government, delivering what reporting called the biggest internal shock to the Israeli government since October 7.[5] In a prime-time speech to the Israeli public, the former general criticized Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] for placing personal interest ahead of the national interest, saying that "fateful strategic decisions are met with hesitation and procrastination due to political considerations."[5] Although observers asked whether the move heralded the beginning of the end of Netanyahu's rule, analysis held there was less to the drama than headlines suggested.[5]

Polling favored Gantz — described as a political pragmatist, [[joe-biden|Biden]] ally, and former chief of the Israel Defense Forces — who led Netanyahu as preferred candidate for prime minister, with 62 percent of Israelis saying they would not vote for any party backing Netanyahu's continued leadership, and large majorities favoring early elections.[5] Netanyahu nevertheless retained the only number that mattered: 64 seats in the 120-seat [[knesset|Knesset]], enough to block early elections and to postpone any contest until the scheduled 2026 date, barring internal defections.[5]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry speculated that Gantz might be acting, even unwittingly, as [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s agent, and that his logical next move would be to persuade Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] — characterized in the source article as Biden's inside man — to resign alongside him, compelling the government to dissolve and forcing new elections that Gantz could plausibly win before initiating a ceasefire.[5] The commentary added that Mahmoud Abbas of Fatah, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, and the Arab states were ready to deal, that Iran might be sidelined by its imminent presidential election, and that [[hamas|Hamas]] should be treated as controlled opposition to be defeated by cutting off funding and engaging the Saudis against the [[muslim-brotherhood|Muslim Brotherhood]].[5]

## The 2019–2020 coalition struggle with Netanyahu

Gantz led the Blue and White party against [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s [[likud|Likud]] in the April 2019 Israeli election, in which the two parties tied at 35 seats each; because Likud had won the popular vote, it received the first opportunity to form a government.[6] President [[reuven-rivlin|Reuven Rivlin]] set Netanyahu a May 29 deadline, after which Gantz would be given the chance to build a coalition.[6] Netanyahu failed, but subverted Gantz by persuading enough members of the [[knesset|Knesset]] to dissolve the legislature on May 30, forcing new elections in September 2019.[6]

In that election Blue and White took more seats than Likud, and Gantz initially rallied the support needed to form a government, only for a far-left party to rescind its backing at the last minute, prompting Rivlin to give Netanyahu another chance.[6] Netanyahu failed again, and after Gantz failed a second time the Knesset was dissolved once more, with elections set for March 2020.[6]

Gantz had pledged to exclude Netanyahu explicitly from his government, but by the time of the March 2020 vote the COVID-19 pandemic was in full swing; after being elected Speaker of the Knesset, Gantz agreed to form an emergency coalition with Netanyahu, under which Netanyahu remained Prime Minister and Gantz served as "Alternate Prime Minister."[6]

## Relationship with Donald Trump

In a December 21, 2021 interview with Israeli journalist [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]], [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] revealed that while president he had quietly backed Gantz against Netanyahu in the April 2019 election.[6] Gantz shared Trump's opposition to Netanyahu's plans to annex large portions of the West Bank and construct Israeli settlements there.[6] Badlands commentary argued that Netanyahu's determination to keep Gantz from becoming Prime Minister stemmed from the knowledge that Gantz would finalize the [[two-state-solution|two-state solution]].[6]
