# Jes Staley

Jes Staley is a former executive of [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] whose private correspondence with financier [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] was disclosed in litigation brought against the bank by the [[us-virgin-islands|United States Virgin Islands]].[1] The emails, reported by [[bloomberg|Bloomberg]] and recirculated by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]], became a focal point of renewed public attention to the institutional relationships that sustained Epstein's activities.[1]

## Epstein correspondence

According to emails cited in the Virgin Islands lawsuit, Staley and Epstein exchanged messages in July 2010 in which the two men appeared to use the names of Disney princesses as code.[1] Staley allegedly wrote, "That was fun... Say hi to Snow White," prompting Epstein to reply, "[W]hat character would you like next?" — to which Staley responded, "Beauty and the Beast."[1] The filing further alleges that Epstein emailed Staley photographs of young women in seductive poses.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] commented that the exchanges appeared to reference women Epstein was presumably trafficking, and questioned how many of them remained alive.[1] DeLarme also observed, with reference to [[bill-gates|Bill Gates]], that the conventional defense strategy for figures caught in Epstein's orbit has been to insist on having shared only "a few dinners with him."[1]

## Significance in the JPMorgan litigation

The Staley emails were introduced as part of a broader government allegation that JPMorgan continued to facilitate Epstein's ongoing trafficking, and that the bank's facilitation "made the sex-trafficking venture possible and allowed ongoing and future sex-trafficking."[1] Badlands commentary noted that although the publicly filed document contains redactions, enough remained visible to establish substantial exposure for the executives involved.[1]

## Allegations against Jamie Dimon

In legal documents reported by the Wall Street Journal and recirculated by [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] in mid-2023, Staley asserted that he and JPMorgan chief executive [[jamie-dimon|Jamie Dimon]] had communicated about Epstein, a claim characterized in the press as Staley throwing Dimon under the bus and setting the stage for a major legal battle.[2] According to the filing, Staley said the two men communicated when Epstein was arrested in 2006 and again in 2008, when Epstein pleaded guilty to soliciting and procuring a minor for prostitution and served 13 months in a work-release program, and that Dimon communicated with him several times through 2012 about whether to retain Epstein as a client.[2] Staley also stated that he knew about Epstein's sex trafficking operation and that he regretted the friendship.[2]

Dimon has maintained that no such conversations occurred.[2] A JPMorgan spokeswoman said there was "no evidence that any such communications ever occurred — nothing in the voluminous number of documents reviewed and nothing in the nearly dozen depositions taken, including that of our own CEO," and added that "the one person who claims this to be true is currently accused of horrific acts and dishonesty."[2]

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] situated the dispute within a wider critique of international banking, linking the same institutions to civil asset forfeiture, to the financial deplatforming of Canadian [[freedom-convoy|freedom convoy]] supporters during the Ottawa protests, and to the development of [[central-bank-digital-currency|central bank digital currencies]] as an instrument of economic control.[2]

## Rakoff ruling (May 2023)

On May 1, 2023, U.S. District Judge [[jed-rakoff|Jed Rakoff]], a Clinton appointee, rejected motions to dismiss the cases brought against Staley, [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]], and [[deutsche-bank|Deutsche Bank]], ruling that plaintiffs had presented enough evidence that all three could be held liable for [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s sex trafficking.[3] The ruling arose from suits filed by several alleged Epstein victims and by the [[us-virgin-islands|United States Virgin Islands]] government, and additionally covered the claim JPMorgan had filed against Staley himself.[3]

## Victim lawsuit naming Staley (January 2023)

In January 2023, documents lodged at a United States court accused Staley, then 66 and the former boss of [[barclays|Barclays]], of "personally observ[ing] the sexual abuse of young women" by Epstein.[4] He was named in a lawsuit filed by one of Epstein's victims, having resigned as chief executive of Barclays in November 2021 following a preliminary investigation by United Kingdom regulators into his relationship with Epstein.[4] The same suit also named [[jpmorgan-chase|JP Morgan]], where Staley worked in 2013 while Epstein was a private client of the firm.[4] [[jordan-sather|Jordan Sather]] noted 2021 reporting that United States financial regulators had examined Epstein and Staley emails containing the cryptic words "snow white," arguing that researchers long dismissed as conspiracy theorists for describing coded language in alleged pedophile networks were being proven right.[4]
