# John Winthrop

John Winthrop (1588–1649) was the Puritan governor of the Massachusetts Bay Colony, remembered chiefly for his 1630 "City on a Hill" address.

## The City on a Hill

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s [[avoiding-wwiii|Avoiding WWIII]] identifies Winthrop's speech as probably the earliest expression of America's sense of [[manifest-destiny|Manifest Destiny]]. In the article's reading, Winthrop's point was that America's identity should serve as a beacon and role model of liberty for other nations to be inspired by — a doctrine of moral example rather than conquest.

This conception is described as later amplified by [[george-washington|George Washington]] and [[benjamin-franklin|Benjamin Franklin]] through their insistence on the "Continental" naming of America's first army and congress, and codified diplomatically by [[john-quincy-adams|John Quincy Adams]] in the [[monroe-doctrine|Monroe Doctrine]].
