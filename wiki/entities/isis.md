## 2026 US prosecution of ISIS supporters

In March 2026 the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] announced terrorism charges against two ISIS supporters accused of attempting to detonate explosive devices during protests, an operation the Department described as an ISIS-inspired act of terrorism that could have killed American citizens.[4] Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pamela Bondi]] said the government "will not allow ISIS's poisonous, anti-American ideology to threaten this nation," while Deputy Attorney General [[todd-blanche|Todd Blanche]] said the defendants had allegedly sought mass casualties in service to ISIS with the hope of exceeding the carnage of the Boston Marathon bombing.[4]

[[fbi|FBI]] Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] said the defendants had tried to follow the group's path by attempting to detonate devices in a crowd, stated that the Bureau and its partners have "no tolerance for terrorist organizations or those inspired by them," and commended NYPD officers who intervened on the streets of New York.[4] The March 10, 2026 [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]] presented the case alongside the [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] wrongful-detention designation under the heading "War on Terror Reloaded?", treating both as resurgent war narratives in the information war.[4]

## 2026 prosecutions and alleged resurgence

Badlands commentary emphasized that ISIS is a [[sunni-islam|Sunni]] organization created to overthrow Bashar al-Assad, whose key strategic ally has always been Shia [[iran|Iran]] — a contradiction it argued undermines mainstream attempts to link Iran to the group.[5] It recalled that Barack Obama and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] once described ISIS fighters as "rebels," and that the insurgency was armed in part with weapons looted from the armory of [[muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]].[5] The piece also raised the possibility that [[nouri-al-maliki|Nouri Al-Maliki]] — alleged by the [[jerusalem-post|Jerusalem Post]] to have helped create ISIS — and Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]], a former public ISIS leader, both once served one side and now serve another.[5]

## New Orleans attack and French strikes (January 2025)

President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] said on January 1, 2025 that the Army veteran who rammed a pickup truck into New Year's revelers on Bourbon Street in the [[new-orleans-bourbon-street-attack|New Orleans attack]] was "inspired by" the Islamic State, citing federal investigators who were examining the man's possible ties to the group.[7] Authorities separately said there was no indication that the same-day [[las-vegas-cybertruck-explosion|Las Vegas Cybertruck explosion]] was connected to ISIS.[7]

Days earlier, [[france|France]]'s defense ministry announced that French warplanes had carried out strikes against ISIS targets in Syria — the first such French strikes since [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] took Damascus.[7] Defense Minister Sébastien Lecornu said that "on Sunday, French air assets carried out targeted strikes against Daesh sites on Syrian soil," using the Arabic acronym for ISIS, and that "our armies remain engaged in the fight against terrorism" in the region.[7] French Rafale fighter jets and American Reaper drones dropped a total of seven bombs on two military targets belonging to Daesh in central Syria.[7] The "ISIS threat" has also been cited by the Pentagon as ongoing justification for keeping some 2,000 or more American troops in [[syria]].[7]

Badlands commentary observed that ISIS had been relatively quiet since the HTS takeover, asking where the large ISIS bombings of prior years had gone, and argued that ISIS fighters are in fact embedded with HTS and its foreign-fighter affiliates — a claim it said recent American television footage had demonstrated.[7]

## Syria raid and Sahel allegations (August 2025)

On August 19, 2025, U.S. forces killed a senior ISIS official during a raid in northern Syria, according to a [[centcom|CENTCOM]] announcement two days later; the unnamed official was described as a key financier who helped orchestrate attacks in Syria and Iraq.[9] CENTCOM Commander Admiral Brad Cooper said the command would continue to pursue ISIS terrorists "with unwavering determination, throughout the region."[9]

Separately, Russian deputy UN representative [[dmitry-polyansky|Dmitry Polyansky]] criticized the UN Secretary-General's latest report on the threat posed by Islamic State in Africa while alleging that [[ukraine|Ukraine]]'s special services were arming and training militants in the Sahel.[9] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] connected the allegations to the 2011 [[nato|NATO]] intervention against Gaddafi and to the creation of [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] and ISIS, contending that ISIS affiliates in the Sahel were being animated by "Ukraine" — which he glossed as the [[five-eyes|Five Eyes]] — while the M23 group in the Democratic Republic of the Congo was being animated by [[israel|Israel]].[9]

## Caliphate declaration and 2024 resurgence warnings

On June 29, 2014, ISIS seized huge swathes of territory in Iraq and Syria and announced the establishment of a caliphate, declaring its leader [[abu-bakr-al-baghdadi|Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]] the caliph and "leader for Muslims everywhere."[10] That same year marked the beginning of Operation Inherent Resolve, the US-led mission in the Middle East to destroy the group.[10]

On the tenth anniversary of the caliphate declaration in June 2024, former [[cia|CIA]] deputy and acting director [[michael-morell|Michael Morell]] warned The Sun that the West could face a repeat of 9/11 if leaders did not act against an ISIS resurgence, saying he was "100 per cent" certain of the risk.[10] Badlands Media rejected the framing, arguing that the CIA created ISIS and questioning how Morell could claim such detailed knowledge of the group's operations and targets — pointing to ISIS attacks inside Russia, including the murder of Jewish and Christian leaders in Dagestan, and to the joint 2019 effort by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] that dismantled the organization.[10]

## Lubero church massacre, DRC (February 2025)

On 13 February 2025, militants of the [[allied-democratic-forces|Allied Democratic Forces]] — an ISIS affiliate operating in the eastern [[democratic-republic-of-the-congo|Democratic Republic of the Congo]] — beheaded seventy Christians with machetes, according to the monitoring group [[open-doors|Open Doors]].[11] The victims were rounded up from their homes in the Lubero district and executed inside a Protestant church in Kasanga, with witnesses reporting that the rebels shouted "Get out, get out" as they drove residents from their houses.[11]

Many Christians fled the region afterward, and local figures reported that churches, schools and health centres had already shut down because of the deteriorating security situation, with families unable to bury their dead for days.[11] Badlands commentary observed that the massacre drew almost no international coverage beyond a single Newsweek report, and framed the killings as part of a global pattern of Christian persecution.[11]

## Taliban killing of the Abbey Gate mastermind (2023)

In April 2023 senior U.S. administration officials disclosed that the [[isis-k|ISIS-K]] leader who planned the 2021 [[abbey-gate-bombing|Abbey Gate bombing]] at Kabul airport had been killed by the [[taliban|Taliban]] in Afghanistan, an assessment U.S. experts held with "high confidence."[13] The attack had killed 13 U.S. service members and more than 100 Afghan civilians.[13] Washington was not informed of the death by the Taliban and instead reached its conclusion through its own intelligence collection.[13]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] characterised ISIS as a "Globalist-funded terrorist threat" and argued that the Taliban had been charged by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] under the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]] with helping to eliminate it — a task he said they were carrying out.[13]

## Global comeback amid the Gaza war (2024)

With much of international attention gripped by the war in the Gaza Strip, ISIS steadily ramped up operations across continents in early 2024, setting the stage for a resurgence of global mayhem, according to Newsweek.[14] The latent threat came to life in March 2024 when the group claimed responsibility for the massacre at the [[crocus-city-hall-attack|Crocus City Hall]] concert venue outside Moscow, the deadliest militant attack on Russian soil since the 2002 theater hostage crisis in the capital.[14] Experts and officials warned that the next operation could target virtually anyone, including U.S. citizens.[14] Russian authorities, however, declined to echo the Western attribution of the attack to the group's Khorasan branch, pointing instead at Ukraine.[14]

## December 2024 CENTCOM strikes in central Syria

On December 8, 2024, [[centcom|U.S. Central Command]] conducted dozens of precision airstrikes against known ISIS camps, leaders, and operatives in central [[syria|Syria]], hitting more than 75 targets with B-52, F-15, and A-10 aircraft.[15] CENTCOM said the operation was intended to prevent the group from conducting external operations and from exploiting the collapse of the Syrian government to reconstitute in central Syria, and reported no indications of civilian casualties pending battle damage assessment.[15] General [[michael-kurilla|Michael Erik Kurilla]] warned that "all organizations in Syria should know that we will hold them accountable if they partner with or support ISIS in any way."[15]

Badlands commentary alleged that ISIS had originally been formed by Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] and Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] out of [[cia|CIA]] proxy groups operating across North Africa and the Middle East, with the assistance of [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] — a claim advanced by the outlet rather than an established finding.[15]

President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] is accused of helping form ISIS and then allowing it to expand, using the label "ISIL" to keep public attention on Al-Qaeda in Iraq rather than Al-Qaeda in Syria, and dismissing the group to David Remnick of The New Yorker with the analogy that "if a JV team puts on Lakers uniforms, that doesn't make them Kobe Bryant."[23] Nine months later, the article notes, ISIS was regarded by U.S. intelligence and senior military officers as a grave threat to the stability of the entire Middle East.[23] The group went on to post brutal public executions and to take women and young girls as sex slaves.[23]

Senator [[rand-paul|Rand Paul]] challenged McCain publicly over arming the Syrian rebels, arguing that weapons sent to them ended up with ISIS anyway; McCain, criticizing Paul on Sean Hannity's show, slipped and appeared to admit he had met with ISIS rather than the [[free-syrian-army|Free Syrian Army]].[23] The article compares the whole arrangement to the CIA's training and funding of the Mujahideen in Afghanistan, who likewise were called freedom fighters before becoming Al-Qaeda.[23] It credits [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] with reversing course by unleashing the U.S. military to destroy ISIS, culminating in the October 2019 raid that killed al-Baghdadi.[23]
