# CapCut

CapCut is a popular video-editing application owned by [[bytedance|ByteDance]], the Chinese parent company of [[tiktok|TikTok]].[1]

## Planned US split

In July 2025, Business Insider reported that ByteDance had laid out plans, according to documentation it viewed, to split US users into a new version of the app called "CapCut US," meaning Americans may need to download a separate build to keep using its video-editing tools. Establishing a US-specific app was described as potentially part of a broader plan to comply with a 2024 law requiring ByteDance to separate from TikTok and its other US assets or essentially cease operating in the country. The Information had reported the previous Sunday that ByteDance was similarly planning a separate US version of TikTok, suggesting a broader corporate push to separate from the United States beyond a TikTok sale.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] expressed long-standing skepticism, dating to the Trump 1.0 proposal for Oracle to host a US version of TikTok, that such a split can work without sacrificing the user experience and the revenue model. The analysis distinguished two scenarios: if the new app runs on the same underlying infrastructure, the separation is a narrative solution only; if the infrastructure changes with the app, success depends on user experience, raising questions about development teams and product roadmaps.[1]

The commentary further argued that corporations and their technology consultants often design aspirational or theoretical solutions to regulatory requirements they know are unworkable, because the requirements themselves are impossible to satisfy — citing cloud exit strategies as a comparable case — and that such solutions are usually accepted because the problem statements are theoretical and the approval bodies are frequently non-technical.[1]
