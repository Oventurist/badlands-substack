# Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project

The Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project (OCCRP) is an international non-governmental organization comprised of journalists in six countries, best known for corruption reporting in Eastern Europe and Russia, and identified in December 2024 reporting as having received roughly half of its funding from the United States government.[1]

## Government funding

According to a report published by Drop Site News and amplified by [[the-national-pulse|The National Pulse]], 52 percent of the funds OCCRP spent between 2014 and 2023 were funneled to it by the U.S. federal government, principally the [[state-department|Department of State]].[1] Since the organization's founding, the U.S. government has contributed $47 million to its budget and is committed to granting it an additional $12 million.[1] The report noted that while OCCRP had disclosed that it receives some government funding, the exact amounts were not previously publicly known.[1]

In addition to American government funding, OCCRP has received an estimated $15 million over the preceding decade from the governments of Britain, France, Sweden, Denmark, and the Netherlands.[1] It is also backed by private donors, including left-wing billionaire [[george-soros|George Soros]]'s [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]].[1]

## Role in the first Trump impeachment

OCCRP, co-founded by entrepreneur Drew Sullivan, was described as intimately involved in the first Democrat-backed impeachment of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] U.S. taxpayer dollars likely contributed to the organization's investigation into former New York Mayor [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]]'s political work in Ukraine; those activities were cited no fewer than four times in the whistleblower letter that sparked the [[first-impeachment-of-donald-trump|first impeachment of Donald Trump]] in 2019, of which Trump was ultimately acquitted by the U.S. Senate.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that of all the promises from the incoming administration, scrutinizing NGOs and holding them accountable was her favorite, arguing that governments rely on NGOs to provide plausible deniability when local, state and federal entities subvert the rights of American citizens.[1] She characterized the OCCRP story as an example of "NGOs-as-narrative centers, funded by the State Department," while contending that NGOs more broadly are "the global glue of public-private partnerships which govern every industry and sector in every geography."[1] She insisted that exposure alone was insufficient and that a "justice phase" with real accountability was necessary to restore public trust.[1]

## Reporting on Russia sanctions enforcement

In March 2023 the OCCRP reported on United States efforts to target smuggling and money laundering networks supporting the Russian regime, documenting more than $500 million in asset seizures over the preceding year and indictments of more than 30 individuals and two corporate entities.[2] Its account described a new wave of [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury]] sanctions on Russia's metals and mining sector, coordinated with [[g7|G7]] partners, together with export controls, price caps and the blacklisting of thousands of individuals and entities, and noted that more than 1,000 foreign companies had scaled back or ceased operations in Russia.[2] The reporting was reproduced and discussed in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]].[2]
