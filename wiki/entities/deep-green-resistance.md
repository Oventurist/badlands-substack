# Deep Green Resistance

Deep Green Resistance is an eco-anarchist organization named by Badlands Media as part of an interconnected mesh of agencies which, together with [[extinction-rebellion|Extinction Rebellion]], provide what the article calls disposable misanthropic eco-terrorist cannon fodder while "above ground" operatives penetrate the official corridors of power within politics, academia, military intelligence and polite society.[1]

The group is described as billionaire-funded and is placed on one side of a [[double-bind-theory|false dichotomy]]: having accepted the premise that technology is the intrinsic enemy of humanity and nature, its activists choose to go to war against the industrial system from below, while figures such as [[yuval-noah-harari|Yuval Noah Harari]] accept the same premise and instead adapt to it from above.[1] Both paths, in the article's argument, converge on a techno-feudal dystopia.[1] Derrick Jensen, associated with this current of thought, is listed among the eco-terrorist theorists whose fundamental assumptions about humanity, technology and history the article says are identical with those of [[prince-philip|Prince Philip]] and [[maurice-strong|Maurice Strong]].[1]

## Founding and doctrine

Deep Green Resistance was co-founded in 2011 by [[derrick-jensen|Derrick Jensen]], a prolific eco-activist author who had corresponded with [[ted-kaczynski|Ted Kaczynski]], and is described as a California-based eco-anarchy movement with deep penetration into Canada.[2] Advocating the complete destruction of major infrastructure worldwide in a bid to save nature from humanity, the movement set out its [[decisive-ecological-warfare|Decisive Ecological Warfare]] doctrine dividing members into "above grounders," who build communities and pursue direct and indirect action, and "under grounders," who "engage in limited attacks on infrastructure, especially energy infrastructure."[2] The stated aim is "to use selective attacks to accelerate collapse in a deliberate way."[2]

The organization publishes a five-fold strategy running from militant action against energy infrastructure through participation in social justice struggles, defense of land against logging and mining, construction of decentralized resistance organizations, and the rebuilding of subsistence agriculture and localized democratic communities.[2]

## Military guidance and the Underground Action Calendar

In a 2011 recruitment video, one member said the group's confidence in its strategy came from talking to people who had been involved in the military, who explained that "going after critical nodes of infrastructure is a classic technique that's been used hundreds or even thousands of times throughout history to win large scale conflicts, and especially asymmetric conflicts."[2] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] questions whether the activists recognize that they may be pawns on a geopolitical chessboard.[2] The group's news service also hosts an "Underground Action Calendar" listing over one hundred successful eco-terror acts against rail and energy infrastructure across the Americas and Europe, stating openly that its purpose is to "publicize and normalize the use of militant and underground tactics."[2]
