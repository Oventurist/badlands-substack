# Michael Sussmann

Michael Sussmann is an American attorney who was one of only two defendants brought to trial by Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] in the course of his investigation into the origins of the [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] inquiry.[1]

## Role in the Durham investigation

Sussmann's prosecution, alongside that of [[igor-danchenko|Igor Danchenko]], became the principal yardstick by which critics measured the Durham inquiry's output.[1] Because neither case produced a conviction, commentators across the political spectrum used the results to argue either that Durham had been obstructed by a corrupt system or that his mandate had never been serious.[1] [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] pointed to the Sussmann and Danchenko outcomes when asking how well the strategy of pursuing individual charges had actually worked, noting that "people wanted to see charges" and were left dissatisfied.[1]

During Durham's June 21, 2023 testimony before the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], Democratic members cited the small number of trials — two — and the absence of major convictions as evidence that the [[durham-report|Durham Report]] exonerated the officials [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had accused of running the [[russiagate-hoax|Russia collusion hoax]].[1] [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued the opposite: that the thinness of the trial record suggested the more serious offenses remained unexposed.[1]

## Evidence surfaced at the Sussmann trial

Badlands Media contributor [[brian-cates|Brian Cates]] argued that Sussmann's prosecution served [[john-durham|Durham]] chiefly as a venue for placing long-suppressed evidence on the public record.[2] The trial established the existence of a private [[hillary-clinton|Clinton]] campaign spy ring operating through cybersecurity contractors with access to federal agency databases; its surveillance of [[donald-trump|Trump]] and his associates at Trump Tower and later at the White House; the campaign's provision of a target list; the trawling of federal databases; and the theft of classified and non-public data used to construct the [[alfa-bank-hoax|Alfa Bank Hoax]] under the direction of the campaign's lawyers at [[perkins-coie|Perkins Coie]].[2] That product was delivered to the news media and to the [[fbi|FBI]] and the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]].[2]

The trial also brought out that after the FBI closed its Alfa Bank inquiry around the time of the inauguration, the operatives revised and expanded the allegation with data taken from [[executive-office-of-the-president|Executive Office of the President]] servers and presented it to the CIA in February 2017.[2] FBI personnel testified that they were themselves subjects of ongoing investigations, and Durham demonstrated that more than 80 percent of the attorney–client privilege claims raised by the campaign, Perkins Coie, [[fusion-gps|Fusion GPS]] and [[rodney-joffe|Rodney Joffe]]'s contractors to resist grand jury subpoenas were bogus on their face.[2] Cates noted that none of this material had been publicly known in the five years before Durham's filings and the trial itself.[2]
