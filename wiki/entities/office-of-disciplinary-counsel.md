# Office of Disciplinary Counsel

The Office of Disciplinary Counsel is the legal ethics office that handles attorney discipline in Washington, D.C. In May 2025 it became a focus of controversy after Justice Department official [[ed-martin|Ed Martin]] disclosed that the office was investigating him.[1]

## Investigation of Ed Martin

Martin, whose nomination to serve as U.S. attorney for the District of Columbia had stalled in the [[us-senate|Senate]], revealed the probe in an office-wide goodbye email on his last day as interim U.S. attorney, alerting roughly 350 attorneys and 400 staff members to the matter.[1] He alleged that the Office of Disciplinary Counsel had inappropriately notified a unit of his U.S. attorney's office of the probe, violating his confidentiality.[1] Martin identified the case number, 2025-D047, but not the specific allegations against him.[1]

## Complaints and leadership

Complaints were lodged with the office's chief, Hamilton P. "Phil" Fox.[1] Democratic lawmakers and outside groups accused Martin of abusing his power and sought the suspension of his law license or other penalties, alleging he had used the threat of prosecution to intimidate and chill the speech of perceived adversaries — lawmakers, protesters, journalists, medical journals and others he said opposed the agendas of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] or adviser [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] — even where no crime had been committed.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[canncon|CannCon]] characterized the action as the disciplinary apparatus going after Martin's law license, noting that Fox had previously pursued [[rudy-giuliani|Rudy Giuliani]] and other attorneys who brought election cases.[1]
