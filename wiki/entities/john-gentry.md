# John Gentry

John Gentry is a former [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] officer and academic who has argued publicly that the American intelligence community became politicized during the administration of [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]].[1] Gentry served in the CIA from 1978 to 1990 and later became an adjunct professor at Georgetown University.[1]

## Neutering the CIA

Gentry set out his case in a book titled *Neutering the CIA: Why U.S. Intelligence Versus Trump Has Long-Term Consequences*, which details what he describes as the agency's drift away from non-partisan analysis.[1] According to Gentry, Obama and his appointees "made and institutionalized significant changes, largely by creating new structures, policies, and incentives designed to alter organizational cultures in ways congruent with Obama's political agenda."[1] He further argued that Democratic activists hired during that period "shifted leftward the collective, politically salient worldview of the intelligence bureaucracy," and characterized the CIA as a "center of partisan political activity."[1]

Gentry traces the origins of the problem earlier, to the 1990s and the tenure of CIA analyst [[robert-gates|Robert Gates]], whom he accuses of ordering reports to be skewed in ways that supported the political narratives of elected officials.[1]

## Reception in the Truth Community

Commentary published by Badlands Media in its [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] treated Gentry as a whistleblower of the first order. Writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Gentry "should be considered one of the most important whistleblowers of our time, and protected with the highest levels of duty," contrasting the treatment of intelligence-community whistleblowers such as [[eric-ciaramella|Eric Ciaramella]] with witnesses appearing before the House committee on the weaponization of government.[1] She invoked [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]]'s remark that those who take on the intelligence community face "six ways from Sunday at getting back at you," and argued that a politicized intelligence apparatus wielded against domestic political opponents and in US elections is "anathema to the American form of government."[1]
