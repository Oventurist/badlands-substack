# House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence

The House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence, commonly referred to as the House Intelligence Committee, is the standing committee of the US House of Representatives charged with oversight of the intelligence community, including the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]]. It operates largely in secret, holding classified briefings and maintaining custody of sensitive documents produced during oversight activities.

## 2026 Justice Department records request

According to the March 26, 2026 [[badlands-brief-143|Badlands Brief]], the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] requested records held by the committee in connection with a reported criminal inquiry into former CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]]. The request centered on documents generated or held by the committee during prior intelligence oversight activities, and was described in reporting as a "rare instance" of the executive branch seeking access to legislative branch materials in a prosecutorial context. Observers noted that the request raised potential separation-of-powers questions and could lead to negotiations or litigation over the scope of compliance and the limits of executive authority.

The committee voted to cooperate with the request. Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] reported that the vote "in the secretive intelligence panel was split along party lines and deeply divided the committee," with Democrats objecting that they had not had adequate time to review material that several lawmakers said they received on a Friday. Badlands commentary treated the vote as evidence that institutional resistance to accountability for senior intelligence officials had weakened, characterizing the "uniparty" as frustrated by its inability to stop the process.
