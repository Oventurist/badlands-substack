# House Intelligence Committee

The House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence — referred to in Badlands coverage simply as the House Intelligence Committee — is the chamber's principal oversight body for the US intelligence community. In March 2026 the committee became the focal point of an unusual inter-branch dispute when the Justice Department requested records held by the panel in connection with a reported criminal inquiry into former CIA Director John Brennan.[1]

## DOJ records request (March 2026)

The [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] asked the committee to turn over documents generated or held during prior intelligence oversight activities that bear on [[john-brennan|John Brennan]], who directed the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]] from 2013 to 2017 and who, according to reporting cited by Badlands, established the 2016 counterintelligence investigation into [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1] Coverage described the request as a "rare instance" of the executive branch seeking access to legislative branch materials in a prosecutorial context, signaling potential escalation beyond routine information-gathering even though no charges had been announced and the scope of the inquiry remained unclear.[1]

Observers noted that the request could raise separation-of-powers questions, prompting negotiations or litigation over compliance and the limits of executive authority with respect to congressional records.[1]

## Internal division over the vote

The committee's decision to cooperate was contested. Badlands contributor Ashe in America reported that the vote in the "secretive intelligence panel" was split along party lines and deeply divided the committee, with Democrats objecting that they had not been given enough time to review material several lawmakers said they received only the preceding Friday.[1]

## McCarthy's 2023 blocking of Schiff and Swalwell

On January 24, 2023, House Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] made good on his pledge to block California Democratic Representatives [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]] and [[eric-swalwell|Eric Swalwell]] from sitting on the committee.[2] Special rules governing the panel allow the Speaker to unilaterally halt the seating of lawmakers without a full floor vote, and McCarthy notified House Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] of the decision by letter, writing that "in order to maintain a standard worthy of this committee's responsibilities, I am hereby rejecting the appointments of Representative Adam Schiff and Representative Eric Swalwell to serve on the Intelligence Committee."[2] Jeffries had recommended the two for the committee despite McCarthy's earlier vow to block them along with [[ilhan-omar|Ilhan Omar]].[2]

## Commentary

Badlands framed the episode as evidence of a broader shift in accountability: two years earlier, the prospect of Brennan facing legal jeopardy was treated as fantasy, whereas by March 2026 the committee was voting to collaborate with the Justice Department while what Badlands calls the "uniparty" appeared frustrated at its inability to stop the process.[1]

## Durham hearing, June 2023

On Tuesday, June 20, 2023, the House Intelligence Committee was scheduled to hold a 3 p.m. hearing on Capitol Hill with Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]], more than a month after the release of his report on the [[fbi|FBI]]'s [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] investigation, with lawmakers expected to probe his findings.[3] Committee Chairman [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]] had said shortly after the report's release that it outlined "serious errors and shortcomings" in the FBI's handling of the investigation.[3] The hearing was the first of two Durham appearances that week, followed by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] on Wednesday morning; Badlands Media announced it would stream the public-facing testimony.[3]

## February 2024 national security threat warning

On Wednesday, February 14, 2024, committee chairman [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]] issued a cryptic public warning of a "serious national security threat," saying all members of Congress had already been briefed on the underlying information and calling on President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] to declassify it so that Congress, the administration and allies could openly discuss a response.[4] Capitol Hill sources told ABC News the matter concerned the alleged [[russian-space-nuclear-weapon-program|Russian space nuclear weapon program]], and unnamed Democratic and CNN sources described it as "destabilizing."[4] The episode placed the committee at the center of a fight over the Senate's $95 billion national security supplemental, which Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] was refusing to bring to a vote.[4]
