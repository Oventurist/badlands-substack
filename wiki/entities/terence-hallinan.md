# Terence Hallinan

Terence Hallinan was the District Attorney of San Francisco who preceded [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] in that office, and who first hired her to run his office's Career Criminal Unit roughly three years after [[willie-brown|Willie Brown]] was elected mayor of the city.[1] Known around San Francisco by the nickname "K.O." on account of his boxing skills, Hallinan cultivated a reputation as a "tough progressive" who was willing to confront the city's most powerful institutions, including a corrupt police force and the sitting mayor himself.[1]

## Background and legal career

Before becoming District Attorney, Hallinan accumulated an unusually colorful legal and political record. He supported the Communist Party USA, represented Church of Satan founder Anton LaVey, reportedly shot heroin with Janis Joplin, served on the Patty Hearst defense team, and successfully obtained the reversal on appeal of the murder convictions of serial killer Juan Corona.[1] He also unsuccessfully defended William Leonard Pickard, who ran an LSD laboratory in Mountain View, California, and later used the District Attorney's official letterhead to personally recommend that Pickard be granted bail after being arrested in what was described as the largest LSD bust in history.[1]

## Hiring of Kamala Harris and the 2003 race

Hallinan maintained that Harris's connection to Willie Brown had no bearing on her hiring, though the article argues he would come to regret the decision.[1] When the number two position in his office opened up, Harris sought it; Hallinan gave the job to someone else, prompting her bitter departure and what the article characterizes as an unspoken vendetta against her former employer.[1] Mayor Brown subsequently shifted from being a qualified supporter of Hallinan to publicly attacking him for "not doing his job," and a Brown insider told the San Francisco Chronicle that "This whole thing was about Kamala Harris … cross one of Willie's friends and there will be hell to pay."[1]

In the 2003 District Attorney race, Hallinan was the incumbent and initially led Harris, who often polled in the single digits and a distant third.[1] Once the Brown political machine engaged, Harris out-raised Hallinan by more than double.[1] Hallinan filed a campaign complaint alleging that Harris had violated the voluntary $211,000 spending cap she had pledged in January to honor; by November she had raised $621,000, and was fined $34,000.[1] Hallinan finished first in the November three-way vote but without a majority, forcing a runoff that Harris won; she was sworn in as District Attorney in early 2004.[1]

## Catholic clergy abuse investigation

Hallinan's most consequential unfinished work concerned clergy sexual abuse. He had already prosecuted several Catholic priests on charges of sexual misconduct involving children and was assembling case files for further prosecutions.[1] His investigation determined that the [[archdiocese-of-san-francisco|San Francisco Archdiocese]] held internal records of similar complaints stretching back more than seventy years, and in the spring of 2002 — about a year before Harris replaced him — he demanded the church turn those documents over, which it reluctantly did.[1]

The documents were reportedly explosive, naming roughly 40 active Bay Area priests against whom complaints had been filed.[1] Hallinan's prosecutions hit a roadblock when the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that California's law extending the statute of limitations in priest abuse cases was unconstitutional, but he remained determined to at least make the documents public.[1] His pursuit was a thorn in the side of the Catholic Church, which was being financially drained by legal fees and settlements, and the records touched institutions at the heart of California's power structure — notably the [[jesuits|Jesuit]]-run [[st-ignatius-college-preparatory|St. Ignatius College Preparatory]], alma mater of figures such as [[jerry-brown|Jerry Brown]] and members of the [[getty-family|Getty family]].[1] After Harris took office, the effort to publicize the documents ended and no priest was prosecuted.[1]

## Coverage in "The Candidate Who Isn't There"

In "The Candidate Who Isn't There," published August 24, 2024, Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] wrote that Hallinan, as the district attorney preceding [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], was preparing to release to the public all of the evidence he had gathered on decades of sexual assault within the [[archdiocese-of-san-francisco|Roman Catholic Archdiocese of San Francisco]].[2] According to DeLarme, the archdiocese made sizable contributions to Harris's 2003 campaign for San Francisco district attorney with full knowledge of that pending disclosure, and once in office Harris squashed the effort rather than allowing the process to finish playing out.[2]
