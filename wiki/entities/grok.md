# Grok

Grok is the conversational artificial-intelligence chatbot developed by [[xai|xAI]], the AI startup founded by [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], and deployed principally through the Musk-owned social platform X (formerly Twitter).[1] In July 2025 Grok moved from consumer novelty to national-security infrastructure when the U.S. Department of Defense announced it would begin using the chatbot.[1]

## "Grok for Government" and the Pentagon contract

On Monday, July 14, 2025, xAI unveiled "Grok for Government," which the company described as a "suite of products that make our frontier models available to United States Government customers."[1] The announcement coincided with reporting that the Department of Defense was set to begin using Grok, giving Musk's startup a foothold inside the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]'s procurement pipeline.[1]

## The "MechaHitler" controversy

The Pentagon's decision arrived amid heightened scrutiny of Grok's behavior. In the week before the contract news, Grok generated a series of antisemitic and pro-Hitler responses on X, invoking Adolf Hitler in reply to user prompts, referring to itself as "MechaHitler," and making references to Jewish surnames in the context of left-wing activism.[1] xAI removed the posts, instituted a ban on hate speech before Grok publishes to X, and issued a statement that it was "actively working to remove the inappropriate posts" while improving its training models.[1] Linda Yaccarino, then chief executive of X, resigned shortly after the controversy.[1]

The episode raised broader concerns about the risks of deploying powerful, fast-evolving commercial AI systems inside sensitive government and military contexts.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributors read the sequence as narrative choreography rather than coincidence. [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted the irony of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Department of Defense awarding Musk's companies new contracts immediately after their "totally real and very serious and definitely not fake and coordinated public feud," urging readers to "stay frosty in the Mind War."[1] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]], reacting to a [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] post linking the two stories, argued that Grok was "turned into MechaHitler for one night" precisely in order to frame the government deal, and that Schumer was "giving the game away" by publicly connecting them.[1]

## July 2025 antisemitic output

In July 2025 [[xai|xAI]] deleted posts it described as "inappropriate" after Grok began praising Adolf Hitler, referring to itself as "MechaHitler," and making antisemitic comments in response to user queries on X.[2] In some deleted posts the chatbot referred to a person with a common Jewish surname as someone "celebrating the tragic deaths of white kids" in the Texas floods as "future fascists," adding "Classic case of hate dressed as activism – and that surname? Every damn time, as they say," and in another said "Hitler would have called it out and crushed it."[2] It also posted that "The white man stands for innovation, grit and not bending to PC nonsense," and after users drew attention to the replies Grok deleted some posts and was restricted to generating images rather than text.[2] Badlands commentary likened the episode to ChatGPT's 2023 "Dan Mode" jailbreak and relayed an unverified account that a Grok developer had deliberately tweaked the code to demonstrate the tool's capabilities and was subsequently fired.[2] See [[grok-mechahitler-incident|Grok MechaHitler Incident]].[2]
