# Ehud Barak

Ehud Barak is a former Prime Minister of Israel and a former chief of the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]], who led the Israeli government between 1999 and 2001.[1] In November 2023, more than a month into the war between Israel and [[hamas|Hamas]], Barak re-entered international commentary through an exclusive interview with [[politico|POLITICO]] in which he warned that Israel had only a narrow window in which to complete its military objectives in [[gaza|Gaza]].[1]

## November 2023 POLITICO interview

Barak argued that while Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] was preparing the Israeli public for a "long and difficult war," Israel in fact had only weeks left to eliminate Hamas as global public opinion — and most significantly American public opinion — swung rapidly against its campaign in Gaza.[1] He observed that the sympathy generated toward Israel in the immediate aftermath of the [[october-7-attacks|October 7 attacks]], described as the deadliest terrorist attack on Israel in the Jewish state's 75-year history, was already diminishing.[1]

In the same interview Barak said the rhetoric of U.S. officials had shifted in recent days, with a mounting chorus of calls for a humanitarian pause in the fighting.[1] He framed the emerging tension in blunt terms: "You can see the window is closing. It's clear we are heading towards friction with the Americans about the offensive. America cannot dictate to Israel what to do. But we cannot ignore them," adding that Israel "will have to come to terms with the American demands within the next two or three weeks, probably less."[1] The remarks were made in explicit reference to Washington's role as the main guarantor of Israeli security.[1]

## Post-war governance proposals

Barak also floated a possible post-conflict arrangement for Gaza, suggesting that a multinational Arab force might have to take control of the territory after the military campaign concluded, in order to help usher in a return of [[mahmoud-abbas|Mahmoud Abbas]]' [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]] to govern in place of Hamas.[1] Even with such a change in the political order of Gaza, however, Barak stressed that a return to diplomacy aimed at the creation of a Palestinian state remained a very remote prospect.[1]

## Coverage in Badlands Media

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] carried the Barak interview in its [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of November 10, 2023, where contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] used it to illustrate a broader macro trend: that whereas Ukraine took roughly eighteen months to exhaust the patience of the American public, the Israel–Hamas conflict had worn down public willingness to fund a foreign war on a dramatically accelerated timeline.[1] The commentary noted bipartisan agreement in the United States against direct involvement in the conflict, with liberals criticizing Israel and conservatives increasingly hesitant to trade one failed proxy war for another.[1]

## Commentary on a wider Trump–Iran deal (2024)

In an interview with Politico published in November 2024, Barak suggested that the incoming administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] might seek to negotiate a wider agreement with [[iran|Iran]] despite hawkish campaign rhetoric: "It isn't inconceivable that in spite of the appearance of being very hawkish vis-a-vis Iran, the overall isolationist mindset of this new administration will lead them to look at a much wider deal."[2] He speculated that such a grand bargain could interlink talks with [[russia|Russia]] over [[ukraine|Ukraine]] and with Iran, with Trump possibly asking President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to help "convince Tehran to halt its proxy actions" and to find "some formula" allowing Iran, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and [[israel|Israel]] to coexist peacefully.[2]

## Badlands framing as a Netanyahu rival

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry noted the significance of such public signalling coming from a former Israeli prime minister, describing Barak as a lifelong political rival of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] who defeated him in 1999, ending Netanyahu's first term, and again in a later contest.[2] The commentary placed Barak in the Labor Party — characterized as socialist and long supportive of the Two-State Solution — and recalled that his political career began in 1995 with an appointment as Minister of the Interior under Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin, the signatory of the [[oslo-accords|Oslo Accords]].[2]

The same commentary grouped Barak with [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] and [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] as figures from different ideological camps converging in opposition to Netanyahu's government, and speculated openly about whether Barak had communicated with Trump.[2]

## September 11, 2001 remarks

Not hours after the [[september-11-attacks|September 11 attacks]], Barak — described by Badlands Media as another Israeli "terror expert" — gleefully advocated a "War on Terror" against "a kind of bin Laden Organization," well before [[osama-bin-laden|Osama bin Laden]] was officially named as the perpetrator.3 It was not until 9:30 PM that night that CIA Director [[george-tenet|George Tenet]] told President [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] and other senior officials that the agency's Counterterrorism Center had determined bin Laden and [[al-qaeda]] were responsible.3
