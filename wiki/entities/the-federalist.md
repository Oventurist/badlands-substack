# The Federalist

The Federalist is an American conservative online magazine frequently cited by Badlands Media for its coverage of election administration and legislative developments in the states.[1]

## Coverage cited by Badlands

In March 2024 The Federalist reported on the passage of [[louisiana-senate-bill-101|Louisiana Senate Bill 101]] through the state Senate, legislation prohibiting [[ranked-choice-voting|ranked-choice voting]] in Louisiana elections, and provided the account of sponsor Blake Miguez's arguments that "complex tabulated ballots" are "un-American."[1] The outlet had previously covered Louisiana's constitutional amendment banning "Zuckbucks," which Miguez also sponsored, as well as the State Freedom Caucus Network's influence on Republican state legislatures.[1]

## Lawsuit against the State Department (2023)

The Federalist was a co-plaintiff, alongside [[daily-wire|The Daily Wire]] and the state of Texas, in a December 2023 suit against the [[state-department|State Department]] alleging that the agency funded censorship technology intended to bankrupt domestic outlets holding disfavored political opinions.[2] Filed by the [[new-civil-liberties-alliance|New Civil Liberties Alliance]] in the Eastern District of Texas, the action centered on the [[global-engagement-center|Global Engagement Center]] and named Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] among the defendants.[2]

## "Help Women Quit Their Jobs" essay (January 2025)

On January 7, 2025, The Federalist published an essay arguing that if Americans want to be healthier, policy should make it easier for women to leave paid employment and return to full-time motherhood.[3] The piece held that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s nomination of [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] as Health and Human Services secretary had broken cultural and media taboos against noticing that Americans pay the most in the world for health care while carrying the most chronic disease, with food a leading cause.[3] While Kennedy and health commentators promoted by Tucker Carlson — including Calley and Casey Means — rightly identified processed food, Big Pharma, Big Food and Big Ag as systemically broken, the author argued that the decline in full-time mothers had gone unnoticed as a driver of that brokenness.[3]

The essay contended that the only healthy and accessible alternative to processed food is home cooking from scratch, a service traditionally provided by mothers and, in an age of socialized medicine, an investment in one's country as well as one's family.[3] The author estimated spending 25–30 hours a week on scratch cooking including shopping, with a serious garden adding another 15–20 hours, making the time required hard to square with a full-time job outside the home.[3]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded by quoting G.K. Chesterton's 1910 *What's Wrong with the World*, in which he wrote that women are "tortured" by "the absurd modern attempt to make them domestic empresses and competitive clerks at the same time," and that the modern working woman "bears a double burden."[3] She argued that the normalization of the two-income requirement ignited with central banking and spread with suffrage, that Chesterton's account of women's "anarchy" — having no rules for one's mind rather than no customs — describes successful homeschool mothers, and that society must reject "the huge heresy of Precedent" rather than compound a wrong turn.[3]
