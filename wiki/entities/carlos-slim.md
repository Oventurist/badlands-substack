# Carlos Slim

Carlos Slim is a Mexican billionaire who in 2008 was the world's richest man.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) He was a Lehman client who had already lost heavily as the bank's stock price collapsed.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

During [[lehman-brothers|Lehman Brothers]]' 2008 crisis, [[jeb-bush|Jeb Bush]]'s assignment under "[[project-verde|Project Verde]]" was to persuade Slim to make a potentially life-saving investment in the firm; on July 5, 2008 Bush reported the meeting had been unsuccessful because Slim "did not express interest in jv (joint venture) or stock purchase."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) Slim declined to comment beyond a spokesman's statement that the two had been "friends for 20 years, but they have never done business together."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair) The Badlands Media account counters that even without direct business, both men were good friends of the [[salinas-family|Salinas family]], described as huge drug traffickers in Mexico.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-family-affair)

## Telmex and the Salinas privatization

Carlos Slim Helú was the foremost beneficiary of the [[privatization-in-mexico|privatization program]] carried out by his friend [[carlos-salinas-de-gortari|Carlos Salinas de Gortari]].[2] Wired reported that during the six years of the Salinas presidency Slim "never seemed to make a wrong move," buying companies at rock-bottom prices, selling stocks at peaks and founding ventures with powerful allies — but that the key to his rise was the sweet deal he received on his 1990 purchase of [[telmex|Telmex]], the national telephone monopoly.[2] He took control with a $1.76 billion bid for 20.4 percent of the stock, after which the government promptly authorized a 40 percent local-service rate increase and guaranteed him a private monopoly on long-distance service until 1997; his personal fortune reached US$4.4 billion by 1994.[2] [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] notes that Slim had served on the Salinas election committee during the 1988 campaign and was rewarded once his candidate took office, calling the sequence "corruption at its finest."[2]

## Cartel allegations

Slim is of Lebanese descent, his parents having emigrated to Mexico, and his elder brother Julián was a member of the CIA-assisted Mexican secret police, the [[direccion-federal-de-seguridad|Dirección Federal de Seguridad]].[2] Lange also cites a teleSur report on Diego Enrique Osorno's book, which recounts that former Mexican presidents were "very fearful" of speaking about Slim, and on WikiLeaks-released Stratfor emails from April 2011 in which analyst Anya Alfano asked intelligence chief Fred Burton where Slim fit into Mexican cartel dynamics; Burton put the question to [[drug-enforcement-administration|DEA]] Special Agent William F. Dionne, who replied that "the MX telecommunication billionaire is" linked to the narcos.[2] Lange characterizes Slim as a private individual and a member of "the protected class," consistently portrayed as a savvy businessman.[2]
