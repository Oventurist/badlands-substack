# Alien Terrorist Removal Court

The **Alien Terrorist Removal Court** is a five-judge federal panel created by [[congress|Congress]] in 1996 to expedite the removal of foreign nationals designated as alien terrorists.[1] Until July 2026 it had never been used.[1]

## Activation in 2026

The Trump administration activated the never-before-used process to quickly deport foreign nationals designated as alien terrorists or their immediate family members.[1] The [[justice-department|Justice Department]] filed the first-ever application with the court; the July 15 filing was sealed, and its cover sheet indicated the contents were classified.[1]

## Structure and statutory authority

Under the 1996 law establishing the court, the Secretary of State can bar members of designated terrorist organizations, people who endorse or espouse terrorist activity and, in some cases, their spouses or children.[1] The law also allows the Secretary of State to designate individuals who intend to engage in activities that could endanger the welfare, safety or security of the United States.[1]

The court's five judges are selected nationwide by Chief Justice [[john-roberts|John Roberts]], a structure similar to the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court, which handles surveillance applications involving suspected foreign agents.[1]

## First proceedings

Chief Judge Joan Ericksen held a sealed hearing on Thursday but ordered the government to submit a more carefully considered application after raising questions about the [[justice-department|DOJ]]'s target and how the alleged conduct connected to the laws authorizing removal.[1] An updated response was due the following Wednesday.[1]
