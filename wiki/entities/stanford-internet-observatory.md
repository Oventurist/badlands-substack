# Stanford Internet Observatory

The **Stanford Internet Observatory** is an academic research program at Stanford University that studies the abuse of internet platforms. It is best known in the Badlands corpus for its joint investigation with the [[wall-street-journal|Wall Street Journal]] into child-exploitation networks operating on [[instagram|Instagram]].[1]

## Instagram pedophile-network investigation

In June 2023, a comprehensive investigation conducted by the Wall Street Journal together with the Stanford Internet Observatory reported that [[meta-platforms|Meta]]-owned Instagram had hosted an organized and large-scale network of pedophiles.[1] The researchers found that Instagram's own recommendation algorithms actively promoted such content to other users with the same interests, because those systems excel at linking people who share niche interests.[1]

The investigation documented a coded vocabulary used by the accounts involved, including an image of a map as shorthand for "minor-attracted person" and the phrase "cheese pizza," which shares its initials with "child pornography," a finding attributed to researcher Levine of the University of Massachusetts.[1] Many accounts described themselves as "lovers of the little things in life."[1] Researchers also reported that Instagram permitted searches on explicit hashtags such as #pedowhore and #preteensex, which connected users to accounts advertising child-sex material for sale, and that sellers signalled a child's purported age using coded phrasing such as "on chapter 14" or "age 31" alongside a reverse-arrow emoji.[1]

Commentary published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contrasted the platform's tolerance of these networks with its enforcement against accounts in the so-called freedom movement, and noted that the "cheese pizza" coding echoed symbolism previously associated with the [[pizzagate|Pizzagate]] controversy surrounding Comet Ping Pong.[1]
