# Roblox

**Roblox** is an online gaming and user-generated content platform, publicly traded since its 2021 direct listing, and one of the most popular entertainment platforms in the world for young children, who both view and play games and videos created by other users on the platform.[1]

## Hindenburg Research report (October 2024)

On October 8, 2024, short seller [[hindenburg-research|Hindenburg Research]] published a report accusing the company both of inflating key business metrics and of operating what it called an in-game "pedophile hellscape."[1] Hindenburg said its research indicated "that Roblox is lying to investors, regulators, and advertisers about the number of 'people' on its platform, inflating the metric by 25-42%+," and that engagement hours, another key metric, were "inflated by an estimated 100%+."[1] The firm noted that because Roblox is not profitable, its stock price — and in turn insiders' ability to sell hundreds of millions of dollars of stock — depends on the growth metrics presented to Wall Street.[1]

To test reported engagement, Hindenburg said it hired a technical consultant who monitored roughly the top 7,200 Roblox games across about 2.1 million servers, collecting 297.7 million rows of real-time player data.[1]

On child safety, the report described the in-game experience as "an X-rated pedophile hellscape, exposing children to grooming, pornography, violent content and extremely abusive speech."[1] As a test of whether the platform had cleaned up after years of scandals, Hindenburg attempted to register an account under the name "Jeffrey Epstein," only to find the name already taken along with more than 900 variations.[1] Roblox shares fell sharply following publication.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Burning Bright argued that the child-exploitation allegations, not the share-price move, were the real signal, contending the platform appears "designed and curated specifically to perpetuate" child predation rather than merely failing to curtail it.[1] He observed that mainstream coverage discussed only the stock decline and closed with the phrase "Tick tock."[1]
