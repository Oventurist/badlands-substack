# RedBird Capital Partners

RedBird Capital Partners is a United States–based private investment firm that in May 2025 led a transatlantic consortium agreeing to acquire the British newspaper [[the-telegraph|The Telegraph]] for £500 million (roughly $675 million).[1]

## Telegraph transaction

The Telegraph, founded in 1855, had previously been purchased by RedBird IMI — a RedBird Capital Partners subsidiary partnered with the United Arab Emirates' [[international-media-investments|International Media Investments]] and headed by former CNN president [[jeff-zucker|Jeff Zucker]].[1] That 2023 deal collapsed after the Conservative government passed legislation restricting foreign state ownership of British media, given IMI's ownership by Emirati royal and UAE vice president [[mansour-bin-zayed-al-nahyan|Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed Al Nahyan]].[1]

Under the 2025 arrangement, IMI would retain only a minority stake once RedBird IMI completed the sale to RedBird Capital Partners, conditional on the [[labour-party|Labour]] government enacting pending changes permitting foreign state-owned investors to hold up to 15 percent of British newspaper publishers.[1] Zucker's continuing role as an operating partner at RedBird Capital Partners meant his influence over the title persisted through the change of ownership vehicle.[1]
