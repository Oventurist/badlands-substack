# Lebanon

Lebanon is a Middle Eastern state whose long-frozen relationship with [[israel|Israel]] became the subject of direct negotiations in April 2026, when President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that the two countries had agreed to a ten-day ceasefire.[1]

## The 2026 ceasefire

Trump said the truce was to begin at 5 p.m. EST on a Tuesday and followed "excellent conversations" with Lebanese President [[joseph-aoun|Joseph Aoun]] and Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1] The agreement came after a meeting in Washington, D.C. at which Israeli and Lebanese officials sat down together for the first time in thirty-four years, alongside Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]].[1] Trump described the encounter as a rare step toward direct engagement between the two countries and said he had tasked Rubio, Vice President [[jd-vance|JD Vance]], and Chairman of the Joint Chiefs [[dan-caine|Dan Caine]] with converting the pause into a durable peace.[1]

## Hezbollah and the limits of the truce

Lebanon's internal politics complicate any settlement because of the position of [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], the armed movement that Israel treats as its principal adversary on the northern front. Shortly after Trump's announcement, Netanyahu released a recorded statement setting out two conditions for peace directed at Hezbollah: that it lay down its weapons and surrender.[1] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that these were terms Netanyahu knew Hezbollah would never accept, implying that the Israeli premier expected the war to continue even as Trump pivoted toward peace.[1]

## Ceasefire extension and war-crimes accusations

Later in April 2026 Trump announced a three-week extension of the Israel–Hezbollah ceasefire following a productive Oval Office meeting with Israeli and Lebanese representatives.[2] He said the United States would work with Lebanon to protect itself from Hezbollah and expressed plans to host Netanyahu and President Aoun.[2] Israeli media presented the two sides as a united front seeking to disarm and dismantle Hezbollah, which is a more significant fighting force than the official Lebanese military.[2]

The Lebanese Prime Minister subsequently accused Israel of war crimes, citing a pattern of [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] attacks on Lebanese journalists, including the bombing of their homes — conduct that had drawn the ire of the Lebanese government only days after its historic White House meeting with Israel.[2] Israel also formally acknowledged an "Advanced Defense Zone" on the Lebanese side of the border where the IDF says nobody is allowed and warns that violators will be shot.[2] Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] separately called for Israel's borders to be expanded to include parts of Lebanon and [[syria|Syria]].[2] Ambassador [[tom-barrack|Tom Barrack]] argued that Hezbollah cannot be annihilated militarily because of its decades-deep integration into Lebanese society.[2]

## Christian villages and the Litani escalation (March 2026)

In March 2026 The Telegraph removed an article reporting alleged cooperation between Christian villages in southern Lebanon and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], published March 23 under the headline "Christians and Hezbollah unite against 'Epstein empire'" and taken down about a day later without explanation.[3] Reporting from Christianity Today described those communities as geographically embedded in Hezbollah-influenced territory and exposed to Israeli military operations, with some residents denying affiliation with the group.[3]

Badlands commentary described a southern Lebanon under Hezbollah control since the 1980s that nonetheless contains entire Catholic villages, marked by monumental crosses: the 240-foot Cross of All Nations at Qanat Bakish, crosses at Rmeim and Kobayat (the latter over 120 feet), the world's largest inhabitable cross at Ijdabra with a 200-person church inside, and one of the world's largest statues of Jesus completed near Al-Qaa.[3]

Israel's posture toward the country sharply escalated the same month, with Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] declaring on March 23 that Israel's border "must be the [[litani-river|Litani]]" and Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] announcing the next day that Israeli forces would occupy territory up to the river and control bridges and a security zone.[3] Lebanese authorities reported more than 1,070 deaths and over one million displaced after fighting intensified on March 2, and the United Nations called the prospect of a sustained Israeli presence "very much concerning."[3]

## Trump's July 2026 pledge

President [[donald-trump|Trump]] met Lebanese President [[joseph-aoun|Joseph Aoun]] at the [[white-house|White House]] in July 2026 and pledged to help Lebanon "a lot," describing the country as having been "very badly treated" and saying it would now receive the respect it deserves.[4] The talks focused on the June 26 U.S.-mediated framework agreement with [[israel|Israel]], which provides for Israeli withdrawal from southern Lebanon, the disarmament of [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], and steps toward a formal peace agreement; Trump said Israeli forces were "in the process" of withdrawing and redeploying but offered no details.[4]

Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] reiterated support for full implementation, arguing that Lebanon cannot achieve lasting peace while Hezbollah remains a powerful armed force and that a strong central government must become the sole legitimate military authority.[4] Badlands commentary described Aoun as widely panned as a Saudi loyalist backed by the Turks and their antagonists, and framed the meeting as a prelude to a Trump-brokered deal with Hezbollah modeled on the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]].[4]

## Annexation proposal and Litani bridge strikes (March 2026)

In March 2026 Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] called for [[israel|Israel]] to annex parts of southern Lebanon, saying the territory should be permanently held as a security measure amid ongoing fighting with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[5] The Israeli government had not formally adopted annexation as policy, and observers expected international opposition on the grounds that annexation of territory by force is widely considered illegal under international law.[5]

The proposal would extend Israel's northern border to the [[litani-river|Litani River]], and the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] bombed seven bridges crossing the river, cutting off access from Beirut and central Lebanon.[5] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the resulting Lebanese reaction as one of the most consequential news narratives since the War of Terror psyop began, arguing that what the adversary cannot afford is Christians and Muslims uniting against a common threat.[5] See [[southern-lebanon-annexation-proposal|southern Lebanon annexation proposal]].[5]

## April 2026 escalation under the ceasefire

In April 2026, [[israel|Israel]] ordered its troops to use "full force" against threats in Lebanon despite a temporary truce, signaling that operations along the border would continue under the existing ceasefire terms.[6] Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] said homes allegedly used by [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] would be leveled, and Arab News reported demolitions were underway that Sunday, citing Israeli state media.[6]

The Israeli military published a map showing a new deployment line placing dozens of southern Lebanese villages inside an Israeli-controlled [[forward-defense-zone|Forward Defense Zone]] extending some 5 to 10 kilometers from the border, with five divisions and naval forces operating there.[6] Badlands analysis described a northeastward push toward [[mount-hermon|Mount Hermon]] and a northward push toward Tyre intended to enable annexation of southern Lebanon and seizure of the [[qana-gas-field|Qana Gas Field]].[6]

The escalation followed [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s Truth Social statement that "Israel will not be bombing Lebanon any longer. They are PROHIBITED from doing so by the U.S.A. Enough is enough!" — remarks that, per Axios reporter Barak Ravid, surprised [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] and his advisers and prompted Israel's ambassador to the US to scramble for clarification.[6] In the Christian village of Debl, an Israeli soldier smashed a statue of Jesus with a sledgehammer, and Lebanese Christians were warned by Netanyahu's government not to return to their villages despite the ceasefire.[6]

## The Beirut strike and the June 2026 escalation

An Israeli strike on Beirut, Lebanon's capital, was the stated impetus for [[iran|Iran]]'s June 8, 2026 ballistic missile barrage against [[israel|Israel]].[7] Although many speculated that the United States had a hand in the Beirut operation, [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]] reported that two separate [[white-house|White House]] sources confirmed Washington played no part and gave no blessing for it — and that the strike was what provoked President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] into chewing out [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] the previous week.[7]

Lebanon also featured in the reported [[defense-intelligence-agency|Defense Intelligence Agency]] assessment of Israeli espionage, which allegedly identified Israeli efforts to gather information on U.S. administration deliberations concerning Iran and Lebanon.[7]

## The Nabatieh offensive (June 2026)

On June 18–19, 2026, Reuters reported that Israel was in talks with the United States about maintaining its troop deployment in southern Lebanon beyond previously anticipated timelines, and that Israeli officials had advanced plans for border demarcation and security arrangements in the area despite ongoing diplomacy.[8] Hours after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted comments on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] concerning the matter, reports emerged of a major [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] operation concentrated around the southern Lebanese city of Nabatieh, involving armored columns and armored bulldozers and heavy close-quarters combat, in which the IDF was said to have lost at least four Merkava tanks.[8]

Israeli journalist [[barak-ravid|Barak Ravid]] affirmed reports that Israel had likely invaded Lebanon in an attempt to seize control of the strait, and The Times of Israel reported that the IDF had confirmed conducting an overnight operation in southern Lebanon; Israeli media subsequently confirmed at least four IDF soldiers killed, including a senior officer.[8] The IDF published a map of the security zone it intended to establish — the first such map, according to Badlands, to include areas north of the [[litani-river|Litani River]], with the Nabatieh operation itself suggesting the map understated the full extent of the territory Israel intended to claim.[8]

The overnight strikes on [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] targets, which Lebanese reports said killed sixteen people, also disrupted diplomacy: formal US–Iran signing ceremonies scheduled at Bürgenstock in Switzerland were canceled, though Washington and Tehran proceeded with implementation of the [[us-iran-memorandum-of-understanding|memorandum of understanding]] anyway.[8]

## June 2026: Beirut strike cancelled amid Trump–Netanyahu rupture
Lebanon was the flashpoint of a reported June 1, 2026 confrontation in which President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] rebuked Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] for escalating the conflict, and intervened to stop a planned Israeli strike on Beirut.[9] Israeli officials confirmed the Beirut operation was shelved, while Netanyahu said [[israel|Israel]] would continue responding to [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] attacks and keep operations underway in southern Lebanon.[9] Trump subsequently said Israeli troops would not enter Beirut and that forces already en route had been turned back, and that Hezbollah had agreed through intermediaries to halt all shooting.[9] [[iran|Iran]] cited Israeli operations in Lebanon and Gaza as grounds for suspending indirect talks with Washington, demanding a complete Israeli withdrawal from areas of southern Lebanon as a condition of resuming negotiations.[9]


## April 2026 Israeli offensive and European backlash

Israeli operations in April 2026 caused extensive destruction in parts of southern Lebanon, with reports that entire villages had been leveled in areas [[israel|Israel]] said contained [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] infrastructure, driving rising casualties and displacement.[10] European governments condemned the strikes and urged de-escalation, warning of worsening humanitarian conditions as the conflict expanded alongside broader regional tensions involving [[iran|Iran]].[10] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] rejected ceasefire calls and, despite pressure from the United States and European allies, maintained that the Lebanon front remained active, leaving the situation volatile with no immediate resolution in sight.[10]

## 2026 talks with Israel

In April 2026 Lebanon participated in the first high-level talks with Israel since 1993, hosted by Secretary of State Marco Rubio at the State Department, with Lebanese Ambassador Nada Hamadeh Moawad representing Beirut.[11] Lebanon urged full implementation of the November 2024 ceasefire understanding and resolution of the humanitarian situation, while agreeing to proceed toward direct negotiations.[11] The talks followed Israeli operations in Lebanon that caused hundreds of deaths and thousands of injuries and prompted Italy to suspend its defence memorandum with Israel after Israeli forces fired warning shots at Italian UNIFIL troops in the south.[11] Pakistan demanded at the UN Security Council that Israel withdraw completely from Lebanon.[11]


## June 2026 escalation and peace-deal claim

Escalating Israeli attacks on Lebanon formed the backdrop to a June 2026 incident in which Iranian forces reportedly shot down a U.S. Apache helicopter, with no casualties.[12] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the United States had mediated a halt to [[iran|Iran]]–[[israel|Israel]] hostilities and that a broader Middle East deal was in its "final throes," but Israeli operations against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] in southern Lebanon and the Beirut suburbs continued regardless.[12] Iran insisted that any comprehensive agreement must address the Lebanon theater, with [[pakistan|Pakistan]] acting as a mediator.[12]

## Israeli Advance and Turkish Reaction (June 2026)

By June 2026 Israeli strikes on [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]]-linked targets in Lebanon had become a central irritant in relations between [[israel|Israel]] and [[turkey|Turkey]], with President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] portraying the operations as destabilizing and warning they could eventually threaten Turkey.[13] Israeli leaders defended the strikes as necessary to prevent attacks by Iranian-backed groups.[13] Badlands commentary described the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] as moving north through Lebanon and taking [[beirut|Beirut]], bringing Israeli forces closer to Turkey's practical sphere of influence in [[syria|Syria]], and argued that Erdoğan would need allies in [[pakistan|Pakistan]], Egypt and [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] to contain the IDF in Lebanon.[13] Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said in the same period that "even with a ceasefire, we will continue to attack both in Iran and Lebanon."[13]

## The 2024 escalation and Operation Northern Arrows

Lebanon's confrontation with Israel escalated sharply in September 2024, when the Israeli leadership announced on September 23 that the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] had launched [[operation-northern-arrows|Operation Northern Arrows]], an offensive aimed at Hezbollah's military infrastructure, with massive Israeli air strikes across dozens of settlements in the south and east of the country.[14] Lebanese Foreign Minister [[abdallah-bou-habib|Abdallah Bou Habib]] estimated that half a million people had been displaced within Lebanon and said the Lebanese prime minister hoped to meet US officials within two days.[14]

Bou Habib criticized US President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s address to the [[united-nations|United Nations]] General Assembly as "not strong" and "not promising," while maintaining that the United States remained the only country that could make a real difference in the region.[14] The [[russian-foreign-ministry|Russian Foreign Ministry]], through spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]], strongly condemned the Israeli attacks and called for an immediate cessation of hostilities.[14] The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] announced it was moving a small number of additional US personnel into the Middle East in response to the rising violence.[14]

## Threatened Israeli ground invasion (September 2024)

In late September 2024 the prospect of an Israeli ground invasion of southern Lebanon dominated regional coverage.[15] The [[pentagon|Pentagon]] stated that the US military was not providing intelligence for Israel's operations against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], said the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration was making a "full-court" press for a diplomatic solution, and assessed that an invasion did not look imminent, even as the head of Israel's military and Israeli media warned that a ground operation could begin at any moment.[15] Lebanese news site LBCI reported that urgent US-led ceasefire talks had produced "significant progress" and reached "a serious and advanced stage," expressing hope for a breakthrough announcement the following morning.[15]

Israel had been teasing the prospect of invading Lebanon since at least June, and Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] publicly warned Lebanese citizens to leave southern Lebanon immediately, assuring them they could return once the conflict was resolved — a tactic Badlands commentary compared to the IDF's approach in 1967 in the West Bank and more recently in Gaza.[15] Former CIA and Army intelligence officer [[phil-giraldi|Phil Giraldi]] suggested the preceding week's pager attack may have been intended to coincide with the invasion's onset, with the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] detonating the devices early out of concern that Hezbollah had detected the scheme.[15] Rabbi [[yitzchak-ginsburgh|Yitzchak Ginsburgh]] of the [[chabad|Chabad]] movement published a letter calling for the conquest, expulsion of the hostile population and Jewish settlement of southern Lebanon as part of the [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] project.[15]

## June 2024 war scare

In June 2024 Lebanon was at the center of a war scare between [[israel|Israel]] and [[iran|Iran]].[16] The Israel–Lebanon border had seen daily exchanges of fire between Israeli forces and [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] since the Gaza conflict began on October 7, and fears of a full-blown war intensified after Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] said Israel was preparing for "a very tense operation" on the border.[16] Iran's UN mission warned that "full-scale military aggression" in Lebanon would lead to an "obliterating war" with all Resistance Fronts involved, while Israeli Foreign Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] demanded Hezbollah cease fire and withdraw from southern Lebanon so displaced residents could return home.[16] Israeli forces struck a series of Hezbollah targets in southern Lebanon early on Saturday, June 29, 2024.[16]

## Prospect of an Israeli ground operation (September 2024)

In September 2024, Israeli forces conducted preemptive strikes on targets deep inside Lebanon amid concern over the response of [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], with northern Israel on high alert.[17] Former Commander of the IDF General Staff Corps, Maj.-Gen. (Res.) [[gershon-hacohen|Gershon Hacohen]], said that "it is entirely possible that the IDF will need to enter Lebanon on the ground," insisting there was "no choice" but to return evacuated northern residents to their homes.[17] Hacohen characterized Washington as seeking de-escalation out of fear that the conflict could destabilize the region and draw in Iranian intervention.[17] Badlands Media linked the prospective invasion to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s declaration of war against Hezbollah and to friction with [[centcom|CENTCOM]] commander [[michael-kurilla|Michael Kurilla]].[17]

## In "Trump Cuts the Head off the Snake"

Joe Lange describes Lebanon as controlled by [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]], which is in turn controlled by [[iran|Iran]].[18] He treats the abrupt resignation of Prime Minister [[saad-hariri|Saad al-Hariri]] — announced in a televised broadcast from [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]] the day before the [[saudi-purge|November 2017 mass arrests]] — as a major and largely forgotten event.[18] Hariri accused Iran of sowing "fear and destruction" in several countries including Lebanon and said he feared for his life; correspondents wrote that his departure plunged Lebanon into a new political crisis and raised fears it would sit at the forefront of the rivalry between Shia power Iran and Sunni stronghold Saudi Arabia.[18]

The resignation angered Hezbollah-backed President [[michel-aoun|Michel Aoun]], and Saudi Arabia was accused of holding Hariri hostage; after about a week and a half he returned home and eventually rescinded the resignation.[18] Lange reads the episode as Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] serving notice on Hezbollah that it would no longer have free rein in the region.[18] The [[quds-force|Quds Force]] under [[qasem-soleimani|Qasem Soleimani]] funded Hezbollah in Lebanon as part of the network Lange says was decapitated by the [[assassination-of-qasem-soleimani|January 2020 strike]].[18]
