# Olivia Chow

Olivia Chow is the mayor of Toronto, Ontario, whose administration drew international attention in March 2024 for contemplating a levy on residents based on the amount of rainwater falling on their properties.[1]

## Proposed stormwater levy

Under Chow, the city advanced a proposal formally described as a wastewater usage tax, assessed according to the volume of rainwater generated from residents' hard surfaces such as driveways and roofs.[1] Properties with more hard surface area would incur higher taxes on the theory that such surfaces contribute runoff that can overwhelm the municipal sewer system during storms.[1] The measure remained in a feedback phase of consideration when it was reported.[1] Beyond widespread mockery, critics questioned the feasibility of accurately measuring and enforcing taxes tied to individual property characteristics.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] argued that while conservative coverage framed the proposal as a communist move — "only a few steps shy of the state saying it owns rainwater while saying it now 'belongs to the people'" — the more troubling element was the city's use of the policy rollout to advertise the quality of services provided by its Toronto Water utility.[1] Esler pointed to reporting that Toronto drinking water contains high concentrations of painkillers, blood thinners, and chemotherapy drugs, questioning why residents should admire the city's stewardship of wastewater and stormwater given that record.[1]

Esler further noted that the city's justification leaned on billion-dollar waterway projects as a pretext for the tax, and that private landowners would be charged using drone footage obtained by the city in combination with the property's water usage — a mechanism he characterized as dystopian.[1] He observed that similar rain-based taxation schemes already operate in other Canadian cities as well as Philadelphia and Seattle, and warned that such programs function as latent instruments for climate-change narratives that raise private utility bills according to the weather.[1]
