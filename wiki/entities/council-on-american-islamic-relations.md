# Council on American-Islamic Relations

The Council on American-Islamic Relations (CAIR) is an American Muslim advocacy organization that functions as a legal-defense and public-relations body for Muslim communities in the United States. It drew renewed attention in January 2025 when a Houston-area mosque instructed its congregation to route [[fbi|FBI]] inquiries through CAIR in the aftermath of the New Orleans New Year's Day attack.[1]

## Role after the New Orleans attack

Masjid Bilal, a mosque near the Houston home of [[shamsud-din-jabbar|Shamsud-Din Jabbar]], posted an Instagram story after Jabbar was publicly identified condemning the "terrible acts" while urging members not to respond to media inquiries and to defer to CAIR and the [[islamic-society-of-greater-houston|Islamic Society of Greater Houston]] if approached by the FBI.[1] Breitbart, reporting on the guidance, described CAIR as "a radical organization that has provided assistance to terrorists' families in the past," citing the group's local branch assisting the families of the ISIS-affiliated attackers who carried out the 2015 mass shooting against San Bernardino county employees at a Christmas holiday party.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer GhostofBasedPatrickHenry treated the episode sardonically — "an NGO that is being accused of being a terrorist organization" — and folded it into a broader argument that the coverage of the New Orleans attack was engineered to build public appetite for renewed Middle East military action, in his framing most likely driven by the [[cia|CIA]] and [[mossad|Mossad]].[1]
