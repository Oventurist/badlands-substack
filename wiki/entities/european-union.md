# European Union

The European Union is the supranational political and economic union of European states that, in January 2026, emerged as the principal institutional counterparty to U.S. tariff pressure over [[greenland|Greenland]].[1]

## Response to the Greenland tariffs

After [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced a 10% tariff on imports from eight European countries opposed to U.S. designs on Greenland, EU officials signaled readiness to coordinate trade counter-measures and to consider suspending parts of EU–U.S. trade agreements should Washington proceed with additional tariffs, citing legal and economic concerns.[1] Press coverage described the prospective response as a "trade bazooka."[1] European leaders warned collectively that linking trade policy to territorial ambitions undermines transatlantic relations and risks a "dangerous downward spiral," while affirming the sovereignty of Greenland and [[denmark|Denmark]].[1]

## Badlands framing

In the Badlands reading, the EU and allied European governments are "the architects and beneficiaries of the old order" that Trump is dismantling, now on the receiving end of a doctrine of raw leverage long practiced by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].[1] Behind closed doors the UK, Germany and France were said to be assembling a counter-proposal — a multinational [[nato|NATO]] Arctic force — to appease Washington.[1]

## Hungary's migration continuity under Magyar (2026)

[[peter-magyar|Péter Magyar]], set to replace [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] after a landslide 2026 election victory, signaled that [[hungary|Hungary]] would maintain a strict anti-immigration stance, keeping border barriers and rejecting the EU's migration pact despite campaigning on restoring rule of law and reengaging with the Union.[2] Under Orbán, Hungary's posture had centered on hard border controls, opposition to asylum quotas, and framing migration as a civilizational and security issue — positions that reshaped Hungary's relationship with Brussels.[2] Analysts described the transition as hybrid: Hungary moving away from Orbán's centralized political system while retaining core nationalist border policies, signaling that Europe may see continuity on migration pressure points even amid broader political change in Budapest.[2]

## Confrontation with Washington (January 2026)

EU foreign policy chief [[kaja-kallas|Kaja Kallas]] said the bloc had discussed how it might respond if the United States advanced efforts to acquire [[greenland|Greenland]], describing US messaging as "extremely concerning."[3] An EU Commission spokesperson reportedly said the bloc does not recognize the legitimacy of Venezuela's interim president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] but would maintain "targeted engagement" with Venezuelan authorities.[3]

These positions accompanied warnings from German President [[frank-walter-steinmeier|Frank-Walter Steinmeier]] and French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] that US policy was eroding the postwar global order.[3] Badlands argued that the EU's readiness to treat the United States as an adversary over Greenland could produce a diplomatic divorce between the two continents and thereby end the ambitions of Agenda 2030.[3]

## Resistance to the 2026 Russia sanctions package

In July 2026 the EU encountered growing internal resistance as it tried to finalize its latest sanctions package against [[russia|Russia]], with several member states objecting to measures they said would disproportionately harm their own economies.[4] Because EU sanctions require unanimous approval, objections from a handful of governments stalled negotiations and exposed divisions within the bloc.[4] Greece opposed restrictions affecting its shipping industry; France and Italy objected to travel restrictions on former Russian combatants; Germany and Portugal resisted measures targeting Russian fish imports.[4] The broader sanctions regime had recently been extended through July 2027.[4] Analysts said the episode highlighted the growing difficulty of maintaining consensus as national economic interests diverge, with the newest proposals meeting resistance from multiple member states rather than a single holdout.[4] Badlands commentary framed the impasse as evidence that President [[donald-trump|Trump]] is letting globalist institutions pursue self-destructive goals on a condensed timeline.[4]

## May 2026 cascade: US drawdown and the Romanian collapse

EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas, speaking at a summit in Yerevan, Armenia, said the timing of the U.S. troop withdrawal from Germany came as a surprise despite prior discussions, and called for a stronger European pillar in NATO.[5] In the same period the pro-EU minority government of Romania collapsed in a no-confidence vote, with Badlands commentary attributing the fall to Brussels-demanded austerity clashing with domestic politics and noting record lows in the national currency.[5] Burning Bright framed these as cascading stress fractures in a structure whose load-bearing beams were supplied by the United States.[5]


## US energy pressure over trade-deal ratification (2026)

In March 2026 the United States pressured the EU to ratify the major trade deal signed the previous summer, warning that failure to do so could cost Brussels its "favorable" access to American energy supplies.[6] US Ambassador to the EU [[andrew-puzder|Andrew Puzder]] told the Financial Times that Washington would continue doing business with the bloc but that the terms "may not be as favorable" absent ratification, emphasizing that the EU "needs energy" to survive economically.[6] The [[european-parliament|European Parliament]] was scheduled to vote on ratification that week.[6]

Under the deal, the EU accepted a baseline 15% tariff on all its goods entering the US market while eliminating its own tariffs on US industrial goods and certain agricultural products, and committed to purchasing $750 billion worth of American energy by 2028.[6] The warning landed as the bloc had become heavily dependent on US liquefied natural gas after sharply reducing Russian imports — Germany sourced 96% of its LNG imports from the United States in 2025 — while the [[european-commission|European Commission]] maintained its plan to fully phase out Russian fossil fuels by 2027 even as global energy prices rose amid the Middle East conflict.[6] Badlands commentary argued that Europe's decoupling from cheap pipeline-delivered Russian gas had handed [[donald-trump|President Trump]] decisive leverage, and predicted he would use it again when negotiating peace between Ukraine and Russia.[6]

## Slovak calls for an EU peacemaker role (2023)

In October 2023 Slovak Prime Minister [[robert-fico|Robert Fico]] called on the European Union to take on a peacekeeping role in the [[ukraine|Ukraine]] war, telling his country's legislature that "the EU should change from an arms supplier to a peacemaker."[7] Having halted Slovak arms transfers to Kiev, Fico urged the rest of Europe to adopt Bratislava's policy and said he would not vote for further sanctions against Russia without analyses of their impact on [[slovakia|Slovakia]].[7]


## Israel sanctions debate and Russian-oil tariffs (September 2025)

In September 2025 Irish Deputy Prime Minister [[simon-harris|Simon Harris]] canvassed EU foreign ministers to co-sign a letter to foreign policy chief [[kaja-kallas|Kaja Kallas]] urging a swift decision on sanctions against [[israel|Israel]] over the Gaza conflict.[8] European Commission president [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]] had called the situation in Gaza "unacceptable," pledged to put bilateral support to Israel on hold and proposed partially freezing the EU–Israel free trade agreement.[8] [[ireland|Ireland]], Spain, Sweden, Denmark and the Netherlands called for suspension of the deal, while Germany, Hungary and the Czech Republic opposed sanctioning Israel.[8]

Separately, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] urged the EU to impose tariffs of up to 100% on goods from Beijing and New Delhi as part of a joint effort to pressure Moscow over Russian oil purchases.[8] The bloc was also cited as a model in Trump's proposal to end mandatory [[quarterly-earnings-reporting|quarterly earnings reports]], since EU companies must report twice a year but may report quarterly.[8]

## Sanctions and the collapse of the DR Congo talks (March 2025)

In March 2025 the [[alliance-fleuve-congo|Alliance Fleuve Congo]] rebel coalition blamed the EU for its last-minute withdrawal from peace talks due to be held in Angola.[9] Brussels had announced sanctions against five senior rebel leaders, including AFC president Bertrand Bisimwa, for "serious human rights violations and abuses," with further measures targeting [[m23|M23]] and Rwandan officials.[9] The coalition said the sanctions, enacted on the eve of the Luanda discussions, "severely undermine direct dialogue and make any progress impossible," and accused the EU of deliberately obstructing the talks.[9] [[rwanda|Rwanda]] simultaneously severed diplomatic ties with Belgium, accusing Brussels of seeking to "sustain its neo-colonial delusions."[9]

## Alarm over the Trump–Putin call

German tabloid Bild reported that EU leaders were "trembling" ahead of the March 18, 2025 telephone call between [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], which was expected to address "the future of Ukraine and possibly also the future of Europe, with potentially devastating consequences for the security of our continent," producing "a mood of alarm in Europe's capitals."[9] A European Council meeting in Brussels that week was expected to discuss the Ukraine conflict, including the Franco-British proposal to deploy peacekeepers that [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] rejected.[9]

## Debate over Russian assets and Kremlin criticism (October 2025)

EU leaders convened in Copenhagen on October 1, 2025 for an informal summit to discuss providing Ukraine with a €140 billion ($164 billion) loan backed by Russia's immobilized central bank assets, though some member states continued to voice concerns about the legal risks of such a step.[10] Belgium's Prime Minister [[bart-de-wever|Bart De Wever]], whose country hosts [[euroclear|Euroclear]] where most EU-held Russian assets sit, cautioned that "there's no free money. There are always consequences," and said, "I want their signature saying, if we take [Russian President Vladimir] Putin's money… we're all going to be responsible if it goes wrong."[10] Luxembourg's prime minister, Luc Frieden, voiced similar apprehensions.[10]

[[kremlin|Kremlin]] spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] said EU countries debating ways they could "steal" Russian assets were behaving like a criminal "gang," likening the bloc to a group of mobsters planning their next heist.[10] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry seized on the phrasing, arguing it was "almost like a mafia controls all of western civilization, enslaving and suppressing us with usury and tribalism."[10]

The EU has also long designated [[hamas|Hamas]] a foreign terrorist organization, a designation cited when [[germany|Germany]] arrested three suspected Hamas members in October 2025.[10]

## Competitiveness crisis and defense militarization (October 2024)

At the Berlin Global Dialogue on October 2, 2024, French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] warned that the EU economy needed to become more competitive with the United States and China or it "could die," saying the 27-nation bloc was falling behind Washington and Beijing in economic output and investment and faced serious problems if it failed to deepen its single market and resolve "fragmentation."[11] "Our former model is over – we are over-regulating and under-investing," Macron said, adding that without urgent regulatory reform the bloc would be out of the market within two to three years and forced into a rescue plan within five to ten.[11]

In the same week, newly appointed EU Commissioner for Defense and Space [[andrius-kubilius|Andrius Kubilius]] — a former Lithuanian prime minister and MEP known for a hardline stance toward Moscow — said the EU and Russia could face one another on the battlefield in less than a decade and that the bloc must "prepare for war."[11] Speaking as his European People's Party group presented its strategic paper on defense, Kubilius called for a "European Defense Union," investment in "defense technology of the future," and readiness "to meet Russia militarily in six to eight years," arguing the EU must complement [[nato|NATO]] by boosting defense production, stocking reserves and continuing to help Ukraine.[11]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the bloc as "a menagerie of wannabe aristocrats, desperately trying to LARP the 1815 Treaty of Vienna," comparing modern EU leadership to the restored monarchies that blacklisted free-thinkers across European universities and newspapers, and likening its dysfunction to the inbreeding of the [[habsburg-dynasty|Habsburg dynasty]].[11] He concluded that the European Union had "proven itself to be an even more egregious disaster than the Habsburgs' genetic sequencing," and closed by invoking [[nigel-farage|Nigel Farage]]'s critique of the bloc.[11]

## Disinformation enforcement (2023)

In late May 2023 [[twitter|Twitter]] withdrew from the EU's voluntary [[eu-code-of-practice-on-disinformation|Code of Practice on Disinformation]], prompting Internal Market Commissioner [[thierry-breton|Thierry Breton]] to warn that anti-disinformation obligations would become legally binding under the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]] from August 25, 2023 and that EU teams would be ready for enforcement.[12] Badlands commentary framed the bloc's rapid threat of consequences as evidence that it feared losing control of European public opinion and characterized the EU as a tyrant that citizens and [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] alike were prepared to resist.[12]

## Trump's 25% tariff threat (February 2025)

At his first Cabinet meeting of his second term on February 26, 2025, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] said the European Union "was formed in order to screw the United States" and threatened blanket 25% tariffs on all imports from the bloc, "on cars and all other things," describing the EU as having taken advantage of the US "in a different way" from Canada.[13] The threat echoed his first-term trade dispute with the bloc, when 25% tariffs on European steel and 10% on aluminum drew EU countermeasures.[13]

Badlands commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry used the occasion to advance the claim that the EU's founding documents were plagiarized from a 1940 memorandum, H.S. 70, "Economic Reorganisation of Europe," written by Reichsbank president [[walther-funk|Walther Funk]], which envisioned a continent-wide economic zone and a single currency capable of subjugating individual nation-states.[13] Citing a 2020 article by Adam LeBor calling Funk a prophet of European integration, he argued the EU is "really just the Fourth Reich with different branding."[13]

## Tariff war strains and the Meloni bilateral question (April 2025)

The reciprocal tariffs announced by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in April 2025, including a 20 percent rate on the EU, exposed divisions within the bloc.[14] Italian Prime Minister [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]]'s planned solo visit to Washington — making her the first European leader to meet Trump after the announcement — reportedly sent the continent "into a tizzy," amplifying fears of a disjointed European front on trade.[14]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that a bilateral Italy–US trade agreement concluded without the EU's consent would mark the end of the Union, which since its creation in 1993 has posited that its collective economic might is what leverages Europe as a premier world power.[14] He characterized the EU as having been created to subvert the sovereignty of the individual nation-state — a claim he attributed to British MP [[nigel-farage|Nigel Farage]]'s confrontation with then President of the European Council Herman Van Rompuy — and held that bilateral trade agreements are the future, with "no more transnational trade regulatory bodies."[14] He also observed that no individual member state appeared interested in war with [[russia|Russia]]; only the collective body of the EU pressed the case, and the momentum of that debate had the potential to shatter the alliance.[14] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] was more dismissive, writing that the EU's attempts to appear relevant amounted to showing their bellies as Trump and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] "weave a masterclass."[14]

## European Democracy Shield (November 2025)

In November 2025, the European Union launched the [[european-democracy-shield|European Democracy Shield]], an initiative to regulate speech and combat disinformation.[15] European Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]] said the initiative "protects democracy and civil society," while critics argued it limits freedom under the guise of battling misinformation.[15] The Shield promotes a Monitoring Centre to address "false content" and an EU-funded network of fact-checkers, an architecture critics said would stifle dissent and perpetuate the bloc's own narratives.[15] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that the EU has no First Amendment, called the bloc a failed globalist experiment that should be dissolved, and urged member-state exits such as "Frexit" and "Gerexit."[15]

## Trump request for 100% tariffs on India and China (2025)

In September 2025, reports that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had asked the European Union to impose tariffs of up to 100% on China and India over their purchases of Russian oil raised eyebrows on both sides of the Atlantic, with Europe seen as unlikely to acquiesce to the White House's request.[16] Trump made the proposal — first reported by the Financial Times and confirmed to CNBC by two sources familiar with the matter — when he was called into a meeting with senior US and EU officials in Washington; the US was also prepared to "mirror" any tariffs Europe imposed on the two countries.[16] A European Commission spokesperson declined to disclose meeting details, citing confidentiality, but noted that "The EU has engaged with all relevant global partners, including India and China, in the context of its sanctions enforcement efforts. This engagement will continue."[16] Asking the EU to tariff key Russian energy clients was seen as another way to punish their trade with Moscow and pressure Russia to end the war in Ukraine.[16] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the maneuver as part of a macro theme of provocation by collectivists "lashing out with desperate bids" for chaos as the noose of sovereignty tightened around their unipolar hegemony.[16]


## Borrell's complicity charge over Gaza, 2025

In August 2025 the bloc's former top diplomat [[josep-borrell|Josep Borrell]] said the European Union could be considered complicit in Israeli war crimes in [[gaza|Gaza]] because it fails to act "in the face of a clear case of massive human rights violations."[17] He argued Brussels is reluctant to punish [[israel|Israel]] even though its transgressions surpass other conduct the bloc has condemned, and claimed that a third of Israeli bombs falling on Gaza "are made in Europe."[17]

## Suspension of counter-tariffs, August 2025

The European Union announced on Monday, 4 August 2025 that it would suspend its planned countermeasures against U.S. tariffs for six months, following the trade deal struck between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and European Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]] on 27 July.[17] The tariffs had been set to take effect that week and were put on hold while both sides worked to finalize a joint statement formalizing the agreement; a Commission spokesperson said the deal aims to restore "stability and predictability for citizens and businesses on both sides of the Atlantic," with the suspension expected in place by Tuesday.[17]

The delay marked a notable concession from one of the United States' largest trading partners after weeks of negotiations.[17] The broad trade agreement announced the previous month includes 15% tariffs on most European imports to the U.S., including automobiles; in exchange the White House said the EU agreed to eliminate all tariffs on U.S. industrial exports to Europe, commit to $750 billion in U.S. energy purchases, and increase investment in the United States by $600 billion beyond existing levels.[17]


## 2025 tariff exposure and enforcement against X

The [[liberation-day-tariffs|"Liberation Day" tariffs]] announced by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on April 2, 2025 placed the European Union among the trading partners hit with additional duties on top of the new 10 percent US baseline levy, alongside Japan, Vietnam, and Cambodia.[18] European coverage of the move focused on a secondary risk: with the American market closing, the Financial Times reported that Europe was bracing for a flood of Chinese goods redirected from the United States — a juxtaposition Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used to argue that Brussels should tariff China as it tariffs the United States, at a rate he put at 40 percent.[18]

Simultaneously, EU regulators were preparing the first-ever penalties under the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]] against [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s platform [[x|X]], expected to combine a fine possibly exceeding $1 billion with demands for product changes.[18] Officials weighed how large a fine to levy against the risk of further antagonizing Trump amid transatlantic disputes over trade, tariffs, and the war in Ukraine, while insisting that the X investigation was advancing independently of tariff negotiations.[18]

## Orban's disintegration claim, 2025

In October 2025, Hungarian Prime Minister [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orban]] declared that the European Union is "in trouble, in the process of disintegration, it is currently falling apart," and ruled out adopting the euro in [[hungary|Hungary]].[19] All member states except Denmark, which holds an opt-out, are obliged eventually to join the Eurozone, yet seven of the 27 still use their own national currencies.[19] [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] commentary characterized the EU as a draconian institution that has "done nothing other than subvert and marginalize the sovereignty of every European nation under its charter."[19]

## Lavrov's corruption-beneficiary allegation (November 2025)

In an interview published on November 25, 2025, Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] suggested that people within the European Union could themselves be benefitting from corruption in [[ukraine|Ukraine]], saying it was otherwise difficult to explain the bloc's determination to keep funding Kiev despite repeated graft and embezzlement scandals.[20] At the time Brussels was seeking to assemble €135 billion ($156 billion) to prop up Kiev through 2026 and 2027, whether by direct transfers from member-state budgets, joint borrowing, or the seizure of frozen Russian assets — an option Moscow has warned would amount to theft of its sovereign funds.[20] Lavrov asked whether "Brussels bureaucrats" had explained to their taxpayers why they must "endure and suffer," concluding, "Maybe, there are some beneficiaries as well. I rule out nothing."[20] The EU did not change its funding plans in light of a major graft scandal that had rocked Ukraine earlier that month.[20]

## Distrust of US Ukraine diplomacy

Der Spiegel reported in December 2025 that EU leaders harbored profound distrust of Washington's attempts to help end the Ukraine conflict, citing a transcript of a recent phone call among European leaders that the outlet had obtained.[21] The EU had outright rejected a US-drafted peace plan condition reportedly requiring Ukraine to cede parts of Donbass it still controls, insisting that Kiev should not give up any territory.[21]

## December 2025: X fine and sidelining over Ukraine

In December 2025 the [[european-commission|European Commission]] fined [[x|X]] 120 million euros ($140 million) under the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]] over its "deceptive" blue checkmark and a lack of advertising-repository transparency, prompting [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] to call for the bloc to be abolished and sovereignty returned to individual countries.[22] Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] called the fine an attack on all American tech platforms and the American people by foreign governments.[22]

Simultaneously, European governments were sidelined from American diplomacy on [[ukraine|Ukraine]]: a 28-point peace plan crafted with input from [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], Rubio and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] would offer Kiev EU membership without [[nato|NATO]] expansion while conceding eastern territories to [[russia|Russia]], leaving leaders such as Emmanuel Macron, Friedrich Merz and Alexander Stubb leaking frustration over lost influence on asset freezes and peace terms.[22] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Trump Corollary to the Monroe Doctrine, integrated into the 2025 National Security Strategy, asserts US primacy in the Western Hemisphere while criticizing Europe's trajectory in terms amounting to a call for de facto regime change in Brussels.[22] Separately, [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]]'s guarantee of Russian gas transit to Hungary via the [[turkstream-pipeline|TurkStream pipeline]] was read as further subverting EU efforts to escalate against Russia.[22]

## Migration Pact, April 2024

In April 2024 the [[european-parliament|European Parliament]] approved the [[eu-asylum-and-migration-pact|EU Asylum and Migration Pact]], obliging member states to accept a share of new arrivals into the bloc or pay a fine for each migrant they refuse.[23] Parliament President [[roberta-metsola|Roberta Metsola]] said the framework had been "10 years in the making" and that the EU had kept its word, while nationalist critics such as [[marine-le-pen|Marine Le Pen]] argued the measure transfers sovereign control over migration to a centralizing Brussels.[23] Badlands commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] cast the pact as part of a decades-old globalist project of mass migration justified through concepts such as "climate refugees," and urged Europeans to follow the path of [[brexit|Brexit]].[23]

## Digital COVID certificates and global vaccine passports

The European Union originated the concept of the digital vaccine passport through the [[eu-digital-covid-certificate|EU Digital COVID-19 Certificate]], an interoperable credential created to preserve free movement within its borders during the pandemic.[24] The EU and the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] signed an agreement on November 30, 2022, to ensure transparency and coordination on future health issues, and met in Geneva in June 2023 to discuss preparations for "future pandemics," including a global [[digital-vaccine-passport|digital vaccine passport]].[24] [[thierry-breton|Thierry Breton]], the EU Commissioner for Internal Market, said that with 80 countries and territories connected to the EU certificate the bloc had "set a global standard," and welcomed the WHO's decision to build its global system on the EU framework.[24] Critics including Armstrong Economics and Badlands writer [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that the initiative amounts to an effort to control populations through vaccination mandates and to normalize linking biometric data to digital identity.[24]

## WTO challenge and tariff retaliation (May 2025)

On 7 May 2025 the European Union declared its intention to contest the United States' reciprocal tariff policy at the [[world-trade-organization|World Trade Organization]], in an announcement made by Commissioner [[maros-sefcovic|Maros Sefcovic]] at the EU Council building.[25] The [[european-commission|European Commission]] simultaneously launched a public consultation on countermeasures targeting U.S. imports valued at $107.4 billion — reported as a roughly 95 billion euro retaliation package — covering agricultural and industrial goods including bourbon and tequila.[25] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had previously threatened a 200 percent tariff on EU alcohol imports.[25] The Commission said its objective was "to reaffirm that internationally agreed rules matter and these cannot be unilaterally disregarded by any WTO member, including the US."[25] See [[2025-eu-wto-challenge-to-us-tariffs|2025 EU WTO challenge to US tariffs]].[25]

In the same week the Trump administration concluded the [[2025-us-uk-trade-deal|US-UK trade deal]], prompting Badlands commentary that the EU and its allies would struggle to hold a hard line once the United Kingdom had settled first.[25]

## Division over China policy (April 2023)

Analysis published in April 2023 during the [[2023-macron-state-visit-to-china|2023 Macron state visit to China]] described the European Union as internally divided over China policy: France, Germany, and Spain sought to deepen relations with Beijing, while member states east of Austria and north of Germany, along with the Dutch, looked to the United States.[26] Michael Every of [[rabobank|Rabobank]] warned this could threaten a schism as national-security concerns came to transcend economics, and argued that Europe was already in a structurally weak, emerging-market-like position in a raw geopolitical world.[26] European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, who travelled to Beijing alongside [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]], was reported to have been ushered in "via the tradesman's entrance," and her call for supply-chain "de-risking" from China was snubbed by Macron.[26]

## Predicted censorship demand on X (October 2023)

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] predicted in October 2023 that the Israel-Hamas conflict would lead the European Union to invoke the [[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]] and ask [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] to censor content on X, requiring him either to shadow ban or remove accounts questioning specific details of the official narrative, or to face fines and possible loss of access to all of Europe.[34]

## Energy crisis and sanctions backfire (2022)

Joe Lange's November 2022 essay "Money and Oil" argues that European sanctions on [[russia|Russia]] over the invasion of [[ukraine|Ukraine]] were predictable but self-defeating, because all of Europe depends on Russia for its energy supply.[28] The sanctions immediately spiked energy prices, drove inflation higher, crushed the European Union's economy and had little effect on Russia itself.[28]

In the same account, attempts by central banks to destroy the ruble failed when [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] demanded payment for Russian oil and gas in rubles or gold, while European central banks went broke exchanging weakening currencies for the dollars needed to buy energy.[28] Lange reports a huge rise in sentiment among European publics for ending the war because of high energy prices, and predicts that European countries in recession will suffer worsening pain.[28]

## Rearmament frenzy and populist backlash (2025)

Following President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2025 threats to abandon [[nato|NATO]] and his pause on Ukraine funding, the European Union scrambled to fill the hypothetical void with increasingly hawkish rhetoric and a push for rapid rearmament.[29] The response included roughly €150 billion in EU loans for rearmament under [[rearm-europe|ReArm Europe]], announced by Commission President [[ursula-von-der-leyen|Ursula von der Leyen]], and exploration by Friedrich Merz, [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] and [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]] of a "NATO-minus" framework excluding an unreliable United States.[29] Merz raised European nuclear sharing with France and Britain, and Polish Premier [[donald-tusk|Donald Tusk]] said Poland must consider nuclear options.[29]

Badlands contributor Ryan DeLarme argued the effort exposed a hollow shell propped up by ideological zeal and shadowy influence rather than genuine strength, noting that the bloc was less cohesive than in previous decades.[29] The Netherlands and others opposed collective defense debt, Hungary was difficult to keep aligned, Italy and Spain forced the rebranding of ReArm Europe to "Readiness 2030," and financing proposals from foreign policy chief [[kaja-kallas|Kaja Kallas]] were rejected, with member states insisting Ukraine contributions remain voluntary and bilateral rather than mandated by Brussels.[29]

DeLarme tied the fracture to a continent-wide war between populism and globalism running since the Brexit vote, citing the popularity of [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] and [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] — neither of whom supported the hawkish line on Russia — and of France's National Rally leader [[marine-le-pen|Marine Le Pen]], who was targeted by what he called a lawfare operation barring her from running for five years despite being the frontrunner for the 2027 French presidential election.[29]
