# Centre for Addiction and Mental Health

The Centre for Addiction and Mental Health, commonly known as CAMH, is a psychiatric hospital and research institution in Toronto, Ontario, Canada. In Badlands Media coverage it appears as the institutional home of the researchers behind the [[toronto-desistance-study|Toronto desistance study]] on childhood [[gender-dysphoria|gender dysphoria]].[1]

Devita Singh, [[susan-bradley|Susan J. Bradley]] and [[kenneth-zucker|Kenneth J. Zucker]] worked out of CAMH on a study begun in Toronto in 1975 that followed 139 birth-assigned males, aged three to twelve at referral, over some three decades.[1] The results reported that only 12.2% of participants persisted in gender dysphoria while 87.8% desisted, with a majority of desisters turning out to be gay.[1]

Badlands contributor [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] treated the CAMH research as one of three international bodies of evidence that should have discredited the [[gender-affirming-care|gender affirmative model]], and asked how Canada could continue to expand that model while being the source of one of the most definitive studies on gender dysphoric youth in the world.[1]
