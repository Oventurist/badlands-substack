# Hollywood

Hollywood is the American film and television industry, treated in Badlands Media commentary as one of the central cultural institutions shaping the American psyche. In the essay "A New Hollywood," [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] argues that the industry is neither wholly redeemable nor wholly evil, but a medium of storytelling whose direction depends on who controls it.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## Formative cultural role

Carlson, a member of Generation X, recounts that cinema was central to his adolescence, recalling specific theaters and seats where he watched *Star Wars*, *The Empire Strikes Back*, *Return of the Jedi*, *Road Warrior*, *ET*, *Rocky III*, *Rambo*, *Top Gun*, *The Terminator*, *Ghostbusters*, *Raiders of the Lost Ark*, *Die Hard* and *Back to the Future*.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He observes that these films share a common structure: brave heroes who risk life and limb to stand against a corrupt and evil enemy or system in order to save humanity, and he asks whether he was "being conditioned by Hollywood … in a good way."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He credits this conditioning with his own decision, years later, to intervene physically to save a man from being beaten by five attackers, describing himself in that moment as "Dirty Harry, Johnny Rambo and Rocky all rolled into one."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## Decline and woke conditioning

Carlson holds that Hollywood has changed markedly over the past decade, replacing heroic tales with what he calls woke conditioning: standing up to the system has been replaced with succumbing to the system, and even heroic protagonists must arrive in demographically re-engineered form.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He argues boys are taught their masculinity is toxic while girls are taught their femininity is weak, with both sexes conditioned toward an androgynous middle he characterizes as "a very Baphomet concept."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He also notes that theatergoing has become too expensive and that few contemporary films justify leaving the house.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

He does not dispute the widespread claim that Hollywood is run by Satanic pedophiles at the top, and accepts that many below them succeeded by submitting to that evil, but insists this does not mean there are no decent people in the industry or that film as a medium is inherently evil.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## Signs of reversal

Carlson points to a cluster of recent productions as evidence that something is changing: Paramount+'s [[lioness|Lioness]], [[showtime|Showtime]]'s *The Agency*, and Paramount+'s *Landman* — the last a series about the Texas oil fields that he glosses as "drill, baby drill."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) Tracing ownership led him to [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]], [[sheri-redstone|Sheri Redstone]], and the pending merger with [[skydance-media|Skydance Media]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He concludes that Hollywood "is not only being saved, but that it has already been infiltrated by Trump's people," and that just as it once used predictive programming to prepare audiences for Deep State agendas, it is now being used to prepare the public for what [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] has in store.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## Precedent under Reagan

Carlson cites [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] as the model: Reagan took control of Hollywood during his eight years in office and pushed for movies promoting strong American characters like Rambo, making the American military appear powerful, inspiring Americans and intimidating foreign adversaries.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He describes Trump as "Reagan 2.0" — a vastly improved version that does not include compromising with the enemy.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

Carlson closes by arguing that Hollywood, Big Tech, the financial system and Big Medicine have all been used to control, brainwash, enslave and sicken the public, yet all four can be transformed to do good.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## Opposition to the Paramount-WBD merger (2026)

On April 13, 2026 more than 1,000 Hollywood writers, actors, and directors signed an open letter opposing Paramount's $111 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, warning of fewer films, narrower story diversity, reduced job opportunities, higher costs, and less audience choice.[2] The protest was driven in part by the deal's Gulf sovereign financing, led by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund under Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman's Vision 2030 push into entertainment.[2] Badlands commentator Burning Bright argued the industry's frantic coordination revealed that its members do not fear consolidation or foreign capital in the abstract, but specifically capital that refuses to genuflect to what he called their inverted morality.[2]

## Chinese import restrictions (April 2025)

On April 10, 2025 China announced it would immediately restrict imports of Hollywood films in retaliation for President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s escalation of tariffs on Chinese goods.[3] After three decades in which China annually imported ten Hollywood movies, its [[national-film-administration|National Film Administration]] said the tariff increase would further sour domestic demand for U.S. cinema after years of decline, pledging to "follow market rules, respect the audience's choices, and moderately reduce the number of American films imported."[3]

Chris Fenton, author of *Feeding the Dragon*, called the move a "super high-profile way to make a statement of retaliation with almost zero downside for China," noting Hollywood films account for only about 5 percent of overall box office receipts in the Chinese market.[3] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] characterized Hollywood's dismay as sadness over reduced access to the Chinese market for "American propaganda."[3]


## Tariff exposure of the ad market (April 2025)

The Hollywood Reporter warned in April 2025 that the timing of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[liberation-day-tariffs|"Liberation Day" tariffs]] could hardly have been worse for Hollywood.[4] Although the media business is not built on imported physical goods, many of its largest advertisers are, and buy-side and sell-side sources said the advertising market was already feeling the pain, with automotive, consumer packaged goods, and food and drink categories reexamining spending as they modeled the effect of the duties and of a possible all-out trade war.[4] A potential decline in tourism from Canada and Europe threatened to weaken the travel sector, where airlines and hotels compete for consumer dollars, and one high-level media source called the combination "a perfect storm of bad news," noting that scatter pricing was acceptable for the moment but that the outlook for the remainder of the year was weaker.[4] Badlands contributor [[brad-cologero-getz|Brad Cologero Getz]] added that Snow White had failed to crack $70 million domestic in two weeks at the box office, calling the performance "brutal."[4]

## Alleged seeding of the Iran war narrative

In June 2025, [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]] claimed that Hollywood screenwriters "who say they are Jewish" had been planting pro-war narratives about Iran in mainstream entertainment for more than a decade, citing Top Gun: Maverick, Homeland, 24, and The Fifth Estate.[5] [[julian-assange|Julian Assange]], in a 2013 Oxford Union speech that WikiLeaks recirculated, noted that The Fifth Estate opens with Iranian scientists in Tehran assembling a bomb said to be six months from completion, even though 16 US intelligence agencies had already concluded Tehran had no nuclear weapons program.[5] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the episode confirmed a longstanding Badlands theme — that Hollywood, "an industry built entirely on fiction," constantly hijacks reality through celebrity and storytelling — and argued that the consequences became visible when large portions of MAGA were convinced that war with Iran would be a good idea.[5] He called for greater discernment within the movement as a precondition for self-governance.[5]
