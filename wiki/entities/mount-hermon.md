# Mount Hermon

**Mount Hermon** is a mountain massif on the border between [[lebanon|Lebanon]] and [[syria|Syria]], rising to over 9,200 feet and constituting the highest point in the entire Levant. Its summit overlooks the Syrian capital, Damascus, which makes it the dominant observation point of the region's southern front. In the Badlands corpus it is treated as the keystone of the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]]'s 2026 campaign in southern Lebanon.

## Strategic role in the 2026 campaign

The Badlands Brief of April 20, 2026, citing updated IDF maps of Lebanon, reports that the Israeli military has moved northeast toward Mount Hermon, capturing the Lebanese lands bordering the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]] and Syria. The commentary identifies the capture of the mountain as the first of two short-term objectives of the campaign to establish Greater Israel: once the IDF has fortified the summit, it will be in a strong position to capture and annex both southern Lebanon and southern Syria, using the heights to dominate the approaches to Damascus.

## Broader significance

Alongside the [[forward-defense-zone|Forward Defense Zone]] on the coast, Mount Hermon anchors the terrain-based logic of the [[greater-israel-project|Greater Israel project]] as described in the corpus — the argument that territorial conquest is proceeding methodically, mountain first and coastline second, regardless of ceasefire agreements. The Brief links the mountain's capture to the warnings that Lebanese Christians should not return to their villages and to the escalating pattern of incidents in the occupation zone.
