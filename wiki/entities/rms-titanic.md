# RMS Titanic

The Titanic is invoked in the article as a "potentially-related event" in the run-up to the [[federal-reserve-act-of-1913|Federal Reserve Act]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

Several prominent names died on the Titanic one year prior to the signing of the Act, including the American millionaire John Jacob Astor IV, industrialist Benjamin Guggenheim, Macy's owner [[isidor-straus|Isidor Straus]], and journalist and social reformer William Thomas Stead.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) The article states that it has long been suggested that these men all opposed the Federal Reserve Act to various degrees, and frames the sinking within its claim that the plan's architects needed to silence their opposition in order to pass the necessary acts through the House of Representatives and [[congress|Congress]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Federal Reserve sinking theory

Badlands Media commentary recounts the theory that the Titanic was sunk in 1912 because three wealthy businessmen aboard — [[benjamin-guggenheim|Benjamin Guggenheim]], [[isidor-straus|Isa Strauss]] and [[john-jacob-astor|John Jacob Astor]] — opposed forming a [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[2] In that account the sinking was meant both to eliminate their opposition and to send a message to others who might speak against the banking plan drafted at the [[jekyll-island-club|Jekyll Island Club]] in November 1910.[2] The article presents the claim as a theory rather than established history.[2]
