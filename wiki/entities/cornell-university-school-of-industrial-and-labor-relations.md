# Cornell University School of Industrial and Labor Relations

The School of Industrial and Labor Relations (ILR) at Cornell University is the academic home of the [[ilr-labor-action-tracker|ILR Labor Action Tracker]], the data project that supplied the strike counts most widely quoted during the 2023 American labor unrest.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Role in 2023 strike coverage

[[johnnie-kallas|Johnnie Kallas]], a PhD candidate at the school and the Tracker's project director, produced the figures reported by CNBC and reproduced by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]]: 312 strikes involving roughly 453,000 workers so far in 2023, compared with 180 strikes involving 43,700 workers over the same period two years earlier.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) These numbers were used to establish that the year's wave of walkouts — spanning Hollywood writers, autoworkers, [[starbucks|Starbucks]] baristas, [[amazon|Amazon]] warehouse staff, electrical workers and [[kaiser-permanente|Kaiser Permanente]] employees — was of a scale unprecedented in recent memory.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

In the Badlands analysis by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], the school's data serves as neutral ground on which a broader argument is built: that the strikes, however well organized, were arriving after the decisive structural changes to the labor market had already been designed and implemented.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)
