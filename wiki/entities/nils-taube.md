# Nils Taube

Baron Nils Otto von Taube was a fund manager described by Badlands contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] as a close associate of both [[george-soros|George Soros]] and [[jacob-rothschild|Jacob Rothschild]].[1]

## Origins

Taube was born in Estonia in 1928.[1] His family was resettled to Reichsgau Wartheland in 1939 under the secret protocols of the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact, which required the resettlement of ethnic Germans from Estonia and Latvia.[1] Carlson characterises Reichsgau Wartheland as essentially Nazi-controlled Prussia, and points readers to the Prussia Gate series by Will Zoll, which connects Prussia to the cabal he says controls the world.[1]

## Career

Taube moved to London in 1946 to study chemistry, but in 1948 joined the stockbroker Kitcat & Aitken as an office junior.[1] He gained notoriety investing in then-undervalued German shares, becoming an analyst in 1951, a fund manager in 1969 and a senior partner in 1975.[1] Between 1969 and 2006 the funds he managed averaged a return of over 15% per year — a record Carlson dismisses as impossible without working inside a rigged system.[1]

In the 1970s Soros invited Taube onto the advisory board of his Quantum Group hedge funds.[1] In 1982 Jacob Rothschild bought a stake in Kitcat & Aitken specifically to gain access to Taube, who became Rothschild's principal investment manager, a post he held until 1996.[1] That year Taube left to establish Taube Hodson Stonex Partners, building assets of $11 billion — another newly established investment firm of the 1990s, which Carlson treats as a recurring theme among "British investment geniuses."[1]

## Significance in the Badlands thesis

Carlson asks what the odds are that a German who moved to England would be so heavily involved with both Soros and Rothschild, and that a man whose family was relocated to Nazi Prussia in 1939 would be so deeply entrusted by two Jewish billionaires.[1] He acknowledges he may sound like a conspiracy theorist, but argues the arrangement fits his claim that the [[british-cabal|British Cabal]] draws Satanists from all walks of life, race and religion, with allegiance owed to the society rather than to race or faith.[1]
