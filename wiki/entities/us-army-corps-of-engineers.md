# US Army Corps of Engineers

The US Army Corps of Engineers is the federal engineering agency responsible for navigable-waterway maintenance, dredging, and major civil works, and it took a lead technical role in the response to the [[francis-scott-key-bridge-collapse|Francis Scott Key Bridge collapse]] in Baltimore.[1]

## Baltimore salvage operation

On Tuesday, April 2, 2024, the Corps' Baltimore District published new underwater three-dimensional images of the bridge wreckage lying at the bottom of the main shipping channel serving the [[port-of-baltimore|Port of Baltimore]].[1] "These 3D images show the sheer magnitude of the very difficult and challenging salvage operation ahead," the agency said in a social-media post.[1]

The Corps explained that its primary survey instrument was the CODA Octopus sonar imaging tool, necessary because visibility on the bottom of the Patapsco River was clouded to one or two feet by four to five feet of mud and loose sediment.[1] Divers were described as working in virtual darkness — illumination scattered off suspended silt in a manner the agency compared to driving through heavy snowfall at night with high beams on — and had to be guided by detailed verbal directions from topside operators watching real-time CODA imagery.[1]

At the time of the article, no local, state, or federal official had offered a timeline for the complete removal of the collapsed span from the main channel.[1] A temporary alternate channel had been opened for small vessels, with a US Air Force fuel barge the first through, but activity at the port remained largely paralyzed pending reopening of the main channel.[1] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted in commentary that the imagery and known conditions implied a formidable undertaking, and raised unresolved questions about fuel and an underground pipeline, contaminated bunker fuel aboard the Dali, and the prospect of climate-framed narratives arising from the incident.[1]
