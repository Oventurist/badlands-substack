# Jimmy Carter

Jimmy Carter was the 39th President of the United States, a Georgia peanut farmer who rose to national office and later became a Nobel Peace Prize laureate for his humanitarian work. He died at his home in Plains, Georgia, on Sunday, December 29, 2024, at the age of 100, according to an announcement by the Carter Center.[1]

## Presidency

A Democrat, Carter served as president from January 1977 to January 1981 after defeating incumbent Republican President Gerald Ford in the 1976 U.S. election.[1] His single term was defined by a struggling economy and the Iran hostage crisis, though he also brokered peace between Israel and Egypt.[1] He was swept from office four years later in an electoral landslide as voters embraced Republican challenger Ronald Reagan, the former actor and California governor.[1]

## Post-presidency and death

Carter lived longer after leaving office than any other U.S. president, and along the way earned a reputation as a better former president than he had been a president — a status he readily acknowledged.[1] His son Chip Carter said in a statement that his father "was a hero, not only to me but to everyone who believes in peace, human rights, and unselfish love," adding that the family had "shared him with the rest of the world through these common beliefs."[1]

## Funeral

Carter's funeral service was held on Thursday, January 9, 2025, at the Washington National Cathedral, drawing all five living American presidents — including President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], seated beside [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] — and every living former first lady except [[michelle-obama|Michelle Obama]], who remained in Hawaii citing scheduling conflicts.[2] Badlands commentary focused on the body language among the assembled presidents and on the observation that, unlike [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]] at the funeral of [[george-hw-bush|George H. W. Bush]], Carter had never received one of the mysterious white envelopes distributed at that earlier service.[2] The event is covered in detail at [[state-funeral-of-jimmy-carter|the state funeral of Jimmy Carter]] page.[2]

## The 1980 hostage crisis and the October surprise

The phrase [[october-surprise|"October surprise"]] originated in 1980 with fears in [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]]'s campaign that Carter would secure the release of the 52 American hostages held in Iran for nearly a year, delivering the embattled president a significant diplomatic success.[3] Reagan campaign manager Bill Casey publicly warned of such a maneuver and the campaign asked contacts in the intelligence and military communities to watch for signs of a release.[3] No release occurred before the election; instead the anniversary of the captivity fell on election eve, reminding the country of the hostages' plight, and the captives were freed minutes after Reagan's inauguration — a fact [[politico|Politico]] offered as a measure of Iran's distaste for Carter.[3] A later theory, examined by biographer [[max-boot|Max Boot]] and investigated by the House in 1993, held that the Reagan campaign had worked to delay the release in order to deprive Carter of the political windfall.[3]
