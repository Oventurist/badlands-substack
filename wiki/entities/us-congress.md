# United States Congress

Congress appears in "A Bright Red Line" as one of the seats of the Western neoconservative faction pressing for Ukraine's entry into [[nato|NATO]], named alongside the [[us-security-state|Security State]] and the [[state-department|State Department]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line) The article states that these actors are aware that such a commitment ends any possibility of negotiation with Russia, and are eager for that result.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

Congress is also implicated in the article's opening premise: from the outset of the war the United States made clear it would take primary responsibility for funding and arming Ukraine, an expenditure the article says unquestionably harms Americans economically while never being explained in terms of the American public's interest.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-bright-red-line)

## Evaded oversight in the sanctions and uranium schemes

"America for Sale — Part 3" casts Congress primarily as the body whose oversight the executive branch and intelligence community worked to circumvent.[2] The article argues that when [[sanctions|sanctions]] cut a country off from the world banking system, the resulting "closed financial system" lets the [[cia|CIA]] launder money without money-laundering law applying and without Congress tracking it.[2]

In the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]], the [[nuclear-regulatory-commission|Nuclear Regulatory Commission]] assured Congress and the public in a November 2010 press release that no uranium produced at the affected facilities could be exported, and then-Chairman Gregory Jaczko repeated the assurance a year later in a letter to Senator John Barrasso of Wyoming.[2] The article, citing The Hill, states that rather than issue [[rosatom|Rosatom]] a direct export license — which would have raised red flags in a Congress already suspicious of the deal — the NRC amended the license of the trucking firm [[rsb-logistics-services|RSB Logistics Services]] to add [[uranium-one|Uranium One]] as a client, and that the move escaped notice in Congress.[2] Clinton's pardon of [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]] is noted as having triggered congressional as well as criminal investigations.[2]

## Dispute with the Trump Administration over the Iran War

In spring 2026 Congress was engaged in a public debate with the [[trump-administration|Trump Administration]] over the [[iran|Iran]] War, a dispute that opened the fifteenth episode of [[the-blitz|The Blitz]] on 2 May 2026.[3] Hosts [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] and [[ghost-of-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used the congressional argument as a device for trying to define when a war is and is not happening at the same time.[3] The discussion then broadened into the hosts' account of the [[global-cartel|Global Cartel]] being dismantled across the Middle East and South America on kinetic, financial and energy grounds.[3]

## Special counsel access to members' communications (disclosed 2026)

In July 2026 [[chuck-grassley|Sen. Chuck Grassley]] revealed that former special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]]'s team had obtained and reviewed text messages from 44 members of Congress — Grassley himself, 19 other senators, top House Republicans such as [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]], and some Democrats — during the January 6 and Mar-a-Lago investigations of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[4] The messages had been sent to senior [[white-house|White House]] officials and were produced by the [[national-archives-and-records-administration|National Archives]] in August 2023 under subpoena.[4]

The joint Grassley–[[ron-johnson|Johnson]] report alleged that prosecutors bypassed document-filtering protocols and accessed communications outside the probes' scope, prompting plans to compel Smith's testimony and reviving comparisons to the contempt-of-Congress prosecutions of [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] and [[peter-navarro|Peter Navarro]].[4]

## March 2024 shutdown bill and the Northeast gasoline reserve

The roughly 1,050-page bill passed in March 2024 to avert a government shutdown — authorizing about $1.7 trillion in discretionary spending — contained a provision selling off and shuttering the [[northeast-gasoline-supply-reserve|Northeast Gasoline Supply Reserve]], which [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] characterized as a pork-filled free-for-all concealing a measure that would leave the continental Northeast defenseless against a supply shock.[5] In May 2024, two Republican congressmen introduced the [[protecting-americans-in-the-idf-act|Protecting Americans in the IDF Act]], prompting Badlands commentary that Congress 'seems more loyal to foreign nations than to Americans.'[5]

## March 2023: Covid origins and deposit insurance

The House and Senate unanimously passed the [[covid-19-origin-act-of-2023|COVID-19 Origin Act of 2023]] in March 2023, requiring the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] to declassify information on possible links between a Chinese laboratory and the origins of Covid-19; President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] signed it on March 20.[6] In the same period, Bloomberg reported that [[us-department-of-the-treasury|Treasury Department]] staff were examining whether regulators could temporarily insure all bank deposits above the $250,000 cap without formal consent from a Congress described as deeply divided.[6]
