# Charles E. Mitchell

Charles Edwin Mitchell (October 6, 1877 – December 14, 1955), known as "Sunshine" Charley Mitchell, was an American banker whose incautious securities policies facilitated the speculation leading to the [[stock-market-crash-of-1929|Crash of 1929]].[1] He headed [[national-city-bank|National City Bank]], now [[citibank|Citibank]], and the bank's controversial activities under his leadership were a major contributing factor in the passage of the [[glass-steagall|Glass-Steagall Act]].[1]

In 1933 the U.S. Senate's [[pecora-commission|Pecora Commission]] called Mitchell as its first witness, investigating his part in tens of millions of dollars in losses, excessive pay, and tax avoidance.[1] In November 1929 Senator [[carter-glass|Carter Glass]] said, "Mitchell more than any 50 men is responsible for this stock crash."[1]

Mitchell made more than $1 million in bonuses in 1929, as the market was about to crash, while paying zero taxes, and he lent millions to stock brokers to sustain the speculation.[1] He was later prosecuted for tax evasion but acquitted, which Joe Lange offers as evidence that "banksters" never face consequences.[1] Mitchell's practice of bundling bad loans into securities and selling them to unsuspecting customers is presented in the article as the origin of a template repeated in the [[2008-financial-crisis|2008 financial crisis]].[1]
