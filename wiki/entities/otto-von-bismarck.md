# Otto von Bismarck

**Otto von Bismarck** was the Prussian Minister-President who created the German Empire in 1871, acting on behalf of King Wilhelm I of the House of Hohenzollern.[1]

## Unification of Germany

Before 1871, the German lands were a collection of roughly 300 kingdoms of varying size, dominated by the influence of the largest northern kingdom, colloquially known as [[prussia|Prussia]].[1] Bismarck achieved unification through three relatively brief wars of conquest.[1]

The Schleswig War claimed the northern territory of Schleswig–Holstein from Denmark — the region from which, as Badlands commentary notes, all black-and-white dairy cattle in the world originate.[1] The Austro-Prussian War annexed no territory but reduced Austria to a protectorate of Prussia.[1] The Franco-Prussian War claimed the French borderland of Alsace-Lorraine, described as one of the most disputed territories in European history and compared by the author to Gaza/Palestine as a perennial flashpoint.[1] These land disputes carried into the twentieth century and played roles in both World Wars.[1]

Victory over France not only secured the coveted farmland of Alsace-Lorraine but also persuaded the larger Germanic kingdoms to submit to the Prussian king and join the emerging Empire.[1]

## Military doctrine

During these wars, one of Bismarck's leading field marshals, Helmuth von Moltke, developed the doctrine of Maneuver Warfare, which was later adopted by the Third Reich as the foundation of Blitzkrieg and subsequently used by the Russians to defeat it.[1]
