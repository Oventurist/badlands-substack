# Leonardo da Vinci

Leonardo da Vinci was an Italian Renaissance polymath cited by Badlands Media as perhaps the most accomplished designer in the field of [[biomimicry|biomimicry]] — the practice of mimicking nature to solve human engineering problems.[1]

## Biomimetic inventions

Da Vinci determined that flight was achievable after studying the skeletons and musculature of birds, realizing that their bones were hollow and flexible and that the glide achieved by articulated wings was more important than the flapping of wings.[1] Once he understood that air possessed currents and properties similar to water, he took the concept of Archimedes' screw — originally developed to move water uphill — and applied it to design the world's first helicopter in 1480.[1] He also developed explosive cluster-bomb munitions after studying pomegranates and their seeds.[1]

## Anatomy and theology of design

Da Vinci made detailed and obsessive comparisons between the human body and nature, particularly the resemblance between the branching of veins in the lungs and the root systems of trees.[1] He concluded that God was the ultimate engineer who had perfected all of His designs, and that rather than attempting to compete with Him, humans should learn from Him by studying and mimicking His work.[1]
