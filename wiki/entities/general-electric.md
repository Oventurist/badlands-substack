# General Electric

General Electric (GE) is a major American industrial conglomerate that, in the 1980s, ranked among the nation's largest defense contractors.[1] Its plant in [[burlington-vermont|Burlington, Vermont]] figures prominently in critiques of [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]]' antiwar record.[1]

## Burlington plant and the Sanders mayoralty

After voters rejected the municipal bond referendum Sanders had sought for his waterfront redevelopment plan, he remained politically popular because Burlington's economy was booming — an economy propped up in part by the GE plant and by the same "profligate military spending" Sanders campaigned against throughout his career.[1] The plant employed three thousand workers and had nearly doubled its defense contracts by 1986.[1] It produced Vulcan Gatling guns, rotating rapid-fire weapons typically mounted on attack helicopters and fighter jets; a 2023 Badlands Media essay noted the irony that such guns were used to kill socialists in Central America.[1]

## Conflict with progressive protesters

Sanders' progressive base opposed what the plant produced and represented, and demonstrators — many of them his own supporters — appeared outside the facility with banners and picket signs.[1] They were reportedly shocked when Sanders sided with General Electric executives, denounced the protests, and had some of the protesters arrested.[1] The episode is cited alongside his later recruitment of a [[lockheed-martin|Lockheed Martin]] research center to Burlington as evidence that Sanders welcomed the [[military-industrial-complex|military-industrial complex]] into his own state while attacking defense corporations rhetorically elsewhere.[1] The World Socialist Web Site described him as a "silent partner of American militarism."[1]

## Defence contracting and NBC ownership

"War Pigs" uses General Electric as its central case study of the [[military-industrial-media-complex|military-industrial-media complex]].[2] For years GE owned 49% of [[nbc|NBC]] while simultaneously acting as a subcontractor for the Tomahawk cruise missiles and Patriot II missiles used extensively during the [[gulf-war|Gulf War]].[2] The company also manufactured components for the B-2 stealth bomber, the B-52 bomber and the E-3 AWACS aircraft, all likewise used extensively in that conflict.[2]

Badlands Media reported that GE received around $2 billion in defence contracts related to weapons that would be used in the Gulf War and in the 2003 invasion and occupation of Iraq, and that Comcast later acquired GE's common equity ownership interest in NBCUniversal.[2] The publication described this overlap as just one example of many showcasing the historic marriage between defence contractors and corporate media.[2]
