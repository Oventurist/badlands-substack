# Sudan

Sudan is a northeast African state that, in April 2023, descended into open warfare between rival military factions, producing a humanitarian and evacuation crisis that drew sharp criticism of the United States government's handling of its citizens abroad.[1]

## The April 2023 crisis

Fighting between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces broke out in mid-April 2023 and continued to unfold as an escalating military conflict, with rival factions vying for control of the country.[1] Contemporary reporting described the situation as complicated and dangerous, and noted that it was exceedingly difficult to form a clear picture of which factions held power on the ground.[1] Coverage aggregated by [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] treated the conflict as one of the defining stories of the period, alongside the [[jeffrey-epstein|Epstein]] revelations and the American banking turmoil.[1]

## Americans left behind

As the crisis deepened, Americans with family in Sudan expressed mounting anger at what they described as abandonment by their own government.[1] Muna Daoud, whose parents attempted to leave Sudan via Port Sudan to Saudi Arabia, said she was "incredibly shocked and disgusted by the American lackluster response to the health and safety of their citizens."[1] While a number of other nations evacuated their nationals, the U.S. government maintained that conditions were not conducive to a civilian evacuation, even after all U.S. government personnel were withdrawn in a military operation over the weekend of 22 April 2023.[1]

U.S. officials said they were in "close communication" with American citizens and "actively facilitating" their departure.[1] Relatives of the "dozens" of Americans seeking to leave told CNN that the [[state-department|State Department]] had provided "barely any assistance" since the violence began.[1]

## Seizure of the Khartoum bio-lab

On April 25, 2023, the [[world-health-organization|World Health Organization]] declared a "high risk of biological hazard" in Khartoum after a military group seized one of Sudan's main bio-laboratories.[2] [[reuters|Reuters]] reported that the seized facility contained cholera, measles and several other deadly pathogens.[2] The WHO Representative for Sudan said the "main concern" was that there was "no accessibility to the lab technicians to go to the lab and safely contain the biological material and substances available."[2] Days earlier, U.S. Special Forces had executed an emergency extraction of personnel from the U.S. Embassy in Khartoum because of fighting between the Sudanese army and the [[rapid-support-forces|Rapid Support Forces]].[2]

## Great-power competition

Badlands writer [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] argued that coups and civil strife in countries like Sudan are "rarely naturally or locally created events," and that outside powers typically facilitate a faction's growth and equipping.[2] He noted that Russia had reached a deal with Sudan to establish a naval base on the country's Red Sea coast, an arrangement the United States was said to view unfavorably.[2] DeLarme framed the central question as whether the RSF was itself divided between [[abdel-fattah-al-burhan|General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan]], head of the Sovereign Council and the Sudanese military, and [[mohamed-hamdan-dagalo|General Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo]], known as "Hemedti".[2] He speculated that the biolab seizure might represent a continuation of the Ukraine biolab narrative, a retaliation by Western intelligence services, or even the seeding of "another Wuhan" — and concluded that the WHO's rapid response indicated a narrative was being deployed regardless.[2]

## Narrative analysis

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that headlines about the crisis should be evaluated less for their inherent truth or falsity than for the claim being advanced, the party advancing it, and the net effect of the narrative deployment.[1] He noted the political awkwardness for the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration of being accused of failing to protect Americans abroad in a second major conflict requiring an evacuation, following [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] — in his words, "not a great look."[1]

## Russian naval base agreement (February 2025)

In February 2025 Sudanese Foreign Minister Ali Youssef Ahmed Al-Sharif confirmed in Moscow, alongside Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]], that Khartoum and Moscow had agreed on the establishment of a [[russian-naval-base-in-sudan|Russian naval base]] on Sudan's Red Sea coast, saying "we have agreed on everything."[3] The facility, first proposed in December 2020, is to serve as a logistical support hub for ship repairs and resupply, limited to 300 personnel and four vessels at a time.[3] Lavrov used the occasion to call for a swift cessation of hostilities and an inclusive national dialogue on the conflict between the [[sudanese-armed-forces|Sudanese Armed Forces]] and the [[rapid-support-forces|Rapid Support Forces]], without external interference.[3]
