# Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf

Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI), commonly rendered in English as the Pakistan Movement for Justice, is the political party founded by the former international cricket star [[imran-khan]] and the vehicle through which he rose to become prime minister of [[pakistan]]. In the Badlands Media essay [[and-iran-iran-so-far-away|"And Iran, Iran So Far Away"]] the party appears as the institutional expression of Khan's populist challenge to Pakistan's entrenched military and intelligence establishment.

## Role in the corpus

The article introduces PTI while describing Khan as "yet another notch in the belt of the regime change machine at the US [[state-department]]." Khan is characterised as a populist figure — a former cricket star and the founder of PTI — who was at first perceived as having ties to the military establishment, including the [[inter-services-intelligence]] (ISI), the agency the article calls "Pakistan's CIA" and describes as kept on a tight leash by the United States.

That alignment is presented as temporary. Although Khan may initially have benefited from ISI support, the source states that his relationship with the agency became strained during his tenure, because — "not unlike [[donald-trump]]" — Khan had his own plans for how to run the country, plans that ran against the preferences of his country's own rogue intelligence services and of the Western foreign policy establishment. The article attributes the decisive breach to Khan's "aggressively neutral" stance on [[russia]] and his threats to block Pakistani arms shipments to [[ukraine]], an independent foreign policy meant to preserve friendly relations with both Washington and Moscow without favouring either.

The party's leader was removed by a parliamentary no-confidence vote in April 2022, which Khan attributed to military and ISI interference; he publicly accused then-ISI chief Nadeem Anjum and former army chief General Qamar Javed Bajwa of orchestrating his removal. In the article's telling the consequence was that "CIA/MIC/deep state-friendly assets" resumed control of Pakistan and the ISI, a restoration the author links to Pakistan's later threats against [[israel]] during the Iran crisis.
