# James Dennehy

James Dennehy was the head of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]]'s New York field office who was forced out of the Bureau on Monday, March 3, 2025, according to five sources familiar with the matter who spoke to NBC News.[1] His removal came roughly a month after he urged employees under his command to "dig in" following the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's removal of senior FBI leaders and its request for the names of all agents who had worked on January 6 cases.[1]

## Forced departure

In an email to FBI staff members in New York on the day of his departure, Dennehy confirmed that he had been ordered to leave. "Late Friday, I was informed that I needed to put my retirement papers in today, which I just did," he wrote, adding, "I was not given a reason for this decision."[1] Two of the sources cited by NBC News said Dennehy had been given a choice between resigning and being fired.[1]

## Commentary and competing interpretations

Badlands contributors treated the episode as a case study in the gap between televised narrative and underlying events. Chris Paul argued that the visible personnel drama functioned as a distraction, writing that "the quiet people in the background are the ones doing everything while the public facing members of the admin are mostly beclowning themselves."[1]

Ashe in America noted that the central narrative framed the ouster as retaliation for Dennehy's response to Trump's order to produce the names of agents who participated in what Badlands describes as the January 6 political persecutions.[1] On social media, however, the immediate assumption was that the firing concerned the New York field office's alleged refusal to produce Epstein files, with commenters expressing outrage that Dennehy was allowed to resign rather than face prosecution.[1] Ashe in America observed that Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] appeared on Sean Hannity's program the same Monday and did not mention the firing at all, despite discussing terminations and turnover at the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] and the FBI.[1]
