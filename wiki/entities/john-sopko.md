# John Sopko

John Sopko is the Special Inspector General for Afghanistan Reconstruction (SIGAR), the U.S. government watchdog charged with auditing American spending in Afghanistan.[1]

## November 2023 aid report

On Monday, October 30 / early November 2023, Sopko issued a report revealing that Afghanistan had received roughly $11 billion in foreign aid from the United States since the country's collapse in August 2021.[1] According to the report, the United States and its allies had been sending "cash shipments" of about $80 million to Afghanistan "every 10-14 days" since the [[taliban|Taliban]] took over the country shortly before the withdrawal of all American forces.[1]

Sopko said the [[united-nations|United Nations]] had assured him that all of the money was "placed in designated U.N. accounts in a private bank," and was not being "deposited in the central bank or provided to the Taliban."[1] The U.N. Assistance Mission in Afghanistan (UNAMA) similarly claimed the cash shipments were "carefully monitored, audited, inspected, and vetted in accordance with U.N. financial rules and processes."[1]

Despite those assurances, Sopko's report noted that the Taliban had stolen foreign aid before and had prevented the poorest elements of the population from receiving aid designated for them. Documented Taliban methods included siphoning cash from U.N. shipments, collecting royalties, and charging fees on cash shipments.[1] The findings were reported by Breitbart and American Greatness.[1]

## Commentary

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], linked the continuing flow of U.S. funds into Afghanistan to arguments advanced by [[just-human|Just Human]] connecting the Kabul withdrawal to the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]] signed under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and framed the ongoing payments as evidence for [[devolution|Devolution]] and "Sovereign Alliance–Regions of Responsibility" theories.[1]

## Testimony on Taliban funding

Sopko told the House Foreign Affairs Committee in April 2023 that he "cannot assure this committee or the American taxpayer we are not currently funding the [[taliban|Taliban]]," and accused the Biden administration of blocking his office's efforts to find out.[2] His warning framed the reception of a subsequent [[special-inspector-general-for-afghanistan-reconstruction|SIGAR]] report finding that $2.35 billion had been provided to [[afghanistan|Afghanistan]] over two years of Taliban rule, with roughly $1.7 billion still available for possible disbursement.[2]
