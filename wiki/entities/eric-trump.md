# Eric Trump

Eric Trump is a son of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and an executive within the family's real estate and development interests. In the coverage compiled by the Badlands Brief, he appears as the public face of the family's most ambitious commemorative project to date.[1]

## Role in the Trump Presidential Library announcement

On March 31, 2026, Eric Trump unveiled plans for a 50-story [[donald-j-trump-presidential-library|Donald J. Trump Presidential Library]] in Miami, a waterfront tower clad in gold and featuring a giant statue of his father, an Air Force One aircraft displayed in the lobby, an amphitheater, and replicas of the Oval Office and a White House ballroom.[1] The land for the project was transferred to the [[trump-library-foundation|Trump Library Foundation]] by [[miami-dade-college|Miami-Dade College]].[1]

Eric Trump described the building as "a masterpiece, the likes of which have never been seen in Florida or really anywhere," and called it a "lasting testament" to the former president, adding that the landmark "will stand as a lasting testament to an amazing man, an amazing developer, and the greatest President our Nation has ever known."[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] remarked that "Eric Trump sounds like his dad," using the announcement as a prompt to reflect that the library signals the approaching close of the Trump era and the movement's need to prepare for "MAGA in a Post Trump World."[1] Critics of the project, including the office of California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] and historian [[marvin-dunn|Marvin Dunn]], attacked both the aesthetics of the golden statue and the terms of the land transfer.[1]

## Tel Aviv hotel talks

In spring 2025, Eric Trump met with the heads of the Israeli firm Nitsba Holdings about the [[trump-organization|Trump Organization]] investing in, branding, and managing the under-construction Sarona Hotel in Tel Aviv, expected to become Israel's tallest hotel when it opens, according to a New York Times report relayed by the [[times-of-israel|Times of Israel]].[2] The talks took place several weeks before Israel launched its preemptive strike on Iran's nuclear program, which the United States joined when President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] ordered strikes on the Fordo, Natanz, and Isfahan facilities.[2] Critics argued a Trump property in Israel could influence American diplomatic decisions and serve as a target for anti-American terror groups, part of broader criticism that the president was mixing national and personal interests to an unprecedented degree.[2] Badlands commentary suggested the timing of Eric's trip implied he was seeking a discount while Tel Aviv was purportedly under attack, and floated the possibility that the deal was a misdirection play within a longer strategic negotiation.[2]
