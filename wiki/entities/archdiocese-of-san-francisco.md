# Archdiocese of San Francisco

The Archdiocese of San Francisco is the Roman Catholic ecclesiastical jurisdiction covering San Francisco, the city where former House Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] maintains her home.[1]

In January 2023 the archdiocese was asked to confirm claims by [[alexandra-pelosi|Alexandra Pelosi]] that priests had been brought to the Pelosi residence over Thanksgiving to conduct "an exorcism of the house" and prayer services following the [[paul-pelosi-attack|attack on Paul Pelosi]].[1] Peter Marlow, the archdiocese's Executive Director of Communications and Media Relations, responded to [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] on Monday, January 23, 2023, stating: "The Archdiocese is unaware of the exorcism question you raised."[1] The statement was treated by commentators as undercutting the exorcism claim.[1]

## Clergy abuse records and the Harris district attorney era

The archdiocese figures centrally in Badlands reporting on [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s tenure as San Francisco District Attorney.[2] An investigation by her predecessor [[terence-hallinan|Terence Hallinan]] found that the archdiocese held internal records of complaints of priest sexual abuse of children stretching back more than seventy years, and in the spring of 2002 Hallinan demanded that the church turn the documents over to his office, which it reluctantly did.[2]

The documents were reportedly explosive, containing the names of roughly 40 active Bay Area priests against whom complaints had been filed, and they touched well-connected institutions at the heart of the California power structure including the [[jesuits|Jesuit]]-run [[st-ignatius-college-preparatory|St. Ignatius College Prep]], associated with [[jerry-brown|Jerry Brown]] and the [[getty-family|Getty family]].[2] Hallinan's prosecutions were curtailed when the U.S. Supreme Court struck down California's extension of the statute of limitations for priest abuse cases, and the church, financially strained by mounting legal fees and settlements, treated his push to publicize the files as a serious threat.[2]

During the same period, donations connected to the church's institutional hierarchy flowed into Harris's campaign: [[joseph-russoniello|Joseph Russoniello]] and his firm Cooley Godward, the firm Bingham McCutcheon which handled archdiocese matters concerning Catholic Charities, and the firm Arguedas, Cassman & Headley all gave at or near maximum levels, while archdiocese board members and their family members contributed a combined $50,950.[2] After taking office Harris ended the effort to release the documents and prosecuted no priests; a 2010 San Francisco Weekly request for the records under the California Public Records Act was refused.[2]
