# Denver

Denver is the capital and largest city of Colorado. In 2024 it drew national attention for municipal policies encouraging residents to house migrants in their private homes amid a sustained influx of new arrivals.[1]

## Migrant hosting hotline

The city encouraged residents to host migrant families in their homes and set up a hotline through which people could volunteer to take in migrants who had nowhere else to go.[1] Local reporting described how the charity [[hope-has-no-borders|Hope Has No Borders]] had begun pairing migrant workers and their families with hosts in Colorado in late 2023, and that with the assistance of United Way the pairing process became accessible by dialling 211.[1] One news segment profiled Erin Lennon, described as a single mother with a spare bedroom, who took in a family of four after learning that arrivals were camping in airports and on the streets because shelters were full; her young son was reported to have been nervous about taking in strangers, while Lennon said that "some of the greatest things that... you do or have done, has been involved with some risk."[1] Coverage noted that comparable programmes were operating in several cities nationwide.[1]

## Fiscal and civic effects

Badlands Media commentary from a Colorado resident described the hotline as an invitation to quarter arrivals after a roughly twenty-minute vetting process, and catalogued associated local consequences: the city cutting police and fire funding by $89.9 million to support newcomer support programmes; a local high school cancelling its graduation at the Air Force Academy because campus security requirements were considered burdensome for newcomer families; and Colorado's homicide rate surpassing the national homicide rate for the first time in more than forty years.[1] The same commentary linked these developments to gun-control efforts in the state and offered Denver as a "cautionary tale" for the rest of the country.[1]
