# Office of the United States Trade Representative

The Office of the United States Trade Representative (USTR) is the executive-branch agency responsible for developing and coordinating US international trade policy, negotiating trade agreements, and administering statutory trade-enforcement tools on behalf of the President. It is headed by the US Trade Representative, a cabinet-rank ambassador; under the second [[donald-trump]] administration that post is held by [[jamieson-greer]].

## Role under America First trade policy

USTR is the principal instrument of the administration's [[america-first]] trade agenda, conducting investigations under [[section-301]] of the [[trade-act-of-1974]] and recommending tariff action where it finds foreign practices unreasonable or burdensome to US commerce.

## Brazil action, July 2026

As reported in the Badlands Brief of July 17, 2026, USTR concluded a yearlong Section 301 investigation into [[brazil]] and the administration imposed a 25% tariff on certain Brazilian imports. The investigation examined Brazilian policies involving digital trade, electronic payment services, preferential tariffs, anti-corruption enforcement, intellectual property protection, ethanol market access, and illegal deforestation.

In June 2026 USTR determined that certain Brazilian practices were unreasonable and restricted US commerce, leading officials to propose tariff action. The agency then reviewed more than 360 public comments and held a July hearing at which 77 witnesses testified before finalizing the tariffs. Greer said the measures were taken at the President's direction, and that Brazil's practices had limited American access to a market of more than 210 million consumers. The United States said it had spent months negotiating with Brazilian officials, including formal consultations in April, without resolving the disputes.
