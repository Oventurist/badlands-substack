# Office of the United States Trade Representative

The Office of the United States Trade Representative (USTR) is the U.S. executive agency responsible for developing and coordinating trade policy and conducting trade investigations. In the second Trump administration it is led by Ambassador [[jamieson-greer|Jamieson Greer]].[1]

## 2026 Brazil Section 301 action
USTR conducted a yearlong investigation into Brazilian trade practices, examining policies involving digital trade, electronic payment services, preferential tariffs, anti-corruption enforcement, intellectual property protections, ethanol market access, and illegal deforestation.[1] In June 2026 the agency determined that certain Brazilian practices were unreasonable and restricted U.S. commerce, leading officials to propose tariff action.[1]

USTR subsequently reviewed more than 360 public comments and held a July hearing at which 77 witnesses testified before finalizing the measure.[1] The resulting action, announced in July 2026 under [[section-301-of-the-trade-act-of-1974|Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974]], imposed a 25% tariff on certain Brazilian goods.[1] The United States said it had spent months negotiating with Brazilian officials, including formal consultations in April, but that the disputes remained unresolved.[1]
