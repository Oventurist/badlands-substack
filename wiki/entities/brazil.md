# Brazil

Brazil is a South American state whose judicial censorship apparatus became the subject of an interim staff report released by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], which presented new evidence that the regime targets speech inside the United States and pressures American social media companies to comply or face legal action and expulsion from the Brazilian market.[1]

## The censorship system

According to the report and newly obtained nonpublic documents, the system is led by Supreme Court Justice [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]] and operates by issuing global takedown orders, coordinating with censorship efforts in other countries, and removing the legal protections that shield platforms from liability.[1]

## Extraterritorial reach

The committee said these actions have directly targeted US-based speech, including posts by Brazilian journalists and political commentators residing in the United States, as well as content praising President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and content criticizing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[1] It warned that forcing American companies to censor US residents' speech creates a broader threat to [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] protections.[1]

## Enforcement and foreign coordination

Platforms including [[x-corp|X]] and [[rumble|Rumble]] that refused to comply fully were fined and threatened with — or ordered into — a shutdown of their Brazilian operations.[1] The report also asserted that Brazil's censorship network coordinates with similar foreign regimes and with [[stanford-university|Stanford University]], which it accuses of having laundered US government censorship requests to social media companies during the 2020 election before pivoting toward support for foreign censorship.[1] The committee said it would continue oversight and pursue legislative responses.[1]

## X shutdown standoff (August 2024)

Brazil became the focus of an international free-speech confrontation in August 2024 when [[x-corp|X]] warned that its service in the country could be shut down after Supreme Court Judge [[alexandre-de-moraes|Alexandre de Moraes]] penalized the platform for refusing orders it described as illegal demands to censor his political opponents.[2] X said de Moraes had threatened its local legal representative with imprisonment and frozen her accounts, and that it would not break Brazilian law at his instruction.[2] [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] said [[spacex|SpaceX]] would keep supplying [[starlink|Starlink]] free to Brazilian schools and hospitals despite the dispute.[2]
