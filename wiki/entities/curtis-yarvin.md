# Curtis Yarvin

Curtis Yarvin is an American anti-democracy author and blogger who argues that the United States should be ruled by a monarch, or at minimum by an all-powerful executive seated in the [[white-house|White House]].[1] In May 2025 [[cnn|CNN]] profiled him as a thinker inspiring a new generation of MAGA supporters, with correspondent Hadas Gold interviewing him about his case for concentrated executive power and what the network called his chillingly accurate prediction of how [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] would take aim at private and public institutions.[1]

## Badlands assessment

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] rejected CNN's framing that Yarvin is making waves within MAGA, arguing instead that his influence runs through the [[peter-thiel|Peter Thiel]] and [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] technology contingent and through establishment figures who attached themselves to Trump and MAGA intending to use the movement as a vehicle.[1]

Paul allowed that Yarvin has interesting ideas and is an entertaining writer, but faulted his premises: that the news is real and that history is real, and that solutions can be derived from those foundations.[1] He described Yarvin as a scientific-materialist atheist and the grandson of a communist who Yarvin believes helped "defeat Hitler," concluding that Yarvin is neither right-wing nor dangerous nor edgy but rather a "Very Serious Intellectual normie."[1]

## Consultation with Elon Musk on a third party

In July 2025 The New York Times reported that as [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] studied how to launch a third political party, one of the people from whom he sought advice was Yarvin — described as a right-wing blogger best known for advocating monarchism and as one of the most influential thinkers on the so-called tech right, noted for his distaste for traditional American democracy.[2] The report observed that Yarvin was not an expert on the mechanics of creating third parties or on third-party campaign strategy, yet the two spoke late the prior week about the task ahead, according to two people briefed on the conversation who requested anonymity.[2] A representative for Musk did not immediately respond to a request for comment and Yarvin declined to be interviewed.[2]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] used the report to argue that the Trump–Musk split and the [[america-party|America Party]] project were exposing an anti-Trump, pro-Regime ideology within the technology industry, contending that tech oligarchs pose as libertarian freedom fighters while building tools for top-down control in conjunction with the state's war machine.[2]
