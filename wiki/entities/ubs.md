# UBS

UBS AG is Switzerland's largest bank. In "America for Sale — Part 2" it appears as a case study in how corporations allegedly bought favorable treatment from the United States government through donations to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] and speaking fees to [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Tax evasion case

In February 2009 the IRS sued UBS and demanded it disclose the names of 52,000 possible American tax evaders holding secret Swiss accounts.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) In the months that followed — with the involvement of Secretary of State [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] and Swiss lawmakers — a legal settlement was negotiated, announced on 19 August 2009, under which UBS would pay no fine and provide the IRS with information on 4,450 accounts.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) Separately, UBS agreed to pay a total of $780 million in fines, interest and restitution to avoid U.S. prosecution on charges that it helped thousands of wealthy Americans evade taxes, and to disclose the names of some secret account holders.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

The author frames this as UBS paying both a fine and donations in order to avoid criminal charges, and asks why the Obama [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] would levy so large a penalty if the bank had already bought protection from the Clintons.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Clinton financial ties

Following the settlement, UBS donations to the Clinton Foundation grew from less than $60,000 through 2008 to a cumulative total of about $600,000 by the end of 2014.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) The bank also teamed with the Foundation on the Clinton Economic Opportunity Initiative, creating a pilot entrepreneur program offering $32 million in loans to businesses; other donations included $350,000 in June 2011 and $100,000 for a charity golf tournament.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2) UBS paid Bill Clinton more than $1.5 million in speaking fees between 2001 and 2014.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

[[paul-keary|Paul Keary]] won UBS's business for [[teneo-holdings|Teneo]], which helped arrange the "Revitalizing America" series in which UBS executive Bob McCann appeared on stage with Bill Clinton and [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]] starting in May 2011; Clinton received $1.5 million for his participation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-2)

## Acquisition of Credit Suisse

UBS acquired fellow Swiss bank [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]] in 2023 after the latter faced insolvency, in a transaction Badlands describes as ostensibly forced by the Swiss government.[2] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] ties that insolvency to the massive asset forfeitures that followed the testimony of [[mathias-krull|Mathias Krull]], the banker arrested in 2018 by [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] for laundering billions embezzled from Venezuela's state oil company [[pdvsa|PDVSA]], with the forfeited money owed to the government of Venezuela.[2]

## Nazi-era accounts review

After completing the acquisition in 2023, UBS rehired independent investigator [[neil-barofsky|Neil Barofsky]] — whom Credit Suisse had fired in December 2022 — to continue a voluntary review of the collapsed bank's Nazi-era accounts, following demands for his reinstatement from Senators [[sheldon-whitehouse|Sheldon Whitehouse]] and [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]].[3] The review uncovered 890 previously undisclosed accounts with potential links to the Nazi regime, including accounts held by the German Foreign Office, the SS, and a German arms manufacturer.[3] UBS and Credit Suisse had previously reached a global settlement in 1999 resolving all Nazi-era claims, including future ones, with both banks issuing apologies.[3]

UBS has described the ongoing investigation as a voluntary initiative, with UBS Americas President Robert Karofsky stating that the company accepts and deeply regrets the dark history of Swiss banking during World War II.[3] The probe was expected to conclude by early summer 2026, with a final report anticipated by the end of the year.[3]

## Litigation and regulatory pressure (April 2026)

By April 2026 UBS was facing mounting legal and regulatory pressure tied to allegations involving its acquired unit [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]] and accounts linked to assets from the Nazi era.[4] A US court rejected UBS's attempt to limit its liability, allowing claims to proceed that the bank failed to fully disclose or investigate accounts connected to assets stolen during the Holocaust.[4] Separate reporting raised concerns that prior internal or external investigations into those accounts may have been incomplete or obstructed.[4]

The litigation coincided with broader European regulatory scrutiny following the emergency takeover of Credit Suisse: Swiss regulators weighed tougher capital requirements that could alter the bank's future operations, while the [[bank-of-england|Bank of England]] introduced an alternative bank-resolution ("bail-in") mechanism as part of a wider push to manage systemic risk.[4] The combined effect placed UBS at the center of overlapping legal, historical, and regulatory pressures.[4]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that UBS — which he said was forced by the Swiss government to acquire its main competitor in 2023 — was imitating the exact behavior of Credit Suisse in its final months by blocking investigator [[neil-barofsky|Neil Barofsky]] from obtaining internal documents that could reveal the depth of alleged money-laundering schemes and, by extension, "the international cabal that controls the US government."[4] He noted that a Barofsky report was due to Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] in the Senate later that year.[4]

## Banking relationship with Ghislaine Maxwell

U.S. Justice Department documents reported in February 2026 show that UBS maintained a financial relationship with [[ghislaine-maxwell|Ghislaine Maxwell]] beginning in 2014, shortly after [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] cut ties with her longtime associate [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[5] The bank opened both personal and business accounts for Maxwell and managed up to about $19 million in her assets in the years leading up to her 2020 arrest and 2021 conviction on sex-trafficking charges.[5]

According to the documents, UBS assigned Maxwell two relationship managers and processed significant transactions on her behalf, including a $130,000 transfer on July 22, 2019 — shortly after Epstein's arrest — to help pay a credit card bill.[5] Before UBS took her on as a client, JPMorgan had flagged Maxwell as a "high risk client" because of her links to Epstein and had closed Epstein's own accounts in 2013.[5] UBS also briefly provided a credit card to Epstein in 2014, though internal emails indicate that account was closed later that year.[5]

In August 2019 UBS received a grand jury subpoena for Maxwell's records and complied, providing wire transfer information to the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]].[5] The bank has not publicly confirmed when or if it ended its relationship with Maxwell, and Reuters reported there is no evidence that UBS or its advisers engaged in wrongdoing.[5]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] linked the disclosure to the preceding week's reporting on [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]] Nazi-era and trafficking-linked accounts, noting that UBS was forced to purchase Credit Suisse in 2023 by the Swiss government when insolvency loomed, and remarking that "nothing about any of this feels organic."[5] He connected the banking thread to Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]]'s statement, after meeting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], that the two had discussed joining forces against the bankers who are the "bosses of bosses" behind the cartels.[5]

## Acquisition of Credit Suisse (March 2023)

On March 19, 2023, UBS agreed to buy [[credit-suisse|Credit Suisse]] for more than $2 billion, in a price that escalated during negotiations from CHF 1 billion to CHF 2 billion and ultimately to CHF 3 billion (about US$3.25 billion), or 0.76 per share, with Credit Suisse shareholders receiving one UBS share for every 22.48 shares held.[6] The Swiss National Bank offered UBS 100 billion francs in liquidity assistance and the Swiss government granted a 9 billion-franc guarantee covering potential losses on assets UBS was taking over, an arrangement ZeroHedge described as a taxpayer-backed bailout.[6] The transaction came days after the failure of [[silicon-valley-bank|Silicon Valley Bank]] set off a cascade of banking-sector distress.[6]
