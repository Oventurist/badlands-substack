# Bill Barr

Bill Barr is the former United States Attorney General who appointed [[john-durham|John Durham]] to investigate the origins of the FBI's [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] probe.[1]

## Role in Truth Community criticism of the Durham probe

Within the Truth Community, Barr is a polarizing figure. Following the [[durham-house-judiciary-testimony-2023|June 2023 Durham testimony]], [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described one prominent interpretation of Durham's limited results as the view that "John Durham is just more rotten, spineless fruit from Bill Barr tree," a theory he attributed to figures such as [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]], [[peter-navarro|Steve Navarro]] and [[steve-bannon|Steven Bannon]].[1] In this framing, Barr's appointment of Durham was itself a controlled-opposition maneuver rather than a genuine accountability effort.[1]

## Durham inquiry retrospective

On January 26, 2023 The New York Times published "How Barr's Quest to Find Flaws in the Russia Inquiry Unraveled," a lengthy account of the origins of the special counsel investigation Barr appointed [[john-durham|John Durham]] to conduct.[2] Badlands contributor Just Human read the article as an anonymously sourced attempt to cast Barr and Durham as Donald Trump's "attack dogs" and to persuade readers that the Durham inquiry lacked the legitimate basis the Trump-Russia investigations supposedly had.[2]

## 2020 postal ballot investigation

Badlands commentary states that Barr, as Attorney General, shut down a Senate-led investigation into the alleged transport of completed 2020 ballots from New York to Pennsylvania by [[united-states-postal-service|USPS]] contract driver [[jesse-morgan|Jesse Morgan]].[3] Contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Morgan was mocked and demonized by local and national media and that the story was memory-holed after Barr's intervention, even though a later USPS report was said to confirm that the Postal Service transported ballots across state lines.[3]

## 2024 endorsement of Donald Trump

On April 26, 2024, Barr — who had previously said that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] should not be near the Oval Office — told CNN's Kaitlan Collins on "The Source" that he would vote for the former president over [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in the 2024 election.[5] "I think that Biden is unfit for office," Barr said, adding: "I think Trump would do less damage than Biden, and I think all this stuff about a threat to democracy – I think the real threat to democracy is the progressive movement and the Biden administration."[5] Pressed by Collins on whether he would vote for a man he believed had tried to subvert the peaceful transfer of power and who faced 88 criminal counts, Barr began to dispute the counts before answering, "The answer to the question is yes. I'm supporting the Republican ticket."[5]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] placed the endorsement in a pattern alongside [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] and [[vivek-ramaswamy|Vivek Ramaswamy]] — figures who "fought the man ... and then endorsed him" — arguing that many of the loudest players the mainstream media deploy against Trump from ostensibly within his own movement are playing scripted roles.[5] He described this as [[kayfabe|kayfabe]] and false feuds functioning as narrative deployments in the [[information-war|Information War]], a technique he said had been used to codify the [[controlled-opposition|controlled opposition]] dynamic by pitting tribal binaries against one another.[5]

## Torridon Group expansion (January 2024)

Barr is the co-founder, with former Facebook general counsel [[ted-ullyot|Ted Ullyot]], of the Virginia-based law and consulting firm [[torridon-group|Torridon Group]].[4] In January 2024 he told Axios that the firm would open a Washington office and add a slate of former [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration lawyers, including former White House counsel [[pat-cipollone|Pat Cipollone]] and former deputy White House counsels [[pat-philbin|Pat Philbin]] and [[kate-todd|Kate Todd]], along with [[fred-fielding|Fred Fielding]] and former acting DEA head [[tim-shea|Tim Shea]] as of counsel.[4] Barr said the firm was now equipped for "every facet of litigation, regulatory and enforcement proceedings, investigations, crisis management, and strategic counseling" while remaining a "smaller boutique firm that can give individual attention at reasonable rates."[4]

## House Oversight testimony on Epstein (August 2025)

On Monday, August 18, 2025, Barr told House investigators that he had never seen anything capable of tying President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s crimes, according to [[house-oversight-committee|House Oversight Committee]] Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]].[6] Comer told reporters partway through the deposition that Barr "said that he had never seen anything that would implicate President Trump in any of this, and that he believed if there had been anything pertaining to President Trump with respect to the Epstein list, that he felt like the Biden administration would probably have leaked it out."[6] Barr was the first witness to appear in the committee's probe into the federal government's handling of the Epstein case, and like others subpoenaed after him his deposition was conducted behind closed doors.[6]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that it was unclear why the Barr interview needed to be held in closed session, adding that the choice becomes less murky if the Epstein matter is treated as an ongoing criminal investigation and "crystal clear" if it is understood as part of the Grand Conspiracy.[6]
