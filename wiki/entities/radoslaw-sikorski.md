# Radoslaw Sikorski

Radoslaw "Radek" Sikorski is the Polish Foreign Minister who publicly argued in September 2024 that Ukraine should be permitted to use Western weapons against targets inside Russia.[1]

Sikorski's justification rested on a self-defense and war-crimes argument: "Russia is committing war crimes by attacking civilian targets," he said, adding that "missiles that hit these civilian targets are fired from bomber aircraft from over the territory of Russia. These bombers take off from airfields on Russia's territory."[1] He concluded that "a victim of aggression has the right to defend itself" and that "Ukraine has the right to use Western weapons to prevent war crimes."[1]

His remarks came as U.S. Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] wrapped up a Ukraine-focused European tour in Poland.[1] Badlands rejected Sikorski's framing, arguing that long-range strikes into Russia would themselves risk civilian casualties and that Russia was going out of its way to avoid intentionally harming civilians — a rebuttal it summarized as "two wrongs make a right?"[1]

## Concentration camp artefact auction (November 2025)

As Poland's Deputy Prime Minister, Sikorski announced on Sunday, November 16, 2025, that a planned German auction of artefacts belonging to Nazi concentration camp prisoners had been cancelled following a public outcry.[2] He thanked his German counterpart Johann Wadephul for agreeing that "such a scandal must be prevented," and wrote on X that "respect for victims requires the dignity of silence, not the din of commerce."[2] The [[felzmann-concentration-camp-auction|Auktionshaus Felzmann sale]] in Neuss had listed more than 600 items, including a letter from an Auschwitz prisoner and a medical diagnosis about the forced sterilisation of a Dachau prisoner.[2]

## Airspace warnings, September 2025

In early September 2025 [[poland|Poland]] accused Russia of violating its airspace with a number of drones, and Foreign Minister Sikorski subsequently warned that any intrusions would be met with force.[3] Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] countered that the drones in question could not have reached Polish territory from Russian launch points given their flight range.[3]
