# Tom Homan

Tom Homan is the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] official responsible for directing field-level immigration enforcement and removal operations, reporting directly to [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]].[1]

## Contrast with DHS leadership

During the March 3, 2026 [[senate-judiciary-committee|Senate Judiciary Committee]] hearing on mass deportations, senators of both parties repeatedly invoked Homan's example approvingly while rebuking Homeland Security Secretary [[kristi-noem|Kristi Noem]].[1] Badlands contributor Ashe in America summarized the dynamic by noting that Homan enjoyed "bipartisan" cover for doing the field work of removing criminal entrants while Noem received "bipartisan" evisceration — and that immigration enforcement continued unimpeded throughout.[1]

## Conclusion of Operation Metro Surge (February 2026)

In February 2026, Border Czar Homan announced from [[minnesota|Minnesota]] that [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]] — the federal immigration crackdown that began in December 2025 and produced more than 4,000 arrests in the Minneapolis–St. Paul area — was concluding.[2] He said that while on the ground he had assessed the operation's progress and conducted extensive federal, state, local and community stakeholder outreach to obtain better cooperation and "deescalate the intense, unlawful, and dangerous situation that had unfolded here."[2]

Homan listed four claimed results: [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]]'s target list had been significantly reduced with many public safety threats removed; counties had agreed to an "unprecedented level of coordination" by notifying ICE before releasing criminal aliens from jail; unlawful agitator activity had notably decreased; and state and local law enforcement had stepped up to shut down unlawful actions by agitators.[2] He said he had proposed concluding the surge and that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] concurred, adding that deployed officers would continue enforcement elsewhere in service of border security and mass deportations.[2] Homan thanked ICE and [[customs-and-border-protection|CBP]] personnel and said that "when it comes to border security and immigration enforcement, no President has delivered more, or has done it better, than President Trump."[2]

## Deployment to Minnesota (January 2026)

On January 26, 2026, [[donald-trump|President Trump]] announced that he was sending Border Czar Homan to [[minnesota|Minnesota]] to take charge of federal immigration enforcement operations there.[3] The announcement came the same day that senior Border Patrol commander [[gregory-bovino|Greg Bovino]] was reported — and officially denied — to have been relieved of his duties in [[minneapolis|Minneapolis]], and the same day Trump held a private call with Governor [[tim-walz|Tim Walz]] that both sides described in conciliatory terms.[3] Badlands framed Homan's arrival as part of a continuing federal posture in Minnesota alongside protests and [[operation-metro-surge|Operation Metro Surge]].[3]

## "Rocky Mountain Showdown" with Denver (2026)

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that she had been predicting a "Rocky Mountain Showdown" between Homan and Denver Mayor [[mike-johnston|Mike Johnston]] since 2024, and identified Johnston's [[denver-executive-order-152|Executive Order 152]] — barring federal immigration agents from city property without a judicial warrant — as an appropriate triggering event, while noting the order is largely symbolic because federal supremacy generally prevails in immigration enforcement.[4]

## Response to the arrest of two judges (April 2025)

Following the April 2025 arrests of Milwaukee Circuit Court Judge [[hannah-dugan|Hannah Dugan]] and former New Mexico judge [[joel-cano|Joel Cano]], Homan issued a statement declaring that "Nobody should be surprised by the arrest of two judges."[6] Writing on X, he added: "If you actively impede our enforcement efforts or if you knowingly harbor or conceal illegal aliens from ICE you will be prosecuted."[6]

Homan also featured in the publicity surrounding the [[colorado-springs-nightclub-raid|Colorado Springs nightclub raid]] of April 27, 2025, with Badlands reporting that national social media deployments came from both Homan and Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] alongside local FBI, DEA, and ICE channels.[6]

## Missing migrant children as a priority (2024)

Shortly after being named incoming border czar in November 2024, Homan said he would prioritize locating or rescuing 300,000 unaccounted-for children who had entered the United States as illegal immigrants and were at risk of exploitation.[5] "The third rail is we got over 300,000 missing children," he told Fox News, adding that "over half a million children have been trafficked into the United States. This administration released them to unvetted sponsors, and they can't find 300,000. And based on three-and-a-half decades, some of these children are in forced labor."[5]

His remarks referenced a report by the Department of Homeland Security's inspector general finding that 323,000 illegal immigrant children were unaccounted for inside the United States: as of May 2024, more than 32,000 children served notices to appear in court had not appeared, while the safety of an additional 291,000 could not be verified because they were never placed into removal proceedings.[5] The figures came from Immigration and Customs Enforcement and covered October 2018 to September 2023; the report urged ICE — which Homan had overseen during the first Trump administration — to "take immediate action" to ensure the children were safe.[5]

"We already found some in forced labor, some of them are in for sex trafficking, some of them are with pedophiles," Homan said. "We need to save these children. That's going to be the third rail."[5]

## Standoff with Gavin Newsom (June 2025)

Homan, serving as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s "border czar," became the focus of a public standoff with California Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]] during the Los Angeles immigration-raid protests, with Newsom daring Homan to arrest him.[7] Asked at the White House by Fox News correspondent [[peter-doocy|Peter Doocy]] whether Homan should follow through, Trump endorsed the idea: "I would do it if I were Tom," adding that "Gavin likes the publicity, but I think it would be a great thing."[7] The exchange unfolded alongside [[california-v-trump-administration-national-guard-deployment|California's lawsuit]] over the federalized [[national-guard|National Guard]] deployment, which the state called an "unprecedented power grab."[7]

## "Collateral arrests" and the mass deportation plan (December 2024)

In an interview with the Washington Examiner published December 18, 2024, Homan — then President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s incoming border czar and a former acting director of [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] — said the incoming administration's deportation efforts would produce "collateral arrests."[8] "In sanctuary cities, expect a lot of collateral arrests," he said, explaining that when jurisdictions block jails from transferring criminal illegal immigrants to ICE, officers must find those targets in the community, encountering others in the process.[8]

Homan and other Trump officials said they would prioritize illegal immigrants who had committed crimes or were deemed national security threats, while also pledging to deport anyone residing in the country illegally; Trump had indicated he would consider allowing so-called Dreamers to remain under certain conditions.[8] Homan said enforcement would begin on January 20, 2025, the first day of the administration — "We're starting across the country on the same day" — with roughly 24 ICE field offices covering two or three states each directed to begin locating, arresting, detaining and removing those arrested for crimes.[8] He added that he wanted access to U.S. military aircraft as a "force multiplier" in the deportation effort.[8]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the remarks as a deliberate "Bat Signal" announcing the activation of Trump's "Eisenhower Model" of mass deportation, and as a signal both to the [[devolution|Devo Plan]] and to domestic officials preparing to obstruct enforcement.[8] He cited Denver Mayor [[mike-johnston|Mike Johnston]], who had said he would direct the city's police to resist ICE officials entering the city and was willing to be arrested over it, and who suggested the country might see a new Tiananmen Square moment.[8]

## Referral of Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (February 2025)

In February 2025 Homan, serving as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s border czar, said he had referred New York Representative [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]] to the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] after her office delivered a presentation giving illegal migrants tips on how to avoid U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement.[9] "Is she crossing the line?" Homan asked during a Fox News Channel interview on Thursday evening. "So I'm working with the Department of Justice and finding out, where is that line that they crossed? … I'm not a prosecutor, but we need some further guidance on that."[9] He said he made the referral to the agency's deputy attorney general.[9]

## Appointment as border czar (November 2024)

Shortly before midnight on November 10, 2024, President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted on [[truth-social|Truth Social]] that he had named Homan — the former acting director of [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] and an advocate of Trump's mass deportation plans — as his border czar, a move CNN compared to the late-night drama of Trump's first term.[10] In a recent CBS [[60-minutes|"60 Minutes"]] interview Homan had argued that "families could be deported together" while ruling out mass sweeps of neighborhoods or "concentration camps."[10] CNN reported that the selection was likely to bolster the concerns of Trump's opponents about his hardline intentions, though the president-elect had made no secret of his plans on the campaign trail.[10]

Speaking to "Fox & Friends" host Steve Doocy on November 11, 2024, Homan said the incoming administration would increase worksite raids to address labor and sex trafficking nationwide: "Where do we find most victims of sex trafficking and forced labor trafficking? At worksites."[10] He said Trump's top priority would be national security, and ZeroHedge tied the trafficking crisis to what it described as the open southern border policies of the Biden–Harris administration and to more than ten million unvetted illegal aliens in the country.[10]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] noted that while the public square argued about [[recess-appointments|recess appointments]] and efforts to obstruct the incoming president's agenda, Trump was moving quickly to place personnel ready to execute on day one.[10] She highlighted a Breitbart report that Homan, working within the [[white-house|White House]], would have political rather than legal authority to push the Secretary of DHS and ICE to enforce immigration law and would not require Senate confirmation.[10] She characterized him as treating congressional Democrats with the level of respect she believed they deserved and as eager to secure the border and prosecute child sex trafficking, including the demand side, quoting his line, "I'm so pissed off, sir, I'll come back for free."[10]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] grouped Homan — the "Real Border Czar" — with [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]], [[stephen-miller|Stephen Miller]], [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]] and [[susie-wiles|Susie Wiles]] as the "lesser" names in the second Trump administration that meant little to normies but a great deal to the Truth Community and to the Deep State.[10]
