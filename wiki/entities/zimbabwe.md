# Zimbabwe

Zimbabwe is a southern African country that appears in the July 2024 Badlands News Brief as the regional counterpart to [[zambia|Zambia]] in a story about American military presence and Russian engagement in Africa.[1]

The Brief recalls its prior coverage of a deal struck by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] supplying fertilizer to Zimbabwe, which it credits with producing a historic crop yield for the struggling nation.[1] It further reports that in June 2024 Zimbabwe reached out to Russia over concerns that the United States was attempting to consolidate its military strength in Zambia and to exacerbate a growing rift between the two historically close neighbors.[1]

Zimbabwean President [[emmerson-mnangagwa|Emmerson Mnangagwa]] is quoted remarking, "The United States is making us feel lonely," which the Brief interprets as expressing a perceived encirclement by Western powers.[1] Zimbabwe's stated concerns about [[africom|US Africa Command]] activity are presented as the precursor to Zambia's own call for a US withdrawal.[1]

## Russian fertilizer supplies and SPIEF 2024

In June 2024 President [[emmerson-mnangagwa|Emmerson Mnangagwa]] told [[tass|TASS]] at the [[2024-st-petersburg-international-economic-forum|St. Petersburg International Economic Forum]] that Zimbabwe had received both grain and fertilizer from Russia and expected an incredible harvest as a result, while noting that Russia offered lower prices and more convenient payment methods than any alternative supplier.[2] A humanitarian shipment of 23,000 tons of Uralchem fertilizers had been delivered and transferred to Zimbabwe in March of that year.[2]

## Colonial history in Badlands commentary

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] recounted that Zimbabwe was formerly called Rhodesia, named in 1895 by the British South Africa Company after [[cecil-rhodes|Cecil Rhodes]], whom he described as an agent of the Rothschild banking family dispatched to Africa in the 1880s to build the "Cape to Cairo" railway connecting the north African coast to South Africa.[2] He noted that Rhodesia shared a long border with German East Africa and invited readers to imagine how local populations were treated by British and Prussian prospectors.[2] He further characterized the [[rhodes-scholarship|Rhodes Scholarship]] as a program used to identify and cultivate talent for what he called the globalist mafia, citing Bill Clinton and Pete Buttigieg as alumni.[2] The commentary concluded that Russia continues to gain ground in Africa through such arrangements.[2]
