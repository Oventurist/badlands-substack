# Federal Bureau of Prisons

The Federal Bureau of Prisons (BOP) is the agency of the U.S. [[justice-department|Justice Department]] responsible for the custody of federal inmates and for carrying out federal executions.[1]

## Role in the 2026 execution protocol expansion

In April 2026 the Justice Department said it had directed the Bureau of Prisons to broaden its execution protocols to allow additional methods, including the firing squad, and to readopt the lethal injection protocol used during [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s first administration.[1] Reuters reported the administration also intends to add electrocution and gas asphyxiation as alternative methods.[1] Per Fox News reporting quoted by Badlands, the department is further directing the BOP to examine expanding the federal death row and constructing additional execution facilities.[1]

The Associated Press reported that three inmates remain on federal death row following former President Joe Biden's commutation of 37 death sentences, a figure that frames the scale of the BOP's current capital caseload against the infrastructure buildout it has been asked to study.[1] See [[federal-death-penalty-expansion-2026|Federal Death Penalty Expansion (2026)]].

## Transfer of Caroline Ellison to community confinement, 2025

A Bureau of Prisons spokesperson confirmed in December 2025 that [[caroline-ellison|Caroline Ellison]], former chief executive of [[alameda-research|Alameda Research]], had been transferred on October 16 from the Danbury Federal Correctional Institution in Connecticut to community confinement after serving about 11 months of a two-year sentence.[2] Spokesperson Randilee Giamusso said Ellison remained in federal custody but was now in either home confinement or a halfway house.[2]
