# Mike Huckabee

Mike Huckabee is the United States Ambassador to [[israel|Israel]] during the second administration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]. In February 2026 he became the focus of a region-wide diplomatic controversy after remarks he made about Israeli territorial claims in a televised interview.[1]

## The Tucker Carlson interview and "Nile to the Euphrates" remarks

In a televised interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], Huckabee said that Israel has a biblical right to a large swath of the Middle East, referencing land "from the Nile to the Euphrates," and added that "it would be fine if they took it all." In the same interview he also said that Israel is not currently seeking to expand its borders.[1] The remarks were widely read as an endorsement of the [[greater-israel|Greater Israel]] concept, and Badlands commentary treated them as an unusually explicit articulation of maximalist territorial ideology by a sitting American envoy.[1]

## Regional backlash

Arab and Muslim governments and regional bodies publicly condemned the remarks in statements over the weekend of February 21–23, 2026, including [[saudi-arabia|Saudi Arabia]], Egypt, Jordan, and others, as well as the League of Arab States ([[arab-league|Arab League]]) and the [[organization-of-islamic-cooperation|Organization of Islamic Cooperation]].[1] Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry said it "categorically" denounced Huckabee's comments and called for clarification, describing the remarks as contrary to international law, the UN Charter, and diplomatic norms.[1]

A US Embassy spokesperson responded that Huckabee's comments did not reflect any change in US policy, and said his full remarks made clear that Israel had no desire to change its current boundaries.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that the Saudi statement was co-signed by countries from every continent, spanning NATO, BRICS, the European Union, the Arab League, the 57-nation Organization of Islamic Cooperation, and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization, arguing that Huckabee had succeeded in uniting not only the Muslim world but seemingly the entire free world against Israel.[1] The Badlands framing set the Huckabee affair alongside statements by Israeli Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] and opposition figures [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] and [[naftali-bennett|Naftali Bennett]] as evidence of an Israeli political class working at cross purposes with President Trump's regional peace efforts.[1]

## Denials of attacks on Christians

In May 2026 commentary on rising settler violence in Israel, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that Huckabee, as US Ambassador to Israel, had for months been "trotted out by the Israelis" to deny that attacks against Christians were occurring, and predicted he would continue ignoring mounting evidence and newly reported incidents.[2] The writer called Huckabee "a tremendous coward who is devoid of any principles" who had "chosen to turn his back on Jesus and on America."[2] The remarks came in the context of the reported attack on a French nun in Jerusalem and the broader phenomenon described as [[jewish-terrorism|Jewish terrorism]].[2]

## Exchange with the Jerusalem patriarchs (January 2026)

On January 17, 2026, the [[patriarchs-and-heads-of-the-churches-in-jerusalem|Patriarchs and Heads of the Churches in Jerusalem]] issued a statement describing [[christian-zionism|Christian Zionism]] as a "damaging" ideology that misleads the public, sows confusion, and harms the unity of their flock, and which had "found favor among certain political actors in Israel and beyond."[3] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the letter as condemning political leaders such as Huckabee who use their government positions to promote war and undermine peace.[3]

Huckabee responded on X, writing that he loved and respected his "brothers and sisters in Christ from traditional, liturgical churches" but did not believe any sect should "claim exclusivity in speaking for Christians worldwide or assume there is only one viewpoint regarding faith in the Holy Land."[3] He argued that "if God can or would break His covenant with the Jews, then what hope would Christians have that He would keep His covenant with us?" and said labels such as "Christian Zionism" are "too often used in a pejorative manner to disparage free-church believers, of which there are millions across the planet."[3] Defining a Zionist as one who "simply accepts that the Jewish people have a right to live in their ancient, indigenous, and Biblical homeland," he said it was hard for him to understand why anyone calling themselves Christian would not also be a Zionist.[3] Badlands described the episode as an awkward situation for the pastor-turned-ambassador.[3]

## April 2026 criticism

In the April 20, 2026 Brief, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] named Huckabee among the "Christians" running cover for [[israel|Israel]] amid what he described as an explosion of [[jewish-terrorism|Jewish terrorism]], including the [[debel-statue-desecration|desecration of a statue of Jesus]] by an Israeli soldier in southern [[lebanon|Lebanon]].[4]

## Rebuke over the Palm Sunday closure (March 2026)

When [[israeli-police|Israeli police]] blocked Catholic clergy from [[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre|the Church of the Holy Sepulchre]] on [[palm-sunday|Palm Sunday]] 2026, Huckabee publicly criticized the decision, saying that "for the Patriarch to be barred from entry to the Church on Palm Sunday for a private ceremony is difficult to understand or justify," and adding that Israel had indicated it would work with the Patriarch to accommodate a safe means of carrying out [[holy-week|Holy Week]] activities.[5] He noted that Home Front Command guidelines restricted gatherings to 50 people or fewer and that the four Catholic representatives were well below that limit.[5] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that the episode was egregious enough that "even Mike Huckabee had to rebuke it," while noting wryly that his statement read like an appeal to COVID-style restrictions as grounds for Israel to relent.[5]

## Tucker Carlson airport dispute (February 2026)

In February 2026 Huckabee publicly disputed [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s claim that he had been detained and interrogated at Ben Gurion Airport while departing [[israel|Israel]], stating that Carlson was not detained or interrogated beyond routine airport procedures.[6] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had deliberately engineered the surrounding controversy by making Huckabee — who has maintained throughout his life that Palestine does not exist and that the land has always been Israel — the first major appointment of his administration, a choice that inflamed [[nick-fuentes|Nick Fuentes]] and the [[groypers|Groypers]].[6]

## Carlson interview and the "Nile to Euphrates" remark

In an interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] conducted in Israel and aired on February 20, 2026, Huckabee said it "would be fine" if Israel controlled the lands described in Genesis 15:18 — a swath running from the Nile to the Euphrates and encompassing much of the modern Middle East — though he added that Israel is not seeking to expand into neighboring countries and is focused on peace and security within its current borders.[7] The scriptural question was raised by Carlson, and the remark set off a diplomatic backlash.[7]

Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said the interview left him convinced Huckabee "seems like a bad guy, full stop," and announced a special stream with [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] to cover it in full.[7] He faulted Huckabee for defending [[tom-alexandrovich|Tom Alexandrovich]] — the Israel National Cyber Directorate cyber-defense chief arrested in Las Vegas in August 2025 over an alleged attempt to meet a 15-year-old girl, who fled to Israel after release on bail — as "innocent until proven guilty" while declining to promise to seek his extradition.[7]

He listed further objections: Huckabee justifying the killing of children forcibly recruited into Hamas, asserting the IDF is a more ethical military than the U.S. military, failing to give a true definition of [[christian-zionism|Christian Zionism]], and failing to address crimes against Christians in the Holy Land that Carlson repeatedly raised.[7] The territorial remark, he argued, was the greatest mistake, since suggesting such a map was [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]]'s endgame had until recently been treated as antisemitic.[7] He closed by thanking the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], describing Muslim partners as level-headed people who do not react to "the fanaticism of radicalized zealots like Huckabee."[7]

## Jerusalem News Syndicate summit (June 2026)

Huckabee spoke at the [[jerusalem-news-syndicate|Jerusalem News Syndicate]]'s 2026 International Policy Summit, where he threw shade at President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over the president's social media habits.[8] Badlands Media described the appearance as one of the more notable moments in a broader pattern of the GOP, what it called Mossad Media, and Israel's proxies attacking Trump and the peace deal he signed with [[iran|Iran]].[8] The outlet argued that Huckabee's apparent concern about being fired stemmed from his having made it known that he stands with [[israel|Israel]] against the Trump administration and against those who question the U.S.–Israel relationship.[8]

## F-35 remarks to Israeli media (2026)

Amid the early-2026 Iran standoff, Huckabee — U.S. Ambassador to [[israel|Israel]] — spoke to Israeli media and assured Israelis that [[turkey|Turkey]] would never receive the F-35 fighter jets [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had promised them, because the Deep State would block approval of the sale in [[congress|Congress]].[9] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the statement as subversion of the president and called Huckabee a traitor to America, framing his prominence as useful in exposing how NeoCons and Zionists in the administration would wield power.[9]

## Cited over Palestinian nationhood dispute (November 2025)

After Israeli security minister [[itamar-ben-gvir|Itamar Ben-Gvir]] declared in November 2025 that "there is no such thing as 'Palestinian people,'" Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that this was "the exact position that US Ambassador Mike Huckabee has been espousing for decades."[10] He added that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s choice of Huckabee as ambassador "at this moment in time is very interesting," and called the underlying claim projection given that most Israelis are of Eastern European descent with Turkic heritage.[10]

## Confirmation hearings and the Qatar divide (March 2025)

Senate confirmation hearings for Huckabee as Ambassador to Israel began in late March 2025.[11] Coverage framed the former Arkansas governor as an Evangelical Christian whose steadfast support for Israel in the battle against terror stood in stark contrast to the pro-[[qatar|Qatar]] strategy pursued by President Trump's chief negotiator [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], who had praised Qatar's role in conversation with Tucker Carlson and called claims that Qatar was "not well motivated" preposterous.[11]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that Huckabee's appointment was intended to send signals, particularly to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], given Huckabee's views on Palestine, leaving the Israeli prime minister with a false sense of confidence as he pressed forward with the war.[11] He contended that by appointing both Huckabee and Witkoff — two opposing sides of an issue already defining Trump's second term — the president deliberately set the MAGA movement on a path of self-reflection, division and intense debate.[11] Collin McMahon's Gateway Pundit article, quoted in the brief, described the resulting split as one between "a New York Jew, Witkoff, who seems comfortable with Qatar and [[hamas|Hamas]], and a Christian Zionist ambassador, Huckabee, who recognizes that Hamas must be destroyed to reach any sort of peace in Gaza."[11]

## Objections to Witkoff's Hamas contacts (April 2025)

In April 2025 Badlands reported that Huckabee, as US Ambassador to Israel, had said he shared the resentment felt by other senior officials toward [[steve-witkoff|Steve Witkoff]], attributing his own objection to what he saw as Witkoff's cavalier attitude about meeting with [[hamas|Hamas]].[12] The account grouped Huckabee with Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], [[keith-kellogg|Keith Kellogg]] and [[mike-waltz|Mike Waltz]] as figures uneasy with the special envoy's independent diplomacy.[12]

## Confrontation with Israel over Christian visas

In July 2025 Huckabee, serving as U.S. Ambassador to Israel, wrote a scathing letter to Interior Minister Moshe Arbel blasting his office for refusing to grant visas to American Christian groups seeking to visit Israel, as reported by Channel 12.[13] Huckabee — a Baptist minister and longtime supporter of Israel — expressed "profound disappointment" that an earlier meeting with Arbel on the issue had produced no improvement, writing that "the situation has actually gotten worse."[13] He stressed that Evangelical Christian groups are friends of Israel but were being treated "like enemies."[13]

Huckabee threatened reciprocal steps, warning that the embassy would be forced to announce publicly that Israel does not welcome Christian groups and harasses them instead, and would advise American Christians that their support was being met with hostility and that tourists should reconsider their travel plans.[13] Badlands commentary cited the letter as evidence that Christians were being hunted in the Holy Land, linking the pattern to the Lehava movement and the legacy of Meir Kahane, and argued that Evangelical supporters of Zionism would need to reassess the story they had been told about the modern State of Israel.[13]
