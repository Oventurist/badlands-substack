# Cosmos Mystery Area

The Cosmos Mystery Area is a roadside tourist attraction in the Black Hills of South Dakota, visited by a group of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] figures during the fifth [[gart|Great American Restoration Tour]] gathering in Deadwood in mid-August 2024.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi) The site presents itself as a location where "gravity works differently," and the Badlands account treats it as a working demonstration of multi-sensory deception rather than a genuine physical anomaly.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi)

## Origin story

According to the attraction's own promotional narrative, "In 1952, two college boys searched the Black Hills looking for the perfect spot to build a summer cabin," stumbled into "the mysterious world of the Cosmos," camped out while investigating the odd phenomena, concluded there was "something of interest to the general public," and repaired the cabin to make it safe — producing the demonstrations shown to visitors today.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi)

## The tour

Admission at the time of the visit was $15 for a roughly 40-minute guided tour, led by what the author describes as a bubbly teenage guide with quick jokes and practiced pitches for tips.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi) The tour opens with an optical illusion about relative height using volunteers from the group, which immediately destabilises visitors' perception of who among them is taller.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi)

A short hike up the hill passes trees described as sporadic gnarly messes of branches growing in illogical directions amid otherwise perfectly straight ponderosa pines, explained to visitors with the refrain "It's the cosmic energy, man."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi) Walking around a fence wall into the courtyard of one of the property's two cabins, visitors' equilibrium distorts sharply; the author reports standing upright but off balance, with his eyes searching for clues.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi)

Demonstrations include a tennis ball — checked by a volunteer for magnets or rigging — placed at the bottom of a ramp, which rolls upward, and water that flows up the ramp and off the side into the dust.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi) Inside the cabin's two rooms, guardrails line the walls and the guide repeatedly asks visitors whether they feel dizzy; a hanging bar in the first room pulls the body toward the front of the cabin when the visitor tries to hang straight down, and the second room features another ramp running up two sides like an old-school "mouse trap" game, on which balls again roll upward.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi) On exiting, the party began to feel normal again almost instantly, though the short walk back down the hill remained harder than it should have been.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi)

## Explanation and significance

The article characterises the attraction as "fake but still fascinating," and as a perfect metaphor for navigating the [[information-war|info war]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi) It argues that the mystery begins with a little bit of truth: real anomalies in the earth's magnetic field exist, such as the South Atlantic Anomaly — described as the area where the Van Allen radiation belt comes closest to the Earth's surface, causing "an increased flux of energetic particles and a weaker magnetic field" — and reported magnetic anomalies on the ocean floor said to have helped scientists understand plate tectonics.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi) Because science allows that such phenomena can occur, non-scientists become more willing to accept that one is occurring in front of them, an effect the article links to the injunction to "trust the experts."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi) The guide's repeated cosmic-energy and anti-gravity messaging then reinforces the belief, illustrating the William James line quoted in the piece: "There is nothing so absurd that it cannot be believed as truth if repeated often enough."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi)

The decisive element, the author argues, is physiological cooperation: the eyes report that a sloped surface is flat and that down is up, and the brain then forces the body to hang or stand at the angle the eyes insist is vertical, producing genuine discomfort.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi) The illusions are attributed to an obstructed horizon combined with a deceptively designed physical environment and intentional disinformation, since without a horizon a person cannot reliably judge the slope of a surface and at the Cosmos the available reference points are deliberately unreliable.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi) A 2003 group of scientists who recreated cosmos-style areas concluded that "antigravity-hill effects follow from a misperception of the eye level relative to gravity, caused by the presence of either contextual inclines or a false horizon line."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi)

The author closes by recommending a trip to the Cosmos to readers, noting that Badlands would return to Deadwood the following summer.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-mysterious-masterclass-in-multi)
