# Ontario Human Rights Commission

The Ontario Human Rights Commission (OHRC) is the Canadian provincial body whose proceedings resulted in the township of Emo, Ontario and its mayor [[harold-mcquaker|Harold McQuaker]] being fined for declining to proclaim Pride Month or fly a Pride flag.[1]

## The Emo case

McQuaker was ordered by the OHRC to pay damages after the township failed to proclaim Pride Month as instructed back in 2020, following a request from the advocacy group [[borderland-pride|Borderland Pride]] that Emo declare June as Pride Month and fly an "LGBTQ2 rainbow flag for a week of your choosing," despite the township lacking an official flagpole.[1] Emo's refusal sparked a years-long arbitration process in which the tribunal found against the township.[1] The commission additionally directed McQuaker to undertake LGBTQ2S+ "reeducation" training as an act of personal contrition, an order he rejected along with the $5,000 penalty, which he called "extortion."[1]

Badlands commentary characterized the OHRC's actions as state enforcement of an official belief system and part of a broader pattern in which "only state approved religious messaging is allowed in the public spaces."[1]

## Definition of gender identity

The OHRC's policy on preventing discrimination because of gender identity and gender expression defines gender identity as "each person's internal and individual experience of gender... their sense of being a woman, a man, both, neither, or anywhere along the gender spectrum," which "may be the same as or different from their birth-assigned sex" and is "fundamentally different from a person's sexual orientation."[2]

## Role in national policy

A February 2024 Badlands Media essay by [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] credits the government of [[ontario|Ontario]] and the commission's policy work with driving the 2016 federal amendment under [[justin-trudeau|Justin Trudeau]] that made [[gender-ideology|gender ideology]] a national legal framework across [[canada|Canada]], including treating the use of wrong pronouns as a crime.[2] See [[bill-c-16|Bill C-16]] and the [[canadian-human-rights-act|Canadian Human Rights Act]].[2] The essay argues that laws requiring citizens to affirm others' gender identity on pain of hate-crime accusation are extreme concepts most people do not hold.[2]
