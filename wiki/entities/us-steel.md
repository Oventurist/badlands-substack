# US Steel

US Steel is the historic American steel producer at the center of a contested acquisition attempt by Japan's [[nippon-steel|Nippon Steel]].[1]

## Blocked and restructured acquisition

The Nippon Steel bid for US Steel was blocked in January by President Joe Biden. [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] subsequently inverted the outcome through renegotiation; Badlands commentary characterizes the final structure as one in which Nippon Steel effectively answers to US Steel and the United States government, rather than US Steel passing into foreign ownership.[1]

The episode is treated in Badlands commentary as part of a broader realignment of the US–Japan economic relationship, alongside the critical minerals agreement signed during Trump's Tokyo visit and the arrival of Prime Minister [[sanae-takaichi|Sanae Takaichi]].[1]

## Origins

US Steel was founded as a holding company in 1903 by [[jp-morgan|J.P. Morgan]], who used it as a vehicle to buy up essentially every steel business in the United States — most of them then located in Trenton, New Jersey — before relocating most operations to Pittsburgh, which is how that city became known as "The Steel City."[2]

## Collapse of the $14.9 billion Nippon sale (February 2025)

The battle over the company's future escalated on Monday, February 10, 2025, when activist investor [[ancora-holdings-group|Ancora Holdings Group LLC]] called for a major leadership shake-up following the collapse of the proposed $14.9 billion sale to [[nippon-steel|Nippon Steel Corp.]][2] In a February 10 letter to the US Steel Board of Directors, Ancora executives Fredrick DiSanto and James Chadwick wrote that "President Trump's remarks on Friday should confirm — once and for all — that the sale has no chance of being resurrected," referring to a February 7 press conference [[donald-trump|Trump]] held after his briefing with Japanese Prime Minister [[shigeru-ishiba|Shigeru Ishiba]] in Washington, D.C.[2] Ancora praised Trump's stance on protecting American manufacturing and accused CEO [[david-burritt|David Burritt]] of leading the company "off a cliff" in pursuit of a $72 million transaction-related payout.[2]

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that 122 years after its founding "the heirs of the company are every bit the vultures as its founders," seeking to sell off the final pieces of the American steel industry to a foreign corporation, and noted that [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s blocking of the sale in January gave Trump time to negotiate an alternative in which foreign investment, rather than a buyout, would flow into America's manufacturing core.[2]
