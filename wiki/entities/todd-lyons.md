# Todd Lyons

Todd Lyons is a United States immigration enforcement official who, as of March 2026, was serving as the senior official performing the duties of the director of [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|Immigration and Customs Enforcement]] (ICE).[1]

## Role in the DOJ–DHS voter data initiative

Lyons was identified in reporting on a prospective data-sharing arrangement between the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] and the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] as the official expected to submit the formal request for access to state voter registration data.[1] Under the reported plan, voter roll data collected from the states by the DOJ's Civil Rights Division would be provided to ICE's Homeland Security Investigations component, with the stated goal of identifying non-citizens who may be registered to vote or who may have voted unlawfully in past elections.[1]

Officials were also reported to be weighing construction of a system permitting federal investigators to cross-reference voter registration records directly against DHS immigration databases through query-based matching.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the initiative as an unremarkable use of government data, arguing that the notion that the federal government cannot identify election, entitlement, or immigration fraud from its own datasets is untenable, and noting that states objecting to the arrangement had previously shared data freely with federal partners.[1]

## Contempt proceedings in Minnesota

In January 2026, Lyons was ordered by Chief U.S. District Judge [[patrick-schiltz|Patrick Schiltz]] to appear in person in Minneapolis federal court on January 30, 2026, at 1:00 p.m. to show cause why he should not be held in contempt of court.[2] The order followed ICE's failure to comply with a January 14, 2026 directive requiring a bond hearing or release within seven days for detainee [[juan-tobay-robles|Juan Tobay Robles]].[2]

Schiltz described the violation as part of "one of dozens of court orders" ignored by ICE in recent weeks, writing that "The Court's patience is at an end... the extent of ICE's violation of court orders is likewise extraordinary, and lesser measures have been tried and failed."[2] The hearing was to be canceled if Robles were released beforehand.[2] The order came amid escalating tensions in Minneapolis over federal immigration enforcement, including the fatal shooting of [[alex-jeffrey-pretti|Alex Jeffrey Pretti]] by Border Patrol agents on January 24, 2026.[2]
