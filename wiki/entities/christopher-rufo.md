# Christopher Rufo

Christopher Rufo is described by Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] as one of the more influential right-wing personalities in American public life.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

On December 20, 2021, Rufo tweeted in response to the firing of Tony Kinnett, an Indianapolis teacher dismissed after posting a video about critical race theory being taught in public schools: "Outrageous: this brave teacher stood against critical race theory indoctrination in his school district—and they fired him."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) DeLarme reads the tweet as highlighting the absurdity of a school terminating an employee for holding a viewpoint on a contentious issue shared by roughly half the nation.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

DeLarme states that he completely agrees with Rufo — along with [[ben-shapiro|Ben Shapiro]] and [[bari-weiss|Bari Weiss]] — at least in these instances, holding that people should be allowed opinions no matter how unpalatable and that it is dangerous for employers to impose beliefs on employees under threat of firing.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard) The example serves in the essay as a benchmark against which the right's silence over the later firings of Israel critics is measured.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-glaring-double-standard)

## Bessent interview on federal fraud (January 2026)

In January 2026, Rufo interviewed Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] in a discussion about the [[doge|Department of Government Efficiency]] and its status, during which Bessent said that roughly 10% of the US federal budget is lost to fraud.[2] The interview was cited in Badlands coverage as evidence that DOGE had not been dissolved despite reports of its demise.[2]

## Harvard plagiarism reporting (2023)

Rufo, with Christopher Brunet, broke the story alleging that [[harvard-university|Harvard]] President [[claudine-gay|Claudine Gay]] had plagiarized portions of her dissertation.[3] He subsequently interviewed political scientist [[carol-swain|Carol Swain]], one of the scholars whose work was allegedly copied, who told him that Gay's "whole research agenda, her whole career, was based on my work."[3]
