# Johnnie Kallas

Johnnie Kallas is a labor researcher whose strike data was cited in coverage of the 2023 American strike wave. At the time he was a PhD candidate at [[cornell-university-school-of-industrial-and-labor-relations|Cornell University's School of Industrial and Labor Relations]] and the project director of the [[ilr-labor-action-tracker|ILR Labor Action Tracker]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

## Strike data, 2023

Kallas's figures, reported by CNBC and quoted by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], recorded 312 strikes involving roughly 453,000 workers in the United States over the first portion of 2023, against 180 strikes involving 43,700 workers over the same period two years earlier.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike) That roughly tenfold jump in participating workers was the statistical backbone of the argument that the year's labor unrest was unprecedented in recent memory, even as it competed for attention with new wars, a House leadership crisis and the escalating weaponization of government.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)

In the Badlands treatment by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], the Kallas numbers frame a wider skepticism: that although the strikes were well-coordinated, on message and popularly supported, employer behavior was governed by "black or red economics" and by a decade of investment in automation, robotics and artificial intelligence that had already shrunk the number of jobs available.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-time-to-strike)
