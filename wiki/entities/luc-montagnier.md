# Luc Montagnier

Luc Montagnier was a prominent French virologist who, in the earliest phase of the coronavirus outbreak, publicly assessed that the apparent gene sequencing of SARS-CoV-2 implied human handiwork rather than natural evolution.[1] He is cited alongside [[judy-mikovits|Judy Mikovits]] and bioweapons expert [[francis-boyle|Francis Boyle]] as among the first credentialed figures to join Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesman [[zhao-lijian|Zhao Lijian]] in disputing the natural-origin narrative promoted by the [[world-health-organization|WHO]], Nature Magazine and editors at [[the-lancet|The Lancet]].[1]

Montagnier's position formed part of a broader body of dissenting scientific opinion which held that the virus had originated in a laboratory, while leaving open the crucial unresolved questions of which laboratory — Chinese or American-controlled — and whether any release was accidental or deliberate.[1] In the account given by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], the pooling of such expert assessments established the plausibility of a laboratory origin well before the [[lab-leak-theory|Wuhan lab leak hypothesis]] was adopted and amplified by western intelligence-linked promoters such as [[richard-dearlove|Sir Richard Dearlove]].[1]
