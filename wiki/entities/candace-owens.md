# Candace Owens

Candace Owens is an American conservative commentator. In October 2022 she appeared alongside [[kanye-west|Kanye West]] at Paris Fashion Week wearing shirts reading "White Lives Matter," an event that [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] identified as the trigger for the intense backlash that preceded West's "Defcon Three" remarks and subsequent cancellation campaign.[1] Badlands characterized the stunt as an attempt to highlight what it described as psychological warfare operations against white populations and to assert that all identity groups should be valued equally.[1]

## Robinson hearing dispute (2026)

In July 2026 Owens was at the center of a public rupture among prominent conservatives after evidence from [[tyler-robinson|Tyler Robinson]]'s preliminary hearing was said to undercut her claims of a conspiracy in the murder of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]].[2] Owens had argued that Robinson did not act alone, implicating [[israel|Israel]], Egypt, the US military, [[turning-point-usa|Turning Point USA]] staff, and Kirk's widow [[erika-kirk|Erika Kirk]].[2]

[[dinesh-dsouza|Dinesh D'Souza]] called her a "malignant narcissist," while [[ben-shapiro|Ben Shapiro]], [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]], Catturd and Lauren Southern also condemned her; [[politico|Politico]] reported that TPUSA staff received death threats and increased security after her broadcasts named them.[2] Owens responded on [[x|X]] alleging a coordinated "Zionist PR" campaign against her, and her spokesperson accused TPUSA figures of targeting her for months.[2] [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] and [[megyn-kelly|Megyn Kelly]] faced backlash for standing by her.[2] Badlands noted that a video Owens played on her show as newly revealing evidence was found by [[canncon|CannCon]] to be the same footage already captured in the Court TV stream.[2]

## Politico investigation of the TPUSA feud (July 2026)

A [[politico|Politico]] Magazine report published July 11, 2026 documented Owens' intensifying public campaign questioning the circumstances of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]]'s killing and accusing people associated with [[turning-point-usa|Turning Point USA]] of withholding information.[3] Owens, who worked for Turning Point from 2017 to 2019, said on her podcast that [[tyler-robinson|Tyler Robinson]] did not act alone and suggested that Turning Point employees, Israel and Kirk's widow [[erika-kirk|Erika Kirk]] deserved scrutiny; the article did not present evidence establishing those allegations.[3]

Politico reported that Turning Point increased security at its Phoenix headquarters and at several employees' homes after staff received threats following Owens' broadcasts, relying on three anonymous people described as having direct knowledge of the arrangements.[3] Contracted camera operator [[terryl-farnsworth|Terryl Farnsworth]] and his family went into hiding for more than a week after he received multiple death threats, according to four anonymous sources cited by the outlet.[3]

Turning Point served Owens with a cease-and-desist letter in January 2026, a copy of which she displayed on her show; the letter demanded that she stop suggesting the organization knew about Kirk's assassination beforehand, participated in it or concealed the truth afterward.[3] Kirk's former bodyguard filed a defamation lawsuit against Owens in May, and that same month a man was arrested for allegedly threatening to kill Erika Kirk before a Turning Point event in Texas.[3] As preliminary proceedings in Robinson's case began, Erika Kirk's attorney asked the court to make evidence presented at the hearing public, arguing that greater transparency could reduce speculation and conspiracy theories surrounding the killing.[3]

## The Kirk donor text messages (October 2025)

In October 2025 Owens published text messages attributed to [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] and sent two days before he was assassinated in Utah, and [[turning-point-usa|Turning Point USA]] subsequently confirmed the messages were genuine.[4] Owens had consistently suggested a role for the Israel lobby in Kirk's death, and on her show revealed group chats in which Kirk said he had lost a major Jewish donor.[4] The quoted messages included Kirk writing, "Just lost another huge Jewish donor. $2 million a year because we won't cancel Tucker," and, "Jewish donors play into all the stereotypes. I cannot and will not be bullied like this. Leaving me no choice but to leave the pro-Israel cause."[4]

Turning Point spokesman [[andrew-kolvet|Andrew Kolvet]] confirmed the chats had taken place, said he had not published them because they were private but had informed government officials, and rejected Owens' suggestion that Turning Point did not care about why Kirk was killed.[4] Turning Point also confirmed that Kirk had written a letter to Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] warning that he was losing the public battle in the United States, with Kolvet adding that Kirk was not someone who could be controlled.[4]

Badlands commentary on the episode divided: [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] derided the layered confirmations as "a game of telephone featuring gossip from and about TV Characters," while [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] treated the messages as "very damning" evidence, noting that the group text included both Jewish and non-Jewish donors and that one participant had reacted to Kirk's threat to invite Owens to the next conference by calling free will a "nasty" thing.[4]

## December 2025 meeting with Erika Kirk

On Monday, December 15, 2025, after weeks of tension, Owens met with widow and TPUSA CEO [[erika-kirk|Erika Kirk]] for a session she described as "an extremely productive 4 1/2 hour meeting that I think we both feel should have taken place a lot earlier than it did."[5] Owens said the two "agreed much more than I had anticipated," while also disagreeing "on various points and people as well," and that they were able to "share intel and clarify intent."[5] She promised a full rundown the following day, saying she was exhausted but wanted to report that "absolutely nothing was held back and the immediate result was that tensions were thawed."[5] Badlands commentary framed the rapprochement as "kumbaya" staged ahead of TPUSA's convention that weekend, with [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] asking why, if the sequence were not scripted, it was so easy to state in advance exactly what the participants would do.[5]

## Criticism of RFK Jr.

In March 2025 Owens criticized [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]]'s focus on antisemitism, suggesting it distracted from other issues — including an alleged affair and, in her framing, the influence of blackmail on his political decisions.[6] Her comments came as Kennedy faced reports of a scandal involving allegations of sexual misconduct and political intrigue.[6]


## Meeting with Erika Kirk (December 2025)
Turning Point USA abruptly postponed a highly anticipated livestream intended to rebut Owens' claims about Charlie Kirk's assassination, with Erika Kirk announcing she would instead meet Owens privately on Monday, December 15, 2025.[7] "Candace Owens and I are meeting for a private, in-person discussion on Monday, December 15," Erika Kirk posted on X, adding that public discussions, livestreams and tweets were on hold until after the meeting; Owens replied, "I am very much looking forward to this discussion."[7]

Owens began spreading theories about Kirk's death soon after his September 10 assassination, leading an investigation on her podcast that quickly gained millions of followers, and her claims became one of the most radioactive flashpoints in MAGA's sprawling civil war, forcing other influencers to take positions and reshaping alliances across conservative media.[7] Badlands contributor Jordan Sather noted the beef appeared to be settling "maybe," while Chris Paul predicted "scripted, coordinated messaging to begin shortly after," and Burning Bright described 2025 as "the year of the Sifting," warning that audiences who had escaped the left-right paradigm would simply be sorted into new ones — MAGA vs. America First, NeoCons vs. Traditional Conservatives, Q vs. Truther.[7]

## Feud with Ben Shapiro over Israel (November 2023)

In November 2023 Owens, then host of the "Candace Owens" talk show at [[daily-wire|The Daily Wire]], escalated a public feud with co-founder [[ben-shapiro|Ben Shapiro]] over [[israel|Israel]]'s war against [[hamas|Hamas]] in [[gaza|Gaza]], calling him "emotionally unhinged" and "unprofessional."[8] The exchange followed Shapiro's post on X telling her, "Candace, if you feel that taking money from The Daily Wire somehow comes between you and God, by all means quit," itself a response to a post in which Owens quoted a verse from the Book of Matthew.[8] Shapiro, though a co-founder, co-owner and "editor emeritus," has said he lacks the authority to fire her; hiring and firing power rests with Daily Wire CEO [[caleb-robinson|Caleb Robinson]].[8]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the episode as the start of a "[[con-inc|Con Inc.]] circular firing squad" unfolding amid the [[uniparty|Uniparty]]'s push for domestic support for a new Forever War, suggesting Owens' turn against her own camp could indicate "patriot assets being activated," or simply truth finding its own level before an increasingly bipartisan audience.[8]


## Feud with Tim Pool (December 2025)

In December 2025 Owens escalated a public feud with podcast host [[tim-pool|Tim Pool]] after Pool accused her of benefiting from the murder of Charlie Kirk.[9] Responding to Pool's on-air rant, Owens said his behavior was "not a display of testosterone" but "actually a display of estrogen," adding, "He's not a man, he's not tough. And he would never dare speak to a man like that."[9]

She further called Pool "a coward" and "less than a man," likening him to "a person who is having financial issues" who gambles, loses, "comes back home and beats his wife and kids," and told him to "get [his] affairs in order" and stop blaming her, noting that she had not focused her content on him while "you have been explicitly attacking me."[9]

## Break with DeSantis (June 2023)

In late June 2023, Owens publicly abandoned [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] and returned her support to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], a shift first flagged within Badlands by [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] and characterized by [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] as "massive."[10] Burning Bright argued that losing Owens amounted to a death blow to even the public facade of the DeSantis campaign, framing it within months of Badlands commentary about the "wolves in sheep's clothing" represented by [[con-inc|Con Inc.]] and the so-called Blue Check Brigade — the paid influencer network that, in his telling, rather than DeSantis himself, had fallen into Trump's trap.[10] He presented the episode as cultural house-cleaning en route to a 2024 presidential campaign that would make the MAGA rhetoric of 2016 look like child's play.[10]

## Feud with Alex Jones (December 2025)

[[alex-jones|Alex Jones]] escalated his conflict with Owens in late December 2025, telling her "I want your war" and accusing her followers of threatening and doxxing him.[11] Jones had publicly broken with Owens earlier in the month over what he called easily refutable accusations she made relating to the murder of [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]], vowing to "rip to pieces" everything she says and does, and subsequently calling her "a globalist agent."[11]

## Paris "White Lives Matter" appearance (October 2022)

Owens appeared alongside [[kanye-west|Ye (Kanye West)]] at the Yeezy Season 9 fashion show in Paris on October 3, 2022, where the two posed for photographers wearing T-shirts reading "White Lives Matter."[12] Badlands Media's December 2023 retrospective argued that both participants and many observers understood the display as a joke — two Black Americans stating what the author considered obvious — and that mainstream coverage manufactured the ensuing outrage by tying the slogan to white supremacist and far-right organizations.[12] The essay treated the episode as the opening move in a cancellation campaign spearheaded by the [[anti-defamation-league|Anti-Defamation League]].[12] See [[white-lives-matter-t-shirt-controversy|White Lives Matter T-shirt controversy]].[12]

Kennedy Jr.]].[13]
