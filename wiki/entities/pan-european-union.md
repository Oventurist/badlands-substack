# Pan-European Union

The Pan-European Union, also called Pan Europa, was founded in 1923 by Count [[richard-coudenhove-kalergi|Richard von Coudenhove-Kalergi]] and is presented by Matthew Ehret as the institutional bridge between post-First World War aristocratic reaction and the design of the modern European Union.[1]

## Founding and membership

Pan Europa was originally set up as a conservative Catholic umbrella organization responding to the spread of Bolshevism, which had recently been used to overthrow the Russian government and threatened to spread across Europe with vast influence in Hungary and Germany.[1] Its early supporters included [[benito-mussolini|Benito Mussolini]], [[winston-churchill|Winston Churchill]], [[walter-lippmann|Walter Lippmann]], Nazi finance minister [[hjalmar-schacht|Hjalmar Schacht]] and Nazi geopolitician Karl Haushofer, while the financiers [[max-warburg|Max Warburg]] and Louis de Rothschild openly bankrolled the organization.[1] [[alexander-parvus|Parvus]] is also linked to the body.[1]

## Warburg funding

In her 2022 book *The Empire on Which the Black Sun Never Set*, [[cynthia-chung|Cynthia Chung]] records Kalergi's account that his introduction to Max Warburg occurred in 1924 through Baron Louis Rothschild, and that Warburg immediately donated sixty thousand gold-marks to carry the movement through its first three years.[1] Kalergi suggested the donation be split equally between Germany and Austria, with Geheimrat Fritsch of the Dresdner Bank as trustee for the German tranche and Vice-President Brosche of the Kreditanstalt for the Austrian tranche.[1] Chung notes that the Dresdner Bank would later be a major stakeholder in the construction company for Auschwitz, that Schacht had been its deputy director from 1908 to 1915 and continued to work closely with it, and that it was the bank of choice of Heinrich Himmler's SS.[1]

## Doctrine

In a 1932 speech Kalergi celebrated the restoration of order that a unified pan-European effort against Bolshevik anarchism would bring, arguing that "this eternal war can end only with the constitution of a world republic" achieved through "a politic of peaceful strength, on the model of the Roman Empire."[1] In 1954 he described the organization's purpose as becoming "the unofficial representative of the multilingual European Nation," a "political club, a kind of open conspiracy as H.G. Wells described in one of his books."[1]

Ehret argues that the governing religion of this projected regime was not authentic Christianity but a Christian costume, in the manner of the Inquisition, over a retooled [[eugenics|eugenics]] program — a reading he supports with Kalergi's own statement that eugenics "will soon be associated with Nietzsche's world outlook and, through it, gain the striking power of a religion."[1]

## Later leadership

[[otto-von-habsburg|Otto von Habsburg]] ran the organization for over thirty years and created a sister body, the [[dignitatis-humanae-institute|Dignitatis Humanae Institute]], intended to "unite the right of the world" under a gnostic Catholic veneer with a Clash of Civilizations rebranding for the alt-right.[1] The design for the European Union, including the use of Beethoven's Ninth Symphony as its anthem, was first outlined in detail by Kalergi himself, and is said to have possibly inspired [[miles-guo|Miles Guo]]'s [[new-federal-state-of-china|New Federal State of China]] a century later.[1]
