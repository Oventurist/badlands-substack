# Joe Rogan

Joe Rogan is an American podcaster whose program, "The Joe Rogan Experience," boasts the largest podcast audience in the United States, with 17.5 million subscribers on YouTube and more than 14 million followers on Spotify.[1]

## 2024 Trump interview

In late October 2024, a campaign official confirmed to Fox News Digital that former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] would record an interview with Rogan that Friday, with the taping to take place at Rogan's studio in Austin, Texas; Politico first reported the story.[1] The appearance was framed as another opportunity for Trump to court younger male voters, who make up the majority of Rogan's audience.[1]

## Context in the 2024 podcast campaign

Both Trump and Vice President [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] made numerous stops at influential podcasts during the 2024 campaign season.[1] Harris appeared on "Call Her Daddy," which has the largest female American audience, to discuss women's issues, while Trump appeared on shows including Barstool Sports' "Bussin with the Boys," "Full Send," and Patrick Bet-David's podcast.[1]

## Remarks on annexing Canada and Mexico

In January 2025, Rogan floated the possibility of the United States annexing Mexico after taking control of Canada, an idea President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had suggested his administration could pursue.[2] Asked by fellow podcaster Theo Von whether he thought Canada would be taken in as the 51st or 52nd state, Rogan replied, "Here's what I think, I think we take Canada and then we go right into Mexico," to which Von exclaimed in agreement.[2] Rogan continued: "Everybody keeps sneaking over, how about we just let them stay here... how about we make this like totally lockdown safe and then expand safety? Expand it. But you got to do it without stripping people of their rights."[2]

## Criticism of the Blue Origin celebrity flight (2025)

In April 2025, Rogan used the opening of a podcast episode to ridicule singer [[katy-perry|Katy Perry]] over the all-female [[blue-origin|Blue Origin]] suborbital flight aboard Jeff Bezos's rocket, which had drawn intense public backlash.[3] "Hey Tim Dillion, I'm much better now that the ladies are back from space, thank you," he said, adding that the trip was "very profound" and that after Perry's remarks about it "she's basically a guru now."[3] He mocked her for bringing a daisy aboard and flashing it to the cameras, noting that "it shows you how quick the flight was" because "the dead daisy that's like snipped from its life source was still alive or still vibrant."[3]

## Civil war warning after the Charlie Kirk assassination (2025)

In a November 2025 episode featuring Brian Redban, Rogan said the September assassination of conservative activist [[charlie-kirk|Charlie Kirk]] had convinced him the United States was on the road to civil war.[4] On a scale of one to ten, he suggested, "this might be like step seven on the way to a bona fide civil war."[4] He added: "Regular people celebrating somebody getting murdered in front of their wife and kid on television in front the whole world, as soon as you celebrate that, like man, you're in dark territory."[4] Rogan called on Americans to talk out their differences rather than condone violence against political opponents.[4]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded critically, questioning why an "anti-woke" figure would seek to install [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] in Kirk's place as a movement leader, and argued that the [[con-inc|ConInc]] media ecosystem had been riding out the Trump term while commandeering his base before moving back toward a Uniparty Right agenda.[4]

## 2024 Trump interview

In October 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] hinted during an appearance on the Nelk Boys' "Full Send" podcast that he would sit down with Rogan, telling host Kyle Forgeard "I think I'm doing it, actually" and, when pressed, "yeah, I am."[5] Both Trump and [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] sat for interviews on popular podcasts during that election cycle.[5]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Rogan as "one of the biggest Mind-Movers at work in the Info War" and predicted his Trump interview would be the most-watched in American history, occurring amid the most contentious national and international power struggle of all time.[5] He argued that Rogan's sometimes-opposition to the emergent MAGA wing — like that of [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]] and RFK Jr. before him — is a feature rather than a bug of such deployments, because these movers can attack the Collective Mind from positions Trump cannot.[5] He framed the interview as a mass culture event of unmatched memetic magnitude, citing Musk's transformed position in American culture pre- and post-Rogan, and predicted Rogan would be attacked relentlessly by the machine, further exposing it in the waning days of its dark empire.[5]
