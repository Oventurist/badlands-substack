# Google Threat Analysis Group

The Google Threat Analysis Group (TAG) is the unit within [[google|Google]] that tracks government-backed cyberattacks and publishes research on state-linked hacking operations.[1]

## August 2024 report on Iranian phishing

In a research report released in August 2024, Google said TAG had detected efforts by Iranian hackers to target both the Trump and the Biden-Harris campaigns in May and June, as part of a larger email phishing operation that was still ongoing at the time of publication.[1] The announcement lent credence to the Trump campaign's claim, made that Saturday, that it had been hacked as part of an [[iran|Iranian]] campaign to interfere with the election.[1]

TAG said it had disrupted a "small but steady" phishing operation run by a hacking unit tied to the [[islamic-revolutionary-guard-corps|Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps]].[1] According to the report, targets in May and June "included the personal email accounts of roughly a dozen individuals affiliated with President Biden and with former President Trump, including current and former officials in the U.S. government and individuals associated with the respective campaigns."[1] Google said it blocked "numerous" login attempts against those personal email addresses.[1]

The company also disclosed that the operation had accessed the Gmail account of at least one high-profile political consultant in July; Google secured the account and referred the matter to law enforcement, without disclosing the consultant's identity or campaign affiliation.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writers read the TAG findings alongside Iran's deepening coordination with Russia — citing reports that Iran would send Vladimir Putin hundreds of ballistic missiles for use in Ukraine — and speculated that a corporate-media outlet caught using material hacked by a foreign government could trigger a major reckoning, raising the possibility that the "Iranian hack" functioned as a honeypot in a larger sting operation.[1]
