# Mohammad Mosaddegh

Mohammad Mosaddegh was the Iranian leader who nationalized his country's oil industry and was removed in a CIA-orchestrated coup in 1953.[1]

In 1950 Mosaddegh won election in Iran on the promise that he would nationalize the oil industry, reclaiming the country's natural resources from the British. The [[anglo-iranian-oil-company|Anglo-Iranian Oil Company]] was displaced by the [[national-iranian-oil-company|National Iranian Oil Company]], while Britain withdrew its personnel and attempted to sue Iran in international court — a case that was dismissed.[1]

A worldwide embargo was launched against Iran in response, and in 1953 President [[dwight-eisenhower|Dwight Eisenhower]] authorized the [[cia|CIA]] to orchestrate the [[1953-iranian-coup|coup]] that overthrew Mosaddegh and installed [[mohammad-reza-pahlavi|Mohammad Reza Shah]] as the new ruler of Iran.[1]

## Carlson's account

In his July 2023 essay for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] described Mossadegh as the democratically elected prime minister whose removal was headed by [[kermit-roosevelt-jr|Kermit Roosevelt Jr.]], grandson of Theodore Roosevelt.[2] In this account, when Mossadegh nationalized the Iranian oil industry and kicked out foreign companies — in particular British Petroleum — his removal was deemed necessary by the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] and its British "puppet masters," who asked the United States to install a CIA-, US- and British-friendly leader in his place, [[mohammad-reza-pahlavi|the Shah of Iran]].[2] Mossadegh was tried for treason and placed under house arrest, while the Shah ruled for 26 years until the [[iranian-revolution|Iranian Revolution]] of 1979.[2] Carlson added that the [[1953-iranian-coup|coup]] involved CIA propaganda, a rigged election and paid thugs creating violence in the streets of Tehran.[2]

## Oil nationalization and removal (Carlson account)

[[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] wrote that Iran democratically elected Mosaddegh Prime Minister in 1953 and that he promptly examined British control of Iranian oil production to verify Britain was paying the agreed amount from Iran's oil fields.[3] After an audit, Iran nationalized oil production and expelled foreign companies, prompting a British-led worldwide boycott of Iranian oil.[3] As a favor to Britain, the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] mounted a [[1953-iranian-coup|coup]] using propaganda, election rigging and paid, bused-in thugs, with pro-Shah riots killing 200 to 300 people; after his removal in favor of [[mohammad-reza-pahlavi|the Shah]], Mosaddegh was convicted of treason in court.[3]
