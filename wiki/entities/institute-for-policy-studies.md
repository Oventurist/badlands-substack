# Institute for Policy Studies

The **Institute for Policy Studies (IPS)** is a Washington-based think tank described by Badlands Media as an umbrella organization encompassing hundreds of diverse groups representing both the Left and the Right of the political spectrum, and as an example of a nonprofit linked to secret societies.[1] It remains active in Washington.[1]

## Coleman's account

The author Coleman is quoted at length in the article: "IPS has shaped and reshaped United States policies, foreign and domestic, since it was founded by James P. Warburg and the Rothschild entities in the United States, bolstered by [[bertrand-russell|Bertrand Russell]] and the British socialists through its networks in America."[1] Coleman writes that the objectives of IPS came from an agenda laid down by the British Round Table, "one of the most notable being to create the 'New Left' as a grassroots movement in the U.S." — with IPS meant to "engender strife and unrest and spread chaos like a wildfire out of control," proliferate left-wing nihilistic socialism, support the unrestricted use of drugs of all types, and serve as the "big stick" with which to beat the United States political establishment.[1]

According to Coleman, the IPS founders — rendered in the article as Riebard Barnett and Marcus Raskin — controlled elements as varied as the [[black-panther-party|Black Panthers]], Daniel Ellsberg, National Security Council staff member Morton Halperin, the [[weather-underground|Weathermen]], the Venceremos organization, and the campaign staff of presidential candidate George McGovern.[1]

## Powell's investigation

The author S. Steven Powell noted that a stated IPS goal was "the dismantling of all economic, political, social, and cultural institutions in the United States."[1] Following an extensive investigation conducted just before the collapse of Communism, Powell concluded that "an ordered accounting of [IPS activities] reveals that much of what the institute does, for all intents and purposes, also serves the interests of the Soviet Union," and that IPS "has been remarkably successful in promoting a sweeping radical agenda by maintaining the facade of a liberal scholarly research center."[1]

## Funding and Skull and Bones ties

Researchers cited by the article hold that much of IPS funding came from organizations connected to the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]], including the Rubin Foundation, which was represented by the New York law firm Lord, Day & Lord.[1] The Lord family has counted members on the [[skull-and-bones|Skull and Bones]] rolls since 1898; Winston Lord (initiated 1959), a former aide to [[henry-kissinger|Henry Kissinger]], chaired the Council on Foreign Relations in 1983 and later served as President Reagan's ambassador to China.[1]

IPS is presented in the article as one of several case studies in the broader argument about [[tax-exempt-foundations|tax-exempt foundations]].[1]
