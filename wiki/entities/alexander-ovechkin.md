# Alexander Ovechkin

Alexander Ovechkin is a Russian professional ice hockey player who has spent his entire twenty-year NHL career with the [[washington-capitals|Washington Capitals]] in Washington, D.C., and who in April 2025 became the league's all-time leading goal scorer.[1]

## Record-breaking goal

On April 6, 2025, at UBS Arena in New York, Ovechkin scored his 895th career goal against fellow Russian [[ilya-sorokin|Ilya Sorokin]], goaltender of the New York Islanders, setting the all-time NHL record and surpassing [[wayne-gretzky|Wayne Gretzky]].[1] Both American and Russian fans in the arena celebrated the moment.[1] He had tied Gretzky's mark the previous Saturday.[1]

## Geopolitical significance

Russian coverage argued the record carried a political message: Moscow nurtured and cultivated Ovechkin's talent and gave him his start in professional hockey, while Washington became his second home and the city where he became a legend, giving the two capitals a shared cultural touchstone.[1] The Kremlin is described as keeping a close watch on Ovechkin, and President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] referred to him as a "great player" in a speech before his inauguration.[1]

During a phone call on March 18, 2025, Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and Trump discussed hockey and the possibility of organizing games between Russian and American players.[1] Badlands commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted that Putin pitched the idea of a series of games between the two countries' professional players as a means of restoring diplomacy and goodwill, and that Ovechkin is a close friend of Putin who plans to lead the Russian national team at the following year's Winter Olympics in Italy.[1]

A further geopolitical wrinkle came when, after Ovechkin tied Gretzky's record and was expected to break it the next day, the goaltender for the Ukrainian national team said, "I hope his legs will be torn off and he won't succeed."[1]

## Commentary

GhostofBasedPatrickHenry argued that politics is downstream from culture and that hockey offers a natural starting point for building a positive cultural foundation between the United States and Russia.[1]
