# United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit is the federal appellate court with jurisdiction over California. In June 2025 it became the pivotal forum in the dispute over President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s federalization of California National Guard troops deployed to Los Angeles during protests against immigration raids.[1]

## The June 2025 National Guard stay

In a late-night order on Thursday, June 12, 2025, the Ninth Circuit paused a district court order that would have required Trump to return control of thousands of [[california-army-national-guard|California National Guard]] troops in Los Angeles to Governor [[gavin-newsom|Gavin Newsom]].[1] The emergency stay came hours after U.S. District Judge [[charles-breyer|Charles Breyer]] of San Francisco ruled that Trump had broken the law in mobilizing the Guard amid the protests, and ordered the troops returned to state control by noon Friday.[1]

A three-judge panel of the court — two of its members appointed by Trump and one by President Biden — scheduled a hearing for the following Tuesday, meaning the [[national-guard|National Guard]] would remain federalized through the weekend.[1] Breyer's 36-page district court opinion had held that Trump's actions "were illegal — both exceeding the scope of his statutory authority and violating the 10th Amendment to the United States Constitution," and expressed concern at the administration's implicit argument that protest against the federal government, "a core civil liberty protected by the First Amendment, can justify a finding of rebellion."[1]

## Commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] observed that California and Newsom "got less than a day of celebrating their win against Trump" before the appellate stay, and noted that the Guard would therefore remain under federal control through the weekend's [[no-kings-protests|No Kings]] demonstrations.[1]

## Administrative stay in the Portland National Guard case (October 2025)

In October 2025 the Ninth Circuit temporarily lifted a lower court order blocking the deployment of Oregon [[national-guard|National Guard]] troops to Portland, while a broader ban on any state's National Guard entering the city remained in force.[2] The administrative stay applied only to U.S. District Judge [[karin-immergut|Karin Immergut]]'s Saturday ruling halting Oregon's deployment; the court explicitly did not assess either side's arguments, stating that the move preserved the status quo while it deliberated.[2]

"The administrative stay will best preserve the status quo," the court wrote, noting that Oregon troops had been federalized but not deployed before the initial order.[2] The panel scheduled arguments for Thursday on whether to extend the stay, in a dispute Badlands commentary expected to reach the Supreme Court.[2] See [[oregon-national-guard-deployment-lawsuit|Oregon National Guard deployment lawsuit]].[2]
