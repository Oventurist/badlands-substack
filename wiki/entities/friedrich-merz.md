# Friedrich Merz

Friedrich Merz is the Chancellor of Germany and, as of early 2026, a deeply unpopular incumbent within a broader European pattern of collapsing support for establishment leaders.[1]

## Transatlantic diplomacy

Despite a marked deterioration in European views of the United States — with unfavorable opinions exceeding 60% in several nations and peaking at 84% in Denmark — Merz planned visits to Washington to maintain dialogue with the administration of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Domestic position

Merz's unpopularity, alongside that of [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] and [[keir-starmer|Keir Starmer]], has helped drive far-right parties to lead or near-lead polls in Germany, the U.K., France, and Italy, propelled by inflation, economic stagnation, and immigration backlash.[1]

## Oval Office appearance during the Iran war

In March 2026, the German Chancellor appeared alongside [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] at an Oval Office press conference during the fourth day of the [[iran-war|U.S.–Iran war]], at which Trump said he may have "forced Israel's hand" into the conflict, confirmed the death of [[ali-khamenei|Ayatollah Ali Khamenei]], and predicted oil prices would spike temporarily before falling below pre-war levels.[2]

## Criticism of US Iran policy and troop withdrawal threat

In April 2026 Merz publicly criticized United States policy in the [[iran-war|Iran conflict]], prompting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to indicate that the United States could reconsider its military presence in [[germany|Germany]].[3] Trump suggested that continued US troop deployments depend on allied support and burden-sharing, reiterating long-standing concerns about the value of US commitments within [[nato|NATO]].[3] Germany hosts tens of thousands of US troops, a key component of NATO's European defense posture and logistics network, and any reduction would represent a significant shift in US force positioning on the continent.[3] The exchange came amid broader tensions over the Iran war and renewed debate about alliance obligations and defense spending.[3]

## May 2026: friction with Trump over the Iran war

Escalation surrounding [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s Iran conflict intensified tensions between Trump and Merz in early May 2026, with Trump criticizing [[germany|Germany]]'s domestic and energy policies — reportedly telling the chancellor to fix his broken country — while weighing repositioning of U.S. troops in parts of Europe.[4] The dispute was described as highlighting broader strain inside [[nato|NATO]] as allies diverged in their responses to the Iran crisis and its economic fallout.[4]

Germany simultaneously faced renewed pressure over energy strategy as oil shocks from the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] rippled through markets, complicating climate and industrial policy decisions.[4] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that energy shocks had already halved Germany's 2026 growth forecast and that its leadership was publicly admitting economic bleeding, presenting the country — "the crown jewel of the Globalist [[collectorate|Collectorate]]" — as a system stripped of buffers and of enforceable sovereignty on kinetic or energy grounds.[4]

## February 2025 election victory

Merz's conservatives won the German federal election of February 2025, finishing well ahead of rival parties but short of the 30% vote share they had expected.[5] "Let's celebrate tonight and in the morning we'll get to work," he told cheering supporters, adding that he was "aware of the responsibility that now lies ahead" of him.[5]

The other winner of the night was the far-right [[alternative-for-germany|Alternative for Germany]], which celebrated a record second-place result of 20.8% and led all parties in the east with a projected 34%.[5] AfD chancellor candidate [[alice-weidel|Alice Weidel]] predicted that Merz's attempt to forge a coalition would end in failure and that fresh elections would follow well before another four years elapsed.[5] Badlands commentary greeted the result by questioning whether German elections are genuine at all.[5]

## Ukraine peace talks phone call

Merz joined French President Emmanuel Macron on a December 2025 phone call with Vladimir Zelensky, Finnish President Alexander Stubb, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, and NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte, in which European leaders voiced deep distrust of the US-led effort to end the Ukraine conflict.[6] Merz was reported to share Macron's assessment that continued peace talks could pose a 'great danger' for Zelensky personally.[6] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry addressed Merz directly, writing that Trump was going to betray him and his ilk 'because that is the morally righteous thing to do.'[6]
