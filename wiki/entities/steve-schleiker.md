# Steve Schleiker

Steve Schleiker is the Clerk and Recorder of El Paso County, Colorado — the state's largest county — and a Republican official who drew national attention in March 2026 when he resigned as Vice President of the Colorado County Clerks Association (CCCA) and withdrew his county from active membership in the organization.[1]

## Resignation from the CCCA

Schleiker described the move as "not a decision I made lightly," citing concerns over transparency and internal governance.[1] He reported having been barred from key executive meetings with the Colorado Secretary of State's office on what he characterized as the false premise of a "lack of trust."[1] In his written statement he explained that "at the core, my decision was driven by concerns about independence, transparency, and the role of external influence within what is intended to be an independent association," and went on to recount obstruction and speculate about political motivations behind it.[1]

He also criticized the [[colorado-county-clerks-association|CCCA]]'s promotion of a uniform "Gold Standard" narrative about Colorado's elections, and questioned whether the association consistently operates in a non-partisan manner focused on professionalism and the rule of law.[1]

## Significance

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], who broke the story, noted that everyone directly involved is a Republican, and paired the Schleiker resignation with the concurrent obstruction investigation of former Maricopa County Recorder [[stephen-richer|Stephen Richer]].[1] Both cases, she argued, center on the heads of professional clerk and recorder associations — non-governmental organizations that function as subject-matter experts, escalation points, and public-relations arms for election officials while operating without meaningful oversight or lawful authority.[1] Taken together, the two episodes illustrate growing friction at the county level over election record-keeping, official independence, and the influence of clerks' associations amid continued legislative and public scrutiny of election processes.[1]
