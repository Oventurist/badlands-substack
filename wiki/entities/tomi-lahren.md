# Tomi Lahren

Tomi Lahren is an American conservative political commentator.[1] She appears in the record of federal pressure on social media platforms as one of the specific voices the [[joe-biden|Biden]] [[white-house|White House]] wanted downranked.[1]

In messages released by the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] as part of the [[facebook-files|Facebook Files]], White House digital strategy director [[rob-flaherty|Rob Flaherty]] asked [[facebook]] about changing its algorithm so users would be more likely to see [[new-york-times|The New York Times]], The Wall Street Journal or "any authoritative news source" over the [[daily-wire|Daily Wire]], Lahren, and other figures he described as "polarizing people."[1] The naming of individual commentators was treated by critics as evidence that the government's interest lay in suppressing identified political speakers rather than in addressing discrete false claims.[1]

## Watchlist remarks after the January 2025 attacks

In the aftermath of the New Year's 2025 attacks in New Orleans and Las Vegas, Lahren posted that anyone "in our streets, waving Palestinian or ISIS flags and chanting an intifada revolution" should "probably be on a watchlist and/or deported immediately."[2] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] singled the post out as an example of conservative figures adopting the surveillance-state logic they had spent a decade denouncing.[2]

DeLarme objected that the position would place Americans on a government watch list for waving the flag of a disfavored cause, and argued there was no discernible difference between what Lahren advocated and what he characterized as the Biden administration's treatment of conservatives after January 6.[2] He also faulted her for conflating the Palestinian flag with the ISIS flag, which he said showed a lack of differentiation between a country whose civilians were being killed and a U.S.-created terrorist organization.[2] He further wondered how the voting bloc in Dearborn, Michigan that had swung toward [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the 2024 election would receive such sentiment.[2]

To underline the reversal, DeLarme juxtaposed the post with one Lahren had published roughly two years earlier reading, "Free speech REALLY triggers the Left. Weird."[2] He concluded that someone arguing for government watch lists over flag-waving is not in a position to lecture others about being triggered by free speech.[2]
