# Sergey Kislyak

Sergey Kislyak served as Russia's ambassador to the United States during the 2016 presidential campaign and the transition to the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration. He is a central figure in the Badlands Media article "Setting the Stage," where Joe Lange casts his phone call to incoming National Security Adviser [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] as the trigger point of the entire entrapment operation — and possibly as bait laid by Trump's own side.[1]

## The Flynn call

Flynn was accused of lying to the FBI about a phone call with Kislyak in the lead-up to Trump's inauguration.[1] Lange notes that Flynn was already under illegal surveillance before the call, which is how [[james-comey|James Comey]] knew precisely what to ask him in order to trap him in a false statement to FBI agents.[1] The article states that [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] had announced new sanctions on Russia for election interference, and that Kislyak called Flynn the same day — timing Lange describes as a deliberate component of the trap, designed to provoke a predictable Russian response.[1]

Declassified material supports the surveillance claim in Lange's account: acting Director of National Intelligence [[richard-grenell|Richard Grenell]] declassified and sent Congress a list of dozens of Obama-era officials who had requested documents leading to Flynn's identity being "unmasked" from intelligence reports between the 2016 election and the inauguration, including then-Vice President Joe Biden, former White House chief of staff Denis McDonough, Comey, and former CIA Director [[john-brennan|John Brennan]].[1] Because the unmasking requests predate the Kislyak call, Lange treats them as proof of an illegal spying operation rather than a reaction to genuine suspicion.[1]

Q drop 4231 (May 13, 2020) frames the question directly: "FISA unmask request(s) [PRIOR TO] Amb. KISLYAK phone call? WHO ASKED AMB. KISLYAK TO CALL FLYNN [SET UP _FBI entrap + FISA [late] justify]?"[1] Lange's suggestion is that someone inside the Trump orbit — possibly [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] or Trump himself — asked Kislyak to raise the Obama sanctions, setting the bait that forced Comey to move.[1]

## Contacts with Jared Kushner

The Independent reported that Kushner had at least three previously undisclosed contacts with Kislyak during and after the 2016 campaign, including two phone calls between April and November of that year, and that Kushner had become a focus of the FBI's collusion inquiry — though he was not a target of it.[1] Before the election, Kislyak's undisclosed discussions with Kushner and Flynn reportedly focused on fighting terrorism and improving U.S.–Russian economic relations; after the November 8 election, Kushner and Flynn discussed with Kislyak the creation of a back channel between Trump and Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] that would bypass diplomats and intelligence agencies.[1]

Lange reads the back-channel proposal as evidence of a [[sovereign-alliance|sovereign alliance]] between Trump and Putin against the cabal and its personnel in the State Department and intelligence community, and argues that the leak of Kushner's Kislyak communications to the press was an attempt to shut those channels down rather than a genuine counterintelligence disclosure.[1] He compares it to Kushner's earlier establishment of back-channel communications with Chinese President Xi Jinping.[1]

## Significance

Because the FBI's extensive surveillance of both Kushner and Flynn produced nothing usable, Lange argues, Comey was forced to manufacture a crime by tricking Flynn into a false statement — and even then had to alter the FBI 302 report, since the interviewing agents had documented that they did not believe Flynn lied.[1] Kislyak's call is thus the hinge on which the whole frame job turned.[1]

## The call to Flynn in "The Biggest Boomerang In History"

[[joe-lange|Joe Lange]]'s article treats the Russian ambassador's telephone call to [[michael-flynn|Michael Flynn]] as the pretext for the [[fbi|FBI]] interview that produced Flynn's false-statement charge.[2] Q drop 4231 of May 13, 2020, quoted in the article, asks whether [[foreign-intelligence-surveillance-act|FISA]] unmask requests preceded the Kislyak phone call and who asked the ambassador to call Flynn, framing the sequence as an FBI entrapment set-up with late FISA justification.[2]

Lange argues there was ample evidence Flynn was being spied on long before the call and before any FISA application on [[carter-page|Carter Page]], and that [[james-comey|Comey]] already held transcripts of the conversation before the interview took place — which is how he knew Flynn was lying.[2] These claims reflect Badlands Media interpretation rather than established fact.
