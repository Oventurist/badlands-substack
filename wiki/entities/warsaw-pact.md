# Warsaw Pact

The Warsaw Pact was the Cold War military alliance that served as [[nato|NATO]]'s counterpart on the eastern side of Winston Churchill's "Iron Curtain".[1] It was established in 1955.[1]

## Membership

Its members were the Soviet Union, Albania, Bulgaria, Czechoslovakia, East Germany, Hungary, Poland and Romania.[1]

## Assessment

The Badlands account holds that neither Cold War bloc had clean hands, but argues that any sober comparison of organized terror, assassinations of heads of state, government overthrows and deliberate subversion of poor nations' industrialization efforts places a far greater share of blame on the NATO apparatus that claimed to represent "freedom and democracy" — an apparatus staffed at senior levels by former Nazi officers.[1]

The Warsaw Pact's post-Cold War successors in the Eurasian space are treated as the [[collective-security-treaty-organization|Collective Security Treaty Organization]] and, in a broader and non-military sense, the [[shanghai-cooperation-organization|Shanghai Cooperation Organization]].[1]
