# Blackstone

**Blackstone** is a major American financial firm headquartered at 345 Park Avenue in Midtown Manhattan, a building so closely identified with the company that it is commonly referred to as the "Blackstone Building."[1]

## 345 Park Avenue

Blackstone shares the tower with the [[national-football-league|NFL]], the accounting firm [[kpmg|KPMG]], and a slew of other corporate tenants; the building itself is owned by the Rudin organization, whose offices occupy the 33rd floor.[1] The firm's headquarters drew national attention on July 28, 2025, when the [[345-park-avenue-shooting|345 Park Avenue shooting]] took place there, with the gunman [[shane-tamura|Shane Tamura]] later found dead on the 33rd floor.[1] Coverage of the shooting in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] emphasized the concentration of elite financial, sports, and professional-services institutions at the single address.[1]

## Harvard endowment ties

In "The Harvard Control Grid" (Badlands Media, May 22, 2025), Blackstone is described as "not just a private equity firm" but "a central lever in global real estate, pharma, infrastructure, defense, and financial tech."[2] Its CFO, [[michael-chae|Michael S. Chae]], sits on the board of the [[harvard-management-company|Harvard Management Company]] while also holding a seat at the [[asia-society|Asia Society]] alongside former Saudi intelligence chief [[turki-al-faisal|Prince Turki al-Faisal]] and membership in the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]].[2] Co-founder [[stephen-schwarzman|Stephen Schwarzman]] is named among the Harvard-linked figures who routinely attend the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] at Davos.[2]

## Harvard endowment liquidation (2025)

"The Ivy Web" reports that the [[harvard-management-company|Harvard Management Company]] offloaded stakes in private equity vehicles managed by Blackstone, [[kkr|KKR]] and [[bain-capital|Bain Capital]] as part of a sale of more than $1 billion in illiquid holdings, which the article characterizes as distress liquidation rather than portfolio rebalancing.[3] Blackstone is also listed among the institutions to which former and current HMC board members had ties as of 2024, alongside [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]], [[morgan-stanley|Morgan Stanley]], the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]] and the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]].[3]
