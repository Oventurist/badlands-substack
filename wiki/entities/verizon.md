# Verizon

Verizon is a major American telecommunications carrier that became a focus of controversy over its compliance with Justice Department demands for the phone records of members of Congress during the [[arctic-frost|Arctic Frost]] investigation.[1]

## Arctic Frost records disclosure

On Tuesday, November 4, 2025, Verizon said it would implement new safeguards to ensure top-level review before complying with subpoenas that target members of Congress, after acknowledging that it had secretly provided the call records of Republican lawmakers to the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] as part of the sprawling Arctic Frost investigation, which served as the foundation for the federal indictment of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

"In 2023 Verizon was ordered to turn over call records for certain members of Congress to the Department of Justice. The Special Counsel and DOJ at the time decided who to target," Verizon spokesman Kevin Israel told the Washington Examiner, referring to former special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] and the Biden administration.[1] "A court ordered Verizon not to tell anyone about that. We had no choice but to comply with the court order. So we did."[1]

Chief Judge [[james-boasberg|James Boasberg]] of the federal district court in Washington, D.C., approved orders preventing the carriers from notifying the affected lawmakers.[1] On the same day, Rep. [[brandon-gill|Brandon Gill]] introduced articles of impeachment against Boasberg related to his role in imposing the gag order.[1]

## Commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] that the episode fit a pattern of intimidation and coercion characteristic of the [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of government]], noting that the carriers believed they had no choice but to comply and therefore violated the trust of their own customers.[1]
