# John C. Bonifaz

John C. Bonifaz is an election-law attorney and activist who appears in the June 9, 2026 *Badlands Brief* in two contradictory capacities: as an early public critic of foreign influence over American voting machines, and as an antagonist of Badlands Media contributors in litigation.[1]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] introduced him as someone "who separately is one of the masterminds in the lawfare against me and my co-defendants," before quoting at length from his letter to the National Institute of Standards and Technology.[1] In that letter Bonifaz argued that "Dominion, Now the Largest or Second Largest Voting System Company, Is Foreign Controlled and Depends Upon Secret Source Code Created and Owned by Smartmatic, a Foreign Controlled Company With Ties to The Venezuelan Government Led by Hugo Chavez."[1] He added that "Dominion is reported by Dun & Bradstreet to be a Toronto-based company with one listed key official, board members and employee, John Poulos, and under $18 million in sales for which the company obtains no income."[1]

Badlands cites the letter as evidence that concerns about [[smartmatic|Smartmatic]] source code inside [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion]] systems were raised contemporaneously by figures who later positioned themselves against election skeptics.[1]

## NIST Letter Cited in the Olsen Dispute (2026)

During the June 2026 controversy over [[kurt-olsen|Kurt Olsen]]'s appointment to a senior [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] role, Badlands contributor [[canncon|CannCon]] noted in *The Gateway Pundit* that Bonifaz — co-founder and president of [[free-speech-for-people|Free Speech For People]] — had himself previously expressed concern about voting software supplied by a "foreign-controlled company" with "ties to the Venezuelan government," in a letter still hosted on the National Institute of Standards and Technology website.[2] The citation was offered to show an inconsistency between Bonifaz's earlier skepticism of voting technology and his organization's later characterization of Olsen as an "election denier" unfit to oversee election matters.[2]
