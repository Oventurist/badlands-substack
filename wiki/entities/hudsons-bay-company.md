# Hudson's Bay Company

The Hudson's Bay Company was the chartered British trading enterprise that held, as private property, the majority of the territory that became [[canada|Canada]] before 1867.[1] It was chartered in 1670 by Prince Rupert, and its lands — [[ruperts-land|Rupert's Land]] — separated Britain's eastern North American colonies from the lone western outpost of [[british-columbia|British Columbia]] by some 3,000 kilometres of undeveloped wilderness.[1]

Matthew Ehret argues that for nearly two centuries this private land was managed by Jesuits and Anglican agents of the Empire, who intentionally kept the frontiers of North America unexplored: Canada was expected to be nothing more than a land of furs, without industry or infrastructure — "an economy made up of fur traders, hewers of wood and drawers of water, but nothing more."[1]

Transferring the Company's possessions into federal hands was, in Ehret's reconstruction, the second of three tasks facing British strategists in the 1865-1867 period, following the confederation of the British North American territories and preceding the persuasion of British Columbia to join.[1] The transfer was completed on 18 July 1868 when the Hudson Bay territories were sold to Ottawa under an operation led by [[georges-etienne-cartier|Sir Georges-Etienne Cartier]].[1] In subsequent decades Saskatchewan and Alberta were formed as provinces out of what had formerly been only Hudson's Bay land.[1]
