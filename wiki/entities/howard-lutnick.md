# Howard Lutnick

Howard Lutnick is the U.S. Commerce Secretary in the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration who faced bipartisan questioning in February 2026 over ties to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] revealed in newly released [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] documents.[1]

## Senate testimony

On February 10, 2026, Lutnick testified before a Senate Appropriations Subcommittee.[1] He acknowledged visiting Epstein's private island with his family, having lunch with Epstein after Epstein's 2008 conviction for soliciting a minor, and other post-2005 interactions, while insisting the relationship was minimal, that he "barely had anything to do" with Epstein, and that he had nothing to hide.[1]

Democratic senators including [[chris-van-hollen|Chris Van Hollen]], [[chris-coons|Chris Coons]], and [[jeff-merkley|Jeff Merkley]] pressed Lutnick on discrepancies with his prior claims of cutting ties earlier and accused him of lacking transparency, with some issuing calls for his resignation.[1] Republican leaders emphasized the need for those named in the files to provide answers but largely avoided direct confrontation on the issue.[1]

## Administration support

The [[white-house|White House]] and House Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]] continued to express support for Lutnick, dismissing resignation demands as absurd.[1] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described Lutnick as the most notable of the Trump allies targeted by media coverage of the files.[1]

## Continued fallout (February 2026)

Reporting by Axios in mid-February 2026 placed Lutnick among the high-profile figures facing investigations or pressure to resign as a result of the latest round of Epstein disclosures, alongside Hyatt heir Tom Pritzker, who stepped down as executive chair, and Paul Weiss chair Brad Karp, who resigned.[2] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that Lutnick and [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]] were "firmly in the crosshairs," and questioned whether a portion of the MAGA base was closing ranks with the same media establishment it had previously opposed.[2]

## Open-source AI policy (July 2026)

In late July 2026 Lutnick, described as the core voice behind President Trump's tariff and trade agenda, was reported to have said that the administration aims to "protect open source AI."[3] The remark was cited by Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] as one of three converging weekend indicators that the White House had aligned with the pro-acceleration side of the dispute over [[open-source-artificial-intelligence|open-source artificial intelligence]], alongside comments by former AI and Crypto Czar [[david-sacks|David Sacks]] and China's strategically timed open-weight model releases.[3]

## February 2026 media campaign

In February 2026 the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] revived scrutiny of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], Lutnick, and [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], lamenting that fallout from the tranches of "Epstein Files" had not reached the [[white-house|White House]]: "Such associations have led to the resignations of business leaders, but President Trump's White House was unfazed."[4]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] identified Lutnick as "currently the prime target of the op," describing him as a man who lost hundreds of his Cantor Fitzgerald colleagues on 9/11, including his brother, and as the figure who "most directly declared the death of the Regime to its face" at Davos two weeks earlier by proclaiming globalism dead.[4] He argued that the coordinated campaign against Lutnick, [[steve-bannon|Steve Bannon]], and [[jared-kushner|Jared Kushner]] arrived immediately after a transformative first month of 2026 in which Trump reasserted [[pax-americana|Pax Americana]], and that these men posed an existential threat to the [[deep-state|Deep State]] precisely because they understand how it works.[4]

## February 2026 tariff-refund allegations

After the [[supreme-court-ieepa-tariff-ruling-2026|Supreme Court struck down IEEPA-based tariffs]] in February 2026, claims spread that [[cantor-fitzgerald|Cantor Fitzgerald]] — the firm Lutnick left to become Commerce secretary, now run by his sons — had profited from the ruling.[5] The firm denied executing any tariff-linked trades, and a spokesperson told Semafor that a product allowing clients to buy discounted rights to potential tariff refunds had been discussed internally but was never offered, citing political sensitivities.[5] Senators Elizabeth Warren and Ron Wyden were among lawmakers raising conflict-of-interest questions about the firm's activities.[5]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the campaign as a coordinated attack timed to Lutnick's effectiveness rather than a genuine scandal, noting that legacy outlets, Capitol Hill figures and online amplifiers converged just as major economic levers began delivering results.[5] He described Lutnick as central to Trump's 2026 push to reclaim American production and leverage, with national-security tariffs on steel, autos and advanced technology that survived the Court's ruling — an outcome he said Lutnick and Trump had pre-signaled.[5]

Burning Bright also credited Lutnick with an overhaul returning semiconductor and high-tech investment to the United States at scale and with locking down critical minerals and supply chains, framing these as direct challenges to arrangements that had sent wealth and capacity abroad for decades.[5] He cited Lutnick's appearance at Davos, where he told assembled power brokers that the long experiment in globalism had failed the working people of the West.[5] Allowing that Lutnick could in theory be a vulnerability inside the administration, he concluded that — whether fully aligned from the start or positioned as a scalpel against the old order, like [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] — what matters is the net effect and the fact that the opposition broadcasts its anxieties about both men in unison.[5]

## Massie resignation demand (February 2026)

Amid the ongoing release of over three million pages of internal documents connected to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]], Republican Rep. [[thomas-massie|Thomas Massie]] publicly called on Lutnick, as Commerce Secretary, to step down over alleged ties to Epstein revealed in the documents.[6] Badlands framed the demand as part of mounting pressure on public officials named in the files, contrasting European resignations and investigations with what commentators described as a lack of consequences for wealthy and powerful Americans.[6]

## Davos 2026 opening address

Lutnick opened for the U.S. delegation at the January 2026 [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]] annual meeting in Davos, a day ahead of [[donald-trump|President Trump]]'s own address.[7] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called him "a solid opener," writing that he stated the main plot plainly, out loud and to the assembled elite's faces: globalism has failed.[7] She cited [[blackrock|BlackRock]] chief [[larry-fink|Larry Fink]]'s bearish message to the forum as corroborating evidence that Lutnick was right.[7]

## Cantor's gold-protected Bitcoin fund

In September 2025, [[cantor-fitzgerald|Cantor Fitzgerald]] — the Wall Street firm Lutnick led before joining the Trump administration as Commerce Secretary — launched the Cantor Fitzgerald Gold Protected Bitcoin Fund, a five-year vehicle pairing [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] exposure with gold-based downside protection.[8] Badlands host [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the product as a "signal-soaked Trojan Horse" launched into the financial world by the fund started by Trump's Commerce Secretary, and asked whether gold-insured Bitcoin might serve as a template for the "Global Economic Reordering" referenced by Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]], adding that central banks were "on notice."[8]

## Nvidia H20 chips and the rare earths deal

On July 15, 2025, Lutnick said that [[nvidia|Nvidia]]'s planned resumption of H20 AI chip sales to China was part of U.S. negotiations on rare earths.[9] "We put that in the trade deal with the magnets," he said, referring to an agreement President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had made to restart rare earth shipments to U.S. manufacturers, declining to offer further detail.[9] The statement came days after Nvidia's chief executive met Trump and a day after the company said it was filing license applications with the U.S. government, and it drew swift criticism from legislators who had backed the April export restriction on national security grounds.[9] See [[nvidia-h20-china-sales-resumption]].[9]

### Call to abolish the IRS
On the evening of February 19, 2025, Commerce Secretary Lutnick said on Fox News' Jesse Watters Primetime that President Donald Trump's goal was to "abolish" the Internal Revenue Service.[10] The remarks came as the IRS was reportedly poised to lay off thousands of workers and built on Trump's pledge to create an "External Revenue Service" to oversee tariffs and other potential foreign revenue.[10] Lutnick said Elon Musk, in his capacity as a senior White House adviser leading DOGE, was "going to cut" $1 trillion, "and then we're going to get rid of all these tax scams that hammer against America, and we're going to raise a trillion dollars of revenue."[10] Asked by Watters whether DOGE savings would be returned to the American people, Lutnick replied: "Think about it, Donald Trump announces the External Revenue Service, and his goal is very simple ... his goal is to abolish the Internal Revenue Service and let all the outsiders pay."[10] Badlands commentary described the newly minted Commerce secretary as beginning his service "with a bang."[10]
