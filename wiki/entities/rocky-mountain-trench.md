# Rocky Mountain Trench

The Rocky Mountain Trench is the long north-south valley in western Canada that the [[north-american-water-and-power-alliance|North American Water and Power Alliance]] designated as the central storage reservoir of its continental water transfer system.[1]

## Function in the NAWAPA design

Under the 1964 [[parsons-corporation|Parsons]] engineering studies, water collected from the Susitna, Copper, Yukon and Taku rivers would first be pumped 300 feet upward into the Stikine reservoir, sitting 2,400 feet above sea level.[1] From Stikine, 70 million acre-feet of water per year would be pumped into the Rocky Mountain Trench, which the plan treated as a naturally occurring reservoir 500 miles long with a total capacity of 650 million acre-feet.[1] Of that stored volume, up to 100 million acre-feet could be released each year, as needed, into the parched American southwest.[1]

Water leaving the Trench would be raised by the Sawtooth lifts in Idaho and Oregon and then carried through canals and tunnels — tunnels chosen specifically to limit evaporation losses — across Utah, Nevada, California, Arizona, Texas and northern Mexico.[1]
