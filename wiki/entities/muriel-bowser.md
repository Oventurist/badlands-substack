# Muriel Bowser

Muriel Bowser is the Mayor of Washington, D.C., and a key figure in the question of National Guard authorization surrounding the events of January 6, 2021.[1] Because the District of Columbia's Guard is not under a governor's command, requests for its deployment in the capital involved coordination between the mayor's office, the White House, and the Department of Defense.[1]

## January 6, 2021

Deputy Chief of Staff [[anthony-ornato|Anthony Ornato]] testified to the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]] in a January 28, 2022 transcribed interview that he overheard White House Chief of Staff [[mark-meadows|Mark Meadows]] push Bowser to request as many National Guard troops as she needed to protect the city.[1] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had suggested that 10,000 troops would be needed to keep the peace at the rallies and protests scheduled for that day.[1] The transcript recording this exchange was withheld from public release by the committee until 2024.[1]


## Response to the 2025 federal intervention in Washington, D.C.

After President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] declared a crime emergency in the District in August 2025 — casting it as "[[liberation-day|Liberation Day]]" — and imposed federal control over the [[metropolitan-police-department|Metropolitan Police Department]] while deploying the [[national-guard|National Guard]] to patrol city streets, Bowser led a renewed call to grant the city [[dc-statehood|statehood]].[2] "It's times like these when America needs to know why your nation's capital, a place where 700,000 taxpaying Americans live should be the 51st state," she said on The Breakfast Club.[2] Protests emerged in the district under the slogan "keep D.C. free," amid fears that [[congress|Congress]] could rescind the [[dc-home-rule-act|Home Rule Act]].[2]
