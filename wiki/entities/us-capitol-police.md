# United States Capitol Police

The United States Capitol Police (USCP) is the federal law enforcement agency responsible for protecting the United States Capitol complex, [[congress|Congress]], and its members. The force stands at the center of the continuing dispute over the security failures of January 6, 2021.[1]

## January 6, 2021 and its aftermath

The department was commanded on January 6, 2021 by Chief [[steven-sund|Steven A. Sund]], who resigned under pressure shortly afterward.[1] Sund has since argued that he was made a scapegoat by [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] and Democratic leadership, and that responsibility for the failure to secure the Capitol lay with congressional leadership rather than with the police force itself.[1]

## Congressional oversight

On September 18, 2023, a House subcommittee held a hearing titled "Oversight of United States Capitol Security: Assessing Security Failures on January 6, 2021," at which Sund testified about the department's posture and the warnings it had received.[1] [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] reported that the hearing received essentially no mainstream press coverage beyond C-SPAN's routine broadcast.[1]

Separately, the conduct of individuals inside the restricted Capitol grounds that day generated hundreds of federal prosecutions, including the belated disorderly-conduct charge brought against [[ray-epps|Ray Epps]] in September 2023.[1]

## Sund's account of January 6 (August 2023)

Former chief [[steven-sund|Steven Sund]], who led the force on January 6, 2021, described in an interview with [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]] released as episode 15 of [[tucker-on-twitter|Tucker on Twitter]] how multiple agencies withheld intelligence from him and denied support for 71 minutes while the U.S. Capitol was under siege.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Sund had been set up to serve as a patsy and that his account had been suppressed for two and a half years — [[fox-news|Fox News]] declined to air Carlson's earlier interview with him and [[60-minutes|60 Minutes]] used less than ten minutes of his story — while he had been interviewed by the Department of Defense and the Senate without the public learning what he said.[2]
