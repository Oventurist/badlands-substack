# Free Syrian Army

The **Free Syrian Army** (FSA) is an armed Syrian opposition formation that fought the government of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] during the Syrian civil war. In coverage of the December 2024 collapse of the Syrian government, the FSA was described as US-armed and as advancing on [[damascus|Damascus]] alongside [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] jihadists while the Syrian Army stood down.[1]

## Role in the fall of Damascus

Over the weekend of the offensive, FSA militants and HTS fighters took the Syrian capital, after which the anti-Assad forces declared Assad deposed and claimed control over the country's government.[1] Badlands commentary placed the FSA within a broader pattern in which extremist and opposition groups were characterised as propped up by what the outlet calls the Western globalist hegemony, and treated the group's US sponsorship as evidence that the [[fall-of-the-assad-regime|fall of the Assad regime]] was an externally driven regime-change operation rather than a spontaneous uprising.[1]

## The 2013 McCain delegation

The Badlands Media feature "Transparency and Prosecution" places the Free Syrian Army at the centre of the presentation of Syria's armed opposition as moderate.[2] [[salem-idris|General Salem Idris]], head of the FSA's Supreme Military Council, is the man photographed beside Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] during his May 28, 2013 visit inside Syria, and the article says McCain wanted Idris to serve as the moderate face of the "freedom fighters" he was seeking American money and military intervention for.[2]

The article rejects the separation between the FSA and jihadist groups, noting that the same photographs place Idris in a room with figures it identifies as [[isis|ISIS]] leaders including [[abu-bakr-al-baghdadi|Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi]].[2] It further records that [[mouaz-moustafa|Mouaz Moustafa]] of the [[syrian-emergency-task-force|Syrian Emergency Task Force]] led Representatives [[adam-kinzinger|Adam Kinzinger]] and Holding to meet various FSA commanders in Antakya, Turkey in 2014.[2] When McCain misspoke on Sean Hannity's show and implied he had met with ISIS, he had meant to refer to the Free Syrian Army — the group he and the Obama administration supported.[2] Defenders of McCain who dismissed the al-Baghdadi identification claimed the circled man was an FSA commander, but would not name or verify him.[2]
