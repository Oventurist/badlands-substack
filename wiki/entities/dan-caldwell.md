# Dan Caldwell

Dan Caldwell is an American defense policy figure who served as a senior adviser to Secretary of Defense [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]] during the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration.[1]

## Suspension in the 2025 Pentagon leak probe

On Tuesday, April 15, 2025, the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] placed Caldwell on administrative leave in connection with an investigation into potential leaks of sensitive information, according to three defense officials cited by [[politico|Politico]].[1] Caldwell was escorted out of the Pentagon by security officers and had his building access suspended pending further investigation, two of the officials said, speaking anonymously because the probe was ongoing.[1]

The leaks under investigation reportedly included military operational plans concerning the [[panama-canal|Panama Canal]], the deployment of a second aircraft carrier to the Red Sea, [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s controversial visit to the Pentagon, and the pausing of intelligence collection shared with [[ukraine|Ukraine]].[1] Caldwell was suspended as part of the same probe that also swept up Pentagon deputy chief of staff [[darin-selnick|Darin Selnick]].[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed the suspensions through the lens of [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]]'s book *[[government-gangsters|Government Gangsters]]*, which argues for purging officials disloyal to the [[us-constitution|Constitution]] from positions of power, and referred to the two officials as "Dastardly Darin and Dark Side Dan."[1] She argued that the breadth of the leaked material — Panama, the Red Sea, Ukraine, and the Pentagon itself — suggested a pattern of disclosures selected for their capacity to generate headlines damaging to the administration, and called for prosecution in addition to dismissal.[1]

## Views on a second-term NATO (2024)

In July 2024, POLITICO Magazine cited Caldwell as a defense expert familiar with the thinking inside [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s national-security advisory circle, reporting that Trump would be unlikely to quit [[nato|NATO]] outright in a second term but would demand a "radical reorientation" of the alliance in Caldwell's phrase, alongside drastically higher European defense spending.[2] "We don't really have a choice anymore," Caldwell told the magazine, citing rising U.S. debt, flagging military recruiting, and a defense industrial base unable to keep up with the challenge from both [[russia|Russia]] and China.[2]

## Criticism of Zelensky's battleground-state visit (September 2024)

Policy consultant Dan Caldwell noted in September 2024 that Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] had attacked [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s running mate Sen. [[jd-vance|JD Vance]] in the pages of The New Yorker while touring the United States, and argued that "the Biden-Harris admin is using military assets to fly a foreign leader into a battleground state in order to undermine their political opponents."[3] Zelensky had been flown to Pennsylvania aboard a US Air Force C-17 at taxpayer expense.[3]
