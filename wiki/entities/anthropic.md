# Anthropic

Anthropic is an artificial intelligence company and a principal rival to [[openai|OpenAI]].[1]

## Pentagon negotiations

Anthropic was in negotiations to supply AI models for classified U.S. military operations before walking away from the deal over strict red lines it sought — bans on domestic surveillance of Americans and on lethal autonomous weapons.[1] The collapse of those talks led the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] to announce a contract with OpenAI instead, a deal that itself triggered employee protests and was subsequently rewritten to add prohibitions on domestic surveillance of U.S. persons.[1]

## Position in the open-source AI dispute (2026)

By July 2026 Anthropic, alongside [[openai|OpenAI]], had become a focal point of a policy fight over [[open-source-artificial-intelligence|open-source artificial intelligence]], having generally supported stronger safeguards for frontier AI systems.[2] Open-source advocates argued that such regulation would entrench incumbent firms and reduce America's ability to compete globally, particularly against China, and warned that a government ban on open-source AI would tank markets and crater Anthropic and OpenAI alike.[2] Former White House AI and Crypto Czar [[david-sacks|David Sacks]] said he believed the two companies sought regulation to stave off competitors, a reading that matched Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]'s claim that chief executive [[dario-amodei|Dario Amodei]] and [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]] were seeking to slow and control the industry rather than accelerate it.[2]

## Department of War dispute and supply-chain risk designation (2026)

In March 2026 a dispute between Anthropic and the US [[department-of-war|Department of War]] intensified after a leaked internal memo of roughly 1,600 words from chief executive [[dario-amodei|Dario Amodei]] to staff circulated outside the company, detailing objections to certain military uses of its AI systems.[3] The DoW had been expanding its use of artificial intelligence in intelligence analysis, cyber operations, logistics, and battlefield planning, and Anthropic had previously worked with the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] through an AI contract valued at roughly $200 million.[3]

Anthropic established "red lines" for military applications, with Amodei saying the company was unwilling to allow its systems to be used for autonomous weapons or certain surveillance functions.[3] Following the dispute the DoW designated Anthropic a "supply-chain risk," a classification that can restrict or complicate participation in government contracts, and Pentagon officials allegedly threatened to cancel or reconsider the defense contract if the restrictions were maintained.[3] Amodei subsequently said Anthropic was attempting to "de-escalate" while continuing discussions about participation in government AI programs.[3]

Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the DoW was demanding unchecked war applications of AI and casting dissent as a threat to national security, and criticized the "Truth Community" for siding with the federal government and [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]] in favor of mass domestic surveillance and AI-autonomous weapons — a "principled conservatism," she wrote, of the kind that won Americans the [[patriot-act|Patriot Act]].[3]

## SpaceXAI compute agreement (2026)

In May 2026, Anthropic signed an agreement with [[spacexai|SpaceXAI]] for access to [[colossus-1|Colossus 1]], a large-scale AI supercomputing system designed for advanced model training and high-performance computing workloads.[4] Anthropic said the additional compute capacity would be used to expand performance and availability for Claude Pro and Claude Max subscribers.[4] The agreement also included early discussions on potential collaboration in orbital AI compute infrastructure, with both companies exploring multi-gigawatt systems in space.[4]

## Refusal of Pentagon terms and government-wide ban (2026)

By March 2026 the dispute between Anthropic and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] had widened after the company refused to relax restrictions in its AI technology to permit broad use by the U.S. military.[5] Federal officials, including President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], subsequently ordered all U.S. government agencies to halt use of Anthropic's AI systems, with the company potentially being designated a "supply-chain risk."[5]

Anthropic's leadership said it declined the Pentagon's terms on ethical grounds, arguing that existing law does not adequately address the risks of powerful AI in sensitive applications.[5] Its specific concern is that AI dramatically increases the scale and power of data analysis, so that even where the government collects only data it is technically legal to gather — such as public social media posts — AI can combine and analyze that material in ways that effectively constitute large-scale surveillance; the law, on this view, was written for human investigators rather than systems able to process billions of data points instantly.[5] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] judged Anthropic's point to "ring true," observing that the administration is simultaneously blocking state regulation of AI so that insufficient federal law is all that remains, and that loopholes identified and then deliberately left unaddressed are generally loopholes the authorities want preserved.[5]
