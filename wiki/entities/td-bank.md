# TD Bank

TD Bank N.A. (TDBNA) is the United States retail banking subsidiary of the Canadian TD financial group and, at the time of its October 2024 guilty plea, the tenth largest bank in the United States.[1] Together with its parent, TD Bank US Holding Company (TDBUSH), the institution is referred to collectively as TD Bank.[1]

## 2024 guilty plea and $1.8 billion resolution

On October 10, 2024, TD Bank pleaded guilty and agreed to pay more than $1.8 billion in penalties to resolve a [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] investigation into violations of the [[bank-secrecy-act|Bank Secrecy Act]] (BSA) and money laundering.[1] TDBNA pleaded guilty to conspiring to fail to maintain an anti-money laundering (AML) program compliant with the BSA, to fail to file accurate Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs), and to launder money.[1] TDBUSH pleaded guilty to causing TDBNA to fail to maintain a compliant AML program and to fail to file accurate CTRs.[1]

The pleas formed part of a coordinated resolution involving the Board of Governors of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]], the Treasury Department's [[office-of-the-comptroller-of-the-currency|Office of the Comptroller of the Currency]] (OCC), and the [[financial-crimes-enforcement-network|Financial Crimes Enforcement Network]] (FinCEN).[1]

Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick B. Garland]] said that "by making its services convenient for criminals, TD Bank became one," describing the institution as the largest bank in U.S. history to plead guilty to Bank Secrecy Act program failures and the first U.S. bank ever to plead guilty to conspiracy to commit money laundering.[1] Garland added that the bank had "chose[n] profits over compliance with the law" and that the investigation was continuing, with "no individual involved in TD Bank's illegal conduct" off limits.[1] Deputy Attorney General [[lisa-monaco|Lisa Monaco]] said the bank had "starved its compliance program of the resources needed to obey the law" and characterized the plea as carrying the largest penalty ever imposed under the Bank Secrecy Act, a "case study of what not to do" for bank compliance officials, CEOs, and board members.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands Media contributor Burning Bright framed the plea as personal as well as political, recounting that his Canadian wife's TD branch had interrogated the couple over a withdrawal of only a few thousand dollars — behavior he attributed to the bank's need to "keep an eye on them balance sheets when you're laundering your customers' funds on behalf of a global crime syndicate."[1] He treated the case as evidence that "every conspiracy theory involving 'the banks' is true" and that physical possession of assets matters ("if you don't hold it, you don't own it").[1]

Burning Bright further read the prosecution as a data point against the claim that the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] under the incumbent administration was uniformly hostile to the populist right, listing it alongside the same week's antitrust progressions against Apple, Amazon, and Google — the last facing the prospect of the first major American corporate break-up in three decades — as evidence of a "Devo-controlled" DOJ moving against "core pillars of the Global Cabal."[1] His conclusion was that it was "not actually a great time to be a banker, a cartel member ... or, in TD's case, BOTH."[1]
