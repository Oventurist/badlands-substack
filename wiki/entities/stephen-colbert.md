# Stephen Colbert

Stephen Colbert is an American late-night television host whose program, *The Late Show*, airs on [[cbs|CBS]], a network owned by [[paramount-global|Paramount Global]].[1]

## Dispute over FCC equal-time guidance

During his program on Monday, February 16, 2026, Colbert addressed the regulatory environment governing broadcast licenses, criticizing the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]] as "bullies."[1] He claimed that CBS's lawyers had told him "in no uncertain terms ... that we could not have [Talarico] on the broadcast," a reference to Rep. [[james-talarico|James Talarico]], according to reporting by CNBC.[1]

CBS disputed Colbert's framing. In a statement, the network said that "The Late Show was not prohibited by CBS from broadcasting the interview with Rep. James Talarico," adding that the show "was provided legal guidance that the broadcast could trigger the FCC equal-time rule for two other candidates, including Rep. [[jasmine-crockett|Jasmine Crockett]] [D-Texas], and presented options for how the equal time for other candidates could be fulfilled."[1] Colbert's objection therefore stemmed from network lawyers advising him about the show's potential exposure under FCC broadcast regulations requiring equal time for political candidates.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the [[badlands-brief|Badlands Brief]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized Colbert as "the unfunny late night host that danced around like a vaccine in an effort to convince people to poison their children," arguing that he was "throwing a tantrum because his lawyers told him to follow the law."[1] She noted that equal-time regulations "have always been a thing," but were simply not enforced by the FCC "during the age of Trump," and argued that networks seeking "special access and protections from the government" must accept the accompanying rules.[1] She further contended that broadcast networks survive only because of government protections and pharmaceutical advertising, and that neither is necessary.[1]
