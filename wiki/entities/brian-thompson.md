# Brian Thompson

Brian Thompson was the chief executive officer of [[unitedhealthcare]], one of the largest health insurers in the United States, until his killing in midtown Manhattan on December 4, 2024.[1]

## Killing

Thompson, aged 50, was shot outside the Hilton hotel at 1335 Avenue of the Americas shortly after 6:45 a.m., having arrived early for his company's annual investor conference.[1] Surveillance video reviewed by police showed Thompson walking alone toward the Hilton at 6:44 a.m., and a masked gunman who had arrived on foot roughly five minutes earlier approaching him from behind and firing repeatedly.[1] Police said the gunman appeared to ignore other pedestrians, shooting Thompson in the back and then continuing to fire as he moved toward the victim.[1]

New York Police Department chief of detectives Joseph Kenny said Thompson was found on the sidewalk in front of the hotel with gunshot wounds to his back and leg.[1] He was taken to Mount Sinai hospital in critical condition and pronounced dead at 7:12 a.m.[1] New York police commissioner [[jessica-tisch]] described the shooting as a "brazen, targeted attack" that "does not appear to be a random act of violence," adding that "every indication is that this was a premeditated, pre-planned targeted attack."[1] The [[nypd|New York Police Department]] launched a search for the suspect.[1] The killing was carried in Badlands Media's news brief for December 4, 2024 as a bonus item without accompanying commentary.[1]

## Public reaction and the social banditry framing

In the wake of the early-morning killing of Thompson in New York, social media lit up not with shock and horror but with something more akin to joy, with one X user posting "This needs to be the new norm — EAT THE RICH" and another asking whether the chief executive "die[d] quickly or over several months waiting to find out if his insurance would cover his treatment."[2] The glee with which so many people responded online shocked the consciences of politicians and pundits alike.[2]

A December 2024 Politico essay proposed that history offered an explanation in the concept of [[social-banditry|social banditry]], introduced in 1959 by the Marxist scholar [[eric-hobsbawm|Eric Hobsbawm]] to describe outlaws revered for meting out justice in an unjust world, from Robin Hood to Jesse James and Pancho Villa.[2] Badlands commentary rejected the framing as a Marxist inversion, arguing that Robin Hood in fact stole from the government treasury and returned to the people what was rightfully theirs, and that celebrating a self-proclaimed Marxist named Luigi for murdering a private citizen in the street was a counterfeit of that idea.[2]
