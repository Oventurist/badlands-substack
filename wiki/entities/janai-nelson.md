# Janai Nelson

Janai Nelson is a civil rights attorney associated with the [[naacp|NAACP]] who argued in defense of a majority-Black congressional district before the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court of the United States]] in the [[louisiana-v-callais|Louisiana redistricting case]] heard on October 15, 2025.[1]

## Supreme Court argument in the Louisiana redistricting case

During roughly two and a half hours of oral argument, Nelson defended the 2024 [[louisiana|Louisiana]] congressional map containing two majority-Black districts against a challenge brought by a group of "non-African American" voters who characterized the map as unconstitutional racial gerrymandering under the Fourteenth Amendment's equal protection clause.[1]

Nelson pushed back when Justice [[brett-kavanaugh|Brett Kavanaugh]] raised the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]]'s argument, telling the justice that such a "suggestion would swallow Section 2 whole." She warned that adopting her opponents' position would amount to "a staggering reversal of precedent that would throw maps across the country into chaos." In the alternative, she argued that if the Court concluded the 2024 map was unconstitutional, it should remand the case to the lower court so that it could "use one of the many alternatives that are available that meet Section 2 and also comply with the Constitution."[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America criticized Nelson's presentation, singling out her argument that white Democrats do not vote for Black candidates as the foundation for why a second majority-Black district was necessary. The commentary framed the exchange as evidence that decades of layered remedial doctrine under the [[voting-rights-act-of-1965|Voting Rights Act]] have produced, in the author's words, "unintelligible civil rights laws," and observed that at the Supreme Court an advocate "cannot just call everyone racist and claim dog whistles and get your way."[1]
