# Oath Keepers

The Oath Keepers are an American organization whose leaders and members were prosecuted in connection with the January 6, 2021 Capitol riot, most prominently on charges of seditious conspiracy.[1]

## Seditious conspiracy prosecutions

Federal prosecutors obtained seditious conspiracy convictions against leaders of the Oath Keepers and the [[proud-boys|Proud Boys]] tied to the events at the Capitol on January 6, 2021.[1] Among those convicted was Oath Keepers founder [[stewart-rhodes|Stewart Rhodes]], who — unlike many other January 6 defendants — was not pardoned earlier in 2026.[1] Other Oath Keepers members covered by the litigation include Kelly Meggs, Kenneth Harrelson and Jessica Watkins.[1]

## 2026 motion to vacate

On Tuesday of the week reported in April 2026, the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] asked a federal appeals court to vacate the seditious conspiracy convictions against Oath Keepers and Proud Boys leaders and to dismiss the cases entirely.[1] Court filings asked the US Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit to erase the convictions and to permit prosecutors to permanently drop the indictments.[1] The filing, signed by US Attorney [[jeanine-pirro|Jeanine Pirro]], described the move as following standard practice when the government determines that dismissal is in the interest of justice, and noted that similar motions are routinely granted.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor Ashe in America characterized the filing as "the end of an era" and possibly the conclusion of an operation, recalling marching alongside Proud Boys and Oath Keepers in Washington, DC and Denver, Colorado during the 2020–2021 post-election protests.[1] The commentary suggested that the presence of such groups at protests correlated with the appearance of counter-protesters, and questioned whether the vacatur signals the end of an operation or the beginning of a new phase.[1]

## Invocation in the 2023 speech-crackdown debate

In July 2023, [[msnbc|MSNBC]] commentator [[paul-rieckhoff|Paul Rieckhoff]] named the Oath Keepers alongside [[patriot-front|Patriot Front]] while arguing that leaders, messages and programs were needed to steer Americans "on the fence" into community organizing and non-profit organizations and away from such groups.[2] The remark accompanied his call for a post-9/11 style government crackdown, which [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized as an attempt to restrict the speech of political opponents.[2]

## Jeremy Brown pardon dispute, 2025

In February 2025 the case of Oath Keeper [[jeremy-brown|Jeremy Brown]] drew attention when the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] acknowledged in court filings that Brown's Florida weapons-possession case — which sent him to prison for more than seven years — was directly related to January 6.[3] Brown's attorney [[carolyn-stewart|Carolyn Stewart]] argued that both of his criminal cases were therefore covered by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s January 6 pardon and demanded his release, while the DOJ continued to keep him imprisoned.[3] Badlands contributor [[canncon|CannCon]] noted that Brown had not been permitted to raise January 6 during his trial.[3]

## January 2023 seditious conspiracy convictions

In January 2023, four Oath Keepers members were convicted of seditious conspiracy in Washington, D.C., in proceedings overseen by Obama-appointed judge [[amit-mehta|Amit Mehta]].[4] [[the-gateway-pundit|The Gateway Pundit]] characterised the verdicts as the product of a "DC Kangaroo Court," reporting that the four had been convicted for standing outside the U.S. Capitol without weapons, marching up the steps in a stack and singing the national anthem on January 6, 2021.[4] Badlands contributor [[sean-morgan|Sean Morgan]] argued that the outcome illustrated a "two-tiered system of justice" designed to produce a chilling effect on protest against disputed election results.[4]
