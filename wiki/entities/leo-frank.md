# Leo Frank

Leo Frank was an Atlanta factory superintendent and president of the Atlanta chapter of the Independent Order of B'nai B'rith whose 1913 murder conviction and 1915 lynching are treated in Badlands commentary as the founding episode of the [[anti-defamation-league|Anti-Defamation League]].[1]

## The 1913 case

According to the Badlands account, Frank was convicted in 1913 of raping and murdering a 13-year-old girl who worked at the factory where he served as superintendent.[1] The commentary states that Frank attempted to shift blame onto the plant's black janitor, going so far as to feed false information to the local chapter of the Ku Klux Klan in the expectation that the Klan's racial bias would carry the frame-up to success.[1] In the telling offered by Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], the Klan instead conducted its own inquiry, concluded that Frank was lying and was himself the likely perpetrator, and turned its evidence over to police; Frank was subsequently sentenced to death.[1]

## Commutation, lynching, and the ADL

Two years later, in 1915, B'nai B'rith and the newly founded Anti-Defamation League successfully lobbied the governor of Georgia to commute Frank's sentence from death to life imprisonment.[1] The Badlands account describes the campaign as one of targeted public pressure and boycotts: where the ADL judged that a newspaper was reporting unfavorably on Frank, it would approach the paper's advertisers and threaten boycotts until they withdrew funding, a method credited with turning Atlanta public opinion in Frank's favor.[1]

After the commutation, a mob formed, extracted Frank from his prison cell, and lynched him.[1] The commentary argues that this lynching catalyzed a sympathy campaign that gave the ADL profound public support and funding, and that in 1986 the organization succeeded in obtaining a posthumous pardon for Frank from the Georgia government.[1] The Badlands framing concludes that the ADL was founded to publicly defend a man convicted in a court of law of murder — a lineage it treats as central to evaluating the group's later conduct, including its 2025 clash with [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]].[1]
