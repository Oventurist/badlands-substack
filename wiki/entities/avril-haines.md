# Avril Haines

Avril Haines is the Director of National Intelligence, the official who leads the [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] and coordinates the constituent agencies of the United States intelligence community.[1]

## Commercially available information review

In late 2021, Haines tasked a panel of senior advisers with untangling what was described as a web of secretive business arrangements between commercial data brokers and members of the US intelligence community.[1] More than a year before the panel's findings became public, those advisers informed Haines that the United States government had been secretly amassing a "large amount" of "sensitive and intimate information" on its own citizens.[1]

The resulting report — declassified in June 2023 and first reported by Wired — described soberly and at length the size and scope of the government effort to accumulate data revealing the minute details of Americans' lives, and was authored by the director's own panel of experts rather than outside critics.[1] Privacy advocates characterized the findings as a "nightmare scenario," and attorney Sean Vitka of the nonprofit Demand Progress said the report revealed "what we feared most," accusing intelligence agencies of flouting the law by buying information about Americans that Congress and the Supreme Court had made clear the government should not possess.[1]

Because the review was commissioned by Haines herself, coverage in alternative media treated the disclosure as an unusual instance of the intelligence apparatus documenting its own circumvention of privacy protections through what the report called "craven interpretations of aging laws."[1]

## COVID-19 Origin Act declassification mandate (2023)

Under the [[covid-19-origin-act-of-2023|COVID-19 Origin Act of 2023]], signed by President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] on March 20, 2023, Director of National Intelligence Avril Haines was given 90 days to declassify all information on possible links between the [[wuhan-institute-of-virology|Wuhan Institute of Virology]] and the origins of Covid-19.[2] The mandate followed unanimous passage of the bill in both chambers of [[us-congress|Congress]] and the Energy Department's low-confidence assessment favouring an accidental laboratory leak.[2]

## The commercially available information report (2023)

A report issued under Haines as Director of National Intelligence documented federal agencies' bulk purchase of Americans' personal data from data brokers — material designated [[commercially-available-information|commercially available information]] (CAI).[3] The [[office-of-the-director-of-national-intelligence|ODNI]] report warned that "CAI Increases the Power of the Government," observing that "the government would never have been permitted to compel billions of people to carry location tracking devices on their persons at all times, to log and track most of their social interactions, or to keep flawless records of all their reading habits."[3] Badlands Media presented the findings as confirmation that federal agencies are buying their way around constitutional limits on [[mass-surveillance|mass surveillance]].[3]
