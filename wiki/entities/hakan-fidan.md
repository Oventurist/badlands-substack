# Hakan Fidan

Hakan Fidan is the foreign minister of Turkey.[1]

## Board of Peace charter ceremony

Fidan appeared alongside Saudi Foreign Minister [[faisal-bin-farhan|Faisal bin Farhan]] at the Davos ceremony formalizing the Charter of the [[board-of-peace|Board of Peace]] in January 2026.[1] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the pairing of the Saudi and Turkish foreign ministers on the same stage as a strategic symbol, noting that the two countries had just aided President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in negotiating a peace deal with Syrian president Al Jolani.[1] Saudi Arabia and Turkey have long had a troubled and inconsistent relationship, but the appearance was read as a signal that they were ready to bury the hatchet and work together.[1]

Turkey's growing regional role was also noted in reporting that Turkey and Qatar were filling a vacuum in Gaza created by Israel's snub of the [[palestinian-authority|Palestinian Authority]], with an Arab diplomat remarking on the irony of [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s frustration given that "his policies brought us to this situation."[1]

## Meeting with Tom Barrack (July 2026)

Fidan met U.S. Ambassador to [[turkey|Turkey]] and Special Presidential Envoy for Syria and Iraq [[tom-barrack|Tom Barrack]] in Ankara on 27 July 2026.[2] The discussions focused on strengthening the bilateral partnership and on regional stability, security, and prosperity, particularly with regard to [[syria|Syria]] and [[iraq|Iraq]].[2] The meeting immediately preceded the Ankara visit of Iraqi Prime Minister [[ali-al-zaidi|Ali al-Zaidi]], during which Turkey and Iraq signed five bilateral agreements and the [[turkish-petroleum-corporation|Turkish Petroleum Corporation]] took a stake in the Kirkuk oil project.[2]

## Israel–Turkey deconfliction talks (2025)

On April 9, 2025 Fidan, as Turkish Foreign Minister, publicly said that [[israel|Israel]] and [[turkey|Turkey]] had held talks aimed at reducing tensions in [[syria|Syria]], telling CNN's Turkish affiliate CNN Türk: "We have no intention of confronting any country within Syria, including Israel."[3] A source familiar with the discussions told The Jerusalem Post that the two countries were at a preliminary stage of exploring a coordination mechanism modeled on the arrangement that had existed with Russia during the rule of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]], which the source said had "successfully prevented unnecessary friction" between Moscow and Jerusalem.[3]

## Response to the December 2024 Syria offensive

As [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] and allied militants advanced from Idlib toward Aleppo, Hama and Homs in early December 2024, Fidan appeared to blame the government in Damascus for the jihadist onslaught.[4] President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] said the same week that Türkiye's foreign minister and head of national intelligence were in "continuous consultations" with their counterparts and that Ankara was "constantly monitoring the process on the ground."[4]

## Refusal of a NATO role in Ukraine and the BRICS overture

In late May 2024 Fidan stated publicly that "we do not want NATO to take part in this war," referring to the conflict in Ukraine.[5] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] cited the statement to ridicule reporting that [[nato|NATO]] planners were counting on Turkey to supply one of the "land corridors" for rushing American troops into the Balkans in the event of war with [[russia|Russia]], arguing that whoever supplied the talking point had failed to check the newswire.[5]

Days later, during a visit to Beijing, Fidan said that Turkey would like to join [[brics|BRICS]], and the Kremlin immediately issued a statement welcoming Turkey to the BRICS summit that Russia was to host later that year.[5] The sequence was presented as evidence of Ankara's drift away from the Atlantic alliance and toward the emerging Eurasian bloc.[5]


## Offer to take over ISIS prisons in Syria

In January 2025 Fidan said that Turkey was ready to take control of the camps holding [[islamic-state|Islamic State]] detainees and affiliates of the group in northern Syria, while reiterating Ankara's demand that Washington end its alliance with the Kurdish-led [[syrian-democratic-forces|Syrian Democratic Forces]].[6] Roughly a dozen ISIS prisons in northern Syria sit in areas controlled by the SDF, which Turkey deems a top national security threat and equates with the [[kurdistan-workers-party|Kurdistan Workers Party]] militants who have fought for self-rule inside Turkey since 1984.[6] While the PKK is designated a terrorist organisation by the United States and the European Union, the SDF remained Washington's top ally in the fight against ISIS in Syria.[6] Fidan argued that the SDF's contribution to the war against ISIS had been limited to managing the prisons where members of the Salafi jihadist group and their affiliates are held.[6]

Fidan's offer came as President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly praised President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] and Turkey's role in Syria — a pairing that Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read as the "good cop" half of a Trump–[[vladimir-putin|Putin]] pincer against Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[6]

## Participation in the Trump–Sharaa White House meeting (November 2025)

On Monday, November 10, 2025, Fidan met his American counterpart, Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], at the [[white-house|White House]] and joined part of Syrian President [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed al-Sharaa]]'s landmark meeting with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[7] "At a certain point in the meeting we were invited to join," Fidan told reporters afterward, describing talks that covered Turkey's stance on Syria, potential areas of cooperation with the United States, Syria's reconstruction and unity, and regional security.[7] His participation came as part of a surprise visit to Washington, after which he also met jointly with Rubio and Syrian Foreign Minister Assad al-Shibani, as well as with Vice President [[jd-vance|J.D. Vance]], to discuss steps to advance Syria's security and economic development.[7]

Turkish officials kept a tight lid on the trip, an unusual approach for a high-level visit to the US capital: on Sunday the Turkish Foreign Ministry announced it in a one-sentence statement without even specifying that Fidan would travel to Washington.[7] The low-key approach aligned with Ankara's effort to present al-Sharaa as an independent leader rather than a Turkish proxy, a strategy aimed at reassuring regional powers and encouraging broader engagement with Damascus, [[turkey|Turkey]] having emerged as Damascus' closest regional ally after the fall of the Syrian regime in December.[7] Badlands commentary noted that Fidan strongly implied Syria would likely join the [[abraham-accords|Abraham Accords]] in the near future.[7]


## Turkey's intervention in the ICJ genocide case

Speaking at a news conference in Cairo on Monday, August 5, 2024, Fidan said that [[turkey|Turkey]] would formally submit its declaration of intervention in [[south-africa|South Africa]]'s genocide case against [[israel|Israel]] at the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]] in The Hague on the following Wednesday.[8] Turkey had announced in May 2024 that it had decided to join the case as part of a broader set of measures against Israel over the war in [[gaza|Gaza]], stating at the time that it would submit its bid once the necessary legal preparations were complete.[8] Badlands Media read the filing as evidence that President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] was pivoting away from [[nato|NATO]] and the European Union and toward what the outlet calls the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[8]
