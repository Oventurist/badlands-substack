# Peter Marks

Peter Marks is a senior official of the U.S. [[food-and-drug-administration|Food and Drug Administration]] who served as a public spokesman for the agency's vaccine authorization decisions.[1]

When the FDA authorized updated [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]] on September 11, 2023, Marks said in a statement that "vaccination remains critical to public health and continued protection against serious consequences of COVID-19, including hospitalization and death," and added, "we very much encourage those who are eligible to consider getting vaccinated."[1] His statement accompanied clearance of shots from [[moderna|Moderna]] and [[pfizer|Pfizer]] for people as young as six months of age.[1]

Badlands Media commentary by [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the FDA's framing, arguing the approvals were issued without clinical trial data and describing the decision as part of a wider pattern of institutional harm.[1]

## Ouster from the FDA (2025)

In late March 2025, Health and Human Services Secretary [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] ousted Marks from the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]], a move reported alongside the shuttering of [[usaid|USAID]] and a wave of scandal coverage aimed at Kennedy himself.[2] The removal was treated in aggregator coverage as part of the broader reorganization of federal health agencies under the second [[trump-administration|Trump administration]].[2]

## February 2024 testimony on long-term vaccine surveillance

At a February 15, 2024 hearing of the House [[select-subcommittee-on-the-coronavirus-pandemic|Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Pandemic]] titled "Assessing America's Vaccine Safety Systems," Marks was asked by Rep. [[nicole-malliotakis|Nicole Malliotakis]] whether the [[food-and-drug-administration|FDA]] was conducting active surveillance of recipients of the earliest [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]] and whether specific health markers were being tracked for emerging trends.[3] Marks replied that "every time we go through and do the safety surveillance, we start back, and it goes back to 2020," adding that different windows might be used for certain questions but that analysis had to run from the beginning of the surveillance period, before deferring to [[daniel-jernigan|Daniel Jernigan]] of the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]].[3] Reporting characterized the exchange as officials side-stepping the question, and [[liz-willner|Liz Willner]] of [[openvaers|OpenVAERS]] said no long-term vaccine safety surveillance system exists in the United States.[3]
