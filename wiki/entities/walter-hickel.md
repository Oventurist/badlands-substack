# Walter Hickel

**Walter Hickel** (1919–2010) was a former governor of Alaska and a late-life advocate of the [[bering-strait-tunnel|Bering Strait Tunnel]] as a link between the United States and Russia.[1]

## Advocacy for the Bering Strait link

In 2007, Hickel addressed a Moscow conference sponsored by Russia's Council for the Study of Productive Forces, telling the gathering that as the world looked at goals for the twenty-first century it was fitting "that we bring Russia and America together," adding that "there couldn't be a more important symbol."[1] He said he had believed for many years that such a link would happen and that "the place to start is the Bering Strait," calling for a tunnel "to move people, resources and goods east to west, and west to east."[1]

Hickel framed the project in terms of the resource wealth of the circumpolar north, arguing that "the world's greatest reserves of natural resources await in Siberia, Alaska and Northern Canada," and calling for a rail connection to take that wealth to world markets, a fiber optic cable link to improve global telecommunications, and long-distance transmission lines to reach the 1.6 billion people on earth without electricity.[1] "Show me any area in the world where there is a lack of energy," he said, "and I'll show you basic poverty," asserting a direct tie between energy and poverty, energy and war, energy and peace.[1]

## Context

Hickel's 2007 remarks came in the same year that Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] formally offered the United States the opportunity to build the tunnel, with Russia paying 60% of the estimated $60 billion cost — an offer rejected by the Bush administration and again by the Obama White House when renewed in 2011.[1] [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] cites Hickel as part of a lineage of Bering Strait advocates running from [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] and [[william-gilpin|William Gilpin]] through [[henry-wallace|Henry Wallace]] and [[lyndon-larouche|Lyndon LaRouche]].[1]
