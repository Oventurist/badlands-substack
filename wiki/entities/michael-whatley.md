# Michael Whatley

Michael Whatley is the chairman of the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]].[1]

## Nevada mail-ballot lawsuit

In June 2024 Whatley was the public face of a lawsuit brought by the RNC, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s campaign and the Nevada Republican Party against the state of Nevada over its practice of counting mail-in ballots received the day after an election that carry no postmark.[1] The RNC noted that under Nevada law mail ballots are required "to be postmarked on or before Election Day," and said it had discovered that state election officials routinely counted non-postmarked mail ballots received after Election Day in violation of that law.[1]

In a press release, Whatley said that "counting ballots received after Election Day without a postmark creates a concerning and potentially fraudulent impact on Nevada's elections," and that the suit was filed "to ensure that Nevada law is followed and promote election integrity in the Silver State."[1] The action formed part of the RNC's broader [[mail-in-voting|mail-in voting]] litigation strategy ahead of the November 2024 election.[1]

## Arizona voting law ruling (2024)

As chairman of the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]], Whatley celebrated the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]]'s August 22, 2024 order partially reinstating Arizona's proof-of-citizenship voter registration requirement at the RNC's emergency request, writing on X that it was "a seismic win in the fight to stop non-citizens from voting - more to come!"[2]

## RNC takeover and staff purge (March 2024)

Whatley, the former North Carolina GOP chair, was elected chair of the [[republican-national-committee|Republican National Committee]] on Friday, March 8, 2024 with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s endorsement, alongside co-chair [[lara-trump|Lara Trump]].[3] Trump's campaign took operational control of the committee the following Monday, and the new leadership immediately began pushing out more than 60 staffers across the political, communications and data departments, with chief operating officer [[sean-cairncross|Sean Cairncross]] telling some staff they were being asked to resign and reapply.[3] Trump senior campaign adviser [[chris-lacivita|Chris LaCivita]] was named chief of staff as part of the same overhaul.[3]
