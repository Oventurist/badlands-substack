# World Zionist Organization

The World Zionist Organization is the international body that convened the Zionist Congresses of the early twentieth century. Its Sixth Zionist Congress, held in Basel, Switzerland, in 1903, is noted in Badlands reporting for the vote on the Uganda Programme — a proposition calling for the creation of a Jewish nation in British East Africa, in modern-day Kenya.[1]

[[nathan-mileikowsky|Nathan Mileikowsky]], grandfather of Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], attended that Congress and vocally opposed the Uganda Programme, which was defeated in the vote.[1] Mileikowsky subsequently immigrated to Mandatory Palestine in 1920 and joined the [[revisionist-zionism|Revisionist Movement]] under [[zeev-jabotinsky|Ze'ev Jabotinsky]].[1]

## World Zionist Congress and the 2025 election fraud allegations

The organization's deliberative body, the [[world-zionist-congress|World Zionist Congress]], was created by Theodor Herzl in 1897 and is often called "the parliament of the Jewish people"; it is charged with budgeting more than $1 billion a year in allocations to various Jewish and Israeli causes.[2]

In 2025, investigations continued into widespread alleged voter fraud in the WZO's United States election as ballots were tallied and other countries prepared to hold their own local votes.[2] More than 15,000 ballots were considered likely to be thrown out by late May, up from an initial estimate of 10,200 when voting closed on May 4 — more than six percent of the record 238,000 votes cast in the two-month online US election.[2] Results were due at the end of May but were expected to be delayed by the investigation.[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] joked that the episode might be what Q meant by "saving Israel for last," and imagined [[jonathan-greenblatt|Jonathan Greenblatt]] and the ADL condemning the election as antisemitic, which would in turn spur [[congress|Congress]] into finally passing bipartisan election-integrity legislation — for Israel and the Diaspora.[2] More seriously, he argued this might be the only route to congressional action on election integrity, since both parties tend to move on bipartisan bills only when the subject relates to Israel.[2]

## 2025 leadership deal collapse

In November 2025 the process of appointing new leadership at the large Zionist institutions collapsed when [[yesh-atid|Yesh Atid]] leader [[yair-lapid|Yair Lapid]] announced that his party would withdraw from all negotiations, condemning what he called "a culture of corruption and political appointments."[3] The withdrawal upended a long-negotiated agreement dividing senior jobs at the WZO and the [[jewish-national-fund-kkl|Jewish National Fund-KKL]] among the political factions, and reopened the prospect that [[yair-netanyahu|Yair Netanyahu]], elder son of Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]], would receive a plum executive role at the WZO.[3] Lapid said he would instead advance legislation to nationalize the Jewish National Fund-KKL, arguing that these bodies could only be cleaned up by bringing them "under state law, state audit, and the strictest transparency regulations."[3]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the coverage portrayed the WZO — which he described as largely responsible for creating the modern State of Israel — as sitting a level above typical NGOs in what he called the globalist hierarchy, controlling billions of dollars awarded annually to other groups and functioning in part as a patronage jobs program.[3] He framed the incentive structure sustaining such institutions as an instance of what [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] calls the Party of False Decorum.[3]
