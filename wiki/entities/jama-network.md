# JAMA Network

JAMA Network is the family of peer-reviewed medical journals published by the American Medical Association, cited in Badlands Media coverage as the venue for a study on pediatric COVID-19 mortality.[1]

## 2023 pediatric COVID mortality study

A study published in JAMA Network Open in early February 2023, reported by [[just-the-news|Just the News]], found that children are more than 100 times less likely to die from COVID-19 than adults, even though the virus ranked as the eighth most likely cause of death for children.[1] Children aged 0–19 died from COVID at a rate of 1 per 100,000, falling to an average of 0.5 per 100,000 for children aged 1 to 14 between August 2021 and July 2022, while adults died at a rate of 109 per 100,000 over the same period.[1] COVID constituted 2 percent of all deaths in children, compared with 18.4 percent from unintentional injuries and 6.8 percent from suicide, meaning children were over nine times more likely to die in an accident and over three times more likely to die by suicide than from COVID.[1]
