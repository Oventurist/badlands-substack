# Washington Post Guild

The Washington Post Guild is the labor union representing editorial and commercial employees of [[washington-post|The Washington Post]].[1] In December 2023 it organized the largest labor action against Post management since the 1970s.[1]

## 2023 strike

After 18 months of negotiation failed to produce an agreement, the Guild announced that reporters and other employees would stage a 24-hour strike, in effect all Thursday, December 7, 2023, from midnight to midnight.[1] "Despite a year and a half of efforts, Post management has refused to bargain in good faith for a fair contract that keeps up with inflation and our competition," the union said in its statement.[1] The union said the walkout would include more than 700 employees, "including reporters, editors, cartoonists, visual journalists, advertising sales people and circulation drivers."[1]

The Guild's stated grievances included pay equity, raises keeping pace with inflation and competitors, remote work policies, mental health supports, and opposition to a buyout package aimed at reducing the workforce by 10 percent.[1] Commentators linked the mental-health demand to Post reporter [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]], who had broken down on MSNBC describing PTSD and suicidal ideation arising from public reaction to her work.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] dismissed the action, comparing it to the Hollywood strikes and arguing that the announcement video showed "overpaid shills bragging about their participation in state-sponsored propaganda."[1] She contended that the optimization and automation pressures now reaching newsroom staff had displaced ordinary workers more than a decade earlier, and that many of the same journalists had promoted universal basic income as an inevitable consequence of that transformation while assuming they were exempt.[1] She added that [[jeff-bezos|Jeff Bezos]] could easily replace the strikers with generative AI.[1]
