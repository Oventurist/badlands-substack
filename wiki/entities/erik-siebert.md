# Erik Siebert

Erik Siebert is a former United States Attorney whose departure from the Justice Department became a reference point in the October 2025 debate over the prosecutions of [[james-comey|James Comey]] and [[letitia-james|Letitia James]].[1] Reporting cited in the Badlands News Brief described a U.S. attorney who resigned rather than present those cases to a grand jury, with career prosecutors subsequently telling his replacement that the government would probably lose at trial.[1]

## Role in the Comey and James cases

In an October 2025 essay carried by [[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]], the Comey and James prosecutions were characterized as so weak that the responsible U.S. attorney stepped aside rather than seek indictments, in contrast to the [[john-bolton|John Bolton]] indictment, which the piece portrayed as detailed, precise and built on Bolton's own words.[1] The Badlands commentary treated that framing skeptically, arguing that the Comey and James charges were "starter charges" likely to be followed by superseding indictments adding new counts and new defendants as evidence developed.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] questioned why The Atlantic appeared to be rewriting the Siebert story, noting that the magazine's account of the resignation differed from the original headline treatment of the episode — a Reuters report on the Trump administration threatening to fire the U.S. prosecutor overseeing the Letitia James case.[1] The commentary further suggested that the administration should take note of any government employee who argues for abandoning public abuses rather than prosecuting them, and posed the question of who exactly the U.S. attorney was who resigned rather than prosecute Comey.[1]
