# Creative Associates International

Creative Associates International (CAI) is a Washington-area contractor described by Mint Press News as one of the largest and most powerful non-governmental organizations operating anywhere in the world, and by Badlands Media contributor Joe Lange as "a CIA instrument to push the foreign policy of the shadow government."[1]

## Activities

A pillar of American soft power, the group has been an architect in privatizing the Iraqi education system, designed messenger apps intended to help overthrow the government of Cuba, served as a front group for the Blackwater mercenary force (now rebranded Academi), and liaised with Contra death squads in Nicaragua.[1] Professor Kenneth Saltman of the University of Illinois, Chicago, described it as functioning "both as an instrument of foreign policy and as a manifestation of a broader imperial project."[1]

## Ownership and funding

The company was founded in 1979 by M. Charito Kruvant, the scion of a wealthy landowning Bolivian family who fled the country after the 1952 revolution there.[1] It has grown into a for-profit behemoth working in at least 85 countries with roughly a thousand full-time staff and many more contractors.[1] Although technically private, the vast majority of its funding comes directly from Washington: over the past twenty years the government awarded it $1,998,138,515 in contracts according to journalist Tracey Eaton, of which [[usaid|USAID]] supplied over $1.8 billion.[1] Lange argues this makes it another scam in which taxpayers' money is used to fund America's enemies and oppress people in the third world.[1]

## Board and the foreign aid debate

Former USAID head Andrew Natsios, a member of CAI's board, argued vehemently against the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's consideration of cutting the foreign aid budget, warning that doing so would be "eviscerating the most important tool of American influence in the developing world."[1] Natsios added that "USAID's mission directors are among the most influential foreigners in the country" — a statement Lange reads as confirming that the organization's focus is less about helping others than promoting Washington's interests.[1]
