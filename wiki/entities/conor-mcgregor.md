# Conor McGregor

Conor McGregor is an Irish mixed martial arts fighter who, on St. Patrick's Day 2025, used an appearance at the [[white-house|White House]] to deliver a broadside against Ireland's handling of immigration.[1]

## White House appearance (March 17, 2025)

Invited by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], McGregor visited the White House dressed in a green business suit to mark St. Patrick's Day.[1] He praised Trump's work ethic as "inspiring," and in what was described as an unprecedented moment for a visiting athlete, took questions from the press about the problems facing the people of Ireland.[1]

McGregor argued that Ireland was on the "cusp" of losing its "Irishness."[1] "Our government is the government of zero action with zero accountability, our money is being spent on overseas issues that have nothing to do with the Irish people," he said, adding that "the illegal immigration racket is running ravage on the country, there are rural towns in Ireland that have been overrun in one swoop."[1] He appealed directly to the 40 million Irish Americans, warning that without action "there will be no place to come home and visit."[1] His remarks were condemned by Ireland's Taoiseach, Michael Martin.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands writers situated McGregor as a populist megastar who established himself circa 2016, and characterized his subsequent fall from grace in the public mind as "vicious and emphatic" in proportion as its underlying claims were unsubstantiated.[1] The White House invitation was read as a hint at the ubiquity — and the ending — of what the commentary called the anti-populist template.[1]

## November 2023 Dublin unrest

In November 2023, hours before a mass stabbing outside a Dublin school in which five people including three children were wounded, McGregor posted a series of messages attacking the Irish government over immigration.[2] He objected that Ireland's government was proposing to let non-residents vote in elections, writing: "US allows non residents to vote? Visitors can vote? ... Surely not. However, Ireland's current government is proposing this. A disgrace! ... The Working man is the real hero! Ireland, we are at war. Do not let any irish property be took over unannounced. Evaporate said property. It's a war."[2]

The attack, attributed to an Algerian immigrant who was subdued at the scene, was followed by [[2023-dublin-riots|riots in Dublin]] that made parts of the city impassable, and McGregor's remarks gained new prominence, with many calling for him to run for office.[2] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that while the media blamed "the far right," McGregor was "rightly" declaring that the citizens of [[ireland|Ireland]] were at war with their government.[2]

## Irish presidential bid (March 2025)

On Thursday, March 20, 2025, McGregor solidified his bid for Ireland's presidency with a promise to preserve his native country by tackling migration, days after meeting with [[donald-trump|President Trump]] at the [[white-house|White House]].[3] He decided to campaign as a leader in opposition to the European Migration Pact, which would create a streamlined approach to border security and immigration and speed up the asylum process for European Union members.[3]

"The next presidential election must take place by 11 Nov 2025. Who else will stand up to Government and oppose this bill," he wrote in a post on X. "Any other Presidential candidate they attempt to put forward will be of no resistance to them. I will!"[3]
