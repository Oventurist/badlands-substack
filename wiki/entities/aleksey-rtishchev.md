# Aleksey Rtishchev

**Major General Aleksey Rtishchev** is the head of Russia's Nuclear, Biological and Chemical Protection Troops, who in December 2025 alleged that the U.S. Agency for International Development ([[usaid|USAID]]) may have been involved in testing pharmaceutical drugs on Ukrainians.[1]

## Allegations

Speaking on Friday, December 12, 2025, Rtishchev said U.S. officials had acknowledged defense-related work at biological laboratories in Ukraine, naming among others former National Security Council spokesman John Kirby, former senior [[state-department|State Department]] official [[victoria-nuland|Victoria Nuland]], and Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.[1]

He cited an August interview in which Cornell University organic chemistry professor Dave Collum told journalist Tucker Carlson that pharmaceutical drugs had been tested on the Ukrainian population in 38 laboratories.[1]

"To ensure secrecy, the customers behind such research are not military agencies but civilian agencies and non-governmental organizations. One such organization is the US Agency for International Development (USAID), which was dismantled by a decision of US President Donald Trump," Rtishchev said.[1]

Rtishchev further asserted that USAID had provided funding for [[event-201|Event 201]], a pandemic simulation exercise focused on responding to a coronavirus outbreak, noting that the exercise was held in October 2019 "shortly before the start of the Covid-19 pandemic."[1]

## Successor to Kirillov and Africa briefing

Rtishchev succeeded the late Lieutenant General [[igor-kirillov|Igor Kirillov]] as Chief of Nuclear, Chemical, and Biological Protection Troops of the Russian Armed Forces after Kirillov was killed by a scooter bomb in December 2024.[2] In his capacity as Deputy Chief of Russia's Radiation, Chemical and Biological Protection Troops, he told a briefing in late December 2024 that documents available to the Russian Defense Ministry confirmed that "the US biological warfare presence on the African continent is growing at a rapid pace," with research institutes of the US Department of Defense active in the region.[2]

He described branches of the US National Naval Military Medical Center stationed in Ghana and Djibouti, a network of field stations deployed by the US Army Military Medical Center in Kenya to monitor infectious disease across Equatorial Africa, and a joint medical research centre and military medical laboratory established in Nigeria in 2024 with ten [[department-of-defense|Department of Defense]] specialists permanently on staff.[2] He identified [[metabiota|Metabiota]] as one of the "real clients" behind these military-biological projects and recounted allegations that the company's personnel concealed their engagement with [[pentagon|Pentagon]] employees during the 2014 Ebola outbreak in Sierra Leone and illegally exported Ebola samples to the US Army's Infectious Diseases Research Institute.[2] These claims are collected on the page for the alleged [[us-biological-warfare-presence-in-africa|US biological warfare presence in Africa]].[2]
