# Mark Carney

Mark Carney is a Canadian economist and central banker — former Governor of the Bank of Canada and of the Bank of England — who by September 2025 was serving as Prime Minister of Canada. In the Badlands Media essay [[a-re-alignment-of-world-systems|A Re-Alignment of World Systems]], [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents Carney as the figure who removed [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]] from the Canadian cabinet and as the current custodian of what the author calls the London-directed Great Game.

## The Freeland reassignment

The article reports that on September 16, 2025 Carney's office thanked Freeland for her service as Finance Minister and Deputy Prime Minister and announced: "I have asked Chrystia to serve as Canada's new Representative for the Reconstruction of Ukraine." Ehret, following the analyst John Helmer, describes the move as an ouster executed so rapidly that no staff or diplomatic rank had been arranged for the new envoy role.

## Background as described in the source

Carney is identified in the article as an Oxford-trained investment banker at Goldman Sachs who took part, alongside the IMF's Jeffrey Sachs, Vice President Al Gore and Rhodes Scholar Strobe Talbott, in the Western side of Perestroika — the wave of privatization and liberalization applied to the former Soviet bloc in the early 1990s.

## Assessment

The essay argues that Carney is pursuing a new Canadian agenda oriented toward friendship with China, in which Freeland's anti-China hawkishness had no place, but cautions that China should not trust this "new Canada." Ehret contends that Canada remains a chess piece in a British imperial strategy aimed at weakening the Russian-Chinese alliance, and closes by asking whether Carney "will be able to salvage the decaying British Empire and the dream of [[cecil-rhodes|Cecil Rhodes]] to recapture the USA in the 21st century." The article also notes that Canadian taxpayers had given $22 billion to the Ukraine proxy war since February 2022, indicating that Canada's anti-Russian position had not ended under Carney.
