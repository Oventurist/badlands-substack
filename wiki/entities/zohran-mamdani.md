# Zohran Mamdani

Zohran Mamdani is the Mayor of New York City, referred to in Badlands coverage by the nickname "#OurBoyMom."[1] In July 2026 he became a central figure in the narrative surrounding the [[international-criminal-court|International Criminal Court]]'s 2024 arrest warrant for Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[1]

## ICC arrest narrative

Mamdani released a video declaring Netanyahu a war criminal and stating his intent to have the prime minister arrested should he set foot in the state.[1] His administration reviewed what authority the city possessed, but Mamdani later clarified that New York City cannot itself execute an ICC arrest warrant.[1] Reuters reported that U.S. law provides no domestic mechanism obliging state or local officials to enforce ICC warrants, because the United States is not a party to the [[rome-statute|Rome Statute]].[1]

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly defended Netanyahu and said he would not be arrested while visiting the United States.[1] Netanyahu responded to Mamdani on Fox News, accusing the mayor of fomenting hatred and of antisemitism.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] placed the Mamdani episode on a timeline beginning with Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]]'s July 13 announcement of a campaign to dismantle the ICC, and asked whether Mamdani was working with Trump to generate these narrative developments or whether Trump was simply "playing the Game Theory board like a master."[1]

## Fiscal year 2027 budget reversal (May 2026)

In May 2026 Mamdani scrapped a planned property tax hike in a revised budget unveiled on a Tuesday, according to Bloomberg.[2] The unpopular measure had been floated to help close a roughly $5.4 billion two-year budget deficit.[2] Mamdani presented a $124.7 billion executive budget for the fiscal year beginning July 1, and instead of raising property taxes on New Yorkers the revised plan counts on revenue from second homes as an alternative funding source.[2] The decision marked a shift from earlier proposals and came amid ongoing negotiations over the city's spending plan.[2]

[[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] reported that Mamdani's preliminary budget proposal had earned the city a credit rating downgrade to negative, citing long-term structural problems, and that he had also proposed raiding the city's emergency savings account for $1 billion to help close the gap.[2] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that she was "enjoying this too much," arguing that the consequences of the policies must be felt for people to reject the ideology, and noted that the [[democratic-socialists-of-america|Democratic Socialists of America]] — who lionize Mamdani — were rising in her state with the goal of making Denver resemble New York City.[2] The reversal was framed as coming amid a great exodus of wealth from New York State.[2]

## FY2027 preliminary budget and property tax proposal

On Tuesday, February 17, 2026, Mamdani released a preliminary $127 billion budget for fiscal year 2027 that included a proposed 9.5 percent hike in property tax rates — the first such increase in more than two decades.[3] He described the move as a reluctant "last resort" to address a $5.4 billion deficit inherited from the previous administration, which he blamed on years of fiscal mismanagement.[3]

Mamdani laid out two paths for closing the gap: pressing Governor [[kathy-hochul|Kathy Hochul]] and state lawmakers in Albany to raise taxes on high earners and profitable corporations, or relying on the limited tools available to the city, which would mean a broad-based property tax increase on millions of homes and commercial properties together with withdrawals from reserve funds.[3] The proposed hike would generate roughly $3.7 billion.[3] City Comptroller [[mark-levine|Mark Levine]] called the property tax option "a pretty extreme option."[3]

## First executive orders and the Weaver appointment

On January 1, 2026, Mamdani signed an executive order "revitalizing" the [[mayors-office-to-protect-tenants|Mayor's Office to Protect Tenants]], the body that coordinates city agencies to defend tenants' rights, improve housing affordability, address complaints about unsafe or unlawful conditions, and hold landlords accountable.[4] He appointed [[cea-weaver|Cea Weaver]], former coordinator of "Housing Justice for All" and a key figure in passing New York's 2019 [[housing-stability-and-tenant-protection-act|Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act]], as the office's director.[4]

The appointment drew immediate criticism after clips resurfaced in which Weaver described home ownership as "rooted in a racist and classist society," argued for treating property as "a collective good," and said the city could tell a landlord "we're gonna take this building away from you."[4] Weaver reportedly deactivated her social media account amid the controversy.[4]

The same day, Mamdani issued a separate order revoking all remaining executive orders signed by his predecessor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] on or after September 26, 2024, Adams' indictment date — a step expected to affect various city policies.[4]

## Inauguration as mayor of New York City

Mamdani was inaugurated as the 112th mayor of New York City on January 1, 2026, marking several historic firsts: he is the city's first Muslim mayor, its first of South Asian descent, and the first born in Africa, in Kampala, Uganda.[5] The inauguration occurred in two parts, beginning with a private midnight swearing-in shortly after New Year's, administered by New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]] in the decommissioned old City Hall subway station, where Mamdani placed his hand on two Qurans — one belonging to his grandfather and another from the Schomburg Center.[5] A larger public ceremony followed in the afternoon on the steps of City Hall, where U.S. Senator [[bernie-sanders|Bernie Sanders]] administered the ceremonial oath, accompanied by an open-to-the-public "Inauguration of a New Era" block party along Broadway in the Canyon of Heroes that drew tens of thousands despite cold weather, with performances by Javier Muñoz singing the national anthem and Mandy Patinkin with a school chorus.[5] Notable attendees and speakers included Representative [[alexandria-ocasio-cortez|Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez]], who introduced the event, Public Advocate Jumaane Williams, former mayors [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] and Bill de Blasio, Senator [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]], and Mamdani's family — his wife Rama Duwaji, his mother, the filmmaker Mira Nair, and his father, the academic Mahmood Mamdani.[5]

In his inaugural address, Mamdani — a self-described democratic socialist and former state assemblyman — vowed to govern "expansively and audaciously," emphasizing the uplift of working-class New Yorkers, affordability and especially housing, and the ushering in of a "new era" of progressive politics.[5] He promised bold action on campaign pledges such as tenant protections and rejected any moderation of his vision while pledging to represent all New Yorkers, and immediately afterward signed executive orders focused on housing, including reviving an office to protect tenants and establishing task forces to accelerate development.[5] Badlands coverage highlighted his vow to "replace the frigidity of rugged individualism with the warmth of collectivism."[5]

## Antisemitism policy and the IHRA definition

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] noted reporting that while Mamdani said he would keep the Office to Combat Antisemitism, its function would likely shift, because his revocation of prior executive orders included Adams's endorsement of the [[ihra-definition-of-antisemitism|International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance definition of antisemitism]], which encompasses some forms of criticism of [[israel|Israel]].[5] Writing "#OurBoyMam strikes again," he called the move a lesson for Republican governors, observing that state legislatures across the country have officially endorsed the IHRA definition even as it has been changed and updated since those laws and executive orders were signed, and asking why governments outsource their guidance to NGOs when that guidance is fluid and subject to change.[5]

## Plan for social workers on 911 calls

As mayor-elect in November 2025, Mamdani announced plans to deploy social workers on 911 calls instead of police officers, an approach whose stated mission, per the initiative's proposal, is "to prevent violence before it happens by taking a public health approach to safety."[6] One America News reported that the plan "does not look promising," pointing to the city's existing [[b-heard|Behavioral Health Emergency Assistance Response Division (B-HEARD)]] pilot, launched in 2021 and still limited to some neighborhoods, whose teams pair two FDNY officers and EMTs with one social worker.[6] A city comptroller audit found that more than 60% of calls received were ineligible for a B-HEARD response and that 35% of eligible calls went unserviced, with reasons unknown because the Mayor's Office of Community Mental Health does not track them.[6]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the coming New York experiment would be instructive, noting that her own Democratic Socialist–run Colorado government idolizes the new mayor and asking New Yorkers to "set a dramatic example with a blast radius that reaches the Rocky Mountains."[6] She recounted arguing with normie New Yorkers during travel, including a Coast Guard veteran who was on active duty in New York on 9/11 and took offense at her suggestion that Mamdani's presumably short tenure would benefit Republicans in the midterms.[6] She closed by advising residents to take personal precautions because dialing 911 was likely to become even more unreliable.[6]

## November 2025 White House meeting with Trump

On Friday, November 21, 2025, as mayor-elect, Mamdani met President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at the [[white-house|White House]] in an encounter The New York Times described as a "break-the-internet moment" that scrambled the online MAGA ecosystem.[7] What Fox News had billed as a "socialist showdown" instead featured smiles, good vibes, and Trump's endorsement of Mamdani's leadership, with the president telling reporters, "I feel very confident that he can do a very good job."[7] With no ideological confrontation to rally around, MAGA-aligned influencers and commentators struggled to summon a unified response and were uncharacteristically muted.[7]

Badlands writer [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] used the episode to catalogue what he described as a recurring pattern of online influencers alternately trashing and pretending to support Trump across the 2016, 2020, and 2024 cycles, concluding that the "Trump needs to..." influencer crowd should be disregarded.[7]

## Election as mayor and clash with the White House (November 2025)

CNN projected on November 4, 2025 that Mamdani would win New York City's mayoral race, making him the city's first Muslim mayor and the first South Asian to hold the office, as part of a Democratic sweep of the first major elections of Trump's second term.[8] On the same day, as New Jersey voters cast ballots in the gubernatorial race won by [[mikie-sherrill|Mikie Sherrill]], bomb threats — later found to be non-credible — forced voters out of multiple polling places.[8] Mamdani called the threats "incredibly concerning" and "an illustration of the attacks we're seeing on our democracy," adding: "We have to understand this as part of the general approach the Trump administration has taken to trying to intimidate voters with baseless allegations of voter fraud as a means of trying to repress the voice of Americans across this country."[8] White House press secretary [[karoline-leavitt|Karoline Leavitt]] called the remarks "completely irresponsible" and not based in fact.[8] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] dismissed the episode with the line that "stories of bomb threats are stories only. Always."[8]

## Closing rally with Letitia James (October 2025)

On Monday, October 13, 2025, Mamdani — then the Democratic front-runner for mayor of New York City — held a Manhattan rally billed as the closing argument of his campaign, sharing the stage with New York Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]].[9] The two presented themselves as a united front against President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], assailing his agenda and arguing that he was pursuing political retribution.[9] Coverage described the event as capturing Mamdani's improbable rise from unknown state lawmaker to Democratic nominee, propelled by a grass-roots volunteer army and, later, by unions and more mainstream Democratic figures such as James.[9] "My friends," he concluded, "our time is now."[9]

James's participation carried added weight because she had been indicted the previous week on federal charges of bank fraud and making a false statement, and the rally was her first public appearance since.[9] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the pairing as an indicted official teaming with "an overt communist," arguing that abolition of private property as government policy would violate the Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments and that both figures had shown hostility to the [[us-constitution|Constitution]] — noting that Mamdani, unlike James, had not yet sworn an oath of office.[9]

## October 2025 mayoral debate controversy

Mamdani came under fire following the first general mayoral election debate in October 2025, at which former Governor [[andrew-cuomo|Andrew Cuomo]] — running as an independent — attacked him over his stance on Israel and Gaza and his refusal to denounce [[hamas|Hamas]] or the phrase "Globalize the intifada."[10] "The Assemblyman will not denounce Hamas," Cuomo said, adding that Mamdani would also not denounce [[hasan-piker|Hasan Piker]], "who said America deserved 9/11."[10] Cuomo further quoted Mamdani's response on the ceasefire deal brokered by the Trump administration as "Well, it depends on occupation."[10]

The attacks came as Mamdani, a socialist state assemblyman from Queens and the front-runner in the race, took blows over his belated response to the landmark peace deal announced that week; the anti-Israel critic called for an end to "occupation and apartheid" in his statement while living Israeli hostages were returned after two years in Hamas captivity.[10] He also refused to say that the terrorist group should lay down its weapons during a Fox News interview.[10] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] dismissed the framing, arguing that "based'n'redpilled" influencers had been reduced to calling people antisemitic in hopes of beating "the left" in what he called fake elections.[10]

## Trump opposition and DOJ foreign-interference review (November 2025)

On the eve of the [[new-york-city-mayoral-election-2025|November 4, 2025 New York City mayoral election]], President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] endorsed independent candidate [[andrew-cuomo|Andrew Cuomo]] specifically to block Mamdani, whom he described as a "Communist," and warned that a Mamdani victory would make it "highly unlikely" that he would contribute federal funds to the city beyond the required minimum.[11] Trump said he would rather see "a Democrat, who has had a Record of Success, WIN, than a Communist with no experience and a Record of COMPLETE AND TOTAL FAILURE," dismissing Mamdani's tenure as an assemblyman as "nothing" and "ranked at the bottom of the class," and stated that a vote for Republican [[curtis-sliwa|Curtis Sliwa]] was effectively a vote for Mamdani.[11]

Separately, the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] reviewed concerns over a November 2 phone-banking event featuring Mamdani and former British Labour Party leader [[jeremy-corbyn|Jeremy Corbyn]], organized by the New York City chapter of the [[democratic-socialists-of-america|Democratic Socialists of America]] on the Action Network platform.[11] Federal law bars foreign nationals from contributing money to or making decisions for campaigns, though [[federal-election-commission|Federal Election Commission]] guidelines permit unpaid volunteer work in limited circumstances; legal experts said Corbyn's participation likely did not violate the law, and no formal investigation was launched.[11] Badlands host GMONEY, noting reports that a million New Yorkers claimed they would leave the city if Mamdani won, welcomed a Mamdani victory.[11]

## Resignation of appointments director

In December 2025, one of Mayor-elect Mamdani's new appointees resigned after just one day on the job over decade-old social media posts invoking antisemitic tropes.[12] Newly appointed Director of Appointments [[catherine-almonte-da-costa|Catherine Almonte Da Costa]] tendered her resignation on the afternoon of December 18, 2025, hours after posts including "Money hungry Jews smh" and "Far Rockaway train is the Jew train" were surfaced by the Judge Street Journal.[12]

The episode compounded Mamdani's difficulties with Jewish communities, which had staunchly opposed his candidacy over his past criticism of Israel and his failure to condemn the phrase "globalize the intifada," and drew a rebuke from the [[anti-defamation-league|Anti-Defamation League]]'s New York and New Jersey chapter, which called the posts "indefensible."[12]

## Outreach to Trump before taking office (November 2025)

In an interview aired Tuesday, November 11, 2025, Mamdani said he intended to hold a call with President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] before formally assuming office as Mayor of New York City.[13] "I will be proactive," he said, describing an approach aimed at deterring the federal government from deploying [[immigration-and-customs-enforcement|ICE]] agents or National Guardsmen to the city, and adding, "I will be reaching out to the [[white-house|White House]] as we prepare to actually take office because this is a relationship that will be critical to the success of the city."[13] He also said he had been in discussions with Governor [[kathy-hochul|Kathy Hochul]] about strategies for responding to potential federal actions by the Trump administration.[13]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Mamdani would be terrible for New Yorkers but declined to blame voters "because elections are fake," arguing that if he governs the city as a Democratic Socialist he should improve national Republican midterm prospects — an outcome that likewise could not be credited to voters.[13] The underlying point was that without verifiably accurate elections no outcome can be trusted, a condition the commentary likened to a narrative return to the 1860s, closing with "Mammany Hall: Accelerate."[13]

## New York Post opposition (August 2025)

In an August 7, 2025 editorial urging President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] to endorse Mayor [[eric-adams|Eric Adams]] for re-election, the [[new-york-post|New York Post]] editorial board described Mamdani as a socialist and "defund the police" activist who would reverse the city's public-safety gains.[14] The board argued he would hobble the [[new-york-city-police-department|NYPD]], drive away businesses and make the streets unsafe, concluding that "Mamdani must be stopped."[14] The editorial cited the lowest number of shootings on record for the first half of 2025 as progress it said was at risk.[14]

## White House meeting with Trump (November 2025)

On Friday, November 21, 2025, Mamdani met President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the Oval Office, and the two men appeared to set aside their differences in what CBS News called a remarkable scene, saying afterward that they looked forward to working together after months of bruising public criticism.[15] Speaking to reporters, both described a productive conversation focused mostly on improving New York; "the better he does, the happier I am," Trump said, while Mamdani, who takes office on January 1, said he was "really looking forward to delivering for New Yorkers in partnership with the president on the affordability agenda."[15] Trump added that "some of his ideas are really the same ideas that I have" and that "we agree on a lot more than I would have thought. I want him to do a great job, and we'll help him do a great job."[15] The tone contrasted sharply with months of hostility, during which Trump — a native New Yorker — had opposed Mamdani's campaign, threatened to arrest and deport him, and repeatedly called him a communist, while Mamdani accused the president of acting like a "despot."[15]

Badlands commentators used the meeting as a lens on political theater. [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] observed that the news cycle was telling audiences Trump had a better relationship with a communist than with [[marjorie-taylor-greene|Marjorie Taylor Greene]], whom Trump had labeled a traitor.[15] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] tied the meeting to his framing of [[kayfabe|kayfabe]] and the information battlespace, arguing that most public figures respond continuously to an incentive structure and that the real question is who controls that structure.[15] He cautioned against dismissing a figure merely because their branding is an identity audiences have been trained to hate, noted that he had predicted Mamdani's primary victory because he was the only true populist in the field, and remarked on the irony that Mamdani's most contested position concerns Israel in a city with one of the world's largest Jewish populations.[15]

## Democratic mayoral primary victory (June 2025)

As first-choice votes were tallied in the New York City Democratic mayoral primary held in June 2025, Mamdani, then a state assemblyman, led the field ahead of former New York Governor [[andrew-cuomo|Andrew Cuomo]], who declared his rival the winner even though no candidate was set to secure a majority in the first round of the [[ranked-choice-voting|ranked choice]] election.[16] Mamdani had surged in the race's final weeks while touting a progressive vision for the city that included rent freezes, city-run grocery stores, price caps, and free city buses.[16] He boosted his appeal with energetic direct-to-camera videos and gestures such as spending a weekend night before Election Day walking the length of Manhattan, stopping to chat with voters and record clips along the way, and he won the backing of prominent liberal politicians in the city as the flag-bearer of a unified progressive effort aimed at denying Cuomo a political comeback.[16]

Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] dismissed the result as a "Muslim socialist wins fake election decided in comic fashion against Andrew Cuomo," calling the outcome "hilarious."[16]

## Criticism over crime policy (August 2025)

Badlands commentary in August 2025 described Mamdani as New York City's "socialist mayor-in-waiting," accusing him of pushing a "crime carnival" by downgrading violent misdemeanors as "non-serious" acts of survival and of framing policing as oppression.[17] The commentary argued this invited San Francisco-style collapse to the city, and placed Mamdani alongside Maryland Governor [[wes-moore|Wes Moore]] as an opposition figure President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] deliberately spotlights to build a mandate against him.[17]

## Oval Office meeting with Trump (November 2025)

President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] agreed to meet Mayor-elect Mamdani for the first time at the [[white-house|White House]] on Friday, November 21, 2025, an unexpected sit-down with the Democratic socialist whose election he had opposed.[18] Trump announced the meeting on Truth Social, writing: "Communist Mayor of New York City, Zohran 'Kwame' Mamdani, has asked for a meeting. We have agreed that this meeting will take place at the Oval Office on Friday, November 21st."[18] Trump had urged New York voters to back Mamdani's opponent, former Governor Andrew Cuomo, and had repeatedly attacked him publicly.[18] Mamdani spokesperson Dora Pekec said the two would discuss public safety, economic security and "the affordability agenda that over one million New Yorkers voted for just two weeks ago"; Mamdani said he had reached out to the White House to discuss affordability, a central plank of his campaign.[18]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] warned the meeting could "undermine everything for which we've worked so hard," arguing that "Mammany Hall" needed to serve as a cautionary example for the nation and that legitimizing the Democratic Socialists of America in the Oval Office amounted to "unity with communists," which she called treason.[18]
