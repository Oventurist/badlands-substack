# Kazakhstan

Kazakhstan is a Central Asian state whose enormous state-controlled uranium reserves sit at the origin point of the [[uranium-one-deal|Uranium One deal]], according to the Badlands Media series *America for Sale*.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) In the Badlands account, the chain of transactions that eventually handed Russia roughly one-fifth of United States uranium production capacity did not begin in Wyoming or Washington but in Almaty in September 2005.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## The 2005 Almaty trip

Citing the *New York Times*, the article states that "the path to a Russian acquisition of American uranium deposits began in 2005 in Kazakhstan," where the Canadian mining financier [[frank-giustra|Frank Giustra]] orchestrated his first major uranium deal with [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] at his side.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The two men flew aboard Giustra's private jet to Almaty and dined with Kazakhstan's authoritarian president, [[nursultan-nazarbayev|Nursultan Nazarbayev]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Clinton publicly endorsed Nazarbayev's bid to head an international elections-monitoring organisation — a statement the *Times* characterised as a propaganda coup that undercut American foreign policy and the criticism of Kazakhstan's human rights record voiced by others, including his wife, then a sitting senator.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

Within days of the visit, Giustra's fledgling company [[urasia-energy|UrAsia Energy Ltd.]] signed a preliminary deal giving it stakes in three uranium mines controlled by the state-run uranium agency [[kazatomprom|Kazatomprom]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) Badlands describes this as a "sweetheart deal" obtained in exchange for a kickback, and notes that several months later Giustra donated $31.3 million to the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] — characterised in the article as *quid pro quo*.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## Kazakh mines as the crown jewel

The article emphasises that the Kazakh mines, not the American ones, were the most lucrative assets involved: the *New York Times* described mines in Kazakhstan "that are among the most lucrative in the world" as the prize alongside the U.S. production capacity transferred to Russian control.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The Kazakh holdings were the asset that [[urasia-energy|UrAsia]] carried into its 2007 merger with [[uranium-one|Uranium One]], a transaction valued at roughly $3.5 billion.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

Kazakhstan reappears later in the story: [[ian-telfer|Ian Telfer]]'s Fernwood Foundation reported a $1 million donation in 2009, the year Telfer's company appealed to the American Embassy for help keeping its Kazakh mines.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3) The Russian investor [[sergei-kurzin|Sergei Kurzin]], a former employee of [[marc-rich|Marc Rich]], is likewise described as having been involved in lucrative deals in Kazakhstan and in the Uranium One transaction itself.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/america-for-sale-part-3)

## 2025 Central Asia Summit

Kazakhstan was one of the five Central Asian states whose leaders President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] welcomed to the White House at the [[central-asia-summit-2025|2025 Central Asia Summit]] in early November 2025, alongside Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and [[uzbekistan|Uzbekistan]].[2] The summit reflected a US foreign policy calculus centered on supply chain security, critical minerals, and new transport corridors designed to bypass both Russia and China, while offering the participating governments political recognition, investment, and leverage among competing great powers.[2]

## 2025 locomotive deal and strategic geography

On September 22, 2025, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] announced that Kazakhstan had signed a [[kazakhstan-united-states-locomotive-deal|$4 billion deal]] to purchase American locomotives and rail equipment, which he called "the largest railroad equipment purchase in history," following a phone call with President [[kassym-jomart-tokayev|Kassym-Jomart Tokayev]].[3] Earlier that day Tokayev met in New York with US Presidential Special Envoy for South and Central Asia [[sergio-gore|Sergio Gore]] and a [[meta-platforms|Meta]] vice president, reaffirming Kazakhstan's role as Washington's main economic partner in Central Asia.[3]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry described Kazakhstan as sitting inside the triangle formed by the capitals of Russia, China, and Iran — the alliance [[zbigniew-brzezinski|Zbigniew Brzezinski]] warned about in [[the-grand-chessboard|*The Grand Chessboard*]] (1998).[3] He noted the 2022 coup in Kazakhstan, which he interpreted as [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s move to take benevolent control of the country, and identified the capital Astana as the site of the long-running [[astana-process|Astana Process]] concerning Syria.[3] He argued Kazakhstan's flat terrain makes it an ideal site for large infrastructure projects and noted that it is the world's largest producer of uranium, speculating about a future railroad running from Alaska through Kazakhstan to the Middle East and Africa.[3]
