# Kathy Hochul

Kathy Hochul is the Governor of New York, who in May 2026 signed legislation preserving state authority over vaccine access and requirements amid federal policy shifts.[1]

## Vaccine legislation

On Friday, May 15, 2026, Hochul signed two bills into law.[1] The first expands pharmacist authority to administer COVID-19 vaccines to children ages 2 to 18.[1] The second requires health insurers to cover all vaccines recommended by the New York State Commissioner of Health, decoupling state policy from the federal Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) guidelines, which had recently reduced routine recommendations from 17 to 11 diseases.[1]

The bills also preserve state authority over school immunization schedules, newborn vaccination protocols, and related requirements.[1] Hochul and state health officials framed the action as safeguarding public health tools against federal changes under the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration and ensuring New Yorkers retain broad access to immunizations.[1] The measures followed earlier executive orders expanding vaccine administration, and drew criticism from some who view the laws as overreach or as resistance to national policy adjustments.[1] Core cooperation on other public health issues was reported to be continuing.[1]

## Pressure from New York City over the FY2027 budget

In February 2026 Mayor [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]] identified Hochul and state lawmakers in Albany as one of two possible routes to closing New York City's $5.4 billion budget deficit, urging them to raise taxes on high earners and profitable corporations.[2] The alternative, which Mamdani built into his preliminary $127 billion fiscal 2027 budget, was a 9.5 percent property tax increase and withdrawals from reserve funds — a step he described as a reluctant last resort if Albany did not act.[2]

## Retail theft legislation (2024)

In April 2024 the [[bodega-and-small-business-group|Bodega and Small Business Group]], representing thousands of New York City bodegas, cited the failure of Hochul's public safety agenda in Albany when endorsing [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[3] The group noted that legislative Democrats had formally rejected Hochul's proposal to make it a felony to assault a retail worker, describing the rejection as "a reflection of how the party remains split over criminal justice issues and how to address voters' concerns over public safety."[3]

## Response to the 2024 drone sightings

In mid-December 2024, amid [[2024-northeast-drone-sightings|swarms of unidentified drones over the tri-state region]], Hochul announced that the federal government was finally "deploying a state-of-the-art drone detection system" to New York.[4] She did not elaborate on what the system was, or how and where it would be used, but stressed that the area still needed more help to address the baffling sightings.[4] Senator [[chuck-schumer|Chuck Schumer]] followed her announcement by calling for the [[department-of-homeland-security|Department of Homeland Security]] to send the declassified Robin 360-degree radar.[4]

## Congestion pricing appeal to Trump (2025)

On Wednesday, February 26, 2025 Hochul told a laughing [[metropolitan-transportation-authority|Metropolitan Transportation Authority]] board that she had used "big words" and "nice colors" in a presentation to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] during a White House visit the previous week, hoping an attractive framing of New York City's congestion pricing plan would persuade him to drop his opposition.[5] The plea did not work, and the federal [[us-department-of-transportation|Department of Transportation]] sent a letter that Wednesday to top New York transportation officials informing them that termination of the plan would take effect on March 21.[5] Hochul signalled defiance: "We're not done. We are in court… you may have asked for 'orderly cessation'… I will propose something in the alternative: 'orderly resistance.' We will keep standing up for New Yorkers."[5]

## Linda Sun prosecution (2024)

In September 2024, Hochul's former deputy chief of staff [[linda-sun|Linda Sun]] was charged with acting as an illegal agent of the Chinese government, alongside her husband Chris Hu; both pleaded not guilty in Brooklyn.[6] Prosecutors alleged Sun used her New York state government positions to further Beijing's agenda in exchange for compensation and gifts worth millions of dollars.[6] In a WNYC interview Hochul said she was "furious… outraged and absolutely shocked at how brazen her behavior was," calling it "a betrayal of trust—the trust of government, the trust of the people," and stating that her office had fired Sun the previous year upon discovering misconduct and alerted the authorities immediately.[6] Former Governor [[andrew-cuomo|Andrew Cuomo]] also responded to the charges.[6]
