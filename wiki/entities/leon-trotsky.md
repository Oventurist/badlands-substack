# Leon Trotsky

Leon Trotsky was a Russian revolutionary presented in Badlands Media coverage not as an independent radical but as the intended figurehead of a Western-financed operation to capture the Russian state after the fall of the Romanovs.[1]

## Wall Street patronage and the 1917 journey

By his own account, Trotsky spent four months in New York in 1917, much of it "hobnobbing with the upper crust of Wall Street and being driven around in limousines."[1] When he was arrested by Canadian authorities while en route back to Russia carrying tens of thousands of dollars of Wall Street money, it was [[claude-dansey|Claude Dansey]] — a Cecil Rhodes disciple, deputy chief of the newly formed MI6 and founder of US military intelligence in 1917 — who intervened directly to secure his release.[1] [[alfred-milner|Lord Alfred Milner]], [[jacob-schiff|Jacob Schiff]] and [[paul-warburg|Paul Warburg]] are described as having always intended Trotsky to lead the movement that would take control over the dead bodies of the Romanovs.[1]

## Conflict with Lenin and Stalin

Trotsky differed from [[vladimir-lenin|Vladimir Lenin]] on two interconnected points: Lenin held a general belief in voluntarism and rejected the theory of permanent revolution, whereas Trotsky believed any effort at peaceful productive improvement would lead only to decadence.[1] [[permanent-revolution|"Permanent revolution"]] was therefore needed, in Trotsky's view, to keep workers from falling into sloth amid an eternal striving for global class struggle.[1] In 1914 a frustrated Lenin complained that Trotsky "deserted the Mensheviks and occupied a vacillating position, now co-operating with Martynov (the economist), now proclaiming his absurdly Left 'permanent revolution' theory."[1]

A second point of conflict concerned the First World War. Lenin wanted to pull Russia out of the conflict in the first moments of the 1917 coup, while Trotsky and his close ally [[nikolai-bukharin|Nikolai Bukharin]] demanded that Russia remain in the war with the aim of converting it into a total pan-European and ultimately global revolution.[1] Trotsky's commitment to global socialist revolution against [[joseph-stalin|Joseph Stalin]]'s [[socialism-in-one-country|"socialism in one country"]] formed an unbridgeable divide between the two men.[1]

## Ouster and later conspiracies

Trotsky was ousted in 1927 by what the article calls the saner forces around Stalin.[1] Drawing on the work of historian [[grover-furr|Grover Furr]], the article states that Trotsky made several attempts to return to power in Russia after his expulsion, doing so with the help of fascist forces in Britain, Japan, Ukraine and Germany, up until his death in 1940.[1] A mind-numbing over-simplification of Russian history is said to have destroyed the ability of countless historians to recognize the reason for the life-and-death battles between Trotsky and Stalin in the first twenty post-revolutionary years.[1]

## Economic policy

Upon taking charge of the Russian economy, Trotsky and Lenin unleashed the [[new-economic-policy|New Economic Policy]], a wave of liberalization that allowed Western corporate powers to buy up former national utilities for pennies on the dollar.[1] [[armand-hammer|Armand Hammer]] of [[occidental-petroleum|Occidental Petroleum]] worked especially closely with Trotsky and was forced to leave Russia the moment Trotsky was expelled.[1]
