# Edgar Buell

Edgar "Pop" Buell was an Indiana farmer from Steuben County who went to Laos in 1960 as an agricultural volunteer for International Voluntary Services (IVS) and became, in the account cited by Badlands Media, a participant in the [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]'s clandestine operations there.[1]

## Laos and the Meo

Quoting the drug-policy archive DrugText.org, Badlands described Buell as "the most curious of this CIA triumvirate."[1] He was assigned to the Plain of Jars, where the CIA was building up its secret Meo (Hmong) army, and became involved in the agency's activities "largely through circumstance and his own God-given anti-Communism."[1] As part of an effort to strengthen the Meo economy and increase the tribe's effectiveness as a military force, Buell used his agricultural skills to improve Meo techniques for planting and cultivating opium, reportedly telling them, "If you're gonna grow it, grow it good."[1]

Roger Warner's book *Shooting at the Moon: The Story of America's Clandestine War in Laos* was quoted describing Buell's departure: he would be in Laos for two years doing "people-to-people" work, showing farmers "modern farming methods and at the same time a better way of life."[1] Buell noted that Laotian farming was 150 to 300 years behind American methods, relying on hoes and wooden plows drawn by oxen and elephants, and that opium and rice were the country's chief exports.[1]

## Significance in the Badlands account

Badlands writer [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] presented Buell as concrete proof that the CIA was actively participating in the opium trade during the [[secret-war-in-laos|secret war in Laos]], noting that the agency "had literally recruited a farmer from Indiana to go to Laos in order to help them improve the opium production."[1] The episode is offered as evidence that the CIA was becoming the largest drug trafficker in the world and was willing to fight covert wars to protect that trade.[1]
