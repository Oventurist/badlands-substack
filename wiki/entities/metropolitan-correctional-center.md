# Metropolitan Correctional Center

The Metropolitan Correctional Center (MCC) was a federal detention facility in New York City operated by the [[federal-bureau-of-prisons|Bureau of Prisons]]. It is best known publicly as the jail where financier [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] died on August 10, 2019, while awaiting trial on federal sex trafficking charges.[1]

## Epstein's death and the Inspector General report

A [[department-of-justice-office-of-inspector-general|Justice Department Inspector General]] report released in June 2023 detailed the failures at MCC beginning one month before Epstein's death, and concluded both that Epstein died by suicide and that foul play "was not possible."[1] According to the report, Officer Michael Thomas — who was charged criminally in the case — said "Breathe, Epstein, Breathe" when officers found Epstein unresponsive in his cell, and on seeing him dangling from the bed remarked, "We're going to be in a lot of trouble."[1] The report stated that Epstein hanged himself with an orange noose fashioned from "a sheet or a shirt," and that a search of his cell afterward turned up extra bedsheets, another mattress and a noose.[1]

## Commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the account as "a series of unfortunate coincidences," noting that MCC was understaffed and underfunded and that the failures amounted to incompetence beyond belief in the case of the world's most wanted villain.[1] She catalogued the specific lapses: cameras that did not work and were known not to work, the removal of Epstein's cellmate despite a rule that he not be left alone, guards skipping required checks, the moving of the body, and photographs showing a startling quantity of linens available to an inmate on suicide watch.[1] She singled out the report's inability, after months of investigation, to determine whether the noose was made from a shirt or a sheet, calling the document not merely silly but insulting to public intelligence, and closed with the rhetorical question of whether 51 former intelligence officials had contributed to it.[1]
