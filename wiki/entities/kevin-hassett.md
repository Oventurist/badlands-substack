# Kevin Hassett

Kevin Hassett is an American economist serving as Director of the [[white-house|White House]] National Economic Council in the second [[trump-administration|Trump administration]].[1]

## Statements on the Iran war

In March 2026, Hassett said the war with [[iran|Iran]] had cost the United States about $12 billion to that point, adding that the administration currently had the funding it needed but might seek additional money from [[us-congress|Congress]].[1]

Hassett argued that the American economy would not be harmed by Iranian actions, contrasting the situation with the energy shocks of the 1970s: "in the '70s we didn't produce much oil, but now we do. So America is in a very strong position."[1] He said Iranian leaders who believed they could damage the U.S. economy and force [[donald-trump|President Trump]] to back down were mistaken, noting that America's trading partners were far more exposed to imported oil than the United States itself.[1] He called it "completely unacceptable" that a government which, in his account, had murdered 40,000 of its own people months earlier would blackmail countries over shipping, adding that the administration intended to "fix it and we are going to fix it fast."[1]

Badlands commentary drew on Hassett's remarks to argue that the three parties insulated from the energy shock — the United States, [[russia|Russia]] and Iran — were precisely the energy-independent ones, leaving Europe as the party with no winning move.[1]

## Federal Reserve chair speculation (January 2026)

As Director of the National Economic Council, Hassett was named among the candidates to replace [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] as Chair of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] in January 2026, on a shortlist vetted by Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]].[2] He was reported to be the early favorite in some accounts, though President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] indicated a preference to keep him in his current role.[2] Like the other contenders — [[kevin-warsh|Kevin Warsh]], [[christopher-waller|Christopher Waller]], and [[rick-rieder|Rick Rieder]] — Hassett was described as supportive of cutting interest rates.[2]

## Defense of the April 2025 tariffs

As Director of the [[national-economic-council|National Economic Council]], Hassett defended President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s sweeping global tariffs in a 6 April 2025 interview on ABC's "This Week," saying he did not believe there would be a "big effect on the consumer in the U.S." and noting that more than 50 countries were "coming to the table" to negotiate.[3] His remarks followed a market rout in which the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 2,200 points and the S&P 500 lost 10 percent over two days, leaving vulnerable House Republicans on the defensive.[3]


## Powell Removal Comments (April 2025)

As Director of the National Economic Council, Hassett said on Friday, April 18, 2025, when asked whether removing [[jerome-powell|Jerome Powell]] was an option, that "the president and his team will continue to study that."[4] The remark was cited by traders as the trigger for a sudden collapse of the dollar index to a three-year low in illiquid early Asian trading over the Easter holiday, alongside a record gold price and a spike in [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]].[4] Hassett further argued that the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] under Powell — a Trump first-term appointee — had acted politically to benefit Democrats, raising rates immediately after Trump's first election on the theory that supply-side tax cuts would be inflationary, declining to warn about "runaway spending" under Joe Biden, and then cutting rates just before the 2024 election.[4]

## Fed chair speculation (August 2025)

As [[national-economic-council|National Economic Council]] Director, Hassett was among the leading contenders to chair the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] in 2025, with his implied probability on the prediction market [[kalshi|Kalshi]] standing at about 35 percent on August 7, 2025 — ahead of former Fed official [[kevin-warsh|Kevin Warsh]] at roughly 20 percent but behind Governor [[christopher-waller|Christopher Waller]], who had just crossed 50 percent.[5]
