# Lanz von Liebenfels

Lanz von Liebenfels was an Austrian follower of Theosophy who officially created the [[new-templar-order|New Templar Order]] in 1907 and founded the occult journal Ostara.[1]

A former Cistercian monk, Liebenfels built his order on Cistercian principles in imitation of the original Templars founded by Bernard de Clairvaux, and established its first headquarters at Werfenstein Castle, decorated in honour of Hugues de Payens, the first Templar Grand Master.[1] He is depicted in Cistercian garb alongside the logo of the New Templar cult.[1]

Through Ostara, Liebenfels popularized [[helena-blavatsky|Blavatsky's]] Theosophical cosmology and a racist spiritual eugenics for a German audience, emphasizing a Nordic Wotan pagan ethos rather than the Indian cosmology favoured by the Theosophists.[1] Ostara found an enthusiastic subscriber in the young [[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]].[1] The organization Liebenfels created alongside Otto von List was named "The Ariosophists," and retained eastern symbology including the swastika alongside an array of druidic runes.[1]

## Ostara and racial theology

Ehret identifies Liebenfels, alongside [[guido-von-list|Guido von List]], as one of the [[ariosophy|Ariosophist]] high priests driving the esoteric Luciferian inner core of [[helena-blavatsky|Blavatsky]]'s followers.[2] He published a Teutonic mystical magazine called *Ostara*, to which a young [[adolf-hitler|Adolf Hitler]] was a devoted subscriber.[2] Together with the Theosophical Aryan supremacist Franz Hartmann he promoted a German branch of the New Templars, and both associated closely with the [[thule-society|Thule Society]].[2]

The occult scholar Samuel Koehne describes Liebenfels as supporting the notion of Christianity as a "racial cult religion," interpreting the Bible through an exegesis in which the Old Testament taught the dangers of racial admixture and Moses figured as a "Darwinist" and preacher of "racial morality," rewriting the Ten Commandments as laws for racial purity and advocating a dualistic heresy of Good and Evil typified by Aryan race-men and their savior Frauja — a Gothic name for Jesus — who calls for the sacrificial extermination of "sub-men," the "apelings," and all other racial inferiors.[2]
