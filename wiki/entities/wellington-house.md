# Wellington House

**Wellington House**, also known as Britain's War Propaganda Bureau, was established in 1914 as the United Kingdom's central organ for wartime propaganda during the First World War.[1] Future Prime Minister David Lloyd George appointed the writer and fellow Liberal Member of Parliament [[charles-masterman|Charles Masterman]] to head the organization, which operated from Wellington House in London under the supervision of the British Foreign Office.[1]

Masterman invited 25 leading British authors to discuss the best ways of promoting Britain's interests during the war, and their biggest single focus was luring the United States into the conflict — most countries, the US included, having wished to stay out of it.[1] Famous authors who attended included Arthur Conan Doyle, G. K. Chesterton, Thomas Hardy and H. G. Wells; Rudyard Kipling was invited but unable to attend.[1] Propaganda created by some of the greatest literary minds in England was then distributed throughout the world, again with the primary aim of convincing Americans to ally with Great Britain against Germany.[1]

Badlands Media contributor [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] treats this arrangement as an early demonstration that propaganda is mind control, and points to H. G. Wells's *War of the Worlds* (1898) and Orson Welles's 1938 radio adaptation as, in his opinion, a test of how effectively propaganda could be used to create trauma.[1] He argues that America has effectively been overseen by British intelligence structures since the formation of [[mi6|MI6]], the War Propaganda Bureau, and eventually the [[tavistock-institute|Tavistock Institute]].[1]
