# Andrew Bailey

Andrew Bailey is a co-Deputy Director of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]], appointed to the role in September 2025.[1] He previously served as the Republican Attorney General of Missouri.[2]

## Missouri attorney general

As Missouri attorney general, Bailey formally opened an investigation on Monday, December 11, 2023 into the left-wing watchdog [[media-matters|Media Matters for America]] after the group pushed numerous advertisers to leave [[x-corp|X]].[2] In a notice of investigation addressed to Media Matters president and CEO [[angelo-carusone|Angelo Carusone]], Bailey cited the organization's allegedly fraudulent solicitation of donations from Missouri residents while targeting the platform.[2] He wrote that the federal lawsuit filed against the group raised "serious allegations that your firm falsely and deceptively manipulated the algorithm on X (formerly known as Twitter) through coordinated, inauthentic behavior" in an attempt to defame the company and drive advertisers away, "thus harming free speech."[2] The action followed [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s own lawsuit against Media Matters by roughly three weeks.[2]

## Demand for a DOJ autopen investigation (2025)

As Missouri attorney general in March 2025, Bailey questioned the legitimacy of executive orders signed by former President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] after the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]]'s Oversight Project documented the extensive use of an autopen device to approve them.[3] "I am demanding the DOJ investigate whether President Biden's cognitive decline allowed unelected staff to push through radical policy without his knowing approval," Bailey said.[3] The 82-year-old former president's use of the machine-generated signature became a focal point for critics questioning whether he was fully mindful of the documents issued in his name; autopen use by presidents dates back over half a century.[3] See [[autopen-scandal|the autopen scandal]].[3]

## Succession to Bongino

Following the January 3, 2026 departure of [[dan-bongino|Dan Bongino]] as Deputy Director, Bailey took over as the bureau's No. 2 official, serving under Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]].[1] Bongino had assumed the deputy director post in March 2025 and served less than a year before returning to civilian life.[1]

## Media Matters litigation as Missouri Attorney General (2023–2024)

As Missouri Attorney General, Bailey opened an investigation in November 2023 into [[media-matters|Media Matters for America]] after evidence surfaced concerning solicited donations, conduct he said violated the Missouri Merchandising Practices Act.[4] The inquiry followed allegations that the nonprofit had deceitfully manipulated the algorithm of [[x-corp|X]] so that advertisers' content appeared next to controversial posts, causing the platform to suffer heavy financial losses as advertisers pulled out.[4] In March 2024 Bailey filed suit to compel the organization to turn over documents related to the investigation, with litigation continuing in state court.[4]

On Wednesday, May 8, 2024, Bailey announced he had filed a motion to block Media Matters after the nonprofit sought, in a federal court in Washington, D.C., to halt his investigation and lawsuit.[4] "Media Matters is attempting to shut down Missouri courts by filing a motion in a D.C. court to halt our investigation and lawsuit into their fraudulent practices," he said, adding that "D.C. courts have no control over Missouri courts. I have filed a motion to keep this suit in Missouri, where it belongs. I will not allow Media Matters to run to a D.C. court to evade Missouri law."[4]

## Consideration for FBI director (November 2024)

Following the withdrawal of [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] and the selection of [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] to lead the Justice Department, Missouri Attorney General Bailey was reported by Axios in November 2024 to be under consideration for director of the [[fbi|FBI]] in the second [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration, alongside [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]]; transition sources said no consensus pick had emerged.[5]

## Warnings on private credit

Ahead of the Bank of England's December 2025 stress test of the private equity and private credit industries, Governor Andrew Bailey had said in October that the collapse of two large US companies — car parts maker First Brands and auto dealership and lender Tricolor — might be a warning of bigger problems to come.[6]
