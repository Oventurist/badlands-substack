# William Pitt the Elder

William Pitt the Elder, also known as Lord Chatham, was the British statesman whose death in 1778 became the subject of a celebrated painting by [[benjamin-west|Benjamin West]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## Death in Parliament

The story that Chatham died in Parliament is apocryphal: he collapsed there in a seizure but in reality died thirty-four days later in his bedroom, the romanticised version having been created after the fact.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) He had come to Parliament specifically to fight a bill pushed by the Duke of Richmond, who on 23 March had called for the withdrawal of all British troops from the United States after word arrived that the French would assist the American cause; the bill was defeated by 56 votes in the House of Lords.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) On 5 April Chatham came out to fight the measure and the "soft" lords who would let the Americans go free.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

In his 7 April 1778 speech he declared that he rejoiced the grave had not closed upon him, that he would never consent to deprive the royal offspring of the House of Brunswick of their fairest inheritance, asked whether a people who seventeen years earlier were the terror of the world should now stoop to beg peace from its ancient enemy, and concluded that "any state is better than despair. Let us at least make one effort; and if we must fall, let us fall like men!"1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) Badlands Media commentary by [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] reads his death as expressing not only the death of the man but of the toxic imperial idea to which he had devoted his life.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)

## In painting

West's treatment of the scene functions as a devastating republican critique of empire, but his subtlety and the oligarchist inability to recognise irony meant the British elite liked it, believing he was honouring Pitt.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance) [[john-singleton-copley|John Singleton Copley]] produced a rival 1781 rendition of the same moment, juxtaposing it with the 1588 defeat of the Spanish Armada depicted on the wall — the moment England became the seat of a world maritime empire in place of the Spanish Habsburgs — so that the past grandeur of imperial memory dies with the man.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-american-renaissance)
