# Internews

Internews is an international media-development nonprofit that has featured prominently in criticism of United States foreign-aid-funded media networks. In February 2025, [[wikileaks|WikiLeaks]] published a widely circulated thread identifying Internews as the hub of a sprawling network of media outlets funded through [[usaid|USAID]].[1]

## WikiLeaks allegations

According to the WikiLeaks item republished in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], Internews head [[jeanne-bourgault|Jeanne Bourgault]] is "secretly married" to [[ray-jennings|Ray S. Jennings]], described as a high-profile regime-change specialist for USAID.[1] WikiLeaks stated that Bourgault paid herself $451,000 while telling her alma mater's alumni magazine that she goes to the office "once or twice a month," and that Jennings had authored numerous papers recommending funding for his wife's methods.[1]

WikiLeaks further asserted that the relationship between Bourgault and Jennings appears to have only one obscure reference on the entire internet — a University of Washington alumni magazine feature titled "Alumna brings an information revolution to the developing world" — and that it had not been disclosed by USAID, Internews, or the 4,291 media outlets connected to the organization.[1] Badlands presented the item under the heading "The Ultimate USAID Couple?" as an example of undisclosed conflicts of interest inside the US-funded media ecosystem.[1]

## USAID funding and censorship allegations

[[usaid|USAID]] has been a significant funder of Internews, providing roughly $473 million to the organization, which is described by critics as a U.S. intelligence-linked body created to promote "press freedom" and "train journalists."[2] Internews has been accused of supporting online censorship efforts, notably through its "Countering Disinformation Guide" and other initiatives intended to shape media narratives both abroad and domestically.[2] The relationship became a focal point of criticism in February 2025 during the [[days-of-thunder|days of thunder]], when USAID's role in the [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship industrial complex]] came under scrutiny alongside the agency's internal [[usaid-disinformation-primer|Disinformation Primer]].[2]

## Role in the Orange Revolution

Badlands Media names Internews — "our old friend," in [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]]'s phrasing — among the U.S. organizations sponsored by [[usaid|USAID]], the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] and other foundations during Ukraine's [[orange-revolution|Orange Revolution]], alongside [[freedom-house|Freedom House]], the [[international-republican-institute|International Republican Institute]], the [[national-democratic-institute|National Democratic Institute]], the [[solidarity-center|Solidarity Center]] and the [[eurasia-foundation|Eurasia Foundation]].[3] The article refers readers back to its earlier treatment of the organization in the piece "The Information Imperium."[3]

## Origins and scale

Internews traces its beginnings to 1982, when [[david-m-hoffman|David M. Hoffman]] launched a project to connect American and Soviet citizens by satellite television, becoming a full nonprofit by 1986 under the banner of "access to information" as a human right.[4] It now describes itself as a media-support nonprofit working in more than 100 countries, training journalists, advancing internet freedom and helping outlets become financially sustainable.[4] In 2023 alone it reported training over 9,000 journalists, and it maintains ties to 4,291 media outlets globally, producing thousands of hours of broadcasts reaching hundreds of millions of people.[4]

## Funding network

The organisation's lifeblood is [[usaid|USAID]] money — $472.6 million over the decades — supplemented by grants from the [[national-endowment-for-democracy|National Endowment for Democracy]] and from foundations including [[ford-foundation|Ford]], [[open-society-foundations|Open Society]] and [[rockefeller-foundation|Rockefeller]].[4] Badlands Media notes that USAID was itself flagged by the [[church-committee|Church Committee]] in the 1970s as a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]] front used for Vietnam-era intelligence work, and has co-funded "democracy promotion" schemes with the NED since the 1980s.[4]

## Ukraine and post-Soviet operations

By 2001 Internews was deeply embedded in Ukraine's media sector, reportedly training 1,900 journalists and supporting 203 television stations and 126 radio outlets reaching 50 million viewers and 18 million listeners — figures the article concedes are hard to verify.[4] This activity is presented as synchronised with the NED's effort to orient Ukraine westward, culminating in the 2004 [[orange-revolution|Orange Revolution]], in which the [[national-democratic-institute|NDI]] and [[international-republican-institute|IRI]] coached the ostensibly spontaneous uprising while Internews amplified pro-U.S. voices.[4] Comparable networks were built across the former Soviet states, and in Africa, Asia and Latin America its "disinformation fighting" projects are characterised as narrative control rather than truth-seeking.[4]

## Domestic implications and 2025 cuts

Internews does not operate inside the United States, being barred from doing so by NED rules, but Badlands Media argues its playbook has already migrated home through alumni and partners cycling into Washington consultancies and think tanks, shaping fact-checking regimes and platform content moderation.[4] President [[jeanne-bourgault|Jeanne Bourgault]], who took the helm in 2011, steered the organisation deeper into "information integrity" work, described in the article as censorship with extra steps.[4] In February 2025 [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]]'s [[department-of-government-efficiency|Department of Government Efficiency]] blocked Treasury disbursements to the NED and cut NDI and IRI staffing by two-thirds; although Internews' USAID money was not NED cash, the ripple effects prompted furloughs and scaled-back projects.[4] The article's closing framing pairs Internews abroad with [[newsguard|NewsGuard]] at home as complementary instruments of the same USAID–NED–CIA apparatus.[4]

## Senior technical partner to CEPPS

Badlands Media identifies Internews as a "senior technical partner" of the [[consortium-for-elections-and-political-process-strengthening|Consortium for Elections and Political Process Strengthening]], describing it as having shifted from supporting journalists abroad to censoring content labeled "disinformation" under the guise of protecting democratic processes.[5] Its role is placed within a wider USAID-funded censorship architecture — reportedly as much as $799 million routed to CEPPS — that critics argue suppresses dissenting voices challenging U.S. foreign policy.[5]
