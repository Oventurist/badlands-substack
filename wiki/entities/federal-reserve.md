# Federal Reserve

The Federal Reserve, formally the Federal Reserve System, is the central banking system of the United States. According to the article, it was created by the Federal Reserve Act of 1913 and signed into law by President Woodrow Wilson on December 23, 1913. The source characterizes the Federal Reserve as a "privately owned bank" that became "the most powerful organization in the US" following its creation.

The article frames the Federal Reserve as the capstone of a long-term plan by bankers to establish a fiat monetary system over which citizens would have no control. It asserts that with the Federal Reserve's establishment, "every American had to pay Income Tax, see the continued devaluation of their money," and lost the ability to demand gold redemption from bankers. The source claims this gave banks "the ability to manipulate society in any way they want to."

The article draws a direct connection between the Federal Reserve Act and the [[Pujo Committee]] investigation of 1913, noting that Arsene Pujo formed a special committee to investigate the monopoly of J. P. Morgan and New York's other powerful bankers after the creation of the Federal Reserve and IRS. The Pujo Report, as cited in the article, found that "No less than eighteen different major financial corporations were under control of a cartel led by J.P Morgan, George F Baker and James Stillman," and identified "over $22 billion in resources and capitalization controlled through 341 directorships held in 112 corporations by members of the empire headed by J.P. Morgan."

The Federal Reserve Act was passed by the 63rd United States Congress. The article notes that the [[Jekyll Island meeting]] in 1910, where seven leading bankers and politicians secretly drafted plans for a central bank, provided the blueprint for the legislation. The National Monetary Commission, established by [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson W. Aldrich]] after the Panic of 1907, produced reports and recommendations that became "one of the principal bases in the enactment of the Federal Reserve Act of 1913."

The source claims the Federal Reserve has persisted ever since, creating a system where citizens have "less power" and the central bank can manipulate society. The article calls for the end of the Federal Reserve and a return to a "hard money" system with inherent value.

## The LIBOR-to-SOFR transfer

The April 2025 Badlands essay [[a-journey-of-ever-changing-truths|A Journey of Ever-changing Truths]] gives the Federal Reserve an unusually favorable role. [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] writes that since early in his first presidency, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] "has replaced the [[libor|LIBOR]] system with the [[sofr|SOFR]] system, taking control of the American banking system away from the [[city-of-london|City of London]] and giving it to the American Federal Reserve." In that essay the Fed is treated as the domestic recipient of authority repatriated from London rather than as the instrument of foreign financial control described elsewhere in the corpus.

## FedAccounts and the public banking option

[[a-seismic-shift|A Seismic Shift]] (January 12, 2024) by [[joe-lange|Joe Lange]] discusses the Federal Reserve in two registers. Institutionally, the article claims that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] appointed every member of the board of governors except one, a digital-dollar supporter who was therefore not forced to resign, and treats this as evidence that "taking over the Fed was a big part of the plan to take down the global debt system."

Substantively, the article examines the [[fedaccounts|FedAccounts]] proposal, under which the Fed — currently "a bank for bankers," facilitating inter-bank payments and paying interest on bank accounts — would offer accounts directly to individuals and small businesses. Quoting the Berkeley Economic Review, the article notes that such accounts could not be defaulted on and would hold "pure sovereign money," the liability of the Federal Reserve itself, thereby lessening or eliminating the need for [[federal-deposit-insurance-corporation|federal deposit insurance]]. Work by [[morgan-ricks|Morgan Ricks]] proposes that the Fed partner with the [[united-states-postal-service|U.S. Postal Service]] as a physical branch network for these accounts.

Lange nonetheless maintains that the Federal Reserve is itself unconstitutional, instructing readers to substitute "Treasury" for "Federal Reserve" throughout the Berkeley passage, and arguing that the [[treasury-department|Treasury]] rather than "a private bank like the Federal Reserve" should be in charge of the people's money. He also accuses the Fed of decades of "back door bailouts" to Wall Street banks using what he characterises as taxpayer money.
