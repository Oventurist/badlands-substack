# Chris Wright

Chris Wright is the United States Secretary of Energy in the second Trump administration.[1]

## Nuclear microreactor demonstration

In February 2026 Wright publicized the first air transport of a [[nuclear-microreactor|nuclear microreactor]] prototype, a joint effort between the [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] and the [[pentagon|Department of War]]. Writing on X, he said: "President Trump promised the American people that he would unleash American energy dominance. Yesterday, @ENERGY and @DeptofWar partnered to move a nuclear reactor by air for the very first time. This is the next chapter for U.S. energy."[1]

The demonstration was tied to an executive order signed by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] on May 23, 2025 directing deployment of advanced nuclear reactor technologies for national security purposes.[1]

## US–Saudi 123 Agreement (2026)

Wright was expected to sign the U.S.–Saudi [[123-agreement|123 Agreement]] on civil nuclear cooperation alongside Saudi Energy Minister [[abdulaziz-bin-salman|Abdulaziz bin Salman]], following a preliminary agreement reached in Riyadh the previous year.[2] The [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration signaled in July 2026 that it would submit the pact to [[congress|Congress]] within days.[2]

The agreement omits the [[gold-standard-nonproliferation|Gold Standard]] prohibition on uranium enrichment and reprocessing, as well as the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] Additional Protocol permitting snap inspections at undeclared sites, though it does not obligate the United States to transfer enrichment technology.[2]

## Planned Venezuela trip (2026)

In February 2026 Wright told Politico in an exclusive interview that he would soon travel to [[venezuela|Venezuela]] to meet the country's leaders and discuss the future of its state-run oil sector.[3] The planned visit came as the administration sought to reshape ties with Caracas and address oil production and management issues at [[pdvsa|PDVSA]].[3]

## CERAWeek and the 2026 price surge

During the March 2026 [[2026-gasoline-price-surge|gasoline price surge]] driven by the conflict with [[iran|Iran]], Wright called for increased domestic oil production to offset global supply disruptions.[4] He appeared at CERAWeek in Houston urging oil and gas leaders to raise output immediately, while the White House held direct meetings with executives and diplomats to coordinate higher domestic production amid the Iran tensions.[4] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the push as a return to "Drill, Baby, Drill" and as the first stage of a longer arc toward sovereign energy independence and accelerated nuclear power.[4]

## Venezuela oil field tour (February 2026)

In February 2026, Energy Secretary Wright toured oil infrastructure in [[venezuela|Venezuela]] as the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] moved to normalize energy relations with Caracas.[5] Posting on X, he wrote: "Visiting Venezuelan oil fields today to explore ways to boost oil production, update infrastructure, and unlock the country's enormous economic potential. Stronger commerce will benefit Americans AND Venezuelans, while delivering peace and prosperity across the Hemisphere!"[5] His visit coincided with a Truth Social post by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] declaring relations with Venezuela "extraordinary" and reporting that oil had begun to flow.[5]

## Strait of Hormuz reopening assessment (April 2026)

In April 2026 Wright said the [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] — closed since late February after Iranian mining — could reopen quickly by clearing a single ship pathway rather than every mine, a process that might otherwise take months.[6] He warned that prolonged Iranian production shut-ins could be "devastating" for the country's aging, low-pressure oil fields.[6] His remarks came amid a two-week U.S. blockade of Iranian oil exports and the same-day announcement of the [[united-arab-emirates|UAE]]'s withdrawal from [[opec|OPEC]], with the Strait handling roughly 20% of world oil and gas transit.[6]

## Nomination and the nuclear revival

In December 2024, Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] identified Wright as one of the "most influential figures" of the re-emerging nuclear industry whom President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] was appointing to his second-term cabinet.[7] The commentary placed Wright's selection alongside [[javier-milei|Javier Milei]]'s [[argentine-nuclear-plan|Argentine Nuclear Plan]] and its emphasis on [[small-modular-reactor|small modular reactors]], framing both as evidence of a broader push to restore nuclear power as the foundation of Western energy policy.[7]
