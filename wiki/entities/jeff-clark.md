# Jeff Clark

Jeff Clark is a former Assistant Attorney General at the United States [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] whose conduct during the aftermath of the 2020 presidential election became the subject of protracted attorney disciplinary proceedings before the [[dc-bar|District of Columbia Bar]].[1]

## The draft memo

While serving at DOJ, Clark drafted an internal memo setting out his personal legal theory about election irregularities in Georgia.[1] According to Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], that fact is not in dispute, and in the later federal filing all parties conceded that Clark believed the substance of his memo.[1] The memo was never finalized and never sent.[1]

## Disciplinary proceedings

DC bar authorities held a hearing on the matter — covered at the time by Ashe in America and [[canncon|CannCon]] — and subsequently recommended disbarment over what they characterized as dishonest conduct tied to the draft letter, despite the admissions in the record.[1] The case remained tied to ongoing litigation nearly six years after the underlying events.[1]

## Justice Department intervention

In May 2026 the Justice Department filed a complaint against DC disciplinary authorities alleging that they were improperly using the attorney discipline process to regulate the official actions of federal government lawyers.[1] The filing seeks to nullify what the department called the DC Bar's unlawful prosecution of Clark, which centers on internal deliberations related to potential fraud in the 2020 election.[1]

The United States asked the court to declare that the defendants' investigations, proceedings, adjudications and findings against Clark violate the Supremacy Clause and Article II and are therefore void; to enjoin the defendants, their successors, agents and employees from commencing or maintaining any investigation or disciplinary action against Clark based on his conduct as a federal government attorney; and to award costs and fees.[1] Department officials argued that the disciplinary process improperly targets executive branch deliberations and risks restricting federal attorneys from providing candid legal advice.[1] The action was framed as advancing President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s executive order on ending the [[weaponization-of-government|weaponization of the federal government]] and a related memorandum on preventing abuses of the legal system and federal courts.[1]

Commentators at Badlands argued the disciplinary campaign "may be boomeranging," and that the complaint's core claim — that local bar regulators have no oversight of presidential legal decision-making through "ethics" reviews — could establish a valuable precedent for federal officials at all levels of government.[1]

## 2024 disciplinary trial

The disciplinary trial before a three-member panel of the D.C. Bar's Board of Professional Responsibility opened in late March 2024.[3] Bar investigators argued that Clark, who had worked closely with former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in what they described as a bid to subvert the 2020 election, should face professional consequences including the potential loss of his law license for an effort that they said threw the nation into chaos.[3] Investigators charged Clark with violating professional rules of conduct in late 2020 by attempting to coerce his superiors into sending a letter to Georgia lawmakers encouraging them to reconsider the outcome of the election there based on "significant concerns" about the integrity of the vote.[3]

A lawyer for Clark responded that it would be unreasonable to punish him for his work during the tumultuous days ahead of January 6, 2021, when he spearheaded a proposal to encourage state legislatures to consider overturning the results — a plan that was never adopted and that Trump ultimately turned down.[3] Punishing Clark for being on the losing side of a policy dispute, his team argued, would set a dangerous precedent.[3] Clark had spent two years fighting to scrap the case altogether, contending that the [[dc-bar|D.C. Bar]] has no jurisdiction over the conduct of federal government lawyers, but a federal court rejected that position and an appeals court declined to block the case from moving ahead.[3]

Badlands covered the hearing live, with [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] describing it as "a bonus master class in the rules and codes of professional conduct" featuring extensive witness testimony on why exploring 2020 election fraud was not merely proper but compulsory for officers of the court; [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] attended the proceedings.[3]

## Oversight Project role

Clark serves as Vice President of Litigation at the [[oversight-project|Oversight Project]], the organization that in July 2026 published more than 70 hours of [[biden-ghostwriter-tapes|recordings of Joe Biden speaking with his memoir ghostwriter]] [[mark-zwonitzer|Mark Zwonitzer]].[2] Commenting on the release, Clark said the recordings should force a full reexamination of the decision to give [[joe-biden|Biden]] a pass for keeping and disclosing classified information.[2]
