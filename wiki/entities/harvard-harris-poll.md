# Harvard-Harris Poll

The Harvard-Harris Poll is a regular American public opinion survey whose results are widely cited in coverage of presidential politics.[1]

## November 2023 survey

A Harvard-Harris poll released in November 2023 found that [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] was among Democrats' top picks for the party's primary should President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], then 81, decline to run in 2024 because of age or other questions of fitness for office.[1] Most of the more than 2,000 respondents said they had doubts about Biden's mental fitness to serve as commander-in-chief, and more voters said he was worsening as president than improving.[1]

Around three in five of all likely voters said Biden should not run for a second term, with sharp partisan differences: 33 percent of Democrats held that view, compared with 81 percent of Republicans and 66 percent of independents.[1] Strong majorities across the political spectrum agreed that the country needed "another choice" other than a rematch between Biden and former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[1]

## Reception

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] wrote that the Clinton finding "landed with a dud among liberals and unintentional comedy among MAGA & Anons," treating the poll as one of several narrative "deployments" seeded into the [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]] over the holiday period.[1]

## July 2023 survey

The Harvard CAPS/Harris poll released on Sunday, July 23, 2023, with data collected July 19–20, found [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] the most favorable public figure at 47 percent, a two-point gain, ahead of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at an unchanged 45 percent.[2] The FBI also rose two points, from 52 to 54 percent favorable, despite ongoing revelations about bureau weaponization, and the poll ranked Google (78%) as the most favorable U.S. institution alongside the U.S. Military (78%), with Amazon (77%), Microsoft (69%) and the police (66%) completing the top five.[2] The addition of the three tech giants, absent the previous month, pushed Facebook out of the top five even as it gained two points to 54 percent.[2]

On the horse race, the poll found Trump comfortably defeating his Republican challengers and leading [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] by five points and [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] by nine, with [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] in a dead heat against both Democrats.[2] In a hypothetical Biden-less Democratic primary, Harris drew 44 percent to Kennedy's 22 percent.[2] Badlands commentator Ashe in America urged readers to treat the results with skepticism given the sponsoring institution.[2]
