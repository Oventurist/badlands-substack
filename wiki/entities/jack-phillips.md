# Jack Phillips

**Jack Phillips** is a Colorado baker who became a national figure in American religious-liberty litigation after refusing to bake a cake for a same-sex wedding.[1] As described in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], Phillips spent more than eleven years in court over the matter, took his case all the way to the Supreme Court and won, and then, upon returning to Colorado, saw the process begin again over a transgender-themed cake.[1]

## As a case study in lawfare

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] invoked Phillips as the paradigm case of [[lawfare|lawfare]] when commenting on the [[studio-8-hair-lab-discrimination-charge|Studio 8 Hair Lab discrimination charge]] against Michigan salon owner [[christine-geiger|Christine Geiger]].[1] She argued that for defendants like Phillips and Geiger "the process is the punishment," with the regime fundraising and saturating the narrative while the accused struggles to find "an attorney with a spine" to take the case, and that each successive case functions as "a potential building block to a new precedent, a new normal."[1]

## Colorado Supreme Court dismissal (October 2024)

On October 8, 2024, the [[colorado-supreme-court|Colorado Supreme Court]] declined in a 4–3 decision to rule on whether Phillips could refuse to make a celebratory gender-transition cake for transgender attorney [[autumn-scardina|Autumn Scardina]], dismissing the case on what the Colorado press described as a technicality.[2] Justice Melissa Hart wrote for the majority that the underlying question of how governments should balance the rights of transgender individuals in public accommodations against those of religious business owners "has become the focus of intense public debate," but that "we cannot answer that question."[2] Phillips's attorney Jake Werner of the [[alliance-defending-freedom|Alliance Defending Freedom]] said his client had been pursued and mocked for years and that "Jack has been dragged through courts for over a decade. It's time to leave him alone."[2] Scardina's attorney, John McHugh, said the court had avoided the merits "by inventing an argument no party raised."[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the "technicality" was in substance the constitutional protection of religious liberty and free expression, and noted that Colorado's district and appeals courts had ruled against Phillips where the high court would not.[2] She observed that the ruling was the first contentious decision from the court — widely considered the most progressive state high court in the nation — since [[monica-marquez|Monica Márquez]] became Chief Justice in September 2024, and that Márquez joined the majority.[2] Reiterating that "the process is the punishment," she congratulated Phillips while expecting further litigation against him and his bakery, [[masterpiece-cakeshop|Masterpiece Cakeshop]].[2]
