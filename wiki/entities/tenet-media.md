# Tenet Media

Tenet Media was a Tennessee-based media company identified in a federal indictment unsealed on Wednesday, September 4, 2024, as the recipient of nearly $10 million from employees of the Russian state-backed broadcaster [[russia-today|Russia Today (RT)]].[1] The [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]] characterized the arrangement as part of "a scheme to create and distribute content to U.S. audiences with hidden Russian government messaging."[1]

## The indictment

According to the DOJ, RT and two of its employees — Kostiantyn "Kostya" Kalashnikov and Elena "Lena" Afanasyeva — worked to funnel money to Tenet Media as part of a series of "covert projects" aimed at shaping narratives within Western audiences.[1] Reporting on the case noted that the front company was set up and directed by the Russian scheme to produce online content aimed at sowing divisions among Americans, and that it targeted millions of American news consumers with what Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] described as "hidden Russian government messaging."[1]

## Commentators involved

The indictment specifically noted that the conservative influencers associated with the company — including [[tim-pool|Tim Pool]], [[benny-johnson|Benny Johnson]], [[dave-rubin|Dave Rubin]] and [[lauren-southern|Lauren Southern]] — had no idea they were taking Russian money and were deceived.[1] They were told by Tenet founder [[lauren-chen|Lauren Chen]], who allegedly knew the true source of the funds, that the money came from a wealthy private investor named "Eduard Grigoriann."[1]

Writing in the Badlands News Brief, [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] connected the story to an earlier [[breaking-history|Breaking History]] discussion with [[canncon|CannCon]] about integrity and "grifters," defining a grifter as somebody who feigned being MAGA or MAGA-adjacent in order to build an audience and then betrayed [[donald-trump|Trump]] and the movement at an opportunistic moment, while explicitly declining to accuse any of the named influencers.[1] He allowed that the funding might have nothing to do with the commentators' public statements but urged readers to keep the episode in mind while observing the information and media landscape.[1]
