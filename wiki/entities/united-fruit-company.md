# United Fruit Company

The United Fruit Company, now known as Chiquita, was an American agricultural corporation with extensive holdings and political influence across Latin America.[1]

## Badlands interpretation

In commentary published in September 2025 on the escalating U.S. confrontation with [[venezuela|Venezuela]], Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that the origins of the Latin American drug cartels can be traced to the United Fruit Company, which funded the construction of the railroads running from the United States to South America.[1] The claim was made in the context of an argument that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had been working with Venezuelan president [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] to bring a transnational corporate cartel to heel, using deception and misdirection, and was pivoting toward dismantling the drug cartels.[1]

The same commentary situated this alongside international prosecutions of executives from major commodity trading houses such as [[glencore|Glencore]], in which Maduro was said to have contributed significantly.[1]

## Origins and the "banana republic"

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], relaying research from War Hamster presented on [[breaking-history|Breaking History]] with [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]], traced United Fruit to [[minor-cooper-keith|Minor Cooper Keith]], who moved to Costa Rica in 1880, took over his uncle's railroad company, bought out the defaulting Costa Rican government with London bank financing, and completed the railroad in 1890 before turning to bananas for want of passengers.[2] Keith partnered with M.T. Snyder and the Tropical Trading and Transport Company, expanding the banana industry from Costa Rica into Panama and Colombia until bananas were the second-most consumed food in America by the turn of the century.[2]

In 1899 Keith merged his company with the Boston Fruit Company, which held an established empire in the West Indies, forming the United Fruit Company.[2] The combined firm's stranglehold on the fruit trade translated into control over the logistics and transport of all freight, earning it the nickname "The Octopus" and giving it political power it exerted freely over developing nations, propping up dictators and originating the term "banana republic."[2]

## Sullivan & Cromwell and the CIA

The same account holds that the Spanish-American conflict of 1898 destabilized the region while the law firm [[sullivan-and-cromwell|Sullivan & Cromwell]] — inventor of the holding company and longtime employer of the Dulles brothers and other notable CIA figures — was on the ground and may have helped foment the unrest.[2] The relationship between those lawyers and United Fruit is said to have led the company to play a starring role in the Iran-Contra scandal, among lesser-known revolutions.[2] After exposure of its CIA connections in the 1980s, United Fruit rebranded as Chiquita Banana, now [[chiquita-brands-international|Chiquita Brands International]], which in June 2024 was found liable by a Florida jury for funding the Colombian paramilitary group [[autodefensas-unidas-de-colombia|AUC]].[2] The broader Badlands argument is that bananas were created and marketed as a product so that oligarchs could cultivate the political clout needed to control Central American governments and thereby trade in other commodities from the region.[2]

## Land holdings in Central America

Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] identifies United Fruit as a component of the [[international-syndicate|International Syndicate]] and writes that a Central American government's decision that the company should not own 42% of the country's farmable land triggered the installation of a [[central-intelligence-agency|CIA]]-backed dictator.[3] That land, she says, had been acquired by paying off government officials to confiscate it, primarily from middle-class coffee farmers.[3] When the [[sandinista-national-liberation-front|Sandinistas]] removed the dictator, the United States responded on the syndicate's behalf by sending Special Forces, [[operation-gladio|Gladio]]-trained Cuban exiles and mercenaries to overthrow the new government, while the [[nicaraguan-contras|Contras]] were publicly presented as the good guys.[3]
