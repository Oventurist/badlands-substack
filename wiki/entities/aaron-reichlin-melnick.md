# Aaron Reichlin-Melnick

Aaron Reichlin-Melnick is a senior fellow at the [[american-immigration-council|American Immigration Council]] (AIC) known for his public analysis of U.S. immigration enforcement costs.[1]

In testimony before the U.S. Senate Judiciary Committee in December 2024, Reichlin-Melnick said that mass deportations would cost "at a minimum" $316 billion.[1] His organization's supporting estimate placed the annual cost of deporting one million illegal aliens at $88 billion — $7 billion for arrests, $66 billion for detentions, $12.6 billion for legal processing, and $2.1 billion for transportation — or about $88,000 per deportee, with a total of $967.9 billion over more than a decade.[1]

Critics noted that actual per capita deportation costs from 2015 to 2022, in 2024 dollars, averaged $12,124, ranging from $9,767 under Obama to $11,637 under Trump and $15,499 under Biden — less than one-sixth of the AIC estimate.[1] Badlands commentary held that the value of his testimony was in exposing how NGOs build resource estimates to justify funding, citing the AIC assumption that 83 new 500-bed facilities at $35.91 million each would cost roughly $3 billion, far below the $66 billion detention line item.[1]
