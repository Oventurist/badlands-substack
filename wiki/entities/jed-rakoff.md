# Jed Rakoff

Jed Rakoff is a United States District Judge, appointed by President Bill Clinton, who presided over the civil litigation brought against major banks over their relationships with the financier [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[1]

## Ruling on bank liability

On May 1, 2023, Rakoff rejected motions to dismiss cases brought against [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]], longtime JPMorgan executive [[jes-staley|Jes Staley]], and [[deutsche-bank|Deutsche Bank]], holding that plaintiffs had presented enough evidence that each could be liable for Epstein's sex trafficking.[1] In a 54-page decision, Rakoff wrote that "Plaintiffs have pled sufficient facts to support their allegations that JP Morgan had … knowledge of Jeffrey Epstein's sex-trafficking venture, either directly or by recklessly disregarding what was plainly to be seen."[1]

The judge similarly found that one plaintiff — a woman who said she was abused by Epstein and his associates — had pled sufficient facts to support her allegation that Deutsche Bank knew of, or recklessly disregarded, Epstein's sex-trafficking venture.[1] The ruling came in cases brought by several alleged Epstein victims and by the government of the [[us-virgin-islands|United States Virgin Islands]] against the two banks, and also covered a claim filed against Staley by JPMorgan.[1]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the ruling as an instance of the "political, cultural, financial, clandestine and spiritual" pillars of what he termed the Deep State's "System of Systems" being exposed one by one, and described a broader "narrative convergence" in which previously separate threads become visible to a wider public.[1]

## Ruling on service of Larry Page (2023)

On May 4, 2023, Rakoff ruled in federal court in Manhattan that the [[us-virgin-islands|U.S. Virgin Islands]] could serve legal papers on Google co-founder [[larry-page|Larry Page]] as part of the territory's lawsuit against [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase]] over the bank's ties to [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]].[2] The ruling did not specify what information the territory was seeking from Page, whose company grew into [[alphabet|Alphabet Inc.]][2] The underlying suit accuses JPMorgan of missing red flags about Epstein's abuse of women on [[little-st-james|Little St. James]], the private island he owned in the territory.[2]

## Ruling on the Epstein bank lawsuits (March 2023)

On March 20, 2023, Rakoff ruled that [[jpmorgan-chase|JPMorgan Chase & Co.]] and [[deutsche-bank|Deutsche Bank]] must face lawsuits alleging they enabled [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] to traffic his victims.[3] The suits had been brought by two women identified as "Jane Doe" in November 2022 and by the [[us-virgin-islands|U.S. Virgin Islands]] against JPMorgan in December 2022.[3] In a four-page order, Rakoff allowed claims that the defendants "knowingly benefited from participating in a sex-trafficking venture," "obstructed enforcement of the [[trafficking-victims-protection-act|Trafficking Victims Protection Act]]," and "negligently failed to exercise reasonable care to prevent physical harm," along with a claim of negligent failure to exercise reasonable care as a banking institution providing non-routine banking, while dismissing all other claims.[3] He similarly permitted the U.S. Virgin Islands to pursue its claim that JPMorgan "knowingly benefited from participating in a sex-trafficking venture."[3]
