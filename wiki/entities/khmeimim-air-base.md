# Khmeimim Air Base

Khmeimim Air Base is a Russian military airfield on Syria's Mediterranean coast, operated by Moscow alongside a logistics support center at [[tartus-naval-base|Tartus]].[1] In 2017, Russia and Syria agreed to station Russian troops at the two facilities for 49 years.[1]

After militant groups led by [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]] captured Damascus and forced President [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] to resign and seek asylum in Russia, the fate of the Khmeimim and Tartus bases came into immediate question.[1] Syria's de facto leader [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed Hussein al-Sharaa]] — better known as [[abu-mohammad-al-jolani|Abu Mohammad al-Julani]] — said in an interview with Al Arabiya that his government would not necessarily seek the withdrawal of Moscow's installations, arguing that Syria has "strategic interests" in maintaining good relations with "the second most powerful country in the world."[1]

Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] said the deployment of the bases is stipulated by valid interstate agreements concluded under the norms of international law, and that their continued presence, operating conditions, maintenance and provision could become the subject of negotiations with the new Syrian leadership.[1]

Badlands commentary argued that Assad fled west to the Russian air base in Latakia before being evacuated to Moscow, and that NATO- and Israeli-aligned forces bombed weapons depots around the Russian coastal bases out of concern that pro-Assad forces might use them to mount a resistance.[1] The same analysis held that Russia would likely retain the bases, which serve as a staging point for Russian operations in Africa.[1]
