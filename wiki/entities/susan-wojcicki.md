# Susan Wojcicki

Susan Wojcicki was an American technology executive who served as chief executive officer of [[youtube|YouTube]] and was one of the earliest employees of [[google|Google]]. She died on August 10, 2024, at the age of 56, after living with non-small cell lung cancer for two years, according to an announcement by her husband Dennis Troper.[1]

## Career

Wojcicki was involved with YouTube's parent company Google from the firm's earliest days. In 1998, Google founders [[sergey-brin|Sergey Brin]] and [[larry-page|Larry Page]] rented her Menlo Park, California garage while building their search engine; she later became Google's 16th employee and worked at the company for nearly 25 years.[1] She was widely described as one of the most prominent women in Silicon Valley, and Google chief executive Sundar Pichai said following her death that she was "core" to Google's history.[1]

## Family

Wojcicki's father, Stanley Wojcicki, was a physicist and chair of the physics department at Stanford University.[1] One of her sisters, [[anne-wojcicki|Anne Wojcicki]], is the founder and chief executive of the genomics company [[23andme|23andMe]]; another sister, Janet, is a pediatrician and anthropologist.[1] Sergey Brin eventually married Anne Wojcicki, tying the Google founding circle to the consumer-genetics industry.[1] Wojcicki was married to Dennis Troper for 26 years and had five children; Troper announced her death in a Facebook post, calling her "not just my best friend and partner in life, but a brilliant mind, a loving mother, and a dear friend to many."[1]

## Badlands Media commentary

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] treated Wojcicki's death as bitterly ironic in its August 12, 2024 news brief. Two days earlier, on Friday August 9, the outlet's YouTube channel had been permanently removed from the platform for "harmful conspiracy theories"; the following day, the writer noted, "the chief architect of YouTube's dystopian ban hammer died, reportedly of lung cancer," calling it possibly "the fastest boomerang ever."[1] Writing under the byline [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]], the commentary described Google as having become "a critical and foundational element of dystopia's shadow government," noted that Wojcicki died with a net worth of nearly $800 million, and questioned why "one of the most privileged women in the world" — with immediate family members in medicine and genomics and effectively unlimited resources — died so young.[1] The piece closed by observing that speculation about her death was itself something that could not be voiced on YouTube.[1]
