# Lekem

Lekem, formally the Israeli Bureau of Scientific Relations, was an Israeli intelligence agency that dealt mainly in acquiring scientific and technical intelligence, particularly for Israel's nuclear program.[1] It operated alongside the better-known components of the Israeli intelligence community — [[mossad|Mossad]] for external intelligence and covert operations, Aman for military intelligence, and [[shin-bet|Shabak or Shin Bet]] for internal security.[1]

## Krytron smuggling

Lekem became embroiled in a series of controversies, including the smuggling of over 800 krytron switches — a component used in nuclear weapons — to Israel without the required U.S. State Department Munitions Export License.[1] The Badlands account identifies the Hollywood producer and alleged Israeli operative [[arnon-milchan|Arnon Milchan]] as the conduit for that transfer.[1]

## Disbandment after the Pollard affair

Lekem was disbanded in 1986 following the arrest of [[jonathan-pollard|Jonathan Pollard]], described as "the most damaging spy" to have operated on behalf of Israel.[1] Pollard was a United States Navy intelligence employee at the Naval Antiterrorist Alert Center in Washington, D.C., and was paid by Israel for the delivery of large volumes of classified documents to Lekem.[1]

## Role in the PROMIS operation

At the time the [[promis-software|PROMIS software]] came to Israeli attention in the early 1980s, Lekem was headed by the veteran intelligence officer [[rafi-eitan|Rafi Eitan]].[1] Eitan, introduced to the software by the former CIA operative [[earl-brian|Earl Brian]], conceived with Brian the plan to install a "trapdoor" or backdoor in PROMIS and market the bugged product worldwide — an operation later carried forward by the publisher [[robert-maxwell|Robert Maxwell]].[1]
