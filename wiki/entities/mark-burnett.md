# Mark Burnett

Mark Burnett is a British-born American television producer who, in December 2024, was named by President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as special envoy to the United Kingdom in the incoming administration.[1] Announcing the selection on a Saturday, Trump said that "with a distinguished career in television production and business, Mark brings a unique blend of diplomatic acumen and international recognition to this important role."[1]

## Television career

Born in London, Burnett produced a string of reality-television hits including "Survivor" and "The Voice," but is best known for teaming with Trump on [[the-apprentice|The Apprentice]], which first aired on NBC in 2004.[1] Although Trump had already been well known in real-estate and pop-culture circles for decades, the program again made him a household name; Trump severed ties with NBC in 2015, the same year he launched his first White House run.[1]

In 2018 The New Yorker published an article titled "How Mark Burnett Resurrected Donald Trump as an Icon of American Success," acknowledging the role Burnett played in elevating Trump from cultural icon to cultural phenomenon, though Badlands commentary criticized the magazine's framing of Trump as "a floundering D-lister" before the show, noting that The Apprentice debuted at number one in the ratings and ran for thirteen seasons.[1]

Burnett's wider catalogue includes Shark Tank and Sarah Palin's Alaska, formats that Badlands commentary credited with encouraging curiosity and enterprise even amid a genre the writer otherwise regarded as detrimental to society.[1] In 2013 Burnett and his wife produced a ten-episode miniseries called "The Bible," acclaimed among Christian audiences and seen by more than 100 million viewers worldwide, followed by a sequel series.[1] The Celebrity Apprentice drew particular Badlands interest because several contestants — such as Dennis Rodman — later played roles in Trump's presidency and in geopolitics.[1]

## Military background and Badlands interpretation

Before entering television, Burnett served in the British Army's Parachute Regiment and had originally planned to travel to Central America to work as a "weapons and tactics advisor."[1] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] suggested that the two career paths may prove "far more similar than we would have ever guessed," and announced a forthcoming Badlands Media show, to launch in January, documenting the Apprentice and Celebrity Apprentice saga with [[patrick-gunnels|Patrick Gunnels]] as a guest.[1]

The appointment was described as continuing Trump's trend of filling out his incoming administration with figures drawn from television or politics, including defense secretary pick [[pete-hegseth|Pete Hegseth]], a former "Fox & Friends Weekend" co-host, and [[mehmet-oz|Mehmet Oz]], a former television doctor and unsuccessful Pennsylvania Senate candidate.[1]
