# McLaughlin & Associates

McLaughlin & Associates is an American polling firm whose surveys of the Republican electorate and of general-election matchups were cited prominently in conservative media during the 2024 presidential cycle. The firm's principals had polled for [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in both his 2016 and 2020 campaigns, a history the pollsters themselves invoked when contrasting Trump's earlier weakness in the national popular vote with his stronger position in 2023.[1]

## March 2023 national survey

In a national survey of 1,000 likely voters conducted between March 17 and March 21, 2023 and published through Newsmax, the firm reported that efforts by Democrats to prosecute Trump would backfire, summarising its finding with the line that "the only real crime Donald Trump has committed is being the frontrunner to beat President Biden, and the voters know it."[1] The poll went into the field while national news media and social media were dominated by reports that Trump would be indicted by Manhattan District Attorney [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]], and the firm characterised the timing of the indictment push as "the classic political diversion."[1]

The results showed Trump leading [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] 48 percent to 43 percent nationally despite the general election being twenty months away, and leading 51 percent to 41 percent across the battleground states — a margin the firm described as a landslide.[1] McLaughlin noted that Trump had held a decisive lead over Biden for more than a year, dating the shift to the American withdrawal from Afghanistan.[1]

Within the Republican primary electorate, 61 percent of respondents said they wanted Trump to run again in 2024, up from 57 percent the previous month.[1] In a field of thirteen declared and potential candidates, Trump led with 46 percent, 23 points ahead of [[ron-desantis|Ron DeSantis]] at 23 percent, with [[mike-pence|Mike Pence]] at 8 percent and all others lower; Trump's lead in the crowded field had grown by 11 points since January.[1] In a head-to-head test against DeSantis, Trump led 61 percent to 31 percent, a net 19-point improvement since January.[1]

## Reception

[[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the polling as evidence that the pre-indictment narrative had benefited Trump, arguing that he had drawn "all of the optical benefits of an Actual Event" from a purely potential one while further positioning himself as anti-establishment.[1]
