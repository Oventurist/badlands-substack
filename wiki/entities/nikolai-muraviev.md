# Nikolai Muraviev

General Nikolai Muraviev was a Russian statesman who rose to influence under Czar [[alexander-ii|Alexander II]] after the [[crimean-war|Crimean War]], alongside [[grand-duke-constantine|Grand Duke Constantine]], Foreign Minister [[alexander-gorchakov|Gorchakov]] and Ambassador [[eduard-de-stoeckl|Eduard de Stoeckl]].[1]

As early as 1853, Muraviev had already urged Russia's sale of Alaska to the United States in a letter to the Czar, writing: "Due to the present amazing development of railroads, the United States will soon spread over all North America. We must face the fact that we will have to cede our North American possessions to them."[1] The judgment made him one of the earliest advocates of what became the 1867 [[alaska-purchase|Alaska purchase]].[1]
