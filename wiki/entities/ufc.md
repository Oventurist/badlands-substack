# Ultimate Fighting Championship

The Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) is the leading mixed martial arts promotion in the United States, led by president [[dana-white|Dana White]].[1]

The organization has repeatedly intersected with American politics through its association with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who has attended numerous UFC events and maintains a close relationship with White.[1] In April 2023 Trump attended [[ufc-287|UFC 287]] at the Kaseya Center in Miami, sitting ringside with White for a card headlined in its co-main event by Trump supporter [[jorge-masvidal|Jorge Masvidal]] against Gilbert Burns.[1]

Commentary published by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] noted that Trump drew a loud ovation on the pay-per-view broadcast while ESPN declined to feature him during the prelims, framing UFC crowds as a venue where the popularity of the [[america-first-movement|America First movement]] is visible "in the wild."[1]

## UFC 295 and the politics of the sport (November 2023)

On November 11, 2023, Trump entered Madison Square Garden for UFC 295 to a loud ovation, flanked by an entourage of [[kid-rock|Kid Rock]], [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]], Dana White and son [[donald-trump-jr|Don Jr.]], arriving moments before the pay-per-view event began and taking ringside seats as Kid Rock's "American Badass" played through the arena.[2]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that such appearances carry meaning beyond the anecdotal, citing four points: that [[john-mccain|John McCain]] attempted to shut the UFC down in the early 2000s, successfully pressuring New York State into a campaign against the league; that Trump offered the promotion a free venue and transportation to save a last-minute event which, had it not gone forward, likely would have bankrupted the company and the emerging sport with it; that the UFC is the counter-culture sport of the new century, with athletes who are nearly impossible to control and a fan and personality base that scores exceptionally high on the "Based" meter; and that Trump timed his 2023 UFC appearances to coincide with major narrative deployments, producing clips that represent his appeal to both the American everyman and the counter-culture.[2] He summarized the thesis as "Politics IS Culture. Culture IS Politics."[2]

## Merger with WWE

In April 2023, UFC's owner [[endeavor|Endeavor Group]] agreed to merge the promotion with [[world-wrestling-entertainment|World Wrestling Entertainment]] into a single publicly traded combat sports and entertainment company, the [[wwe-ufc-merger|WWE–UFC merger]].[3] The deal valued UFC at $12.1 billion and WWE at $9.3 billion, with Endeavor holding 51 percent of the combined entity.[3] [[dana-white|Dana White]] was to remain president of UFC, while Endeavor chief executive [[ari-emanuel|Ari Emanuel]] led the new company and described it as "UFC 2.0."[3] [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] commentary held that UFC's audience skews heavily MAGA and countercultural and that Endeavor's earlier acquisition had already produced stagnation and homogenization, though the company "still can't quite control the athletes."[3]

## UFC 309 and the Trump entourage

[[ufc-309|UFC 309]] at Madison Square Garden in November 2024 became a political spectacle when President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] entered the arena alongside chief executive [[dana-white|Dana White]] with an entourage that included [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]], [[donald-trump-jr|Donald Trump Jr.]], [[robert-f-kennedy-jr|Robert F. Kennedy Jr.]] and [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]].[4] Champion Jon Jones handed Trump his heavyweight belt and thanked him in a victory speech.[4]

## "Cage-match politics" and the Bud Light détente

An April 2024 [[new-york-times|New York Times]] feature titled "Dana White, Donald Trump and the Rise of Cage-Match Politics" examined the promotion's political valence, centring on chief executive [[dana-white|Dana White]]'s reported nine-figure deal making [[bud-light|Bud Light]] the UFC's official beer sponsor and his role in unwinding the conservative boycott of [[anheuser-busch|Anheuser-Busch]].[5] Much of the UFC's audience had joined the political right in boycotting the brand over a promotion featuring a transgender influencer, and the renewed sponsorship left White accused of selling out before he helped broker a détente that ended with [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] urging that the company be given "a second chance."[5]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the piece as a hit article masquerading as cultural analysis that nonetheless approached a "third rail truth": that the rise in the cultural importance of the UFC and its athletes — figures virtually impossible for the establishment to control by virtue of their makeup — has closely mirrored and even front-run the rise of the America First movement.[5] He argued the promotion's athletes and fans are threatening to the establishment globally as well as domestically, citing Brazilian fighter [[renato-moicano|Renato Moicano]]'s use of his post-fight microphone time to push Austrian economics as evidence that "sovereignty and self-actualization is becoming a popular sentiment."[5]

## Paramount broadcasting deal and cultural role (September 2025)

In September 2025, Badlands commentary highlighted the UFC's announcement of a nearly $8 billion broadcasting deal with [[paramount-global|Paramount]] beginning in 2026 — an agreement with the Ellison-controlled company that contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] said almost nobody in traditional sports media had seen coming.[6] The same analysis cast the UFC as a cultural "Trojan Horse," noting that [[dana-white|Dana White]]'s influence on Meta's board had injected a counter-cultural ethos into Big Tech's sanitized domain and may partly explain the rebranding of [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] and other tech elites.[6] Gulf sovereign funds taking stakes in the UFC were described as part of the same broader realignment of American sports and entertainment.[6]
