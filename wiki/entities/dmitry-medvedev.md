# Dmitry Medvedev

Dmitry Medvedev is a senior Russian official and former president and prime minister of Russia, known in recent years for hawkish public commentary on Western military policy.[1]

## Commentary on a U.S. ground war in Iran

Amid the 2026 [[iran-war|Iran conflict]], Medvedev warned that a potential U.S. ground war in [[iran|Iran]] could mirror the Vietnam War and carry "fatal consequences" for the broader region.[1] His remarks followed more cautious statements from President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], who described the conflict as unpredictable.[1] Badlands writers read the crisis as shifting relative power toward Russia and [[china|China]], with [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] arguing that the escalation is the visible shield over a deeper realignment he calls Sovereign Disentanglement.[1]

## Turn against the West

Medvedev served as President of Russia from 2008 to 2012, a period in which he was widely considered the pro-West alternative to Putin and, in the Badlands framing, "the friend to the globalist Deep State."[2] Badlands cited a September 2024 comment from Medvedev on Western moves to authorize long-range strikes into Russia as evidence that this alignment no longer holds, punctuating the point with the observation "not so much, anymore, it seems."[2]

The remark appeared in the Brief alongside Putin's warning that permitting Kyiv to use longer-range Western weapons would mean [[nato|NATO]] countries were "at war" with Russia, and [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]]'s argument that the roster of world leaders still sympathetic to Western "black hats" was rapidly shrinking.[2]

## Exchange with Lindsey Graham (July 2025)

In late July 2025, Medvedev — serving as deputy chairman of the Russian Security Council — publicly rebuked US Senator [[lindsey-graham|Lindsey Graham]] over demands that Moscow negotiate with Kyiv, telling him it was not up to Washington to dictate the timing of a peace deal and urging him to focus on domestic issues instead.[3] The exchange followed President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s decision to shorten his 50-day ceasefire deadline for Russia and Ukraine to just 10–12 days, backed by threats of additional sanctions on Moscow and 100% tariffs on its trading partners.[3]

Medvedev had cautioned Trump against issuing ultimatums, writing on X that "Russia isn't Israel or even Iran" and warning that each threat marked "a step towards war" between the United States and Russia.[3] After Graham claimed that Russia and its "customers" would "soon be sadly mistaken" and ordered Moscow to "get to the peace table," Medvedev replied: "It's not for you or Trump to dictate when to 'get at the peace table,'" adding that negotiations would end only "when all the objectives of our military operation have been achieved," and closing with "Work on America first, gramps!"[3]

## Commentary on the US–EU trade deal

Medvedev also remarked of the [[2025-us-eu-trade-agreement|2025 US–EU trade agreement]] that "President Trump wiped the floor with the EU," a line Badlands commentary endorsed.[3] Contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described Medvedev as having "no chill" and as "the turd in the Globalist punchbowl" for several years, recalling his threat to strike London, Paris and Washington over long-range missile transfers to Ukraine, and argued the episode illustrated how little respect US government officials now command abroad.[3]

## July 2024 threat against NATO and Ukraine

Responding to the Washington Summit Declaration issued at the [[2024-nato-washington-summit|2024 NATO Washington Summit]] — which pledged continued support for Ukraine "on its irreversible path to full Euro-Atlantic integration, including NATO membership" — Medvedev said Moscow needed to intensify efforts to keep Ukraine out of the alliance and to end both the former Soviet republic and the bloc.[4] "One thing is for certain," he told TASS. "We must do all we can to make sure that Ukraine's 'irreversible path' to NATO ends in the demise of either Ukraine or NATO. Or — even better — both."[4]

Badlands commentary paired the remark with Polish army chief General [[wieslaw-kukula|Wiesław Kukuła]]'s call for [[poland|Poland]] to prepare for full-scale conflict, arguing that "the stage is set" and that the world had been "reforged into a powder keg" with the [[state-department|State Department]] cast as "a scorned child playing with matches."[4]

## Call to "eliminate" Zelensky (May 2023)

After the overnight [[2023-kremlin-drone-attack|drone attack on the Kremlin]] in early May 2023, Medvedev — described as the outspoken former Russian president and current deputy chairman of the security council — wrote on social media that the strike had left Moscow with no options but to "eliminate" Ukrainian President [[volodymyr-zelensky|Volodymyr Zelensky]] and his "clique," in effect calling for a decapitation strike against the government in Kiev.[5] Zelensky vehemently denied responsibility for the attack, which Russia asserted was an assassination attempt targeting [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], while US Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] said he could not validate Moscow's claims.[5]

## Nuclear rhetoric and Trump's submarine order, August 2025

In late July and early August 2025 Medvedev said that increased involvement from Washington in ending Russia's war against Ukraine could bring the United States and Moscow closer to direct conflict.[6] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] slammed the former Russian leader for the inflammatory remarks and announced he had ordered two U.S. nuclear-powered submarines repositioned to "appropriate regions."[6]

Kremlin spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] responded on 4 August 2025 by urging that "everyone should be very, very cautious with nuclear rhetoric," implicitly distancing the Kremlin from Medvedev's framing.[6] The exchange coincided with the Russian Foreign Ministry's declaration that Moscow no longer considers itself bound by its moratorium under the [[intermediate-range-nuclear-forces-treaty|Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty]] framework.[6]

## 2024 nuclear threat over Russia's 1991 borders

In February 2024, in his capacity as Deputy Chairman of the Russian Federation's Security Council, Medvedev threatened the United States and Europe with nuclear war if attempts were made to force Russia back to its internationally recognized 1991 borders.[7] He asked rhetorically whether Western leaders truly believed the Russian people would accept "such a consequential partitioning of their country," and rejected the notion that Russia's leadership, headed by the Supreme Commander-in-Chief of the Russian Armed Forces, would hesitate to make difficult decisions.[7]

Medvedev argued that "the collapse of Russia will have far worse consequences than the outcome of a conventional, even long-term war," and stated that any attempt to restore the 1991 borders "will only lead to one outcome: to a global war with Western countries, utilising our entire strategic arsenal," naming Kyiv, Berlin, London, and Washington among targets already included in Russia's nuclear triad attack plans.[7] Asked whether Russia would have the resolve to follow through if "a thousand-year-old country" faced extinction, he answered that "the answer is obvious."[7]

## November 2024 Telegram post on the U.S. election

Days after Russia conducted a sweeping readiness drill of its nuclear forces, Medvedev posted to his nearly 1.4 million Telegram followers a five-point assessment of the imminent U.S. presidential election, arguing that its outcome would not matter because both candidates share a bipartisan consensus that "Russia must be defeated."[8] He wrote that the world stood "frozen in uneasy anticipation" over the vote and that there was no reason for high expectations.[8]

Medvedev characterized [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]] as "dumb, inexperienced, and easy to control," predicting that real decision-making would fall to a coterie of top ministers and advisors plus, indirectly, the Obamas.[8] He described a "low-energy" [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] as someone who would be forced to comply with the system and its rules and who would not stop the war "not in one day, not in three days, not in three months" — adding that if Trump actually attempted to do so, "he could end up becoming the new JFK."[8] In Medvedev's framing, the only variable that mattered was how much cash the next president could extract from [[congress|Congress]] to finance a foreign war, money he said would feed the American military-industrial complex and line the pockets of "the Banderite scum in Ukraine," concluding that the best course on November 5 was to keep striking Kyiv.[8]

Separately, Medvedev reiterated to Russian state news agency RT that adding Ukraine to [[nato|NATO]] could lead to World War III.[8]
