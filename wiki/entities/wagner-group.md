# Wagner Group

The Wagner Group is a Russian private military organization that has operated in Syria, Ukraine and across Africa, functioning in practice as an extension of Russian state security policy abroad.[1]

## Role in the Sahel

Badlands commentary on Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]]'s February 2026 remarks about French activity in Africa notes that it was the Russian military and the Wagner Group who trained the forces now defending the [[alliance-of-sahel-states|Alliance of Sahel States]], with that training originating in Syria.[1] The commentary frames those Sahelian forces — associated with Burkinabè President Captain [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] — as the targets of a subversion campaign said to involve terrorist groups, coup plotters and Ukrainian militants.[1]

The same commentary speculated about whether Russia would re-mobilize and redeploy forces back to the Sahel in response to escalating pressure on the region's nationalist governments.[1]

## Withdrawal from Mali (2025)

In June 2025 the Wagner Group announced the end of its mission in [[mali|Mali]], saying its fighters had completed their objectives and were returning home.[2] Wagner operatives had reportedly been active in the West African country since 2021, when its transitional authorities turned to the group for support following a coup and a surge in attacks by Tuareg rebels and al-Qaeda-linked militants.[2] "For three and a half years, the 'musicians' fought shoulder to shoulder with the people of Mali against terrorism," the group said, using the informal name for its contractors, claiming to have eliminated thousands of militants and their commanders.[2] It added that it had helped Malian patriots build "a strong and disciplined army capable of defending its land" and that all regional capitals had been returned to "the control of the legitimate authorities," declaring that "the mission is complete. Wagner PMC is returning home."[2] Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov had previously confirmed that Mali requested Wagner's help in 2021 and has since also received official Russian military support.[2]

## July 2024 Mali ambush and the Ukraine dispute

In late July 2024 a military convoy carrying Wagner Group contractors and Malian soldiers was ambushed by insurgents in northern [[mali|Mali]], killing dozens.[3] Senegal's foreign ministry attributed the attack, carried out between 25 and 27 July, to Tuareg rebels and members of [[jnim|the Support Group for Islam and Muslims]] operating against the Armed Forces of Mali.[3] Badlands commentary recounted video circulating online in which a Wagner soldier feigning death rose to attack a jihadist with a rock before being killed.[3]

A spokesman for Ukraine's [[main-directorate-of-intelligence|GUR]], [[andrey-yusov|Andrey Yusov]], said his agents had helped plan the attack "against Russian war criminals" and promised more; Ukraine's embassy in [[senegal|Senegal]] amplified the interview with a caption by Ambassador [[yury-pivovarov|Yury Pivovarov]] before deleting it.[3] [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] condemned the statements as justification of terrorism.[3] Badlands commentary treated the episode as evidence that US aid to Ukraine was reaching al-Qaeda- and ISIS-linked groups in Africa, and as confirmation of a Russia–[[nato|NATO]] proxy war fought through groups such as Wagner and ISIS.[3] It also credited Wagner with assisting [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]]'s 2022 seizure of power in Burkina Faso and, more broadly, with helping to loosen what it called globalist control over Africa.[3]

## Bakhmut and the feud with the Russian defence ministry (May 2023)

During the [[battle-of-bakhmut|Battle of Bakhmut]], Wagner's public feud with the Russian military chain of command intensified.[4] In an audio statement late on Saturday, May 13, 2023, Prigozhin said that while regular Russian forces had "retreated" from parts of Bakhmut's north, his own Wagner fighters were still advancing, sustaining his contested narrative that only his company was spearheading gains and engaged in heavy fighting akin to the battle for Soledar.[4] He claimed [[ukraine|Ukraine]] held only one square kilometre of the city and roughly twenty-nine tall buildings.[4] Prigozhin's characterization of the regular forces as "fleeing" was denied by the Kremlin, though the defence ministry had itself acknowledged a limited pullback in northern sectors the day before.[4] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the episode as a "soft mutiny" whose true nature was impossible to determine from Western media coverage.[4]

## Blinken's Sahel warning (August 2023)

In August 2023 US Secretary of State [[antony-blinken|Antony Blinken]] told the BBC that Wagner was "taking advantage" of the instability in [[niger|Niger]] following the junta's removal of President [[mohamed-bazoum|Mohamed Bazoum]], amid suggestions that the coup leaders had sought the group's help.[5] Blinken said he did not believe Russia or Wagner had instigated the coup, but expressed concern about the group "possibly manifesting itself" across the Sahel, noting its established presence in neighbouring Mali.[5] He characterized its record bluntly: "Every single place that this Wagner group has gone, death, destruction and exploitation have followed," adding that "insecurity has gone up, not down."[5]

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] commentary questioned the framing. [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that the alignment of backers — the [[european-union|European Union]], the [[united-nations|United Nations]] and the [[state-department|State Department]] behind the deposed government, Russia and Wagner behind the coup — was itself revealing, while [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that the western establishment was "fully on board with the Wagner coup narrative" and treated the events instead as a popular revolt against puppet leadership in Africa.[5]

## Kayfabe interpretation of the defence ministry feud (May 2023)

In May 2023, Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] reported that many Russia-aligned citizen journalists regarded the ongoing public feud between [[yevgeny-prigozhin|Yevgeny Prigozhin]]'s Wagner Group and the [[russian-ministry-of-defense|Russian Ministry of Defence]] as [[kayfabe|kayfabe]], on the reasoning that Prigozhin would be in a gulag if his continued and highly publicized tirades were not directly approved by the [[kremlin|Kremlin]].[6] In the same commentary Wagner was described as pressuring Moscow to mount a counter-strike, which Russian forces were said to be gearing up to deliver with public backing amid the [[battle-of-bakhmut|Battle of Bakhmut]].[6]

## Deployment alongside Hezbollah in Lebanon

In September 2024 Badlands reported that the Wagner Group and the Russian military were both dug in alongside [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] in Lebanon, and had been throughout the conflict, awaiting an Israeli attack.[7] The analysis held that when the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] signaled months earlier — by moving most of its armor north to the Lebanese border — that its strategy would be a blitzkrieg, the Russians responded that they knew exactly how to defeat such an offensive from their Ukraine experience.[7]

On this reading, the principal reason [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had not already invaded Lebanon was not [[iran|Iran]], whom he had publicly wanted to fight for forty years, but the presence of Russian forces and Wagner personnel who had already demonstrated both willingness and ability to invade and occupy a belligerent sovereign nation.[7]

## Countering Western influence in Africa (2024)

Badlands Media argued in September 2024 that a major obstacle to French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]]'s stated ambition of "building bridges" with Africa is the Wagner Group, which has operated on the continent for several years alongside the Russian military, training local security forces to counter forces trained by NATO and the EU.[8] The outlet characterized Macron's "bridges" as a euphemism for violent coups and destabilizing color revolutions, and noted that Wagner and Russia are active in both the Israel theater and the Ukraine theater.[8] The commentary appeared alongside reporting that [[chad|Chad]] — described as the last bastion of French imperialism — was pressing for the expulsion of French forces.[8]
