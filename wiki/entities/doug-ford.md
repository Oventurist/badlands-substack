# Doug Ford

Doug Ford is the Premier of Ontario, Canada's most populous province, who emerged in March 2025 as one of the most confrontational Canadian figures in the escalating tariff dispute with the United States.[1]

## Electricity surcharge and threats

Ford followed through on a promise to impose a 25% tariff on Canadian electricity exported to Michigan, New York and Minnesota, applying the surcharge on a Monday in March 2025.[1] He subsequently declared that he was prepared to "shut the electricity off completely" to those states if the United States continued to "escalate" the trade conflict.[1] Asked by Fox Business whether he would relent, Ford said he would do so only once President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] stopped threatening his allies.[1]

## Trump's response

Trump mocked Ford's plan, arguing that his own reciprocal tariff program would render Ontario's measures useless.[1] Writing on Truth Social, Trump complained that "Canada is charging the USA from 250% to 390% Tariffs on many of our farm products" while "Ontario just announced a 25% surcharge on 'electricity,' of all things, and you're not even allowed to do that."[1] He pledged that the United States would "just get it all back on April 2," the date set for the administration's reciprocal tariff plan to take effect, and characterized Canada as "a Tariff abuser" that the United States would no longer subsidize.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized Ford's posture as "an unserious flex, executed through emotionally blackmailing 47 for continued subsidies," arguing that the American public was largely unaffected by the trade war because the United States is the party doing the subsidizing and can source domestic lumber and electricity.[1] She added that Ford's insistence that Trump should not threaten allies in fact reinforced Trump's point — that America's nominal allies behave as "entitled freeloaders."[1]

## Reversal of the electricity surcharge (March 2025)

Ford backed down within days. On March 11, 2025, Ontario reversed course on the surcharge on power sales to the United States after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] vowed to impose 50 percent tariffs on Canadian metals from the following day.[2] The whipsaw of announcements from both governments rattled global markets, with the S&P 500 falling as much as 1.5 percent before rebounding and the White House abandoning the 50 percent metals plan hours before implementation.[2] Trump characterized Ontario's proposed electricity surcharge as an "abusive threat."[2] See [[2025-us-canada-trade-war|the trade war]].[2]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described Ford's original threat against three states as "an attempt to flex on Trump" and arguably an act of war, and said that when Trump mentioned 50 percent steel tariffs in retaliation "Ontario immediately changed course. Because, of course they did."[2] She argued that the United States subsidizes Canada and holds all the cards, and that Mexico had shown itself "much smarter than Canada."[2]

## October 2025 Reagan tariff advertisement

In October 2025 the Ontario government under Ford paid for a television advertisement using audio from an address by former President [[ronald-reagan|Ronald Reagan]] criticizing tariffs.[3] President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] called the ad "fraudulent," suspended trade talks with Canada, and said he would impose another 10 percent tariff on Canadian goods as punishment.[3] Ford announced on Friday, after speaking with Prime Minister [[mark-carney|Mark Carney]], that the ad would stop airing the following Monday — a withdrawal consistent with Canada's declared strategy of removing all irritants from the relationship.[3] The episode is treated in more detail at [[ontario-reagan-tariff-advertisement|the Ontario Reagan tariff advertisement]].[3]

## "How about we buy Alaska?"

In January 2025 Ford responded to President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s repeated calls for a merger between the United States and Canada by suggesting that Canada could instead buy the states of Alaska and Minnesota.[4] Calling Canada "the greatest country in the world," he told journalists that there was no way it would fall under the rule of Washington.[4] "I will make him [Trump] a counteroffer. How about if we buy Alaska and throw in Minnesota and [its main city] Minneapolis at the same time?" Ford quipped, adding, "So, you know, it is not realistic."[4] He said he was aware that the president-elect "likes making these comments, and he likes joking around" with proposals such as annexing Canada, but insisted: "I take that seriously. He may be joking, but under my watch that will never, ever happen."[4] Since winning the November election Trump had repeatedly referred to Canada as the "51st state" and claimed most Canadians would "love" to join the US.[4]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] responded by tracing Canada's constitutional history — a French colony ceded to Britain under the Treaty of Paris in 1763 after the Seven Years' War, becoming the Dominion of Canada in 1867 under the British Empire — and argued that the exercise "is not about bringing Canada IN," but "about kicking the Regime OUT."[4]
