# The Fatherland

The Fatherland was a pro-German propaganda newspaper published in New York during World War I by [[george-sylvester-viereck|George Sylvester Viereck]], one of two such journals he ran alongside The International.[1]

The English occultist [[aleister-crowley|Aleister Crowley]] wrote for the paper from early 1915 and served as editor of its companion title The International, where he promoted both political propaganda and his "revealed doctrine" of Thelema and assorted occult and new-age theories; the articles Crowley published in this period were violently anti-British, as the scholar Marco Pasi records.[1] The evidence assembled by [[william-james-flynn|William James Flynn]] from the captured papers of Dr. Heinrich Albert documented direct payments by the German government to Viereck and The Fatherland, alongside German ownership of the Bridgeport Projectile Company and bribes to American politicians.[1]

Richard B. Spence argues that through The Fatherland and Viereck, Crowley's influence reached the top of the German clandestine and diplomatic hierarchies in the United States, bringing him into contact with Albert, von Papen and Ambassador Bernstorff, and that the Germans came to treat "the Beast" as an indispensable guide to American and British mentality without suspecting he was a British double agent feeding them disinformation.[1]
