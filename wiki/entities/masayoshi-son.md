# Masayoshi Son

Masayoshi Son is the Japanese billionaire founder and chief executive of [[softbank|SoftBank]], and one of the three principals placed at the helm of the Stargate artificial-intelligence infrastructure venture announced by [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in January 2025.[1]

## Stargate

Stargate was announced as a $500 billion AI infrastructure project to be funded by [[openai|OpenAI]], [[oracle|Oracle]] and SoftBank, and was described by Trump as "a resounding declaration of confidence in America's potential."[1] The venture was to begin with an initial $100 billion private investment, with Son, Oracle's [[larry-ellison|Larry Ellison]] and OpenAI chief executive [[sam-altman|Sam Altman]] leading it.[1]

The announcement immediately drew fire from [[elon-musk|Elon Musk]], who responded to OpenAI's statement that it would begin deploying $100 billion immediately by writing "They don't actually have the money," and later adding that "SoftBank has well under $10B secured. I have that on good authority."[1] Altman rejected the claim as "Wrong, as you surely know," invited Musk to visit the first site already under construction, and urged him to put the United States first in his new role.[1]

## Commentary

Badlands Media commentator [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] used Son's pledge as a foil for the competing $600 billion Saudi commitment announced the same week, joking that Son was "still my favorite name to say" before Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]] raised the stakes, and grouping Trump, MBS and Musk together as visionaries whose energy reshapes society regardless of whether the end product is a net positive.[1]

## Mar-a-Lago pledge (December 2024)

On December 16, 2024, Son visited President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at Mar-a-Lago in Palm Beach, Florida, and announced a $100 billion [[softbank|SoftBank]] investment in the United States over the following four years, promising at least 100,000 jobs focused on artificial intelligence and related infrastructure, with the money deployed before the end of Trump's term.[2] "My confidence level to the economy of the United States has tremendously increased with his victory," Son said. "President Trump is a double down president. I'm going to have to double down."[2] The two had made a similar joint announcement in 2016, when SoftBank agreed to invest $50 billion and create 50,000 jobs.[2] The new funding could be drawn from the Vision Fund, capital projects, or Arm Holdings, and could include already-announced commitments such as SoftBank's $1.5 billion investment in [[openai|OpenAI]].[2]

Badlands highlighted that in 2017 Son created the $100 billion SoftBank Vision Fund in tandem with Saudi Crown Prince [[mohammed-bin-salman|Mohammed bin Salman]], anchored by a $45 billion stake from the Saudi [[public-investment-fund|Public Investment Fund]], and treated the 2024 announcement as "pure signal" within its broader read of the Trump–Gulf–tech alignment.[2]
