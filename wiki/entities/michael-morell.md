# Michael Morell

Michael Morell is a former deputy director and acting director of the [[cia|Central Intelligence Agency]], serving in those roles between 2010 and 2013.[1] A career intelligence analyst, he briefed President George Bush on the morning of September 11, 2001, and stood with President [[barack-obama|Barack Obama]] in the [[white-house|White House]] situation room ten years later as US forces killed Osama Bin Laden.[1]

## Warnings about an ISIS resurgence

In June 2024, Morell told The Sun that the West could face a repeat of 9/11 if leaders did not act against a resurgence of [[isis|ISIS]], saying the world "feels the same now" as it did before the 2001 attacks and answering "Yes. Absolutely. 100 per cent." when asked whether he feared another mass-casualty attack.[1] He said the post-9/11 conviction inside the Bush White House and the CIA that "we can never let this happen again" needed to be recovered.[1] His remarks were published around the tenth anniversary of ISIS's June 29, 2014 seizure of territory in Iraq and Syria and its declaration of a caliphate under Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi, the year that also saw the launch of Operation Inherent Resolve, the US-led campaign against the Islamic State.[1]

## Role in Russiagate and Badlands criticism

Badlands Media commentator GhostofBasedPatrickHenry characterized Morell as "an old character from this movie," noting that Morell broke with CIA tradition by publicly endorsing Hillary Clinton for president in 2016 and that he was the first to accuse [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] of being an "unwitting agent" recruited by [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to infiltrate the White House on the Kremlin's behalf, in an August 2016 op-ed for Politico — in the Badlands telling, the origin of the "Russia, Russia, Russia" hoax.[1] The same commentary questioned how Morell could claim such intimate knowledge of ISIS operations and targets given the Badlands position that the CIA created the group, pointing to ISIS attacks in Russia including the murder of Jewish and Christian leaders in Dagestan, and to the 2019 joint Trump–Putin effort that dismantled the organization and killed al-Baghdadi.[1]
