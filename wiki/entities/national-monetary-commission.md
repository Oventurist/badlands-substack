# National Monetary Commission

The National Monetary Commission was established by Senator [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson W. Aldrich]] in the wake of the [[panic-of-1907|Panic of 1907]] to investigate a better economic system for the United States.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The Commission concluded that the European model was superior, a model that included the Central Bank of England.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913) Its reports and recommendations became one of the principal bases for the enactment of the [[federal-reserve-act-of-1913|Federal Reserve Act of 1913]], which led to the creation of the modern [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]] system.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

The article situates the Commission alongside the secret 1910 [[jekyll-island-club|Jekyll Island Club]] meeting as the two mechanisms — one public, one hidden — by which the [[cabal]] achieved central banking in the United States.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/1913)

## Creation and Composition

The National Monetary Commission was created by a bill signed into law by President [[theodore-roosevelt|Theodore Roosevelt]] in the wake of the turmoil of the [[panic-of-1907|Panic of 1907]].[2] Several economic "waves of panic" and the resulting bank runs had generated demand for a body tasked with brainstorming ideas for a more stable economic system.[2] The commission was supposed to study the banking problem and make recommendations to Congress, but Badlands Media states it was naturally packed with friends and cronies of the financial elite.[2]

Its chairman was Senator [[nelson-aldrich|Nelson Aldrich]] of Rhode Island, who represented Newport, home of America's richest banking families.[2] Following the commission's establishment, Aldrich embarked on a two-year fact-finding tour of Europe, consulting private central bankers in England, France and Germany, and on his return the [[jekyll-island-meeting|Jekyll Island meeting]] produced the [[aldrich-plan|Aldrich bill]].[2] The commission inevitably led to the creation of the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve]].[2] Representative [[charles-a-lindbergh-sr|Charles A. Lindbergh]] argued that the real purpose behind obtaining a monetary commission was to frame amendments to currency and banking laws that would suit the [[money-trust|Money Trust]].[2]
