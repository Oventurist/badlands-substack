# Boaz Weinstein

Boaz Weinstein is an American hedge fund manager and the founder of [[saba-capital|Saba Capital]], a credit-focused hedge fund he launched in 2009.[1] In Badlands Media commentary he is presented as a case study in how ostensibly allied [[deep-state|Deep State]] actors turn on one another for financial gain, on the theory that "there is no honor among thieves."[1]

## Background

According to the account, Weinstein grew up on the Upper West Side of Manhattan in a Jewish household; his father was born in the United States and his mother immigrated from Israel.[1] He was a master-level chess player in his youth and later a high-stakes poker and blackjack player who was barred from some casinos.[1] At age 15 he interned at the investment firm [[merrill-lynch|Merrill Lynch]], and he graduated from the University of Michigan in 1995 with a philosophy degree while spending summers working at [[goldman-sachs|Goldman Sachs]].[1] After graduation he returned to Merrill Lynch before settling at [[deutsche-bank|Deutsche Bank]] in 1998, where he built his reputation as a credit derivatives trader.[1]

## Family

In 2010 Weinstein married Tali Farhadian, an Iranian-Jewish woman whose family fled Iran when she was a child.[1] [[tali-farhadian-weinstein|Farhadian Weinstein]] attended Yale and Oxford as a Rhodes Scholar, served as a federal and state prosecutor, and ran unsuccessfully in the 2021 race for New York County District Attorney — the office now held by [[alvin-bragg|Alvin Bragg]], who is prosecuting [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the so-called "hush money" case.[1] The article treats this near-miss as evidence that the network around Trump's prosecutors is small and interlocking rather than coincidental.[1]

## The London Whale trade

Weinstein became widely known for his role in the 2012 [[london-whale|"London Whale"]] episode, which produced roughly $6 billion in losses for [[jp-morgan|JP Morgan]].[1] Having learned that JP Morgan was shorting a particular class of credit investments, Weinstein bought into that class himself and then — speaking at an investment conference hosted by JP Morgan — urged attending investors to buy the same instruments.[1] The resulting buying pressure produced a short squeeze against the bank's position, costing JP Morgan billions while Weinstein profited.[1] The article compares the dynamic to the later [[gamestop|GameStop]] short-squeeze episode.[1]

## BlackRock closed-end fund campaign

In 2024 Saba Capital moved to seize control of the boards of ten [[blackrock|BlackRock]] closed-end funds, arguing that BlackRock had structured shareholder proxy voting so that incumbent directors could not realistically be challenged.[1] The author initially welcomed the campaign as a strike against a quintessentially Deep State asset manager, but reversed that view after examining Weinstein's proposed slate for the board of BlackRock Capital Allocation Trust: [[shavar-jeffries|Shavar Jeffries]], [[jennifer-raab|Jennifer Raab]], and Lieutenant Colonel [[alexander-vindman|Alexander Vindman]].[1] The author concludes that Weinstein's stated aim of better fund management masks an intent to "churn and burn" the funds at the expense of BlackRock and its investors.[1]

## DWAC shares

Weinstein owned 2.4 million shares of [[digital-world-acquisition-corp|Digital World Acquisition Corp]] (DWAC) when it was announced in September 2021 that the SPAC would merge with [[trump-media-and-technology-group|Trump Media]].[1] He sold his entire stake immediately upon the announcement — attributed in the article to personal antipathy toward Trump — capturing only a small part of the roughly 1,400% two-day run-up in the share price.[1]
