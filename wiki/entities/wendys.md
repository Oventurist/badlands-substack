# Wendy's

Wendy's is an Ohio-based fast-food restaurant chain that drew national criticism in February 2024 after disclosing plans to test an "Uber-style" surge-pricing model in which menu prices would fluctuate through the day based on demand.[1]

## Dynamic pricing plan

Under the plan, the cost of items such as a Dave's burger would rise during the lunch or dinner rush.[1] The test was set for a high-stakes rollout the following year, and critics argued it would squeeze more money out of inflation-battered Americans who may not have the option to eat during "off-peak" hours.[1]

CEO Kirk Tanner, who had risen to the chief role earlier that month, announced the system on a call with investors and said the company would invest $20 million in high-tech menu boards capable of updating prices in real time without incurring additional overhead costs.[1] "As we continue to show the benefit of this technology in our company-operated restaurants, franchisee interest in digital menu boards should increase further supporting sales and profit growth across the system," Tanner said.[1] He did not put a ceiling on how far the dynamic pricing model could spike the cost of a meal, nor commit to base prices actually falling during slower periods.[1]

## Commentary

In the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] linked the story to the fight for health freedom and medical sovereignty ignited by the COVID-19 mandates, arguing that outrage over Wendy's potentially overcharging customers missed the deeper point that the food itself was harmful.[1] He dismissed fears that McDonald's and Burger King might follow suit, urging readers to "stop paying to poison yourselves" and let what he called weaponized food systems destroy themselves, adding that an era of medical sovereignty requires radical individual responsibility for everything a person consumes.[1]
