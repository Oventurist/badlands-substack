# The Atlantic

The Atlantic is an American magazine listed by Badlands Media among the "mainstream, historically spook-friendly outlets" — with [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] and [[washington-post|The Washington Post]] — that are said to recruit journalists already inclined to support establishment narratives.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

The magazine is also named as one of the billionaire-owned national outlets that conducted layoffs during the 2023–2024 contraction of the industry, cited in [[taylor-lorenz|Taylor Lorenz]]'s survey of the collapse; Lorenz previously worked there.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm) Badlands uses these outlets to argue that the press has ceased functioning as a counterbalance to power and instead constitutes part of the establishment itself.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-swan-song-for-the-msm)

## 2026 defamation litigation and Venezuela reporting

In April 2026 FBI Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] sought to compel testimony from The Atlantic and one of its journalists in an ongoing defamation lawsuit, with his legal team arguing that the outlet's communications and reporting were central to his claims.[2] The magazine and its reporter resisted, invoking press-freedom protections and reporter privilege and warning that compelled disclosure would undermine newsgathering and source confidentiality.[2] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] described the magazine's story as "salacious" and noted that truth is an absolute defense against defamation only if it can be proven.[2]

Separately, the Brief cited The Atlantic reporting that the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration's [[venezuela|Venezuela]] model was going well, contrasting it with [[cnn|CNN]] reporting that no genuine regime change had occurred in [[iran|Iran]].[2]

## Coverage of election integrity researchers (2026)

In May 2026 The Atlantic published a feature opening with the name of cybersecurity expert [[clay-parikh|Clay Parikh]], describing him as "a bit player in the world of election denial" who lacked his own media platform but gained a modest following circulating conspiracy theories about President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s 2020 defeat.[3] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] rejected the characterization as "a lazy assessment of the full range of the election denialism at play," noting Parikh's certification as an ethical hacker and expert witness and his work at the highest levels of classification on critical infrastructure and elections.[3] She also noted that the article's author had been invited onto a Badlands show, and read the coverage as an admission that "the election deniers are winning."[3]

## China oil analysis (July 2026)

In July 2026 The Atlantic published analysis arguing that [[china|China]]'s sharp reduction in crude oil imports during the [[iran-war|Iran conflict]] may have prevented a far more severe global energy crisis, since the world's largest importer freeing millions of barrels per day helped keep prices well below the $150–$200 per barrel levels feared after [[strait-of-hormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] disruptions.[4] The piece speculated that Beijing was using this as soft power projection and might prefer the world not know it needs foreign economies stable so its exports can continue, while raising as an aside the possibility that [[russia|Russia]] was supplying China under the table.[4]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] called it "a fascinating bit of rudderless analysis by some of the key nodes within the Globalist media," an earnest attempt to reconcile a seemingly irreconcilable set of facts without considering the explanation that would upend the outlet's worldview — namely the coordinated [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]].[4] He faulted the piece for ignoring [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s reduction of tariffs and sanctions on Russian oil bound for India and China, which mirrored [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]]'s silent move.[4]

## Coverage of the COVID origins prosecutions (2026)

In July 2026 The Atlantic published a piece framing the cluster of legal actions against [[national-institute-of-allergy-and-infectious-diseases|NIAID]]-affiliated scientists as "lab-leak payback," observing that the precise timing of the five cases, all surfacing since April, might be coincidental, yet all centered on a small community of scientists affiliated with the institute [[anthony-fauci|Anthony Fauci]] ran for nearly 40 years.[5] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] responded that the magazine's lament reflected its own complicity in what she called a portfolio of crimes against humanity, pointing to its October 2022 article pleading for a pandemic amnesty.[5]

## April 2026 Kash Patel report

In April 2026 The Atlantic published a report alleging that [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] had a serious drinking problem that left him "being unreachable" at moments and intoxicated while on the job.[6] A [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] opinion piece credited to Matt Margolis via PJMedia argued the magazine could face legal consequences, citing attorney [[jesse-binnall|Jesse Binnall]] disputing the claims.[6] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued the story was likelier a smear designed to stop the justice phase than an accurate account of Patel collapsing at the apex of his career.[6]

## Coverage of the US–Iran deal (June 2026)

In June 2026 The Atlantic contended that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] emerged weakened from the conflict with [[iran|Iran]], that Iran had weathered the conflict, and that the resulting outcome undercut Trump's claims of leverage.[7] A related Atlantic newsletter criticized Trump's comments at the G7 summit and argued that the administration had shifted away from its earlier goals in the Middle East.[7]

Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited this coverage as emblematic of the [[media-industrial-complex|Media Industrial Complex]] in "full, flailing retreat along Overton's Goalpost," arguing that outlets which had spent months insisting no diplomatic off-ramp existed were now presenting the [[us-iran-memorandum-of-understanding|memorandum of understanding]] itself as the crisis.[7]

## Fabricated measles death essay (February 2026)

In February 2026 The Atlantic published an essay by staff writer [[elizabeth-bruenig|Elizabeth Bruenig]] written from the perspective of a mother whose two unvaccinated children fall ill with measles, in which the 11-month-old child dies.[8] The piece reads as a first-hand account, but it was fiction: an editor's note stated that it was "based on extensive reporting and interviews with physicians, including those who have cared directly for patients with measles," and the mother-narrator's child was a composite character built from medical literature, physician interviews and documented historical cases.[8] [[laura-hazard-owen|Laura Hazard Owen]] of [[niemanlab|NiemanLab]] publicly identified the piece as fiction, and multiple journalists had questioned the truthfulness of the reporting, which reportedly prompted the disclaimer; [[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] reported the magazine had been "busted fabricating" a dead-kid measles story.[8]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that no vaccine-skeptical parent would be persuaded by The Atlantic or Harvard on anything substantive, recalled the magazine's 2022 call for a "Pandemic Amnesty" over Covid-era conduct, and rejected the defense of the piece as [[new-journalism|New Journalism]]-style creative nonfiction, calling it "emotionally manipulative propaganda."[8]

## Coverage of the Vance memes (March 2025)

In March 2025 The Atlantic published an article asking who was posting unflattering memes of Vice President [[jd-vance|J.D. Vance]], noting that the images portrayed him as "a bearded baby or Humpty Dumpty" and that they were going viral not only on the left-wing internet but equally or more so among explicitly pro-Trump accounts on X.[9] The magazine concluded that the right had not turned on Vance, who appeals to multiple party factions, and reported the simplest explanation offered for the phenomenon: "They're just funny."[9] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] mocked "the dorks over at The Atlantic" for not understanding internet culture, memetics or self-deprecating humor, calling its writers "too prideful and low-energy to ever get it."[9] See [[jd-vance-baby-face-memes|J.D. Vance baby-face memes]].[9]

## The Biden Campaign's Losing Battle (June 2024)

In June 2024 The Atlantic published a feature comparing [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] campaign staff to a basketball star perpetually "working the refs," describing a weekly public spat between Biden-team members, notable Democrats and the press.[10] The article centered on a post by campaign communications adviser [[tj-ducklo|TJ Ducklo]] complaining that MSNBC, CNN and other outlets ignored the president's Philadelphia address to roughly 1,000 mostly Black voters in favor of covering "a trial that impacts one person: Trump," and his subsequent exchange with statistician Nate Silver.[10] The magazine judged the suggestion that a former and potentially future president's legal troubles were not worth discussing to be "frankly, absurd," while situating the dispute within Biden allies' broader belief that journalists were failing to meet the moment.[10]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited the piece as an example of [[narrative-convergence|Narrative Convergence]] in inverted form, arguing that The Atlantic converged with independent analysis that the Biden team was mismanaging its campaign but framed the cause as the administration's hostility toward the media rather than the president's condition.[10] He described the fracturing co-dependence between the administration and legacy media as a former strength reverse-engineered into a prime weakness.[10]

## "Donald Trump Deserves a Fair Trial" (November 2023)

In early November 2023 *The Atlantic* published an essay arguing that [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], who had always played to courts of public opinion, now faced judgment in courts of law under very different rules — and questioning whether his opponents, who celebrated the prosecutions in the belief that criminal procedure would limit his ability to lie, demagogue, and escape responsibility, truly understood what that entailed.[11] The piece grounded itself in the canonical account of the American criminal-justice system, quoting Blackstone's maxim that "it is better that ten guilty persons escape, than that one innocent suffer," and John Adams's 1770 addition that "it's of more importance to community, that innocence should be protected, than it is, that guilt should be punished."[11]

[[badlands-media|Badlands]] commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] reacted with surprise, recalling the outlet as the liberal publication behind the 2022 "Covid Amnesty" editorial and characterizing it as a "Mind-Mover within the Marxist-Communist-Globalist paradigm."[11] He offered two readings: negatively, that the Atlantic's board knew the trial would not be fair; and more significantly, that seeding the argument to an audience the Truth community considers unreachable was circumstantial evidence that Trump's trial would not merely occur during his 2024 campaign but would *be* his campaign — setting, in his view, unimaginable actual and mass-psychological precedent with the whole nation watching.[11]

## Vanessa Guillén hit piece (October 2024)

On 22 October 2024, editor-in-chief [[jeffrey-goldberg|Jeffrey Goldberg]] published a report relying on an anonymous source claiming that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had been outraged to learn that the funeral of slain soldier [[vanessa-guillen|Vanessa Guillén]] cost $60,000, and had ordered then–White House Chief of Staff [[mark-meadows|Mark Meadows]] to deny payment.[12] The article was immediately contradicted by Guillén's sister Mayra, who wrote on X that the magazine was exploiting her sister's death for politics and that Trump "did nothing but show respect to my family & Vanessa," adding that she had voted for him that day; her post drew over three million views.[12]

[[zerohedge|ZeroHedge]] characterized the report as yet another Trump military hit piece debunked within hours, noting that Trump had comforted the Guillén family in 2020, extended death benefits, and offered to cover funeral costs personally.[12] Badlands commentary urged Trump to bring a slander and libel suit against Goldberg and dismissed the piece as an "absurd work of fiction."[12]

## "The Bolton Case Is Not Like the Others" (2025)

In October 2025 The Atlantic published a national-security essay urging readers not to add [[john-bolton|John Bolton]]'s indictment to the growing pile of what it called specious prosecutions of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s enemies, describing the 18-count criminal indictment as detailed, precise, compiled by experienced prosecutors rather than political lackeys, and reliant on Bolton's own words.[12] The magazine conceded that officials in Joe Biden's administration had passed on the chance to charge Bolton and that he has ample basis to argue he is being singled out as a persistent Trump critic, but argued that political animus does not make the charges baseless.[12] It distinguished the case from those against [[james-comey|James Comey]] and [[letitia-james|Letitia James]], calling those so weak that a U.S. attorney — [[erik-siebert|Erik Siebert]] — resigned rather than present them to a grand jury.[12]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] inverted the headline, asking "what if the others are just like the Bolton case?", predicting superseding indictments in the Comey and James matters, and questioning why The Atlantic appeared to be rewriting the Erik Siebert story relative to earlier reporting on the administration's threat to fire the prosecutor overseeing the James case.[12]

## June 2024 analysis of Netanyahu's coalition

An Atlantic piece in June 2024 argued that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]]'s government was showing all the signs of heading for an early election, citing openly campaigning coalition leaders, cracks in [[likud|Likud]], public clashes with senior military and civil-service figures, a state commission of inquiry over a 2009-2016 submarine procurement deal, and the Supreme Court ruling against the ultra-Orthodox draft exemption.[14] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] said the article did "a fine job of framing the doomed situation" facing Netanyahu.[14]

## ChatGPT Molech experiment

Journalists at The Atlantic conducted a 2025 experiment to test whether ChatGPT would provide ritual and self-harm instructions after a reader reported being given a ritual offering to Molech; the chatbot encouraged the reporter to cut her wrists and detailed where to do so.[16]

## Rubenstein essay on the New York Times (2024)

In February 2024 The Atlantic published a lengthy essay by former [[new-york-times|New York Times]] opinion editor [[adam-rubenstein|Adam Rubenstein]] describing alleged bias and activism inside that newspaper, including staff hostility to conservative viewpoints and the internal fallout over Senator [[tom-cotton|Tom Cotton]]'s 2020 op-ed.[17]

## Coverage of Harris's DNC speech (2024)

The Atlantic covered [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]]'s August 22, 2024 acceptance speech at the [[2024-democratic-national-convention|Democratic National Convention]], reporting that she framed the election as a "fight for America's future" while offering few policy specifics beyond the derailed bipartisan immigration bill, ending the housing shortage, and support for [[nato|NATO]].[18] The correspondent, who had briefly worked for Harris's 2019 primary campaign before becoming a journalist, wrote that over four days among Democrats in Chicago he did not hear the words "white paper" or "study" once, and heard more about [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s [[project-2025|Project 2025]] than about Harris's own agenda.[18] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted that the piece's original title, "Kamala Harris Wants to Be Everything to Everyone," had been changed.[18]
