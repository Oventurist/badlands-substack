# Paramount Global

Paramount Global is the American media conglomerate that owns [[cbs|CBS]], [[showtime|Showtime]] and the Paramount+ streaming service. In "A New Hollywood," [[erik-carlson|Erik Carlson]] identifies the company as the hub of what he reads as a pro-Trump realignment inside [[hollywood|Hollywood]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## Ownership and leadership

Paramount Global is run by [[sheri-redstone|Sheri Redstone]], described by Carlson as a Trump ally, who took over from her father [[sumner-redstone|Sumner Redstone]] in 2016; Sumner, also a Trump ally, died in 2020.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) The company is in the process of merging with, and will eventually be controlled by, [[skydance-media|Skydance Media]], run by [[david-ellison|David Ellison]], son of Oracle cofounder [[larry-ellison|Larry Ellison]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## Valuation

Carlson notes that Paramount Global is worth roughly $8 billion, having fluctuated between $20 and $40 billion in the seven years before Covid and spiking to $60 billion three years earlier when it launched its streaming service before immediately falling back.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## The CBS lawsuit

[[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] sued CBS for $10 billion over the edited [[60-minutes|60 Minutes]] interview with [[kamala-harris|Kamala Harris]], which he viewed as election tampering.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) Carlson observes that Trump is suing for more than the parent company is worth, and that Paramount Global — which does not want anything to interfere with the Skydance merger — has recently discussed settling out of court despite critics initially calling the suit frivolous.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He raises the possibility that what would induce a settlement is control over the company.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## Programming

Carlson points to Paramount+ programming as evidence of a shift: [[lioness|Lioness]], the oil-field drama *Landman*, and Showtime's *The Agency*.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood) He remarks that even before the merger, Paramount+ "almost seems like a [[tom-cruise|Tom Cruise]] streaming app."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/a-new-hollywood)

## Action against ByteDance (2026)

In February 2026 Paramount joined [[disney|Walt Disney]] in sending cease-and-desist letters to [[bytedance|ByteDance]] over the AI video model [[seedance-2-0|Seedance 2.0]], alleging that the tool used copyrighted material and characters without authorization after viral clips depicting recognizable Hollywood actors and characters circulated online.[2] The letters were not publicly distributed but were first reviewed and reported by Axios; ByteDance responded by promising improved safeguards against IP theft.[2] Badlands commentary framed the studios' litigation posture as an attempt to retain monopolistic control over an industry already transforming beyond their reach.[2]

## February 2026 Warner Bros. Discovery talks

In February 2026 [[warner-bros-discovery|Warner Bros. Discovery]] reopened talks with Paramount — by then owned by [[skydance|Skydance]] — about potential business arrangements after receiving a waiver from [[netflix|Netflix]], a development disclosed in a Warner regulatory filing and reported by AP News.[3] Warner continued to back its merger agreement with Netflix even as it revisited a Paramount takeover.[3] Any such transaction would require regulatory approval because it involves broadcast assets owned by Paramount, including [[cbs|CBS]], and their related licenses.[3]

The same week, [[stephen-colbert|Stephen Colbert]], host of CBS's *The Late Show*, publicly criticized the [[federal-communications-commission|FCC]] as "bullies" over guidance concerning the [[fcc-equal-time-rule|equal-time rule]].[3] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that Paramount and Warner were "wigging all the way out," and that consolidation was the worst available response to an industry being transformed by decentralized production and distribution: "If you cut off a spider's head, it dies."[3]

## Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition (2026)

Paramount's proposed $111 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery drew an open letter on April 13, 2026 signed by more than 1,000 writers, actors, and directors warning that the deal would accelerate consolidation, reduce film output and story diversity, cut creator job opportunities, raise production costs, and narrow audience choice.[4] The deal is heavily financed by Gulf sovereign wealth funds, with Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund committing roughly $12 billion within a $24 billion equity package alongside Qatari and Abu Dhabi investors, structured as passive stakes to ease U.S. regulatory review.[4] Paramount defended the transaction, saying it would preserve independent creative leadership and increase theatrical output.[4] Badlands commentator Burning Bright framed the Trump-allied Ellisons' Paramount Skydance consolidation, backed by Gulf capital, as a Sovereign Alliance maneuver breaking the legacy culture apparatus's monopoly.[4]

## December 2023 merger talks with Warner Bros. Discovery

Merger discussions between Paramount Global and [[warner-bros-discovery|Warner Bros. Discovery]] were first reported in December 2023, when Warner chief executive [[david-zaslav|David Zaslav]] and Paramount chief executive [[bob-bakish|Bob Bakish]] met over lunch in New York on Tuesday, December 19, 2023.[5] Zaslav also discussed a possible combination with Shari Redstone, whose National Amusements Inc. owned a controlling stake in Paramount Global; both companies declined to comment and [[axios|Axios]] first reported the talks, which *Variety* subsequently confirmed.[5]

At the end of the third quarter of 2023 Paramount Global carried long-term debt of $15.6 billion, well below Warner's $43.5 billion, but its market capitalization of $10.3 billion as of the December 20, 2023 close was far smaller than Warner's $28.4 billion.[5] Bankers had been engaged on both sides, though the status of the talks was characterized as very preliminary and terms could not be learned.[5] The rationale reported at the time was pooling television, film, sports, and streaming assets for scale and operational efficiency, especially by combining Paramount+ with Max to compete against [[netflix|Netflix]], [[disney|Disney]]'s Disney+, and Hulu.[5] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], read the approach as distress rather than strength, invoking his rule that "consolidation precedes collapse."[5]

## Skydance combination (2025)

In July 2025 [[skydance-media|Skydance]] founder [[david-ellison|David Ellison]] was on the verge of completing a roughly $8 billion combination that would install him as chief of Paramount and its CBS News division.[6] Commentators observed that the cachet once conferred by control of the network built by Edward R. Murrow, Mike Wallace, and Walter Cronkite had long since faded.[6] CBS management settled a lawsuit brought by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in order to keep the merger on track with his FCC regulators, a sequence Badlands contributor Burning Bright read as part of a deliberate restoration of Hollywood rather than its destruction.[6]

## FCC approval of the Skydance merger (July 2025)

On July 24, 2025, the [[federal-communications-commission|Federal Communications Commission]] said it would allow Paramount to merge with the Hollywood studio [[skydance-media|Skydance]], clearing the way for one of the most highly scrutinized media deals of the previous decade.[7] Chairman [[brendan-carr|Brendan Carr]] said the agency approved the deal after receiving assurances that the new company was committed to unbiased journalism and would not establish diversity, equity and inclusion programs, and welcomed "Skydance's commitment to make significant changes at the once storied CBS broadcast network."[7]
