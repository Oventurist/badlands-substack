# Christopher Porter

Christopher Porter served as the U.S. National Intelligence Officer for Cyber and was the author of a January 15, 2020 [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] memorandum on election infrastructure vulnerabilities.[1]

Porter's assessment warned that Russia, China, [[iran|Iran]], North Korea and non-state actors possessed the capability to disrupt voting, steal data, or undermine confidence in the 2020 presidential election, singling out internet-connected voter registration databases, pollbooks, and election websites as the most exposed systems.[1] The memo was declassified in 2026 by Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], prompting an Intelligence Community inspector general investigation into why its warnings were withheld from the public while officials asserted the election had been secure.[1]

## Role in the 2026 Gabbard memos

Unclassified memos underpinning Director of National Intelligence [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]]'s 2026 investigation report that Porter, as a former national intelligence cyber officer, raised internal concerns about how intelligence related to [[china|China]]'s election-related activities was handled during the 2020 cycle.[2] The same memos allege the [[national-intelligence-council|National Intelligence Council]] concluded in 2020 that China had accessed voter registration databases in an estimated 12 to 18 states, far beyond what was publicly disclosed at the time.[2]
