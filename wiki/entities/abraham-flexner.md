# Abraham Flexner

**Abraham Flexner** was the author of the 1910 report *Medical Education in the United States and Canada*, universally known as the [[flexner-report|Flexner Report]].[1]

Flexner was hired for the project at the proposal of his brother [[simon-flexner|Simon Flexner]], who sat on the board of directors of the Rockefeller Institute. Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] stresses that Abraham Flexner "knew nothing about medicine" and that, because the Flexners already worked for Rockefeller, they were a natural choice for a survey funded by the Rockefeller and Carnegie interests. Flexner toured the nation's medical schools in the company of the [[american-medical-association|AMA]]'s head of the Council on Medical Education, and certification of schools rested on his recommendations alone.[1]

His report concluded that it was far too easy to start a medical school and that most schools were not teaching "sound medicine." Schools that did not conform to his model lost funding and prestige and generally closed. DeLarme observes, in an author's note, that although their names are not well known, the Flexner brothers "have probably influenced the lives of more people in a more profound way than any other brothers in the last century, with the possible exception of Wilbur and Orville Wright."[1]
