# Pierbattista Pizzaballa

Pierbattista Pizzaballa is the Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem, the senior Roman Catholic prelate in the Holy Land and the head of the [[latin-patriarchate-of-jerusalem|Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem]]. Elevated to the College of Cardinals, he is customarily referred to in press coverage as Cardinal Pizzaballa. His office carries responsibility for Catholic worship at the region's principal shrines, including the [[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre|Church of the Holy Sepulchre]] in Jerusalem's Old City.

## Palm Sunday 2026 exclusion

Pizzaballa became the central figure in an international religious-freedom controversy on March 29, 2026, when Israeli police blocked him and other senior Catholic leaders from entering the Church of the Holy Sepulchre to celebrate a private [[palm-sunday|Palm Sunday]] Mass. According to the Associated Press reporting summarized in the Badlands Brief, the planned service was limited to a small number of clergy and was not open to the public; Catholic authorities said the episode marked the first time in centuries that top church leaders had been unable to conduct Palm Sunday services at the site.

Israeli authorities justified the exclusion on security grounds tied to the ongoing conflict involving [[iran|Iran]], citing missile threats, limited shelter availability and restricted emergency access in the Old City. The Latin Patriarchate condemned the decision as "a manifestly unreasonable and grossly disproportionate measure" that impeded freedom of worship.

## International response

The barring of the Patriarch drew rapid criticism from United States and European officials. U.S. Ambassador to Israel [[mike-huckabee|Mike Huckabee]] — generally a staunch defender of Israeli policy — said that "for the Patriarch to be barred from entry to the Church on Palm Sunday for a private ceremony is difficult to understand or justify," noting that Home Front Command guidelines restricting gatherings to fifty people or fewer were not exceeded by the four Catholic representatives involved. Italian Prime Minister [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] was more forceful, stating that preventing "the Patriarch of Jerusalem and the Custos of the Holy Land from entering, especially on a solemnity central to the faith such as Palm Sunday, constitutes an offence not only against believers but against every community that recognises religious freedom."

Following the backlash, Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] instructed authorities to allow access to the church, saying that holy sites of all three faiths had been "repeatedly targeted" by Iranian missile strikes and that missile fragments had landed meters from the Holy Sepulchre. Netanyahu insisted the decision involved "no malicious intent whatsoever."

## Badlands commentary

Writing in the Badlands Brief, [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] placed the incident at the end of a multi-year "guided progression" of restrictions on the Holy Sepulchre beginning with the 2020 [[covid-19-pandemic|COVID-19]] closure, arguing that while access had been progressively narrowed since then, at no prior point had church leaders themselves been denied entry or prevented from holding Mass. [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] tied the exclusion of Pizzaballa to an earlier Israeli property tax on church holdings and the freezing of church bank accounts, arguing the sequence demonstrated official hostility toward Christians and predicting that adherents of [[christian-zionism|Christian Zionism]] would rationalize it.
