# Bernard Baruch

Bernard Baruch was a Wall Street banker who, according to Badlands Media, was placed in charge of the "Wilson project" — the effort by pro-central-bank financiers to cultivate and elect [[woodrow-wilson|Woodrow Wilson]] after the [[aldrich-plan|Aldrich bill]] stalled in Congress.[1]

## Role in the Wilson campaign

When the bankers realized they lacked the votes for the Aldrich bill and Republican leadership never brought it to a vote, they switched their attention to the Democrats and began heavily financing Wilson, the Democratic presidential nominee.[1] Historian James Perloff is quoted as writing that Baruch "brought Wilson to the Democratic Party headquarters in New York in 1912, 'leading him like one would a poodle on a string,'" and that Wilson received an "indoctrination course" from the leaders convened there.[1]

## The Federal Reserve System

After Wilson's election on November 5, [[jp-morgan|J.P. Morgan]], [[paul-warburg|Paul Warburg]], Baruch and others advanced a new plan that Warburg called the [[federal-reserve|Federal Reserve System]], embodied in the Glass-Owen Bill — a measure Democratic leadership hailed as totally different from the Aldrich bill when, the article contends, it was virtually identical.[1]
