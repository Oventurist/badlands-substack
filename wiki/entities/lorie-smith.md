# Lorie Smith

Lorie Smith is a Christian graphic and web designer from Colorado who became the plaintiff in a landmark United States Supreme Court free-speech case decided in June 2023. Smith owns the design company 303 Creative and objected to being compelled under Colorado law to design wedding websites for same-sex couples, a service she said would require her to author messages contrary to her religious convictions.[1]

## Background and business

Smith operates 303 Creative, a design firm that, according to reporting summarized in the Badlands News Brief, had previously served gay customers in other contexts. Her objection was not to the identity of prospective clients but to the specific expressive content of same-sex wedding websites: she wished to limit her wedding-website line of business to heterosexual couples.[1] Commentary published in the brief stressed this distinction, noting that Smith "had no problem creating websites for same sex couples" and objected only to participating in a ceremony that violated her religious beliefs.[1]

## Litigation against Colorado

Smith sued the state of [[colorado-civil-rights-commission|Colorado]]'s enforcement apparatus over the state's anti-discrimination statute, which prohibits businesses from denying services on the basis of a customer's sexual orientation. She argued that applying the law to her design work would force her and other artists to produce customized messages that violated her beliefs and her rights under the [[first-amendment|First Amendment]].[1]

On Friday, June 30, 2023, the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]] ruled in her favor by a 6–3 vote that split along ideological lines. The Court's conservative majority held that the First Amendment bars Colorado from forcing "an individual to speak in ways that align with its views but defy her conscience about a matter of major significance."[1] The decision is documented on the case page [[303-creative-llc-v-elenis|303 Creative LLC v. Elenis]].

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] framed Smith's victory as the second time the Supreme Court had checked Colorado's treatment of religious dissenters, following the long-running litigation involving baker [[jack-phillips|Jack Phillips]] of [[masterpiece-cakeshop|Masterpiece Cakeshop]]. The commentary cautioned that the ruling might offer Smith limited practical protection, arguing that Colorado's government and courts had demonstrated that unfavorable appellate outcomes did not deter continued action against dissenters.[1]
