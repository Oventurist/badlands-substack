# Chiang Kai-shek

**Chiang Kai-shek** was the leader of the Chinese Nationalist army, described by Colonel [[roxane-watkins|Roxane Watkins]] in her primer on [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]] as a drug lord.[1] He commanded the [[kuomintang]], which functioned as the Chinese Nationalist Army, and met Colonel [[paul-helliwell]] while the latter was assigned in Southern China.[2]

## Opium financing

Chiang sold opium to Chinese addicts to fund his war efforts, and is described as allegedly the world's largest drug trafficker at that point.[2] The Chinese had first been introduced to opium by the United Kingdom, leading to two conflicts the article calls the Boxer wars, in which China tried to stop the drug trafficking, lost both, and lost control of Hong Kong to Britain.[2] OSS chief [[william-donovan]] directed Helliwell to covertly supply Chiang's army with weapons, and Helliwell added opiate trafficking to the mission.[2]

## Nationalist versus communist

Chiang was labelled a "nationalist" because he opposed [[mao-zedong]], the "communist"; Mao opposed selling opium or heroin to the Chinese population and opposed outside interference by foreign powers after China's experiences with the United Kingdom and Japan.[2] The two Chinese leaders' armies fought for territory, and Chiang was eventually pushed out of China into Burma, from which he was later expelled as well.[2]

## Formosa and Taiwan

[[claire-chennault]], [[william-pawley]] and [[allen-dulles]] set Chiang up on Formosa — recently vacated by the Japanese — to continue supplying the opium that paid for the covert operations of Operation Gladio.[2] Chiang renamed the island [[taiwan]], declared martial law, and for roughly forty years, into the 1980s, Taiwan functioned as what the article calls a drug-dealing dictatorship.[2] Heroin from this source was first imported into New York by [[lucky-luciano]], the beginning of the American leg of the network code-named "[[operation-x]]".[2]

## Legacy in Taiwan

Watkins states that the present-day Taiwanese [[kuomintang|KMT]] political party descends from Chiang's KMT army.[1] She raises this lineage alongside the Ukrainian case — in which the [[right-sector|Right Sector]] and [[azov-battalion|Azov]] battalions trace their heritage to [[otto-skorzeny|Otto Skorzeny]]-trained [[stepan-bandera|Stepan Bandera]] and [[andriy-melnyk|Andriy Melnyk]] of the [[organization-of-ukrainian-nationalists|OUN]] — to argue that wartime paramilitary networks associated with Gladio have persisted into contemporary party politics.[1]
