# Victoria Alonso

Victoria Alonso is an Argentine-born film executive who spent more than a decade at [[marvel-studios|Marvel Studios]], rising to the position of President of Physical, Post Production, VFX and Animation.[1] Though she lacked the public name recognition of studio head [[kevin-feige|Kevin Feige]], she was described as one of the principal architects of the Marvel Cinematic Universe, with involvement dating back to *Iron Man* (2008).[1] In her role she served as one of Feige's primary deputies, responsible for ensuring that both the physical and — increasingly, and controversially — the digital visual-effects production of Marvel films proceeded smoothly.[1]

## Firing by Disney

In late March 2023, [[disney|Disney]] abruptly terminated Alonso "for cause," a move reported as massive and drastic within the trade press but little discussed in wider media.[1] Reporting indicated she was dismissed by a committee of Disney executives, and that Feige ultimately chose not to intervene in the decision.[1]

Disney's stated reason was Alonso's participation in *Argentina, 1985*, an Argentine legal drama nominated at that year's Academy Awards which the Argentina-born Alonso produced; the company said she breached her contract by working on and continuing to promote a non-Disney film.[1] Alonso's team responded that she had received permission to do so.[1]

## Legal threat and LGBTQ+ advocacy claim

Following the firing, reports emerged that Alonso might pursue legal action over her termination.[1] Her representatives issued statements rejecting Disney's stated grounds as "absolutely ridiculous" and asserting that she had been "silenced" by the company over her advocacy for LGBTQ+ rights.[1]

Alonso's lawyers suggested the real cause was that she had been among the most vocal internal voices pressing Disney to fight Florida's so-called "Don't Say Gay" legislation in 2022.[1] That advocacy included appearing before GLAAD and calling then-CEO Bob Chapek out by name, saying: "So I ask you again Mr. Chapek: please respect—if we're selling family—take a stand against all of these crazy outdated laws. Take a stand for family."[1] Trade reporting stated that Alonso was subsequently "benched" from press duties by Disney and refused to participate in promotion of recent Marvel films; her side also claimed she was asked to do something "reprehensible," left undescribed, by a Disney executive, which served as the last straw preceding her firing.[1]

## Badlands framing

Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] cited Alonso's removal — described as the unceremonious firing of "the second in command at Marvel Studios" and the ensuing "circular firing squad of lawsuits" — as one element in a pattern of accelerating trouble at major "woke" entertainment brands, alongside Marvel's box-office losses, Disney+ streaming losses, and the arrest of [[jonathan-majors|Jonathan Majors]].[1]
