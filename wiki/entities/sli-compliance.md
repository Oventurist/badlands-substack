# SLI Compliance

SLI Compliance is a Voting System Testing Laboratory (VSTL) accredited by the [[election-assistance-commission|US Election Assistance Commission]] to test election technologies for certification.[1] It is named, alongside [[pro-v-and-v|Pro V&V]], in the Badlands Media essay "US Elections Must Be Independent" as one of the labs that occupy the pivotal position in the [[trusted-build|Trusted Build]] certification chain.[1]

In that chain, the lab tests a vendor's completed Trusted Build and reports to the Secretary of State whether it complies with all federal and state voting system standards; in practice, the author notes, the builds always appear to be judged good enough on the available record.[1] Because the Secretary of State typically has little or no technology background, she effectively outsources her certification responsibility to the lab, making the lab's assessment the load-bearing element of the entire system's claimed independence.[1]

The essay questions whether that assessment can be trusted, citing certified ethical hacker [[clay-parikh|Clay Parikh]], who has performed testing for the VSTLs and reported that testers are prohibited from examining critical parts of US voting systems, and that no one else demonstrably tests those components either.[1] The result, in the author's analysis, is that the labs' seemingly independent authority conceals a gap filled only by vendor self-review.[1]
