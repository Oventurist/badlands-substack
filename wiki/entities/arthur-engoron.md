# Arthur Engoron

Arthur Engoron is the New York judge who presided over the [[new-york-civil-fraud-trial|New York civil fraud trial]] against former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and his companies.[1]

Engoron issued an order that Trump's businesses be dissolved and their business certificates cancelled. On Friday, October 6, 2023, an appeals court granted a stay pausing that order, though it allowed the trial itself to continue.[1] The [[trump-organization|Trump Organization]] called the order "overzealous," stating that "Judge Engoron's order erroneously sought to adjudicate the rights of non-party business entities that employ nearly 1,000 hard-working New Yorkers, have never been accused of any wrongdoing and, were never given their day in court – in clear violation of their fundamental Constitutional rights and Due Process."[1]

The episode was reported as a bonus item in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] of October 9, 2023.[1]

## Loomer allegations against Dawn Engoron (November 2023)

In November 2023, conservative activist [[laura-loomer|Laura Loomer]] accused Engoron's wife, [[dawn-engoron|Dawn Engoron]], of running an X account that frequently posted anti-Trump messages, prompting calls for a mistrial in the civil business fraud case Engoron was overseeing.[2] The judge had already faced criticism of his own conduct on the bench.[2] *Newsweek* was unable to verify that the account belonged to Dawn Engoron, and she issued a firm denial, saying she had no Twitter account and had never posted anti-Trump messages.[2]

## Reversal of the civil fraud penalty (August 2025)

On Thursday, August 21, 2025, a New York appellate court struck down the roughly $527 million civil fraud penalty Engoron had imposed on President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], his two sons, other executives and the [[trump-organization|Trump Organization]], ruling the fine "excessive" and in violation of the Eighth Amendment's ban on excessive fines.[3] The penalty stemmed from Engoron's February 2024 ruling ordering the defendants to pay nearly $364 million in "ill-gotten gains" plus years of interest, with Trump responsible for nearly 98% of the total.[3] Badlands contributor [[jon-herold|Jon Herold]] responded by asking when "this gremlin" would be held accountable for allowing the excessive fine in the first place, while [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] wrote that the whole case looked "fake from the beginning."[3]

## Appeal of the fraud penalty (April 2024)

On April 1, 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted a $175 million bond in the New York civil fraud case, staying collection of the more than $454 million judgment arising from Engoron's ruling while the appeal proceeds.[4] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Trump was appealing "Engoron's egregious abuses of power and discretion in the NY Civil Fraud trial," arguing that the judge's decisions produced "manifest injustice" and that his penalties were "cruel and unusual by any definition."[4] She noted that the Appellate court had agreed to hear arguments in September, calling the timing interesting, and that Engoron's decisions and choices in the case would now be reviewed.[4]

## Bailey ex parte contact allegation (May 2024)

In May 2024, high-profile New York real estate attorney [[adam-leitman-bailey|Adam Leitman Bailey]], whose law license had once been suspended, told NBC New York that he had approached Engoron to offer unsolicited advice about a law at issue in the civil fraud case.[6] Bailey said the conversation took place roughly three weeks before Engoron's February 2024 decision fining [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] $454 million for falsely inflating the value of his assets.[6] Engoron, through a court spokesman, denied any impropriety and said he was "wholly uninfluenced" by Bailey.[6] New York's judicial oversight body subsequently launched an investigation into the alleged interaction, according to sources familiar with the matter.[6] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] framed the episode as part of a pattern in which probes, motions and inquiries were being launched against those judging Trump rather than against Trump himself.[6]

## Appellate rebuke

On March 25, 2024, a New York appeals court agreed to halt collection of the more than $454 million civil fraud judgment Engoron had entered against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] if Trump posted $175 million within 10 days.[5] Syracuse University law professor Greg Germain told Newsweek that "the $175 million reduction ... shows that the appellate division has serious concerns about the validity of Judge Engoron's decision," and said Trump might have a strong case to challenge the February ruling requiring payment of $455 million.[5] Badlands writer [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that legal and financial experts across ideological lines were calling out Engoron's abuses and that the appeals court had rebuked "the tyrannical judge," leaving his credibility destroyed.[5]

## Challenge to Engoron's definition of fraud

In February 2024, following Engoron's $355 million judgment against [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] in the New York civil fraud case, Trump's legal team announced it would challenge the judge's underlying definition of fraud.[7] Trump's principal lawyer [[chris-kise|Chris Kise]] said the matter "raises serious legal and constitutional questions regarding 'fraud' claims/findings without any actual fraud," and indicated an appeal would be filed within the court's 30-day clock.[7] Kise accused Engoron and Attorney General [[letitia-james|Letitia James]] of attempting to drive Trump out of New York, arguing the effort was damaging to the city.[7]

## First Day of the 2023 Trial

Engoron presided over the opening day of the New York civil fraud trial on October 2, 2023, with Donald Trump seated directly facing him in a hushed courtroom guarded by Secret Service agents.[8] During proceedings Trump largely confined himself to scoffs and eye rolls, but outside the courthouse he attacked Engoron directly, calling him "a judge that should be out of office," suggesting that "some people say" he "could be charged criminally for what he's doing," and branding him deranged while calling for his removal.[8] Badlands commentary treated the attacks as part of Trump's broader "witch hunt" messaging rather than a conventional defendant's complaint about a presiding judge.[8]

## The $454 million bond problem (March 2024)

In March 2024, [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s attorneys reported that he was unable to post a $454 million bond to cover the judgment Engoron issued in the New York civil fraud case in February.[9] Law professor [[jonathan-turley|Jonathan Turley]] cited the figure as evidence that the legal system had been distorted by politics, saying that forcing a litigant "to come up with half a billion dollars just to get an appeal" was a point at which "someone has to say enough."[9]
