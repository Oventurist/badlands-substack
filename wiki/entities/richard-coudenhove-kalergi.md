# Richard Coudenhove-Kalergi

Count Coudenhove-Kalergi was an early twentieth-century pan-European theorist whose writings are cited in Badlands Media commentary as the intellectual origin of what the author calls the [[kalergi-plan|Kalergi Plan]].[1] The Badlands article "On Infiltration and Israel" describes him bluntly as "a genocidal maniac."[1]

## Patronage and writings

According to the article, Kalergi's works were championed by the banker [[max-warburg|Max Warburg]], placing him within the network of financiers the author terms "ziobanksters."[1] Kalergi is said to have formulated his program during the 1910s and 1920s — a full century, the author stresses, before the demographic changes in Europe that the article characterizes as an Islamic invasion.[1]

## The plan attributed to him

Badlands summarizes Kalergi's vision as flooding Europe with non-Europeans in order to produce a single "euro-asiatic, negroid race," a phrase the author explicitly attributes to Kalergi himself rather than to the article.[1] The writer argues that the zeal with which Western governments have facilitated mass migration leaves "no doubt" that many Western leaders subscribe, directly or indirectly, to Kalergi's vision.[1]

## Role in the infiltration thesis

Kalergi functions in the article as the hinge between financial history and contemporary demographic politics. The author contends that as Europeans begin to resist the changes described, they will discover that nominally Jewish groups, leaders, and financiers have agitated for open borders for decades, "going all the way back to the time of Kalergi" — a discovery the writer presents as a driver of rising anti-Jewish sentiment and, ultimately, as evidence that the financier class endangers ordinary Jews.[1]
