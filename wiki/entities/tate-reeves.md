# Tate Reeves

**Tate Reeves** is the Republican Governor of [[mississippi|Mississippi]].[1]

## Income tax repeal

In 2025 Reeves signed a controversial bill eliminating Mississippi's personal income tax, positioning the state as the first in the country to phase out an existing income tax entirely.[1] He hailed the measure as a "generational change" that would attract businesses and workers to Mississippi.[1]

The law he signed reduces the 4% income tax rate to 3% by 2031, with further reductions accelerated after a typographical error nullified the revenue-growth safeguards originally written into the bill.[1] Critics warned that the resulting revenue loss — an estimated $2.6 billion from a roughly $7 billion budget — could devastate public services in the nation's poorest state.[1] See [[mississippi-income-tax-elimination|Mississippi income tax elimination]].[1]

## Refusal of COVID-19 mask mandates (2023)

Amid renewed national chatter in late August 2023 about the possible return of COVID-19 mask mandates, Reeves declared that such mandates would not return to Mississippi under any circumstances.[2] "Mississippians will not and should not submit to fear again," he said in a statement.[2] He argued that while there had been understandable uncertainty in the early days of COVID, it later became clear that there were "two pandemics": a disease that spread easily and was deadly for many vulnerable people, and "a pandemic of fear stoked by 'the expert class' that demanded total subjugation."[2]

Reeves said the "simple answer" to mandates was "no," adding, "We will not return to widespread masking or COVID rules."[2] The statement was issued in response to a CBS News report asking whether mask mandates were coming back, at a time when the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]] recorded a small uptick in US COVID-19 cases that remained considerably smaller than previous waves since 2020.[2]

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] praised the move as "an elected official doing something that actually represents the will of his constituents," using the episode to argue that the United States is a constitutional republic rather than a pure democracy, in which representatives are expected to weigh the popular will against what is objectively true.[2]
