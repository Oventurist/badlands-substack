# D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals

The United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit is the federal appellate court that, on July 11, 2025, ruled that the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] could withdraw from plea agreements that would have ruled out death sentences for three Guantanamo Bay prisoners accused of plotting the September 11, 2001 attacks.[1]

## 9/11 plea agreement ruling

A panel of the court concluded that the plea agreements — signed in July 2024 by Brigadier General [[susan-escallier|Susan Escallier]], the convening authority overseeing military commission proceedings against [[khalid-sheikh-mohammed|Khalid Sheikh Mohammed]], Walid bin Attash and Mustafa al-Hawsawi — were not binding on the government.[1] Judges Patricia Millett, an Obama appointee, and Neomi Rao, a Trump appointee, wrote in a joint majority opinion that "the Secretary of Defense indisputably had legal authority to withdraw from the agreements," referring to then–Defense Secretary [[lloyd-austin|Lloyd Austin]]'s decision to void the deals and reinstate death-penalty prosecutions.[1]

## December 2023 rulings on January 6 and Trump immunity

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit figured in two of the December 2023 lawfare storylines. In a split decision it rejected Judge Carl J. Nichols's narrow reading of the 2002 obstruction statute — under which "otherwise obstructs ... any official proceeding" would cover only document tampering — allowing the government to continue charging January 6 defendants under the provision; the Supreme Court agreed on December 13 to review that holding in Joseph Fischer's appeal.[2] The same evening, the D.C. Circuit issued an unsigned order fast-tracking its review of Judge Tanya Chutkan's rejection of Donald Trump's presidential immunity claim, setting a briefing schedule for Trump and special counsel Jack Smith with arguments to be heard by Judges Karen Henderson, Michelle Childs and Florence Pan.[2]

## Trump gag order rehearing petitions

On January 23, 2024, the D.C. Circuit rejected [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s requests that it reconsider its decision mostly upholding the partial gag order imposed in the Washington, D.C. election-subversion case brought by special counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]].[3] In a pair of one-page orders, the court denied Trump's petitions for either a rehearing before the same original three-judge panel or an en banc review before the full slate of judges sitting on the court.[3] Petitions for en banc consideration are frequently the final step before federal appellants seek review by the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[3]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that such rulings were a net win for Trump's standing in the [[collective-mind|Collective Mind]], since his central 2023–24 campaign theme was the weaponization of the justice system against him, and a panel composed of two Obama appointees and one Biden appointee cementing a gag order while he campaigned only reinforced that message.[3]
