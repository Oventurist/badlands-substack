# Paul Abbate

Paul Abbate is a former senior official of the [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] who served as the bureau's acting director in the interval between the resignation of Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] and the inauguration of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] for a second term.[1]

## Resignation

Abbate stepped down from the acting directorship hours before Trump was sworn in on January 20, 2025, ending a short caretaker period that had begun when Wray — Trump's own first-term nominee — announced in December 2024 that he would leave the bureau.[1] With Abbate's departure the White House named Brian Driscoll acting director, a role expected to last only until Kashyap "Kash" Patel was confirmed by the [[us-senate|U.S. Senate]].[1]

## Significance

Abbate's exit was reported as part of the broader day-one turnover at the bureau and the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]], in which key senior roles were reassigned and as many as twenty additional staffers were moved alongside the head of the Justice Department's office of international affairs, Bruce Swartz.[1] Coverage of the shakeup emphasized that the FBI had been entangled in repeated scandals throughout [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s term, giving Trump a mandate he claimed for rooting out corruption at the bureau.[1]

## Line of succession after Wray (December 2024)

Reporting in December 2024 indicated that following Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]]'s anticipated resignation, Deputy Director Abbate would become acting director of the [[fbi|FBI]] and would appoint an acting deputy director.[2] Abbate's original plan had been to remain in post until May or June so that he could help manage the transition to a new FBI director.[2]

## Senate testimony on the Butler assassination attempt

As FBI Deputy Director, Abbate testified before a Senate panel on Tuesday, July 30, 2024 alongside Acting Secret Service Director [[ronald-rowe|Ronald Rowe]] about the attempted assassination of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] at his rally in Butler, Pennsylvania earlier that month.[3] The two officials were questioned on the security arrangements at the site and on the status of the investigations into the events of July 13 and the days preceding the shooting.[3] Officials confirmed at the hearing — after ambiguous comments by the FBI director the previous week — that Trump had in fact been struck by a bullet.[3]

Badlands commentary highlighted Abbate's testimony that the Bureau still did not know how the gunman had carried a long gun onto the roof of a secured area under Secret Service protection, treating the answer as emblematic of institutional failure at the top domestic investigative authority.[3] Senator [[ron-johnson|Ron Johnson]] used the hearing to demand the FBI's 302 interview reports from the investigation, while Senator [[chuck-grassley|Chuck Grassley]] pressed on communication failures.[3] The Badlands assessment was that after four congressional hearings a large gap of uncertainty remained, with the officials falling back on plausible deniability and "lessons learned."[3]
