# Kim Gardner

Kim Gardner is a former St. Louis Circuit Attorney whose office became a focal point of national criticism over progressive prosecution practices. She was widely described in coverage as a "Soros-backed" prosecutor, a reference to political spending by [[george-soros|George Soros]] in local district attorney races.[1]

## Judicial sanction

In early April 2023, a St. Louis judge sanctioned Gardner's office for allegedly withholding evidence in a double-murder case while the suspect was allowed out on bond.[1] The reporting came amid rising criticism of left-wing prosecutors accused of allowing crime to flourish in major U.S. cities.[1]

The defendant, Alex Heflin, 23, had been held without bond since January after initially being charged with two counts of second-degree murder and armed criminal action. Those charges were later reduced to involuntary and voluntary manslaughter before his release, and his April 17 trial was postponed to June 12.[1] Judge [[theresa-counts-burke|Theresa Counts Burke]] ruled in favor of Heflin's lawyers after they filed a motion accusing a prosecutor under Gardner of violating discovery rules, alleging that the office had failed to turn over evidence including a 911 call recording and DNA evidence.[1]

Gardner had previously appeared at a disciplinary hearing in St. Louis in April 2022.[1]

## Commentary

Writing for [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] used the sanction to argue that "the weaponization of the legal system is nothing new" and may be "one of the oldest tactics for societal degradation," contending that legal illiteracy among the public prevents recognition of fraud and corruption.[1] He urged the formation of think tanks, charities and lobbying groups to expose such conduct, and warned that citizens cannot rely on "the occasional good judge or DA" to correct the system.[1]

## Resignation (May 2023)

Gardner resigned as St. Louis Circuit Attorney on May 4, 2023, hours after a judge denied her motion to dismiss the effort to remove her from office.[2] The removal process had begun on February 23, 2023, when the Missouri Attorney General's office filed a quo warranto action stemming from what was described as serious allegations of malfeasance on the part of her office, following a request that she resign immediately.[2]

In late April 2023, St. Louis Circuit Judge [[michael-noble|Michael Noble]] described Gardner's office as "a rudderless ship of chaos" and held that there was sufficient evidence to find that Gardner and Assistant Circuit Attorney Chris Desilets had acted with "intentional disregard for the judicial process," announcing that he would appoint a special prosecutor to pursue indirect criminal contempt against them both.[2] Commentary in the [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] news brief characterized the resignation as the end of the road for the [[george-soros|Soros]]-funded prosecutor once her attempt to quash the case failed, and framed it as evidence that voters across the political spectrum had tired of violent, crime-ridden streets without prosecution — and as part of a wider exposure of Soros-funded district attorneys and judges.[2]
