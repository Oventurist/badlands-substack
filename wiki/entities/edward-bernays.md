# Edward Bernays

Edward Bernays was an American pioneer of public relations and propaganda theory, frequently cited in Badlands commentary as an articulator of elite management of mass opinion.[1]

Bernays famously wrote that "the engineering of consent is the very essence of the democratic process, the freedom to persuade and suggest."[1] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] invoked the line while discussing [[klaus-schwab|Klaus Schwab]]'s departure from day-to-day management of the [[world-economic-forum|World Economic Forum]], presenting Bernays as an early expositor of the belief system she attributed to the global elite — a worldview she compared to the *Hunger Games* prequel's President Snow and his claim that "the whole world is an arena."[1]

## Freudian roots of public relations

Badlands Media commentary identifies Edward Bernays as the American nephew of [[sigmund-freud|Sigmund Freud]], who graduated from Cornell University in 1912 and chose journalism as a career.[2] He began applying his uncle's techniques and understanding of the unconscious mind, becoming the father of public relations and arguably of modern advertising.[2] In his 1928 book *Propaganda*, Bernays wrote that propaganda is not merely beneficial but necessary in governing a nation.[2] The article places him at the center of its claim that the 1910s were the decade Americans began to be altered, affected and controlled on an unconscious level — brainwashed — alongside the arrival of [[psychoanalysis]] in America through [[james-jackson-putnam|James Jackson Putnam]] and the [[american-psychoanalytic-association|American Psychoanalytic Association]].[2]
