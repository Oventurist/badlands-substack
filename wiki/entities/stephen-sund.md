# Stephen Sund

Stephen Sund was the Chief of the United States Capitol Police at the time of the January 6, 2021 breach of the Capitol, and became a central figure in disputes over what security warnings were and were not passed to Capitol leadership beforehand.[1]

## Claims regarding pre-January 6 security briefings

Independent commentators writing at Badlands Media have argued that Sund was left out of security briefings in the weeks before January 6 and was denied critical intelligence by his second in command, who they say was subsequently rewarded with a high-paying position in [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]]'s district.[1] This claim is cited in support of the broader contention that the [[january-6-committee|January 6 Committee]] functioned as a cover-up of the events of that day rather than an investigation of them, including by treating Pelosi as off limits despite her role in Capitol security arrangements.[1]

The same argument was advanced in commentary on the sentencing of [[peter-navarro|Peter Navarro]], where Badlands writers contended that Navarro was under no obligation to assist a committee they characterized as engaged in subverting the Constitution.[1]

## Rebuttal of Pelosi over January 6 National Guard requests (2025)

In August 2025 Sund, described as the former chief of the [[united-states-capitol-police|United States Capitol Police]], accused former House Speaker [[nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] of rewriting history after she criticized President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s decision to activate the D.C. [[national-guard|National Guard]].[2] Pelosi had written on X that Trump "delayed deploying the National Guard on January 6th when our Capitol was under violent attack and lives were at stake," calling his new Guard order a distraction from his handling of tariffs, healthcare, education, and immigration.[2] Sund responded with a lengthy rebuttal accusing Pelosi of blocking his multiple requests for Guard assistance before and during the attack, writing, "Ma'am, it is long past time to be honest with the American people."[2]
