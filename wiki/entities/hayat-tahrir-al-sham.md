# Hayat Tahrir al-Sham

Hayat Tahrir al-Sham (HTS) is the Syrian militant coalition that led the offensive which collapsed the government of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] in December 2024.[1] Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] described the group's rise as "significant and apparently unexpected," noting that HTS and its allied factions drove the campaign from its opening to the fall of Damascus.[1]

## The December 2024 offensive

HTS and allied factions launched their offensive on November 27, 2024, quickly capturing Aleppo, Syria's second-largest city, before turning south toward Damascus.[1] By December 8, 2024, the rebels had taken Hama, Homs, and finally Damascus, leading to Assad's reported resignation and departure for Moscow.[1] The article attributes the speed of the advance to the withdrawal of Syrian government forces, possibly because allies such as Russia and Iran were preoccupied with other conflicts.[1]

## Al-Qaeda lineage and rebranding

HTS's links to [[al-qaeda|al-Qaeda]] are central to the article's skepticism of Western coverage, which it says raises obvious questions about the future governance and stability of Syria.[1] DeLarme characterizes HTS as "a cleverly rebranded Al-Qaeda with 'woke' trappings" designed to be palatable to Western audiences — the same audiences, he argues, that previously cheered for the Ghost of Kyiv and the Azov Battalion.[1] Its leader, [[abu-mohammad-al-jolani|Abu Mohammad al-Jolani]], was recast in Western media as a "blazer-wearing revolutionary" rather than a radical jihadist.[1]

## Foreign backing

The article contends that the rebels who overthrew a well-armed government in a matter of days were "clearly backed by foreign influences with money, arms and motives" far beyond bringing freedom to the Syrian people.[1] It points to the documented history of the [[cia|CIA]] and the [[pentagon|Pentagon]] funding and bolstering Syrian proxy groups, including the CIA's [[timber-sycamore|Timber Sycamore]] program and Pentagon support for the [[syrian-democratic-forces|Syrian Democratic Forces]].[1] Turkey denied supporting HTS while aiding some rebel factions.[1]

## Aftermath

The power transition raised questions about its impact on Kurds, Turkish-backed groups, and other opposition forces, while [[russia|Russia]] called for UN Security Council consultations over the fallout, particularly regarding the buffer zone near the Golan Heights.[1] The conflict displaced millions and remained a humanitarian crisis, with the UN reporting a significant need for assistance in Syria.[1]

## Post-Assad governance and Russia

Following its capture of Damascus and the resignation of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]], HTS became the governing authority in Syria under de facto leader [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmed Hussein al-Sharaa]], known by the nom de guerre [[abu-mohammad-al-jolani|Abu Mohammad al-Julani]].[2] In a late-December 2024 interview with Al Arabiya, al-Sharaa said the new authorities would like to remain on good terms with Russia and would not necessarily seek the withdrawal of Moscow's military installations at [[khmeimim-air-base|Khmeimim]] and [[tartus-naval-base|Tartus]], stressing that Damascus wanted to avoid conflicts with foreign powers.[2] Russian Foreign Minister [[sergey-lavrov|Sergey Lavrov]] noted that a transition period had been declared until March 1, 2025 under the interim Syrian government, imposing limitations on its policy-making.[2]

The group's rapprochement with Moscow coincided with a sharp turn against it by Western and Israeli officials. Israeli Foreign Minister [[gideon-saar|Gideon Sa'ar]] began calling the outfit a "gang" and its government illegitimate, while the [[state-department|State Department]] formally disclosed memos unmasking the long terror ties of Syria's new governing authority — confirming, in Badlands' framing, that the jihadis were former [[al-qaeda|Al-Qaeda]] and ISIS henchmen.[2]

## Erdoğan's response to the Idlib offensive (December 2024)

At the outset of the offensive, HTS and allied groups pushed out of Idlib province toward Aleppo, Hama and Homs, prompting Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdoğan]] to state that Ankara remained committed to Syria's territorial integrity and wanted the conflict resolved according to the "legitimate wishes" of the Syrian people.[3] Turkish Foreign Minister [[hakan-fidan|Hakan Fidan]] appeared to blame the government in Damascus for the jihadist onslaught.[3] Badlands commentary characterized the militants as another iteration of [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]] run through Turkey, and connected them to the weapons pipeline that ran from Benghazi through Turkey into Syria beginning in 2012.[3]

## December 2024 Syria offensive

In late November 2024, Hayat Tahrir al-Sham and allied Islamist militant groups launched a large-scale offensive out of Idlib province toward Aleppo, Hama and Homs, resuming full-scale fighting in Syria.[3] Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] responded on December 2 by affirming Ankara's commitment to Syria's territorial integrity and calling for resolution in line with the "legitimate wishes" of the Syrian people, while Foreign Minister [[hakan-fidan|Hakan Fidan]] appeared to blame the government in Damascus for the offensive.[3]

Writing in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]], [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that these "rebels" were another iteration of [[operation-gladio|Operation Gladio]] run through Turkey, that they were fighting Russian forces despite Erdogan's simultaneous courtship of [[brics|BRICS]] and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization, and that the Syrian theater constituted an exposure operation likely to implicate both the Israeli government and the Republican establishment in Washington through their support for [[isis|ISIS]].[3]


## Territorial control and alleged ISIS embedding (January 2025)

By the start of January 2025, following the overthrow of President Bashar al-Assad on December 8, 2024, the hardline Islamist HTS held Damascus and major cities across southern, central and coastal [[syria]], while [[turkey|Turkey]] and its Syrian National Army proxies held parts of the north, the United States occupied the northeast through the [[syrian-democratic-forces|Syrian Democratic Forces]], and the Israeli military held an expanse extending out of the Golan Heights.[4] [[france|France]] launched its first airstrikes on [[isis]] targets in Syria since the HTS takeover in late December 2024.[4]

Badlands commentary noted that ISIS had been relatively quiet since the takeover by [[abu-mohammad-al-jolani|al-Jolani]] and asked where the large ISIS bombings of prior years had gone, arguing that ISIS fighters are in fact embedded with HTS and its foreign-fighter affiliates — a claim it said American television footage had inadvertently demonstrated.[4] Contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry speculated that the Deep State would "burn al-Jolani" and use his ISIS connections as an excuse to allow the IDF to move north and take Damascus, particularly if Turkish forces moved south from Aleppo first.[4] He noted that Turkey is a member of [[nato]], and argued that under Article 5 an Israeli attack on Turkey would obligate the United States to respond — with grey area introduced by the use of proxy forces.[4]

## Aleppo offensive (November 2024)

In late November 2024 HTS led an assault that captured the central and northwestern parts of Aleppo along with the city's international airport, then pressed toward Hama, claiming six towns and villages in the countryside including Morek on the highway linking central Syria to the north — claims the Syrian government disputed.[5] The offensive broke a fragile truce brokered by [[russia|Russia]] and [[turkey|Türkiye]] in 2020 and drew Russian and Syrian airstrikes on occupied districts, the first major bombardment of Aleppo since 2016.[5] Iranian Foreign Minister [[abbas-araghchi|Abbas Araghchi]] called the operation "an American-Zionist plot," arguing Washington and Israel were using HTS as a proxy against Damascus after Israel's ceasefire with [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]].[5] See [[2024-hts-aleppo-offensive|2024 HTS Aleppo offensive]].[5]

## Ukrainian drone support

In December 2024 the Washington Post confirmed earlier reporting from independent and Russian outlets that Ukrainian intelligence had provided HTS with direct drone support in the weeks preceding the offensive that toppled Syrian President [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]].[6] Columnist David Ignatius wrote that "Ukrainian intelligence sent about 20 experienced drone operators and about 150 first-person-view drones to the rebel headquarters in Idlib, Syria, four to five weeks ago."[6] Ukrainian officials had previously boasted openly that they would help strike Russian assets and bases in Syria in order to bog down Russian forces and distract Moscow's command from the Ukrainian front.[6] Analysts described small drone warfare as a key factor further demoralizing Syrian Army positions after years of grinding war and Western sanctions had hollowed out Assad's military and state institutions.[6]

Western intelligence sources cited in the report believed the aid from Kyiv played only a modest role in the overthrow itself, but described it as notable within a broader Ukrainian effort to strike covertly at Russian operations in the Middle East, Africa and inside Russia.[6] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the confirmation as evidence that NATO and the Deep State are backing Al Qaeda and ISIS terrorists and that Ukraine under Zelensky constitutes a terrorist state, and asserted that the "broader Ukrainian effort" referenced the financing of ISIS in Africa — placing figures such as [[ibrahim-traore|Ibrahim Traoré]] in Burkina Faso and [[kim-jong-un|Kim Jong Un]] among the targets of the same campaign.[6]

He also noted that villages in southwest Syria, where Israeli Defense Minister [[israel-katz|Israel Katz]] was said to be establishing a "sterile zone," were opting to submit to Israel rather than become vassals of the emerging ISIS-linked state — an outcome he judged favorable, for the moment, to [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]].[6]

## Revocation of the terrorist designation (July 2025)

On Monday, July 7, 2025, the United States announced that it had formally revoked the foreign terrorist organization designation for HTS, Syria's ruling group — coming nearly two months after President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] met with its leader, Syria's self-declared interim president [[ahmed-al-sharaa|Ahmad al-Sharaa]], formerly known as Abu Mohammad al-Jolani.[7] ZeroHedge described it as not merely ironic but scandalous that Trump had met a US-designated terrorist during his Gulf tour while in Saudi Arabia, noting that any individual American citizen doing the same would likely be investigated and prosecuted by the FBI, and that al-Sharaa's status as the "former" al-Qaeda in Syria man who helped overthrow [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] was apparently all Washington cared about.[7]

The newly published [[state-department|State Department]] memo, signed by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], read: "In consultation with the Attorney General and the Secretary of the Treasury, I hereby revoke the designation of al-Nusra Front, also known as Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham."[7]

Badlands commentary by [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] called the al-Jolani/Syria situation one of the bigger head-scratchers of 2025, describing the spectacle of the Trump administration and the GOP anointing a "head-chopping jihadi" as a great national leader as almost cartoonish.[7] The commentary revisited the long-standing suspicion that al-Jolani has always been a well-trained television actor now under new management, alongside the possibility that he was a legitimate operator deployed by the CIA or Mossad who has simply been offered a better deal by Trump and the Saudis — a tactic likened to Sun Tzu's advice on the enemies of one's enemy.[7] It argued the affair offers an opportunity to expose the GOP establishment and intelligence community, with the added benefit of bringing Syria back into the Arabian sphere with minimal bloodshed, and pointed to [[tucker-carlson|Tucker Carlson]]'s interview with historian [[scott-horton|Scott Horton]] as encapsulating the bizarre nature of the narrative.[7]

## Russian accusations of Ukrainian support (December 2024)

On Tuesday, December 3, 2024, Moscow made official the long-rumoured allegation that Kyiv was backing HTS when Russian Ambassador to the United Nations [[vassily-nebenzia|Vassily Nebenzia]] accused Ukraine of stoking the armed insurgency seeking to seize Syrian territory and threaten [[bashar-al-assad|Assad]].[8] "Ukrainian military instructors from the GUR are present… training HTS fighters for combat operations," Nebenzia told the [[united-nations-security-council|UN]], adding that HTS had "not only not concealed the fact that they are supported by Ukraine, but they are also openly flaunting this."[8] Reports and images of Ukrainian equipment and drones in jihadist hands had persisted for days during the capture of Aleppo, and Nebenzia criticised UN officials for being "unable to summon the courage to call a spade a spade and to condemn these terrorist attacks" against Syria.[8] A December 1 report in the pro-Ukraine Kyiv Post suggested the activity was part of a Ukrainian intelligence effort to strike Russian targets beyond the immediate war theatre.[8]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry framed Syria as the site of "the final battle in the War of Sovereignty," arguing the campaign was [[nato]] operating out of Turkey to overthrow Assad, expel Russia from the Middle East and damage [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s power projection, and citing Colonel Towner-Watkins's claim on Breaking History that the ISIS-K attack at Moscow's Crocus City Hall was likewise a NATO operation staged from Turkey.[8] The commentary connected the story to [[mouaz-moustafa|Mouaz Moustafa]] and Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]]'s 2013 Syria trip as evidence of long-running Western sponsorship of jihadist groups.[8]


## Capture of Damascus and stated attitude toward Israel

Over the weekend of December 7-8, 2024, HTS jihadists advanced on [[damascus|Damascus]] alongside US-armed [[free-syrian-army|Free Syrian Army]] militants as the Syrian Army stood down, taking the capital and declaring [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]] deposed.[9] Badlands described the group as a long-known [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]] affiliate that had fought alongside the Islamic State against [[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and Assad across twelve years of the Syrian civil war.[9]

Shortly before the capital fell, [[times-of-israel|Times of Israel]] published video interviews aired on Israeli television in which HTS-aligned rebels credited Israeli strikes on Hezbollah with enabling their advance.[9] One fighter said that "nobody knows if Iran and the regime would have been weakened without the recent Israeli attacks in Syria, which have allowed us to return and free the lands and the country," while a rebel figure from the Idlib area thanked Jerusalem and said the opposition was "very satisfied" with Israel's actions against Hezbollah and other Iran-backed players.[9] The same source said, "They accuse us of cooperating with you because we were quite happy when you attacked Hezbollah, really happy, and we're glad that you won," and added: "We love Israel and we were never its enemies... [Israel] isn't hostile to those who are not hostile toward it. We don't hate you, we love you very much."[9] Badlands treated these statements, together with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] claiming credit for the regime's collapse and promising diplomacy with the victors, as confirming its thesis that the jihadist groups function as instruments of the same interests they nominally oppose.[9]

## Washington reaction and sectarian killings, December 2024

Commentary published in Newsweek in December 2024 noted that a 2018 entry in the Federal Register had declared HTS a "Specially Designated Global Terrorist" organization on account of its lineage with [[al-qaeda|Al Qaeda]], and predicted that a Washington parlor game would now be to guess how quickly that designation would be stricken from the record.[10] The piece compared the swift rehabilitation of the Syrian militants to the public-relations makeover afforded the [[azov-battalion|Azov Regiment]] in Ukraine, decried in that same year, 2018, by progressive Democrats as a "Neo-Nazi" formation before being rebranded as freedom fighters.[10] It allowed that both formations might to some extent have moderated, but observed that without more independent reporting from Syria the supposed evolution of the group was impossible to verify, whatever pluralistic overtures its leadership was publicly making.[10]

Reporting compiled by The Cradle described extremist armed factions across Syria carrying out executions of civilians and soldiers amid the chaos following the fall of [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]]'s government.[10] Al-Mayadeen reported that a video circulating on social media showed armed HTS militants conducting field executions of unarmed men in the village of al-Rabia in the Latakia countryside, referring to the men as "Shabiha," a derogatory term long applied to pro-government Syrian soldiers and civilians.[10] The HTS military operations administration reported ongoing clashes in al-Rabia, including the encirclement of a group of officers inside a fortified farm in the village.[10] In the Nawaha and Al-Asadiya neighborhoods, SNA militants burned civilian houses, stole property and executed at least three people including a woman, according to SOHR, while [[islamic-state|ISIS]] militants killed 54 Syrian army soldiers fleeing an attack in the central province of Homs.[10]

Badlands commentary argued that western and Israeli media had tripped over themselves to rebrand HTS leader [[abu-mohammad-al-jolani|Abu Mohammed al-Jolani]] as a "tolerant terrorist," and questioned how the celebration of a former Al Qaeda commander could be squared with continued 9/11 commemoration.[10]

## Fall of Damascus and its aftermath (December 2024)

Damascus fell in early December 2024 to a coalition of armed opposition forces led by HTS, an event that immediately preceded Israeli forces moving deeper into Syrian territory and seizing the post-1973 demilitarized buffer zone adjacent to the [[golan-heights|Golan Heights]].[11] Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] observed that even globalist media found it strange that the advancing forces met little to no resistance from [[bashar-al-assad|Assad]]'s Syrian army as they marched on Aleppo and Damascus, questioning whether Assad had truly fled on a whim or had already been in Moscow for some time.[11] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that "ConInc" and [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] were effectively celebrating Al Qaeda — the group purportedly responsible for 9/11 and for much of the subsequent destabilization of the Middle East.[11]
