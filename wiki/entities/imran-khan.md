# Imran Khan

**Imran Khan** is the former Prime Minister of [[pakistan|Pakistan]] whose removal from office Badlands Media contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] presents as "yet another notch in the belt of the regime change machine at the US [[state-department|State Department]]."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Background

Khan was a populist figure, a former cricket star and the founder of the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) party.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) He was at first perceived as having ties to the military establishment, including the [[inter-services-intelligence|Inter-Services Intelligence]] (ISI) — described in the article as Pakistan's CIA and as an organisation kept on a tight leash by the United States — particularly during his ascent to political prominence.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## Break with the security state

While Khan may initially have benefited from ISI support, his relationship with the agency became strained during his tenure.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Not unlike [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], DeLarme writes, Khan seemed to have his own plans about how to run the country — plans that ran against his country's rogue intelligence services and the foreign policy establishment.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) Relations deteriorated significantly after his ouster by a parliamentary no-confidence vote in April 2022, which Khan attributed to military and ISI interference; he openly accused the military, including then-ISI chief Nadeem Anjum and former army chief Gen. Qamar Javed Bajwa, of orchestrating his removal.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)

## The Russia factor

A primary reason a target was placed on Khan's back, according to the article, was his "aggressively neutral" stance on Russia and his threats to block Pakistani arms shipments to [[ukraine|Ukraine]].1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) He was pursuing an independent foreign policy aimed at maintaining friendly relations with both the United States and Russia without favouring either side, and "the establishment dislikes leaders making their own foreign policy decisions."1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away) DeLarme frames Khan's overthrow alongside a subsequent attempt to regime change [[narendra-modi|Modi]] in India as evidence that [[joe-biden|Biden]] was effectively partnered with China.1(https://badlands.substack.com/p/and-iran-iran-so-far-away)
