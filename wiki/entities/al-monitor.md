# Al-Monitor

**Al-Monitor** is an online news site that conveys and translates news from the Middle East, founded and owned by Syrian-born businessman [[jamal-daniel|Jamal Daniel]].[1]

## Editorial controversy

Citing TheTower.org and a Tablet Magazine expose, Badlands Media commentary reports that Al-Monitor faced questions over perceptions that it had been conveying the line of Bashar al-Assad's Syrian government.[1] Observers asserted that the arguments and positions of the Assad government received heavy coverage in the site's "Lebanon Pulse" section, with an emphasis on translated material from pro-[[hezbollah|Hezbollah]] and pro-Assad media outlets, as well as original content produced for Al-Monitor by writers who also worked for those outlets.[1]

The site's critics argued that until Al-Monitor was founded, pro-Hezbollah journalists could publish only in resistance media; at Al-Monitor their work appeared alongside reporting and analysis within the mainstream of public policy discourse.[1] Critics pointed specifically to August 2011, when Daniel bought a large share of the Beirut daily [[as-safir|As-Safir]].[1]

## Interpretation

The article treats Al-Monitor as an instrument by which Hezbollah-aligned voices were legitimized within Western policy discourse, and links its ownership through Daniel to the [[bush-family|Bush family]] and ultimately to [[alwaleed-bin-talal|Prince Alwaleed bin Talal]].[1]

## Assessment

The criticism reported here reflects the cited commentary of Al-Monitor's critics rather than an adjudicated finding. Badlands Media notes that contributor articles represent the opinions of their authors.[1]
