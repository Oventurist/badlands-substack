# International Criminal Court

The International Criminal Court (ICC) is the permanent international tribunal empowered to prosecute individuals for genocide, war crimes, crimes against humanity, and aggression. In the Badlands corpus it appears principally through its 2024 arrest warrant for Israeli prime minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and the political dilemmas that warrant creates for European states that are party to the court's framework.

## The Netanyahu warrant and Hungary

The April 21, 2026 [[badlands-brief-1ef|Badlands Brief]] reports that Hungary's incoming prime minister [[peter-magyar|Péter Magyar]] invited Netanyahu to Budapest — including for a ceremony marking the 70th anniversary of the 1956 Hungarian uprising — while simultaneously stating that [[hungary|Hungary]] intends to remain in or rejoin the ICC framework. Membership would legally oblige Hungary to act on the outstanding warrant, and Magyar explicitly acknowledged that any individual subject to an ICC warrant must be detained if Hungary is a member. Under [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]], Hungary had defied the court and moved to withdraw from it; Magyar's stance represents a reversal that realigns Budapest with ICC obligations while maintaining engagement with [[israel|Israel]].

The source frames this as one instance of a broader European dilemma: balancing bilateral diplomatic ties with Israel against legal commitments to international institutions. Badlands commentator [[ghost-of-based-patrick-henry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], referring to the court as "the UN's International Criminal Court," argues that the episode is ultimately less about the prosecution of Netanyahu personally than about the progressive ostracism of Israel and its citizens from the international community.
