# International Criminal Court

The International Criminal Court (ICC) is the tribunal at the center of a July 2026 United States campaign, announced by Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]], aimed at dismantling the court on the grounds that it threatens U.S. sovereignty and improperly targets American military personnel and officials.[1] The effort explicitly includes encouraging other countries to distance themselves from the court.[1]

## U.S. campaign against the court

Rubio announced what Badlands described as a sweeping "whole-of-government" campaign against the ICC on July 13, 2026.[1] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] characterized the court as having been founded in 2000 by George Soros in order to prosecute world leaders such as [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] who refused to comply with the New World Order — a framing that reflects the outlet's editorial position rather than the court's official founding history.[1]

The [[state-department|State Department]] subsequently welcomed Venezuela's decision to withdraw from the court and called on other countries to follow suit, according to a statement reported by Anadolu Agency.[1] Badlands read the sequence as a demonstration of President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s ability to turn what it calls the Deep State against itself.[1]

## Netanyahu warrant

Debate over the ICC's 2024 arrest warrant for Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] resurfaced alongside the Rubio announcement.[1] Trump said Netanyahu would not be arrested while visiting the United States, and [[israel|Israel]] rejects the court's jurisdiction and denies the war-crimes allegations underlying the warrant.[1] New York City Mayor [[zohran-mamdani|Zohran Mamdani]] said his administration had reviewed what authority the city possessed before clarifying that New York City cannot itself execute an ICC warrant; Reuters reported that U.S. law provides no domestic mechanism requiring state or local officials to enforce ICC warrants because the United States is not a party to the [[rome-statute|Rome Statute]].[1]

## Venezuela investigations

The ICC has been examining the [[venezuela|Venezuelan]] government since [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] took office in 2014, opening formal investigations after receiving referrals from what Badlands characterized as NeoCon-aligned governments in 2018.[1] Additional referrals arrived as recently as 2025, including from Ecuador — the country Colombian President [[gustavo-petro|Gustavo Petro]] has identified as the new home base of the cocaine cartels.[1] The court denied all Venezuelan requests to end the inquiries, which Badlands interpreted as evidence of an intention to indict Maduro and interim president [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]] and to bring Venezuela under UN control.[1]

## 2026 withdrawal wave

By mid-2026 the court faced an accelerating [[icc-withdrawal-wave|wave of withdrawals]].[2] [[chad|Chad]] announced in late July 2026 that it was leaving, accusing the Hague-based body of ineffectiveness and bias against African countries, and submitted a formal letter to the United Nations to begin a process that takes at least one year under the [[rome-statute|Rome Statute]].[2] Niger, Mali, and [[burkina-faso|Burkina Faso]] had all begun withdrawing the previous year, and [[venezuela|Venezuela]] announced its formal withdrawal days before Chad, citing "geographical bias" that disproportionately targets countries in Africa and Latin America.[2]

US Secretary of State [[marco-rubio|Marco Rubio]] said in July 2026 that the United States was launching a campaign to dismantle the ICC's threat to national sovereignty, and a February 2025 executive order signed by President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] had imposed sanctions on the court over its investigations of [[israel|Israel]].[2] The [[state-department|State Department]]'s Bureau of African Affairs welcomed Chad's move on X and called on more of the court's 125 member states to withdraw and "reclaim their sovereignty from this flawed institution."[2]

## Sovereign Alliance pressure (2026)

In May 2026 Badlands contributor [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] described the ICC as an "illegitimate" institution sitting squarely in the crosshairs of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]], alongside the [[nato|NATO]] bloc, as emblems of globalist ideology.[3] He wrote that the court's protectorate feared the moment acutely: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] had long been its favored target and [[xi-jinping|Xi]] had already moved to shield him from what was characterized as collectivist overreach, while [[donald-trump|Trump]] now appeared prepared to lend decisive weight to dismantling whatever remained of the court's legitimacy.[3] The commentary argued that once the court's selective "justice" is exposed as a political weapon, the wider architecture of globalist lawfare begins to crumble.[3]

## British threats over the Israel warrants

Middle East Eye reported that former British Prime Minister [[david-cameron|David Cameron]], then Foreign Secretary in Rishi Sunak's government, threatened to defund the ICC and withdraw the United Kingdom from the [[rome-statute|Rome Statute]] if Chief Prosecutor [[karim-khan|Karim Khan]] sought arrest warrants for Israeli leaders.[4] Cameron reportedly telephoned Khan after the prosecutor notified London of his intentions and warned that pursuing the warrants would be "like dropping a hydrogen bomb."[4] The court issued warrants that November for Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and then Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]], as well as three [[hamas|Hamas]] leaders, over alleged war crimes in the Gaza conflict, after which Washington sanctioned Khan for what it called abuse of power in going after US allies.[4] Badlands commentary framed the episode as part of a broader confrontation in which the "Zionist-controlled GOP" appeared prepared to defund the UN over the warrants.[4]

## May 2024 warrant applications over Gaza

In May 2024 the ICC Prosecutor applied to Pre-Trial Chamber I for warrants of arrest against Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] over alleged war crimes in Gaza, alongside [[hamas|Hamas]] figures [[yahya-sinwar|Yahya Sinwar]], [[mohammed-deif|Mohammed Deif]] and [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]].[5] [[european-union|EU]] foreign policy chief [[josep-borrell|Josep Borrell]] said all EU member countries would be legally obliged to execute the warrants, meaning Netanyahu or Gallant would face arrest if they travelled to a European state, since every European country except Ukraine and Turkey is a signatory to the [[rome-statute|Rome Statute]].[5]

Netanyahu responded by warning U.S. leaders that "you're next."[5] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] argued that the episode marked the breaking of a controlled-opposition dynamic, with globalist institutions turning on the very actors who built them and forcing those actors to campaign for the delegitimization of the court itself.[5]

## May 2024 arrest warrant applications and European responses

In May 2024, ICC prosecutor [[karim-khan|Karim Khan]] made a dramatic announcement seeking arrest warrants covering Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] alongside [[hamas|Hamas]] leaders.[6] [[norway|Norway]] became the first European state to declare unprompted that it would execute any such warrants, while Britain criticized the decision, objecting in particular to the equivalence drawn between the Israeli leaders and [[yahya-sinwar|Yahya Sinwar]], Muhammad Deif and [[ismail-haniyeh|Ismail Haniyeh]].[6] France, which had once helped Israel secretly develop a nuclear weapons program, also came forward in support of the warrants.[6]

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] urged readers hung up on the claim that the ICC was created by [[george-soros|George Soros]] — and therefore illegitimate — to look past that framing, arguing the more important development was that institutions built by the globalist hegemony were being turned against it.[6]

## April 2024: Netanyahu's appeal to Biden

Weeks before the warrant applications became public, Israeli officials had grown increasingly concerned that the ICC was preparing to issue arrest warrants for [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]], Defense Minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] and [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] chief of staff [[herzi-halevi|Herzi Halevi]].[7] According to two Israeli officials cited by Axios, Netanyahu asked President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] in a phone call on Sunday, April 28, 2024 to help prevent the court from issuing the warrants; the same call covered hostage negotiations, Israel's defense against Iran's missile attack, and the need to increase humanitarian aid to Gaza.[7] U.S. officials said they had no clear indication whether the ICC would act, but noted the prosecutor's office was under pressure from NGOs and several ICC member states to do so.[7] The court, based in The Hague, had been investigating possible war crimes by both Israeli forces and Palestinian militants dating back to the 2014 Israel–Hamas war.[7] Netanyahu declared in a statement on X that "under my leadership, Israel will never accept any attempt by the ICC to undermine its inherent right of self-defense."[7]

Badlands commentary at the time drew a sharp distinction between the ICC and the [[international-court-of-justice|International Court of Justice]], both seated at The Hague: the ICJ was formed when the United Nations was created after the Second World War as a forum in which bickering nations could resolve differences peacefully and claims no power to enforce measures against offending states, whereas the ICC — described as created some two decades earlier by [[george-soros|George Soros]] — was portrayed as a weapon against leaders opposing the Unipolar World Order, such as [[vladimir-putin|Putin]], and as a vehicle for expanding UN power toward global government.[7] The commentator nonetheless cautioned against dismissing the warrant threat as merely a Soros-driven political prosecution, observing that enormous international pressure had been applied to the ICC and other UN organizations in an effort to bring Israel to heel, and noting that Putin had appeared entirely unfazed by the ICC warrant issued against him.[7]

## Italian non-enforcement of the Putin warrant (2025)

In April 2025 Italian media reported that Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] would not be at risk of arrest in Italy under the ICC warrant, because Italian officials never completed the procedures required to make it valid.[8] The Hague-based court had issued warrants for Putin and Russian Commissioner for Children's Rights [[maria-lvova-belova|Maria Lvova-Belova]] in 2023 over the alleged unlawful deportation and transfer of children from former Ukrainian territories, and a year later issued additional warrants for senior Russian commanders including former Defense Minister [[sergei-shoigu|Sergey Shoigu]] and General [[valery-gerasimov|Valery Gerasimov]] over attacks on civilian infrastructure.[8] Moscow does not recognize the court's jurisdiction, denies the accusations, and has declared the warrants null and void.[8] Badlands commentary noted that Italy under [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] had also declined in January to enforce ICC warrants against [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]], and read a major European state treating the court as illegitimate as evidence of waning UN clout.[8]

## Arrest warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant (November 2024)

On Thursday, November 21, 2024, the ICC issued arrest warrants for Israeli Prime Minister [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] and former defense minister [[yoav-gallant|Yoav Gallant]] in a ruling that shook military, diplomatic and legal circles.[9] The court cited reasonable grounds to believe the two had committed the war crime of starvation as a method of warfare, along with the crimes against humanity of murder, persecution and other inhumane acts.[9]

Israel and the United States condemned the ruling — the first time the court had issued arrest warrants for officials of a democratic state — as biased or beyond the ICC's mandate, while the European Union, France, Ireland and several other countries said they would honor the warrants.[9] As a practical matter the warrants meant Netanyahu and Gallant would likely be unable to travel to most European countries and many of the ICC's 120-plus member states, though they could still travel to the United States, which is not a member, as well as to various Middle Eastern states; some states have simply ignored ICC warrants, as there is no effective enforcement mechanism.[9] Badlands commentary noted that [[benny-gantz|Benny Gantz]] was not among those indicted and predicted that [[aipac|AIPAC]]-aligned members of Congress would rush to denounce the United Nations over the ruling.[9]
