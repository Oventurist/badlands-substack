# Charles McGonigal

Charles McGonigal is a former [[fbi|Federal Bureau of Investigation]] official who, at age 54, was accused in a five-count indictment of working for the sanctioned Russian aluminum magnate [[oleg-deripaska|Oleg Deripaska]].[1] McGonigal previously served as the special agent in charge of the FBI's New York counterintelligence division, the office responsible for some of the bureau's most sensitive Russia-related work.[1]

## Charges

Federal prosecutors alleged that McGonigal and [[sergey-shestakov|Sergey Shestakov]], a former Russian diplomat turned U.S. citizen who worked as an interpreter for federal courts and prosecutors, worked on Deripaska's behalf in 2021 to investigate an unnamed rival Russian oligarch, in violation of U.S. sanctions that had applied to Deripaska since 2018, and then acted to conceal that activity.[1] The reported charges included violations of Russia sanctions and money laundering.[1]

Two separate indictments were filed: one out of the Southern District of New York concerning the Deripaska work, and one out of Washington, D.C., charging the retired FBI executive with concealing $225,000 in cash received from a former foreign intelligence officer.[1]

## Commentary and analysis

Badlands Media contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] argued that the case does not appear to be directly related to [[russiagate-hoax|RussiaGate]], but that it nonetheless involves two figures who feature in that story — McGonigal himself and Deripaska, described as a key RussiaGate character.[1] Just Human noted that these were not [[john-durham|John Durham]] indictments, but suggested it was plausible that investigative work by Durham and possibly [[michael-horowitz|Michael Horowitz]] contributed to them, raising the prospect of a "cross pollination of evidence" between the already-filed case against Deripaska and matters still on Durham's desk.[1]

## Guilty plea and sentencing (2023)

McGonigal, described as a 22-year veteran of the FBI and the former head of counterintelligence for the bureau's New York field office, pleaded guilty in August 2023 to one count of conspiracy to violate U.S. sanctions and money laundering for working for Deripaska after leaving government.[2] On December 14, 2023, Judge [[jennifer-rearden|Jennifer Rearden]] sentenced him to 50 months in prison — just over four years, below the five-year statutory maximum sought by prosecutors — along with a $40,000 fine, and ordered him to surrender to prison on February 26.[2]

Rearden said McGonigal "repeatedly flouted and manipulated the sanctions regimes vital" to U.S. national security, and that "the undeniable seriousness of this and the need to respect the law … compels a meaningful custodial sentence," while adding that his conduct did "not all together stamp out" his distinguished career and the "profoundly important contributions" he had made to the country.[2] Before sentencing, McGonigal told the court he felt a "deep sense of remorse and sorrow," saying that as a former FBI special agent his felony caused him "extreme mental, emotional and physical pain – not to mention the shame I feel in embarrassing myself and the FBI, the organization I love and respect," and asking humbly for a second chance.[2] Prosecutors had argued for five years, contending that a foreign government choosing between military supplies and having the former FBI counterintelligence chief "on their payroll" faced an easy decision.[2] U.S. Attorney [[damian-williams|Damien Williams]] stated that "it is not an overstatement to say that no one knew better the gravity of McGonigal's crimes than McGonigal himself."[2]

While heading the New York counterintelligence division, McGonigal had been responsible for supervising and participating in investigations of Russian oligarchs, including Deripaska — the same individual he later worked for.[2]

## Badlands commentary on the sentence

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] argued that the sentence was a glaring example of asymmetrical justice: the four corruption charges carried a theoretical combined maximum of 80 years, and the court's reasoning effectively treated all of McGonigal's other investigative work as above reproach.[2] She wrote that the same high-ranking FBI agent who investigated Trump–Russia collusion at its inception had pleaded guilty to corruption involving collusion with Russians, describing him as "a poison tree" whose fruit should all be tossed out.[2] In her view, every U.S. defendant prosecuted or convicted on the basis of McGonigal's work — "beginning with [[carter-page|Carter Page]] and President [[donald-trump|Trump]]" — should pursue civil claims against him and the relevant agencies and immediately appeal any criminal convictions.[2] She framed the episode as part of what she characterized as an illegal RICO-style operation by government against the America First movement, and called for an end to the government hiding behind "matters of national security" while committing what she termed deprivation of rights under color of law.[2]
