# Huawei

Huawei is a Chinese technology company identified as a priority supplier under Beijing's [[china-ai-data-center-buildout|AI data center buildout]] plan.[1] The roughly 2 trillion yuan ($295 billion) five-year program prioritizes Chinese suppliers — Huawei among them — for at least 80 percent of core technology, notably AI chips, explicitly aiming to displace foreign providers such as [[nvidia|Nvidia]] and [[amd|AMD]].[1]

Huawei's role in the plan reflects Beijing's broader push for technological self-reliance, positioning domestic hardware as the substrate for [[china|China]]'s frontier AI ambitions and its [[six-networks|Six Networks]] infrastructure mobilization.[1]

## US export restrictions

Huawei has long been the focus of United States export controls, which national security officials justify on the grounds that the telecom equipment giant assists China in espionage.[2] The [[donald-trump|Trump administration]] imposed severe restrictions on exporting American technology to Huawei, though the [[department-of-commerce|Commerce Department]] continued granting licences to some companies for products unrelated to high-speed 5G telecom networks.[2] In late January 2023 the Financial Times reported that the [[joe-biden|Biden]] administration had stopped issuing such licences altogether, notifying some companies that no further export authorizations would be granted as Washington moved toward a total ban on the sale of American technology to the company.[2]

Badlands Media contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] observed that the "Beijing Biden" administration had not only continued the Trump administration's Huawei policies but was now heading toward a complete prohibition.[2]

## Mate 60 resurgence against Apple (2023)

Shenzhen-based Huawei abruptly released its Mate 60 and 60 Pro smartphones in the weeks leading up to the launch of [[apple|Apple]]'s iPhone 15, drawing buyers and attention with its made-in-China Kirin processor, an apparent breakthrough in the company's fight to overcome US trade sanctions.[3] The Mate 60 series recorded sales of close to 1.5 million units in its launch month — more than doubling from a year earlier despite supply constraints — according to market researcher GfK, whose China senior analyst Hayden Hou said the line would "continue to maintain its strong sales momentum going forward."[3]

Huawei's return was identified by both GfK and IDC as the key event of the period in which Apple's iPhone 15 series posted a 6 percent decline in China launch-month sales and Apple shipments fell 4 percent in the third quarter.[3] Badlands commentator [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] read the shift as a hint of the [[sovereign-alliance|Sovereign Alliance]] he believed had formed on the international game board, arguing that when America, Russia and China each adopt nation-first philosophies, the parties most affected are the globalist oligarchy and their slave labor-utilizing, market-dominating monoliths.[3]

## French assurances (April 2023)

During the [[2023-macron-state-visit-to-china|2023 Macron state visit to China]] in April 2023, French President [[emmanuel-macron|Emmanuel Macron]] promised Huawei fair treatment in France, a pledge cited by critics as part of a pattern of deepening French dependency on Beijing while rejecting European Commission calls for supply-chain "de-risking."[4]
