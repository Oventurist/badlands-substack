# The Hill

The Hill is a Washington, D.C.-based news outlet covering Congress, the executive branch and national security policy.[1]

## Red Sea coverage

In January 2024, The Hill reported that Houthi rebels had unsuccessfully targeted another U.S.-owned tanker in the Red Sea, firing two anti-ship ballistic missiles at the M/V Chem Ranger, a Marshall Islands-flagged and Greek-operated vessel, at 9 p.m. local time. According to [[centcom|U.S. Central Command]], the missiles fell into the water without causing damage or injury, and the tanker continued on its way.[1]

The outlet noted that the [[houthis|Houthis]] had launched more than thirty attacks on commercial shipping from Yemen since late November 2023, a mounting problem for the Biden administration that showed no signs of abating, and that the United States had conducted retaliatory strikes on five occasions since an initial round the previous week. The Hill also reported the [[pentagon|Pentagon]]'s assessment that U.S. strikes were damaging Houthi infrastructure and forcing the Iranian-backed group to scale down its attacks, even as the Houthis proved resilient.[1]

Badlands Media reproduced The Hill's reporting as part of its coverage of the [[red-sea-shipping-crisis|Red Sea shipping crisis]].[1]

## Coverage of Biden's departure from office

In December 2024, The Hill reported that President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] was readying to leave the [[white-house|White House]] "with little fanfare," describing Democrats as eager to turn the page on a year in which they lost again to President-elect [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]].[2] The outlet noted Biden's post-election foreign trips, the pardon of his son, efforts toward a Gaza ceasefire, increased assistance to [[ukraine|Ukraine]], and his commutation of the sentences of 37 people on federal death row shortly before Christmas.[2] It contrasted his low public profile with Trump's cabinet announcements, network television interview and first post-election press conference at Mar-a-Lago, and observed that Biden and the White House were largely silent during year-end government funding negotiations, a fight led instead by House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries.[2] The report quoted GOP strategist Doug Heye saying Biden had "been disappeared for a long time," and noted that Democrats themselves had done little to bolster him.[2]

Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] read the piece's tone as one of disappointment and "pouting," arguing that legacy media's portrayal of the Biden presidency had been "ineffective at best, criminal at worst" and ultimately a losing strategy.[2] She wrote that the chief takeaway of the preceding four years was that the media could not be trusted, that those charged with preserving public trust had betrayed a sacred duty, and that the period would likely be remembered as "The Dumb Ages."[2] Describing Biden as ending his presidency "with a whimper," she concluded that scrutiny of his tenure would lead more Americans to feel "it's almost like he was never president at all."[2]
