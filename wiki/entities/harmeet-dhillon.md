# Harmeet Dhillon

Harmeet Dhillon is an attorney prominent in conservative election-integrity litigation, described by Badlands Media contributor [[kitty-gillespie|Kitty Gillespie]] as one of the "savage lawyers fighting for election integrity in Arizona" and as "our beloved Harmeet Dhillon."[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

Dhillon was among the lawyers active in Arizona in the weeks after the [[2022-arizona-general-election|2022 general election]], a period Gillespie characterized by lawyers "swarming again" while candidates' paid staff and volunteers worked to document irregularities in [[maricopa-county|Maricopa County]].[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero) Republican gubernatorial candidate [[kari-lake|Kari Lake]] stated on November 21, 2022 that "attorneys are working diligently to gather information" and that whistleblowers were coming forward.[[1]](https://badlands.substack.com/p/an-sos-from-ground-zero)

## Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights

By January 2026 Dhillon was serving as Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights at the [[department-of-justice|U.S. Department of Justice]].[2] After dozens of left-wing activists stormed [[cities-church|Cities Church]] in St. Paul, Minnesota during a Sunday service, Dhillon stated that the incident could violate the [[ku-klux-klan-act|Ku Klux Klan Act]], which prohibits conspiracies to intimidate or violate civil rights, and the [[face-act|FACE Act]], which protects houses of worship from interference.[2] She emphasized zero tolerance, noting that disrupting religious services is not protected speech and that the Department's Civil Rights Division, together with the [[fbi|FBI]], was investigating.[2] The Department placed former CNN host [[don-lemon|Don Lemon]], alleged to have joined the mob, "on notice" for potential federal charges, while Attorney General [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] spoke directly with the affected pastor.[2]

## Federal election integrity effort (2026)

As head of the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s Civil Rights Division, Dhillon and her deputies took part in May 2026 [[white-house|White House]] interagency talks to expand federal involvement in voter verification and mail-in ballot rules, alongside USPS chief executive [[david-steiner|David Steiner]] and [[department-of-homeland-security|DHS]] election integrity official [[heather-honey|Heather Honey]].[3] The effort implements President [[donald-trump|Trump]]'s executive order [[ensuring-citizenship-verification-and-integrity-in-federal-elections|"Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections"]], which instructs the DOJ to prioritize prosecution of state and local officials who distribute ballots to ineligible voters.[3]

## Civil Rights Division and corporate DEI

Billionaire investor and Trump adviser [[marc-andreessen|Marc Andreessen]] pointed to Dhillon's appointment as head of the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]]'s Civil Rights Division as a catalyst for the end of corporate wokeness, telling Erik Torenberg on the Moment of Zen podcast that "every signal is being sent" that she would move against woke corporations.[4] Andreessen argued that with her in place the federal government might soon begin to challenge and reverse many of the DEI-driven policies that had dominated corporations, universities, and other large institutions over the past decade, triggering a major private-sector pullback as companies scrambled to comply with the law.[4] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] was skeptical, arguing that making corporate culture less "woke" would do little to stop the spread of global communism and would merely force it to rebrand.[4]

## Voter registration list litigation

As Assistant Attorney General for the [[justice-department|Justice Department]]'s Civil Rights Division, Dhillon oversaw the [[doj-voter-registration-list-lawsuits|18 federal lawsuits]] filed by December 2025 against states and localities that failed to produce their voter registration lists on request.[5] She said that "States have the statutory duty to preserve and protect their constituents from vote dilution," adding that the department would "not permit states to jeopardize the integrity and effectiveness of elections by refusing to abide by our federal elections laws. If states will not fulfill their duty to protect the integrity of the ballot, we will."[5]

## Fulton County election-records demand (November 2025)

As Assistant Attorney General for the [[department-of-justice|Department of Justice]]'s Civil Rights Division, Dhillon signed a formal letter in early November 2025 directing the Fulton County Board of Registration and Elections to produce all election records responsive to a subpoena from the [[georgia-state-election-board|Georgia State Election Board]].[6] The letter instructed Chairperson Sheri Allen and Elections Director Nadine Williams to make the records available "in its entirety and most original form" for inspection and reproduction, invoking the [[civil-rights-act-of-1960|Civil Rights Act of 1960]], which authorizes the Attorney General to review and copy preserved election materials.[6]

Dhillon wrote that the purpose of the request was "to ascertain Georgia's compliance with various provisions of the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act]] and the [[help-america-vote-act|Help America Vote Act]]," including standards governing election technology and administration.[6] She cited a July 30, 2025 resolution of the State Election Board inviting Justice Department assistance in ensuring transparency, and a November 7, 2024 board letter referencing "unexplained anomalies in vote tabulation and storage related to the 2020 election."[6] The letter further referenced August 1, 2025 correspondence from transparency advocates alleging "multiple instances of government obstruction" of records requests for ballot scans, signature verification data, and metadata, and gave [[fulton-county|Fulton County]] 15 days to comply.[6]

## Illinois mail-ballot case (July 2025)

As Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights, Dhillon announced in July 2025 that the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]] had entered an Illinois election case at the [[supreme-court-of-the-united-states|Supreme Court]].[7] She noted that federal law sets a single day for federal elections, yet Illinois allows ballots to be received after Election Day, and that when Congressman [[mike-bost|Michael Bost]] sued, federal courts held that he lacked standing.[7] "Today we told the SCOTUS that result was wrong," she wrote. "He should have been allowed to sue."[7]

## Orange County voter records lawsuit

As Assistant Attorney General for the Civil Rights Division, Dhillon announced a June 2025 Justice Department lawsuit against Robert Page, the Orange County, California Registrar of Voters, over his refusal to provide records on the removal of non-citizens from county voter rolls and alleged failure to maintain an accurate voter list under the Help America Vote Act.[8] "Voting by non-citizens is a federal crime, and states and counties that refuse to disclose all requested voter information are in violation of well-established federal elections laws," Dhillon said.[8] She added that removing non-citizens from the rolls is critical to ensuring California elections are conducted without fraudulent voting, and that the Department would hold non-compliant jurisdictions accountable.[8]

## Civil rights division mission statement

As assistant attorney general for civil rights in the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]], Dhillon oversaw a revised mission statement under the [[trump-administration|Trump administration]] committing the division to "ensure free, fair, and honest elections unmarred by fraud, errors, or suspicion."[9] The UK Guardian reported on the change with alarm in April 2025, as the department pursued a series of voter fraud prosecutions assisted by [[doge|DOGE]].[9]

## Publicizing the California ballot ruling (January 2024)

As former Republican National Committee for California chairwoman, Dhillon posted online the January 3, 2024 order by U.S. District Judge [[david-carter|David O. Carter]] dismissing with prejudice the [[california-ballot-disqualification-case|California lawsuit]] seeking to keep [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] off the state's primary ballot.[10] "The remnants of the last California case to keep President Trump off the ballot here were dismissed today by Judge David O. Carter!!" she wrote on X.[10]

## Illinois voter roll statement of interest

In July 2025 Dhillon, as Assistant Attorney General for the Civil Rights Division, was the public face of the [[department-of-justice|Justice Department]]'s statement of interest in [[judicial-watch-v-illinois-state-board-of-elections|Judicial Watch v. Illinois State Board of Elections]].[11] "It is critical to remove ineligible voters from the registration rolls so that elections are conducted fairly, accurately, and without fraud," she said, adding that under the [[national-voter-registration-act|National Voter Registration Act]] states have the responsibility to conduct a robust list maintenance program and that the Department would vigorously enforce those requirements.[11]

## Colorado prisons investigation, December 2025

As Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights, Dhillon announced on December 8, 2025 a Justice Department investigation into 33 facilities operated by the [[colorado-department-of-corrections|Colorado Department of Corrections]] and the state's Department of Youth Services.[12] *Westword* reported that the probe appeared politically motivated rather than a response to years of complaints from [[disability-law-colorado|Disability Law Colorado]], tying it to President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s efforts to free former Mesa County Clerk [[tina-peters|Tina Peters]]; Dhillon reshared several X posts celebrating the investigation as "how we FREE TINA PETERS."[12] Local disability advocates nonetheless welcomed the federal intervention as "an important step toward accountability."[12]
