# Jim Jordan

Jim Jordan is Chairman of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]].[1]

In July 2026 reporting, Jordan was described as having separately referred Special Counsel [[jack-smith|Jack Smith]] to the Justice Department for possible criminal investigation over testimony related to his office's access to members of [[congress|Congress]]' text messages.[1] The referral came amid a broader push — including a Center to Advance Security in America request to Acting DNI [[william-pulte|William Pulte]] — to scrutinize the security clearances and conduct of Smith's former investigative team.[1]

## ActBlue contempt letter (2026)

As Chairman of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], Jordan joined House Administration Chairman [[bryan-steil|Bryan Steil]] and House Oversight Chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] in June 2026 in sending a letter to [[actblue|ActBlue]] CEO [[regina-wallace-jones|Regina Wallace-Jones]] warning that the organization could face [[contempt-of-congress|contempt of Congress]] for failing to comply with subpoenas issued on July 22, 2025.[2] The chairmen said ActBlue continued to withhold records — including at least 420 documents listed in a June 5, 2026 privilege log — and rejected what they called baseless attorney-client privilege claims, arguing the noncompliance hindered Congress's investigation into possible foreign interference in political donations.[2]

## Questioning of John Durham (June 2023)

As Chairman of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], Jordan questioned Special Counsel [[john-durham|John Durham]] at the [[durham-house-judiciary-testimony-2023|June 21, 2023 hearing]] on the [[durham-report|Durham Report]], eliciting Durham's statement that [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[james-comey|Jim Comey]] had withheld a [[john-brennan|Brennan]] referral memorandum from [[crossfire-hurricane|Crossfire Hurricane]] investigators.[3] Jordan responded: "They kept key intelligence from the investigators. This is how bad this investigation was but here's the scary part: I don't think anything has changed."[3]


## COVID-19 origins select subcommittee (2023)

Jordan played a leading role at the first hearing of the House select subcommittee investigating the origins of COVID-19 on March 8, 2023, where former [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] Director [[robert-redfield]] testified that he had been sidelined from internal origins debates and that [[anthony-fauci]] did not appreciate his support for the [[lab-leak-theory|lab-leak theory]].[4]

Jordan focused his questioning on two top Fauci advisers, [[kristian-andersen]] and [[robert-garry]], who emailed Fauci on January 31, 2020 stating that anomalies in the virus suggested a non-natural origin and that it had "unusual features" that "(potentially) look engineered."[4] Jordan argued that the scientists reversed their position three days later, that the only intervening event was a conference call with Fauci and [[francis-collins]], and that roughly $9 million in later grant funding supplied "nine million reasons" for the change.[4] Commentary in the [[badlands-news-brief]] called Jordan's questioning a commendable and relentless effort to expose fraud within the biomedical research system.[4]

## 2023 speakership bid collapse

In October 2023 House Republicans abruptly removed Jordan as the conference's nominee for Speaker after three failed floor ballots, touching off a scramble in which nearly a dozen members entered the race and [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]], [[kevin-hern|Kevin Hern]], [[byron-donalds|Byron Donalds]] and Austin Scott emerged as frontrunners.[5] Jordan had been expected to support a bipartisan plan to temporarily expand the powers of Speaker Pro Tempore [[patrick-mchenry|Patrick McHenry]] through January so the [[us-house-of-representatives|House]] could pass funding measures, but he backtracked hours before his third ballot after multiple Republicans objected.[5] Badlands contributor [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the establishment Republicans who blocked Jordan as "looking for another McCarthy."[5]

## 2023 Speaker candidacy

Jordan was one of the Republican nominees who failed to win the Speaker's gavel during the three-week leadership vacuum of October 2023.[6] After his candidacy and that of [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]] collapsed for want of conference support, [[tom-emmer|Tom Emmer]] became the third nominee before withdrawing within hours, opening the path for [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]], who won 220 votes on the first floor ballot.[6]

## Letters to intelligence officials over the Hunter Biden laptop

On 1 March 2023 Jordan, as chairman of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], joined [[house-intelligence-committee|House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence]] chairman [[mike-turner|Mike Turner]] in sending 29 letters to Central Intelligence officials who had publicly discredited the [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] laptop story, noting that the officials had not responded to previously requested interviews.[7] The letters, first obtained by the Daily Caller, reiterated the requests for transcribed interviews and asked the officials to "comply promptly" and to arrange production of outstanding material.[7] Both the [[washington-post|Washington Post]] and the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] had acknowledged the laptop's authenticity in March 2022, more than a year after the Daily Caller News Foundation verified it.[7]

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] wrote that "the patriot hammer just might be coming down on DS actors," suggesting the House GOP's moves were the beginning of a longer coordinated effort to expose corruption and speculating that the laptop had been left behind deliberately as part of a broader sting operation.[7]

## Subpoena of Biden's ghostwriter (March 2024)

As chair of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], Jordan signed a March 2024 subpoena to [[mark-zwonitzer|Mark Zwonitzer]], the ghostwriter of President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s memoir, seeking recordings and transcripts referenced in special counsel [[robert-hur|Robert Hur]]'s report.[8] In a letter accompanying the subpoena Jordan wrote that on March 11, 2024 — more than two weeks after the return date on the original request — Zwonitzer's attorney informed the committee that he would not produce the documents without a subpoena compelling cooperation.[8] The subpoena was one of numerous House Republican efforts to obtain materials from the Hur investigation.[8]

### Biden impeachment inquiry (September 2023)

On 12 September 2023, House Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] designated Jordan and Oversight chairman [[james-comer|James Comer]] to lead the newly announced impeachment inquiry into President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]], which proceeded without a formal House vote.[9] McCarthy justified the inquiry by alleging that Biden had used his office to coordinate with [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]]'s business partners about Hunter's role at the Ukrainian energy firm [[burisma|Burisma]].[9]

## Cafe Milano dinners and the Biden bribery claim

On Fox News Channel's "Hannity" on July 31, 2023, Jordan — chairman of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] — said it "sure looks that way" when asked whether the [[department-of-justice|DOJ]] had been helping to cover up the [[joe-biden-bribery-allegation|Joe Biden bribery allegation]].[10] He pointed to two dinners at the Cafe Milano restaurant in Washington, D.C., both attended by [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]] for their entirety, one of which included [[elena-baturina|Elena Baturina]], "this Russian — the wealthiest woman in Russia, worth over $1 billion," which Jordan said was "a different story than what he's been telling us all along."[10]

## October 2023 speakership bid

Jordan, a combative right-wing Republican and close ally of former President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], sought the speaker's gavel following the unprecedented October 3, 2023 ouster of [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]].[11] In the first floor ballot on Tuesday, October 17, 2023 he took 200 votes — short of the 217 required and the lowest tally McCarthy had recorded in fifteen rounds of voting the previous January — with twenty Republicans defecting to [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]] (7), McCarthy (6), [[lee-zeldin|Lee Zeldin]] (3) and four other single-vote candidates.[11] Jordan postponed further action until 11 a.m. Eastern on Wednesday while pressuring the holdouts, and it was not clear whether he would gain or lose ground on a second ballot.[11] The prolonged vacancy left [[us-congress|Congress]] unable to respond to the wars in the Middle East and Ukraine with a partial shutdown deadline a month away.[11] The episode is covered further at [[2023-speaker-of-the-house-election|the 2023 Speaker of the House election]].[11]

## Pfizer vaccine trial inquiry (May 2025)

As chairman of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], Jordan led an investigation launched in May 2025 into allegations that [[pfizer|Pfizer]] executives deliberately slowed COVID-19 vaccine clinical trials to affect the 2020 presidential election.[12] The committee demanded that former Pfizer research chief [[philip-dormitzer|Philip Dormitzer]] hand over all relevant trial documents from March 1, 2020 to the present and sit for a transcribed interview by May 29, 2025.[12]

## The Facebook Files (2023)

As chairman of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], Jordan obtained and released a cache of communications between [[facebook]] and the [[joe-biden|Biden]] [[white-house|White House]] that he branded the [[facebook-files|"Facebook Files."]][13] The committee secured the material only after bringing considerable pressure to bear on the company, including a threat to hold [[mark-zuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] in contempt of [[congress]].[13] The August 2023 tranche showed White House digital strategy director [[rob-flaherty|Rob Flaherty]] suggesting the platform change its algorithm to promote establishment outlets such as [[new-york-times|The New York Times]] over competitors including [[tomi-lahren|Tomi Lahren]] and the [[daily-wire|Daily Wire]].[13] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] credited the disclosures as proof that government used private companies to end-run [[first-amendment|First Amendment]] limits, while arguing that Republican legislators' rhetoric had produced no accountability and that the censorship apparatus continued to operate.[13]

## Loss of the October 11, 2023 conference nomination

Jordan, then chairman of the House Judiciary Committee, lost the Republican conference's nomination for Speaker of the House on Wednesday, October 11, 2023, falling to Majority Leader [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]] 113–99 in a closed-door secret ballot held a week after [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]]'s ouster.[14] The result did not settle the contest: because Republicans held only 221 seats and Scalise needed at least 217 votes on the floor, five defectors could block him, and Jordan's supporters showed no sign of falling in line — Rep. [[lauren-boebert|Lauren Boebert]] said immediately after the vote, "I'm voting for Jim Jordan on the floor."[14]

## December 2023 contempt statement on Hunter Biden

House Judiciary Chair Jim Jordan joined House Oversight Chair James Comer in a joint statement on December 13, 2023 announcing contempt of Congress proceedings against Hunter Biden for defying subpoenas for a closed-door deposition, declaring that the committees would "not provide special treatment because his last name is Biden."[15]

## Justice Department refusal of document requests (January 2023)

In January 2023 the [[justice-department|Justice Department]] signaled that it was unlikely to share information about ongoing criminal investigations with the newly GOP-controlled House, a move certain to frustrate Republicans in the chamber.[16] Responding to a flurry of document requests from Jordan as House Judiciary Chairman, the department wrote that "any oversight requests must be weighed against the department's interests in protecting the integrity of its work," adding that its mission to independently and impartially uphold the rule of law required maintaining the integrity of its investigations, prosecutions and civil actions and avoiding even a perception of influence by anything but the law and the facts.[16]

Badlands contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] framed the refusal as concerning records related to President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]]'s handling of classified documents potentially dating from his time as Vice President in the Obama administration, arguing that the department was invoking ongoing investigations to supersede the records request.[16] He contrasted this with the treatment of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] over his taxes, the Steele dossier, the "perfect phone call" and January 6, calling the episode an illustration of two-tiered justice and demanding disclosure of the contents and dates of the classified documents.[16]

## Second failed speaker ballot

On Wednesday, October 18, 2023, Jordan failed for a second time to win the speaker's gavel, telling reporters in the Capitol that the House would not hold a third vote that day.[17] He had been nominated the previous week after House Majority Leader [[steve-scalise|Steve Scalise]] (R-La.) withdrew from the race, and would most likely have needed the support of 217 of 221 Republicans; he received 200 votes on the first ballot and 199 on the second.[17] The chamber had been left leaderless after a group of hard-line conservatives led by Rep. [[matt-gaetz|Matt Gaetz]] (R-Fla.) succeeded in ousting Speaker [[kevin-mccarthy|Kevin McCarthy]] (R-Calif.), while Democrats backed Minority Leader [[hakeem-jeffries|Hakeem Jeffries]] and signaled openness to working with interim Speaker [[patrick-mchenry|Patrick McHenry]] (R-N.C.).[17] Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that a paralyzed Congress might temporarily serve the [[information-war|Info War]], since it denied "these cretins" the ability to declare war and displayed a rudderless United States, concluding that "sometimes, bad news is good news in the Mind War."[17]

## September 2025 FBI oversight hearings

During the September 2025 congressional oversight hearings on the [[fbi|FBI]], Jordan — as chair of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]] — thanked Director [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]] for bringing to light matters that former Director [[christopher-wray|Chris Wray]] had left in the dark.[18] "Because of the work of Director Patel, we learned that the former chair of the House Intelligence Committee and current United States senator leaked classified information," Jordan said, referring to Sen. [[adam-schiff|Adam Schiff]].[18] He accused Schiff and former FBI Director [[james-comey|James Comey]] of conspiring together to "sabotage and undermine" the president, allegations Schiff has repeatedly denied.[18]

Badlands contributor Ashe in America connected Jordan's role to the fifteen-round McCarthy speaker election, recalling that Jordan was a popular candidate for speaker but refused the post, saying he was set on chairing the Judiciary Committee and building a record to hold prior administrations accountable.[18] She argued that justice was not possible until President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] returned to office and controlled Executive Branch functions, noting that referrals might be acted upon under [[pam-bondi|Pam Bondi]] where there had been zero chance under [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]].[18] With Republicans controlling both chambers and the executive alongside a conservative majority on the High Court, she wrote that the record was thick, public expectation for accountability equally thick, and that "it's time for the justice phase."[18]

## January 5 pipe bomb letter to the FBI

In May 2023, as chairman of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], Jordan joined Representative [[andy-biggs|Andy Biggs]] — head of the Subcommittee on Crime and Federal Government Surveillance — and Representative [[bill-posey|Bill Posey]] in a letter to [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] about the [[january-5-2021-pipe-bombs|January 5, 2021 pipe bombs]] left at the DNC and RNC.[19] The letter recorded that briefing requests made on September 2, 2021, March 9, 2022, and January 17, 2023 had gone unfulfilled for more than a year and a half, and disclosed that a whistleblower had raised questions about the "unusual" nature of the bureau's investigation.[19]

## First Judiciary subpoenas, February 2023

Days after taking the Judiciary gavel in the 118th Congress, Jordan fired off the committee's first subpoenas, targeting Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]], [[fbi|FBI]] Director [[christopher-wray|Christopher Wray]] and Education Secretary [[miguel-cardona|Miguel Cardona]] over the [[doj-school-board-memo-2021|2021 school board memo]]; the demands followed more than 100 letters Judiciary Republicans had sent on the subject.[20]

## Forcing the October 17, 2023 speaker vote

Amid the [[2023-speaker-of-the-house-election|2023 speaker of the House election]], Jordan sent out a "Dear Colleague" letter vowing to bring "all Republicans together" if elected speaker, writing: "The role of a speaker is to bring all Republicans together. That's what I intend to do. We will make sure there are more Republican voices involved in our major decision beyond the Five Families."[21] He added that his goal would be "to empower our committees and committee chairs to take the lead on the House's legislative work through regular order."[21] Reporting indicated Jordan planned to force a Tuesday floor vote with or without the 217 votes needed to win, saying: "If you don't have a speaker, so we get a speaker, we get the House open and we get to work on the resolution and supplemental for Israel. And we get back to work for the American people."[21]

## September 2023 Garland hearing

As chairman of the [[house-judiciary-committee|House Judiciary Committee]], Jordan opened the committee's roughly five-hour oversight hearing with Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]] on September 20, 2023 by declaring, "The fix is in."[22] He added that "even with the face-saving indictment of [[hunter-biden|Hunter Biden]] last week, everyone knows the fix is in," and accused Garland several times of "slow walking" the Hunter Biden investigation to benefit President [[joe-biden|Joe Biden]].[22] The hearing, covered at [[september-2023-garland-house-judiciary-hearing|September 2023 Garland House Judiciary Hearing]], also ranged over the department's handling of probes into [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and the events of January 6.[22]
