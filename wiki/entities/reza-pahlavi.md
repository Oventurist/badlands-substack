# Reza Pahlavi

Reza Pahlavi is the son of the deposed Shah of Iran and a figure frequently promoted in Western circles as a potential post-clerical leader of [[iran|Iran]].[1]

## Trump's rejection

Appearing on the Hugh Hewitt Show in early January 2026, [[donald-trump|President Donald Trump]] said he did not think Pahlavi would be an appropriate pick to lead Iran.[1] Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry wrote that this "throws cold water" on the NeoCons and Zionists who had been pushing Pahlavi "down our throats for months."[1] The contributor pointed to Pahlavi's meeting with [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] during the previous year as one of many trips to Israel in which, in the contributor's telling, Pahlavi performed "all the necessary Talmudic rituals in order to gain the favor of the powers that be."[1]

## Context

The remarks came against the backdrop of nationwide protests and an internet blackout in Iran, and were contrasted with Trump's 2024 campaign appearance on the Patrick Bet-David podcast, where he was asked whether he would support regime change in Iran.[1] The Badlands assessment was that regime change is unlikely, though a scenario in which the Ayatollah, the mullahs, and the IRGC are expunged while the socially moderate political administration remains was considered plausible.[1]

## Trump's January 2026 remarks

On January 15, 2026, President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] publicly questioned whether Pahlavi could unify opposition support inside [[iran|Iran]], telling reporters: "He seems very nice, but I don't know how he'd play within his own country... And we really aren't up to that point yet... I don't know whether or not his country would accept his leadership, and certainly if they would, that would be fine with me."[2] [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] read the comment as Trump dismissing the idea of installing Pahlavi — whom he described as a "Deep-State darling" — as leader of Iran, and grouped it with other instances in which Trump declined to follow through on regime-change expectations.[2]

## Response to protest deaths (January 2026)

During the January 2026 protest wave in [[iran|Iran]], Pahlavi was asked whether he felt any responsibility for the reported deaths, and replied in substance that they were a necessary cost in the war to "liberate" Iran.[3] Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]], noting Pahlavi's descent from the CIA-installed Shah, cited the remark as part of a pattern he read as a textbook [[strategy-of-tension|Strategy of Tension]] regime-change operation, alongside reported [[mossad|Mossad]] encouragement of protesters and video of rioters attacking security personnel.[3]

## Claiming the throne (March 2026)

After the death of [[ali-khamenei|Ali Khamenei]] and the selection of [[mojtaba-khamenei|Mojtaba Khamenei]] as Iran's new supreme leader, Pahlavi released a video "accepting" calls to be crowned king of Iran, jumping to claim the throne amid the war involving Iran, [[israel|Israel]] and the United States.[4] Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described him as "the wannabe princeling who has spent his entire adult life living in a mansion in the DC suburbs working as a lobbyist," and wrote that "literally nobody wants this, nor asked for it."[4]

He argued that President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s abrupt signal that he might accept the new Ayatollah was designed in part to shut down this "attempted usurpation," and speculated that the Israelis had put Pahlavi up to the move, noting that [[benjamin-netanyahu|Netanyahu]] and the NeoCons were the only figures calling for Pahlavi to be installed as ruler of Iran.[4] A photograph of Pahlavi visiting Netanyahu in Israel months earlier was cited as likely preparation for the moment.[4]

## Endorsement of the December 2025 protests

On December 29, 2025, Pahlavi issued a video message backing the demonstrations then erupting across [[iran|Iran]], greeting "the bazaar merchants, and the people who have taken the streets into their own hands" and warning that "As long as this regime remains in power, the country's economic situation will continue to deteriorate."[5] He called for greater solidarity, urging "all segments of society to join your fellow citizens in the streets and raise your voices demanding the downfall of this system."[5] [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands]] likened him to "the Maria Corina Machado of Iran, supporting the Color Revolutionists," and questioned why self-described America First conservatives were backing such operations.[5] The events are covered at [[2025-iran-protests|the 2025 Iran protests]].[5]
