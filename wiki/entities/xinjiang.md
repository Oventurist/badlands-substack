# Xinjiang

**Xinjiang** is an autonomous region of western China that [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]] presents in "Avoiding WWIII" as a test case for what he calls a "Manifest Destiny with dignity" — development that does not destroy the cultural heritage of minority peoples.[1]

## Ehret's account of development

Ehret writes that, contrary to denunciations from the western Five Eyes-managed political class, what has been seen in Tibet and Xinjiang are cultural heritage centres, exploding literacy rates and the celebration and teaching of traditional languages, songs, stories and dances under full government patronage.[1] He states that per capita GDP in Xinjiang increased a hundredfold since 1979 and that average life expectancy rose from 30 years in 1949 to 72 years.[1] On the religious level he counts over 24,400 mosques in Xinjiang, along with 59 Buddhist temples and 253 churches, and argues that in only eight years the problem of Saudi-US funded terrorism in China was resolved without a single Arab state "bombed back to the stone age."[1] He attributes contrary reporting to [[national-endowment-for-democracy|NED]]-controlled propaganda outlets.[1]

## Rail projects

A high-speed line running from Lanzhou to Urumqi totals 1,776 km and runs parallel to the electrified Lanxin passenger railway completed in 2014; with the high-speed route finished, the Lanxin line is to be converted to freight service for the Eurasian Continental Landbridge, carrying goods 11,000 km from East China to Rotterdam.[1] Other projects include the Ruoqiang-Hotan railway completing a loop around the Taklamakan desert and the 600 km Kashgar to Osh railway linking China to Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan, which Ehret argues will boost Uzbekistan's Ferghana Valley, a landing point for Saudi Wahhabism since the 1990s from which radicalized jihadis were infused into Pakistan, Afghanistan and back into China.[1] A new highway from Kashgar City in southern Xinjiang to Pakistan is being finalized as an extension of the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor, together with a new railway cutting through the desert.[1] These works form part of the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]].[1]
