# Xinjiang

Xinjiang is an autonomous region in western [[china|China]] and a central node of the [[belt-and-road-initiative|Belt and Road Initiative]]. The Badlands Media corpus presents it as a test case for whether large-scale development can proceed without destroying local cultural heritage.

## Development claims

[[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s [[avoiding-wwiii|Avoiding WWIII]] states that in Xinjiang per capita GDP has increased a hundredfold since 1979 and average life expectancy has risen from 30 years in 1949 to 72 years today. He reports cultural heritage centres, exploding literacy rates, and the celebration and teaching of traditional languages, songs, stories and dances under full government patronage, alongside gains in longevity, population density, poverty reduction, infant mortality reduction, and access to advanced industrial skills, clean water, internet and abundant electricity. On the religious side he counts over 24,400 mosques in Xinjiang, along with 59 Buddhist temples and 253 churches, and asserts that Saudi- and U.S.-funded terrorism in China was dealt with in eight years "without a single Arab state bombed back to the stone age."

## Rail projects

The article describes a high-speed line running from Lanzhou to Urumqi, totalling 1,776 km and paralleling the electrified Lanxin passenger railway completed in 2014; with the new route in service the Lanxin line is to be converted to freight for the Eurasian Continental Landbridge, carrying goods 11,000 km from East China to Rotterdam. Other projects include the Ruoqiang–Hotan railway completing a loop around the Taklamakan desert, and the 600 km Kashgar-to-Osh railway linking China with Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan. The latter is credited with bringing needed growth to Uzbekistan's Ferghana Valley, described as a landing point for Saudi Wahhabism since the 1990s from which radicalized jihadis were infused into [[pakistan|Pakistan]], Afghanistan and back into China. A new highway from Kashgar City to Pakistan is being finalized as an extension of the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor, along with a new railway cutting through the desert.

## Contested claims

Ehret rejects allegations of cultural genocide, attributing them to "[[national-endowment-for-democracy|NED]]-controlled propaganda outlets" and the Five Eyes-managed western political class, and citing Chinese state broadcaster CGTN reports of Xinjiang residents refuting the claims. These assertions are contested and are presented here as the source's position.
