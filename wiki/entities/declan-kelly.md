# Declan Kelly

Declan Kelly is an Irish-American businessman, co-founder and former chief executive of [[teneo|Teneo]], and served as [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]]'s economic envoy to Northern Ireland during her tenure as Secretary of State. The Badlands Media essay America for Sale — Part 2 treats his appointment as a case study in the blending of official position and private commercial interest.

## Appointment as special envoy

*Politico* reported that when Clinton became Secretary of State she personally sought out Kelly — then 41 and a former fundraiser for her — to serve as economic envoy to Northern Ireland, "giving her 41-year-old former fundraiser a special status outside normal diplomatic channels." While serving in that role and reaching out to global corporations for Irish investment, Kelly was simultaneously working as a private consultant for two of them, earning about $2.4 million from [[dow-chemical|Dow Chemical]], a longtime client and a participant in Clinton's Ireland initiative.

The Badlands article notes that Kelly, like [[huma-abedin|Huma Abedin]], held "special government employee" status, took no [[state-department|State Department]] salary, and hired his own staff. *Politico* reported that despite job titles resembling State Department positions and regular interactions with department staff and Irish diplomats, "none of them were official government employees," and the department stated it had "no record of these individuals being employed by the Department." Some of Kelly's envoy-office employees were simultaneously doing consulting work for Declan Kelly Consulting and would become among Teneo's first employees, avoiding the oversight applied to department staff. The author argues this structure was designed to conceal how Kelly was compensated and what he was doing.

## FTI Consulting and the noncompete

Kelly had been a top executive at [[fti-consulting|FTI Consulting]] before stepping down in July 2009, signing a two-year noncompete agreement that barred outside consulting but allowed him to continue part-time work for FTI on the Dow Chemical and Coca-Cola accounts. Between August 2009 and January 2011, in the midst of his State Department work, Dow paid Kelly $2.4 million through FTI. The article identifies this noncompete as the reason Teneo's launch was delayed from 2009 to June 2011, and both companies later became among Teneo's first and largest accounts.

## Teneo

Kelly co-founded Teneo Holdings in 2011 with [[doug-band|Doug Band]] and [[paul-keary|Paul Keary]]. The essay argues that Kelly used his official envoy position to build corporate relationships that would later benefit both Teneo and the Clintons, and that lucrative corporate deals arranged through the post led to donations flowing into the [[clinton-foundation|Clinton Foundation]] — an interpretive claim beyond the cited reporting.
