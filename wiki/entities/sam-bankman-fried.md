# Sam Bankman-Fried

Sam Bankman-Fried, commonly abbreviated "SBF," is the founder of the collapsed cryptocurrency exchange [[ftx|FTX]] and a defendant in a federal fraud and conspiracy prosecution in New York.[1]

## Bail proceedings

In February 2023 a federal judge in New York raised the possibility of revoking Bankman-Fried's bail and remanding him into custody over possible witness tampering, but opted instead to give defense attorneys and federal prosecutors additional time to propose stricter conditions of pre-trial release, as reported by [[abc-news|ABC News]].[1] Bankman-Fried had pleaded not guilty to charges accusing him of misappropriating billions of dollars from FTX and was free on a $250 million bail package.[1]

Federal prosecutors sought to bar him from contacting potential witnesses and to restrict his use of encrypted communications and the internet; during the hearing the judge expressed concern that the government's proposed rules did not go far enough.[1]

## Badlands commentary

Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] wrote that Bankman-Fried had made "several incredibly stupid errors" since making bail, including publishing spreadsheets of finances to Twitter and contacting Witness-1 in the criminal case in an apparent attempt to reestablish a relationship and influence that witness.[1] Just Human added that, after such conduct, Bankman-Fried's own lawyers likely would not object to the judge revoking their client's bail.[1]

## Imprisonment

Bankman-Fried is serving a 25-year sentence on fraud charges after stealing $8 billion from customers of his FTX crypto exchange, funneling money into his crypto-trading fund Alameda Research, venture capital investments, political donations and personal spending.[2] He has been held at the [[metropolitan-detention-center|Metropolitan Detention Center]] in Brooklyn since 2023, and in September 2024 was reported to be sharing a dormitory-style room there with [[sean-combs|Sean "Diddy" Combs]], who had been denied bail on federal sex trafficking and racketeering charges.[2]

## VPN and bail conditions (February 2023)

Prosecutors discovered that Bankman-Fried was using a virtual private network to access the internet and raised concerns that he might be using it to trade crypto in violation of his bail terms or to access the dark web.[3] In a February 13, 2023 letter to U.S. District Judge [[lewis-kaplan|Lewis Kaplan]], Assistant U.S. Attorney [[danielle-sassoon|Danielle R. Sassoon]] conceded that "many individuals use a VPN for benign purposes" but cited "several potential concerns" given the charges he faced.[3] His attorneys responded on February 14 that he had used the VPN only to access an NFL Game Pass international subscription purchased while living in the Bahamas so he could watch playoff games, including the Super Bowl between the Kansas City Chiefs and the Philadelphia Eagles, and that he would not do it again.[3] Kaplan ordered him to stop using the VPN and also ruled that encrypted messaging apps would not be permitted under his bail conditions.[3]

U.S. Attorney [[damian-williams|Damian Williams]] of the [[southern-district-of-new-york|Southern District of New York]] obtained approval to push back both civil trials during the criminal proceedings, without prejudice, so the Justice Department could complete its criminal case without overlap; prosecutors observed that all three cases would rest heavily on the same or similar evidence.[3] Badlands contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] argued that considerable corruption remained to be uncovered in the fraudulent slush funding, which he said led directly to the Democratic National Committee's doorstep, and suggested the encrypted-messaging restriction indicated concern about whom Bankman-Fried might be contacting.[3]


## Pretrial release dispute (February 2023)

Bankman-Fried returned to court on February 9, 2023 for a hearing to determine the conditions of his pretrial release, after prosecutors discovered his encrypted communications with FTX's general counsel.[4] Senior U.S. District Judge [[lewis-kaplan|Lewis Kaplan]] pressed both the government and the defense on how to prevent him from contacting key witnesses while awaiting trial, telling defense counsel: "I am far less interested in the defendant's convenience, given the record here."[4] The brief hearing proved inconclusive, with Kaplan urging the parties to ratchet up monitoring of all avenues of communication and to submit a more restrictive proposal for his approval as part of the $250 million bond package.[4]

Badlands Media contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] noted that Bankman-Fried had been caught texting and emailing "Witness 1" in the criminal case, proposing to "reconnect," build a "constructive relationship," and "use each other as resources when possible, or at least vet things with each other" — which Just Human called a preamble to witness tampering.[4]

## Conclusion of testimony (October 2023)

Bankman-Fried stepped down from the stand on Tuesday, October 31, 2023, after four days of testimony in his criminal fraud trial in Manhattan federal court.[5] "That concludes the presentation of evidence in this case," Judge [[lewis-kaplan|Lewis Kaplan]] announced just after noon Eastern as he sent the jury home, with closing arguments set for 9:30 a.m. the following day.[5] Reporting characterized that final day of testimony as delivering some of the most potentially damning blows yet to a defense hinging on the narrative that he had made honest mistakes as a startup founder.[5]

Prosecutors argued that the former billionaire used his crypto exchange [[ftx|FTX]] as a personal piggy bank, taking customer money to enrich himself and his family, buy luxury beachfront property in the Bahamas, and funnel millions into U.S. political campaigns.[5] Bankman-Fried, then 31, testified that he knew as early as 2020 that FTX customer funds were being held in a bank account controlled by FTX's sister hedge fund [[alameda-research|Alameda Research]], and said he did not recall giving any directions to Alameda employees to safeguard those funds.[5] The government countered that he was well aware he was misrepresenting key aspects of his business to investors, customers, and lawmakers in Congress; he pleaded not guilty to seven counts of fraud and conspiracy.[5] His decision to testify at all was widely seen as a Hail Mary from a defense that had struggled to poke holes in the testimony of several high-ranking executives from his former inner circle.[5]

## Dismissal of the second trial (December 2023)

In late December 2023, prosecutors told US District Judge [[lewis-kaplan|Lewis Kaplan]] that they would not pursue a [[bankman-fried-second-trial-dismissal|second trial]] on remaining charges, including conspiracy to bribe foreign officials, bank fraud and operating an unlicensed money transmitting business.[6] The government cited expediency and the fact that the Bahamas, which had extradited him, had not consented to the additional charges as required by treaty.[6] Much of the evidence for the second trial had already been introduced at the first and could be weighed at sentencing, then set for 28 March.[6] Badlands writer [[just-human|Just Human]] argued the more revealing question was why the Bahamas withheld consent, hinting that Bankman-Fried's parents — never extradited and still in the United States — were most likely behind the campaign finance scheme.[6]

## Motions to dismiss (May 2023)

In a filing in Manhattan federal court on Monday, May 8, 2023, Bankman-Fried asked a judge to throw out all but three of the criminal charges arising from the collapse of [[ftx|FTX]].[7] His lawyers argued that many cryptocurrency exchanges collapsed during the broad market crash of 2022 and that prosecutors had charged their client in a "rush to judgment," writing that "rather than wait for traditional civil and regulatory processes following their ordinary course to address the situation, the Government jumped in with both feet, improperly seeking to turn these civil and regulatory issues into federal crimes."[7] The three counts he did not contest were conspiracy to commit commodities fraud, conspiracy to commit securities fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering.[7] Then 31 and a former billionaire, he had been largely confined to his parents' home since his December arrest in the Bahamas, where he lived and where FTX was based, and was extradited to the United States just over a week after that arrest.[7]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] wrote that Bankman-Fried, celebrated after reaching an estimated $26.5 billion net worth through bitcoin and other digital assets, had used that wealth to buy "law enforcement, politicians, celebrities, regulators," and was untouchable "until he wasn't."[7] She argued the case carried narrative themes rhyming with [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]] and Las Vegas shooter Stephen Paddock — the "lone bad guy narrative" that in her view "reeks of cover up" — and insisted that the reported $8 billion fraud could not have been executed alone.[7] She criticized attempts to portray [[alameda-research|Alameda]] co-chief executive Caroline Ellison, who reportedly cut a deal with federal prosecutors, as a victim of the wage gap, and called for the "shadowy network" that enabled the fraud to be dragged into the light alongside him.[7]

## Sentencing

On March 28, 2024, Bankman-Fried was sentenced to 25 years in prison in Manhattan federal court for the massive fraud and conspiracy that doomed [[ftx|FTX]] and the related hedge fund [[alameda-research|Alameda Research]].[8] The sentence was significantly less than the 40 to 50 years federal prosecutors had sought, but far more than the five to six-and-a-half years suggested by his attorneys.[8] Judge [[lewis-kaplan|Lewis Kaplan]] also ordered him to pay $11 billion in forfeiture to the U.S. government, along with forfeiture of specific properties, and recommended placement in a medium- or lower-security facility near San Francisco.[8]

"There is a risk that this man will be in position to do something very bad in the future," Kaplan said before sentencing the 32-year-old, adding, "And it's not a trivial risk at all."[8] Kaplan noted he had never heard "a word of remorse for the commission of terrible crimes" from Bankman-Fried, and said that in 30 years on the federal bench he had "never seen a performance" like Bankman-Fried's trial testimony, which he characterized as "outright lying" or, at minimum, evasive under cross-examination.[8]

## Badlands reaction

[[just-human|Just Human]] said he was disappointed in the sentence, considering it insufficient for the criminality in the case given that Bankman-Fried was wholly unrepentant, engaged in witness tampering while awaiting trial, and perjured himself.[8] He expressed doubt that the sentence served the ends of justice or deterred others, while conceding that 25 years and $11 billion was "certainly nothing to scoff at."[8] He recalled that many had once predicted Bankman-Fried and the other FTX executives would escape consequences entirely, noting instead that the other executives were indicted and flipped against him, and that FTX, Alameda and related entities were taken from him and placed in bankruptcy by new owners who began a restitution process for victims.[8] Further restitution was expected as a consequence of the conviction, though victims would never be made completely whole.[8] With SBF's counsel stating they would appeal, Just Human speculated an appeal could go badly given Bankman-Fried's ego, and closed by asking, "What about the parents?"[8]

## Clinton ties and Crypto Bahamas

In January 2023 the New York Post reported, and the Daily Caller relayed, that Bankman-Fried had allegedly paid former President [[bill-clinton|Bill Clinton]] roughly $250,000 to speak at his Crypto Bahamas Conference in April 2022.[9] The conference featured Clinton alongside other world leaders and top crypto executives, and not long afterwards Bill and [[hillary-clinton|Hillary Clinton]] invited Bankman-Fried to speak at the 2022 [[clinton-global-initiative|Clinton Global Initiative]] in September.[9]

[[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued that the sequence might reveal how the Clinton machine operates, asking whether Bill Clinton vetted Bankman-Fried as a future money-laundering stooge in the Bahamas — a short flight from [[jeffrey-epstein|Jeffrey Epstein]]'s island — before the CGI invitation followed five months later.[9] Deschamps contended that in 2022 Bankman-Fried acted as a bagman for globalist interests in Ukraine, the Biden administration and the Democratic National Committee through the [[ftx|FTX]] scandal.[9]

## False pardon report (July 2025)

On Saturday, July 19, 2025, [[ftx|FTX]]'s native token FTT surged roughly 43 percent to $1.30 following false reports that Bankman-Fried had received a pardon from President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]; no such clemency grant appeared on the Justice Department's website.[10] Bankman-Fried was actively seeking a presidential pardon with the support of his parents, Joseph Bankman and Barbara Fried, and other allies, while serving a 25-year prison sentence following his fraud conviction related to FTX's collapse.[10] Several crypto industry figures had previously received presidential pardons from Trump, including BitMEX co-founders Arthur Hayes, Benjamin Delo, and Samuel Reed, as well as Silk Road creator Ross Ulbricht.[10]

## Conviction, November 2023

On Thursday, November 2, 2023, after 15 days of testimony and roughly four and a half hours of deliberation, a jury in a lower Manhattan courtroom found Bankman-Fried guilty on all seven counts of fraud and money laundering.[11] U.S. District Judge [[lewis-kaplan|Lewis Kaplan]] had read the jury about 60 pages of instructions before the case was handed over at approximately 3:15 p.m.[11] Sentencing was scheduled for March 28, with Bankman-Fried facing more than 100 years in prison if given the maximum on all counts; he indicated he would appeal.[11] The trial concluded far faster than the anticipated run through Thanksgiving.[11] The 31-year-old MIT graduate and son of two Stanford legal scholars, who founded [[ftx|FTX]] in 2019 two years after forming [[alameda-research|Alameda Research]], had pleaded not guilty to all charges.[11]

## Bail restrictions and gag order, July 2023

On Wednesday, July 26, 2023, U.S. District Judge [[lewis-kaplan|Lewis Kaplan]] tightened Bankman-Fried's bail conditions by restricting his ability to communicate publicly, and said he would consider jailing him before the trial over the collapse of the [[ftx|FTX]] cryptocurrency exchange.[12] At the hearing in Manhattan federal court, prosecutors asked Kaplan to detain the 31-year-old immediately, saying he had "crossed a line" by sharing the personal writings of his former romantic partner [[caroline-ellison|Caroline Ellison]] with a reporter, in what they said amounted to a second instance of witness tampering.[12] Kaplan imposed the gag order prosecutors had previously requested and gave both sides until August 3 to explain their views on whether jail was necessary, telling the defendant, "I'm very mindful of the government's interest in this issue, which I take seriously. Mr. Bankman-Fried, you'd better take it seriously too."[12]

Defense lawyer Mark Cohen said his client had been trying merely to protect his reputation by communicating with journalists, and that it "really would be almost impossible" to prepare for the October 2 trial if Bankman-Fried were jailed.[12] Since his extradition in December from the Bahamas, where FTX was based and where he had been arrested, Bankman-Fried had been largely confined to his parents' home in Palo Alto, California.[12] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] described the prospect of jailing him only after a second tampering allegation as "a half-hearted virtue signal by the judge," argued that the preferential treatment he had received since news of his fraud broke had already destroyed public confidence that he would get a legitimate trial, and contrasted his lawyers' complaints with the pretrial detention of January 6 defendants.[12]

## Bail revocation, August 2023

A federal judge revoked Bankman-Fried's bail in August 2023, citing probable cause that the accused crypto fraudster had attempted to tamper with witnesses.[13] He was remanded, at least temporarily, to the Metropolitan Detention Center in Brooklyn while the parties worked out how he would review discovery materials ahead of a trial scheduled for October; following the ruling, U.S. marshals handcuffed him and escorted him from the courtroom.[13]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] of [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] observed that the news was lost in the shuffle of a hectic weekend dominated by island fires and special counsels.[13] He argued that while Bankman-Fried was not one of the "puppet masters" of what he called the Globalist [[deep-state|Deep State]], his entire financial operation was "surely a key node in their centralized machine," and that seeing true justice visited on such a node provided light in the darkness — with "plenty more where that came from."[13]
