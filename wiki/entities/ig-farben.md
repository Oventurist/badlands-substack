# IG Farben

IG Farben was the German chemical conglomerate — described in this account as the fourth largest company in the world — that formed the industrial core of the international cartel binding Nazi Germany's war economy to Anglo-American finance.[1]

## The 1929 cartel

Rockefeller's [[standard-oil|Standard Oil]] created a new international cartel alongside IG Farben in 1929 under the [[young-plan|Young Plan]].[1] The largest of these cartels saw Henry Ford's German operations merging with IG Farben, Dupont industries, Britain's Shell and Standard Oil.[1]

## Synthetic fuel transfer

The 1928 cartel agreement made it possible for Standard Oil to pass off all patents and technologies for the creation of synthetic gasoline from coal to IG Farben, allowing Germany to rise from producing merely 300,000 tons of natural petroleum in 1934 to 6.5 million tons — 85% of its total supply — during the Second World War.[1] Ehret argues that had this patent and technology transfer not taken place, the modern mechanized warfare that characterized [[world-war-ii|World War II]] could never have occurred.[1]

## Rockefeller investment

Badlands Media commentary states that [[john-d-rockefeller|John D. Rockefeller]] bought shares of I.G. Farben, a pharmaceutical company based in Germany that would become one of Nazi Germany's biggest companies, as part of his shift from oil into the medical industry and the broader transition of American medicine from natural to petrochemical treatment.[2]
