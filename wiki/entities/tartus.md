# Tartus

Tartus is a Syrian port city on the Mediterranean coast that hosts a naval facility used by Russian forces as well as a military ship repair yard.[1] Together with the Hmeimim airbase in [[latakia|Latakia]] province, it forms the backbone of Russia's military presence in the eastern Mediterranean.[1]

## December 2024 Israeli strikes

On the night of December 16, 2024, the Israeli Air Force carried out powerful strikes on military targets in Tartus, with additional strikes reported against sites in Latakia province.[1] A Syrian military observer quoted by Israeli media described the attack on the Tartus facilities as the most powerful strike in the area in more than ten years, since 2012.[1] The Syrian Observatory for Human Rights reported that Israeli aircraft hit several sites including air defense units and ground-to-ground missile bases, and Syrian sources said the Tartus targets included an air defense base and a storage facility for ground-to-ground missiles.[1] Badlands coverage noted that the bombing of weapons caches triggered an explosion registering as a 3.0 magnitude event on the Richter scale.[1]

## Strategic context

The strikes followed the fall of the Syrian government and the flight of Bashar al-Assad to Moscow.[1] Western outlets reported that Russia was abandoning both Tartus and Hmeimim, a claim Russian Foreign Minister [[sergei-lavrov|Sergei Lavrov]] publicly rejected.[1] On December 13, Business Insider reported that Russia was sending ships to Tartus to extract weapons from strategic caches maintained there since the Cold War, while the al-Qaeda-derived group [[hayat-tahrir-al-sham|Hayat Tahrir al-Sham]], now governing the country, indicated it was prepared to continue accommodating the Russian military on its own conditions.[1]

Badlands contributor GhostofBasedPatrickHenry emphasized that Tartus and Hmeimim serve as the staging ground for the [[wagner-group|Wagner Group]]'s Africa Corps, which has operated across the African continent, and argued that by striking them [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] had landed a blow against Russian operations in both Syria and Africa — even as he sought diplomacy with the new rulers in Damascus.[1]
