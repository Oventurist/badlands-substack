# F-16 Fighting Falcon

The **F-16 Fighting Falcon** is a single-engine multirole fighter aircraft that featured in September 2025 reporting on prospective United States arms deals with [[turkey|Turkey]].[1]

When President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] posted about his invitation to Turkish President [[recep-tayyip-erdogan|Recep Tayyip Erdogan]] to visit the [[white-house|White House]], he teased a potential F-35 deal and also referenced an F-16 deal that Badlands commentary described as sounding like "a certainty."[1]

## Design and significance

Badlands contributor [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] described the F-16 as a small and nimble aircraft named for the falcon — characterized as the most notorious of the avivore raptors, a bird of prey that attacks smaller birds in flight — because it was designed first as a dogfighting aircraft.[1] Its airframe is built to withstand nine G of force in combat, and its cockpit seat is reclined to help pilots retain blood in their brains under high G-loading and avoid losing consciousness.[1] For these reasons, fifty years after its introduction, the F-16 remained the most in-demand and most widely used fighter jet in the world, with more than 2,000 in service.[1]

## Regional context

The commentary argued that the F-16 was the sort of aircraft Syria would need to protect itself from Israeli F-35s, and that Turkey was the ideal candidate to step in and stop [[benjamin-netanyahu|Benjamin Netanyahu]] from annexing southern Syria — territory that, in this reading, would be used as a land bridge to start a war with Iran.[1] The argument arose alongside reporting on the Syrian president's expressed outrage over Israeli airstrikes on Syria as the [[israel-defense-forces|IDF]] used F-35s to dominate Syrian airspace.[1]

## Proposed transfers to Ukraine (June 2023)

Beginning in May 2023 the Biden administration signaled to European allies that it would not block their export of F-16s to Kyiv, a move widely viewed as a potential escalation toward direct Russia-[[nato|NATO]] conflict.[2] Observers noted that training Ukrainian pilots alone would likely take a year or more before the aircraft could fly combat missions.[2] At a June 2023 meeting of NATO defense ministers in Brussels, Secretary-General [[jens-stoltenberg|Jens Stoltenberg]] said it was too early to set a delivery timetable but welcomed allied pilot-training decisions as enabling later decisions to deliver "fourth generation fighter aircraft like, for instance, the F-16s."[2]

Badlands contributor [[johnny-q|Johnny Q]] wrote that the [[pentagon|Pentagon]], Poland and others had been reluctant to supply fighter planes, that the F-16 would be a comparable match-up to Russia's MiG-29 without conferring much advantage, and that placing freshly trained pilots in new aircraft against seasoned Russian pilots would be suicidal.[2] He noted the Netherlands' claim that it would send pilots while its aviation training center was not yet functioning.[2]
