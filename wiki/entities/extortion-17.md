# Extortion 17

**Extortion 17** was the callsign of a U.S. Army CH-47 Chinook helicopter shot down in Afghanistan in August 2011. In the Badlands Media "Q for Dummies" treatment of the event, the shootdown is presented as a suspicious loss that occurred only a few months after the announced death of [[osama-bin-laden|Osama bin Laden]], and is highlighted because the aircraft was carrying seventeen Navy SEALs — a number that carries symbolic weight in Q research because "Q" is the seventeenth letter of the alphabet.[1]

## The 2011 shootdown

The article states that Extortion 17 was a Chinook shot down in August of 2011, "only a few short months after Osama's 'death,'" and stresses the coincidence that the helicopter was carrying 17 Navy SEALs.[1] The author raises the question directly, without asserting it as proven: did the men aboard know the truth about bin Laden, and did they therefore have to be "silenced"?[1] The framing is characteristic of the series' method, which presents numerical and chronological coincidences as prompts for reader investigation rather than as settled findings.

## Role in the Army/Navy game "comms"

Extortion 17 re-entered the Badlands narrative in December 2024 through [[kash-patel|Kash Patel]], who visited Arlington National Cemetery on the same day as the annual Army/Navy football game and posted imagery from the site.[1] The author notes that Patel visited the gravesite associated with Extortion 17, treating the timing as an intentional signal rather than a coincidence — "because of course he would visit the gravesite of Extortion 17."[1] Patel also reposted an article that featured the number 17 and discussed intelligence "spy games" at the [[fbi|FBI]], reinforcing the numerical motif for the author.[1]

## Interpretation within Q research

Within the interpretive frame used by the series, Extortion 17 functions as a node connecting several recurring themes: the contested official account of the bin Laden raid, the recurrence of the number 17 as a Q signature, and the belief that public figures aligned with the movement communicate through timing and symbolism.[1] The series presents these connections as suggestive patterns for readers to evaluate, and the author repeatedly frames such alignments as things that strain the credibility of coincidence.[1]
