# Pepin the Short

Pepin the Short was the Frankish king who founded the Carolingian Dynasty, ruling from 751 to 768, and the father of [[charlemagne|Charlemagne]].[1]

In 751 the Umayyad Caliphate in Spain lost the territory of [[septimania|Septimania]] to Pepin's new dynasty.[1] Rather than exploit the Jewish-versus-Christian-versus-Muslim conflicts that the article says the oligarchy would have preferred, Pepin allied with Septimania's large Jewish and Muslim population against the intrigues of Venice.[1] He invited a Jewish leader from Baghdad descended from the House of David, Natronai al Makhir (725–765), to become king of Septimania, and gave his own daughter Alda to Makhir in marriage; al Makhir in turn gave his daughter in marriage to Charlemagne as part of a diplomatic flank against the war party in Rome.[1]

Both Pepin and Charlemagne ignored the Vatican's repeated demands that they renounce this ecumenical program.[1] When Pepin's father-and-son line established diplomatic embassies with the Muslim [[abbasid-dynasty|Abbasids]], the envoys selected were always Jewish.[1] The article credits Pepin, alongside Charlemagne, with bold reforms in infrastructure — roads, bridges over the Rhine, canals, cathedrals and schools — the Irish monastery movement, and financial reforms that stripped private financiers of control over coinage and credit.[1]
