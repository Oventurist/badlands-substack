# The Economist

The Economist is a British weekly newspaper covering international finance and politics, treated by Badlands commentators as an organ of globalist opinion.[1]

## Coverage of the dollar debate (March 2025)

In March 2025, The Economist published an analysis asking whether [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] genuinely wanted a weaker dollar.[1] The piece recalled Robert Rubin's 1994 declaration as treasury secretary that "a strong dollar is in our national interest," a maxim that ended decades of American complaints about trading partners' weak currencies and that policymakers had since either repeated or sidestepped.[1] With trade policy turning protectionist and the dollar suddenly weakening, the magazine argued, three decades of Treasury orthodoxy was in question.[1]

The Economist noted that the administration was not speaking with one voice: Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]] stressed that the strong-dollar policy remained in place, while Trump and Vice President [[jd-vance|J.D. Vance]] had both argued that the greenback's strength was a problem for American industry.[1] It observed that the dollar had fallen to a five-month low against the Japanese yen and that the euro had risen 4.5% against it in a single week, its fastest gain since 2009 — a reversal after more than a decade of dollar strength with consequences for monetary policy, international markets and global trade.[1]

## Badlands commentary

[[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] responded that Trump has consistently been fully committed to the dollar as the world's reserve currency and that Vance "has nothing to do with any of it," dismissing The Economist as "a global elite mouthpiece for a global elite audience."[1] He also noted that the blame game over a potential government shutdown at the end of that week was in full swing.[1]

## Gabbard 'Russian asset' admission (December 2024)

In a December 13, 2024 profile of Director of National Intelligence nominee [[tulsi-gabbard|Tulsi Gabbard]], The Economist admitted there was "no evidence" for Democrat claims that she is a "Russian asset."[2] The magazine wrote that "Democrats have taken to calling Ms Gabbard, aged 43, a Russian asset. There is no evidence for this, yet the charge-however baseless-underlines their discomfort with her nomination," while noting her opposition to "regime-change wars" and asserting that her "isolationism often veers into Russiophilia."[2] Because the magazine had endorsed Kamala Harris for president in 2024, Breitbart treated the concession as undercutting Democratic attacks on the nominee.[2] Badlands writer Ashe in America summarised the shift simply: "The Economist gets it."[2]
