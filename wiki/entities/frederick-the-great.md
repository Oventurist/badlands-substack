# Frederick the Great

**Frederick the Great** was the 18th-century king of [[prussia|Prussia]] whose military doctrine is treated in Badlands analysis as one of the two Prussian roots of [[blitzkrieg]].[1]

## Preventive war doctrine

In the latter 1700s Frederick adopted what became known as [[preventive-war|preventive war]] doctrine: preemptively striking a perceived enemy before that enemy reaches full potential and gains the ability to strike.[1] The method was to "bloody their nose" with multiple simultaneous attacks intended to disorient and overwhelm the opposition and prevent it from mounting a defense, with the theoretical aim of forcing a negotiated surrender rather than annihilation through a prolonged campaign.[1]

## Later reversal

In his old age Frederick soured on the concept, famously remarking that "preventive war is like suicide for fear of death."[1] Badlands commentary notes the irony that the aging Frederick came to see statecraft and virtuous leaders avoiding brash military action as the crux of achieving peace and prosperity, while later analysts compared President George W. Bush's invasion of Iraq to the younger Frederick's doctrine.[1] The Nazis fused Frederick's preventive-war logic with [[helmuth-von-moltke|Helmuth von Moltke]]'s [[maneuver-warfare|maneuver warfare]] to create blitzkrieg, ignoring Frederick's later wisdom at their own peril.[1]
