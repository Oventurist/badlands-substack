# Deadspin

Deadspin is an online sports magazine that became the defendant in a Delaware defamation suit after publishing an article accusing a nine-year-old Kansas City Chiefs fan of wearing "blackface" to a football game.[1]

## The Holden Armenta article

On November 27, 2023, the day after a Chiefs–Las Vegas Raiders game, Deadspin writer Carron Phillips published an article calling out the attire of a young fan who had painted his face black and red — two of the colors in the Chiefs logo — and worn a Native American headdress.[1] The featured image accompanying the piece showed only the side of the child's face that was covered in black paint.[1] The boy, Holden Armenta, is of Chumash Indian descent, and his parents Raul Armenta Jr. and Shannon Armenta sued on behalf of themselves and their son.[1]

## Litigation

Deadspin argued that the article was opinion and thus shielded from defamation liability, but in an October 7, 2024 ruling Judge Sean Lugg of the Superior Court of Delaware rejected that defense and refused to dismiss the case.[1] Lugg wrote that Deadspin "published an image of a child displaying his passionate fandom as a backdrop for its critique of the NFL's diversity efforts and, in its description of the child, crossed the fine line protecting its speech from defamation claims."[1] The case is documented at [[armenta-v-deadspin|Armenta v. Deadspin]].[1]

## Contemporaneous coverage of the 2023 article

At the time of publication in late November 2023, the Deadspin piece by [[carron-j-phillips|Carron J. Phillips]] demanded that the [[nfl|NFL]] "speak out" against a [[kansas-city-chiefs|Kansas City Chiefs]] child fan who wore a Native American–style headdress with his face painted black and red, accusing the roughly nine- or ten-year-old of displaying "black face" using an image that hid the red-painted half of his face.[2] When challenged, Phillips doubled down and claimed the red paint "made it worse," despite red and black being the team's colors.[2] Reporting later that day indicated the child was Native American and the son of a tribal board member.[2] [[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] called the episode a fabricated race-baiting narrative and said Phillips should lose his job and Deadspin its audience.[2]

## Retraction demand and Clare Locke involvement

In early December 2023 the parents of the child, Shannon and Raul Armenta, retained the defamation firm [[clare-locke|Clare Locke LLP]] to demand a retraction from Deadspin and threatened to sue the outlet, publishers G/O Media and Great Hill Partners, and reporter [[carron-phillips|Carron Phillips]].[3] Coverage noted that Clare Locke had previously won a $787.5 million settlement against Fox News on behalf of [[dominion-voting-systems|Dominion Voting Systems]], an outcome that also preceded Tucker Carlson's ouster from the network.[3] The child at the center of the dispute, [[holden-armenta|Holden Armenta]], was nine years old at the time.[3]
