# Richard Mosley

Richard Mosley is a Justice of the Federal Court of Canada who, on January 23, 2024, issued the ruling striking down the [[justin-trudeau|Trudeau]] government's invocation of the [[emergencies-act|Emergencies Act]] against the 2022 [[freedom-convoy|Freedom Convoy]].[1]

Mosley held that the government's use of the act did "not bear the hallmarks of reasonableness—justification, transparency and intelligibility—and was not justified in relation to the relevant factual and legal constraints that were required to be taken into consideration."[1]

The decision was received by convoy plaintiffs as the opening of a second phase of litigation against the federal government, banks, and police.[1] Alberta contractor and convoy participant Jeremiah Jost praised "Justice Mosley's ruling and his courage to put his neck out," saying it gave hope to Canadians disillusioned with the justice system.[1] Badlands writer [[simon-esler|Simon Esler]] treated the ruling as the most precedent-setting Canadian legal development of the period and as evidence that parts of the legal domain remain uncompromised.[1]

Contemporaneous reporting on the lengthy ruling noted that Mosley found the convoy protests of early 2022, while causing harm to Canada's economy, trade and commerce, did not rise to the level of a threat to national security as defined by the statute.[2] He sided with the civil liberties groups that had argued the Liberal government exceeded its powers and violated the Charter in its historic February 2022 invocation, writing that the decision to issue the proclamation "does not bear the hallmarks of reasonableness – justification, transparency and intelligibility – and was not justified."[2] On the same day the ruling was released, Deputy Prime Minister [[chrystia-freeland|Chrystia Freeland]] stated that the government disagreed with Mosley's decision and promised to appeal it.[2]
