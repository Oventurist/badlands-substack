# Stuart W. Bowen Jr.

Stuart W. Bowen Jr. is an American attorney who served from 2004 as Special Inspector General for Iraq Reconstruction, appointed to investigate corruption and waste in Iraq. A Texas friend of President [[george-w-bush|George W. Bush]], he is presented in the Badlands Media article "[[bad-actors|Bad Actors]]" as a rare figure of integrity within an otherwise compromised system.

## The missing Iraqi billions

Following the 2003 defeat of Saddam Hussein's government, caravans of trucks delivered pallets of shrink-wrapped $100 bills to Andrews Air Force Base for airlift aboard Air Force C-17 transports to Baghdad, in cash withdrawn from Iraqi government accounts held in the United States. Over the following eighteen months $12 billion to $14 billion was airlifted and a further $5 billion sent by electronic transfer. What became of the money after arrival in Baghdad remained one of the major unanswered questions of the occupation.

Bowen's pursuit of the answer became, in the Boston Globe's description, first his job and then his obsession. He concluded that much of the money was probably used by the Iraqi government in some way, but for years could not account for billions more until his investigators discovered that $1.2 billion to $1.6 billion had been stolen and moved to a bunker in rural [[lebanon|Lebanon]] for safekeeping. "I don't know how the money got to Lebanon," Bowen said. "If I knew that, we would have made more progress on the case."

## Brick Tracker and its obstruction

Bowen kept the bunker discovery and the associated investigation, code-named [[brick-tracker|Brick Tracker]], secret until interviewed by the Boston Globe. He said "billions of dollars have been taken out of Iraq over the last 10 years illegally" and expressed personal disappointment that the case could not be closed "for reasons beyond our control." He was equally frustrated that the Bush administration never investigated reports of the missing money apart from his own office, and that the Obama administration did not pursue the bunker lead after his investigators briefed the [[cia|CIA]] and the [[fbi|FBI]]. He believed one reason American officials declined to act was "because it was Iraqi money stolen by Iraqis."

Reporting cited in the article records that Bowen met resistance from the U.S. embassy in Beirut, was not permitted to travel to Lebanon himself, and that two investigators who did travel were denied embassy permission to view the bunker; Lebanon's prosecutor general initially agreed to cooperate before deciding against it. Bowen's office was shut down in 2013. The Badlands Media article argues Bush installed his friend expecting the story to disappear amid congressional scrutiny, but that Bowen "was actually a man of character, and put his country over his friendship."
