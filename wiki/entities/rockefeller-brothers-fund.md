# Rockefeller Brothers Fund

The Rockefeller Brothers Fund is a tax-exempt American philanthropic foundation identified in Badlands Media coverage as one of the funders of non-governmental organizations that helped coordinate pro-Palestine campus protests in the United States.[1]

## Role in the civil society network

Badlands contributor [[ryan-delarme|Ryan DeLarme]] grouped the Rockefeller Brothers Fund with [[george-soros|George Soros]]' [[open-society-foundations|Open Society Foundations]] and the [[tides-foundation|Tides Foundation]] as backers of [[jewish-voice-for-peace|Jewish Voice for Peace]] and comparable organizations.[1] The article places such foundations inside what it defines as "civil society" — the "third sector" distinct from government and business, consisting of NGOs, tax-exempt foundations, think tanks and the generational wealth of families that retain influence over the levers of power.[1]

## Relationship to the censorship apparatus

The article argues that the same civil society and intelligence-linked interests that fund protest organizing also underwrite the [[censorship-industrial-complex|censorship-industrial complex]], a network that spans government agencies, the private sector and tax-exempt foundations rather than existing as a single institution.[1] In this framing, foundation money functions as a cut-out mechanism allowing state-aligned objectives to be pursued at arm's length.[1]

## Incorporation and grantmaking

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 5" states that the Fund was incorporated in 1940 by the five sons of [[john-d-rockefeller-jr|John D. Rockefeller Jr.]] and has since dispersed hundreds of millions in grants to causes and institutions including the Smithsonian Institute, Planned Parenthood, the NAACP, Yale University, the Brookings Institution, the National Audubon Society, the Society for International Development and the National Academy of Sciences.[2]

In 1977 the Fund contributed $1 million to the [[council-on-foreign-relations|Council on Foreign Relations]]; by 1997 — perhaps because of unwanted publicity from conspiracy writers, the article suggests — that figure had fallen to $45,000, of which $25,000 went to study the "economic and political implications of Korean unification."[2] The [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]], which received $120,000 from the Fund in 1977, went unmentioned in its 1997 annual report.[2]

## Environmental grants

The Fund is described as especially active on environmental issues, donating to the National Environmental Trust, Greenpeace Environmental Trust, the National Wildlife Federation, the American Conservation Association and the Environmental Defense Fund among others.[2] Conspiracy authors quoted in the article ask what better way there would be for an owner of environmentally damaging companies to gain a measure of control over activists than through hefty contributions.[2]

## Funding the Trilateral Commission

Badlands Media's "The Men Behind the Curtain – Part 2" identifies the Rockefeller Brothers Fund as a principal financial backer of the [[trilateral-commission|Trilateral Commission]].[3] Although spokesmen insisted the Commission received no government funding, a 1978 report showed funding of $1,180,000 from mid-1976 to mid-1979, much of it from tax-exempt foundations such as the Fund, which in 1977 alone put up $120,000.[3] Other donors included the Ford Foundation, the Lilly Endowment, the German Marshall Fund, and corporations such as Time, Bechtel, Exxon, General Motors, Wells Fargo and Texas Instruments.[3]
