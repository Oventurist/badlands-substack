# Jay Cooke

**Jay Cooke** (1822–1905) was an American pro-industrial financier and Philadelphia banker who served as an ally of President [[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]] during the [[american-civil-war|Civil War]].[1]

Cooke organized the sale of the [[five-twenty-bonds|5:20 bonds]], the government securities named for their five-to-twenty-year maturation, which were sold in small denominations to average citizens so that ordinary Americans would have a vested interest in directly participating in saving their nation.[1] Between 1862 and 1865, these bonds accounted for $1.3 billion in financing.[1] The bonds provided loans to manufacturing and also served as the basis for the issuance of more [[greenbacks|greenbacks]], forming a core part of Lincoln's revival of the [[american-system-of-political-economy|American System]] of political economy.[1]

In Badlands Media contributor [[matthew-ehret|Matthew Ehret]]'s telling, Cooke belongs to the patriotic, industry-oriented banking tradition that stood opposed to the Wall Street financiers and anglophile families who worked to sabotage Lincoln's ability to acquire the funds necessary to execute the war.[1]
