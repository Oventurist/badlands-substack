# Israeli Settler Movement

The Israeli Settler Movement is the organized political and religious current promoting Jewish settlement in territories occupied by [[israel|Israel]].[1]

Badlands coverage in April 2026 grouped settler violence with the conduct of the [[israel-defense-forces|Israel Defense Forces]] under the heading of [[jewish-terrorism|Jewish terrorism]], noting that Israeli officials routinely issue condemnations when such acts occur while, in the Badlands assessment, nothing ever happens to those responsible.[1] [[pinchas-wallerstein|Pinchas Wallerstein]], a founder of the modern movement, told [[haaretz|Haaretz]] he was horrified by the rise of such violence and called it treason against the State of Israel.[1]

Finance Minister [[bezalel-smotrich|Bezalel Smotrich]] renewed calls in the same period for Israel to occupy the Gaza Strip and establish settlements there, arguing the war should conclude with territorial expansion.[1]

## Detention of a US congressional delegation (July 2026)

On July 8, 2026, armed Israeli settlers blocked a delegation led by U.S. Representative [[ro-khanna|Ro Khanna]] near the Palestinian village of Khirbet Zanuta during a three-day visit to the occupied West Bank.[2] Photographs supplied to the Associated Press show armed men blocking the delegation's vehicles, and Khanna said the men carried M4 rifles and prevented the group from leaving.[2] Delegation member [[cameron-kasky|Cameron Kasky]] said the group was held for more than an hour and appealed to the U.S. Embassy in Jerusalem before apparent police officers intervened, while the Israeli military said its troops dispersed the civilians and reopened the road and denied that soldiers took part in the blockade — an account Khanna disputed.[2]

[[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] wrote that the settlers "are getting more bold," describing them as zealots who cannot be reasoned with, negotiated with, or intimidated into abandoning their cause, and who will fight anybody who tries to stop them — including the United States.[2] He argued that while the settlers may reflect only a minority of Israeli culture, they operate inside a highly insular society that protects its own, and that the United States created and fostered the movement from its infancy: never telling it "no," never denying it an indulgence, granting it every luxury and complete security, and supplying it with weaponry.[2] He characterized the result as "a society of spoiled rotten children" unleashed upon the world, with no intention of listening to or negotiating with the United States because they believe they are on a mission from God.[2]
