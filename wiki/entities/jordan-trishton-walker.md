# Jordan Trishton Walker

Jordan Trishton Walker — also rendered in contemporaneous coverage as "Jordon Walker" — is the individual identified in a January 2023 undercover video published by [[project-veritas|Project Veritas]] as a [[pfizer|Pfizer]] executive discussing an internal "directed evolution" program said to be aimed at anticipating future strains of COVID-19.[1] The recording, and the reaction to it, became one of the defining media-criticism stories of that news cycle for the alternative press.[1]

## The Project Veritas video

In the undercover footage, the figure identified as Walker purportedly described a plan to mutate the virus in-house in order to keep COVID-19 a "cash cow" for the company.[1] When [[james-okeefe|James O'Keefe]], the founder of Project Veritas, confronted him on camera, Walker reversed course, calling himself a "liar" who had been "trying to impress a person on a date."[1] According to the account carried by [[just-the-news|Just the News]] and summarised by [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]], Walker also invoked race in requesting police involvement and physically attacked O'Keefe and his film crew during the confrontation.[1]

## Media and platform response

The episode drew what the reporting described as intense curiosity from members of Congress alongside intermittent Big Tech throttling and a partial media blackout.[1] Most conspicuously, a prominent tabloid — identified in the Badlands commentary as the [[daily-mail|Daily Mail]] — removed its article about the sting without a clear explanation.[1]

Badlands contributor [[justin-deschamps|Justin Deschamps]] argued that the muted institutional response indicated the video had caught Big Tech and pharmaceutical interests off guard, and that the rebranding of [[gain-of-function-research|gain-of-function research]] as "[[directed-evolution|directed evolution]]" had not been intended for public exposure at that stage.[1] Deschamps framed the reaction as a textbook case of narrative management — coordinated spin delivered through talking heads and influencers in the mould of [[operation-mockingbird|Operation Mockingbird]] — and cited both the [[asch-conformity-experiments|Asch conformity experiments]] and the disclosures of Google whistleblower [[zack-vorhies|Zack Vorhies]] as evidence that public belief can be shaped through search ranking and article placement.[1]
