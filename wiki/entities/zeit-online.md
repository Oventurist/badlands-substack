# Zeit Online

Zeit Online is the digital edition of the German weekly newspaper Die Zeit. In March 2023 it became a central participant in the European investigative reporting effort into the sabotage of the Nord Stream natural gas pipelines, publishing findings jointly developed with the ARD-Hauptstadtstudio and SWR broadcasters.[1]

## Nord Stream investigation

Zeit Online's reporting, released in parallel with the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] account of new U.S. intelligence, stated that the outlets had identified the boat allegedly used in the covert operation against the pipelines: a yacht rented by a company based in Poland that reportedly belonged to two Ukrainians.[1] According to the investigation, a team of six people — five men and one woman, comprising a captain, two divers, two diving assistants and a doctor — transported the explosives to the sites and placed them.[1] The nationality of the perpetrators was described as unclear, and the team was said to have used professionally produced fake passports, including for the boat rental.[1]

The reporting placed the departure in September 2022 from Rostock, with equipment brought to the port by van, and traced the vessel afterwards to Wieck on the Darß peninsula and later to the Danish island of Christiansø, northeast of Bornholm.[1] The yacht was returned to its owner in an uncleaned condition, and investigators reportedly found traces of explosives on a table in the cabin.[1] Zeit Online and its broadcast partners further reported that a Western intelligence service had, shortly after the destruction of the pipelines in autumn 2022, sent a note to European partner services attributing responsibility to a Ukrainian command.[1]

## Reception in alternative media

Commentators in the Badlands Media orbit treated the Zeit Online piece as unusually specific compared to the American reporting, noting that it identified where, when, and with what vessel the operation was allegedly carried out, and that it rested on an investigation drawing on entities and countries both inside and outside Europe.[1] The Badlands contributor [[just-human|Just Human]] argued that overlapping the Zeit Online account with the [[seymour-hersh|Seymour Hersh]] Substack report, the New York Times article, and open-source intelligence would move readers closer to the truth of the [[nord-stream-sabotage|Nord Stream sabotage]], while conceding the German account could also be false.[1]
