# Harun al-Rashid

Harun al-Rashid was the Caliph of the Abbasid Dynasty of Baghdad, ruling from 786 to 809 CE. In Matthew Ehret's telling, he is presented as a "co-thinker" of [[charlemagne|Charlemagne]] and one of four great ecumenical leaders of the era, alongside Charlemagne, King Bulan of [[khazaria|Khazaria]] and the Chinese Emperor Xuanzong.[1]

## Ecumenical diplomacy

The article argues that the alliances Harun al-Rashid oversaw with the Carolingian Empire were precisely what the Venetian Empire and the Ultramontane Church hated, since those alliances cut against a primitive "clash of civilizations" doctrine of divide-and-rule.[1] In 800 CE, Harun al-Rashid gave control of the Holy Land to Charlemagne, declaring that the Christian leader's land would be protected by Muslim rule — a Muslim-Christian agreement described as an act of creative war avoidance and win-win collaboration.[1] According to the records of the Monk Zacharias, this diplomatic entente was negotiated by Charlemagne's Jewish Ambassador to Baghdad, Isaac of Rachen.[1]

## The Abbasid Renaissance

Harun al-Rashid was the son of Caliph al-Mahdi and the father of al-Mamun. Under the leadership of these three rulers, networks of humanist education centers known as [[houses-of-wisdom|Houses of Wisdom]] were created, bringing Muslim, Christian and Jewish scholars together to translate ancient Greek and Latin works and to study astronomy, literature, medicine and engineering.[1] The article describes the governing philosophy shared by Harun al-Rashid and Charlemagne as an anti-oligarchical concept in which a leader's right to rule was valid only through obedience to the laws of nature and the common good — an Islamic parallel to the Confucian Mandate of Heaven.[1]
