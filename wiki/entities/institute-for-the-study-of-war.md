# Institute for the Study of War

The Institute for the Study of War (ISW) is a Washington-based think tank that publishes battlefield assessments and conflict analysis, and is routinely cited by American media as an independent monitor of the war in Ukraine.[1]

## Role in Ukraine war coverage

In May 2025, NBC News cited ISW among the independent monitors reporting that Russian forces under [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] were continuing to make slow but steady battlefield advances in eastern Ukraine — context offered for the assessment that Putin had shown little appetite for any ceasefire falling short of his maximalist goals.[1] That assessment framed reporting on the two-hour telephone call between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] and Putin that produced warm personal language but no breakthrough toward ending the war.[1]

## Badlands framing

The May 21, 2025 [[badlands-news-brief|Badlands News Brief]] reproduced the NBC report, including its reliance on ISW, under commentary from [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]], who argued that "the enemy matrix runs almost entirely on the basis of reality construction through narrative" and that such reporting can be reverse-engineered toward something closer to the truth.[1] Burning Bright added that anti-Russia propaganda had been his own "gateway into the Info War."[1] In that framing, institutional conflict monitors like ISW are treated not as neutral referees but as narrative infrastructure supporting a preferred storyline about the war.[1]

## The O'Bagy dismissal

The Badlands Media feature "Transparency and Prosecution" recounts the institute's role in the 2013 Syria intervention debate through its former analyst [[elizabeth-obagy|Elizabeth O'Bagy]].[2] Days after O'Bagy's Wall Street Journal op-ed was cited by Secretary of State [[john-kerry|John Kerry]] and Senator [[john-mccain|John McCain]] in congressional hearings on possible U.S. military action in Syria, the institute posted an online statement announcing that it had "learned and confirmed that, contrary to her representations, Ms. Elizabeth O'Bagy does not in fact have a Ph.D. degree from [[georgetown-university|Georgetown University]]," and that it had terminated her employment effective immediately.[2]

O'Bagy had received a Master of Arts in Arab Studies from Georgetown that May and was reportedly working on a doctoral dissertation.[2] The article notes that she had appeared as an analyst on BBC, CNN, Fox and PBS, and that CNN said she would not appear on its networks again.[2] The author interprets the timing of the institute's disclosure as a deliberately timed intervention that helped derail the congressional push to commit U.S. forces against [[bashar-al-assad|Bashar al-Assad]].[2]
