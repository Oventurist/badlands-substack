# District of Columbia National Guard

The District of Columbia National Guard is the National Guard component of the US capital district. Unlike state National Guard organizations, which report to their governors, the DC National Guard reports directly to the President of the United States.[1]

## Activation of the 260th Special Purpose Brigade

In March 2026, the DC National Guard activated the [[260th-special-purpose-brigade|260th Special Purpose Brigade]], creating the Army's only brigade dedicated to coordinating military support to civil authorities and protecting critical infrastructure in the nation's capital.[1] The brigade was formed following [[executive-order-14339|Executive Order 14339]] and a directive from the [[department-of-war|Department of War]] calling for a trained, equipped National Guard unit to support public safety and integrate with federal and local security partners.[1]

Brig. Gen. [[craig-maceri|Craig Maceri]] said the brigade provides a command structure to manage a wide range of missions while ensuring rapid coordination with federal and local agencies during major events, emergencies, and security operations.[1] One of its primary partners is the [[us-marshals-service|US Marshals Service]], which regularly works alongside the Guard in the capital.[1]

## DC lawsuit over the 2025 deployment

In September 2025, Washington, D.C. officials sued the [[donald-trump|Trump]] administration over the deployment of thousands of [[national-guard|National Guard]] troops into the city without the consent of local leaders, accusing the president of violating the Constitution and federal law.[2] The lawsuit, filed by Attorney General [[brian-schwalb|Brian Schwalb]], claimed that the troops — many from out of state — had been deputized by the [[us-marshals-service|US Marshals]] office and were patrolling neighborhoods, conducting searches and making arrests, despite federal laws that generally bar the military from acting as local police.[2]

The suit argued that the deployment undermined the city's autonomy, eroded trust between residents and law enforcement, and damaged the local economy by discouraging tourism and hurting businesses.[2] Schwalb said the deployment was "not only unnecessary and unwanted, but it is also dangerous and harmful to the District and its residents," warning that "It's DC today but could be any other city tomorrow."[2] Badlands contributor [[chris-paul|Chris Paul]] welcomed the filing as a chance to settle the question of the federal city's status.[2]
