# Rishi Sunak

**Rishi Sunak** served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom in **2023**, during the passage of the **Online Safety Bill** and the simultaneous targeting of **Russell Brand** over sexual abuse allegations. In the Badlands Media article *5GW Counter-Offensive – The Censorship Regime and the Road to 2024* (September 27, 2023), Sunak is framed as a politically self-interested actor exploiting the Brand allegations to advance a censorship agenda.

## Public Statement on Russell Brand Allegations

The Badlands source reports that Sunak issued a public statement on the Brand allegations through his spokesman, **Max Blain**, who said the claims were *"very serious and concerning, and those making the allegations should be treated seriously and treated with sensitivity."* The article notes that this intervention was notable because "these unproven allegations are so important that the current Prime Minister is taking the time to address them." DeLarme questions Sunak's motivation: *"Do you think the British PM is acting out of compassion for these women or out of self-interest?"* — noting that Brand is "one of Rishi Sunak's biggest critics."

## Legislative Context

The source emphasizes that Sunak's government was the same government that had just passed the Online Safety Bill, which DeLarme characterizes as *"that atrocity of a bill"* and a mechanism for locking down the internet in the UK. The article frames the government's request to big-tech platforms to censor Brand's content as a direct practical application of the OSB's provisions, coordinated with the **Trusted News Initiative**.

## Relationship to Censorship Framework

Within the Badlands corpus, Sunak is positioned as a key node in the transatlantic censorship regime that activates during election cycles. The article connects his government's actions to broader patterns of targeting dissident communicators, drawing parallels to previous alleged smear campaigns against **Julian Assange**, **Matt Gaetz**, and **Donald Trump**. The source argues that Sunak's intervention in the Brand case illustrates the pattern whereby political leaders protect establishment interests by amplifying unproven allegations against effective critics.
