# Euroclear

**Euroclear** is the Belgium-based securities depository where most of the [[european-union|European Union]]-held immobilized Russian central bank assets are held.[1]

## Role in the Russian asset debate

In early October 2025, EU leaders convened in Copenhagen for an informal summit to discuss a plan to provide Ukraine with a €140 billion ($164 billion) loan backed by Russia's immobilized central bank assets.[1] Because Euroclear hosts the bulk of those assets, Belgium's exposure to the legal risks of the scheme was central to the debate.[1]

Belgium's Prime Minister [[bart-de-wever|Bart De Wever]] cautioned his colleagues that "there's no free money. There are always consequences," and said, "I want their signature saying, if we take [Russian President Vladimir] Putin's money… we're all going to be responsible if it goes wrong."[1] Luxembourg's prime minister, Luc Frieden, voiced similar apprehensions.[1]

The [[kremlin|Kremlin]] characterized the deliberations harshly: spokesman [[dmitry-peskov|Dmitry Peskov]] said EU countries debating ways they could "steal" Russian assets were behaving like a criminal "gang," likening the bloc to a group of mobsters planning their next heist.[1]

## Role in the frozen Russian assets dispute

Western nations froze an estimated $300 billion in Russian assets after the escalation of the Ukraine conflict in February 2022, and roughly €200 billion ($213 billion) of that total is held by Euroclear in Brussels.[2] Ukraine's Western backers have already used the revenues generated by those holdings to finance Kyiv.[2] By October 2025 the [[european-union|European Union]] was weighing a "reparations loan" of up to €140 billion ($163 billion) using the [[frozen-russian-assets|frozen Russian assets]] as collateral for bloc-issued bonds — an arrangement that would effectively amount to seizure, since Ukraine would repay only once Russia compensated it for war damages.[2] The United States declined to join the plan, citing risks to market stability.[2]
