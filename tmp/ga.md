## The 1960s precedent and the present awakening

Badlands Media's February 2023 essay "Heroes of the Great Awakening," by [[sean-morgan|Sean Morgan]], argues that the term describes a recurring rather than unique phenomenon: "in the 1960's, there was a 'Great Awakening' happening, not unlike what we're seeing in the United States today."[n] In that earlier period, the author writes, "the American people were waking up to the sham that was the [[military-industrial-complex|Military Industrial Complex]] and more," a process he says was interrupted by the killings of the Kennedys, [[martin-luther-king-jr|Martin Luther King Jr.]], [[john-lennon|John Lennon]] and [[bob-marley|Bob Marley]].[n]

The present awakening is described as materially different in one respect: technology. The author asks readers to "imagine the 1960's anti-war movement with smart phones," noting that "the technological tools that the enemy attempts to wield against us are also in the palm of our hands."[n] Participation is defined broadly — "if you're reading this now … you're part of the true resistance. The true peaceful revolution."[n]

The essay characterises the awakening as a nonviolent contest in which "our 'bullets' take the form of articles, songs and videos," waged on platforms such as [[rumble|Rumble]] and [[substack|Substack]].[n] It frames the outcome as assured on anthropological grounds: "freedom is part of the human spirit and tyranny is instantly recognized as a constrictive and parasitic force," so "as long as the choice is presented, people will choose freedom every time."[n] Crucially, the author insists the awakening cannot be delegated to a leader — "it was not one man who beat fascism in the 1940's, and it will not be one man in the current epoch either" — and that victory will come because ordinary people "carried on the spirit and legacy of figures like JFK."[n]
