## September 2023 COVID-19 vaccine authorization

On September 11, 2023 the FDA authorized updated [[covid-19-vaccines|COVID-19 vaccines]] from [[moderna|Moderna]] and [[pfizer|Pfizer]] in a bid to counter the poor effectiveness of the existing shots, approving them for people aged 12 and older and granting emergency authorization down to six months of age.[{n}] The shots targeted the XBB.1.5 Omicron subvariant, which the [[centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention|CDC]] reported had already been largely displaced by newer strains such as EG.5; no mention was made of [[novavax|Novavax]]'s U.S.-available vaccine.[{n}] Agency official [[peter-marks|Peter Marks]] said vaccination "remains critical to public health" and encouraged eligible people to get vaccinated.[{n}] Critics at [[badlands-media|Badlands Media]] argued the clearance was issued without clinical trial data and predicted renewed mandates.[{n}] See [[2023-fda-authorization-of-updated-covid-19-vaccines]].[{n}]
