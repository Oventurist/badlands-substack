## Investigation of Americans tied to Russian state media (2024)

In August 2024 the [[new-york-times|New York Times]] reported that Americans with ties to Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]]'s state TV networks were under investigation by Attorney General [[merrick-garland|Merrick Garland]]'s Department of Justice, as part of a sweeping effort to quash potential Kremlin interference ahead of the 2024 presidential election.[n] The development followed [[fbi|FBI]] searches earlier that month of the homes of [[scott-ritter|Scott Ritter]] — a former [[united-nations|United Nations]] Special Commission weapons inspector, U.S. Marine Corps intelligence officer and convicted sex offender — and of Russian-born [[dimitri-simes|Dimitri Simes]], a former adviser to [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s 2016 campaign.[n] U.S. officials familiar with the matter said the FBI was likely to conduct further searches soon and did not rule out criminal charges.[n]

Badlands commentary treated the probe as an attempt to criminalize dissenting commentary, asking whether the Department would label writers Russian propagandists merely for posting videos of Putin, and connecting the effort to its own coverage of Russian claims about [[organ-trafficking|organ trafficking]] and to [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]]'s "Righteous Russia" thesis.[n]
