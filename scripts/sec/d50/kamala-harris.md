## 2024 Democratic National Convention acceptance speech

In her August 22, 2024 acceptance speech at the [[2024-democratic-national-convention|Democratic National Convention]] in Chicago, Harris framed the election as a "fight for America's future" and painted a second [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] presidency as chaotic and dangerous, arguing that Trump would take the country backward while she would take it forward.[n] She pledged to be "a president who leads and listens, who is realistic, practical, and has common sense, and always fights for the American people."[n]

[[the-atlantic|The Atlantic]] observed that Harris offered comparatively few policy specifics, mentioning that she would push through the recently derailed bipartisan immigration bill but otherwise gesturing at large goals such as ending the housing shortage or affirming general commitments such as support for [[nato|NATO]].[n] According to campaign advisers and Democratic officials cited in the piece, the campaign's purpose was to lay out a vision, convince voters Harris was on their side, and reach 270 electoral votes; the reporter noted hearing more about Trump's policy agenda — particularly the [[project-2025|Project 2025]] transition blueprint produced by the [[heritage-foundation|Heritage Foundation]], which Democrats repeatedly brandished on stage and which Harris herself invoked — than about Harris's own.[n] Harris had no Project 2025 equivalent, and Democrats appeared at peace with that.[n] [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] noted an editorial oddity: the original title of the Atlantic piece had been "Kamala Harris Wants to Be Everything to Everyone" before being changed.[n]

[[ashe-in-america|Ashe in America]] characterized the event as an "astroturfed joy campaign" reaching a fever pitch, describing the speech as an awkward word salad in which Harris attempted to project inclusive community while, in Badlands' framing, advocating communism and repeating debunked claims about her political adversaries.[n] The commentary argued that Harris invoked the civil rights movement to distract from her record as a California prosecutor, urged audiences to "just imagine Donald Trump with no guardrails," and claimed to have fought cartels on border security despite Badlands' contention that the evidence pointed the other way.[n] Badlands also charged that she defended an abysmal record by rewriting it and treated big government as a virtue.[n]

In the same period, Trump attacked Harris's record on crime, accusing her of an "anti-police crusade" and of supporting "extremist, pro-crime, anti-police" policies such as defunding the police, and blaming her for a crime spike after he left office.[n] Badlands commentary on the anti-DEI corporate backlash further argued that Harris was "openly bragging about expanding global communism" while Trump represented American interests.[n]
