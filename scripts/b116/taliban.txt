## Post-withdrawal aid flows (2021–2023)

A report issued in early November 2023 by [[john-sopko|John Sopko]] of the [[special-inspector-general-for-afghanistan-reconstruction|Special Inspector General for Afghanistan Reconstruction]] found that Afghanistan had received roughly $11 billion in U.S. foreign aid since the country's collapse in August 2021, delivered in part as cash shipments of about $80 million every 10 to 14 days since the Taliban's takeover.[N] The [[united-nations|United Nations]] assured Sopko that the money sat in designated U.N. accounts at a private bank and was not deposited in the central bank or provided to the Taliban.[N] The report nevertheless noted that the Taliban had stolen foreign aid before and had prevented the poorest members of the population from receiving aid designated for them, using methods including siphoning cash from U.N. shipments, collecting royalties, and charging fees on cash shipments.[N]

[[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] argued that after billions of dollars of U.S. military equipment were left in Kabul, the Taliban used that equipment to hunt down and largely eradicate what remained of ISIS in the country — behaviour he read as consistent with the original structure of the [[doha-agreement|Doha Agreement]] signed under President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], and as evidence for [[devolution|Devolution]] and "Sovereign Alliance–Regions of Responsibility" theories.[N]
