## Appropriations and the November 2023 shutdown deadline

Congress must pass twelve appropriation bills to fund the government through its fiscal year; when it fails to do so it relies on stopgap [[continuing-resolution|continuing resolutions]].[N] In early November 2023, with funding due to expire on November 17, Congress faced another shutdown deadline while Republicans controlling the [[us-house-of-representatives|House]] showed no sign of agreement on a funding plan.[N] Speaker [[mike-johnson|Mike Johnson]], the top Republican in Congress, said members wanted to avoid a shutdown because "It's a dangerous time around the world right now."[N] House Republicans simultaneously worried about being forced to accept a competing measure from the Democratic-led [[us-senate|Senate]].[N]

Congress also featured in the same period's fight over Israel policy, including a 2021 bill to replenish Israel's [[iron-dome|Iron Dome]] missile defense system and an October 2023 resolution to stand with Israel — both of which Rep. [[rashida-tlaib|Rashida Tlaib]] voted against, drawing a six-figure ad campaign from [[democratic-majority-for-israel|Democratic Majority for Israel]].[N]
