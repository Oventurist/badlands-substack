## Israeli non-signatory status (2023 controversy)

Israel's refusal to sign the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty returned to prominence in November 2023 after Israeli Minister of Heritage [[amihai-eliyahu|Amihai Eliyahu]] said that dropping a nuclear bomb on the Gaza Strip was "one of the possibilities."[N] Because Israel has never openly tested its nuclear weapons nor signed the NPT, it is described as the world's only unacknowledged atomic power, and has never been subjected to an inspection by the U.N. nuclear watchdog.[N] Russian foreign ministry spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]] used Eliyahu's remark to ask whether it amounted to official acknowledgement of Israel's arsenal, and where the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] inspectors were.[N] Israel's first deliverable nuclear weapon is thought to have been completed in late 1966 or early 1967, making it the sixth country in the world to develop them.[N]
