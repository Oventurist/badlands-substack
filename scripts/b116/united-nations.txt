## Afghanistan cash shipment oversight (2023)

The United Nations played a central role in channelling U.S. assistance into Afghanistan after the country's collapse in August 2021. A November 2023 report by [[john-sopko|John Sopko]] of [[special-inspector-general-for-afghanistan-reconstruction|SIGAR]] recorded that the U.N. had assured him that all of the roughly $11 billion in aid was "placed in designated U.N. accounts in a private bank," and was not "deposited in the central bank or provided to the [[taliban|Taliban]]."[N] The U.N. Assistance Mission in Afghanistan (UNAMA) stated that the cash shipments — about $80 million every 10 to 14 days — were "carefully monitored, audited, inspected, and vetted in accordance with U.N. financial rules and processes."[N] SIGAR nonetheless warned that the Taliban had previously siphoned cash from U.N. shipments and charged royalties and fees on them.[N]

Separately, Russian foreign ministry spokeswoman [[maria-zakharova|Maria Zakharova]] invoked the U.N. nuclear watchdog in November 2023, asking where the [[international-atomic-energy-agency|IAEA]] and its inspectors were after an Israeli minister suggested nuclear weapons use in Gaza was "a possibility."[N]
