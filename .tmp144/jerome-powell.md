## Final Jackson Hole speech as chairman (August 2025)

Powell was scheduled to speak on Friday, August 22, 2025 in what NPR described as his final appearance as Federal Reserve chair at the high-profile meeting in Jackson Hole, Wyoming.[{n}] The speech came as the central bank faced mounting pressure from the [[white-house|White House]] to lower interest rates, three and a half weeks before the Fed's next rate-setting meeting, and as the wide-ranging tariffs President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]] imposed by executive order on April 2 began taking shape after delays and adjustments.[{n}] Powell was expected to address the economic outlook and the central bank's longer-term balancing act between fighting inflation and unemployment; Joe Brusuelas, U.S. chief economist at RSM, cautioned that he "wouldn't hold my breath waiting for Jay Powell to tip his hand on whether the Fed is going to cut rates" in September.[{n}]

Badlands writer [[burning-bright|Burning Bright]] asked "who watches the watchmen?", describing the establishment framing of the speech as a careful balancing act between taming inflation still above the 2% target and bolstering a weakening labor market in which July's jobs report showed fewer additions than expected and downward revisions erased prior gains.[{n}] He argued Powell was trapped: raising rates would deepen the jobs slowdown and risk a deflationary slump, while lowering them would spark inflation amid tariff pressures even as it juiced asset valuations and delivered a market surge aligned with Trump's "Golden Age" promises.[{n}] In this reading, rate cuts would prime the system for a full monetary reset, flooding liquidity into a debt-riddled structure until it forced a shift toward sound money and severed central bankers' control over value creation.[{n}]

The commentary tied the episode to the [[lisa-cook|Lisa Cook]] controversy as a key fracture point, to the fast-tracking of [[stephen-miran|Stephen Miran]] for a Fed board vacancy, and to Treasury Secretary [[scott-bessent|Scott Bessent]]'s anticipation of a "global economic reordering."[{n}] Badlands contributor GMONEY added that the Abolish the IRS movement "can only succeed when enough of us stop voluntarily participating in outright theft."[{n}]
