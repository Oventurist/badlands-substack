## The "Trump–Maduro kayfabe" thesis (August 2025)

Following Bolivian President [[luis-arce|Luis Arce]]'s accusation that Washington was using the drug war as cover for controlling Latin America, Badlands writer [[ghostofbasedpatrickhenry|GhostofBasedPatrickHenry]] argued that "the Trump–Maduro kayfabe is starting to make more and more sense."[{n}] In this reading, the strange tension between President [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]]'s camp and Maduro gives Maduro the narrative shielding he needs to avoid being targeted by the cartels, providing plausible deniability that he is aiding Trump's efforts to bring down the drug trade — with the [[state-department|State Department]]'s $50 million bounty on his head, announced about a week earlier, serving as convincing cover.[{n}]

The commentary noted this was in addition to Maduro's indictment on drug charges by the first Trump administration in 2020, but concluded from independent research that Maduro and Trump were both targeting the same corrupt corporate executives at the world's most powerful commodity trading firms, such as [[glencore|Glencore]].[{n}] Taking down former Glencore CEO Alex Beard was described as one thing, and taking down all of the Central American cartels simultaneously as something entirely different; two weeks earlier it had been reported that Trump was preparing to use the U.S. military against those groups.[{n}] The writer judged that if the cartels believed Maduro was helping Trump, he would not be safe anywhere in the world, let alone in [[venezuela|Venezuela]] — though they were probably busy trying to stay alive in the face of U.S. military action.[{n}]
