## The Mirrored Quadrant ("Mirror, Mirror," December 2022)

"Mirror, Mirror" (December 14, 2022), the fifth entry in the Badlands Media China series following "Taiwan Tipping," "[[tigers-and-flies|Tigers and Flies]]," "The Two Chinas" and "Empire's Enigma," advanced the fullest statement to that point of the Sovereign Alliance thesis by proposing a "Trinity" of [[donald-trump|Donald Trump]], [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] and [[xi-jinping|Xi Jinping]] whose combined mandate could, in the author's view, usher in an age of stability, peace and prosperity.[N] The piece acknowledged that a "secret" alliance among the three men would seem outlandish to some and self-evident to others, and urged readers to work through the cognitive dissonance by noting that all three had faced coordinated and mirrored attacks from the same globalist Deep State media apparatus.[N] Swapping the headlines of globalist outlets between Trump, Xi and Putin, the essay argued, leaves the reader disoriented as to which figure most strongly draws the ire of the "collective of collectivists" and financiers such as [[george-soros|George Soros]].[N]

The article organised its case as a "Mirrored Quadrant" of four shared attributes.[N] The first, **populist rise**, held that each leader tapped a pre-existing well of national sovereign sentiment rather than creating it: Trump broke decades of administrative tradition by extolling independence and strength in the manner of Reagan and Kennedy; Putin rose after a century of communist infiltration and Soviet subjugation of the Russian people, as chronicled in the [[righteous-russia|Righteous Russia]] series; and Xi rode a nationalist populist wave on a campaign promise to war against the "tigers and flies" of an entrenched enemy.[N]

The second quadrant, **military backing**, observed that Trump lacked a public military background — though he attended the New York Military Academy in the 1960s — yet leaned heavily on his support within the US military, particularly the Marines, delivering his inauguration speech ringed by Marines in what the author read as a shot across the bow to collectivists.[N] Putin possessed a far more extensive military career and made similar appointments on assuming the Russian presidency in the late 1990s; Xi broke with long-standing Chinese political tradition by forming his own security service rather than relying on one funded by the [[chinese-communist-party|CCP]], and restructured the [[peoples-liberation-army|People's Liberation Army]] chain of command to make himself effective commander-in-chief.[N]

The third, **war on the oligarchy**, argued that Trump went to war on a narrative level with the US political class, producing the steady exposure of the American "elite" as Fortune 100 companies and both opposition and intra-party officials closed ranks against him.[N] Putin's rise came not directly after the USSR's collapse but after a decade of faux-capitalist rule in which Russian production and lending were divided among an oligarchy the author called perhaps the most infamous in the world; Putin then spent the first decade of the new millennium waging legal and sometimes kinetic war on those oligarchs and transferring their assets back to the Russian people.[N] Xi, on this account, went furthest of all, rooting out, arresting and imprisoning the Chinese communist oligarchy of legacy families and bloodlines, officially punishing millions charged with corruption along with thousands of government insiders.[N] The net effect claimed for Xi's campaign was the purging of legacy families from a CCP that had served its people up to the American oligarchy represented by [[apple|Apple]], [[google|Google]] and [[amazon|Amazon]], farming out a slave class to the collectivist West.[N]

The fourth quadrant, **war on the globalists**, ran concurrent with the third: the purging of national oligarchies coincided with the decoupling of these nations from the collectivist system represented by the American oligarchy and the administrative power base of the [[european-union|European Union]].[N] Putin's approach was described as the least subtle, effectively neutering the globalist system through the Special Military Operation in the "proxy state" of Ukraine; Xi's antagonism toward [[joe-biden|Biden]], [[justin-trudeau|Trudeau]] and Brussels, together with strengthening ties to the [[brics|BRICS]] nations and the "SATE" nations of Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Egypt, was read as evidence of the sovereign cooperative he seeks to forge.[N] The essay added that the engineered COVID pandemic and ensuing lockdowns had produced a rapid decoupling of eastern and western economic systems, inviting a recasting of the previous years' trauma.[N]

The article stressed that the Trinity is not alone — naming [[jair-bolsonaro|Jair Bolsonaro]] in Brazil, [[viktor-orban|Viktor Orbán]] in Hungary and "perhaps" [[giorgia-meloni|Giorgia Meloni]] in Italy as leaders rising to strike at their own heads of the "Hegelian Hydra" — but held that only the US–China–Russia trinity, if realised and actualised, would be capable not merely of creating the conditions for peace and sovereignty but of mandating it.[N] It closed by suggesting that in a macro, zoomed-out view the pieces were already being moved into place for "the Sovereign world to come," and directed readers onward to the follow-up piece "CHIPS and Dips."[N]
